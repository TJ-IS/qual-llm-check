## a b s t r a c t

When confronted with competitive strategies in a rapidly changing globalized environment, enterprises gradually shift from <sup>fi</sup>ghting their battles individually to engaging in more collaborative competition. However, for this particular period shift to the cooperative partnership paradigm, only a few empirical studies exist on implementing a practical strategy for collaborative commerce, such as collaborative product commerce (CPC). This research explores the implementation of the CPC system by Taiwanese enterprises from the perspective of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t – the core spirit of CPC – and discusses its relationship with “implementation success,” “implementation satisfaction,” “inter-organizational trust,” and “organizational interdependence.” Findings indicate that successful implementation of CPC should consider the <sup>fi</sup>t between the CPC system and the facilitative processes of the corporation. Additionally, inter-organizational trust and interdependence should be considered because these also affect CPC system implementation.

## 1. Introduction

Collaborative commerce (C-Commerce) is a set of technologies and business practices that allows companies to build stronger relationships with their trading partners by integrating complex and cross-enterprise processes governed by business logic and rules, as well as by work<sup>fl</sup>ows [9]. From 2000 to 2005, the Gartner Group de<sup>fi</sup>ned C-Commerce as the mainstream application of information techniques in developed countries [5]. C-Commerce was implemented because it improved the preciseness of decisionoriented information and was particularly suitable for a collaborative manufacturing environment. The study of the Aberdeen Group [2] optimistically reported that the scale of the C-Commerce market would soon be about 10 times greater than it had been in the year 2000.

Collaborative product commerce (CPC) is a more speci<sup>fi</sup>c and detailed concept than C-Commerce. It uses a new software or service to allow all partners in an industry develop, manufacture, and manage a product collaboratively during its Internet lifecycle ([7]; [32]). CPC utilizes supplier knowledge and the convenience of the Internet to improve product quality [32]. The result of CPC implementation is a CPC system that can integrate information from different information systems (IS), as well as coordinate management processes [6]. Due to the limited number of companies with knowledge of CPC, and even fewer companies implementing CPC, case studies have been employed to explore issues related to CPC system implementation ([40]; [54]; [59]). To date, empirical results on CPC system implementation remain limited.

Since the CPC system assists in collaborating with suppliers and customers, companies tend to be responsive to customers' needs. Consequently, their products become more competitive compared with those of other <sup>fi</sup>rms. The Taiwanese government recognized this trend and created the cash, delivery, and engineering collaboration (CDE<sup>1</sup>) plan. The implementation of CDE came after the e-AB<sup>2</sup> plan of the industry, which was similarly aimed at assisting enterprises with attaining their collective status as a hub for global logistics and decision-making. Another objective of the plan was to combine CDE with the original e-supply chain system and to construct operational competence as a means to enhance C-Commerce among all upstream and downstream companies.

After establishing the basic environment of the inter-organizational electronic business, the Taiwanese government continued to promote the “industry global operation electronic enhancement plan”<sup>3</sup> and the subsequent “industry collaborative design e-plan.”<sup>4</sup> The purpose of these two plans was to make Taiwan the decision-making center of business operations and a “business operation headquarters.” The plans also hypothesized that Taiwan would have a higher business value when enterprises managed and implemented their global arrangement strategies. Thus, it has been proposed that C-Commerce and CPC can be the solution for enterprises, especially when faced with <sup>fi</sup>erce competition brought about by globalization, which in turn has led to a rapidly changing business environment.

While the <sup>fi</sup>t between the ERP system and the organization's characteristics is viewed as critical for a successful ERP implementation ([8]; [27]; [28]; [29]; [55]; [57]), the <sup>fi</sup>t between the CPC system and the organization's characteristics is even more crucial [47]. Organizational <sup>fi</sup>t is based on the consistency between the CPC system and the organizational environment [38]. The degree of <sup>fi</sup>t is imperative for companies utilizing the collaborative product development approach to strengthen their advantages and competitiveness in the global market. Thus, this study focuses on the process of achieving CPC system implementation from the perspective of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t, which includes issues of “implementation success,” “implementation satisfaction,” “inter-organizational trust,” and “organizational interdependence.”

In summary, the research objectives are as follows: (1) to understand the development of the current CPC situation, (2) to explore the concept of <sup>fi</sup>tness of IS implementation and the success factors during CPC system implementation, (3) to study the relationship between CPC organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic and its implementation success, and (4) to propose feasible solutions for CPC system implementation. Using the perspective of Taiwanese enterprises as an example for the application of our <sup>fi</sup>ndings, the applicability of the <sup>fi</sup>ndings to other countries worldwide can therefore be expected. The <sup>fi</sup>ndings can be particularly applicable to the Greater China region where most companies are segmented into niches in the form of vertical disintegration [6].

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Collaborative product commerce (CPC)

Collaborative product commerce is a new concept that supports inter-enterprise collaboration throughout a product lifecycle [32]. The Aberdeen Group [1], a globally renowned research institute, reports that CPC essentially means “to integrate various product-oriented business formulas of different enterprises into the software and service of a single, closed-loop solution”; it further describes CPC as “the software and service using Internet technology.” With CPC, individual enterprises can collaboratively develop, manufacture, and manage products throughout the whole lifecycle. Companies also need not worry about their roles in the product commercialization process, the computers used, or even actual geographic locations or positions in the supply chain network [6].

The CPC has two aspects that are signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly different from supply chain management (SCM) and customer relationship management (CRM) tools. First, CPC is a collaborative process across organizations with common suppliers or service consumers. Second, CPC processes are transparent to every stakeholder in the supply chain. CPC uses an open communication platform that reaches beyond a speci<sup>fi</sup>c supply chain network where only a single purchaser is embedded. Effective communication among disparate purchasers, which often include competitors, can provide additional bene<sup>fi</sup>ts to these purchasers [18].

The goal of CPC is to cope with a new business environment of virtual enterprises and to improve enterprise competitiveness by ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently developing and quickly producing innovative and highquality products [32]. Furthermore, CPC has two major business goals: (1) to connect supplier knowledge on certain products into a single and expanded experience base as a means to improve product quality and (2) to use the features of the Internet to reduce production time and distance in order to seize opportunities and allow for quick entry into the market. A set of typical CPC solutions combines functions like information management, version control, distribution management, and work process management. It also has additional capacities for checking and modifying <sup>fi</sup>les and drafting.

## 2.2. Collaborative product commerce implementation

The result of CPC system implementation should be a CPC system that is capable of integrating information from different IS and management processes, which may include ERP, SCM, KM, EDI, BPR, and strategic alliances [6]. Thus, the aspects involved in CPC system implementation are extremely wide-ranged. Implementation success factors for different IS may provide guidance for the implementation of the CPC system. Thus, for this research, the different aspects have been reorganized, as shown in Table 1 ([3]; [4]; [15]; [23]; [37]; [43]; [45]). According to Table 1, support from top executives appears to be the most frequent success factor among different IS, whereas other success factors are contributed by different IS. For example, support from system software suppliers can be sizeable if the required functions are integrated into the current IS of partners. Organizations that do not have access to this professional knowledge might encounter serious problems in the integration ([22]; [25]; [42]; [48]; [49]).

Although there have been a number of studies and discussions on ERP research with respect to the planning of a highly complicated IS, the application of these studies to the planning of the CPC system remains dif<sup>fi</sup>cult. The latter not only has to consider ERP in the organization plan but also has to deal with the IS of the corporate partners [47]. Thus, companies may face more problems that are complicated when implementing the CPC system, as compared to those implementing ERP. Some challenges in the implementation of the CPC system may emanate from internal integration (i.e., business process integration, resources allocation, and distribution) and external challenges (i.e., communication, partnership management, and requirements of partners) [6].

As the objective of the CPC system is to cooperate with other companies while discovering business advantages, partnership management should be one of the preliminary steps of CPC system implementation. Partnership is “a tailored business relationship based upon mutual trust, openness, shared risk, and shared rewards that yield a competitive advent” [34]. Studies have found trust and interdependency to be critical factors in facilitating the formation of a partnership. Both factors also greatly affect the result of the partnership ([35]; [39]). Since the establishment of the CPC system may force corporate partners to exchange classi<sup>fi</sup>ed information, not all partners will be willing to join the trans-organizational CPC system. Thus, trust and interdependency should be required for future CPC system implementations ([13]; [25]; [26]; [35]; [44]).

## 2.2.1. Current vendors and methods to implement CPC

Currently, software companies do not have common architectures or standards to implement the CPC system. Nevertheless, the implementation of the CPC system has been attempted in three ways. The <sup>fi</sup>rst is by utilizing the existing ERP system in the company. Connecting the ERP systems of companies is often conducted by an Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) or Original Design Manufacturer (ODM). This can greatly improve the operational ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency; however this cannot be achieved without rich and detailed knowledge of these ERP systems. The ERP vendors for this method include i2, Oracle, and SAP.

The second involves developing the CPC system based on the framework of the RosettaNet. In Taiwan, the government leads the development of the RosettaNet standard for business communications. Thus, it is a well-accepted standard for most companies wanting to implement the CPC system. This type of CPC system is independent of the existing IS of the company and thus possesses the <sup>fl</sup>exibility to connect various kinds of IS. This type of vendor, which includes Agile, Dassault, Lotus, W&Jsoft, and Com2B, provides a complete CPC system to companies.

The third approach involves integrating all existing IS of related companies and redesigning business processes in order to achieve the required CPC system. Vendors need to have knowledge of one industry to facilitate the provision of suggestions to enhance the CPC process. Thus, the implementation of the CPC system can accelerate the exchange of information and also improve the ef<sup>fi</sup>- ciency of the collaboration. The implementation usually requires more time than other methods, but brings companies the maximum bene<sup>fi</sup>t. Vendors of this method are usually system integrators, such as Accenture, HP, Sun, and IBM.

## 2.3. Organizational fit

The concept of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t originates from the contingency theory, a knowledge body developed in the 1980s to explore the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of a contingency factor on organizational structure and management. It suggests that an appropriate organizational structure depends on the business environment. Thus, no two organizations will have the same organizational structure, business strategy, or process in different environments [10]. Hence, the <sup>fi</sup>t among all the contingency factors in<sup>fl</sup>uences the success of an organization. Managers must identify appropriate contingency factors (e.g., strategy, organizational structure, environment, technique, and scale) with signi<sup>fi</sup>cant in<sup>fl</sup>uence on the organization and deal with them carefully.

There have been plenty of recent studies extending the concept of <sup>fi</sup>t to the <sup>fi</sup>eld of IS ([11]; [14]; [16]; [20]; [21]; [36]; [51]; [57]). Weill and Olson [58] reviewed research papers using the contingency theory in the <sup>fi</sup>eld of management information systems (MIS) and found that over 70% of the papers agreed that if contingency factors (strategy, structure, technology, etc.) could <sup>fi</sup>t the business environment well, the organizational performance would be better. For small-scale enterprises, Janson and Subramanian [29] pointed out that a correlation exists between successful factors of set software establishment and compatibility of software <sup>fi</sup>rms and buyers, as well as between the compatibility of the software and the organization. The concept of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t has also been explored in many ERP implementation studies, which found that the organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of an ERP had a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant in<sup>fl</sup>uence on its successful implementation ([8]; [27]; [28]; [41]; [55]; [57]). The interactions between IT resources and contingency factors (i.e., size, culture, and structure) were signi<sup>fi</sup>cant to the success of ERP [28]. Based on the above studies, it can be stated that the concept of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t is important in understanding the factors contributing to the success of the different IT implementation processes.

The concept of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t may aid in understanding whether or not the CPC system matches the request of a user, as well as provide a higher degree of satisfaction. The design, production, and product delivery of CPC entail collaboration with corporate departments, suppliers, resellers, and customers [32]. However, relationships in the organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of the CPC system and inter-organizational relationships, as well as from results on CPC system implementation, have yet to be empirically studied. Therefore, this research references the research model of Hong and Kim [27] to investigate further the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t factors.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

The purpose of this research is to understand the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of the organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of the CPC system based on the result and ultimate success of its implementation. In the research model (Fig. 1), <sup>fi</sup>ve variables are included: CPC organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic, inter-organizational trust, organizational interdependence, implementation success, and implementation satisfaction. Below are the developments of the research hypotheses.

The contingency theory suggests that organizational performance can be better if organizational strategy, structure, and technology <sup>fi</sup>t the business environment well ([10]; [58]). The implementation of the CPC system is geared at improving enterprise competitiveness through the ef<sup>fi</sup>cient and quick production of a product that is responsive to customer needs and maintains high product quality [32]. To achieve this goal, the CPC system should <sup>fi</sup>t the organizational process, data process, and user interface in order for the organization to easily utilize the information supplied by suppliers and customers. Thus, the contingency theory and the concept of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t are important in understanding the factors contributing to the success of CPC system implementation.

A better <sup>fi</sup>t between the organization and the CPC system may provide a higher degree of performance and satisfaction after implementation. The CPC system provides a communication channel that enables a collaborative process across organizations [18]. Moreover, the CPC system can contribute to obtaining supplier information, increasing collaboration among companies, and enhancing <sup>fl</sup>exibility in product development. The processes of CPC relate to collaboration with different corporate departments, suppliers, retailers, and customers [32]. The degree of <sup>fi</sup>t between the CPC system and the original IS in the company can reduce the degree of process redesign and increase the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of information integration rather than independent information. Therefore, organizational <sup>fi</sup>tness positively affects the successful and satisfactory CPC implementation. For this reason, $\mathrm { H } _ { 1 \mathrm { a } }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 1 \mathrm { b } }$ are proposed.

$\mathrm { H } _ { 1 \mathrm { a } } \colon$ The organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of the CPC system positively affects the success of the implementation of the CPC system.

$\mathrm { H } _ { 1 \mathrm { b } } \colon$ The organizational fit characteristic of the CPC system positively affects the satisfaction with the implementation of the CPC system.

Trust is the foundation of inter-organizational cooperation [35]. Previous papers have mentioned the importance of trust in electronic business and electronic commerce. The implementation of the CPC system can help companies rapidly deliver products to customers, obtain a <sup>fl</sup>exible product design to produce customized products, and reduce time to market. Before the implementation of the CPC system however, the <sup>fi</sup>rst step requires different organizations to trust each other and then to share information among partners ([13]; [25]; [52]; [59]). When inter-organizational trust is high, partners can share information and obtain the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts even before implementation of the CPC system. When inter-organizational trust is low, partners may not want to share information until implementation of the CPC system forces them to do so. Thus, when high inter-organizational trust exists, the organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic has minimal impact on the success of and satisfaction with the implementation. On the contrary, when low inter-organizational trust exists, the impact of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic is higher. For companies with low interorganizational trust, high organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic has a larger impact on implementation success and satisfaction than in companies with high inter-organizational trust. Therefore, $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 \mathrm { a } }$ and H are proposed.

H : At a high level of inter-organizational trust, the effect of low organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic on the success of the implementation of the CPC system is larger than that of a high organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic.

$\mathrm { H } _ { 2 \mathrm { b } } { \mathrm { : } }$ At a high level of inter-organizational trust, the effect of low organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic on the satisfaction with the implementation of the CPC system is larger than that of a high organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic.

Effective CPC system implementation requires cooperation and information sharing among companies. However, the cooperation and information sharing by a company with other companies may also damage its competitiveness. Companies may have doubts about the implementation of a system that may damage their competitiveness. This dilemma can be solved by high organizational interdependence among companies and through available mutual bene<sup>fi</sup>ts [39].

Organizational interdependence has proven important in interorganizational system integration and utilization ([25]; [26]; [35]; [48]). Since the CPC system helps utilize and coordinate resources among companies, it can maximize the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts for companies with high organizational interdependence. Nevertheless, the resources and information of companies with low organizational interdependence may have few bene<sup>fi</sup>ts for other companies. Thus, the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of the CPC system are limited for these companies. Therefore, when organizational interdependence is high, the higher organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic increases the success of and satisfaction with implementation. When organizational interdependence is low, the higher organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic does not substantially increase the success of the implementation and level of satisfaction. As a result, $\mathrm { H } _ { 3 a }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 3 \mathrm { b } }$ are proposed.

H : At a high level of organizational interdependence, the effect of high organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic on the success of the implementation of the CPC system is larger than that of a low organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic.

H : At a high level of organizational interdependence, the effect of high organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic on the satisfaction with the implementation of the CPC system is larger than that of a low organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic.

## 4. Research method

## 4.1. Research design

The subjects of this research were managers of IS departments belonging to Taiwanese companies that had implemented the CPC system. There were three types of vendors providing CPC system implementation support: ERP vendors, CPC system vendors, and system integrators. Companies that adopted support from these vendors were the candidate subjects in this research and were contacted mainly through three channels: (1) software companies with CPC solutions, (2) CPC-related seminars, and (3) a company list that the government facilitated to implement the CPC.

For data collection, this research adopted a survey (e.g., three approaches, as mentioned previously). A total of 303 questionnaires

## 4.2. Measures

The organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of the CPC system adapted from Hong and Kim [27] contained three sub-dimensions: data <sup>fi</sup>t, process <sup>fi</sup>t, and user interface <sup>fi</sup>t. The process <sup>fi</sup>t measured the <sup>fi</sup>tness between the organizational processes built into the CPC system and the organizations that implemented the CPC system. The data <sup>fi</sup>t determined whether the data format and the input and output data of the CPC system integrated directly with the data in the organization of implementation. The user interface <sup>fi</sup>t determined whether the user interface of the CPC system met the requirements of the organizational implementation.

The successful implementation of the CPC system was measured in two ways: implementation success and implementation satisfaction. To measure the successful implementation of the CPC system, we considered implementation success measures like cost, time, performance, and pro<sup>fi</sup>t as mentioned by Hong and Kim [27] and implementation satisfaction based on the work of Premkumar et al. [49]. Furthermore, inter-organizational trust adapted from Lee and Lim [35] and organizational interdependency from Mohr and Spekman [39] were considered as the two moderators in the relationship between the organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic and the successful implementation of the CPC system. The Chinese version of these measures was examined by <sup>fi</sup>ve experts in the <sup>fi</sup>eld of information management, and certain terms and statements were revised to ensure that the questionnaire would be understood by the participants. All measures were speci<sup>fi</sup>ed according to sevenpoint Likert's scales. These measures are attached in Appendix A.

## 5. Analysis and results

## 5.1. Data stability analysis

To ensure the uniformity between the traditional questionnaire and the e-questionnaire samples, these questionnaires were grouped to test sample stability. The result showed that years of corporation establishment, number of employees, business size, percentage of budget for the information department, number of employees in the information department, and length of cooperation with the partners between these two groups were not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly different at the signi<sup>fi</sup>cance level of 0.05. The result indicated that the samples from different sources had a certain degree of homogeneity. To ensure uniformity in samples before and after the distribution of follow-up questionnaires, the question sheets were also grouped according to the sequence of the collected samples. Results also showed that the samples before and after the distribution of follow-up questionnaires were not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly different at the signi<sup>fi</sup>cance level of 0.05.

## 5.2. Reliability and validity analysis

Reliability of the measurement showed the stability and consistency of each construct. Validity of the measurement is the possibility to measure what researchers want to measure. Results from the reliability analysis showed that Cronbach's α of all variables was larger than 0.7, which was acceptable [46]. The content and face validity of the measure were achieved by adopting items from previous literature, which were revised by three MIS scholars. Finally, this research used the principal component analysis to examine the convergent and discriminant validity. Items with factor loadings smaller than 0.6 were deleted [24]. Results from the reliability and validity analyses are shown in Table 3 and Table 4.

Analytical results shown in Table 5 revealed that no serious collinearity problem exists. The correlation between the moderators and the independent variable was lower than 0.7 [24].

## 5.4. Discussion

The organizational <sup>fi</sup>t characteristic of the CPC system revealed a positive in<sup>fl</sup>uence on the implementation success and satisfaction of the project, which corresponds to the research results in the works of Lucas [37] and Hong and Kim [27]. During the implementation of the CPC system, coordinating the proprietary resources among the partners played a critical role in implementation success. When the project managers plan the implementation of the CPC system, they must evaluate in advance the proprietary level of each resource among partners as well as the integration of the CPC system into the work<sup>fl</sup>ow of corporations. Since most companies involved in this study were from the manufacturing industry, fewer changes to the current work<sup>fl</sup>ow and IS meant fewer risks and potential problems. A better <sup>fi</sup>t between the CPC system and the existing IS can lead to implementation success and satisfaction.

High switching costs decrease the perceived bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of IS implementation [31].

## 6. Conclusions and limitations

When enterprises select the proper CPC set software for implementation, they tend to include various complicated and advanced functions in the IS. However, the CPC system is more complex than other IS. System vendors usually underestimate the importance of <sup>fi</sup>t between the CPC system and the organization implementing the system. Thus, enterprises should carefully choose competent advisers for optimal guidance. If the CPC system cannot <sup>fi</sup>t well within the organization, its adjustment might increase future system maintenance cost. Hence, recognizing organizational needs and selecting the appropriate CPC system should be cautiously conducted at all times. The company should never be blinded by the exaggerated advertisement of CPC vendors and their announcement of low initial cost of implementation.

Before enterprises adopt a CPC system, they must carefully choose appropriate partners. The degrees of inter-organizational trust and organizational interdependence are critical factors in selecting partners. The term “collaboration” refers to the state of connection between two parties. While enterprises perform the core activity, collaboration should be implemented to handle other activities. This is a process that produces bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, creates success, and requires an open attitude. Through the formation of industrial value, an understanding among collaborative corporations therefore emerges. This becomes a truly strong advantage over the competitors.

During the process of completing the questionnaires, 41 enterprises (13.53%) reported that they had not implemented the related CPC system and were not clear on the concept of CPC. Therefore, CPC vendors should enhance the promotion of the concept of CPC and its bene<sup>fi</sup>ts to each enterprise of CPC. Ferreira et al. [17] also found that the CPC system provided by CPC vendors was too complicated and that most enterprises were at a loss on what to do.

The collaborative system of the lifecycle management of various products in Taiwan was based on ERP. However, this method led to an overly complicated solution because ERP was originally not suitable for a dispersed environment. It is therefore suggested that CPC vendors focus on one solution (i.e., collaboration design) and target the management of a collaboration system in a dispersed environment. The structure of industries in Taiwan is still simple, composed mostly of medium-sized enterprises with tight budgets for IS. Thus, it requires a specialized CPC system, which can implement the complete concept of C-Commerce among Taiwanese enterprises.

This research has several limitations. First, results indicate that the CPC system is still regarded as a new system in Taiwan, which further suggests that the system has not yet become a mainstream application [5]. The length of collaboration among partners in almost half of the samples is about one year. However, crossorganizational collaboration projects usually require more than one year. This indicates that these organizations still have a long way to go in their implementation of the CPC system. Second, most samples come from the manufacturing industry. Thus, the <sup>fi</sup>ndings of this research should be considered cautiously. Third, the targets of this research are information department supervisors, but their cognition is different from that of CPC system users. This difference cannot be inferred from the current study.

The topic of this research is the potential impacts of organizational <sup>fi</sup>t to deal with the CPC project. Thus, other factors, such as the culture of the organization, strategy, and similar factors were not included. It is also important to note that the research scope was limited to enterprises that have implemented the CPC system in Taiwan. A follow-up study may reveal different results in other countries.
