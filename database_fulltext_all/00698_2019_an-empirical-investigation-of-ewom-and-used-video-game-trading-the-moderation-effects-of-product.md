# An empirical investigation of eWOM and used video game trading: The moderation efects of product features

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Electronic word-of-mouth User generated content Used video games Consumer behavior

## A B S T R A C T

With the rise of social media platforms and online communities, the impact of electronic word of mouth (ewOM increasingly interests academics and practitioners. Psychological theories of consumer choice suggest that consumers' reliance on online reviews varies based on their heterogeneous individual traits and preferences. We propose that firms can infer consumer heterogeneities through product features consumers are interested in. The video game market provides us an ideal research context to investigate whether and how consumers in each product sub-category would respond to eWOM differently. Specifically, this research empirically examines the efect of eWOM on used video games' price decline and investigates the moderating role of two product features: games' violence level and social orientation. In general, we find that the extent of used video games' price decline is positively and significantly associated with review volume, negativity, subjectivity, and readability. Furthermore, our results reveal interesting contrasts between games with diferent violence levels as well as games with diferent social orientation. We find that players of high-violence games react more negatively to review volume than players of low-violence games. Meanwhile, compared to users of single player games, users of socially interactive games react more negatively to review readability and negativity. We contribute to eWOM literature by theoretically emphasizing and justifying the important moderating role of individual predispositions based on the psychological choice model. The results suggest how review platforms and firms can tailor review recommendations and presentations across various products even within the same product market.

## 1. Introduction

Electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) is any statement made by consumers about a product or service shared through various digital channels [63]. The extant research in the past decade has demonstrated the efectiveness of eWOM on consumers' purchase decision [20]. De spite its importance, eWOM only represents one type of contextual influences received by consumers during their shopping process. Psychological theories of consumer choice [40] suggest that individual predispositions interact with environmental impacts and jointly afect consumers' decision making. In other words, consumers' reliance on online reviews varies based on their heterogeneous individual traits and preferences [20,94].

Understanding those individual diferences can enable firms to adapt their marketing strategies to better serve diferent customer needs. However, it is challenging for firms to efectively uncover customers' predispositions. We propose a simple yet practical way to infer consumer heterogeneities through product features consumers are interested in. Nevertheless it is unclear whether and how consumers in each product sub-category would respond to eWOM diferently, and the video game market provides us an ideal research context to fill the gap.

There is a rich body of literature suggesting that two product features in the video game market are closely related to consumer psychological diferences, which in turn afect how consumers process and depend on eWOM messages. The first is games' violence level, which induces players' aggression and physiological arousal as well as limits their information processing capabilities [2,27,50]. The second is games' social orientation (i.e., single player vs. multiple player games), which reflects players' diferent self-construal and dependence on others' comments [18,19,64,91]. Theoretically, customers' predispositions associated with those two types of game sub-categorizations can afect how they internalize eWOM. Practically, industry standards such as Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) rating enable us to easily classify games along those two dimensions. Prior literature explores eWOM's diferences between search and experience products [26]. We further suggest that consumers' reliance on online reviews could vary across products even within the same market, and such idea can be generalized to other contexts, such as various types of entertainment markets and social commerce [94].

Another reason why we choose the video game market to examine eWOM's diferential roles across product sub-categories is its economic importance. The global video game industry is one of the fastest growing sectors in recent years. It generates more revenue than the movie and music industries [69]. According to a most recent report released by the Entertainment Software Association,<sup>1</sup> the industry contributed \$11.7 billion to US GDP in 2016, and two third of US households owned a video game device.

Prior studies [63,94] have investigated the impact of eWOM on new video game sales, and we extend their research into the used video game market for two reasons. First of all, the used-good market is crucial for the video game industry, since players tire quickly with use [48,63,79]. Consequently, many consumers trade in games after playing, generating a large number of used games and creating a very vibrant market. According to Shiller [79], 40% of sales are used copies one year after new games' release. However, empirical research on used video game business is still sparse among academics [48,79]. Second, significant uncertainties exist in online used-good markets, where consumers heavily rely on eWOM to make their purchase decisions [34]. Ghose [34] studies used-good markets for search goods (i.e., PDAs, digital cameras, audio players, and laptops), and we extend the research into used-good markets for digital experience goods. Following Ghose [34], we use price decline to quantify market reaction to eWOM in used-good markets. Price decline is defined as “ratio of diference between the new good price and the used good price (sale price) to the new good price” [34], which determines used goods' sales [45]. Sales data at individual game levels for used video games are not readily available, thus we focus on price decline instead. Moreover, instead of numerical ratings, we consider more fine-grained and nuanced textual analyses of online user reviews and investigate how eWOM afects used video games' price decline in multifaceted ways.

To answer our research questions, we empirically analyze monthly price decline of all used video games released in 2015 for the top three video game consoles in the US market. In general. we find that the extent of used video games' price decline is positively and significantly associated with review volume, negativity, subjectivity, and read ability. Furthermore, our results reveal interesting contrasts between games with diferent violence levels as well as games with diferent social orientation. We find that, compared to low-violence game players, players of high-violence games react more negatively to review volume. Meanwhile, compared to users of single player games, users of socially interactive games react more negatively to review readability and negativity. We contribute to eWOM literature by theoretically emphasizing and justifying the important moderating role of individual predispositions based on the psychological choice model. We propose to infer consumers' choice-specific preferences based on product features consumers are interested in, and empirically validate diferential impact of eWOM across those features. Comprehensive text analytics en able us to further diferentiate eWOM's impact along various textural features. Our research also sheds insights on the used video game market, which is economically important yet under-studied. The results suggest how review platforms and firms can tailor review recommendations and presentations across various products even within the same product market

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review related literature and propose our conceptual framework. We develop individual hypotheses in Section 3, and explain our research context, data collection procedures, econometric analyses, as well as results in Section 4. Finally, in Section 5, we summarize our findings, contribu tions, and point out directions for future research.

## 2. Literature review and conceptual framework

eWOM literature has examined various antecedents associated with its helpfulness as well as its impact in diferent contexts [7,67]. Cheung and Thadani [20] conduct a comprehensive review of eWOM studies and propose an integrative framework explaining how eWOM influences consumer behavior. In their model, stimuli (i.e., various factors of eWOM messages such as valence and volume) directly afect responses such as consumers' attitude, purchase intention, as well as final pur chase decisions. Moreover, the strength of consumer responses towards eWOM messages varies, depending on consumers' individual characteristics, reviewers' characteristics, and the types of review platform.

Our study extends and strengthens Cheung and Thadani's model [20] with psychological theories of consumer choice, which systematically explain why consumer diferences influence their responses to conflict situations. Hansen [40] proposes the psychological choice model where consumers' responses are jointly afected by individual predispositions and environmental impacts. The model suggests that individual characteristics influence consumers' choice behavior through interaction with situational factors. We propose our conceptual framework based on both eWOM literature as well as the psychological choice model (Fig. 1). In our research, consumers decide whether or not to purchase used video games and their choices drive price decline experienced in the market. Consumers read online reviews before purchasing used games, and eWOM literature suggests various textural features of review messages as the environmental impacts. However, consumers' salient reliance on online reviews varies based on heterogonous individual predispositions, and choice-specific preferences can be inferred from the product features consumers are interested in. We explain each major component of our research model and review related literature as follows.

Responses towards eWOM messages are usually measured by attitude, purchase intention, and purchase decisions [36]. Many researches have demonstrated that positive reviews are associated with increased product sales [30,32,36]. In our research, we study used video games, and accurate sales information at the individual game level is not accessible. As aforementioned we follow Ghose [34] and use price decline to assess market reactions and economic impacts of eWOM [34].

Messages in eWOM are situational influences acting on consumers during their decision-making processes, and detailed text reviews convey more information than simple numerical star ratings [46]. The eWOM literature has identified many textural features related to reviews' efectiveness to convince consumers [20]. These features can be extracted and quantified via emerging text analytical tools [36] to predict eWOM's economic impacts. Following eWOM literature, we also use natural language processing (NLP) techniques to identify key text features relevant to eWOM usefulness, including volume, length, valence, subjectivity, and readability. Volume refers to the number of reviews available to consumers, and is important in creating market awareness and publicity for new products [6,12,30]. Another measure reflecting the amount of information available to consumers is review length [36]. Valence captures emotions embedded in review texts. Reviewers' feelings can be shared with consumers reading their reviews. and consequently afect others' judgements and choices [71]. Further more, due to negativity bias, negatively valenced reviews usually have stronger influence than positively valenced reviews [8,71]. As we examine price drop of used video games, we choose to focus on the negativity aspect of eWOM messages. When reviewers leave comments for products and services, they can describe product features objectively while also provide own opinion subjectively [36]. Subjective positioning for experience goods, such as movies and video games, is influential. We thus focus on eWOM subjectivity [35]. Lastly, review quality is a key central route through which consumers form their attitude and purchase decisions towards a product [20], and we use review readability to measure the quality of reviews' textual content [36].

Predispositions in individuals, such as personality, interests, and beliefs, influence how they respond to environmental impacts. There are many types of variables to deal with predispositions, and some are choicespecific, including preferences and intentions [40]. Consistent with the theory, we propose to diferentiate customers based on the underlying features of products they prefer. Two closely related studies have ex amined the impact of eWOM on new video game sales [63,94]. Our re search difers from those two studies as we focus on used video games instead of newly released ones. Marchand et al. [63] explore diferential roles of microblogs and eWOM, while we explore diferential roles of eWOM across diferent features of games. Zhu and Zhang [94] use star ratings, while we apply text mining to quantify detailed textual features of eWOM. We later reveal important diferences across various dimensions of eWOM textural features. The moderating product features investigated in our study are crucial in the video game market, yet they have never been explored in prior literature. Zhu and Zhang [94] examine product popularity, and consider products that are newly released within the first four months of their life cycle as popular products. Since we examine used video games, their proposed operationalization would not work well for our case. Instead we focus on two critical game features. violence level and social orientation, since consumers' psychological profiles are very diferent based on these two game features. We discuss our hypotheses in detail in the following section.

## 3. Hypotheses development

Our model intends to investigate the relationship between eWOM messages and price decline of used video games. Product uncertainty, defined as the buyer's dificulty in evaluating the product and predicting how it will perform, can negatively afect the final auction price of used goods in online markets [28]. Since online reviews provide indirect experiences and help prospective consumers reduce uncertainties involved in inferring product quality [71], eWOM could be valuable in preserving the value and reducing price decline of used video games. The eWOM communication can be seen as the interaction between opinion seekers and opinion givers in online systems, where opinion givers exert great influence on others' attitudes and behavior [25]. The first set of hypotheses (H1a-H5a) discuss direct impact of eWOM on the consumer decision-making process, and relevant textual features such as volume and valence are selected based on eWOM literature [20,31]. Furthermore, the psychological choice model suggests that interaction between individual predispositions and situational in fluences, such as eWOM, determines individuals' final response selection [40,94]. Thus, we propose moderating roles of two product fea tures, which signal important individual psychological diferences relevant to their reliance on eWOM.

## 3.1. The direct impacts of eWOM messages

One of the most studied eWOM factors, review volume, is defined as the total number of reviews of each product. We propose that a larger review volume is detrimental to used goods, and is associated with a steeper price decline. The abundance of product information could dilute the usefulness of any single review [70]. With online information increasingly available to consumers, such information may impose a heavy cognitive burden on consumers, resulting in a decrease in perceived usefulness [22]. Moreover, information overload can undermine interpersonal trust among online users [10], and consumers might pay less attention to overwhelming re views. Accordingly, we contend that a larger review volume is associated with information overload incurred by consumers, who may stop reading reviews and even lose interests in exploring and eventually buying the used video game. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1a. Review volume is positively associated with price decline of used video games.

Another textural feature related to the amount of information provided by online reviews is review length, measured as the number of words used in reviews. Review length could afect consumers' responses both positively and negatively [23]. On the one hand, if reviews are lengthy with many words in them, consumers are more likely to find detailed and useful information about the product [70]. However, on the other hand, information overload is more likely to happen when review length increases, and useful information may be diluted by long and wordy comments [33]. Consumers may choose to stay away from products whose reviews require too much cognitive eforts to process. Since online reviews are in general abundant in used video game markets, we expect that the negative impact of review length will dominate the positive impact:

H2a. Review length is positively associated with price decline of used video games.

Negative online reviews, supplying undesired outcomes experienced by other consumers, can increase the perceived risks and potential losses associated with a purchase [73]. The negativity that has an impact on individuals' purchase decisions can translate into a long-term impact such as the decrease of cash flows and stock prices [58]. Compared to positive comments, negative comments are found to be more influential, leading to negativity bias [71]. Consumers will form unfavorable opinions towards a product after reading negative comments from online peers, thereby decreasing their willingness-to-pay for a product. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H3a. Review negativity is positively associated with price decline of used video games.

Product attributes can be diferentiated into objectivity-subjectivity dichotomy [56], and both can afect information inferred by consumers from eWOM messages. Objective messages encounter less cognitive resistance and the message obiectivity enhances the usefulness of information for consumers to utilize in product judgments [55]. Subjective comments reflect reviewers' individual preferences towards the underlying products and services, tend to be emotional, and provide limited support for their arguments [72]. When subjective comments are added in eWOM messages, consumers find it dificult to sort out factual and precise descriptions of video games they try to evaluate [36]. Challenges associated with subjective reviews may cause consumers to discontinue their product search and evaluation process, and leave the used video games before reaching final purchasing decisions. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4a. Review subjectivity is positively associated with price decline of used video games.

Arguments delivered by eWOM messages need to be persuasive and convincing in order to change consumers' attitudes towards the product [20]. However, if those messages' information quality is low, consumers will be distracted and incur higher information-processing costs. eWOM information quality can be reflected by its readability, which quantifies how easy a text message can be understood by readers. It is easier to read and comprehend texts with higher readability. However, Ghose and Ipeirotis [36] find that readability is negatively associated with products sales. The plausible explanation ofered by them is that reviews with higher readability are written in less authoritative and so phisticated language, which reduces the credibility and informativeness of such reviews. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis:

H5a. Review readability is positively associated with price decline of used video games.

## 3.2. The moderation efect of games' violence level

eWOM is a type of environmental stimuli received by consumers during their shopping process, and the psychological choice model suggests that consumers react to environmental stimuli diferently based on their individual traits and preferences [40,94]. In this study, we infer consumer characteristics from the feature of products they prefer. One product feature examined in our study is games' violence level, since high-violence video games induce higher levels of physiological arousal in game players than low-violence games [2,27,50]. Violent entertainment (such as music, television, movies, and video games) appeals to people who seek excitement and sensation in a fantasy world [38,81]. The excitation transfer framework reported by Zillmann [95] posits that physiological arousal generated by an individual's exposure to violence depicted in media content could potentially intensify subsequent aggressiveness in the individual. A laboratory experiment shows that the efects of violent video games can cause a rise in aggression at least 24 h after playing the game [17]. Moreover, Zillmann [95] finds that this residual efect could facilitate arousal to later experiences even if the experiences are not directly related. Players of more violent games would have a greater physiological arousal than players of less violent games, and experience diminishing information processing capabilities. Therefore, games with varying violence levels attract diferent types of players, who then respond to situational influences diferently [61,76].

Although social media provides opportunities to have diagnostic information regarding likely product experience, the increased information load imposes a cognitive processing burden on consumers [15]. When going through a larger volume of messages, the players of high-violence games likely have limited information processing capabilities and experience dificulty in making their purchase decisions. Accordingly, we suggest that the players of high-violence games are less likely to adopt information from a large volume of messages, leading to the following hypothesis:

H1b. The positive efect of review volume on price decline of used video games is stronger for games with a higher level of violence.

Likewise, we contend that messages with longer text have more information. Such messages are more likely to increase the decision dificulty (lower information adoption) for the players of high-violence games. As a result, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H2b. The positive efect of review length on price decline of used video games is stronger for games with a higher level of violence.

Physiological arousal can potentially moderate the influence of environmental factors (such as happy vs. angry voices) on purchase intentions, where the arousal reflects the consumers' emotional states. When the arousal is combined with the need to alleviate the negative emotion, the heightened arousal reduces attentional capability and negative environmental stimuli are more likely ignored in the associated decision process [62]. In addition, players of high-violence games may be accustomed to unfavorable communications. Therefore, we suggest that the negativity in review texts (stimuli) has less negative impact on the players of high-violence games than players of low-violence games. The following hypothesis is thus proposed:

H3b. The positive efect of negativity on price decline of used video games is weaker for games with a higher level of violence.

The higher the strength of the message's argument is, the lower the level of consumers' physiological arousal is needed to permit more cognitive elaboration upon the message [76]. Cheung et al. [21] suggest that a message with stronger arguments tends to increase consumers' attitude towards the message through the consumers' careful deliberation about the merits of the arguments, consequently yielding more favorable responses. Past literature determines argument strength in a persuasive communication by the criteria of message quality, including objectivity-subjectivity [82,86]. Although subjective messages tend to provide less useful and low-quality information for consumers to judge products, they require less cognitive capacity to process the messages. As we have discussed earlier, the players of higher-violence games likely have a higher level of physiological arousal. These players are more likely to adopt a subjective message without further questioning the reasons of its arguments nor the supporting factual evidence. Therefore, compared to players of low-violence games, players of high violence games may be less uncertain about the purchase of their games while reading subjective comments, and the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4b. The positive efect of subjectivity on price decline of used video games is weaker for games with a higher level of violence.

Consumers tend to perceive the messages with higher readability as less authoritative [36]. Due to limited capacity, aroused consumers tend to selectively process information perceived to have higher value [74]. Since authoritativeness is one key element in Web information value [80], compared to players of low-violence games, players of highviolence games are likely to have lower capability and become likely to pay attention to more authoritative messages. As a result, messages with higher authority (lower readability) are more useful for players of high-violence games, leading to the following proposed hypothesis:

H5b. The positive efect of readability on price decline of used video games is stronger for games with a higher level of violence.

## 3.3. The moderation efect of games' social orientation

The second product feature that we expect to moderate the relationship between eWOM and consumer responses is games' social orientation, which afects players' dependence on eWOM. The video game consoles of the latest generation include PlayStation 4, Xbox One, and Wii U [54]. There are two types of games for those game consoles. The first type is single player games, where only one person can play throughout the gaming session and there are no social interactions facilitated by the game console's platform. The second type is socially interactive games, where in-game social interactions between players are facilitated by the game console's platform. Socially interactive games enable players to interact with each other in the game [18]. They are built on social platforms, which provide players online identities and enable communications among them. Some games enable the players to team up and be aware of others' actions in games. One such example is massively multiplayer online games (MMOs), which foster sociability and cultivate virtual communities among players [78]. For instance, one of the best-selling video games is the Call of Duty game, a socially interactive game that allows players on the PlayStation Network to take part in fast-paced virtual shootouts with real players [49].

There are clear diferences between players of single player games and players of socially interactive games. Interpersonal ties are formed among players of socially interactive games, but such game-enabled social interactions among players do not exist in single player games. Consequently, diferent social orientation of games leads to diferent self-construal of their players. While single game players tend to be independent, socially interactive game players are more interdependent due to their inherent connections to other players [19,64]. Thus, we can view self-construal as an individual predisposition that can interact with situational factors, and it has been shown to moderate eWOM's valence dimension [91]. We further explore how self-construal inferred from games' social orientation moderates other textual features of eWOM.

Matook et al. [65] suggest that excessive postings and the related information overload are perceived as annoying by online users and their social networks, consequently leading to the users' distrust of posted messages. As the number of messages in social networking in creases, users feel tired of activities related to the usage of online social networks (social overload); therefore, social media's influences on those users become diminished [59]. Similarly, based on the message dynamics of online interaction spaces, users are more likely to respond to simpler messages in overloaded mass interactions [52]. Individuals with independent self-construal make own choices based on careful internal evaluation after processing information extensively by them selves. By contrast, individuals with interdependent self-construal rely on social interactions and relationships with others to guide their thoughts and actions. Consequently, individuals with interdependent self-construal have even lower tolerance towards information overload than individuals with independent self-construal. Socially interactive games provide a social networking environment for their players, who prefer seeking information from social relationships rather than reading online reviews by themselves. They may react to review volume as well as review length more strongly than single game players. Players of socially interactive games are less likely to have capacities or interests to deal with large number of reviews, or very detailed descriptions provided by unknown reviewers, who have weak social ties with them [16]. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H1c. The positive efect of review volume on price decline of used video games is stronger for socially interactive games than for single player games.

H2c. The positive efect of review length on price decline of used video games is stronger for socially interactive games than for single player games.

As we have discussed before, negative online reviews negatively afect consumers' attitude towards a product due to undesired experiences shared by others. As experience goods, the characteristics of video games can only be fully assessed after consumption [24,68]. If players play the game only by themselves, they may still be curious about the game after reading others' negative reviews. Hence, they can truly assess the game's quality after their own usage. However, if games are socially interactive, players' experience will also dependent on other players' existence and behavior on the same game platform. The inter player dependency motivates these players to conform to social norms in their player communities, and they are more sensitive to negatively valenced emotions shown in reviews than single game players. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H3c. The positive efect of review negativity on price decline of used video games is stronger for socially interactive games than for single player games.

Self-construal studies suggest that the receivers with independent self-construal incline to make their final judgments through extensive cognitive processing on the content of eWOM information [91]. As previously discussed, subjective comments likely provide less comprehensive and factual knowledge. Increased subjectivity imposes more burden on individuals' information processing. Single game players, who play games by themselves and do not interact with other players, are more likely to embrace and exhibit independent self-construal. Accordingly, they are more likely to view subjective messages as unhelpful and the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4c. The positive efect of subjectivity on price decline of used video games is stronger for single player games than for socially interactive games.

Socially interactive game players connect in a strong social network with other players who share similar interests or hobbies. Sun et al. [85] suggest that those consumers tend to more actively seek opinions from others due to their interdependent self-construal. They are also more likely to believe messages from an authoritative opinion leader to be useful and resend the messages with perceived high authority [47]. As discussed earlier, messages with higher authority are potentially less readable. Accordingly, we contend that messages with higher readability (lower authority) are less acceptable for socially interactive game players than single game players. Therefore, the following hy pothesis is proposed:

H5c. The positive efect of readability on price decline is stronger for socially interactive games than for single player games.

## 4. Empirical analyses

## 4.1. Variable description

This study used a panel dataset where prices and customer reviews of each used video game were followed monthly from their release dates to April 2017. Therefore, the unit of analysis is a game and the temporal unit is a month. Table 1 provides detailed descriptions of the variables included in this study.

## 4.2. The data collection process

We obtained the data on used video game prices from a video game trading marketplace (www.pricecharting.com) that tracks the market prices of console video games over time. The marketplace collects used game prices from completed sales from eBay, listing data from Amazon and Half, and sales data from other online retailers. Based on these data, it formulates estimates of used game prices monthly for the entire U.S. market. Various tools, such as outlier detection, are used to identify the sales listed/classified incorrectly and to exclude them from price calculation; therefore, the monthly reports provide accurate and current market prices.

We restricted our data collection to only games released in 2015 to ensure that the games are still relatively recent and there is at least one year of price history for each game. The games tracked by the marketplace include those of PlayStation 4, Xbox One, and Wii U, which are the three largest brands in the market of the most up-to-date (i.e., the 8th generation) game consoles [54]. Up to February 2017, the PlayStation 4 had 57% market share (55,910,684 units), the Xbox One had 29% market share (28,717,251 units), and the Wii U had 14% market share (13,869,740 units). These are global figures for the 8th generation of game consoles.<sup>2</sup> We obtained monthly prices of the consoles' used games from January 2015 to April 2017 in the U.S. market. Moreover, we obtained each game's release dates and release prices from www. pricecharting.com.

Through the Amazon product identifier, the data obtained from www.pricecharting.com provide links between each used video game's price and customer reviews of verified purchases on Amazon. We collected the game reviews from the Amazon website using the crawler software developed by the authors. For each review, the software retrieved its textual content and counted the text length. Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoning (VADER) is one of the best performing tools for text sentiment analysis [83,84]. We used VADER to compute negativity scores for each review. VADER uses a rule-based approach to measure the intensity of expressed sentiment (positivity or negativity) in texts, where the sentiment intensity is scored based on lexicons of sentiment-related words as well as grammatical and syntactical conventions for expressing and emphasizing sentiment intensity [37]. Negativity scores vary between 0.0 and 1.0, where a higher score indicates stronger negativity. NLP is able to distinguish between factual and subjective information using subjectivity classifiers, which rely on existing resources of subjective words, phrases, and word sequences as well as patterns representing subjective expressions [75]. We used Textblob for NLP to perform subjectivity analysis on review texts. Subjectivity scores of a text vary between 0.0 and 1.0, where 0.0 is very objective and 1.0 is very subjective. We used a Python package named TextStat to compute the Flesch-Kincaid grade level score for each review's text [9]. The score, a widely-used measure of text complexity that indicates a text's level of difficulty [66]. gives the U.S. grade level required to understand a given text. The Flesch-Kincaid grade level is commonly available (a readability statistic within Microsoft Word), historically reliable, and popularly used in research [60]. To ensure that the higher score is associated with higher readability, we reverse-coded the score by multiplying the grade level score by a negative one.

To build the panel dataset of this study, we computed review volume, length, negativity, subjectivity, and readability monthly after the release date of each game. We calculated the game's review volume of each month by counting all of the reviews captured since the game's release date. We calculated the game's negativity of each month by averaging all the reviews' negativity scores of the game since its release date. In a similar fashion, we calculated the game's review subjectivity, text length, and readability for each month.

We obtained game information from online game databases and online game stores, such as gamepressure.com and Amazon.com. Games rated as E (Everyone) or E10+ (Everyone 10+, suitable for those aged 10 years and older) are considered low-violence games, containing possibly mild violence and mild language according to the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB). Games rated as M (Mature) and T (Teen) are considered high-violence games. In T-rated games,<sup>3</sup> content may “contain violence, suggestive themes, crude humor, minimal blood, simulated gambling and/or infrequent use of strong language”. In M-rated games, content may “contain intense violence, blood and gore, sexual content and/or strong language”. The T-rated and M-rated games include various contents not appropriate for children, such as violence, sexual themes, and substances. Some research has particularly examined the violent content of these two types of games due to the concern about their potential health impacts. The findings indicate that almost 100% of the T-rated and M-rated games contain significant amounts of violence, involving prolonged and intentional acts that cause physical injury or death to another character [43,87].

The games providing only single-player game mode are considered single player games. Games containing the multiplayer mode are considered socially interactive games, which allow players to interact with others on the same console or with others online [88].<sup>4</sup>

Overall, our dataset includes 297 video games for PlayStation 4 (147 games), Xbox One (115 games), and Wii U (35 games). Regarding the games' violence level, there are 102 low-violence games and 195 high-violence games. Regarding the games' social orientation, there are 103 single player games and 194 socially interactive games. Among 194 socially interactive games, 187 games are with both single-player and multiplayer modes; and 7 games are with multiplayer only mode. Our dataset consists of cross-sectional observations over time. To capture both cross-sectional and longitudinal properties of our data, we used a two-step dynamic generalized method of moments (GMM) to run the econometric panel data estimation developed by Arellano/Blundell Bond [3–5,14]. The panel data analysis could allow us to control for unobserved variables that difer across diferent games. This method could also increase eficiency relative to OLS.

Prior studies have suggested that consumers' preferences could af fect other consumers' purchase behavior and review rating could have a self-selection bias [3,57]. There are some omitted variables (error terms), for instance, games' popularity, consumers' preferences for dif ferent game attributes, and other individual game level characteristics (e.g. piracy of games), that may be correlated with review rating, and might also cause games' price decline. Therefore, review rating may be an endogenous explanatory variable, which depends on some omitted variables. If omitted variables not only afect price decline but also afect review rating, we would face endogeneity issues. To address the endogeneity concern, we used the well-established instrumental variables (IV) approach [90]. This approach requires that instrumental variables afect review rating but do not directly afect price decline of used video games [1,44]. The two instruments that we find suitable are the average rating of all other games belonging to the same type, and lagged review rating. They are denoted as ratingIV1 and ratingIV2 respectively. The average rating of all other games in the same game category is correlated with review rating because they both are driven by the underlying consumers' specific unobserved variables, such as taste. However, the price decline of a game is not directly afected by the average rating of other games, after controlling for a number of exogenous variables. In addition, the review rating in the prior month is likely to be correlated with the review rating in the current month but unlikely to be directly correlated with the error term that determines the price decline for the current month [36]. Indeed, our results indicate that ratingIV1 and ratingIV2 are correlated with review rating (βs = 0.019 and 0.690 respectively, ps = 0.000).

To ensure that our estimation and instruments are valid, we checked the Arellano-Bond test for AR (2). The results do not reject the null hypothesis that there is no second-order serial correlation in errors (p = 0.209). Therefore, there is no serial correlation concern in the GMM estimation. We also conducted the Hansen J test to check the validity of the instruments. The Hansen J statistics (p = 0.07) cannot reject the null hypothesis that the instruments are uncorrelated with the error terms, which indicates that the instruments used in the GMM estimation are valid. In addition, to further ensure the potential weak identification issue of our instruments, we checked the F-statistics value. The value (F = 326.126) is much larger than the critical value of 10 [1], indicating that our instruments are not weakly identified.

In order to examine the moderating efects of diferent game features,<sup>5</sup> we conducted sub-sample analyses and comparison. We ran a ztest as well to check the significance of diferences between groups. Table 3 reports the empirical results. Our results reveal that the estimated coeficient of review volume is positive and significant (β = 0.031, p = 0.000). This indicates that an increase in the review volume leads to a steeper decline of a used video game's price, supporting our H1a. The estimate is statistically significant for games of diferent violence levels (βs = −0.047 and 0.044 respectively, ps = 0.001 and 0.000 respectively). Moreover, this impact is stronger for players of high-violence games (p = 0.000 for the z-test), and our H1b is supported. However, z-test indicates that there is no diference between single player games and socially interactive games (p = 0.339). Thus, our H1c is not supported.

Our findings suggest that there is no association between review length and used games' price decline, which in fact does not support our H2a. The estimate is also statistically insignificant for games of diferent violence levels, which suggests that there is no relationship between comment length and used games' price decline regardless of violence level. Thus, H2b is not supported. The coeficients of comment length are also statistically insignificant for both single player and socially interactive games, indicating that there is no association between comment length and used games' price decline regardless of social or ientation. H2c is therefore not supported.

## 5. Discussion

## 5.1. Conclusion

The impact of eWOM on consumer decision-making has attracted significant interests from scholars, resulting in a well-established body of literature. With the prevalence of Internet and social networking sites, it is critical for business to gain better understanding of eWOM. In this paper, we apply the psychological theories of consumer choice to theoretically explain why consumers may react to eWOM diferently [40,94]. We propose to infer consumers' choice-specific preferences based on the types of products they are interested in, and empirically test our conceptual model using data collected from the used video games market.

Our results show that an increase in the review volume is associated with a steeper used video game price decline. The impacts are diferent between games of diferent violence levels, as players of high-violence games react more negatively towards review volume than players of low-violence games. However, our results do not find such impact varies between single player games and socially interactive games. We find that review length is not related to used-games' price decline overall. Furthermore, we find that review comments with higher textural sentimental negativity, subjectivity, as well as readability are more likely to cause steeper price decline. Both the negativity and readability impacts are stronger for socially interactive games. However, there is no diference between players of single player games and socially interactive games in terms of handling subjective comments. Interestingly, the impacts of negativity, subjectivity, as well as readability do not vary based on games' violence levels.

Based on our findings, we conclude that eWOM has an impact on price decline of used video games. More importantly, such impact is moderated by game features which signal their associated players varying predispositions.

## 5.2. Contributions

This paper makes several contributions to eWOM literature. First, we use Hansen [40]’s psychological choice model to theoretically justify why consumer heterogeneities matter in their responses to eWOM messages. We propose to use product features to diferentiate customers, and empirically validate such moderating efects. Two new moderators (i.e., games' violence levels and social orientation) that are relevant and critical in the video game research context are identified and tested. These two product features signal variation of players predisposition in terms of their emotional arousal and self-construal. The results suggest the expansion of the integrative eWOM model [20] by including the moderating roles of consumer heterogeneities inferred from product features to better understand diferential impact of eWOM communication even within the same product market. Second. this is one of the first studies to explore eWOM in an under-researched context - the used video game market. Prior literature of Internet-based used goods market has investigated various product categories, such as laptops, digital cameras, audio players, PDAs, and cars [28,34]. However, there is limited eWOM research in the used video game market, which represents an important product category in digital experience goods [24]. While used video games tend to become cheaper over time, the extent of their price decline is subject to influence from online re view comments generated and shared by players. Third, we use text mining techniques to comprehensively quantify eWOM characteristics in a multifaceted way, including volume, length, negativity, sub jectivity, and readability. Our results indicate that these characteristics have diferential associations with the price decline of used video games. The findings provide strong empirical support for propositions in eWOM research to demonstrate how content of online reviews in fluences consumer decision-making [e.g., 20, 21]. Overall, our research demonstrates that the efect of eWOM depends on various dimensions of textural features, and these efects also vary diferently across dif ferent product categories.

Practically, our findings provide interesting implications for merchants who are active in used game trading markets as well as for ecommerce platforms hosting these markets. Since product uncertainty afects the trading value of used video games, merchants need to carefully communicate product information to mitigate transaction risks faced by consumers. Overall, we find that review volume, negativity, subjectivity, and readability are associated with steeper price decline in the used video game market. Merchants, retailers, as well as review platforms can collaborate to improve presentation of online reviews to mitigate such negative influences. For instance, they can limit the number of reviews presented to customers on the initial landing page to reduce potential information overload perceived by consumers. To alleviate the negative impact of review readability, they may highlight and promote reviews from expert reviewers, whose authoritative comments are associated with lower readability scores. They may also provide online management responses for reviews with high levels of negativity or subjectivity. Intervening negative eWOM dissemination has been proven efective in hospitality industries [92].

Our results suggest that the review systems can be tailored and customized based on more fine-grained customer segmentation, due to customers' varying reliance on eWOM. We further propose an easy-toimplement strategy to customize online reviews. In cases where product features can signal diferent psychological preferences of their associated consumers, businesses can tailor online reviews based on product features. However, in order to provide more efective reviews for the targeted customer segment, they should first carefully examine how customers respond to eWOM in each product sub-categories. For instance, review ranking mechanisms have been utilized to sort reviews according to various matrixes and provide consumers with the most helpful reviews within a size limit [89]. Since high-violence game players react very negatively to review volume, the ranking algorithm may further reduce the number of reviews available on the initial landing page. Tailoring online reviews based on the content violence levels can be used in other media markets, such as TV, movies, and books. Similarly, the ranking algorithm may place lower weights on negative and easy-to-read reviews for socially interactive game players, due to their stronger negative responses to review negativity and readability. Our research shows the important role of consumers' selfconstrual, and it can be extended to other context, such as social networking products as well as social commerce [19].

## 5.3. Limitations and future research

We need to recognize several limitations of this study and ofer suggestions for future research. First, used games' price decline can be a result of other unforeseen factors in addition to eWOM messages. Future studies could consider to enrich our research by incorporating those additional factors, such as peer influence received by game players [29]. Second, our analysis focuses on the game reviews posted via the largest ecommerce website. However, merchant-generated ad vertisements and other types of activities on social media could also be relevant [53]. Additional work in other online contexts, such as You-Tube, will be needed to evaluate eWOM's impact on consumers' decision making. Third, our study is restricted to the efect of eWOM on measurable economic consequences of paid video games. The result about eWOM's impact may not be applicable to the growing free-to-play (F2P) gaming market, where publishers give video games away for free and monetize the games through in-game items of purchase or gamerelated services [41]. It would be interesting to build a similar model to examine eWOM's economic impact on F2P games, which may help to understand game players' lifetime value [42]. Fourth, we are unable to collect data of many interesting game-level variables, such as individual games' piracy threat and compatibility levels. and future research could consider to do further analysis when data become available. Last, our study focuses on consumers' varying responses to eWOM in the used video game market. Future research may investigate how individual traits inferred through video games' sub-categorizations afect con sumers' motivation to contribute eWOM to digital platforms.
