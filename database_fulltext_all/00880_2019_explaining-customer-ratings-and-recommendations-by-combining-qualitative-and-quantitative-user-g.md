# Explaining customer ratings and recommendations by combining qualitative and quantitative user generated contents

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Text mining Sentiment analysis Emotions Service evaluation Recommendation

## A B S T R A C T

Customer ratings and recommendations are not only important performance indicators for businesses alone, but they act as an important source of information for potential ‘uninformed’ customers. In this study, we explore the qualitative and quantitative used generated contents to find what explains customer ratings and recommendations disentangling them based on user-generated content collected from an online airlines review website. We performed text mining to find the overall sentiment and emotions expressed by customers in general, and focused on sentiments expressed over various service aspects. We found the relationships of such sentiments and emotions expressed in textual reviews and the relationships of quantitative ratings given to various core and augmented service aspects with the customer ratings and recommendation decisions. Then, we explored how such relationship strengths change over various business contexts. The results yielded important theoretical contributions to extant literature on service evaluations, construal levels, online reviews and recommendations etc. Moreover, we believe that our results would be useful for practitioners who may want to use the methodology in order to create quick and meaningful insights combining both qualitative and quantitative user generated contents, thereby helping them in service design, communication design and post-purchase strategies. As a matter of fact, such insights could also help potential travelers with an aggregated view of service quality, which in turn could help them in purchase decision making.

## 1. Introduction

Understanding consumer perceptions about a product or a service is one of the most important aspects, which an organization should focus on for dealing with both positive and negative feedbacks along with its repercussions. Extant literature has documented the need for measuring consumer promoter score (NPS), and consumer loyalty, both of which have a long-term impact on the profitability and success of an organi zation [49]. Consumer reviews and recommendations are one of the major sources of information, which can help organizations measure metrics like customer satisfaction (CSAT) and NPS [25].

With the advancement of technology and internet penetration, more and more consumers are engaging in information sharing in online platforms [45], whereby they're expressing their views and opinions, giving ratings on various parameters to diferent services, and openly recommending or discouraging purchase of products and services. While some websites have standardized fields where the consumers can share their opinion based on which ratings have to be given, others have textual reviews or both [55]. Such mixed reviews make the operationalization of NPS dificult for organizations.

Ratings in various pre-defined aspects gives an idea how an organization is performing in some of the areas; delving deeper into textual reviews is certainly much more informative, whereby an organization can not only get an idea about the overall sentiments and emotions of its consumers, but also understand how various aspects are evaluated at the first place [65]. One could in fact also try and find out how such evaluations, sentiments and emotions lead to customer ratings (CR) and recommendation behavior (RBV).

Online reviews provide an important data source even in the case of tourism. Extant literature has discussed how consumer reviews impact consumer decision making and consumer perceptions [57]; there have also been studies, which have documented the rationales of making an online review helpful, making it evident that online reviews are certainly invaluable for travel and tourism organizations [30]. However, not many studies have dealt with RBV of consumers, specifically in the tourism sector [55].

Our study analyzes consumer reviews and ratings in a travel and tourism context, particularly in the context of airline travel. First, we try to do a text analysis of the review data to find out what are the various core and augmented service aspects based on which people provide textual reviews. We then try to find how the expressed emotions and sentiments in an overall sense and towards such service attributes, along with the ratings given on certain pre-defined aspects leads to CR and RBV. Third, we also try to show how the relative importance of the above-mentioned service attributes and emotions vary for diferent types of consumers based on the service context. For instance, diferent airlines have diferent business models, such as fullservice, low cost, leisure etc.; important attributes in each of these business models are expected to be diferent too; our analysis sheds light on this diference.

In the next part of the paper, we discuss our theoretical model, followed by the methodology and results of our data analysis; followed by discussion and understanding of the theoretical and managerial implications of the results, and how they could help in decision-making. We conclude the paper highlighting the limitations of the current study, and mention the future scopes.

## 2. Background and research questions

## 2.1. Customer ratings and recommendations

Customer ratings (CR) are one of the best possible quantitative measures of customer satisfaction. Customer ratings have remained one of the major variables that marketers focus on while evaluating the success of their initiatives [2]. The monetary implications of CR has been well documented; it's known to lead increased purchases from consumers, repeat purchase, and RBV, all of which leads to the profitability of an organization ([2,56]). Drivers of customer ratings under various contexts have remained a primary research area for decades [3,34,37]. In fact, studying such drivers gain further relevance in the context of services where consumers' needs and wants are heterogeneous; so are the business models [22]. Such heterogeneity will lead to diferential importance given by the consumers to diferent service attributes. For decades, extant literature has used latent constructs such as service quality, value, and trust to explain customer satisfaction [43,60]. In most of the cases, these studies have used survey-based methods for data collection using the Likert scale of the latent variables. Thereby, it is imperative to explain consumer generated ratings using consumer generated information, which are free of self-selection bias and other biases related to data collected from sample surveys; moreover, combining qualitative and quantitative data to find the drivers of CR gains more importance. This study is crucial as it bridges the gap in extant literature and practice by proposing a methodology of creating insights from customer generated ratings and review by using text mining and statistical analysis technique, contributing thereby to ex isting literature on customer satisfaction.

One of the major outcomes of consumer satisfaction is consumer loyalty [11,23]; the behavioral outcome of loyalty includes repeat purchase, re-patronage and positive word-of-mouth or RBV [6]. While re-patronage and repeat purchase increases the revenue generated from the consumer, RBV improves the net promoter score, as new consumer acquisition becomes easier and profitable [55]. Many researchers have documented the importance of net promoter score or consumer RBV as one of the major indicators of success of promotional initiatives [28,55]; it becomes further important to marketers thereby to identify what exactly are the drivers of RBV. In the era of online reviews, one should further focus how opinions given in textual reviews or online ratings are related with RBV of the consumers [55]. It should be noted that not all online review platforms explicitly ask for RBV; this, leads to a strategy gap. Moreover, the drivers of customer ratings and those of recommendation could be diferent due to the fact that the former is an attitudinal variable and the latter is a behavioral outcome [12]. Ad ditionally, even in the case of RBV, the drivers may vary based on business models of the services, the heterogeneity of the consumers etc. [59]. The current study tries to bridge these gaps by focusing not only on customer satisfaction, but also delving into the customers' recommendation behavior, whereby we checked for diferential impacts of explanatory variables of these two constructs. We have also tested how the two constructs are diferentially related with explanatory variables under diferent service contexts related to diferent psychological states of the consumers. Thus the study is expected to contribute in the customer loyalty literature too.

## 2.2. Online reviews

Online reviews act as an important source of information for consumers, especially the ones who're uninformed; they impact the purchase decision-making process, the amount, frequency and propensity of purchasing a product or a service depending on the valence, volume and nature of online reviews. Due to this, online reviews are so im portant, whereby companies often tend to go out of their way in taking initiatives to get favorable ratings and reviews so that they can leverage the benefits of positive reviews from those customers whose decision relied on past online reviews.

There are a number of literature streams that focus on online reviews and its importance in business in terms of impacting consumer decision-making and overall economic performance of the business, including revenue, profitability etc. [14,18]. Another stream of literature focuses on pricing and promotion strategies that companies can take in a multi –period game while they generate positive online reviews in the first period and leverage the positive impact of such reviews on their price, sales and profits in the second time period [1]. Another stream of literature focuses on what are the underlying psychological mechanisms that leads to posting of reviews, and how companies can motivate their customers to put positive reviews [24,38]. Motivations for herd behavior in online and incongruous reviews are also studied [13]; in fact, extant literature also explores characteristics of a review, which makes it more useful to future customers [26,39,40].

Our focus through this study has been on both the qualitative and quantitative information generated from online reviews, consisting of both textual reviews and numeric ratings; extant literature has rarely considered both these aspects while explaining customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior; in that sense, our study extends upon extant literature. Additionally, while we explore the impact of various sentiments and emotions expressed in textual reviews on the overall satisfaction, extant literature has solely focused on an attribute-wise sentiment mapping of customer reviews and its impact thereof [55]. However, one must also consider consumer ‘emotions'. which have a vital role to play in customer satisfaction; mere positive and negative classification has minimal information content. It must be understood that emotions expressed in consumer reviews can impact the degree of customer satisfaction significantly. Again, while extant literature has tended to overlook the ‘emotional’ aspect [55], the current study has looked to fill in that gap too.

## 2.3. Text analytics of online reviews

Unstructured text documents such as online reviews have huge in formation content, which needs to be analyzed quantitatively to generate insights, for which both text analytics and text mining techniques are extensively used; furthermore, these techniques help in extracting meaningful information from unstructured text data, and then analyzing such information. Some of the major areas where text mining is used include topic modelling, sentiment analysis, natural language processing, document classification etc. In this study, we have followed the text mining approach suggested by Hotho et al. [27] where information extraction and text pre-processing are done to extract data from text; such data is further used in creating actionable insights.

Text pre-processing is an important part of data mining, where data is processed to make it usable for text mining methods. The three major parts of text pre-processing include stop wards removal, stemming and term frequency – inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) algorithm [62]. Stop words removal are done to remove unimportant words, prepositions, pro-nouns etc., to make the text data leaner and more meaningful. Such stop-words are removed so that they do not get included as important keywords while analyzing data [20]. Methods such as classic method, mutual information method, Zipf's law method and term-based random sampling are used for such activity. Stemming is done to create connections within sentences to reduce the baggage of words [62] for which truncating, statistical and mixed methods are some of the commonly used systems. TF-IDF method is used to find the importance of a word within a text; in here, when a word appears repeatedly within a document, the score increases; it decreases as a word is used more in the corpus. This score is then used as a weight to find important terms in the data [20,62].

Bag-of-words technique is a widely used method in text mining where frequency of occurrence of words is used as an important measure of the importance of a word. The importance of the weight of a word is found by using the TF-IDF matrix [54], which is then used for various other data mining algorithms such as classification, regression etc. In our study, we have used bag-of-words method along with partsof-speech (POS) tagging method for creating insights from the data [8]. POS tagging as feature selection method is also well established in text mining literature, used in analyzing sentiment and emotions [4].

Sentiment analysis can be of diferent levels- document, sentence or aspect. While document level sentiment analysis gives the overall sentiment expressed in a whole document, the aspect level, focuses on that subpart of the document, which talks about a particular aspect. Lexicon based sentiment analysis is one of the most common methods used, where a crucial set of wordlist are created, having sentiment and emotion scores attached to them. Such a list is created based on su pervised learning using manual approach, corpus based approach or double propagation approach [21]. Another method that's widely used is supervised learning-based sentiment prediction, where support vector machine based technique is used in tagged text data to train a model, which is used later for sentiment predictions [21].

With an increase of data availability and data extraction from online reviews and user generated contents, adoption of sentiment analysis and opinion mining has become very common. Such sentiment and emotion analysis can be done at the overall document level or at the sentence level [21]. Moreover, topic-based or aspect-based sentiment analysis has also become useful in text mining research [55]; however, the application of such techniques in various contexts such as e-commerce, tourism etc. are few [47,55]. Interestingly, this is probably the first study to include emotions expressed in a text during the analysis, thereby making this study unique.

## 2.4. Research questions

Both antecedents of customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior have been studied extensively in extant literature. Latent variables such as service quality, trust, perceived value etc. have been found to be the primary drivers of customer satisfaction, leading to loyalty intention and loyalty behavior, including recommendations. Another stream of literature has focused on how various service attri butes impact customer satisfaction; according to accessibility-diag nosticity (AD) model, human behavior is driven by accessibility and diagnosticity of input variables. In the context of customer satisfaction rating and recommendation behavior, such input variables can include diferent service aspects, which may difer in terms of accessibility and diagnosticity. For instance, for a public transit service, punctuality, safety, cleanliness, reliability etc. can be the major input variables that drive customer satisfaction. However, such variables do have a possibility of having diferent levels of impact on consumer behavior due to their diference in the accessibility and diagnosticity. Therefore, ratings given on such service attributes would tend to have diferential impacts on customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior.

Following the multiple pathway anchoring and adjustment (MPAA) model, we can suggest that customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior is a result of consumer attitudes towards various service aspects (outside-in), and the characteristics along with personal factors of the consumers (inside-out); going by this model, it is the combined force of both the internal and external variables that lead to consumer behavior.

It is clear by now that consumer ratings given for various service aspects are accessible and diagnosable; as per the AD model, we can use such ratings as being the primary drivers of consumer satisfaction and recommendation behavior. On the other hand, per the MPAA model, such ratings serve as pathways in which consumers can express their attitude towards a service they've taken, influencing thereby the overall rating, which could be diferent from an individual's rating on the service undertaken. Importantly, the overall rating is thereby more holistic as regards a service, and is a logical resultant of multiple attitudes formed on various service aspects taken in amalgamation.

Extant literature has also focused on types of service aspects that impact customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior; the service aspects can be broadly divided into two, i.e. core and augmented. The core service aspects include the basic benefits that a service provides; for instance, the food in a restaurant vis a vis the value for money it provides. Augmented service aspects include aspects that ‘diferentiate a service provider from its competitors; for instance, the ambience, the music, the parking facility etc. within a restaurant. In our context, we try to analyze how the core and augmented service aspects impact customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior, and then try to explain the results based on the AD and MPAA model.

RQ1. Do the ratings given about core and augmented service aspect explain customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

Textual reviews are meant to have a vivid description of a customer's experience about a service. The overall sentiment expressed in textual reviews provides information about whether the experience was positive or negative, which in turn would allude to whether a customer has been satisfied or not, reflecting thereby his/her recommendation behavior [55]. Not only sentiments, textual reviews in fact also provide information about a customer's emotions about his/her service experience. However, these emotions may not necessarily be one-dimensional like sentiments [63]; for instance, emotions such as fear, disgust, anger, joy, trust etc. are not ‘necessarily’ opposite to each other; they, either in amalgamation and/or singularly express distinct psychological processes in the consumer's mind. As a matter of fact. such emotions explain the customer's satisfaction level and recommendation behavior [29]. Having said this, one needs to keep in mind that powerful emotions like anger or fear, should have much higher explanatory power than subtler emotions, like trust, anticipation etc. Our research question thereby is:

RQ2. Do the overall sentiment and emotions expressed in textual reviews explicitly explain a customer's satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

While ratings provide a quantitative measure of consumers' satisfaction with both the core and augmented service aspects, consumers also tend to express their feelings on the service aspects through textual reviews. Sentiments expressed on service aspects in the form of textual reviews, do explain customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior; however, similar to ratings, the customer's ‘sentiments’ about the core and augmented service aspects would have diferent impacts. Core service aspects such as punctuality, check-in service and staf behavior have diferent explanatory power than augmented service aspects such as food and beverages quality, in-flight entertainment, comfort such as bigger seat, more space, extra leg space etc. Our next research question thereby:

RQ3. Does a sentiment related to the core and augmented service aspects efectively explain customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

The explanatory models above are expected to vary based on service models. For instance, in case of airlines, the explanatory powers of various attributes vary depending on whether the business model is full service or low cost [31]. Low cost airlines have ‘no frills’ and provides just the core benefits at a lower cost; for every extra service, there is an additional cost. On the other hand, full service airlines include all the associated services of airlines such as club, lounge, restaurant, priority check in, premium bag handling etc., thereby inflating their costs, which in turn also afects the expectations of the customers travelling in these two diferent types of airlines (Saha & Theingi) [52]. Thus quite naturally, the core and the augmented service aspects vis a vis the emotions and sentiments of customers would also difer, justifying thereby his/her satisfaction level and recommendation behavior thereof [42]. Based on the same, we ask the following research question:

RQ4. How does the above-mentioned explaining power change based on the model of service?

## 3. Methodology

## 3.1. Data and data processing

Online data source is one of the major information sources for customer reviews. To facilitate our research and understanding thereof, we have restricted ourselves to view customer reviews given only for airlines. Data has been collected from airlinequality.com, a website, which collects customer reviews on various airlines all over the world. The data contained 41,397 customer reviews on 362 airlines by cus tomers of 158 countries. These customer reviews were posted between 2005 and 2015. The dataset contains the overall CSAT rating given by the consumers in a 1 to 10-point rating scale, where 1 means highly dissatisfied and 10 means high satisfied. The dataset also contains whether the customer has recommended the airline or not to other customers. Along with the above, the dataset contains textual reviews, attribute-wise ratings for some of the following attributes: seat comfort, cabin staf behavior, food and beverages, inflight entertainment, ground service, wifi connectivity and value for money. We first looked for missing data, and found the same in the attribute-wise ratings. Ratings on wifi connectivity and ground service had 83% and 88% missing data; we dropped these two variables from our analysis. The attribute-wise ratings on seat comfort, cabin staf behavior, food and beverages, inflight entertainment, value for money had < 8% missing data; we imputed the data using median imputation.

For insight extraction for the text data, we have followed pre-pro cessing techniques. We have first cleaned the data by removing numbers, stop-words using classic method, blank spaces, punctuations etc. so that the corpus becomes suitable for data analysis. Extant literature in marketing, information systems and data science have used lexicons developed by researchers in the domain of computational linguistics for finding out the sentiment and emotion scores of the set of texts [15,36,58]. While often the statistical learning-based method of sentiment prediction works better than lexicon based method within the training data, the generalizability or out-sample performance of such methods are lower [58]. Therefore lexicon based methods are preferable when we try to find the linguistic sentiment expressed. Such lexicons give scores related to positive/negative polarity, and various emotions to the words and set of words available in a text. While positive/negative polarity gives an overall idea about what is the valence of the text, emotion scores gives an afective-cognitive level under standing about the content of the text. In our study we have used NRC Word-Emotion Association Lexicon (also called EmoLex) created by Mohammad and Turney [35], which provides scores about the following sentiments and emotions (sentiments: negative, positive; emotions: anger, anticipation, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, surprise, trust). We checked the frequency in which the words related to the above sentiments and emotions occurring in the text; the overall sentiment was measured as (pos − neg) / (pos + neg) where pos = frequency of positive words and neg = frequency of negative words.

We then tested the accuracy of the sentiment and emotion scores through a pre-test on 100 randomly selected reviews from the dataset. We asked 3 independent experts to read the reviews and mention the same in a 5-point Likert scale (1 meaning strongly disagree and 5 meaning strongly agree) to assess if positive sentiment and the eight emotions are being expressed by the reviews. The average responses of the experts are found to be correlated with the overall sentiment (0.67), whereby the eight emotions count (anger = 0.69, anticipation = 0.75, disgust = 0.88, fear = 0.62, joy = 0.65, sadness = 0.77, surprise = 0.59, trust = 0.61). This suggests that the sentiment and emotion scores received, have enough validity to go ahead for further analysis.

We also found the attribute-specific sentiments expressed in the text. Often the ratings given in a survey are not similar to the sentiments expressed in the text; therefore, it is important to understand what consumers are expressing about various service attributes, which have efectively gone on to afect their satisfaction levels and behavior thereof. We followed the two-step approach suggested by Siering et al. [55] to find the attribute-wise sentiments, whereby we first created bag of words, which best explain the aspects; however, the method applied for creating the bag of words was diferent for our case. First, we identified the words, which were occurring at least in 5% of the whole dataset. We wanted to exclusively focus on nouns, as aspects are primarily defined by them; herein, we used the POS tagging based aspect selection method. Once that was collected, we tagged parts of speech of the words using R package for Ripple Down Rules-based Part-Of-Speech Tagging (RDRPOS), which is known to have pre-trained parts of speech tagging facility of 45 languages including annotation-based parts of speech tagging for English language. The general architecture of the package is developed by Nguyen, Nguyen, Pham and Pham [41]. We then created a subset of words using only the nouns, resulting in 116 nouns. Further, we used opinions of 3 experts and 10 users of online reviews and airlines to identify how these nouns were actually explaining some of the service attributes related to airlines. In doing this, we did have to drop a few nouns, which were not explaining any specific service attribute. We also dropped a few service attributes, which did not have > 3 unique words to explain it. The final list can be found in Table 1.

Once such bag of words was created, we got the sentiment expressed for each service aspect. Then, we divided the reviews in sentence level units and analyzed if at least one term related to a service aspect was present. We found the sentiment of only such sentences, which contained at least one word of the service aspect. The overall frequency of positive and negative words from the sentences containing at least one word related to one service aspect, efectively gave out the total sentiment of the service aspect expressed in a particular review. ## 4. Discussion

## 4.1. Discussion of the results

Customer satisfaction, the attitudinal measure of consumer outcome, recommendation behavior, along with the behavioral measure of consumer outcome, are very important metrics, which businesses use to calibrate short-term and long-term outcomes [10,56]. In this study, based on user-generated real data, we have tried to explain the drivers of customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior. We have used the quantitative responses given by users towards various service attributes related to the core and augmented benefits of the services. We have also used the textual qualitative review given by users. We have used text mining and natural language processing techniques to create insights from the textual qualitative reviews. Our first substantial finding includes the sentiments and emotions expressed in the text re garding the overall service; additionally, we have also found the sen timents expressed in specific service attributes based on keywords and bag of words as stated in the methods section. We have used such in sights generated from qualitative and quantitative reviews to explain the consumer outcomes. Further, we explored how the above ex planatory relationships vary over various business models.

RQ1. Do the ratings given about core and augmented service aspects explain the customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

The results show that core aspects of a service such as value fo money, seat comfort and cabin staf behavior have stronger positive relationships with CSAT and RBV than the ancillary aspects such as food and beverages and in-flight entertainment. This finding is in line with previous researchers who focus on the relative importance of core vs. augmented service aspects [9,48].

RQ2. Do the overall sentiment and emotions expressed in the textual review explain customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

RQ3. Does the sentiment expressed related to the core and augmented service aspects explain the customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior?

Overall sentiment is a major predictor for both types of consumer outcomes, and for both types of service contexts. We have also found the relative importance of positive and negative emotions. It has been observed that negative emotions such as disgust, fear, anger have negative relationship with consumer outcomes. On the other hand, positive emotions such as surprise, trust and joy have positive relationship with consumer outcomes. These results are coherent with the extant literature [16]. Sentiment of service aspects such as comfort, food and beverage, staf behavior, in-flight entertainment, check and punctuality are important predictors too.

RQ4. How does the above-mentioned explaining power changes based on the model of service?

We found that the relative importance of concrete service aspects such as value for money is higher in case of low-cost airlines in comparison to full service airlines. On the other hand, the relative importance of abstract service aspects such as seat comfort, cabin staf behavior food and beverages, in-flight entertainment etc. are higher for full service airlines. The reason of such diferential relationships can be explained based on the construal representation related to the service context [61]. Low cost airlines are providing ‘no frills’ basic services; thus, they are expected to have a low psychological distance with the customer. Low cost airlines therefore are related to low construal representation, which may lead to higher importance to concrete service aspects [46]. However, full service airlines are related to higher construal representation, which may lead to higher importance to abstract service aspects. Extant literature has shown evidence regarding the diferential importance of concrete and abstract service aspects based on construal representation [46,50]. Our findings strengthen such stream of literature.

We have also found that most of the aspect-wise sentiment scores have positive relationship with consumer outcomes related to full service airlines, and not related to low cost airlines. This can also be explained based on construal representation of the service contexts. Full service airlines are related to high construes where the information processing of abstract ideas is much easier than concrete ideas [46]. The sentiment scores are derived from qualitative data, which is an abstract representation of the feelings of consumers over various service aspects. As such, abstract representation is easier and more accurate in service contexts with higher construal representation; relationship with the information generated from such qualitative expressions should be more prominent in case such service contexts [61]. This explains why the relationships with the aspect-wise sentiments are more prominent in case of full-service airlines.

The relationship of emotions with consumer outcomes is stronger for full service airlines. The above can also be explained based on cognitive and afective information processing of consumers [32,44]. Low cost airlines being ‘no frills' airlines. both the consumer and the provider are resource constrained, leading thereby to an objective evaluation of the services, along with higher cognitive and lower af fective information processing. On the other hand, full service airlines being relatively higher in both costs and services, consumer expectations from such airlines are also abstract. Therefore, the cognitive information processing of the consumers during the evaluation of fullservice airlines are also lower, whereas the afective information processing is higher. This leads to the fact that the relationship strength of emotions with the overall consumer outcomes are much lesser in case of low-cost airlines than full service airlines [5].

## 4.2. Theoretical contribution

The theoretical contribution of the paper is manifold; first, this is one of the very few papers, which has explored the importance of quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the service aspects on customer outcomes, such as customer satisfaction and recommendation behavior [55]. Though the importance of textual reviews are well established [8,27], the method to combine qualitative and quantitative data is less explored [55].

Moreover, extant literature of textual reviews has predominantly focused on the overall or aspect-wise sentiments expressed by textual reviews [53,55,65]. However, the informational value of textual sentiment scores is limited. In this study, we have also extracted the emotions expressed in the reviews, and tried to find how such emotions are related to consumer outcomes, thus contributing to extant literature related to consumer insights from textual reviews [53,65].

In this study, we have found how the relative importance of various aspect-wise qualitative and quantitative evaluations are diferently related to consumer outcomes based on the service context i.e. the business model. We have also found that the importance of sentiment and emotions expressed in the textual reviews in explaining consumer outcomes also change based on such service context. Extant literature has focused on how service context can impact consumer evaluations [19,64]. Researchers have also found how the construal representation of the service context along with the information processing strategies of the consumers impacts such consumer evaluations in diferent service contexts [17,46]. The current study is an extension of such literature.

Moreover, the current study identifies diferential importance of the information contents of reviews and ratings while explaining customer satisfaction, an attitudinal construct and recommendation behavior, a behavioral construct. Extant literature has identified these two con structs to be separate and carrying unique importance [7]; the current study re-establishes the same. While many emotions impact consumer satisfaction, which lay deep, they may not necessarily impact re commendation behavior, which is a behavioral outcome of the attitude. This also strengthens the threshold theory of behavior [51].

## 4.3. Managerial implications

Our study gives a number of managerial implications, which will be useful for service managers for serving their customers better. The primary implication of this study is in service design, which is a tough decision for service managers due to the presence of numerous service aspects whose importance is not known properly. This study focuses on core and augmented service aspects, and suggests that core service aspects are generally more important than augmented service aspects. However, in case of service contexts, which are represented as high construal, such as full-service airlines, luxury hotels, spas and beauty salons etc., augmented and abstract service aspects have higher relative importance than for service contexts related to low construal. Therefore, managers should carefully prioritize and assign resources in service improvements of concrete and abstract service aspects when they are designing the services. When there's a resource allocation problem, consumers can use Model 1.3 or 2.3 as objective functions depending on their objective of maximizing satisfaction or loyalty, and decide how much to invest in each service aspect which will improve the sentiment and ratings of such aspects and hence, the output (satisfaction or loyalty).

Our study finds service aspect-wise sentiments have stronger relationships for full service airlines. This finding suggests that managers should use the quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the service aspects diferently. While quantitative evaluations are more concrete in nature, qualitative evaluations are more abstract and all round. Therefore, the explanation power of qualitative evaluations is higher in abstract contexts such as full-service airlines and not in concrete service contexts such low cost airlines. This has implication in the method of feedback collection and information generation for studying post purchase customer evaluations. Service contexts which are abstract, for instance experience and credence services like amusement park, hotels, restaurants, hospitals etc., should focus more on textual reviews than quantitative ratings for information generation. While concrete service contexts, such as search services like low cost airlines, fast food counters etc., should focus more on quantitative rating based feedback collection system.

Finally, we have also shown that, along with consumer sentiments, consumer emotions can also explain consumer outcomes, more so in case of full-service airlines. Service contexts that promote objective and cognitive evaluations, such as low-cost airlines, leads to lower importance of consumer emotions in explaining consumer outcomes than service contexts that promote abstract information processing, such as full-service airlines. This will have important implications for service managers in designing the service aspects and integrated communication strategies. Whether they will be able to use consumer emotions for driving consumer outcomes are also context dependent. While the former service context will require informative advertisements or tangible benefits more, the later will require more emotional advertisements and intangible benefits. Moreover, the emotion of joy works much stronger than surprise, anticipation or trust. Therefore, the advertisement content should also be designed accordingly.

## 4.4. Limitations and future scope

While the current study gave an idea on the explanatory power of quantitative and qualitative consumer reviews in explaining consumer attitude and behavior, the underlying psychological process that leads to such attitude formation and behavior outcome has still remained unexplored. Future studies could look to focus on such underlying mechanisms that result in consumer outcomes, and how such mechanisms are diferent in diferent service contexts resulting in diferential relative importance of various service aspects. Due to lack of data, we have also not considered multiple variables, which possibly could have explained consumer outcomes better; for instance, the origin of the airline or the country of the reviewer could influence consumer outcomes. Future research could look to include such vari ables too.

The current study has used data from the airline industry. The service aspects related to other industries could be diferent; however, we expect the basic findings to remain the same. Future studies could recheck the results in various other service contexts, which are characteristically diferent from the airlines industry [33]. The study has also focused on only two service contexts based on the airlines model of business. However, there are many other service contexts, which can be important; for instance, a travel in business purpose vs. a leisure travel is very diferent in terms of expectation; business class travel and economy class travels are also diferent. These diferent service contexts are also expected to impact consumer outcomes diferently. Future re search could thereby look to focus on the same.
