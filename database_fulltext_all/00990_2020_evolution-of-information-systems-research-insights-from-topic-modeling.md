# Evolution of Information Systems Research: Insights from Topic Modeling

## Abstract

Information systems (IS) research has continued to consistently evolve with the transitions in the IS discipline over time. There has been a general interest in the IS discipline over time as researchers have examined various aspects such as the intellectual core, diversity, and impact of IS research. On the basis of 2962 articles published in six leading IS journals between 2003 and 2017, topic modeling using latent semantic indexing was applied to author-supplied keywords. Topics such as IS development, IT adoption, and IS usage had endured over time; topics such as e-commerce and IT outsourcing had gained momentum and then waned over time; and topics such as social media, design science, and online communities have gained momentum in recent times. Results suggest that IS research has evolved considerably over time and generally included new frontiers while serving its core strength and identity.

Keywords: Information systems, Research, Keywords, Topic modeling, Latent semantic indexing

# Evolution of Information Systems Research: Insights from Topic Modeling

## 1. Introduction

There has been general interest in the information systems (IS) discipline over time. Research has examined the intellectual core of the IS discipline (Alavi & Carlson, 1992; Larsen & Levine, 2005), whether IS research can draw from other mature disciplines or be a reference discipline (Baskerville & Myers, 2002; Benbasat & Weber, 1996), whether IS research has established a cumulative research tradition (Grover, Gokhale, Lim, Coffey, & Ayyagari, 2006; Keen, 1980); the maturity of the IS discipline (Grover, 2012; Wade, Biehl, & Kim, 2006); and the diversity of IS research over time (Benbasat & Weber, 1996; Dickson, Benbasat, & King, 1982). Prior studies have also assessed the scientific progress, scientific impact, and reference disciplines for IS (Grover, 2012; Katerattanakul, Han, & Rea, 2006; Lowry, Karuga, & Richardson, 2007), as well as the influence of journals, authors, and articles (Bernroider, Pilkington, & Córdoba, 2013; Huang & Hsu, 2005; Loebbecke & Leidner, 2012).

In these endeavors, a typical approach has been to examine articles published in various journals, at times restricted to IS journals such as MIS Quarterly and Information Systems Research (Barki, Rivard, & Talbot, 1993) or inclusive of interdisciplinary journals such as Management Science and Decision Sciences (Vessey, Ramesh, & Glass, 2002). Such studies have included articles published during various years, including the early 1970s (Ives, Hamilton, & Davis, 1980) and extending into the 2000s (Nevo, Nevo, & Ein-Dor, 2009). Depending on the specific goals of the research, the specific data analyzed by those studies included the meta-data of articles, such as the titles, abstracts, authors, university affiliations of authors, and number of citations, using various techniques such as co-citation analysis, co-word occurrences, content analysis, semantic analysis, and categorization (Culnan, 1986; Evangelopoulos, 2016; Grover, Lee, & Durand, 1993; Love & Hirschheim, 2016; Sidorova, Evangelopoulos, Valacich, & Ramakrishnan, 2008).

Our study strives to map the evolution of IS research over the last 15 years (2003-2017) based on articles published in six leading journals. Using keywords supplied by the authors for each article, the analysis uses topic modeling to extract the major topics of research as well as differences in topics published between subgroups of journals and different time periods. The analysis shows classical topics that have been the mainstay of IS research and also novel topics that have emerged in recent years, which demonstrates the organic nature of IS research that includes new frontiers while retaining its unique core that gives its identity.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The next section introduces prior research work related to the IS discipline and its core. The following two sections describe the research methods and the results. The discussion section provides an overall summary and implications. The final section of the paper is the conclusion.

## 2. Related work

## 2.1. The Information Systems discipline

Researchers have periodically striven to examine whether or not IS may be considered a reference discipline. During the early years, IS was positioned as an applied discipline that can draw on knowledge from other more mature disciplines (Keen, 1980). Such a notion gained considerable traction partially because the then IS researchers were trained in other disciplines such as computer science, engineering, and management science, which was a contributing factor in the lack of a cohesive conceptual paradigm for IS research (Benbasat & Weber, 1996). Efforts were made to determine the reference disciplines into those that provide fundamental theory, underlying disciplines, and related applied disciplines (Culnan, 1987) and to also expand the reference disciplines to include others such as economics (Bakos & Kemerer, 1992), anthropology (Avison & Myers, 1995), and new product development (Nambisan, 2003). IS research had drawn from a variety of disciplines and has also considered its own impact on other disciplines (Wade et al., 2006a; Wade et al., 2006b). Researchers have also engaged in debates about the diversity of IS research over time. Debates have dealt with frameworks, research problems, research methods, theoretical foundations, and paradigms (Banville & Landry, 1989; Benbasat & Weber, 1996; Dickson et al., 1982; Nolan & Wetherbe, 1980). Studies concluded that the IS field had developed into a mature discipline with strong evidence of a cumulative research tradition (Grover et al., 2006; Wade et al., 2006a).

## 2.2. Intellectual core of the Information Systems discipline

Assessments of the intellectual core or the diversity of IS research have been periodically offered by researchers over time. Broadly, such studies have variously examined the keywords, titles, abstracts, or the full text of prior research and adopted techniques such as classification,

co-citation analysis, and co-word analysis to develop a high-level abstraction of the IS discipline. Whereas classification techniques may have relied on pre-defined categories and required coding to classify the articles (Alavi & Carlson, 1992), others such as co-word analysis may have let the themes emerge from the analysis (Larsen & Levine, 2005). During the early years, such studies had included articles published in both IS journals (i.e., MIS Quarterly) and interdisciplinary journals (e.g., Harvard Business Review); however, during the later years, such studies generally included all or a subset of the journals in the Senior Scholars’ Basket of IS journals<sup>1</sup> or a few additional IS journals (e.g., Information & Management). Using the same set of journals and articles, it was found that computer-related concepts were handled to a greater extent in computer science and software engineering journals (Glass, Ramesh, & Vessey, 2004).

Studies published in MIS Quarterly and Information Systems Research until 2006 since inception were examined in an attempt to understand the IS identity. An analysis of abstracts extracted from articles published between 1991 and 2014 in eight prominent IS journals revealed five broad topics (i.e., IT and organizations, IT artifact and IS development, IT and individuals, IT and markets, and IT for teamwork and collaboration) around which IS research has been established (Evangelopoulos, 2016).

## 2.3. This Study

Different perspectives into IS research have been offered by the considerable body of prior literature as discussed above. While recognizing the many insights from these studies, it is possible to identify a few opportunities for this study. First, the corpus used for analysis ranged from the meta-data of articles (such as titles, abstracts, and keywords) to the content of articles (such as theory, research propositions, and research methods), which can influence differences in findings and conclusions. For instance, Liu et al. (2016) found adoption/acceptance to be a core although none of the other studies uncovered either topic. Second, the sources (i.e., publication outlets) for the corpora differed between studies, which can also lead to different findings. For instance, Vessey et al. (2002) and Glass et al. (2004) found differences in content across different types of journals. Finally, the data analysis techniques employed in the study can be another influencing factor. For instance, both Love and Hirschheim (2016) and Evangelopoulos (2016) applied different techniques on data from similar sources but offered different summaries of IS research. Further, prior studies of IS research have provided insights using articles from multiple journals published until 2012-2013, but the IS field has experienced changes in recent times.

This study examines the evolution of IS research in studies published between 2003 and 2017 in IS journals. Specifically, it applies topic modeling on the corpus of author-supplied keywords available in the published articles. This study uses keywords rather than abstracts or other text from articles because the keywords provided by authors are likely to represent the major aspects of their studies. Although abstracts can provide additional textual data on the studies, they may also be confounded by textual data not directly representative of the study especially in the absence of pre-defined dictionaries of potential keywords that could be extracted from the abstracts. Moreover, this study complements prior research by also examining the more recent research since 2012-2013 and the diversity of knowledge in IS research.

This study, therefore, makes several contributions. First, unlike prior studies in which abstracts are analyzed to uncover important topical trends, this study examines the changes and advancements in IS research through the lens of keywords. Second, this study identifies topics that have evolved over the years, including those that have endured, emerged, or faded with the changes in the IS profession. Finally, this study provides an overall status of IS research as well as implications and potential avenues for further research.

In this study, the keywords—also referred to as terms later in the context of topic modeling—in the articles were used for analysis. The keywords are generally supplied by the authors and represent the central elements of the research reported in articles. Keywords have been used to highlight concepts (e.g., “cognitive absorption” in Goel, Johnson, Junglas, and Ives (2011)), theories (e.g., “resource-based view” in Wade and Hulland (2004)), models (e.g., “technology acceptance model” in Sharma, Yetton, and Crawford (2009)), and empirical methods (e.g., “meta-analysis” in Sabherwal and Jeyaraj (2015)) in research. Keywords have had a rich tradition and recognition since the inception of journals (e.g., “information analysis techniques” in Munro and Davis (1977); “data dictionaries/directories” in Konsynski (1984); and “strategic information systems” in Reich and Benbasat (1990)) before keyword classification

schemes for ISs were developed (Barki et al. 1988); electronic databases began using keywords for indexing; or online systems offering keyword suggestions were used by journals for the peer review of research articles. To verify that keywords are an acceptable corpus for our analysis and not unduly impacted by electronic archival or indexing systems, we reviewed the manuscript submission systems of the journals in our research. Our review showed that the Manuscript Central system used by MISQ, ISR, EJIS, and JAIS provides a list of keywords, which allows authors to not only choose but also add their own keywords; the ISJ system does not offer a list of keywords, and hence, the authors can specify the keywords for their research; and JMIS does Assuming that the authors are most familiar with and would desire to specify the keywords that best describe their research, the keywords were used as the corpus for our analysis.

The data required cleansing owing to different issues. First, articles such as editorials were dropped because they did not contribute analyzable keywords to the dataset. Second, the keywords were standardized to eliminate duplicate references to the same concept. For instance, “IS” and “information systems”; “IT” and “information technology”; “ERP” and “enterprise resource planning”; “DSS” and “decision support system”; “SEM” and “structural equation model”; “PLS” and “partial least squares”; “use,” “usage,” and “utilization”; “TAM” and “Technology Acceptance Model,” and “UTAUT” and “Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology” were coded as similar words. Finally, phrases such as “information technology,” “information systems,” and “enterprise resource planning” were preserved by replacing spaces with underscore characters. More than 17000 keywords were extracted from all articles, which resulted in more than 9000 unique keywords. The Text filter in SAS Enterprise Miner was used to eliminate keywords that appear very rarely across the articles. The majority of keywords were used one to three times, whereas about 1000 keywords had been used between four and nine times across the articles. The keywords, which appeared in less than four articles, were excluded from the analysis to create more relevant topics in the analysis. The interactive filter viewer was used to inspect keep/drop decisions to preserve important information. The text filter output indicated that 660 keywords, referred to as “descriptors,” were retained for topic modeling. ## 3.2. Topic Modeling and Visualization

Figure 2 summarizes the topic modeling strategy. The overall analysis was based on all available author-supplied keywords. Additional analyses were conducted based on different considerations. First, the articles were divided into two subgroups on the basis of publication type: the first subgroup included articles published in MISQ, ISR, and JMIS, generally recognized as the highest quality journals for the IS discipline (Lowry, Moody, Gaskin, Galletta, Humphreys, Barlow, & Wilson, 2013), and the second subgroup included articles published in EJIS, ISJ, and JAIS. Second, the articles were divided into two subgroups on the basis of the year of publication: the first subgroup included articles published during 2003-2009 and the second subgroup included articles published during 2010-2017.

The Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI) algorithm in SAS Enterprise Miner was used to analyze keywords and summarize articles by identifying topics. In doing so, text parsing was first applied to transform documents into vectors of document-by-keyword matrix. Various actions such as tokenization, lemmatization, stemming, spell-checking, and synonyms were performed to prepare the data set for analysis. The document-by-keyword matrix was very large and sparse. Therefore, the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) method was applied to reduce the dimensionality of the data, yet retain most of the meaningful information. Various techniques such as entropy, inverse document frequency, and mutual information are available for term keywords. Evangelopoulos, Zhang, and Prybutok (2012) suggested that the entropy method is better suited for short text documents. Therefore, the entropy index was used to calculate keyword weights based on the overall frequency and document frequency as follows:

$$
w _ {i} = 1 + \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {n} \frac {\left(f _ {i j} / g _ {i}\right) . \log_ {2} \left(f _ {i j} / g _ {i}\right)}{\log_ {2} (n)}
$$

where $f _ { i j }$ is the number of times the $i ^ { t h }$ keywords appear in the $j ^ { t h }$ document, $g _ { i }$ is the frequency of occurrence of keyword i in the document collection, and n is the total number of documents in the collection (Chakraborty, Pagolu, & Garla, 2014). The keyword-by-document matrix contains these weights in addition to the term and document frequencies. Topics were extracted using the latent semantic analysis. The LSI topic modeling algorithm relies on SVD to transform the weighted keyword-by-document matrix into a dense but low dimensional representation. The resultant SVDs contained the latent semantic dimensions of the text documents, which provide insights into how documents and keywords are related. A term topic weight was calculated for each term. Each topic is essentially an SVD dimension, and the coordinates of a term in an SVD dimension is the term topic weights for that term. Similarly, each document was given a score (document topic weight), which indicated the strength of association to a topic. The document topic weight of a document toward a topic is the normalized sum of entropy weightings for each term in the document multiplied by their term topic weights. The term topic weights and document topic weights were used to establish cutoff values for each multi-term topic. A term cutoff value is equal to the mean + one standard deviation of all term topic weights for a topic,

which determines whether a term belongs to that topic or not. Any term with an absolute term topic weight greater than this threshold is assigned to this topic. The same approach is used to calculate a document cutoff value for a given document and to determine whether a document belongs to a topic (Chakraborty et al., 2014).

LSI applies the principal component analysis to textual data. The number of dimensions (topics) may be determined using different methods (McDonald et al 2014). The scree plot is one of the more commonly used methods (Cattell, 1966). It involves sorting the eigenvalues of the SVD dimensions and plotting them to determine where they level off (known as the “elbow” of eigenvalues to determine the exact elbow point on the scree plot (Zhu & Ghodsi, 2006). The dimensions that appear before the elbow are considered meaningful and retained for further analysis and interpretation.

Consistent with the focus of our research on articles published after the dot-com bust, “ecommerce” and “online trust” have been prominent during the study period. These are reflective of the general expansion to online operations (e.g., e-commerce) and external-facing applications (e.g., Customer Relationship Management: CRM) by organizations (Chen & Popovich, 2003; Dehning, Richardson, Urbaczewski, & Wells, 2004). “Business value of IT” and “IT outsourcing” were seen more frequently in MISQ, ISR, and JMIS journals than others, whereas “knowledge management” and “social media” were found to a greater extent in MISQ, ISR, and JMIS journals but during 2003-2009 and 2010-2017, respectively. “IS implementation” and “IT capability” were seen more frequently in EJIS, ISJ, and JAIS journals than others but during 2003-2009 and 2010-2017, respectively. These topics may be viewed as indicators of managerial challenges for harnessing IT and gaining overall benefits. “IS security” was more prominent during 2010-2017 and “research methodology” was more prevalent in EJIS, ISJ, and JAIS journals.

The results of the period subgroups and the post hoc analyses yielded insights into IS research and its evolution. “Knowledge management” and “IS implementation” were prominent topics during 2003-2009, whereas “IS security,” “privacy,” “open source,” and “social media” were prominent topics during 2010-2017. In conjunction with the results of the trend analysis (Table 12), “IS security,” “online trust,” and “social media” are topics gaining considerable attention in IS research. Topics such as “IS architecture” “IS evaluation,” “IS management,” and “IT infrastructure” emerged from the post hoc analysis indicating the variety of considerations crucial for IS. Topics such as “e-government” and “computer-mediated communication” further reveal the internet-based technological environment in vogue, whereas topics such as “enterprise systems,” “inter-organizational systems,” and “decision support systems” show a renewed interest in new generations of different types of IT.

There is also evidence of IS research attending to its own craft with several topics relating to research emerging from the analysis. These include “research methodology,” “case study,” “game theory,” “IS research,” “ontology,” “structural equation modeling,”, and “design science.” Studies employing grounded theory, action research, and case study are increasingly common, and a greater attention toward design science and ontology is apparent. Several topics from this category have emerged in the recent past, although self-evaluation and progress in IS research have been evident over time.

These findings demonstrate that IS research has been adept at examining various IS artifacts, IS environments, and IS professional activities. This trend is likely to continue as newer technologies evolve with the changing nature of work and use of IS. Breakthroughs in recent times include blockchains, internet of things (IoT), and machine learning, which raise novel and unique questions for the IS profession. The example of blockchains may be used to illustrate the ways in which IS research is likely to respond based on Table 16—e.g., blockchains could be viewed as an IS artifact that relies on online capabilities and challenges the extant notions of the control, provision, and management of data. This implies that IS research can focus on the challenges with acceptance, adoption, and implementation of blockchains; the changes in professional training that may be required for both developers and users; and the extent to which blockchains can provide value to the adopters. These can be extended into other areas of inquiry within the IS profession.

Considering such emerging technologies and applications such as IoT, artificial intelligence (AI), and virtual reality (VR)/augmented reality (AR), it is possible to surmise how IS research may evolve in the near future. First, it is likely that humans would have considerable latitude in merging with or immersing in technology. This is already seen with the use of AR/VR technologies for different applications in healthcare, utility, and retail sectors. Second, it is likely that AI applications that act independent of humans will be developed and deployed. The use of AI is currently seen in autonomous vehicles, customer service, and healthcare settings. Finally, humans may interact with IoT-enabled self-regulating fully-automated environments in both workplace and non-workplace settings. The IoT infrastructure is already seen in action in smart appliances and devices controlled using smartphone apps. Although the development of these emerging technologies span multiple disciplines, they raise opportunities for IS research in the domains of privacy, acceptance, and benefits.

To gain insights into the evolution of IS research over time, this research applied topic modeling techniques to author-supplied keywords in articles and identified the top topics across all journals and years, by journal and year subgroups, and post hoc analyses. A combination of results across different analyses yielded 35 topics, including both enduring and emerging topics. Trend analyses also show the topics that have gathered research interest (e.g., IS security and

social media) and those that have waned over time (e.g., e-commerce and IT outsourcing). The results of this study are consistent with prior findings (e.g., topics such as IS development, IT adoption, and IS usage) and also provide insights beyond prior literature (e.g., topics such as design science, ontology, social media, online communities, open source, online trust, and privacy).

## 5.2. Limitations

The findings of our study should be interpreted in light of its limitations. First, our study analyzed the author-supplied keywords of published articles. Prior studies have examined titles, abstracts, keywords, and main text of articles. While the authors know their research and are in a vantage position to summarize their work through the most-relevant keywords, it can be argued that the keywords alone may not adequately capture the research. Second, our study included articles published in only six journal outlets and during 2003-2017. IS research is published in hundreds of publication outlets and over a long period of time (e.g., MIS Quarterly, included in our study, dates back to 1977 and Data Base, not included in our study, has been published since 1969). Although topics such as “IS development” and “IT adoption” identified in this study have endured over time, it is not unreasonable to expect that the topics and findings in our study could be different when other journals and publication years are included. Finally, our analysis is based on the LSI algorithm built into the SAS Enterprise Miner software package and has not been compared against other algorithms for topic modeling. It is possible that there could be slight variations in the topics and findings of our study when other topic modeling algorithms are employed.

## 5.3. Future Research

Although the study has revealed useful insights into IS research, future research can plan extensions for additional perspectives. First, the list of journals can be expanded to include others from the AIS Senior Scholars’ Basket of IS Journals (i.e., Journal of Information Technology and Journal of Strategic Information Systems), other prominent or prestigious IS journals (e.g., Information & Management, Decision Support Systems, Data Base for Advances in Information Systems), and interdisciplinary journals that publish IS research (e.g., Management Science, Decision Sciences, Communications of the ACM, Academy of Management Journal,

Organization Science). Second, the publication years can be expanded to include earlier years such that a historical perspective of the evolution of IS research may be determined, which can offer insights into trends, fads, and features of IS research over time. Such perspectives have the potential to yield patterns that can be used to predict the changes that could be expected within the IS discipline. Third, it may be possible to apply different algorithms for topic modeling that may enable a finer or a comparative identification of topics, which has the potential to enable robustness tests of the topics identified. Although the subgroup analysis in the study provides a reasonable level of verification based on similar and related topics that emerged, it may be possible to further expand the confidence levels of the topics with additional tests. Fourth, the topic modeling may be applied to an expanded corpus that includes other metadata such as abstracts and titles in addition to the author-supplied keywords. Although the author-supplied keywords provide a valid assessment of the research article, journals may impose an arbitrary limit on the number of keywords that may be specified for an article, which could limit the analysis. Finally, the entire set of extracted topics may be used for interpreting and assessing the status of IS research over time.

## 6. Conclusion

The study was initiated with a goal of mapping the evolution of IS research over time. Using articles published in six leading journals between 2003 and 2017, topic modeling was applied to more than 600 unique author-supplied keywords. Results based on all journals in all years, by journal and year subgroups, and post hoc analysis revealed various topics, some of which were of enduring interest (e.g., IS development, IT adoption, and IS usage), whereas others had gained momentum and then waned (e.g., e-commerce) and still others have emerged in more recent times (e.g., IS security, privacy, open source, design science, and social media). The results collectively demonstrate the evolution of IS research and its dual character by including new frontiers while serving its core strength and identity.
