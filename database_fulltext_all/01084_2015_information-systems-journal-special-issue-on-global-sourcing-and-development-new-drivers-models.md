# Information Systems Journal Special Issue on: Global Sourcing and Development: New Drivers, Models and Impacts

## Background

Since the <sup>fi</sup>rst software contracts were outsourced to India in the late 1970s, the relationship between global sourcing and development has expanded in various ways. The continuing reduction in cost and increase in capacity of computing coupled with improved of telecommunications links (wireless, mobile) has expanded global sourcing geographically to encompass many other countries in Asia, Africa and Latin America/Caribbean (e.g. Abbott & Jones, 2012; Graham & Mann, 2013); and also marginalised areas in the global North including rural locations and prisons (e.g. Lacity 2010; Lacity 2014). Global sourcing has expanded in scope from software to encompass a wide variety of IT-enabled services including call centres, digitisation, business, knowledge and lega process outsourcing. Recent forms of global sourcing include cloud sourcing which promises to reduce costs of infrastructure and microsourcing platforms that globally connect clients and providers (Amazon mechanical turk, freelancer, guru etc.)

These expansions of geography and scope enabled by an expansion of digital infrastructure and skills have caused the relationship between global sourcing and development to be revisited. Global sourcing was originally seen only in the light of neo-liberal and human capita paradigms; adding national export earnings, GDP and skills. Today, global sourcing is increasingly related to inclusive and sustainable development paradigms, and to corporate social and environmental responsibility (Babin & Nicholson, 2012; Carmel 2014; Heeks, 2013; Madon & Sharanappa, 2013; Malik 2013; Sandeep 2013).

This special issue seeks to re<sup>fl</sup>ect on this changing context for global sourcing: analysing these new forces driving and shaping global sourcing; characterising the new models of sourcing these forces have created; and reassessing the impacts of global sourcing in light of these new criteria.

To re<sup>fl</sup>ect this, Information Systems Journal is planning a special issue; "Global Sourcing and Development: New Drivers, Models and Impacts".

## Call for Papers

For this special issue, we are seeking papers that help to conceptualise, analyse and critique topics on global sourcing and development including:

\* The new or extended development paradigms and worldviews that are being brought to bear on global sourcing.

The relation between these new paradigms and client demand, including orientation to pro<sup>fi</sup>t and/or philanthropy, Corporate Social Responsibility, and the selling and marketing of globa sourcing’s development connection.

\* The new global sourcing models that are emerging in both industrialised and developing countries: "impact sourcing", "rural sourcing", "socially-responsible sourcing", "ethical sourcing" and others.

\* Intermediaries, relationship management, control, risk, trust, governance and contracting in developmentally sensitised global sourcing.

Assessments of readiness and enablers/barriers to these new models.

Regulations, standards, and codes of practice to guide the practice of global sourcing.

Supply-related practice in developing capacities and infrastructure for new forms of global sourcing.

\* Metrics and models of use in assessing the socio-economic development impact of global sourcing.

\* The role of the state in facilitating global sourcing via creation of an enabling environment, capacity building, procurement, enforcement of minimum standards, etc

Impact of environmental responsibilities on global sourcing and development.

\* Implications of new technology-enabled models for global sourcing and development: cloud sourcing, micro-sourcing, crowd-sourcing, etc.
