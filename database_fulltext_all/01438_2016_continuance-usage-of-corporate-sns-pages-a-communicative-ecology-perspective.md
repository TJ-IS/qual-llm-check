# Continuance usage of corporate SNS pages: A communicative ecology perspective

## Abstract

This study develops a research model that explains the continuance of corporate SNS pages. To find antecedents that influence end-users’ continuance intention, this study adopts communicative ecology theory from media and communication studies. Results indicate that the quality of social interaction is the most salient factor, as indicated by the fact that it has the greatest impact on perceived usefulness and enjoyment. Moreover, content quality also increases both perceived usefulness and enjoyment, while SNS platform quality and service quality increase perceived usefulness only. In addition, perceived network size moderates the impact of social interaction quality on perceived enjoyment.

Keywords: social network services (SNS), corporate SNS pages, communicative ecology theory, social interaction quality, continuance intention

## 1. Introduction

In the past few years, social network services (SNSs) have gained much popularity among millions of people and have consequently become a part of their daily routines. Their dramatic diffusion and growing influence on society have increased the commercial value of SNSs. In accordance with this trend, many firms have competitively sought and evaluated the potential benefits of SNSs to achieve their commercial goals. As a result, an increasing number of firms have created community pages in public SNSs (e.g., Facebook and Twitter). These corporate SNS pages are a new way to manage relationships with customers and improve brand image, thus further enhancing customer loyalty and encouraging positive word-of-mouth (WOM) recommendations. Moreover, recent trends demonstrate the high business value of corporate SNS pages, which generate a significant proportion of e-commerce traffic. In summary, corporate SNS pages have become an essential part of businesses.

Despite their increasing reliance on corporate SNS pages for business, most companies find it difficult to achieve all of the desired benefits by using a corporate SNS page. For example, Gartner [26] estimated that 80 percent of social business initiatives will not achieve their intended benefits because of inadequate management and an overemphasis on technology. In addition, according to a survey by Manta [51], 61 percent of small and medium-sized companies have seen no return on their social media investment. This significant gap between expectation and reality, as a phenomenon, has not yet been examined by IS scholars. So far, most IS scholars have focused on user behavior in general SNS settings. This is because, at least during the early period of SNS diffusion, they viewed SNSs as communities for human interaction with no commercial purpose. However, consumers have used SNSs to ask companies about their favorite products, access information, participate in company-sponsored events, and communicate with peer members of the same corporate SNS page.

To enhance the commercial use and value of a corporate SNS page, many firms are agonizing about the ways to induce more people to join and continuously visit their SNS pages. As an example of such activity, firms often offer advantageous incentives such as free prize lottery events. These phenomena raise two important questions for research. First, do people actually perceive more benefits (i.e., usefulness and enjoyment) if there are more people to communicate with? Second, do incentives really attract users to become regular visitors or perceive greater benefits from the system? For the first question, previous studies have demonstrated the positive impacts of perceived network size on users’ benefits by introducing constructs

#

reflecting the size of potential communication targets, such as referent network size, the number of members (or peers), and network externalities. However, they have mainly examined the direct effect of perceived network size [44, 49] and their results are also far from consistent. Lin and Bhattacherjee [44] argued that network size significantly impacts both usefulness and enjoyment. Conversely, Lin and Lu [49] found that the number of members is only positively associated with usefulness, while Wu, Chen and Lin [69] suggested that network externalities do not have any significant impact on usefulness. Regarding the second question of incentives, prior research has found that usage-based incentives (e.g., point-based reward system) are a salient moderator of the impact of usefulness on continuance intention [6]. In terms of one-time incentives that are generally offered on corporate SNS pages, however, whether they have a positive influence on users’ continuance remains a question. In previous studies, one-time incentives were found to be weak but sometimes significant [21, 59]. Accordingly, it is necessary to discuss those inconsistent and fragmented results and verify how network size and incentives affect consumer’s behavior in the context of corporate SNS pages.

The purpose of this study is to develop a theoretical model to explain the continuance usage of corporate SNS pages. Recent information system studies have revealed diverse approaches toward individual acceptance of SNSs including examining the impact of information quality and source credibility on information sharing [33], assessing factors that influence trust and perceived risk and their impact on SNS usage intention [50], and studying the corporate use of the SNS as a new ctive communication tool within organizations [62]. However, there is no solid theoretical understanding of the overall SNS structure nor have corporate SNS pages been explored. In order to fill this research gap, we introduce communicative ecology theory (CET), used in the fields of sociology, media, and communications, to explain the impact of technology on both online and offline social networks. As CET applies not only to technology but also to content and social interaction dimensions from a balanced perspective, we believe that CET can provide a holistic foundation for examining SNSs (including corporate SNS pages). In other words, the dimensions (technological, discursive, and social) of CET can provide a firm foundation for embracing both the social dimension and the theoretical range of existing IS models, thus fully exploiting existing findings on utility-based IS in an SNS context. Based on CET, this study proposes a comprehensive research model by validating the independent and moderating factors that encourage end-users’ continuance intention. This research aims to advance our theoretical understanding of the overarching dimensions of SNSs, while also exploring the impact of user adoption behavior on corporate SNS pages—a topic which has not yet been covered in IS studies. This study also offers practical insights to organizations on

how they can successfully manage their SNS pages.

## 2. Theoretical Background

## 2.1. Corporate SNS Pages

Social networking services (SNSs) are “applications that enable users to connect by creating personal information profiles, inviting friends and colleagues to have access to those profiles, and sending e-mails and instant messages between each other” [35, p. 63]. This information technology provides a new way for organizations to become involved with users. Currently, 87% of Fortune Global 100 companies host their communities on at least one SNS platform in order to engage with users [9]. Furthermore, 10% of these companies have multiple accounts on Twitter, Facebook, and YouTube, in order to meet the diverse needs and interests of users. The size of corporate SNS pages has also increased quickly. For example, the average number of corporate Facebook page followers increased by 275% between 2010 and 2012.

Corporate SNS pages offer several benefits. First, they provide an additional communication channel and link to devoted users [32]. Second, they help firms enhance brand image by managing customer relationships, thus increasing sales and reducing the cost of retaining customers [4]. Third, firms can easily engage with users through corporate SNS pages. New postings are shared directly with users who follow the firm. Fourth, corporate SNS pages help to build a trusted location for sharing information related to certain firms. Users are more likely to perceive such consumer-dominated communication channels as reliable [58]. Lastly, corporate SNS pages enable firms to access people who are not interested in their work, as the long chain of followers’ friendships helps to diffuse information [66]. Peer recommendations made through an SNS also have a significant impact on the behavior of members’ friends [61].

As one of a number of corporate information systems, SNS pages provide critical elements that can help achieve success; they should therefore be properly managed to exploit such factors. According to DeLone and McLean [19], the measure that should be chosen for system success depends on the objective of the study. This study proposes end-users’ continuance intention as a success measure for corporate SNS pages for the following reasons. First, the primary reason for implementing a corporate SNS page is to build and manage excellent longterm relationships with users [35]. Having a good relationship with customers is helpful for firms in many ways.

For example, a positive company image will influence users, making them more likely to intend to purchase the firm’s products. Second, the value of corporate SNS pages is judged by the number of users who visit those pages repeatedly. A sufficient number of users should continuously visit the page and share their opinions [71]; otherwise, a corporate SNS page will lose its value and impact. Lastly, continued customer usage has a direct impact on company costs. Acquiring new customers costs five times as much as retaining existing customers

## 2.2. Communicative Ecology Theory

During the last few years, IS scholars have studied online communities, including SNSs, from various perspectives. Among these, a growing interest in social factors has been noticeable. Several IS scholars have taken a new approach to exploring the unique characteristics of online communities; perhaps as a result, only a few attempts have been made to incorporate social dimensions into the existing theoretical models. Lin [47] constructed a model of the impact of system characteristics (e.g., information and system quality) and social factors (e.g., trust and social usefulness) on the implementation of successful online communities by expanding DeLone and McLean’s IS Success Model. In addition, Kwon and Wen [39] developed a model showing the impact of individual differences (e.g., social identity, altruism, and telepresence) on SNS participants by amending the technology acceptance model (TAM). Lorenzo-Romero et al. [50] examined the influence of the levels of trust and perceived risk associated with SNS participation, also using TAM. Although previous studies have clarified the significance of social factors, their contributions combine particular social perspectives with existing IS models partially with no solid theoretical understanding of the overall composition of SNSs. In each study, social factors were presented as a fragmented collection with no underlying common characteristics; no theoretical foundation justifies the inclusion of social factors into the models. The missing element is a holistic theoretical foundation, which will allow for all of the dimensions of SNSs to be comprehensively defined and understood from an integrated viewpoint that incorporates various aspects of technology, information, and social interaction. Such a holistic theoretical background would enable both the social dimensions and the inherent characteristics of SNS as an information technology to be synthetically analyzed, while allowing the theoretical range of existing IS models to be fully exploited. Metaphorically speaking, each previous study has observed the forest from a single known vantage point, without understanding its overall size or shape. There is now a need to

#

find a way to view the whole forest comprehensively, and to examine specific factors in detail based on this holistic understanding. To fill this gap, this study introduces communicative ecology theory (CET), which is used in the fields of sociology, media, and communication; CET enables us to examine the impact of technology on online and offline social networks. We believe that using CET to articulate the dimensions of technology, content, and social interaction will provide a holistic context for comprehensively understanding corporate SNS pages and other SNS settings.

CET is a conceptual framework in the field of media and communications; it focuses on the dynamic interrelationships that link technology, content, and social interaction [25]. CET provides a holistic approach by considering not only the technical infrastructure but also the discursive content and communicative social interactions that take place. CET was initially proposed by Altheide [3] and has been further developed in several studies. Tacchi, Slater and Hearn [63] defined communicative ecologies as “processes that involve a mix of media, organized in specific ways, through which people connect with their social networks” (p. 17). Foth and Hearn [25] further broadened the conceptual boundary of communicative ecology to include “the context in which the communication process occurs” (p. 756). As the concept of ecology makes clear, communicative ecology is a network composed of various elements. For this reason, many types of people, media, activities, and relationships should be comprehensively examined; consider, as an analogy, the need to focus on many plants and animals when investigating a forest ecology [63]. To handle the immensity and complexity of ecology research, three layers of interpretation were proposed by Foth and Hearn [25]. These three layers include a technological layer, a discursive (content) layer, and a social layer. The technological layer is composed of IT devices and media, which connect people and enable interaction among them (e.g., mobile phones and internet websites). The discursive layer is composed of content that involves an idea or conversational theme. The social layer is composed of people in the process of communicating. The three layers of CET have allowed many researchers to actively research IT-mediated communicative ecologies. Examples include public internet adoption in a community [56], a media-mediated virtual organization [70], tablet PC adoption in education [5], and a media-mediated urban apartment complex [25]. More recently, new types of media (such as various web 2.0 applications) have become popular topics. Neighborhood website communities were studied as a form of new media connecting local neighborhoods [11]. In addition, applications and implementations of web 2.0 services, such as blogs and wikis, have been investigated within the context of corporate communications [30].

A holistic picture of communicative ecology provides a much clearer picture of the impact and potential of a particular medium [63]. SNS has now become a daily routine that occupies a significant part of a person’s communicative ecology [16]. Thus, this particular instance of a new communicative ecology can be examined through the three dimensions of CET. [31] CET provides a theoretical lens through which to comprehensively investigate the critical success factors of corporate SNS pages. Figure 1 represents the communicative ecology of corporate SNS pages. The technological layer is composed of SNS platforms, such as Facebook and Twitter, which firms use to create their corporate pages. The discursive layer is composed of the firm’s own content, related to products, services, events, and gossip. The social layer consists of social interaction activities involving members of the corporate SNS page. There are two types of members: firms and peer users. Users usually interact with a firm by asking a question, requesting a certain service, or writing a comment in response to an interesting posting that that the firm has uploaded. Users also engage in various forms of communication with other peer users; for example, they share information about their own experiences with a certain product or gossip with others about their daily lives.

## 3. Research Model and Hypotheses

Based on the three dimensions of corporate SNS pages, this study proposes that the quality of each dimension determines users’ continuance usage of these pages, thus leading to IS success. In particular, SNS platform quality, content quality, service quality, and social interaction quality have been developed to measure the comprehensive properties of corporate SNS pages. We suggest that these affect the continuance intention of users through the following general perceptual measures of IS continuance: perceived usefulness, perceived enjoyment, and user satisfaction [7]. We regard perceived usefulness, perceived enjoyment, and user satisfaction as variables, rather than examining the direct impact of quality factors on the continuance intention of users, in order to clarify the importance of each perceptual construct. In particular, we explore whether hedonic value, which generally significantly influences hedonic IS such as an SNS, exists in the hybrid setting of corporate SNS pages where organizational and hedonic IS characteristics intermingle. This approach is therefore more sophisticated than those adopted in previous studies, which deemed that only studying the direct relationship between quality measures and the behavioral intention of users in organizational settings where a system was

#

used only because of its usefulness was adequate. In previous studies, the causal connections among exogenous variables (e.g. characteristics of IS), endogenous variables (e.g. user perception), and dependent variables (e.g., behavioral intention) have been studied as a whole, without articulating the direct impact of particular factors on a final variable. For example, in revised TAM, Davis et al. [18] posited that exogenous variables such as the characteristics of a system are mediated by endogenous variables that explain the perception of users (for example, perceived usefulness and ease of use). Moreover, according to Seddon [60], DeLone and Mclean’s IS Success Model argued that the determining factors leading to IS success included users’ perceived benefits and satisfaction, influenced by system and information quality. As a result, the proposed research model delicately articulates the endogenous dimensions that lead to IS success through an expanded consideration of the hedonic aspect (i.e. perceived enjoyment); by doing so, it provides a theoretical foundation for understanding how perceived usefulness and enjoyment act within corporate SNS pages.

According to existing research, the presence of a large number of members does not guarantee more value for SNS users [23]. That is why previous studies used expressions constraining the boundary of network size, such as “referent” and “peers,” to reflect actual relevance toward user value. Since service performance (i.e., customer values) has previously been evaluated by two major perspectives of “quantity” and “quality” [55, 67], to fully address customer-relevant externality impacts of network size, we propose that network size plays a role as a booster (i.e., moderator) of the impacts of networking (i.e., social interaction) quality under our holistic quality-based research framework. In the case of incentives, the moderating effect on the relationship between perceived usefulness and IS continuance intention is not clear. Bhattacherjee [7] has argued that incentive programs work effectively when firms offer a useful service. He also showed that incentives do not have a direct effect but that an interaction effect exists between the incentive and the site’s perceived usefulness, which can promote IS continuance intention. However, later studies rarely discussed the moderating role of the incentives and one study failed to empirically demonstrate the effect [42]. Thus, in order to confirm the moderating role of perceived network size and incentives on corporate SNS pages, two constructs are included in the research model. ## 3.1. Relationships among Continuance Intention, User Satisfaction, Perceived Usefulness, and Perceived Enjoyment

User satisfaction is defined as an individual’s affective attitude, based on an overall assessment of experience [20]. Accordingly, when individuals are more satisfied with an IS, they are more likely to express a positive behavioral intention to use it continuously. Previous studies have highlighted user satisfaction as a salient factor that determines continuance intention [7]. It is therefore expected that highly satisfied users will tend to revisit corporate SNS pages more often as well

H1. User satisfaction will positively affect continuance intention in relation to corporate SNS pages.

Perceived usefulness refers to the degree to which individuals believe that using a particular system will enhance their performance of tasks [17]. As perceived usefulness is a primary motivation for IS use, it has been considered an important antecedent of better satisfaction and continuance intention [7, 17]. As the motivation to visit corporate SNS pages usually involves a perception of usefulness (e.g., acquiring information about products or services, sharing useful tips, or participating in a promotion event), perceived usefulness is expected to positively affect continuance intention and satisfaction.

H2a. Perceived usefulness will positively affect continuance intention on corporate SNS pages. H2b. Perceived usefulness will positively affect user satisfaction on corporate SNS pages.

Perceived enjoyment refers to “the extent to which fun can be derived from using the system” [64, p. 697]. Perceived enjoyment has been considered as a main motivation for IS use, particularly in the case of hedonic IS (e.g., home/mobile Internet, SNS, and instant messenger) [64]. Thus, if individuals feel enjoyment while using a hedonic IS, they are more likely to be satisfied and to continue to engage with it [45, 64]. As corporate SNS pages serve not only utilitarian but also hedonic purposes (for example by providing enjoyable content or opportunities to chat with others), perceived enjoyment is expected to positively affect both continuance intention and satisfaction.

H3a. Perceived enjoyment will positively affect continuance intention on corporate SNS pages.

H3b. Perceived enjoyment will positively affect user satisfaction on corporate SNS pages.

## 3.2. Technological Layer: SNS Platform Quality

SNS platform quality is the system quality of SNS platforms such as Facebook and Twitter. This quality is associated with adaptability, availability, reliability, response time, and usability [20]. A high level of system quality provides more convenient and interactive space through user-familiar functions, audio-visual support, and user customization [2]. SNS requires capabilities such as accessibility, stability, and system speed in real time, as well as interactive communication among users. High quality IS could help individuals have a more fulfilling and enjoyable experience [43]. If an SNS is unst communicating with each other, and this disturbance may prevent them from enjoying the space. Thus, SNS platform quality is expected to have a positive impact on perceived usefulness and perceived enjoyment in corporate SNS pages.

H4a. SNS platform quality will positively affect the perceived usefulness of corporate SNS pages. H4b. SNS platform quality will positively affect the enjoyment of corporate SNS pages.

## 3.3. Discursive Layer: Content Quality

The content quality of corporate SNS pages refers to the quality of communication content provided by a firm; it includes such characteristics as completeness, ease of understanding, personalization, and relevance [46]. Content quality is important for commercial online services that are generally considered sources of useful information. A high level of complete, detailed, accurate, timely, relevant, and reliable content is very likely to meet the information needs of users. Thus, content quality positively influences the perception of usefulness [20]. Prior research has also shown that enriched content can be more interesting [34]. Current SNS environments provide various types of multi-media, and interactive and customized content that sometimes includes interesting events and monetary promotions. This content is likely to enhance the enjoyment of users, thus increasing their perception that corporate SNS pages are useful and enjoyable.

H5a. Content quality will positively affect perceived usefulness on corporate SNS pages.

H5b. Content quality will positively affect perceived enjoyment on corporate SNS pages.

## 3.4. Social Layer: Service Quality

Service quality is the quality of social interaction between users and a firm; it reflects the assurance, empathy, and responsiveness of a firm [46]. Service quality is important because poor service quality can alienate customers [20]. In the online context, service quality is particularly important because firms aim to provide extensive services that customers want (information, a complaints process for the offline store, and real-time answers) [2]. A high level of service quality can address users’ needs efficiently, quickly, and sensitively. Thus, service quality improves perceived usefulness and increases user enjoyment through the provision of a comfortable environment. It is therefore expected that service quality will have a positive impact on the perception that corporate SNS pages are useful and enjoyable.

H6a. Service quality will positively affect the perceived usefulness of corporate SNS pages.

H6b. Service quality will increase the perceived enjoyment of corporate SNS pages.

Their work examined the main reasons why people interact socially with others in a virtual community; they found that information, social support, friendship, and recreation are the main motivations. Thus, it is logical to think that the quality of social interaction reflects the degree to which such needs are fulfilled. Following Ridings and Gefen [57], recreation is described as entertainment; people undertake it “because it is fun” and because “I enjoy reading and posting in the community.” This means that recreation is usually perceived as a consequence of other factors that overlap with perceived enjoyment. Research results suggest that this omission is not problematic. Previous studies have shown that social interaction quality may positively affect perceived usefulness and enjoyment. As one of the key reasons why people use corporate SNS pages is to achieve individual goals through social interaction with others [57], high quality social interaction could help to achieve their goals. Users may also enjoy their involvement more when they can interact with people who have similar interests. Social interaction quality is therefore expected to have a positive impact on the extent to which users perceive the site as useful and enjoyable.

H7a. Social interaction quality will positively affect the perceived usefulness of corporate SNS pages. H7b. Social interaction quality will affect the extent to which users enjoy corporate SNS pages.

## 3.6. Moderators: Perceived Network Size and Incentives

Positive consumption externalities may exist when the quality and availability of post-purchase service for goods depend on the experience and size of the service network [36]. That is, for customer value, there is a positive relationship between the quality of post-purchase service and network size. In the context of corporate SNS pages, the quality of post-purchase service, which seems highly related to network size, is the quality of networking with other members, or social interaction quality. Accordingly, we can assume that perceived benefits that users derive from the positive impacts of social interaction quality may also be enhanced by a large number of members owing to their resources. For example, as more users exchange information, socially support each other, and build friendships, their chances to obtain useful information and feel enjoyment increase. Several previous studies have directly or indirectly addressed these positive effects of network size on users perceived usefulness and enjoyment [44, 49]. Moreover, the moderating effect of network size has been examined in dissimilar settings [22]. Therefore, we propose that perceived network size moderates the positive relationship between social interaction quality and users’ perceived usefulness and enjoyment.

H8a. Perceived network size will moderate the effect of social interaction quality on perceived usefulness. H8b. Perceived network size will moderate the effect of social interaction quality on perceived enjoyment.

Firms often offer incentive programs to enhance utility and motivate customers to continue using an IT service [6]. Such incentive programs are also expected to persuade customers to contribute to firms and adapt their behavior in the direction that the firms intended [65]. According to Bhattacherjee [6], while incentives do not have a direct impact on users’ continuance intention, an interaction effect exists between incentives and the perceived usefulness of the IT service, which further promotes continuance intention. Moreover, his research suggested that incentive programs work effectively only when users’ intended objective for the information system is satisfied. In the same vein, Lee, Lee and Kwon [41] also validated the moderating impact of incentives that enhance the positive relationship between usefulness and usage intention in computerized reservation systems. Moreover, perceived usefulness has been considered as a representative extrinsic motivation in IS adoption studies [64]. From a financial perspective, an incentive is also an extrinsic motivation while perceived usefulness represents an aspect of performance. When these two different types of motivation exist concurrently, the effect on continuance intention can increase. Similarly, users’ continuance intention toward corporate SNS pages is expected to be higher when incentives are provided as goals are achieved. Thus, we may assume that incentives are expected to moderate the effect of perceived usefulness on continuance intention toward corporate SNS pages.

H9. An incentive will moderate the effect of perceived usefulness on continuance intention.

## 5.1. Instrument Validation

The theoretical research model was analyzed with SmartPLS using partial least squares (PLS), because PLS places less restriction on variable distribution and is appropriate for testing theories in the early stages of development (for testing established theories, LISREL is preferred) [14]. The measurement model was assessed using three main criteria: 1) internal consistency; 2) convergent validity; 3) discriminant validity.

First, the internal consistency of each item was checked by calculating Cronbach’s alpha. The recommended value of Cronbach’s alpha is above Nunnally [53]’s criterion of 0.70; however, a Cronbach’s alpha of more than 0.60 is generally considered acceptable. The assessment of Cronbach’s alpha yielded

To ensure convergent validity, the factor loadings and AVE should be at least above 0.50 [24, 68], and CR should exceed 0.70 [28]. Our results confirmed that the convergent validity was acceptable by showing that all indices were above the recommended values (Table 3). Lastly, discriminant validity was assessed by analyzing the overall correlation between the constructs and the squared root of AVE, which for each construct should exceed the correlations between it and all other constructs [24]. The results in Table 4 show that, although a few correlations were slightly high, the analysis of the square root of AVE confirmed a satisfactory discriminant validity. However, we decided to perform Harman’s one-factor analysis because a common method bias could influence the results. Common method bias is high if the results show that more than 50% of variance can be explained by a single factor [29]. Our analysis results showed that the combined seven factors accounted for 68.38% of total variance and the first factor accounted for 38.33%. No general factor accounted for more than 50% of variance. These results indicate that common method bias may not be a serious problem.

## 6. Discussion and Implications

## 6.1. Discussion of Findings

This study has produced several meaningful findings. First, user satisfaction had the greatest influence on predicting users’ intention to continue using corporate SNS pages, followed by perceived enjoyment and perceived usefulness. This finding is consistent with previous studies that have argued for the salience of satisfaction, perceived usefulness, and perceived enjoyment in determining users’ behavioral intentions toward an IS [7, 64]. Second, and most importantly, the quality of social interaction increases perceived usefulness and perceived enjoyment much more than other factors do. This result extends the IS success model perspective [20], which considers system quality, information quality, and service quality to be the main dimensions of IS success, since social interaction quality is the most salient determinant of the success of corporate SNS pages. Third, SNS platform quality, content quality, and service quality increased perceived usefulness. While these results are in line with previous studies that have stressed the importance of system quality, information quality, and service quality as determinants of perceived usefulness [20], it is meaningful to examine those values within the new context of corporate SNS pages. Fourth, this study also found that content quality significantly increases perceived enjoyment. This result is consistent with previous research findings that high quality online service information helps users obtain more fun and enjoyment [34]. Fifth, perceived network size moderates the impact of social interaction quality on perceived enjoyment. Prior research has argued that perceived network size indirectly or directly affects perceived enjoyment [44, 49]. This study extends that idea by finding that perceived network size causes a positive interaction effect linking networking quality to perceived enjoyment.

However, the model involves four insignificant relationships. First, SNS platform quality and service quality have no direct impact on perceived enjoyment. This may be due to the properties of perceived enjoyment related to intrinsic motivations. Although SNS platform quality and service quality may serve as facilitating

#

conditions for users to obtain more fun by providing a more comfortable setting, perceived enjoyment may naturally arise from the intrinsic drivers in corporate SNS pages, where there are abundant opportunities to interact with content and people [64]. Second, perceived network size does not moderate the impact of social interaction quality on perceived usefulness. This result contradicts previous studies, which showed that network size directly or indirectly increases perceived usefulness [44, 49]. However, considering that perceived usefulness is more related to quality attributes, intuitively, a large amount of information does not definitely guarantee increased quality. Some previous studies also demonstrated contradictory results that failed to significantly link perceived network size effects with perceived usefulness [13, 69]. Lastly, incentives do not have both direct and moderating effects on continuance intention. Although the non-significance of a direct effect conforms to Bhattacherjee [6], the moderating role of incentives differs. This discordance may come from the difference between types of incentives. While Bhattacherjee [6] examined usage-based incentives such as mileage and bonus points, incentives on corporate SNS pages usually involve non usage-based incentives, such as an event lottery. This result therefore implies that non usage-based incentives are unlikely to have both a significant direct and a moderating impact on users’ continuance intention. Previous literature also confirms that inappropriate incentives do not work effectively to influence user behavior [40]. On the other hand, a direct effect on satisfaction was observed in an additional analysis. This result is in line with a previous study, which showed that incentive is a significant predictor of user satisfaction in online communities [12]. Overall results show that the existence of an incentive enhances SNS users’ satisfaction, but that the right type of incentive is required for user retention.

## 6.2. Theoretical Implications

This research has several implications and contributes to the theoretical knowledge on this topic in a number of ways. One primary contribution involves the application of communicative ecology theory (CET) to explore determinants that affect user continuance intention in relation to corporate SNS pages. Through its use of CET, this study provides a deeper and more holistic analysis of the success of corporate SNS pages, thereby contributing to IS success research. We recommend that CET be adopted for use in diverse social IS contexts, including SNSs. This study also contributes by operationalizing each dimension of CET and testing the developed model through survey methodology; this represents a precedent trial in IS research.

#

Second, as a result of introducing CET to IS literature, this study extends the theoretical dimensions considered relevant to an examination of IS success by finding that quality of social interaction is the most important factor in the success of corporate SNS pages. Since previous theoretical models [8, 57] have shown that social interaction quality can be successfully developed by combining exchange information, social support, and friendship, this construct is expected to be widely applied in future studies, especially in relation to the only in SNS studies, but also in IS research that incorporates social dimensions, based on a solid theoretical foundation.

Third, this study provides a richer understanding of the role of perceived network size and incentives. While there have been controversial findings about the positive impact of perceived network size on perceived usefulness, this study extends understanding by finding that perceived network size does not have a positive impact on perceived usefulness given the presence of network quality (social interaction quality). In the same vein, this research also clarifies the synergy effect of perceived network size and network quality on perceived enjoyment. Furthermore, this study extends the previous understanding that usage-based incentives indirectly increase behavioral intention by showing that non usage-based incentives have no positive, indirect impact on behavioral intention.

This study has yet another important theoretical implication for the cultivation of new SNS research areas. The main focus of previous IS studies has been understanding user behavior from the perspective of private usage of public SNSs, without taking the business perspective into account [33, 39, 71]. By excavating corporate SNS pages, this study widens the scope of SNS research to include commercial SNS corporate pages, which have attracted much attention from businesses in recent years.

## 6.3. Practical Implications

The results of this study suggest ways in which businesses might successfully manage corporate SNS pages. First, management should be aware of the critical impact of social interaction, which determines the degree to which users perceive that a site is useful and enjoyable. Management can enhance the quality of social interactions by creating a healthy environment that boosts lively communications among group members. To achieve this, management could monitor a small number of vicious users, such as outside advertisers or negative rumor spreaders, to maintain the quality of overall interactions. In addition, managers could run an event to stimulate group cohesion. Through participation, the relationships among members could be strengthened, generating friendly feelings and creating positive values. Furthermore, management could develop a friendly interface to make users feel more comfortable communicating.

Second, management should be aware of the important role played by other factors in enhancing perceived enjoyment and usefulness. Perceived enjoyment could be enhanced through the development of profound, enjoyable interactive content customized for group members. Enhanced interactivity can help to create a better user experience [52]. In terms of perceived usefulness, managers should consider all aspects of SNS platform quality, content quality, and service quality, monitoring their SNS platform status and consulting with SNS platform providers (e.g., Facebook and Twitter) to maintain a high quality system. Managers could try to develop creative and novel content by sourcing ideas in their SNS pages. Management should also focus on educating employees to provide faster, better services to users.

Third, management should aim to acquire enough corporate SNS page users, because perceived network size moderates the impact of social interaction quality on perceived enjoyment. To maintain and increase the number of users on corporate SNS pages, managers should always keep users very satisfied and encourage positive WOM recommendations. On the other hand, managers should think about changing the types of incentives they use. To ensure that incentives positively affect user continuance intentions, managers can adopt usage-based incentives such as attendance bonus points, which are known to have been effective in previous studies [6].

## 6.4. Limitations and Future Research

The results of this study should be interpreted within the context of certain limitations. First, the data was collected from a single country. To ensure the robustness of the model, replications of this study in other cultural contexts are needed. Second, although this study aims to collect data from various SNS platforms, most of the samples came from Facebook and Twitter. Thus, it would be also meaningful to conduct an empirical analysis using this model in other corporate SNS communities. Meanwhile, future research could employ the new theoretical framework proposed in this paper in similar contexts. Moreover, the new construct, social interaction quality, could be used in future studies. Additional studies on the role of perceived network size and non-usage

based incentives will also be necessary.
