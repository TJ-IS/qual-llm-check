## ABSTRACT

This study examines the value implications of web visibility and firm heterogeneity. We theorize and analyze the value creation process of firm web visibility by mapping it onto firm web performance and then business performance. With a comprehensive sample of 2840 US firms, we found that web visibility is a significant determinant of firm web traffic, and it improves both the short- and long-term performances of the firm. The results also reveal that web visibility creates higher values for business-to-consumer (B2C) than business-to-business (B2B) firms, for firms with a value appropriation focus than those with a value creation focus, and for firms of search goods than those of experience goods.

Keywords: Web visibility, Inlinks, Web traffic, Firm performance, Business-tobusiness, Business-to-consumer, Value creation, Value appropriation, Search goods, Experience goods

## 1. Introduction

A corporate website is an essential business element in today’s environment and has become a key channel for firm information dissemination and customer interaction. To stand out from the ever-increasing number of websites and the massive amount of information online, firms need to stay visible, which makes firm web visibility (WV) an important attribute. WV refers to the extent to which users are likely to come across a reference to a website in their web environment [12]. Business managers often believe that, similar to firm visibility on traditional media, firm WV drives business value [12, 43, 44].

In a limited industry setting, several studies have inquired into and confirmed the value implications of firm WV. The majority of these studies have focused on Internet firms, suggesting that WV is a key driver of web traffic (WT) [12, 13, 21, 44]. Wang and Vaughan [43] link firm WV to advertising efficiency and shareholder value by examining a selected sample of six industries. Although these studies provide evidence on the importance and business value of WV, several questions remain unanswered.

First, these studies are constrained to limited industries, particularly Internet companies. It is largely unknown whether business managers in other industries, particularly traditional industries, can apply the knowledge on WV derived from Internet companies. Given that firms of all kinds have websites, validating the value of WV for a broad range of firms is important because it will enable managers of various firms to use WV in strategic planning and management control. Second, and most important, previous studies have failed to inquire into firm heterogeneity in the value effect of WV. Firms of various types, e.g., business-to-business (B2B) and business-to-consumer (B2C) firms, have shown significant differences in their web strategy [7]. As such, WV could have different importance and value for firms of different types, but it is not clear which would benefit more from greater WV. Understanding such firm heterogeneity is an inextricable part of the knowledge on the value effects of WV. It will enable business managers to properly determine their attention to and use of WV in business planning, evaluation, and comparison. In addition, improving firm WV could be a cost- and labor-intensive process. Understanding the differential value of firm WV could help managers prioritize and gauge their web investments.

To address these issues, this study explores the value creation of WV and its associated firm heterogeneity by examining a comprehensive sample of 2840 US firms. Extending previous studies that separately examine the relationship between WV and WT [44] and that between WT and firm market value [11, 35], we propose and examine an integrated value creation chain from WV to WT and then to firm performance. In addition, we assess firm heterogeneity by examining whether and how firm business nature (i.e., B2B vs. B2C), strategic focus (i.e., value creation vs. value appropriation), and product nature (i.e., search vs. experience goods) affect the value of firm WV. Our baseline argument is that the importance of firm WV is determined by the extent to which firms rely on WV to acquire customers and generate business. To account for potential endogeneity of firm WV, we adopted a two-stage least squares (2SLS) approach.

Our empirical results provide support for the proposed integrated value creation chain of WV for a broad range of firms. Using the number of inlinks (i.e., links from other websites to a website) to measure firm WV, we found that WV is a significant determinant of firm WT and that WV-driven WT improves both the short- and longterm performances of the firm, as measured by asset efficiency (ASSEF) and Tobin’s q, respectively. More importantly, we found that WV creates higher values for B2C than B2B firms, for firms with a value appropriation focus than those with a value creation focus, and for firms of search goods than those of experience goods. Additional analysis based on a clustering approach confirms these results and further highlights the product nature (i.e., search goods vs. experience goods) as a more influential firm characteristic for the value heterogeneity of WV. These findings suggest that business managers need to comprehend multiple characteristics when developing web strategies and that, in general, firms of a B2C nature, firms with a value appropriation focus, and firms of search goods should stay more visible online to harvest business values from their websites.

We provide several theoretical and practical contributions. Theoretically, this study proposes an integrated value chain of WV to theorize its value creation process. We show business managers how WV affects WT and then how the enhanced WT by increased WV determines ASSEF and market value. This study generalizes the value

## 2. Literature and hypotheses

Companies have long considered firm or brand visibility as an important attribute [1, 5]. Visible brands/firms naturally draw customer attention, increase aided and unaided awareness, and thus are more likely to appear in customer decision choices [1]. Firm visibility can also help to reduce information asymmetry between managers and stakeholders, thus enhancing investor following and firm market value [5].

Compared with firm visibility in traditional media, WV is a relatively new concept that has quickly achieved high importance. As previously defined, WV refers to the extent to which users are likely to come across a reference to a company’s website in their web environment [12]. Previous research has shown that firm WV affects WT for Internet companies [12, 13, 44] and firm performance in several industries [43]. Considering these previous studies, we propose a firm WV value chain and use it as the conceptual framework for our analysis. ## 2.1. Web visibility to web traffic

Following the framework, we first investigated the relationship between WV and WT and the potential firm heterogeneity of this relationship. Firms create websites to serve potential and current customers for their information and transaction needs. For this reason, WT is a key indicator of firm web performance and success [19, 22], and high WT is valued. Users access a website through three ways: (1) browsing through inlinks, (2) searching keywords using search engines, and (3) going directly to a website by keying in a web address or clicking on bookmarks. The first two ways relate to WV. Inlinks are direct references to a website that reveal a website to potential visitors and bringing them to the site. A key variable in all search engine algorithms is the number of inlinks that directly affect the ranking of a website in search results [15]. Consequently, inlinks are an essential indicator of WV and are regarded equivalent to online word-of-mouth (WOM) referrals [13, 41]. The more the number of inlinks, the more visible is a website to visitors. In this study, we examine the value of WV in relation to inlinks.

Firm WV drives WT, and previous research has tested its effects for Internet companies. Drèze and Zufryden [12] reported that online visibility outperforms awareness and overall advertising spending in determining WT. Ennew et al. [13]

#

examined data from the top 500 companies in five e-tailing sectors (i.e., books, CDs, travel sites, retail brokerage, and retail banking) and found that inlink counts explain >60% of the variance of the cross-section of WT. From a strategic perspective, they argue that developing links is a cost-effective means of generating traffic. While analyzing online paid content providers, Wolk and Theysohn [44] found that WV indirectly affects both the number of visitors to websites and the number of page views per visitor.

Although these studies focus on Internet firms, their findings on the relationship between WV and WT could also be important to non-Internet firms. The web business has considerably developed and extended beyond the limited industry domains in the early Internet age and now affects a broad range of firms and customer groups [46]. Many traditional firms rely on the web as an important business platform and a major information source for their customers. Customers of traditional firms extensively use the web to search for, compare, and evaluate products. For example, surveys show that 81% of consumers search online before making large purchases in stores (http://www.retailingtoday.com/article/study-81- research-online-making-big-purchases). Similar to Internet firms, high WV can help attract customers of traditional firms to discover and rediscover firm websites during their online search process, leading to website traffic and business activities. To generalize the WV–WT relationship, we posit the following hypothesis:

## H1. Firm WV drives firm WT.

Our primary research question pertains to the potential firm heterogeneity in this relationship, which has not been examined in the extant literature. In driving WT,

##

WV is more important for companies whose customers extensively search for information and/or continuously shop around than for those whose customers conduct less search and/or stay in long-term business relationships. To reduce purchase uncertainty and maximize purchase utility, customers search information before purchasing to gain better understanding of the products they consider and make sound purchase decisions [4, 8, 29]. When customers extensively search for information or continuously shop around, they rely more on web references to find and access websites. In contrast, loyal customers show little interest in or are not sensitive to web references and usually do not shop around. We consider three business categorizations that are related to differential customer search and shopping behavior: (1) B2B versus B2C firms, (2) firms with a strategic focus on value creation versus those focusing on value appropriation, and (3) firms of search versus those of experience goods.

## 2.1.1. B2B versus B2C firms

B2B and B2C firms are different in many ways, leading to differential values of WV. Compared with B2C firms, B2B firms are associated with several unique characteristics [24], including (1) a limited number of business customers with large sales orders, (2) higher risks in business transactions, (3) higher switching costs for business customers, and (4) greater involvement and significant product and industry knowledge of business customers. Consequently, B2B firms build and rely more on sustained customer relationships and highly value customer trust and loyalty [23]. In contrast, consumers (i.e., B2C customers) enjoy lower switching costs, bear lower

#

transaction risks, have relatively limited product and industry knowledge, and tend to

shop around. They use the web as a critical information channel and engage more in

web browsing and search. As a result, customer browsing and search frequency is

lower for B2B than B2C firms. B2B firms use their websites more for customer

support than customer acquisitions. When interacting with a website, B2B customers

care more about informativeness than accessibility [7].

Moreover, compared with B2C firms, B2B firms are less dependent on the web for

business because they use alternative e-commerce platforms, including electronic

data interchange, B2B hubs, and e-procurement systems [2]. Historically, B2B firms

such as manufacturers are relatively slower in adopting the web in business practice

and promoting their WV than online retailers [33]. Conversely, B2C firms have been

quick to adopt the web as a critical channel to reach and acquire customers. Many

B2C firms use systematic methods to plan and prioritize online marketing

communication programs [23]. Therefore, web-based e-commerce activities are

more valuable for B2C than B2B firms [39]. With respect to website performance

and management, B2C firms value reach and visit frequency of their websites,

whereas these measures are often less important for B2B firms [23]. For B2C firms,

getting online browsers and (potential) customers to their sites is essential [35].

Because of the higher customer browsing and search frequency for B2C than B2B

firms, B2C firms may depend more on WV to generate WT, whereas a higher

portion of traffic to B2B websites may be generated by direct access. We propose the

following:

H1a. The effect of firm WV on WT is greater for B2C than B2B firms.

## 2.1.2. Firms focusing on value appropriation versus those focusing on value creation

Firms achieve sustainable competitive advantages through processes of value creation and value appropriation [14, 28]. In value creation, firms engage in innovative activities to create (new) value, thus increasing the potential magnitude of competitive advantage. In value appropriation, firms engage in marketing activities to deliver and harvest business values, thus increasing the persistence of competitive advantage. Although both processes are required for firms to achieve competitive advantages and profitability, with resource and capability constraints, firms need to prioritize and trade-off between the two. As a result, firms adopt different strategic focuses, with some focusing on value creation and others on value appropriation.

Heterogeneity in a firm’s strategic focus may lead to variation in customer information search behaviors, resulting in the differential values of WV. Value creation per se is an internally oriented process, of which the web as an interactive channel is of limited use. Firms focusing on value creation typically prioritize innovation activities and constantly innovate when competition erodes profit [28]. Targeting to develop better products/services for specific groups of customers, these firms often focus more on serving core customers through their websites and avoid mass marketing [28].

Conversely, value appropriation is an externally oriented process and is largely performed by interacting with customers and competitors, for which the web is of greater help. Firms focusing on value appropriation try to maximize the value extraction from the market mainly by lengthening the life of competitive advantage or reducing value appropriation opportunities that their competitors may have [28].

#

To this end, they engage in various market-oriented activities to acquire and retain customers and create barriers to prevent competitors from entering the market [28]. For example, they continuously communicate with customers, expand markets beyond initial customer groups, maximize market shares, and encourage brand switching. The web provides a cost-efficient platform to conduct these activities [42]. For firms with a value appropriation focus, WV is critical because it helps to increase firm accessibility and enhance customer awareness. Thus, this type of firm may benefit more from WV to ensure web success. We propose the following:

H1b. The effect of firm WV on WT is greater for firms focusing on value appropriation than for those focusing on value creation.

## 2.1.3. Firms of search goods versus firms of experience goods

Customers bear purchase risk or uncertainty [4] and use prepurchase information search as a risk-reduction strategy [8]. Products can be categorized into search and experience goods [30, 31] according to consumers’ ability to obtain product quality information before purchase. Search goods are dominated by product attributes for which information can be acquired before purchase (e.g., clothing and footwear). Experience goods are dominated by attributes for which quality information cannot be acquired until purchase and use of the product, or information search is costlier and/or more difficult than direct product experience (e.g., cigarettes and drugs). For example, customers can try on a new pair of shoes and understand the quality and fit before purchase, and thus, footwear is a search good; conversely, customers cannot fully know whether they will experience side effects of a drug until they purchase and take the drug, making drugs an experience good.

#

The economics of information search suggests that customers search for information until the marginal search cost equals the marginal benefits [37, 29]. According to this theory, customers continue to search before purchase when they perceive higher search benefit than search cost. Because more product information is available for search goods than for experience goods to enable product evaluation and comparison [30, 31], given the same amount of additional resource put into the search process (e.g., an additional hour), customers can generally obtain a larger amount of information on product quality for search goods than for experience goods. In addition, the intrinsic attributes of search goods are easy to access, concrete, and more objective [47], suggesting lower uncertainty in evaluation and comparison. On the contrary, consumers rely more on extrinsic cues to judge product quality of experience goods [47], suggesting higher uncertainty in evaluation and comparison. Therefore, prepurchase information search generally generates higher information utility for search goods than for experience goods. Rational customers will undertake more search for search goods to discover product information before purchase but rely more on postpurchase experiences for experience goods. Differences in customer search behaviors between search and experience goods can result in profound business differences such as differences in market structure [30] and advertising content/message [25, 31].

The difference in customer information search between the two product categories

can directly affect the importance of WV in business. Huang et al. [20] reported that

search goods involve higher breadth (i.e., total number of pages) but lower depth

(time per page) of online search than experience goods. Because customers search more broadly online before purchasing search goods, WV should be more important for firms of this category. We therefore propose the following:

H1c. The effect of firm WV on WT is greater for firms of search goods than for those of experience goods.

Experience goods can be further classified to nondurable experience goods (e.g., cigarettes and cleaners), durable experience goods (e.g., appliances and drugs), experience services (e.g., vacations and banking), and credence services (e.g., investments and insurance) [10, 32]. The categorization of durable and nondurable experience goods entails the differences in purchase frequency, value of WOM, and experiences [10, 32]. Credence services feature attributes that consumers cannot verify even after use/experience [10, 32]. These subcategories may also relate to differences in consumer search behavior, thus affecting the importance of WV. We consider this as an empirical issue and explore it in our empirical analysis.

## 2.2. Web visibility-driven web traffic to firm performance

Firm WV can drive WT, which then drives firm performance. Improving firm performance is the ultimate goal of business managers. Therefore, understanding the effect of WV on firm performance is critical. Previous research treats the relationship of firm WV to WT and that of WT to firm performance as two separate processes [18, 34, 44]. Because WT can be driven by factors other than WV, the identified mapping of WT onto firm performance by previous work does not fully explain the contribution of WV. Thus, the value chain from WV to WT and then to firm performance cannot be automatically inferred from previous work without testing. We focus on the value creation of WV and explore how WV affects firm performance through increased WT. To this end, we propose and test an integrated

value chain that links the two processes, that is, the chain from firm WV to WT and

then to short- and long-term performances of the firm.

The effect of WV on the short-term performance of the firm is evaluated through

firm ASSEF (i.e., the ratio of sales to total assets). WT can increase sales by

converting visitors into customers. WV can bring more visitors to the web.

Therefore, WV can contribute to increase in sales through increased WT. In addition,

website inlinks serve as a WOM channel [13, 41]. WOM referrals reduce perceived

purchase risks, enhance information targets, and help attract the “right” customers

(i.e., customers with high lifetime value) [41], thus increasing the customer

conversion rate. Furthermore, most inlinks are voluntarily created by reviewers,

presenting no costs to firms. Therefore, given unchanged costs, and all other things

being equal, increased sales caused by WV-driven WT should improve the efficiency

of assets in generating sales. We therefore propose the following:

H2. WV-driven WT improves firm ASSEF.

In addition, WV should contribute to the long-term performance of the firm that

cannot be captured by short-term performance measures. First, customers acquired

through inlinks may provide high long-term value to firms. For example, Villanueva

et al. [41] found that WOM customers acquired through inlinks and other WOM

channels add nearly twice as much long-term value to firms as marketing-induced

customers. Second, similar to media visibility [5], WV may enhance investor

following as investors may access a website through inlinks to gather the information

they need. Although investors’ WT does not contribute to short-term sales increase, it can lead to an increase in firm market value through the attention to the firm’s stock. Da et al. [9] found a positive association between investors’ attention and stock return. We therefore propose the following:

H3. WV-driven WT is positively associated with firm market value.

## 3. Methodology

## 3.1. Sample and data

We collected firm website and financial data for the period 2012–2013 from Alexa Internet and COMPUSTAT. Alexa, a subsidiary of Amazon.com, is a web information company that provides commercial web information and analytics. Alexa collects web information through its toolbars for browsers such as Internet Explorer, Firefox, and Google Chrome and by crawling publicly available websites (http://www.alexa.com/company/technology). Previous research has used Alexa data to examine inlinks and WT [13, 44]. Compiling data from the two data sources generated a sample of 2840 US firms.

## 3.2. Variables of interest and data collection

## 3.2.1. Web visibility

Building on previous work [36, 44], we capture firm WV by natural logs of inlink counts. Inlinks are direct references to a website [43] and also a key variable in all search engine algorithms, and they directly affect the ranking of a website in search results [15]. Inlink counts are an aggregate WV measure that does not directly count in the significance of inlink sources (i.e., who provides the link). However, firms with more inlink counts usually have inlinks from important websites, whereas firms with fewer inlinks are less likely to be linked by important websites. Thus, inlink source is not a critical concern in using inlink counts to measure firm WV. We collected inlink counts from Alexa.

## 3.2.2. Web traffic

Research has measured WT in several ways, including the number of unique visitors in a given period, number of total website visits, and page ranking based on the total number of website visits [13, 22, 44]. We collect WT ranking data from Alexa and use natural logs of WT rankings to measure WT. Alexa’s WT ranking is based on a combined measure of unique visitors and page views per visitor (https://support.alexa.com/hc/en-us/articles/200449744-How-are-Alexa-s-trafficrankings-determined), the two essential components of WT [44]; thus, Alexa’s WT ranking is preferable and widely adopted in the extant literature to measure WT [17, 45]. Research on WT measures suggests that Alexa’s WT ranking outperforms other measures such as those from Google Trends and Compete [40]. WT rankings are expressed in an inverse manner, meaning that a high value in the measure indicates low WT.

## 3.2.3. Dummies of firm characteristics (FirmDum)

FirmDum is a group of dummies of firm characteristics. First, we classified firms into two types: B2B and B2C. Following convention, the B2B firms include manufacturing companies and wholesale firms with two-digit SIC codes in the ranges of 20–39 and 50–51. The B2C firms include all service and retail firms with two-digit SIC codes in the ranges of 52–59 and 70–89. We used two dummies (i.e.,

B2B and B2C) to capture the two types of firms, assigning the value of 1 if a firm is of the designated type and 0 otherwise.

Our second firm classification distinguished firms focusing on value creation from those focusing on value appropriation. We used R&D intensity to capture the value creation focus and advertising intensity to capture the value appropriation focus. To cope with the industrial impact on the level of R&D and advertising intensities, we used industry-adjusted R&D and advertising intensities (i.e., the difference between a firm’s value and the industry average) in analysis. The median of the industryadjusted measure serves to separate firms into a high versus low category. For example, we treated firms with industry-adjusted R&D intensity equal to or higher than the median as high R&D. We created three groups: firms focusing on value appropriation [low R&D and high advertising intensity (RDHADL)], firms focusing on value creation [high R&D and low advertising intensity (RDLADH)], and firms with no specific focus. We generated two dummy variables accordingly: RDLADH took the value of 1 for firms focusing on value appropriation and 0 otherwise; RDHADL took the value of 1 for firms focusing on value creation and 0 otherwise. Third, following previous research [10, 30, 32], we classified firms into five groups according to the nature of goods and services they sell: search goods (e.g., clothing and footwear), nondurable experience goods (e.g., cigarettes and cleaners), durable experience goods (e.g., appliances and drugs), experience services (e.g., vacations and banking), and credence services (e.g., investments and insurance). We used five dummy variables: Search took the value of 1 for firms of search goods among experience goods and services, Nonduexpg took the value of 1 for firms of nondurable experience goods and 0 otherwise, Duexpg took the value of 1 for firms of durable experience goods and 0 otherwise, Expser took the value of 1 for firms of experience services and 0 otherwise, and Expsercre took the value of 1 for firms of credence services and 0 otherwise.

## 3.4. 2SLS approach

Although WV can drive WT, web visitors may create inlinks to a website after their visits, thus increasing WV. Therefore, firm WV could be potentially endogenous. To account for this potential endogeneity of firm WV, we adopted a 2SLS approach [16, 27], which previous research has used when endogeneity is a concern [3, 38]. Alternatively, a fixed effect model can be used to deal with the potential

#

endogeneity. A fixed effect model holds the assumption that the effect of the omitted variable on both the independent and dependent variables is time invariant. Because this assumption may not hold true in our sample, we adopted the 2SLS approach as it does not require this assumption.

We used financial and web data collected for 2013 to perform cross-sectional regression analyses. We used the natural log of lagged firm inlink count (WV<sub>−1</sub>) (i.e., inlink count of 2012) as the instrumental variable. The lagged inlink count may affect the current-period inlink count but not the current-period WT. The 2SLS approach for Eq. 1 is as follows:

The analysis of firm heterogeneity of the impact of WV on WT in Section 4.1 is based on individual consideration of the three firm characteristics. To further understand heterogeneity in terms of firms’ similarity to/distance from one another, we used a clustering approach<sup>1</sup> to consider clusters of firms based on combinations of any two of the three firm characteristics. We did not combine three firm characteristics because doing so results in some clusters of small numbers of observations, which may affect the validity of the analysis. We report the results from the clustering approach in Table 7.

In addition, within B2C firms, the degree of the importance of WV specifically depends on their strategic focus. Similarly, within firms of similar strategic focus, business nature (i.e., B2B vs. B2C) can make a difference.

To obtain sufficient observations for each cluster, we merged firms of four types of experience goods into one group (i.e., Exp). This result is consistent with our previous analysis on individual firm characteristics and is in support of H1a and H1c, i.e., the effect of WV on WT is greater for B2C than B2B firms and for firms of search goods than for those of experience goods. In addition, the effect of WV on WT is greater for the two search goods clusters (i.e., B2C–Search and B2B–Search) than the two experience goods clusters (i.e., B2C–Exp and B2B–Exp), indicating the stronger influence of the product nature than the business nature.

Overall, the results confirm our analysis in Section 4.1, thus supporting H1–H1c, that is, the effect of firm WV on WT is greater for B2C than for B2B firms, for firms focusing on value appropriation than for those focusing on value creation, and for firms of search goods than for those of experience goods. In addition, the results shed light on the relative importance of the three firm characteristics, highlighting that product nature (i.e., search vs. experience goods clarification) is a more influential firm characteristic in differentiating the value of WV.

## 4.3. Robustness check

We performed various robustness checks to verify our findings. To verify our results of testing H1b, in which we defined high versus low R&D or advertising intensity categories on the basis of sample medians, we redefined high versus low R&D or advertising intensity by classifying firms into three categories of equal sizes (i.e., high, medium, and low R&D or advertising intensity). We treated only the top onethird R&D or advertising intensity values as high and the bottom one-third as low and retested H1b. In addition, we performed robustness checks using alternative measures in place of our variables of choice. For example, we used total assets instead of sales to measure firm size and used market returns instead of Tobin’s q to measure firm market performance in robustness tests. Overall inferences from these analyses were similar.

## 5. Discussion and conclusion

The web has become increasingly influential on business and thus has inspired a growing body of research to examine its value implications for firms and customers [26, 42]. In this study, we focused on the value creation of WV and its firm heterogeneity. We proposed an integrated value chain to capture the value of WV. We found that firm WV drives WT and that the increased WT enhances ASSEF and manifests firm market value. Most importantly, we are the first to consider firm heterogeneity of the value of WV. We identified that business nature, strategic focus, and product nature can differentiate the role of WV in creating value. Our results show that WV is more valuable for B2C firms, firms with a focus on value appropriation, and firms of search goods.

## 5.1. Theoretical implications

This research provides several theoretical contributions. First, we proposed and tested an integrated value chain for firm WV, consolidating and enriching our knowledge on the value creation process of WV. Previous studies [12, 13, 21, 43, 44] have focused on individual links of this process, offering partial views on the topic. The proposed value chain of firm WV offers a comprehensive view, thus serving as a well-grounded basis for further research.

Second, we generalized the research and conclusion on the value of firm WV from Internet companies to a broad range of firms. Compared with previous studies that have exclusively focused on Internet firms [12, 13, 21, 44], we examined a comprehensive sample of firms. The results suggest that firm WV should be considered and used as an important business measure for all types of firms. Non-Internet firms account for a large portion of the economy and have widely adopted websites to facilitate business. Generalization to this sector not only allows us to enlarge the body of knowledge but also provides us an opportunity to examine firm heterogeneity more broadly.

More importantly, we are the first to show that the value impact of WV is not crossfirm constant but differential. The priority of firm WV in business strategic planning and evaluation depends on business nature, strategic focus, and product nature such that WV creates higher values for B2C than B2B firms, for firms with a value appropriation focus than for those with a value creation focus, and for firms of search goods than for those of experience goods. Our heterogeneity analysis provides in-depth understanding on the value effects of WV, consisting of an inextricable part of our knowledge.

## 5.2. Managerial implications

Our findings are also practically insightful. We call for management attention from a wide range of firms to the measure of firm WV. With the wide adoption and significant business role of the web in daily business life, the importance of firm WV is no longer restricted to Internet firms. WV needs to be viewed as an essential feature for firms of all types. The value chain of WV can help guide management’s understanding and evaluation of firm WV in business success, i.e., the value of WV can be evaluated and tracked by WT and business performance. Management should continuously monitor and invest in WV to improve firm performance and incorporate it into business planning and performance predictions.

Because improving WV can be a cost- and labour-intensive process, corporate managers care about the efficiency and effectiveness of investments in WV. Our findings on the heterogeneity of the value of firm WV suggest that the return to investments in WV is not the same but differential across firms. Managers of B2C firms, firms with a value appropriation, and firms of search goods should give greater value to WV when allocating resources and conducting web strategies and management control. Furthermore, our results from the clustering analysis suggest that web strategy and management should be decided by jointly considering multiple firm characteristics.

## 5.3. Limitations and further research

Our study invites future investigations in several directions. The results from our empirical explorations, as reported in model 5 in Table 5, indicate the order of the four experience goods subcategories in terms of the economic importance of WV as (from high to low) durable experience goods, experience services, credence services, and nondurable experience goods. The most important role of WV for durable goods than nondurable goods and for general services than credence services is relatively easy to comprehend, whereas the ranking among experience goods and services subcategories is not straightforward. For example, why is the effect of WV on WT greater for durable experience goods than for experience services? More theoretical discussions and empirical testing are necessary to improve overall understanding and validate this comparison.

In evaluating the WV–WT relationship, we controlled for firm advertising intensity, R&D intensity, and firm size. Previous studies have also considered other factors, such as site features and price features [44], which we did not include because of data constraints in analyzing a large sample of a broad range of companies. Further research could address this limitation. In addition, the investigation of the value heterogeneity of WV can be extended. As the first study on this track, we focus on three types of firm characteristics (i.e., business nature, strategic focus, and product nature) in exploring firm heterogeneity. Future studies could be more detailed in examining additional firm characteristics.

##

Our findings highlight the importance of firm WV to business success. Building on this finding and the extant literature on IT-enabled business success, future research could examine the role of firm WV and performance in enabling and enhancing specific business functions such as innovation and customer acquisition/retention. Despite the importance of firm website to business success, little is known on the underlying effect of firm web performance on individual business functions. For example, does firm web performance, such as visibility and traffic, affect firm innovation and marketing success? Which business function is more susceptible to firm web performance? These understandings could help management to adapt firm web strategy and leverage business value.
