# Look to the Future: Enhancing Online Gamer Loyalty from the Perspective of the Theory of Consumption Values

## Abstract

Most online games are commercial systems that attempt to motivate gamers to continue playing by enticing them to progress from one game level to the next. This indicates that the expectancy for character growth (i.e., the anticipation of an increase in game level) may enhance loyalty. However, no research has yet examined the antecedents of expectancy for character growth. Hence, this study adopts the theory of consumption values to examine the relationship among consumption values, expectancy for character growth, and online gamer loyalty. Responses from 1,440 online gamers were analyzed using the structural equation modeling method. Learning, novelty, enjoyment, and social value were positively related to expectancy for character growth, and thus loyalty. This study is the first examining the relation among consumption values, expectancy for character growth, and online gamer loyalty. It contributes novel insights enabling electronic commerce managers to make more targeted decisions on resource allocation to effectively enhance gamers’ expectations and loyalty, thus creating promising revenues and competitive advantage.

Keywords: Online game; loyalty; survey; structural equation modeling; expectancy for character growth; consumption value.

## 1. Introduction

Online games are popular electronic commerce (e-commerce) applications that attracted more than 2.2 billion gamers worldwide in 2017 (UKie, 2017), indicating their importance in e-commerce contexts (Jin, Sun, Wang, & Zhang, 2017; Kaptein, Parvinen, & Pöyry, 2015). Moreover, online games are valid contexts for examining issues around interactive hedonic technologies (Lin & Bhattacherjee, 2010), supporting their relevance to broader communities (e.g., IS scholars).

Online games employ various business models to generate revenue, including advertising within games (Vashisht & Sreejesh, 2017), selling gaming items (i.e., freemium games) (Hamari, 2015; Hamari, Hanner, & Koivisto, 2017; Shi, Xia, & Huang, 2015), and advertising that accompanies gaming competition broadcasts (e.g., eSport) (Hamari & Sjöblom, 2017). This promising market has attracted numerous game providers. For instance, the United Kingdom had more than 2,100 game providers by August 2017 (UKie, 2017). Numerous firms compete to enlarge their market share, making online gamer loyalty (i.e., the intention to repeatedly play a game) of vital importance to e-commerce (Bhattacherjee, 2001a; Yoo, Sanders, & Moon, 2013), and indicating the relevance of research into such loyalty.

Online gamer loyalty has numerous antecedents, including escapism, visual value (Merhi, 2016), ease of use, usefulness, enjoyment (Hamari & Keronen, 2017), service quality (Hamari, Hanner, & Koivisto, 2017), the ability to develop online social identities (Moon, Hossain, Sanders, Garrity, & Jo, 2013), and participation in gaming communities (Teng, 2017b). All these elements provide value to gamers. But as gamers repeatedly play a game, some of these elements may lose some of their novelty value, perhaps reducing gamer satisfaction. One plausible solution to loss of novelty is to help gamers achieve higher game levels, thus exposing them to new stimuli. Anticipation of an increase in game level (or expectancy for character growth) has been identified as a predictor of online gamer loyalty (Liao & Teng, 2017), indicating its importance. Moreover, an increase in game level is significantly related to in-game consumption (Kim, Yoo, & Kauffman, 2013), indicating the importance of consumption values and the anticipation of increased game level, or expectancy for character growth. However, no study has yet examined the mediator role of expectancy for character growth in the impacts of consumption values on online gamer loyalty, indicating a research gap.

Research filling this gap is important both practically and academically. First, it can pioneer an exploration of the mediator role of expectancy for character growth, thus demonstrating its usefulness in the e-commerce area, indicating its academic contributions. Second, it helps e-commerce managers make decisions on the allocation of resources for enhancing the influential values, thus effectively fueling gamers’ expectancy for character growth and loyalty, indicating its practical importance.

To identify an adequate theory for addressing the gap, this study began with the concept of loyalty, which is the tendency to use a product or service in the future (Chen, Huang, & Davison, 2017; Huang, Chen, Ou, Davison, & Hua, 2017). The decision to use a product or service (e.g., an app) likely originates from user-perceived values (Wottrich, van Reijmersdal, & Smit, 2018). The values perceived during consumption have been extensively explained by the theory of consumption values (Sheth, Newman, & Gross, 1991), justifying the inclusion of this theory.

Hence, the purpose of this study is to examine how consumption values influence expectancy for character growth and online gamer loyalty. This study is unique in using the theory of consumption values to examine how values impact gamers’ loyalty, rather than purchases, as the theory is employed in other contexts. Contributing to the theory, this study examined whether expectancy for character growth can be an alternative pathway by which values motivate positive responses.

This study makes several contributions to the relevant literature. First, Hamari and Keronen (2017) meta-analyzed the antecedents of the intention to play online games and placed an emphasis on the role of gamer perceptions of currently fulfilled enjoyment as a determinant. The present study followed theirs in examining the role of perceptions of enjoyment (as one consumption value) in determining online game play, but is new in incorporating gamer expectancy for character growth, which refers to the anticipated, but currently unfulfilled, enjoyment of an increase in game level (i.e., anticipation). Such incorporation provides an alternative means to retain gamers who do not obtain sufficient enjoyment, i.e., instilling in them the expectancy for character growth, and contributes to the literature by breaking a new path for future studies, i.e., including perceptions of the future, in addition to the perceptions of current game play.

Second, Merhi (2016) examined how escapism and visual value could enhance gamers’ intention to play online games. Consistent with Merhi (2016), this study examined escapism and visual value (as two consumption values) and their impacts on gamers’ intention to (repeatedly) play online games, but is new in introducing expectancy for character growth to explain such impacts. The introduction of a novel construct (i.e., expectancy for character

growth) further supports the usefulness of escapism and visual value while contributing new mechanisms to deepen our understanding of such impacts.

Third, Moon et al. (2013) identified two novel antecedents of online gamer loyalty, i.e., psychological ownership of the gaming character (or avatar) and social identity in a guild, showing the importance of avatars and social aspects in determining online gamer loyalty. The present study also examined the determinants of online gamer loyalty but is new in examining five consumption values and their (indirect) impacts on online gamer loyalty. The present study provides a platform on which future studies can build to explore novel determinants of online gamer loyalty.

Figure 1 presents the research framework. All hypotheses on the links between consumption values and expectancy for character growth (i.e., H1 to H7) are new to the literature. Although H8 can be explained by Liao and Teng (2017), it is necessary for building the process leading to online gamer loyalty, motivating the present study to retain it. It may be noted that these values likely directly impact loyalty. The strong impacts of consumption values are well documented (Sheth et al., 1991), justifying the inclusion of their direct impacts on loyalty. For the sake of conciseness and clarity, the present study chose not to develop hypotheses on these direct impacts. Moreover, Liao and Teng (2017) found that the impact of expectancy for character growth on online gamer loyalty would be mediated by skill, challenge, and flow. That is, these mediators and their interrelationships are already known. Therefore, to retain a clear focus, the present study excluded these mediators, while including the impact of expectancy for character growth on loyalty. This impact should represent the overall impact of expectancy for character growth on loyalty.

## 2. Theoretical Background

## 2.1 Online Gamer Loyalty

Loyalty has been a popular focus of e-commerce literature. Specifically, it has been found to originate from trust (Zheng, Lee, & Cheung, 2017) and social capital (Chen et al., 2017; Huang, Chen et al., 2017). This research into loyalty indicates its relevance to e-commerce managers.

In online gaming contexts, loyalty has been defined as the intention to play games in the future (Chang, Liu, & Chen, 2014; Su, Chiang, Lee, & Chang, 2016). Online gamer loyalty has various socially related sources, e.g., critical mass, subjective norms (Chang et al., 2014; Hamari & Keronen, 2017), team participation, social need satisfaction (Teng & Chen, 2014), and social gratification (Li, Liu, Xu, Heikkilä, & van der Heijden, 2015). Such literature motivated the present study to include community factors. Among these, community engagement is important in determining in-game spending (Kaptein et al., 2015), justifying the use of commitment to the virtual community.

Moreover, loyalty among online gamers has other sources, including usefulness, ease of use (Hamari & Keronen, 2017), challenge, control (Huang & Hsieh, 2011), flow (Hamari & Keronen, 2017; Su et al., 2016), visual value, escapism (Merhi, 2016), enjoyment (Hamari & Keronen, 2017; Huang & Hsieh, 2011; Merhi, 2016), and hedonic and utilitarian values (Li et al., 2015). Those sources are important and well-known. Therefore, the present study did not replicate all of them.

## 2.2 Expectancy for Character Growth

Expectancy for character growth has been defined as a gamer’s anticipation of an increase in game level (Liao & Teng, 2017). It is positively related to perceived skill and perceived challenge, subsequently inducing flow and online gamer loyalty (Liao & Teng, 2017). The present study is new in adopting the theory of consumption values in examining how various consumption values positively contribute to expectancy for character growth, providing novel antecedents.

The relevant literature has examined several terms that are similar to but distinct from expectancy for character growth. First, expectancy disconfirmation indicates the discrepancy between gamers’ expectations and their actual experience of the game. Such a discrepancy may enhance online gamer loyalty (Liao, Huang, & Teng, 2016). As expectancy disconfirmation and expectancy for character growth both have the word “expectancy”, they look similar. However, expectancy disconfirmation has a focus on the discrepancy between expectation and actual experience, which is distinctive to expectancy for character growth. On the other hand, expectancy for growth has a focus on an anticipation of game level increase, which is distinctive to expectancy disconfirmation, indicating their conceptual differences.

Second, utilitarian and hedonic outcome expectations have been identified as antecedents to online gamer loyalty (Chang et al., 2014). Utilitarian outcome expectations may overlap

with expectancy for character growth in containing a sense of achievement. However, utilitarian outcome expectations have a unique focus on getting rewards and social status, while expectancy for character growth has a unique focus on game levels, showing the difference between these two constructs and warranting further research on expectancy for character growth.

## 2.3 Theory of Consumption Values

The theory of consumption values (Sheth et al., 1991) posits that consumers form positive attitudes and make purchase decisions according to evaluations of multiple value components (i.e., functional, social, emotional, epistemic, and conditional values). These values are possible factors that motivate consumers’ purchase in the future, or enhance their intention to use or purchase a product or service (Yang & Lin, 2017), which has been defined as loyalty (Chang et al., 2014; Chen et al., 2017). Therefore, this theory should be relevant to a study of loyalty.

This theory has also been used to explain how consumption values contribute to users adoption of online services (Omigie, Zo, & Rho, 2017), adoption of location-based services (Yang & Lin, 2017), and perceived use frequency of social networking sites (Chen & Sharma, 2013). Therefore, this theory is applicable to user adoption issues in various contexts, such as online gamer loyalty herein.

This theory has been frequently used to explain hedonic issues in online contexts, e.g., acceptance of hedonic digital artifacts (Turel, Serenko, & Bontis, 2010) and intention to purchase virtual items in online games (Mäntymäki & Salo, 2015; Park & Lee, 2011). Therefore, this theory should be applicable to online gaming issues. However, the aforementioned literature has a boundary, i.e., focusing on purchases of digital items. Such purchases in online games have a critical antecedent, i.e., continuance intention (or loyalty herein) (Hamari, 2015), justifying the use of this theory in explaining loyalty issues in online games.

The value components might not only impact users’ purchasing behavior, but could also trigger various other positive user behaviors, e.g., intending to express positive

word-of-mouth (Turel et al., 2010) and engaging in playing games (Cheung, Shen, Lee, & Chan, 2015). Exploration of more positive behaviors (as in the present study) could go beyond the aforementioned boundary of this theory. Specifically, playing games could lead to concentration, participation in teams, and exploration of gaming content. Such consequences bolster gamers’ anticipation of obtaining future achievements that is at the core of expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017), building the theoretical linkage between consumption values and expectancy for character growth. Therefore, expectancy for character growth should be an important mediator in the link between consumption values and loyalty.

The theory of consumption values also shows that the relative importance of value components likely varies from context to context, thus indicating the relevance of its application to specific platforms (Sheth et al., 1991) and motivating the present study to

Based on the pertinent literature, this study contextualized the values into specific value elements in online games. Specifically, functional value typically refers to performance, and also includes physical attributes (Sheth et al., 1991). In online games, physical attributes can be visible and audible. Hence, the present study contextualized function value as audio-visual value. Social value is highly applicable to online games as social aspects are highly influential on gamers (Hamari & Keronen, 2017), e.g., encouraging gamers to buy virtual items (Mäntymäki & Salo, 2015), motivating the present study to retain the use of social value. Emotional value indicates the perceived utility in improving feelings (Sheth et al., 1991). Moreover, enjoyment is a key element of online games (Hamari, 2015; Merhi, 2016), and is typically accompanied by positive emotions, justifying the adoption of enjoyment to represent emotional value. Epistemic value has been defined as fulfilling the desire for learning (Chen & Sharma, 2013) and novelty (Yang & Lin, 2017), justifying their adoption herein. Moreover, epistemic value is the utility associated with curiosity (Sheth et al., 1991). Curiosity has been shown to be an important reason for playing online games (Hamari & Keronen, 2017). Curiosity can attract gamers to focus on gaming worlds, thus escaping real life, i.e., experiencing escapism (Merhi, 2016; Yee, 2006), motivating the present study to use

escapism.

As not all value components are highly influential in online games, this study discusses and justifies some exclusions. Conditional value refers to the value pertaining to physical and social contingencies. This value has been excluded in the literature. The reason is that when using social networking services, users are not restricted by physical surroundings, reducing the relevance of conditional value. Hence, we excluded conditional value. Instead, we used value for money because some online games require gamers to pay for play or offer the option of paying for in-game items, i.e., freemium games (Hamari, Hanner, & Koivisto, 2017). The play and/or items purchased by such payments may be perceived as valuable by gamers, i.e., good value for money. Good value for money refers to economic incentives that could predict e-loyalty (Yoo et al., 2013), thus should be important to e-commerce managers, and thus the present study includes value for money to explore its impact.

## 3. Development of Hypotheses

The theory on consumption values has been contextualized to identify learning as one important value component (Chen & Sharma, 2013). Learning can help improve users attitudes to and use of social networking sites (Chen & Sharma, 2013), indicating that learning is influential in triggering positive user responses. Learning satisfies users’ desire for acquiring new knowledge. That is, users who experience intensified learning should perceive that they are acquiring extensive knowledge. Skills and knowledge instill in gamers a confidence in controlling their gaming avatars (Moon et al., 2013), and should also enhance their anticipation of an increase in game level, i.e., expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, it was hypothesized:

## H1: Learning is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Novelty has been identified as an important value component by the theory of consumption values, as it strengthens the intention to use location-based services (Yang & Lin, 2017). The reason may be that novelty induces users to explore more. Active exploration may satisfy users’ need for knowing more, i.e., providing an important value to users (Sheth et al., 1991). In online gaming contexts, exploration of the gaming worlds provides gamers with

advanced knowledge. Such knowledge assists them in upgrading their avatars, or instills in them an expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, it was hypothesized: H2: Novelty is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Escapism is the feeling of temporarily being out of the real world (Merhi, 2016), and is important for creating a sense of virtual community (Keng, Ting, & Chen, 2011). Moreover, it is an essential element of immersion, one of the major motivations for online gaming behavior (Yee, 2006). By feeling transported out of the real world, gamers perceive that they are in the gaming world, i.e., experience telepresence, which creates flow (Huang, Huang, Chou, & Teng, 2017). Flow absorbs gamers’ total concentration (Liao & Teng, 2017), which should lead them to accomplish gaming tasks, moving them to the next game level, or instilling a perception that they can actively upgrade game levels, i.e., boosting their expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Moreover, gamers’ total concentration could also increase their opportunities to obtain future achievements, which is central to expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, it was hypothesized:

## H3: Escapism is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

A feeling of enjoyment generates positive feedback, which in turn encourages individuals to repeatedly engage in the associated activity. Enjoyment has demonstrated its influence in e-commerce, because i strongly encourages user adoption of digital services (Merhi, 2015) and online games (Hamari & Keronen, 2017; Lin & Bhattacherjee, 2010). Online gamers who perceive strong enjoyment likely tend to concentrate more on gaming activities. Concentration in gaming helps accomplish gaming tasks, and such gaming accomplishments could help move gamers toward the next game level and help fuel their anticipation of an increase in game level, i.e., instilling expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Moreover, concentrating on gaming also instills in gamers’ confidence in obtaining future achievements, which is core to expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, the present study hypothesized:

## H4: Enjoyment is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Social interactions can formulate a positive attitude toward a game (Hamari & Koivisto,

2015), influence gamers’ intention to play (Merhi, 2016), and thus help retain gamers (Fu, Chen, Shi, Bose, & Cai, 2017). Moreover, social interaction ties motivate users to share knowledge (Chiu, Hsu, & Wang, 2006), demonstrating the strong influence of social interactions. Social influence can determine platform adoption decisions (Song, Baker, Wang, Choi, & Bhattacherjee, 2018). Furthermore, social influence likely comes from the number of friends a gamer has (who interact in social activities in online games), which can predict in-game spending (Shi et al., 2015), further supporting the importance of the value of social aspects to online gamers.

The theory of consumption values defines social value as the social gains attributed to the use of a product or service (Sheth et al., 1991). A platform that can help users engage in social activities should be regarded as providing high social value. Online games are platforms through which virtual communities can provide enhanced social value to gamers, as evidenced by the positive link between team participation and social need satisfaction (Teng & Chen, 2014). Social values may fulfill gamers’ needs, including the need for teamwork (Yee, 2006). Teamwork can effectively help gamers overcome gaming challenges (Teng & Chen, 2014), building their confidence in obtaining future achievements, because gamers can learn gaming skills and acquire knowledge from fellow team members. Such skills and knowledge raise gamers’ anticipation of an increase in game level, defined as expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, the present study hypothesized:

## H5: Social value is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Among the features of information systems, image, animation, and sound (audio) are important design elements (Morana, Schacht, Scherp, & Maedche, 2017) that create audio-visual value. Audio-visual value refers to the gaming elements that provide gratifying audio-visual experiences. Specifically, audio-visual effects enhance gamers’ perception of gaming achievements, i.e., amplifying the positive feedback associated with gaming achievements, and thus strengthening the gamers’ confidence in obtaining future achievements. Anticipation of future achievements is at the core of expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017), building the link between audio-visual value and expectancy fo character growth. Moreover, visualization can be important in enhancing reuse intention (Scholz, Franz, & Hinz, 2017). Visual value can encourage online game play (Merhi, 2016), and thus should provide value to online gamers. The theory of consumption values indicates that value perceived during consumption leads to positive attitudes and behavior (Sheth et al., 1991). In online games, audio-visual value should create positive attitudes toward a game due to its sensory stimuli. Sensory stimuli amplify the perceptual significance of gaming accomplishments, enhancing the perception of moving toward the next level and forming anticipation of an increase in game level, i.e., fostering expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Hence, it was hypothesized:

## H6: Audio-visual value is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Some online games are free and a free game can be seen as offering gamers the ultimate value for money, which is the degree to which a transaction brings the user benefits that are greater than the cost. Value for money is an important element of free games, freemium games, and pay-for-play games. To generate revenue, freemium games typically sell virtual items. Good value for money is important for triggering purchases (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001) and should also be important for purchasing gaming assets. Specifically, good value for money may be delivered by offering discounts, which can be related to in-game sales (Choi, Ko, Medlin, & Chen, 2018). Most gaming assets boost the performance of gamers (Hamari et al., 2017), help increase game levels, and thus build anticipation of further increased game levels i.e. an expectation of future achievements that is central to expectancy for character growth (Liao & Teng, 2017). Therefore, the present study hypothesized:

## H7: Value for money is positively related to expectancy for character growth.

Confirmation of users’ expectations could lead to users’ loyalty to online services (Bhattacherjee, 2001a), indicating the likelihood of expectations determining gamers’ loyalty. Moreover, gamer loyalty (or continuance intention) comes from expectations of positive outcomes (Chang et al., 2014). Gaming outcomes typically require gamers to make advances in game levels. An increased game level helps gamers fulfill motivations, e.g., achievement (completing gaming tasks), socialization (teamwork with advanced gamers), and immersion (experiencing advanced gaming scenarios). All these are major motivations for online game play (Yee, 2006). Hence, anticipation of an increase in game level (or expectancy for character growth, as defined by Liao and Teng, 2017) should be critical for fueling gamer loyalty. The present study thus hypothesized:

## H8: Expectancy for character growth is positively related to online gamer loyalty.

## 4. Methods

## 4.1 Sample and Data Collection

Invitations asking online gamers to complete an online form were posted on local gaming websites and online forums, including bbs.ppt.cc, www.gamebase.com.tw, and forum.gamer.com.tw. The invitations stated that this study was interested in online gamer perceptions and behavior. The online form contained items on the study constructs, demographics, and online game use behavior.

To increase the external validity of the findings, players of various online games were invited to take part. The participants were instructed to nominate one game they had recently played, and rate the study items based on the nominated game. Each participant nominated one game to rate; thus, various games were nominated. Such an approach increased external validity.

Participants who provided valid responses were eligible to join a lottery, in which 30 winners were selected and each issued with a US\$6 gift certificate. Local online surveys typically hold lotteries to encourage participation; thus, such a lottery may not have created substantial self-selection bias. To test for such bias, this study grouped the sample into early respondents and late respondents; the scores of the two groups did not significantly differ in most of the items (32/34 = 94%), further suggesting that self-selection bias was at a minimum herein.

In total, 1,646 online gamers completed the form. To increase the validity of the data, criteria for determining invalid responses were set. The following were thus regarded as invalid and excluded from the analysis: 2 participants who reported that they had not played any online games in the previous month, 2 participants who reported that they had played

online games for more years than their ages, 5 participants who reported that they had played for more than 168 hours per week, 1 respondent who reported an age of over 70 years, 7 respondents who provided nonexistent game names, 10 who reported duplicate emails, and 179 who failed to pass the attention check.

Thus, a total of 1,440 respondents (87.5%) were deemed valid and included in the analysis. The nominated favorite games were League of Legends (27.8%), Hearthstone levels that gamers expect and are motivated to pursue, fitting this study. ## 5. Results

## 5.1 Hypotheses Testing

The LISREL 8.80 software package was used to carry out structural equation modeling (SEM). To increase the robustness of the findings, this study included gender, age, months of playing online games, weekly hours spent playing online games, and commitment to the virtual community as control variables and further considered their influences on online gamer loyalty.

Learning and novelty were positively related to expectancy for character growth (path coefficient, hereafter, $\mathfrak { p . c . } = . 2 8 \ \mathrm { a n d } \ . 3 0 , p < . 0 5 )$ , supporting H1 and H2. Escapism was not related to expectancy for character growth $( \mathtt { p . c . } = . 0 3 , p > . 0 5 )$ , not supporting H3. The reason may be that escapism immerses gamers in the present, which may distract them from future achievements. Enjoyment was positively related to expectancy for character growth (p.c.= .22, p < .05), supporting H4.

Social value was positively related to expectancy for character growth (p.c. = .09, p < .05); supporting H5. Audio-visual value was not related to expectancy for character growth $( \mathtt { p . c . } = - . 0 1 , p > . 0 5 )$ ), not supporting H6. The reason may be that as standardized audio-visual effects are widely observed across various games, gamers may become accustomed to them, and thus the link between audio-visual value and the expectancy for character growth is negated. Value for money was not positively related to expectancy for character growth $( \mathtt { p . c . } = . 0 6 , p > . 0 5 )$ , not supporting H7. The reason may be that gamers who do not pay for games could be considered less serious about gaming. They perhaps only engage in gaming casually and are thus not motivated to reach higher levels. Expectancy for character growth was positively related to online gamer loyalty (path coefficient = .36, $p < . 0 5 )$ , supporting H8. Figure 2 illustrates the analytical results.

## 5.2 Additional Findings

This study observed that learning has a strong impact on loyalty. Hence, it is interesting to know whether learning has a significantly larger impact than other values. Additional analytical results indicated that learning had a significantly larger impact than all other values $( \Delta \mathrm { d f } = 1 , \Delta \chi ^ { 2 } \ge 1 0 . 0 7$ , exceeding the critical value, $\chi ^ { 2 } [ \mathrm { d f } = 1 , \alpha = . 0 5 ] = 3 . 8 4 )$ , except for novelty and enjoyment. Such results indicate the importance of learning in determining users expectancy for character growth.

This study found that months of playing online games was positively related to loyalty $( \mathtt { p . c . } = . 1 3 , p < . 0 5 )$ . This finding may result because experienced gamers know which game satisfies them most, choose that game, and thus have a strong continuance intention. Commitment to the virtual community was positively related to loyalty $( \mathtt { p . c . } = . 1 4 , p < . 0 5 )$ replicating the relation in Teng (2013).

This study further conducted a sensitivity analysis, i.e., examined the analytical results by removing control variables. Such removal did not affect any of the testing results. Moreover, none of the path coefficients were markedly changed ( .04).

Moreover, it may be noted that social value may lead to value for money, building a riva model. Therefore, this study further added a path from social value to value for money. The addition of such a path significantly deteriorated the model fit with the data, i.e., creating a $\Delta \chi ^ { 2 }$ of 479.98 and a df of 10 (transforming value for money from an exogenous variable to an endogenous variable), exceeding the threshold value (18.31). That is, the rival model is statistically inferior to the original model.

This study conducted an endogeneity test to examine the change in the results, i.e., reversing the direction of important paths (the paths from values to expectancy for character growth). Such a reversal created a significantly higher $\chi ^ { 2 }$ value, i.e., $\Delta \chi ^ { 2 } = 1 , 4 5 2 . 3 4 > 6 8 . 6 7 =$ $\chi ^ { 2 } ( \alpha = . 0 5 , \Delta \mathrm { d f } = 5 1 )$ , indicating that the reversal significantly deteriorates the model fit, i.e., passing the endogeneity test.

It is interesting to know whether the results differed among game genres. Among the participants, 571 nominated massively multiplayer role-playing games. ## 6. Discussion

## 6.1 Main Findings and Contributions

This study investigated whether and how consumption values contribute to expectancy were positively related to expectancy for character growth, and subsequently to online gamer loyalty. Moreover, expectancy for character growth was verified as a mediator in the impacts of consumption values on online gamer loyalty. This study is unique in examining how consumption values can enhance gamers’ expectancy for character growth and thus loyalty. This study contributes novel sources (i.e., consumption values) of expectancy for character growth, thus enabling e-commerce managers to make resource allocation decisions to more effectively enhance user loyalty.

The findings of the current study in turn contribute to the underlying theory, i.e., the theory of consumption values. This study followed the contextualized theorizing process (Hong, Chan, Thong, Chasalow, & Dhillon, 2014) to contextualize the theory of consumption values into the sphere of online gaming, and identified suitable constructs to be the contextualized forms of consumption values (e.g., audio-visual value was used instead of functional value). Moreover, this study provided novel consequences of consumption values, i.e., expectancy for character growth and user loyalty. Such novel consequences advance our understanding of the impact and usefulness of this theory.

This study examined the impacts of the consumption values on expectancy for character growth and found that only four values (i.e., learning, novelty, enjoyment, and social value) significantly contributed to such expectancy. Such findings are significant in theory and in practice. In theory, they provide the contextualized theorization. Specifically, the findings indicate that some values may be more influential in online gaming contexts, enhancing our understanding of the theory of consumption values. In practice, such findings could guide

developers/managers to focus on the influential values, rather than diluting their resources.

## 6.2 Theoretical Implications

Fu et al. (2017) found that social interaction features can effectively retain online gamers, indicating the importance of social value. The present study also verified the strong impact of social value in online games. However, the present study indicated its novel mediator, i.e., expectancy for character growth. This novel mediator supports game providers’ decision making in allocating their resources to retain gamers. Specifically, game providers may choose to improve their game designs to enhance gamers’ anticipation of future achievements, a new alternative to retain gamers.

Choi et al. (2018) found that offering discounts helps boost game sales, indicating the importance of good value for money. The present study is in line with theirs in examining the importance of good value for money, but is new in examining its impact on online gamer loyalty. The present study did not observe the impact of good value for money on online gamer loyalty, warranting further research explaining the boundary condition for the impact of good value for money.

Bhattacherjee (2001a) found that the confirmation of expectations in initial service use could satisfy users and thus enhance their loyalty to e-commerce services. The present study is in concordance with Bhattacherjee (2001a) in examining the role of expectations in enhancing users’ decision to be loyal to e-commerce services, but is new in using a novel expectation, i.e., expectancy for character growth. This novel expectation can also enhance user loyalty. Hence, it assists managers in making decisions on how to enhance user loyalty.

Hu, Huang, Zhong, Davison, and Zhao (2016) found that social value is important in determining purchase intention on social shopping websites. The findings of the present study also verified the strong impact of social value, but are new in indicating that social value may raise gamers’ expectancy for character growth and thus affect intention to repeatedly play (i.e., loyalty). This new mechanism may also apply to online shopping websites and social networking services, contributing to the academic impact of research on social value.

It has been theorized in online gaming literature (i.e., Moon et al., 2013) that online gamer loyalty results from gamers’ ability to control gaming characters and to develop online social identities. The present study joins in efforts to explore the antecedents of online gamer loyalty, but is new in that it uses the theory of consumption values, as well as the key values, to explain such loyalty. Such value components may come from gamers’ ability to control gaming characteristics and online social identities, thus potentially motivating future studies to explore this issue.

Hamari, Hanner, and Koivisto (2017) found that service quality is important in the intention to play games. Moreover, they summarized a list of reasons for playing games, e.g., perceived usefulness, ease of use, playfulness, and subjective norms. The present study joins in such efforts to explore the reasons for playing games. However, it uniquely identified four values (i.e., learning, novelty, social value, and enjoyment) as novel reasons for playing online games. Identification of such novel reasons contributes to building a more comprehensive understanding of the formulation of the intention to play online games.

Cheung et al. (2015) examined sales in online games and found that social interaction has a strong impact on online gamers’ psychological engagement. The present study also examined the impact of social aspects on online games, but uniquely found that social value (the utility of social interactions) could impact expectancy for character growth. The present study could encourage future studies to build on the present framework to incorporate the link between expectancy for character growth and psychological engagement, systematically advancing e-commerce literature.

Jin et al. (2017) found that social ties are related to user engagement, which is further also examined the impact of social aspects on gamers’ in-game behavior, but is new in observing the link between social value and expectancy for character growth, demonstrating the role of expectancy for character growth in further enhancing loyalty. This envisions future studies that will examine the role of expectancy for character growth in the impact of social aspects on purchase intention in online games.

Chang et al. (2014) found that both utilitarian and hedonic outcome expectations predict continuance intention (i.e., loyalty) among online gamers. The present study follows theirs in examining the impact of expectations on online gamer loyalty, but contributes to the literature on expectations by examining a novel expectation, i.e., expectancy for character growth. Such an examination indicates abundant opportunities to explore elements of utilitarian and hedonic outcome expectations, including further research on additional outcome expectations.

Online gaming studies have examined frustration (Liao et al., 2016) and goal proximity (Teng, 2017a) as the correlates for online gamer loyalty. However, the present study has a unique contribution in introducing consumption values and expectancy for character growth toward explaining online gamer loyalty. One recent online gaming study (Liao & Teng, 2017) initiated an examination of expectancy for character growth and its consequences on gamers skill, challenge, flow, and loyalty. The present study is new in introducing novel antecedents to expectancy for character growth, i.e., consumption values.

## 6.3 Implications for Electronic Commerce Managers and Online Game Providers

The findings of this study can be applied in various online gaming contexts. These findings may also be applicable in e-commerce contexts for two reasons: First, consumption values are common to all e-commerce contexts. Second, expectancy for character growth can be seen as the expectancy for an increase in membership level (analogous to game level herein), which may have strong appeal for e-commerce managers to engage in customer relationship management. Such generalizability indicates the practical contributions of the findings to e-commerce practice.

The findings of this study contribute insights enabling online game providers to make decisions to enhance gamer loyalty. Expectancy for character growth was related to online gamer loyalty. Hence, online game providers could incorporate inspiring messages to boost gamers’ confidence in their ability to complete future gaming tasks. Encouraging gamers and providing them with in-game guidance or hints could help instill such confidence. Moreover, the provision of moderately difficult tasks could cultivate basic gaming skills among gamers to build their confidence in playing the game.

This study found that learning and novelty positively (and indirectly) contributed to online gamer loyalty, providing game providers with insights to make personnel and game design decisions. Specifically, online game providers could embed subject-related information and activities (e.g., on nature, history, geography, teamwork, and so on, into games so that gamers could enhance their knowledge in areas of their particular interest. Moreover, they could actively update gaming content and regularly enlarge the games’ virtual worlds, to offer gamers novel environments to explore.

Social value was positively (and indirectly) related to online gamer loyalty. Therefore, it is suggested that online game providers facilitate gamers’ perceived social acceptance and social approval in games. Online game providers may add gaming tasks that require gamers to interact and cooperate with others, which may help provide social value to gamers.

Enjoyment was positively (and indirectly) related to online gamer loyalty. Hence, online game providers could consider including compelling gaming characters (i.e., avatars), non-player characters, gaming character interactions, artifacts, treasure, and sites to heighten gamers’ pleasure, thus providing enhanced enjoyment and subsequently fueling gamer loyalty.

In sum, this study observed that learning, novelty, social value, and enjoyment (but not audio-visual value, escapism, and value for money) were related to online gamer loyalty, providing new insights to guide game providers in making resource allocation decisions. Such allocations could more cost-effectively create and fuel online gamer loyalty.

## 6.4 Research Limitations and Future Research Opportunities

Three major motivations lead online gamers to play, i.e., achievement, socialization, and immersion (Yee, 2006). The achievement component has been further decomposed as fantasy satisfaction, adventure, victory, socialization ability, self-actualization, and advancement of wealth and status (Lin, Hung, Fang, & Tu, 2015). Along with these, the present study provides fertile ground for future studies to examine additional antecedents of online gamer loyalty.

This study used self-reported data for analyses, consistent with the pertinent literature (e.g., Hamari, 2015; Merhi, 2016). However, future studies could collect and use observed data, such as on the usage, duration, and frequency of online game use. The inclusion of both self-reported and observed data would further strengthen confidence in the study findings.

Because a single study cannot exhaustively include all possible antecedents, this study chose only variables relevant to the research purpose and the underlying theory. Future studies could include flow, time distortion, and numerous other antecedents of online gamer loyalty to examine the extent to which those known impacts and the findings herein can be replicated.

Further, this study defined enjoyment as intrinsic enjoyment. However, the term may also include extrinsic enjoyment. The inclusion of both definitions may provide further clarity in future research.

This study asked participants to nominate online games. Such an approach was used to increase the external validity of the findings. Hence, this approach was deemed acceptable (e.g., Liao & Teng, 2017). However, it precluded the incorporation of the specific features of each game type, which could deepen understanding of online games. Future studies could focus on a single game genre and explore how to include its features into the framework herein. Moreover, future studies could aim to test how game genres could determine gamers’ emphasis on values.

Women make up about half of the world’s population. Thus, research aimed at understanding female gamers might help game providers capture this potential market. Although our study is consistent wit h some recent studies (i.e., Cheung et al., 2015; Hamari, Hanner, & Koivisto, 2017) in terms of the gender composition of the sample, future studies could explore gender differences or the (potentially) unique features of women gamers.

When gamers achieve high levels, they may encounter greater difficulty reaching frustration, which is known as influential to online gamer loyalty (Liao et al., 2016). Therefore, future studies could examine whether gamers achieving high levels are more likely to encounter gaming frustration and how such frustration might impact the present findings. Moreover, when gamers reach the highest levels, they may experience a low level of expectancy for character growth. Our analytical results indicated that such reduced expectancy may negatively impact gamer loyalty, indicating the necessity of future research to explore how to enhance loyalty among gamers who reach the highest levels.

Online surveys were used to assess user perceptions and intentions in the e-commerce area (e.g., Teng, 2017a), justifying the adoption herein. It may be difficult to replicate all the data entries in subsequent online surveys. Therefore, future studies could collect behavioral or transactional data that provide an enhanced likelihood of replication.

When there is a large gap between the expected and actual outcomes, i.e., expectancy disconfirmation, gamers may modify their expected outcomes, including expectancy for character growth. Therefore, future research may examine how expectancy disconfirmation modifies the relations built in the present study.

## 7. Conclusion

Online games are popular e-commerce applications that attract gamers to increase game antecedents of expectancy for character growth. By using the theory of consumption values, this study examined key value components that could fuel gamers’ expectancy for character growth and, ultimately, loyalty. This study extends the theory of consumption values by envisioning that expectancy for character growth can be a pathway through which consumption values trigger positive responses, contributing key values that should be focused on by the resource allocation decisions made by e-commerce managers. This study established a platform on which future studies can explore the role of expectancy for character growth in e-commerce apps.
