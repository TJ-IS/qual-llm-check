# STP technology: An overview and a conceptual framework

Wayne Huang <sup>a,\*</sup>, Yining Chen <sup>b</sup>, Jarrad Hee <sup>c</sup>

<sup>a</sup> MIS Department, College of Business, Ohio University, USA <sup>b</sup> College of Business, Western Kentucky University, USA <sup>c</sup> BearingPoint (fomer KPMG), Australia

Received 16 June 2003; received in revised form 22 April 2004; accepted 5 June 2004 Available online 26 January 2006

## Abstract

Today’s capital market and security industry are rapidly changing. With the rising of trading volume in stock markets and the US security industry moving to compress the settlement time from T + 3 (3 days settlement) to T + 1 (same day settlement), a fully automated global approach, based on straight through processing (STP) technology seems the only choice. STP involves moving electronically through a trading process from initiation through post-execution and final settlement without manual intervention. Despite the benefits that have been materialized by early adopters of STP, there are problems and risks involved in implementing STP, one of which being the confusion about the concept of STP itself. The objective of this short research note is to clarify the concept and characteristics of STP and propose an integrated conceptual and theoretical STP framework. Such a framework can facilitate a widespread understanding of STP and provide useful guidance to STP implementations. <sup>#</sup> 2004 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Straight through processing (STP); Electronic security trading; Conceptual framework of STP

## 1. Introduction

The global financial market and security industry are in the process of extensive changes. Based on data from North American Securities Data and Automated Quotations (NASDAQ), the value of equity transactions has increased dramatically from US\$ 27 million daily in 1980 to US\$ 27 million every 3.5 min in 2001. The trading volume of New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) has been increased nearly 30 times in the last 20 years to reach US\$ 11.2 trillion in 2000. With the rapid rising of trading volume and the US security industry moving to compress the settlement time from T + 3 to T + 1, a fully automated global approach is necessary. There is a growing consensus on the need to increase productivity and efficiency by reducing operational costs, mitigating risks to participants, and eliminating volume sensitivity to enable the business to grow [9]. One initiative, straight through processing (STP), is proposed by the Global Straight Through Processing Association (GSTPA) which is made of 40 firms in the financial services industry.

Straight through processing (STP), generally defined as the streamlining and automation of securities processing, represents a major shift from the present T + 3 trading towards same day settlement (T + 1) by removing redundant operations and utilizing electronic transfers. With STP, the security industry will be able to build a global network for cross-border trades to promote the more efficient flow of information, to speed up settlement, to reduce risk, and to facilitate the move toward T + 1 [7,12].

It is increasingly evident that to withstand the stresses of the current and the coming financial services environment, organizations must implement STP [1]. Streamlining the information flows will reduce the number of failed cross-border trades by opening up connectivity among participants involved in post-trade, pre-settlement securities processing. According to GSTPA, a reduction in processing time would reduce US\$ 280 billion daily from being exposed to operational risks in the US. With the Securities and Exchange Commission’s (SEC) impending rule change to mandate T + 1, STP is already making its presence felt and will be one of the top trends in investment-management technology for a few years to come.

Though STP is one of the most obvious benefits that new technology brings to the security industry, corporate actions have been limited due to the fact that standards for the application of STP to corporate actions are relatively few and far between [3]. Mohsen argues that ‘‘those aspiring to do business process reengineering must begin to apply the capabilities of information technology’’ [15]. So far, there has not been a consensus on the definition of STP; neither has there been a commonly recognized conceptual framework to integrate various aspects of STP. This can result in confusion and barrier in the implementation of STP, in the exchange of experience, and in the further development of STP. In light of the above deficiency, this short research note provides an overview of STP in clarifying its definition, char acteristics, and benefits, which can serve as a standard for the security industry to develop concrete STP implementation action plans and lay a foundation for those action plans to fly. This research paper also proposes an integrated conceptual and theoretical framework which can be used to steer research and practice of STP in the future.

## 2. An overview of STP

## 2.1. STP and its characteristics

STP is a strategic operating principle focusing on optimizing process design and technology to improve customer service and reduce operational costs. It involves moving electronically through a trading process from initiation through post-execution and final settlement without manual intervention. The series of uninterrupted electronic processes has the following characteristics:

 Securing an initial transaction as an electronic message (a transaction encompasses any activity, not just orders, associated with trading currency and/or securities).

 Transforming and transporting it completely, accurately, systematically, and in a timely fashion, to its initial execution/processing location.

 Passing it through the processing cycle of core processes with minimum, if any, human intervention; where these core processes can include:

\- order processing from indication of interest to order routing, order execution, and order confirmation;

\- the internal link between traditional front, middle, and back office;

\- external links to clearing and settlement, custody and safekeeping; and to all customers and suppliers at every stage of the processing cycle.

 Flowing transaction information seamlessly and securely through automated systems from front-end to back-end, regardless of the parties involved or their geographic locations.

According to Levi and Blackman [10], STP provides a means of pulling back together disparate components of a securities trade, which are separated by technology, systems, and processes, to achieve a seamless trade. STP aims to achieve no re-keying of information; automatic linkages and paperless processing; and workflow automation to facilitate transaction monitoring and exception alerts. During the end-to-end process of the transaction, value-added functions such as information management, reporting, data enrichment and message routing will play very important roles to supplement the whole process in completing the transaction. To achieve the ultimate benefits from STP, integration and connectivity in both business processes and technology is fundamental. In fact, for full effect, STP requires direct integration among firms throughout the industry to enable uninterrupted electronic processes across and throughout the enterprise and industry network. The level of STP achievement will thus depend on the level of integration across organisations, industries, and all players in the marketplace.

## 2.2. External factors for STP

The imperative of T + 1 implementation in the US will reverberate around the world, impacting almost all the firms in the financial industry. This imperative plus the external and internal motivational factors present a strong call for financial institutions to begin preparation for STP immediately. Soliman and Janz suggested that the factors that significantly affect the adoption decision of Internet-based interorganizational information systems (IBIS) are pressures felt from trading partners, pressure felt from competitors, establishing costs, network reliability, data security, scalability, complexity, support from top management, and trust between trading partners [17].

The first external motivational factor for STP implementation is the movement of T + 1 settlement cycle and real-time processing and collateral management. Targeted implementation is to be achieved by 2006 in US which will reduce US settlement risk exposure by 67% or US\$ 750 billion (shorter settlement cycle) [11]. The second external motivational factor is to reduce cost of capturing deals and order processing as well as error rate and complexity. Without STP it is estimated that the cost of managing increasing volume of securities trades is US\$ 12 billion per annum [2,14].

## 2.3. Internal factors for STP

Financial institutions are internally motivated to implement STP to:

 increase flexibility of current systems to meet future demands of business volume, process change, and market fluctuations;

 reduce staff cost by eliminating manual intervention in transaction processing — reducing errors and labour cost;

 attain cross-marketing at the right time, at the right place.

## 2.4. Benefits of STP

## 2.4.1. Quantifiable benefit — efficient and faster response time

By making trade information available at the right time, STP allows for ‘‘just-in-time’’ processing of trades. According to www.consult.com [19], STPenabled brokers can process up to 15 times faster than brokers without it. Other research has identified that the fastest response time for executing and cancelling orders is one second for the firms that utilize STP, compared with an average of 1 min for brokers without the technology to execute orders, and an even longer time to cancel orders [18]. For instance, the implementation of STP by an Australian bank — Colonial First State (CFS) has enabled it to update its cash and stock positions four hours earlier than previously possible and shortened the transaction process from 20 to 5 min [13].

## 2.4.2. Quantifiable benefit — cost reduction

STP eliminates unnecessary processes and replication of work and automates many processes. Cost reduction is therefore achieved through the elimination of manual processing of paper-based transactions and the consequent re-deployment of employees [16].

## 2.4.3. Quantifiable benefit — risk minimization

According to the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT), 25% of all cross-border trades fail or need serious intervention, and over one-third (37%) of trade failures can be attributed to a lack of STP [13]. A significant reduction in trade failures can substantially decrease the risk exposure of financial institutions. With the introduction of STP, it is expected that the failure rates fall to a range of as low as 5–10%, representing a major improvement in the reduction of trade failures over the pre-STP environment [5].

## 2.4.4. Intangible benefit — increased customer satisfaction

STP can help organizations to improve customer satisfaction by enhanced services. For instance, in the FOREX (foreign currency exchange) environment,

STP allows customers to have easier access to research and analysis; quicker and more comprehensive price discovery; and more efficient processing and transparency. In addition to being able to access their accounts 24 h a day and 7 days a week, customers can also receive broader range of products and services through integrated STP systems among the financial institutions (i.e. banks are able to provide products such as insurance and superannuation, which once were not available to their customers).

## 2.4.5. Intangible benefit — improved data quality

According to GSTPA, global custodians will benefit from more timely notification of trades which could lead to a reduction in costs and improved quality of operations. Under a STP environment, the quality of data will be improved as a result of less error-prone data being processed. Reduced sources of data are stored in a real-time data warehouse, which eliminates redundant reference data sources in a highly fragmented environment. The centralized data warehouse enables one source of access across all entities and provides real-time data and scalable architecture [6].

## 3. A conceptual framework for STP

Although many financial institutions realize the importance of STP and some have already started to implement STP, there are still different definitions and understandings of STP in terms of its scope, characteristics, and structure. A conceptual framework of STP can help financial institutions to have a clearer understanding of STP and thus implement STP more effectively in the future.

While security industry is moving towards integrated connections for direct interactions/transactions with customers, partners, and suppliers, all concerned parties need to work with the same data and information standards. Recent usage has defined different stages of interconnection between firms, such as intranet, extranet, and Internet. The term ‘‘intranet’’ refers to a web-based private network. An intranet facilitates dissemination of company information within the boundaries of the company and its sub-organisations. An ‘‘extranet’’ is a group of intranets that connect companies with suppliers or other business partners. An extranet facilitates information sharing across companies. The Internet is an open system which allows interconnectivity and collaboration across all sectors of industry. Such a global system utilizes standards that are universall deployable. Taking into consideration the distinction between internal processes (within a firm and its subsidiaries) and external processes (with other trading partners or customers’ processes), and the integration level as well as connectivity between the two types of processes, we can propose an integrated conceptual framework for STP as illustrated in Fig. 1.

The proposed conceptual framework includes ‘‘intra-STP’’, ‘‘extra-STP’’, and ‘‘global-STP’’ [7].

Intra-STP refers to STP implementation inside the organisation and among all of its branches. Extra-STP refers to STP between firms which allows direct access into other companies’ internal processes and facil itates an industry-wide integrated straight through process. Global-STP refers to a set of interconnected extra-STPs that covers worldwide boundaries. It represents the level of integration of core processes, systems, and information interchanged within firms, between firms, and between industries. It is the largest and most complicated integration of STP.

There needs to be a distinction made between local and global boundaries for extra-STP and/or global-STP capabilities. This distinction is however becoming increasing blurred as firm utilizes centralized operations centre to process their cross-border transactions and domestic transactions originating from their regional countries. To some extent, local boundaries can place some limitations on the capabilities of STP. For example, in Australia, the Stock Exchange Automated Trading System (SEATS) allows only nationally originated access into the system. An overseas transaction has to relay to a local processing centre to be able to initiate the transaction. As stated by Global Straight Through Processing Association (GSTPA), a worldwide (global)-STP solution will be a multilateral interconnectivity to establish an environment for investment managers, brokers/dealers, and global custodians to inter-operate in the process of trade enrichment and matching. It is obvious that for STP to operate in an international scope, it requires international standard protocols, substantial network backbone, and comprehensive cooperation among all participants in the global system.

## 3.1. The building blocks of STP capabilities

Building STP in a global environment is a journey instead of a destination. A more feasible transition should be an evolution not a revolution. Most STP solutions to date have been designed solely for either domestic or cross-border transactions — yet security industry is becoming increasingly borderless [4]. To achieve global-STP capability in a global environment, firms and industries may need to evolve through a series of stages in terms of IT capabilities, business investment, project management, organisational culture, and staff skills. STP building blocks represent the stages of STP progression from intra-STP to global STP. As a result of this progression, a typical firm would experience a reduction in the transaction cost associated with STP as indicated by the downward sloping curve in Fig. 2.

In the intra-STP stage, the STP workflow exists only within a firm’s boundaries nationally or internationally. Data standards are usually developed in-house and defined within the context of a firm. There is little opportunity to exchange data among other firms since the data formats are not standardized. Data transaction costs are usually highest as data are frequently ‘‘reprocessed’’ manually to enable exchange or transmission to third parties. As a firm develops extra-STP, electronic links are established between third parties. This requires the adoption of compatible data formats which enable exchange of meaningful data between firms. In this extra-STP stage, data transaction costs are lower than intra-STP as reduced manual re-formatting is required. In the global-STP stage, any overlapping processes in the workflow and systems of third parties are significantly reduced through the adoption of universal standards and interfaces. This is the most efficient level of processing as data transactions are optimized through seamless integration into the processes across involved entities. Data transaction costs will be the lowest when a firm reaches global-STP.

## 3.2. Key issues when moving through different stages of STP

The proposed integrated conceptual STP framework can not only clear up confusion over the definition and characteristics of STP, but also help identify some key managerial and technical issues for managers to consider so that STP implantation risk can be systematically reduced and managed along the way. Moving to higher STP functionality increases the complexity of the whole system and requires additional investment, inter-firm cooperation, and skill training. Progression towards global-STP capability demonstrates the importance of the whole industry working in concert to reduce inter-firm process redundancies and adopt universally acceptable standards for data and workflows. Given the scale of a global-STP project, it is likely that firms will take an incremental approach towards its achievement building on their capabilities from each block, rather than a switch completely to a new global-STP environment. This approach enables a firm to capitalize on previous experience and reduce the risk of not achieving the project targets in the end. ## 4. Further discussion and conclusion

The recent T + 1 development and rapid growth of global security trading have presented a challenge to the security industry and the capital markets in which they operate. ‘‘The industry needs to focus on more effective straight-through processing before it is in a position to evaluate a conversion from T + 3 to $\mathrm { ~ T ~ } + \mathrm { ~ l ~ }$ settlement’’ says Allen Morgan Jr., chairman of the Security Industry Association (SIA). To remain competitive, firms need to gain the capability to complete the entire transaction cycle effectively in a global market and address the challenges brought about by STP.

STP is one of the most obvious benefits that new technology brings. It has the potential to substantially enhance the effectiveness of cross-border security trading and the development of global financial markets. However, corporate actions and research have so far been laggard and limited. It is time that they move out of the shadows and take the opportunities as well as challenges lain ahead [8]. One problem that fund managers, custodians, and all other financial services providers encounter is that standards and guidance for the application of STP to corporate actions are relatively few and far between. Firms in the security industry have felt the pressure and seen the benefits for pursuing STP. Once they realize that STP is achievable with clear understanding of it, they would quickly move on to cash the benefits promised by the STP movement.

This study proposes an integrated STP conceptual framework that provides a unified perspective for understanding the various levels of STP in an organization. For STP practitioners, the proposed conceptual framework may help clarify some key issues of STP and provide guidance for financial firms to take actions promptly to address the opportunities and challenges brought about by STP from a global perspective. For researchers, the proposed conceptual STP framework presents key issues of STP implementation for future studies, such as re-engineering business processes in financial firms in terms of intra, inter, and global-STP, and Critical Success Factors (CSFs) of implementing STP. Cost benefit analysis can also be conducted for intra, inter, and global-STP implementation levels to support and justify firms investment in STP projects.
