Abstract: Entrepreneurship has become increasingly important for innovations and economic development. However, there is limited information systems research examining the role of regional ICT for entrepreneurs. This study investigates the direct effect of regional ICT access on individuals’ entrepreneurial performance and the interaction effect between regional ICT access and guanxi in the context of Chinese business and economy. Drawing upon a matched large-scale dataset, we found that regional ICT access in terms of access to the Internet, fixed phone, and mobile phone had a significant impact on performance. Furthermore, regional ICT access had a positive interaction effect with guanxi.

KeyWords: Regional ICT access; Guanxi; Entrepreneurship; China

## 1. Introduction

China witnesses the rapid emergence and prosperity of entrepreneurs and private sectors (Phan et al., 2010) in the process of transitioning from a centrally planned economy to a market economy (Marquis and Qian, 2014). Similar to that in the developed economy, entrepreneurship in China is also believed to be a key driver for breakthrough innovation, employment opportunity, and economic development. Generally, entrepreneurial efforts include activities that integrate available resources to exploit business opportunities and create new values (Zahra, 2005).

Prior research has examined factors that account for entrepreneurship in terms of entrepreneurs’ psychological and demographic characteristics (e.g., McMullen and Shepherd, 2006; Zahra et al., 2009), and social, economic, and market environments (e.g., Begley and Tan, 2001; Edelman and Yli-Renko, 2010; Lerner and Haber, 2001). In the area of information and communication technology (ICT), scholars have investigated the role of ICT for startup companies and small- and medium-sized enterprises (e.g., Francalanci and Morabito, 2008; Rivard et al., 2006; Street and Meister, 2004). It is recognized that ICT can reduce transaction costs, improve organizational routines, and strengthen relationships with clients or suppliers (Giudice and Straub, 2011; Street and Meister, 2004).

However, there still exist two gaps in the literature regarding the impact of ICT (see Appendix). First, scant literature has explored how the development of ICT

infrastructure in a region affects the business performance of firms or individual entrepreneurs. At the macro level, the literature has examined the influence of ICT on economic growth (e.g., Sassi and Goaied, 2013; Vu, 2013). At the micro level, the focus is on how ICT possessed by a firm affects business performance (e.g., Mithas et al., 2011). Only limited studies have tried to examine how macro-level ICT affects microlevel firms and individuals, except for Agarwal et al.’s (2009) study that investigated the influence of regional ICT on an individual’s use of Internet. To our best knowledge, no study has been conducted to examine how regional ICT affects the performance of individual entrepreneurs. Because the reach and coverage of ICT vary across regions (Dobrota et al., 2015), an entrepreneur’s access to market information and business opportunities through ICT is different for different locations, which may have important implications for entrepreneurial success.

Second, current theoretical arguments about the impact of ICT and corresponding empirical evidence are ambiguous and mixed (Chae et al., 2014; Francalanci and Morabito, 2008; Luo et al., 2012; Mithas et al., 2011; Neirotti and Raguseo, 2017). Some studies found that small businesses tended to face a high level of risks in the implementation of ICT because these businesses were constrained by the availability of internal resources and the access to external resources (Riemenschneider et al., 2003). An emerging ICT may lead to negative consequences if it is not appropriately applied in the business (Olsen and Sætre, 2007). Accordingly, it is imperative for a firm to configure ICT with other resources to fully explore ICT benefits (Giudice and Straub, 2011; Wade and Hulland, 2004). Francalanci and

Morabito (2008) stated that “although a necessary factor, (ICT) cannot in and of itself generate sustainable performance advantages” (p. 298). Therefore, we posit that a contingent perspective that investigates how ICT interacts with other factors may be one of the answers to reconcile the findings.

To address these gaps, the first purpose of this study is to examine the direct effect of regional ICT access on entrepreneurial success. In this study, we define regional ICT access as the degree to which ICT infrastructure develops in a regional area (Ayanso et al., 2014; Dobrota et al., 2015; ITU, 2009; Novo-Corti and Barreiro-Gen, 2015). ICT access is one of the three key dimensions (the other two being ICT use and ICT skills) of ICT Development Index developed by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) to measure the development of ICT across different regions or countries over time (ITU, 2009). In particular, regional ICT access captures the extent to which ICT infrastructure is ready or available in a specific region so that individual users and organizations can deploy ICT to enhance growth and development. In China, there is a significant imbalance of ICT infrastructure and access across regions. Urban areas have a better coverage of ICT than rural areas, and there is a digital division between the east and the west regions. Thus, China represents an exemplary context to capture regional variations of ICT access that may influence entrepreneurial activities.

Furthermore, this study examines the interactive effect between regional ICT access and a special type of social capital in China, i.e., guanxi. Guanxi, defined as interpersonal ties, is a very important concept in the context of Chinese business and economy (Peng and Heath, 1996). Different from their counterparts in the market economy with well-developed institutions, entrepreneurs in China have to confront institutional obstacles, such as resource constraints and inefficient enforcement of policies, rules, and other institutions (Guo and Miller, 2010). Therefore, entrepreneurs in China rely on guanxi to succeed in their entrepreneurial efforts (e.g., Chen M-H et al., 2015; Batjargal, 2007). Despite its widely recognized role in business activities, guanxi may also generate negative influences, such as organizational inertia and low sensitivity to market changes (Gu et al., 2008; Zhou, 2013). Empirically, some studies have presented incongruent results (e.g., Faccio, 2010; Guo and Miller, 2010; Troilo and Zhang, 2012). Accordingly, an interactive approach may resolve not only the inconsistency of ICT impact but also that of guanxi impact, responding to recent studies that call for further examination of the contingent value of guanxi (Stam and Elfring, 2008; Phan et al., 2010). It enables us to know how to exploit the full potential of guanxi and minimize its negative influence with the help of ICT.

## 2. Theoretical Background and Hypotheses Development

We argue that the importance of regional ICT access and guanxi for entrepreneurs can be better explained and understood by means of two theoretical perspectives, namely resource-based view (RBV) and social capital theory. The two theories provide different but complementary perspectives to explain entrepreneurial success (e.g., Lee et al., 2001). Drawing upon the logics of RBV (Barney, 1991), prior studies have identified and examined ICT as a crucial source of valuable and heterogeneous resource, which may help a firm to gain the advantages of information and efficiency and enhance business performance (Rivard et al., 2006; Wade and Hulland, 2004). Specifically, in this study, two types of resources could be sourced from regional ICT access to enhance entrepreneurial success. The first is the availability of ICT-based resources, such as information or ideas; the second is related to the use of ICT, which enables the efficient deployment of ICT-based resources and other entrepreneurial resources.

RBV (Barney, 1991) suggested that specific resources, embodying either tangible (e.g., financial capital, equipment) or intangible resources (e.g., information or knowledge, technological), can significantly contribute to competitive advantages and business performance (Newbert, 2007). In line with this basic logic, widespread ICT access could provide entrepreneurs with the availability of market intelligence or business ideas. As posited in the prior research, a better understanding of customers, competitors, and market environments is a type of critical resource that enhances market responsiveness and determines whether superior performance can be achieved (Morgan et al., 2009). Thus, abundant information enabled by regional ICT access provides a critical source of valuable resources for entrepreneurs.

Competitive advantages cannot be attained if the potential of these static resources cannot be fully exploited (Barney, 1997). Following this logic of RBV, entrepreneurs should further familiarize themselves with how to exploit ICT-based resources. Because of the influence of social interactions in an ICT platform (Agarwal et al., 2009), entrepreneurs in a region with widespread ICT access may be more likely to learn and handle information technologies. That is, regional ICT access may promote individuals’ use of ICT and improve their implementation skills. By operating ICT, entrepreneurs can exploit and transform market information, business ideas, and other resources into business performance in an effective and efficient manner (Newbert, 2007). In short, the enhanced likelihood and skills of using ICT provide another critical source for competitive advantages.

Apart from RBV, social capital theory (Burt, 1997; Nahapiet and Ghoshal, 1998) provides another foundational perspective to explain entrepreneurial success (Gedajlovic et al., 2013), which may shed light on the value or benefit embedded in social relationships of individuals. The theory suggests that social relationships or ties are critical for resources acquisition and business performance of small firms (Stam et al., 2014). Specifically, as a special type of social capital, guanxi has been intertwined into Chinese people’s daily life and work. Guanxi is a fundamental philosophy and strategy to conduct business in China. It brings for entrepreneurs not only ordinary benefits, such as financial support (Troilo and Zhang, 2012), but also regulatory benefits, such as political protection of property rights (Zhou, 2013).

Furthermore, we posit that the two different theoretical perspectives may complement each other to account for the interaction effect between regional ICT access and guanxi. On the one hand, ICT access may leverage guanxi to enhance entrepreneurial success. On the other hand, guanxi can assist in exploiting valuable resources sourced from ICT access and achieving competitive advantages.

## 2.1 Regional ICT access and entrepreneurship

ICT not only facilitates start-up businesses but also enhances their development and growth (Giudice and Straub, 2011). “IT is the magic ingredient that inspires and most often enables contemporary entrepreneurial endeavors” (Giudice and Straub, 2011, p. 3). This study clarifies and highlights the advantages that an entrepreneur can benefit from regional ICT access because of the abundant information and opportunities available and accessible on digital infrastructure (Goolsbee and Klenow, 2002).

First, the rapid development of ICT significantly enhances a region’s capabilities of creating, collecting, storing, processing, and interpreting information. The availability of ICT is associated with the production of accessible digital resources (Niles and Hanson, 2003). ICT can develop and broaden a seamless fabric of connectivity across organizational boundaries, as well as regional and national borders, so that a vast amount of information can be processed, transformed, stored, and exchanged. Accordingly, with the rapid diffusion of ICT in a region, a large volume of valuable market information, including multimedia data, could be easily and quickly accessed by entrepreneurs.

Second, ICT plays a pivotal role in cultivating the emergence of business ideas or opportunities (Ma and Wang, 2006). Prior studies have revealed a strong correlation between the diffusion of ICT and the development of new ventures (Giudice and Straub, 2011). As Gonzalez-Sanchez (2013) stated, ICT availability demonstrates the extent to which a region is taking advantage of the opportunities related to information and knowledge. The widespread infrastructure support of ICT creates abundant opportunities for entrepreneurs to conduct online business and boosts the development of entrepreneurial activities. For example, Internet users in China surged from approximately 138 million in 2006 to 721 million in 2016<sup>1</sup>. Along with the boom in smartphones and cellular networks, increasingly more people started to conduct online businesses. According to a report from China<sup>2</sup>, the number of small businesses relying on online transaction platforms exceeded 10 million, and the total revenue of these businesses reached more than 180 billion RMB (Chinese currency - Renminbi Yuan) in 2015 and would double in 2016.

Third, because of the existence of “social influence” in an entrepreneur’s interactions with others, the entrepreneur may be motivated to learn and adopt ICT that other peer entrepreneurs have used in his or her own business activities (Agarwal et al., 2009). “The influence can be a consequence of learning from others...or simply pressure to conform” (Agarwal et al., 2009, p. 278). An entrepreneur can be more aware of the advantages or benefits of ICT through their social and business interactions with other ICT users. Thus, the entrepreneurs may develop strong incentives and knowledge to adopt the same technologies. In addition, the widespread availability of ICT is likely to force an entrepreneur to adopt ICT (Wang and Ahmed, 2009). For example, an entrepreneur has to incorporate widely accepted businessrelated ICT and implement these ICTs in the business process to acquire market legitimacy.

Accordingly, in a region with a higher degree of ICT access, entrepreneurs are more likely to adopt ICT tools and use them to a greater extent. In turn, they are more likely to exploit market information and business opportunities available on ICT infrastructure (Giudice and Straub, 2011). On the one hand, the availability of ICT enables entrepreneurs to acquire real-time market intelligence and enhance market responsiveness (Chen Y et al., 2015; Luo et al., 2012; Nazir and Pinsonneault, 2012). On the other hand, the use of ICT can assist them in capturing business opportunities (Reuber and Fischer, 2011) and reducing transaction and communication costs (Pan et al., 2015). In addition, the availability of ICT can extend the market scope and enable international entrepreneurship (Reuber and Fischer, 2011).

Finally, entrepreneurial activities call for active participations from customers, suppliers, and other business partners. A well-developed ICT infrastructure provides a ubiquitous platform for these participants to connect with one another so that the efficiency and effectiveness of information exchange, communications, and collaborations can be improved (Agarwal et al., 2009). Effective communication and information exchange enable entrepreneurs to coordinate with various participants in entrepreneurial activities, reduce entrepreneurial uncertainties, and enhance the possibilities of entrepreneurial success (Chen Y et al., 2015). As posited by previous research (e.g., Carrillo and Gaimon, 2004), an efficient and effective coordination and management system is one of the crucial resources or capabilities for a firm.

Hypothesis 1: Regional ICT access is positively associated with entrepreneurial performance.

## 2.2 Guanxi and entrepreneurship

Distinct from the notion of relationship based on trust and social norms in the western culture, guanxi is closely bonded with social obligations and reciprocity (Gu et al., 2008; Phan et al., 2010). It is implied for both parties of a guanxi to exchange gifts or favors if they want to maintain a strong and long-term tie (Bond, 1996). Although family tie is the basic form of guanxi, the scope of “family” can be extended to other social groups (Yang, 1994), such as kinship, friendship, and acquaintanceship, by means of developing “ganqing” (emotional attachment) and material investments (Guo and Miller, 2010).

As a substitute for underdeveloped formal rules, policies, and institutions (Xin and Pearce, 1996), guanxi enables individuals or firms to overcome institutional obstacles (Guo and Miller, 2010). It assists in acquiring necessary supports, building legitimacy, and achieving superior performance (Chen M-H et al., 2015; Sheng et al., 2011; Stam et al., 2014). As Steier (2007) stated, guanxi plays a predominant role during the early growth of start-ups by providing a variety of low-cost resources. For example, the initial capital for start-ups usually comes from families or other non-kin ties at rather low or even no interest rate (Troilo and Zhang, 2012). Chen M-H et al. (2015) revealed the positive effects of guanxi on information accessibility, resource availability, and entrepreneurial success. Yu et al. (2013) confirmed the importance of guanxi in building market legitimacy and dealing with institutional constraints. Furthermore, entrepreneurs can learn and accumulate valuable experience or skills

from strong ties (Lee and Tsang, 2001; Sheng et al., 2011). Ma et al. (2009) found that interpersonal network had a positive effect on adaptive capability.

Despite the importance of social capital or guanxi, extant arguments or evidences remain incongruent regarding its impact on business performance. Gu et al. (2008) contended that there existed negative influences of guanxi, such as increased reciprocity and collective blindness to market changes, and that guanxi network might compromise the ability to respond to market dynamism. Likewise, Zhou (2013) stated that connections with governments might result in organizational inertia because firms relied too much on the external support of their partners. Further, Guo and Miller (2010) found that guanxi was not always necessary for entrepreneurial success. Faccio (2010) showed that connections with governments might have a negative impact on performance. Troilo and Zhang (2012) found that guanxi had no or even negative effect on conducting entrepreneurial activities.

To reconcile these mixed results, recent studies suggested a contingent view of social capital or guanxi be examined. Gu et al. (2008) investigated the contextual value of guanxi in shaping market capabilities. Stam and Elfring (2008) confirmed the contingent effect of social capital on new venture performance. Zhao and Lu (2016) found the contingent value of connections with governments in acquiring bank loans. As presented in the previous studies, the value of guanxi depends on the conditions under which it imposes effects. Therefore, apart from examining the direct effect of guanxi on entrepreneurial success, we contribute to this stream of literature by examining its contingency with regional ICT access. Generally, we posit that guanxi may exert more positive influence rather than negative influence on entrepreneurial success in China’s transition economy.

## Hypothesis 2: Guanxi is positively associated with entrepreneurial performance.

## 2.3 Interaction of regional ICT access and guanxi

Over and above the main effects of regional ICT access and guanxi, we posit that there is an interaction effect of regional ICT access and guanxi on entrepreneurial performance. First, regional ICT access may strengthen the guanxi-entrepreneurship link because ICT can increase the magnitude of resources accessed from guanxi network and deploy these resources in an efficient way. On the one hand, the availability of ICT enables efficient and effective communications among guanxi partners, thereby boosting their frequent interactions (Wellman et al., 2002). Accordingly, more resources or benefits can be acquired from guanxi network to support entrepreneurial activities. On the other hand, the widespread use of ICT can leverage the resources accessed from guanxi. Generally, information or opportunities originated from regional ICT access may complement the resources from guanxi (Giudice and Straub, 2011). For example, market intelligence available on the ICT infrastructure enables entrepreneurs to deploy guanxi resources (e.g., financial capital) in response to market changes and then achieve superior performance. Moreover, a variety of valuable business opportunities available on regional ICT infrastructure provide entrepreneurs potential investment alternatives and enable them to transform guanxi resources into profitable business activities.

Second, guanxi may also strengthen the ICT-entrepreneurship link if guanxi can assist in exploiting information and opportunities that originate from regional ICT access. Warschauer (2003) contended that “peer effects” generated from social networks could improve an individual’s computing abilities. Therefore, the accumulated skills to use ICT enable entrepreneurs to better capture and exploit business opportunities available on ICT infrastructure. For example, Nguyen et al. (2015) confirmed the positive effect of network relationships on successful ICT implementation in small businesses.

Furthermore, special support and regulatory resources available in guanxi network can assist entrepreneurs in exploiting business opportunities generated by regional ICT access and in acquiring economic rents. For example, entrepreneurs may catch opportunities of conducting online businesses to sell products and service in a particular niche market (Luo et al., 2012). However, they may have to spend months or even years on applying for administrative permissions because of undeveloped institutions and ambiguous regulations in the business environment. With the support from guanxi, entrepreneurs may attain these permissions quickly (Zhang et al., 2015), and thereby own first-mover advantages in the market (Lieberman and Montgomery, 1988). In addition, business-related resources and skills offered by guanxi (Chen M-H et al., 2015) can strengthen the abilities to exploit business opportunities available on ICT infrastructure. For example, channel capability strengthened by guanxi (Gu et al., 2008) enables entrepreneurs to promote online products or services in appropriate channels.

Hypothesis 3: The interaction between regional ICT access and guanxi is positively associated with entrepreneurial performance.

## 3. Research Method

## 3.1 Data

This study drew upon a matched dataset that contained two data sources collected by two different agencies in China. The first primary data source was from the China Labor-force Dynamics Survey (CLDS) conducted by the Center for Social Science Survey at Sun Yat-sen University<sup>3</sup>. To enhance sample representativeness, CLDS employed a multistage cluster and stratified PPS sampling method (http://css.sysu.edu.cn). More than 800 enumerators were trained to conduct nationwide face-to-face interviews with households in both urban and rural areas under the supervision of experienced interviewers. The survey provided us the individual-level data about demographics, entrepreneurial activities, and guanxi.

An entrepreneur can be defined as “one who owns, launches, manages, and assumes the risks of an economic venture” (Greve and Salaff, 2003, pp.1). As this definition suggested, a business owner, either an employer or a self-employed person, was regarded as an entrepreneur (Shane, 2003). In addition, an entrepreneur usually took the risks of spending time and capital on an uncertain venture. In line with the theoretical definition, we operationally identified an entrepreneur by whether an individual reported the revenue earned from business operations beyond employment wages and property incomes (Parker, 2004) in the CLDS survey. This operational definition excluded other earnings except for the revenue that an individual earned by conducting his or her business activities. It was simple but effective to judge whether an individual was conducting entrepreneurial activities. That is, if an individual reported a revenue in the survey, then he or she could be recognized as an entrepreneur launching an economic venture. Table 1 summarizes the demographics of 1,700 identified entrepreneurs across 116 cities and 29 provinces. As illustrated in Table 1, almost 60% of the total sample was aged between 30 and 50 years. It was understandable that this age group usually accumulated sufficient business experience and capitals for entrepreneurial activities. Further, there were more male entrepreneurs (57%) than female entrepreneurs (43%). In terms of entrepreneurial performance, nearly 58% of the entrepreneurs earned business revenues in the range from 10,000 to 50,000 RMB, and approximately 14% earned more than 50,000 RMB. In addition, the sample had a balanced distribution across three regions, i.e., the East, Middle, and West.

## 3.2 Measures

Entrepreneurial performance. As stated in prior studies, economic indicators were most often used to measure entrepreneurial success (Powell and Eddleston, 2013). Accordingly, in this study, we used the annual business revenue reported by an entrepreneur in the CLDS survey to measure entrepreneurial performance. Because CLDS was conducted in 2012, the business revenue earned by the end of 2011 was reported.

Regional ICT access. There were various measures of regional ICT access used by prior studies or international organizations (Aynaso et al., 2014). Specifically, the indicators related to the Internet, fixed phone, and mobile phone were most often use (e.g., Ayanso et al., 2010; Gonzalez-Sanchez, 2013; ITU, 2009; Novo-Corti and Barreiro-Gen, 2015; Van Dijk and Hacker, 2003). Accordingly, three city-level indicators from the China City Statistics Yearbook were adopted in this study: the number of inhabitants or households with access to Internet broadband, fixed phone, and mobile phone. In addition, we generated a composite indicator of regional ICT access by averaging the three indicators. To reduce the concern about simultaneity in regressions, we adopted the average score of each indicator in the 3 years (2008– 2010) prior to the reported year of entrepreneurial performance (2011).

Guanxi. We measured guanxi as the number of close friends or acquaintances who offered support to an entrepreneur, as reported in CLDS. The measure contained three items in terms of financial support, emotional support, and decision-making

support. We averaged the three items as a composite to measure guanxi. The threeitem measure reflected strong and direct ties that were critical for an entrepreneur. The similar measure was also adopted in previous studies (e.g., Troilo and Zhang, 2012).

Control variables. To reduce the concern about endogeneity caused by omitted variables, we controlled individual-level variables and the regional fixed effect that might influence entrepreneurial activities. First, we included basic demographics such as age and gender. Levesque and Minniti (2006) suggested that entrepreneurial behaviors declined with the increase of age because individuals tended to avoid risks. Olson et al. (2003) argued that there were more men than women in entrepreneurial activities. Next, education and health were also controlled. In the study of Chung (2005), the significant impact of education on entrepreneurship was examined. Good health was also considered to be very important to deal with the mental and physical stress of starting a new business (Troilo and Zhang, 2012). In addition, we controlled political identity to avoid the impact of an entrepreneur’s political membership and legitimacy (Zhao and Lu, 2016). All the above individual-level data were drawn from CLDS. In addition, we added province fixed effects to control for unobserved regional influences. ## 4.4 Robustness checks

To reconfirm the above results, we conducted several robustness checks by using different measures of the corresponding variables. The results are reported in Table 5 and Table 6. First, we replaced regional ICT access with 1- (2010), 2- (2009), and 3- year (2008) in-advance measures separately. As the results in Table 5 suggested, no matter which kind of measure or indicator was used, the direct effect of regional ICT access and its interaction with guanxi remained significantly positive. Second, we adopted telecommunication revenue reported in the China City Statistics Yearbook (2008–2010) to replace the measure of regional ICT access and reflect the development of ICT infrastructure in a region. As shown in the first four models in Table 6, no matter which year of telecommunication revenue was used, its direct and interaction effects with guanxi on entrepreneurial performance remained significantly positive. Third, we replaced guanxi with a composite indicator reported in CLDS. This indicator generally measured the number of friends or acquaintances who might offer support for entrepreneurs, while not specifying what kind of support. The last four models in Table 6 report the positive main effect of guanxi on entrepreneurial performance. In addition, its interaction effects with regional ICT access were

basically consistent with the original results reported in Table 4. We, therefore, concluded that the measurements and empirical results were reliable.

## 5. Discussions and Implications

With a matched dataset from China, this study reveals the important role of regional ICT access in enhancing entrepreneurial success. First, we found that regional ICT access had a positive effect on entrepreneurial performance. This result resonates with the prior studies that draw upon RBV and emphasize the positive influence of ICT (e.g., Francalanci and Morabito, 2008; Olsen and Sætre, 2007; Street and Meister, 2004; Zhang et al., 2013). This study is one of the first to examine the impact of regional ICT access on individual entrepreneurs. The finding provides additional evidence for the role of ICT in entrepreneurial activities.

Second, guanxi, a critical factor in the context of Chinese business and economy, was found to be significantly associated with entrepreneurial performance. Generally, this result is consistent with prior studies (e.g., Chen M-H et al., 2015; Guo and Miller, 2010), although there have been some controversies or ambiguities regarding the role of guanxi in business operations (e.g., Phan et al., 2010; Troilo and Zhang, 2012). This finding supports the view that advocates the positive influence of guanxi.

Third, it is noteworthy that the interaction between regional ICT access and guanxi had a positive effect on entrepreneurial performance. We found that

widespread ICT access would enable the positive impact of guanxi on entrepreneurial performance, while restricted ICT access would inhibit it. This is one of the first important findings of the interaction effect. Although prior studies have addressed the importance of integrating ICT with other resources (e.g., Francalanci and Morabito, 2008; Giudice and Straub, 2011; Wade and Hulland, 2004), there has been very limited empirical evidence revealing how ICT interacts with other factors.

## 5.1 Implications for Research

This study makes several theoretical contributions to the information systems and entrepreneurship literature. First, unlike other academic disciplines concerning the macro-level influence of regional ICT, our study focuses on the influence of regional ICT at the microlevel, i.e., the level of individual entrepreneurship. The information systems literature does not have a rich history of examining the influence of regional ICT on organizational or individual behaviors. This study adopts a unique perspective by performing a multilevel analysis, namely ICT at the regional level and entrepreneur at the individual level. Using different indicators of regional ICT access and examining their effects on individual entrepreneurship, this multilevel study expands the knowledge about the role of regional ICT.

Second, by integrating RBV and social capital theory, this study explores the interaction of ICT with social capital (guanxi) in entrepreneurial activities. It not only addresses the inconsistency of ICT effects in prior literature but also provides a

clearer picture to demonstrate how ICT affects business performance. That is, we posit that ICT should be complemented with other factors (guanxi in the current study) so as to gain a better understanding of how to enhance entrepreneurial success. The findings explain why some entrepreneurs can benefit more from ICT than others. This study also responds to the call of Giudice and Straub (2011), suggesting future research “engage in a concerted effort to better and more deeply study the nature of this marriage (ICT and entrepreneurship).”

Third, this study contributes to the literature of entrepreneurship and social capital by resolving the inconsistency of guanxi effects on entrepreneurship. It is far from enough to rely on guanxi in entrepreneurial activities as guanxi may produce negative influence in dynamic markets (Gu et al., 2008). As a complex business activity, entrepreneurship is the interaction between individual differences and environmental contexts (Baron and Tang, 2009). This study reveals the complementary effect of factors at different levels and contributes to the knowledge about how to enhance entrepreneurial success. In addition, as recent research suggests, the value of social capital depends on what abilities leverage or utilize it (Adler and Kwon, 2002). This study analyzes and examines the role of ICT in leveraging guanxi. The interaction effect between regional ICT access and guanxi suggests that future studies explore the contingencies of guanxi. The contingent approach can contribute the understandings of social capital by identifying the contextual factors that strengthen or weaken its effects (Mehra et al., 2006; Oh et al., 2004). There may be various reasons to account for the inconsistency, one of which i to address the contingencies of guanxi by considering its interplay with other factors (Stam et al., 2014; Zhao and Lu, 2016).

## 5.2 Implications for Practice

This study offers several suggestions for individual entrepreneurs and government agencies. In the transition into market-based mechanisms (Marquis and Qian, 2014), entrepreneurs are suggested to focus on multilevel factors and their interplay in entrepreneurial activities. As suggested in this study, entrepreneurs should gain access to ICT, learn to deploy ICT, and exploit the benefits of information and opportunities available on ICT infrastructure. Meanwhile, entrepreneurs should build strong ties with relatives, friends, colleagues, or fellow villagers to overcome resource constraints, as advocated in the prior literature (Chen M-H et al., 2015). The more important issue is that entrepreneurs should complement the two factors, namely applying ICT to leverage guanxi and exploiting ICT advantages with the help of guanxi. Entrepreneurs are suggested to effectively configure available resources that can be accessed from guanxi network as well as the regional environment.

This study confirms the importance of ICT infrastructure for individual entrepreneurship. The development of infrastructure establishes “the availability of incentives to start a business” (Dubini, 1988), and “the starting point of every entrepreneurial activity” (Giudice and Straub, 2011). Entrepreneurs are suggested to invest their entrepreneurial efforts in the areas with advanced ICT infrastructure. In the meantime, governments should play active roles in building and developing ICT infrastructure to attract entrepreneurs. Additionally, as one of the world’s most attractive markets, China has attracted abundant foreign firms or individuals to start new businesses. Our findings can also provide valuable suggestions for them about how to pursue entrepreneurial success in China.

## 5.3 Limitations and future directions

The research findings should be interpreted with caution considering the possible limitations in this study. First, our study drew on the data sources from China. Although China is the most typical emerging market, there are still many differences between China and other emerging economies (Khanna et al., 2005; Sheng et al., 2011). To generalize our findings, future research may collect data from other countries. Second, as a common concern in survey approach, common method bias may have existed in this study. To address the concern, we matched two different data sources. Some variables were from the CLDS survey, while others were from the statistics issued by the Chinese government, representing a combination of objective and subjective data in this study. Third, according to the ICT Development Index (ITU, 2009), there are three dimensions in terms of ICT access, ICT use, and ICT skills. While ICT access represents the level of network infrastructure and access to ICT, the other two reflect more about the intensity and ability of regional residence to use ICT (ITU, 2009). In this study, ICT access should be suitably used to reflect

#

regional ICT infrastructure and examine its influence on microlevel business activities. However, future research can examine the other two dimensions and add the knowledge about the role of regional ICT. Although the indicators of Internet broadband, fixed phone, and mobile phone are most used to measure ICT access, future research may try more indicators. Finally, individual entrepreneurship can be classified into two categories, namely necessity entrepreneurship and opportunity entrepreneurship. Necessity entrepreneurship refers to that there are no better options except starting new businesses, while opportunity entrepreneurship refers to the entrepreneurial activities that pursue business opportunities (Wong et al., 2005). Future research may explore whether the effects of ICT are different between the two types of entrepreneurial activities.
