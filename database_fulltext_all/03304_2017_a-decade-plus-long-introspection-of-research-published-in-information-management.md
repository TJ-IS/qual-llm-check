## A B S T R A C T

It is insightful for a leading publication in information systems like Information & Management to conduct a deep introspection of what it publishes and how it has evolved over the years. It informs all constituencies: readers. authors. reviewers and editors in future research efforts. Information & Management (I&M for short) is one of the oldest journals in the information systems <sup>fi</sup>eld and has been continuously regarded as a top journal with high quality publications. The purpose of this study is to conduct a detailed examination of the research I&M has published in a 10+ year period from 2004 to 2014. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we consider four dimensions of research: topics, research methodologies, research models, and paradigmatic research approaches. Patterns and trends for these four dimensions are presented in order to provide an in-depth examination. For benchmark purposes, we compare our results to a major US-based journal: the MIS Quarterly (MISQ) and a major European journal: the European Journal of Information Systems (EJIS).

## 1. Introduction

Research in information systems (IS) is characterized by constant change due to a number of reasons. First information technology (IT) itself is inundated by new products, processes and innovations (e.g., social media, smart devices, and new delivery mechanisms). Second, new research paradigms (such as interpretive and critical research) and methodologies (such as design science, action research and qualitative methods) have emerged and are gaining wider acceptance. Third, new researchers, many with different background and training, have entered the <sup>fi</sup>eld while others have left due to natural causes. It is therefore prudent to examine the state of IS research on a periodic basis to identify trends and spot shifts.

In this study, we focus on the journal: Information & Management. Information & Management (I&M for short) is one of the oldest journals in the information systems <sup>fi</sup>eld, having started publication in 1977. It has maintained a high focus on quality and has been continuously regarded as a top journal in the <sup>fi</sup>eld. We conduct a meta-analysis of more than ten years of articles published in I&M. Meta-analysis is a useful mechanism for capturing the information that we need to generate and analyze it further. Stemler [21] has pointed out that meta-analyses enable researchers to navigate a massive knowledge base with relative ease and systematic methods. Similar analyses for I&M were reported by Claver et al. [4] which covered the period from 1981 to 1997, and later by Palvia et al. [19] which covered the period from 1992 to 2005. This study covers the period from 2004 to 2014, thus bringing our readers up to date with the latest information.

We focus on speci<sup>fi</sup>c dimensions of research which will be of wide interest to I&M readers as well as readers in general. These include: research topics, research methodologies, research models, and paradigmatic research approaches. Patterns and trends for these four dimensions are presented in order to provide in-depth understanding of the published research. For benchmark and comparative purposes, we compare our results to a major USbased journal: the MIS Quarterly (MISQ) and a major European journal: the European Journal of Information Systems (EJIS).

Besides introspection, this study contributes to the literature by providing directions to both new and experienced researchers New and budding researchers will bene<sup>fi</sup>t by knowing the trends in IS research; senior scholars can evaluate the trends and focus on the areas that need more attention. ## 2. Method

The research method for this study was adopted from Palvia et al. [15] where methodological and topic trends were identi<sup>fi</sup>ed in information systems research for a ten year period (2004–2013). We used a three phase process based on the work done by Cumbie et al. [5] and Levy and Ellis [12]. This three phase process is illustrated in Fig. 1. In this process, the researchers <sup>fi</sup>rst select journals to pool papers in order to satisfy the boundaries of the study. Second, the classi<sup>fi</sup>cation is developed for coding, and the selected papers are coded based on the classi<sup>fi</sup>cation. Third, the data is synthesized and evaluated.

## 2.1. Phase 1: journal selection and pool of papers

The papers were pooled from three journals from 2004 to 2014, an eleven year period. The journals selected are considered some of the top ranking publications in the IS <sup>fi</sup>eld [13,20]. As this study is focused on Information & Management, we selected all papers in I&M during this period. In addition, we selected papers for the same period from the Management Information Systems Quarterly (MISQ), a high-ranked journal in the IS <sup>fi</sup>eld, and the European Journal of Information Systems (EJIS), another reputed journal that serves as an outlet for research outside of the United States, especially Europe. The selection of these two journals for comparison allows us to compare I&M with cutting-edge research in IS along with diverse research from many parts of the world. A summary of the journals selected and a count of papers pooled from 2004 2014 are presented in Table 1. Fig. 2 visualizes the number of papers published in each of the three selected journals year-by-year.

## 2.2. Phase 2: classification

A four-dimensional framework comprised of topics, research methodologies, research models, and paradigmatic research approaches for classifying research articles was developed by Palvia et al. [15], and we use all four of these dimensions in this paper. In addition to this four-dimensional framework, we also adopt their typologies for these dimensions. Three of the four dimensions are shown in Fig. 3; the fourth dimension is the research topic. Any single dimension can be examined by itself. The same single dimension can be examined over time providing trends and developments over that period of time. Dimensions can also be examined in relation with one another yielding further insights. While we have the data for multi-dimensional analysis, it was not included in this paper due to space limitations. The four dimensions are described below.

## 2.2.1. Dimension 1: research topics

Many of the research topics are originally from Palvia et al. [16]. These were derived by building on the work done by Alavi and Carlson [2] and Barki et al. [3]. Initially, the topics were developed using the top three levels of Barki et al. [3]. Some changes were made, and several topics were added to those in Palvia et al. [16]. The addition of new topics was made due to the emergence of new research topics and trends in IS research during the 11-year since 2004. Some topics were added at the beginning, a few topics were discovered and added during the coding process, and a few topics were eliminated. The <sup>fi</sup>nal topic classi<sup>fi</sup>cation based on this process is displayed in Table 2.

## 2.2.2. Dimension 2: research methodologies

Palvia et al. [17] developed a classi<sup>fi</sup>cation for research methodologies. This classi<sup>fi</sup>cation was slightly modi<sup>fi</sup>ed in Palvia et al. [15] and is adopted in the present study as shown in Table 3.

## 2.2.3. Dimension 3: research models

Research models are logical representations for describing and explaining the relationships between variables and constructs of interest. According to Levy and Ellis [12], a model or a theoretical framework is “a generalized type of theory that indicates relationships between constructs or latent variables” (p.198). Vessey and V Ramesh [24] developed a classi<sup>fi</sup>cation for research models. Their classi<sup>fi</sup>cation included: listing of variables, in<sup>fl</sup>uence diagram, mathematical model, and combination. This categorization was extended and formalized by Palvia et al. [18]. We use the Palvia et al. [18] categorization as being the latest and most comprehensive to date (Table 4).

## 2.2.4. Dimension 4: paradigmatic research approaches

In a landmark paper, Orlikowski and Baroudi [14] de<sup>fi</sup>ned three major paradigmatic research approaches in information system research: positivist, interpretive, and critical. These approaches are widely used in the literature but do not capture the entire domain of IS research. Therefore, we added “mixed” and “descriptive” to this list. ## 2.2.5. Coding & inter-rater reliability

The articles were reviewed and coded by four doctoral students (who are co-authors of this paper). They reviewed and coded a total of 645 papers from Information & Management from 2004 to 2014, an 11-year period. For comparative purposes, papers were also coded for the same period for Management Information Systems Quarterly (a total of 446 papers) and European Journal of Information Systems (a total of 437 papers). For each paper, the intent was to code the paper with one main topic, one predominant research model, one research methodology, and one research approach. However, it was not always possible as some papers addressed more than one topic and employed more than one model or methodology. Therefore, we allowed up to three topics, two models, two methodologies, and one research approach for each paper. Based on our early coding experience, this extra latitude allowed us to code almost all papers accurately. To identify the topic, we examined each paper based on its title, keywords, and abstract; this method has been used by other IS researchers [7,8,10]. For identifying the codes for the other three dimensions, both the abstract and the paper content were examined following the approach used by Weber [25].

Recommendations provided by Weber [25] and Stemler [21] were followed during the coding process in order to make valid inferences and improve reliability. The coders were trained on coding methods as a part of a doctoral seminar on research methods; this has the effect of improving consistency and uniformity in coding, and reducing ambiguity. Standard de<sup>fi</sup>nitions were prepared for research methodologies, research models, and research approaches in order to reduce misinterpretation [11].

Finally, the reliability of the coding was evaluated using inter-coder reliability [25].

The inter-coder reliability was computed and improved in a two-phase process. In the <sup>fi</sup>rst phase, the doctoral students coded 50 papers and compared them. The reliability results of the <sup>fi</sup>rst phase are shown in Table 5.

A Kappa value in the 61%-80% interval is considered substantial and in the 81%-100% interval is considered almost perfect [21]; therefore we have more than adequate Kappa for all pairs. On the other hand, inter-coder reliability for some pairs was not above the 80% level recommended in the literature. Therefore, the students discussed their results with one another in several sessions for differences and interpretation, in order to reach a common understanding. Then they individually coded another set of 30 articles in the second phase. These results are presented in Table 6. This time, the inter-coder reliabilities were greater than 80%. The Kappa index improved further. Thereafter, the remaining articles were about equally divided among the four students for coding.

The paradigmatic approaches are shown in Table 11. IS research was dominated by the positivist research tradition up until the 1990s [14]. ## 4.2. Research methodology comparison

Table 13 shows the use of research methodology in each journal. Survey is by far the most important methodology that is used by I&M and 53% of all published papers in the journal use this methodology. The case study is the second methodology that is used in I&M published by 8.3% of the total. Field research and laboratory experiment are next in the rankings.

A comparison of research methodologies used in I&M vs. EJIS and MISQ shows signi<sup>fi</sup>cant differences. The case study is the most widely used methodology in EJIS followed by qualitative research; this <sup>fi</sup>nding con<sup>fi</sup>rms the widely held belief that the European researchers are more into qualitative and interpretive work where they attempt to seek in depth examination of phenomena in its natural context. Another noteworthy observation is that MISQ is about equally spread among four methodologies: survey, case study, qualitative research, and laboratory experiment. If we combine case study and qualitative research, the percentage exceeds 20%. Other notable observations include: the high use of <sup>fi</sup>eld research in I&M, the high use of conceptual models in EJIS, and the high use of conceptual models, secondary data, literature analysis, and mathematical modeling in MISQ. Field experiments are utilized very little in any of the three journals.

If a paper uses multiple methodologies, it is an indication of triangulation in evidence generation (although we admit that it is not a perfect proxy). If we use this as the measure, we <sup>fi</sup>nd that while triangulation has been emphasized in the literature, its use remains limited. The triangulation in research published in I&M is around 3% of total published articles. This number is about 6% for both MISQ and EJIS. This number is very low and points to the need for researchers to use multiple methods in their research in order to generate more valid and reliable results.

## 4.3. Research model and approach comparison

Table 14 and Table 15 show the research model and paradigmatic approach that are used in each of the three journals. Multi-tier in<sup>fl</sup>uence diagram is the dominant model in I&M and is the highest in percentage share for both EJIS and MISQ. Both EJIS and MISQ show a high percentage of papers without any model, re<sup>fl</sup>ecting the higher share of qualitative and interpretive papers. Other than these differences, the share of the remaining research models is about the same for the three journals.

A vast majority of I&M papers use the positivist approach. This number is 85% for I&M while it is 51% for EJIS and 60% for MISQ. So while EJIS and MISQ use the positivist approach most often, their share of the interpretive approach is much higher than I&M. This is in conformance of our previous <sup>fi</sup>ndings about the higher use of qualitative methods by EJIS and MISQ, as qualitative methods and the interpretive approach often go hand in hand. Once again, the mixed approach is used rarely suggesting the scarcity of triangulation in IS research. Critical research is almost nonexistent. The descriptive approach is used very little suggesting the growing maturity of the IS <sup>fi</sup>eld, where researchers have moved away from describing phenomena to building and testing theory.

## 4.4. Implications

Papers published in IS journals cover a wide range of topics, methodology, models and approaches. An analysis of past research provides useful implications for various stakeholders of the journals. For the new and inexperienced researchers, it is helpful to know the research pro<sup>fi</sup>le of the journal, such as Information & Management in this case, so that they can direct their energies wisely. The more mature and experienced researcher may wish to focus on projects with more risk, e.g., topics that have received less attention and methodologies that have not been widely used. The three dimensions of methodology, model, and paradigmatic approach and their associated typologies would allow researchers to appreciate that there are alternate methods for conducting research leading to a fuller explanation of the phenomenon under investigation. The same can be said about journal reviewers as they may be predisposed to their own preconceived notions of conducting research. Another important implication is for journal editors. The editors, knowing the past trends, would be able to provide useful guidelines for researchers who want to submit their work to the journal. Furthermore, the results provide the editors with decision tools for analyzing their past performance and setting revised goals for future issues of the journal. Lastly, the readers will have a good and quick synopsis of the research pro<sup>fi</sup>le of the journal and can use it in allocating their limited time to reading selected journal articles.

## 4.5. Limitations

One of limitations of this work is the classi<sup>fi</sup>cation scheme used for the topics of research. While a comprehensive list was developed based on prior lists in the literature and newer additions, we realized that our topic list was not exhaustive and some articles did not <sup>fi</sup>t anywhere. We therefore had to add a few topics during the coding process itself. Another concern is the granularity of the topics; it should not be too large or not too small in order to be meaningful and all topics should have about the same level of granularity. We believe we reached a satisfactory and more importantly a useful level of granularity. Another issue is that the coding of this large number of articles required division of labor and four doctoral students together completed this huge task. While there may be differences in coding, suf<sup>fi</sup>cient agreement was reached as demonstrated by inter-coder reliability tests.

Finally, for comparing and benchmarking I&M, we used only two journals, which are both leading journals. One could argue that it needs to be compared to a larger set of journals; but again the scope of the work limited us to comparing with a leading U.S. and a leading European journal. While MISQ is universally acclaimed as one of the top U.S. journals, ISR is another top journal. In another study (Palvia et al., 2015), it was reported that ISR had similar patterns in topics and methods with a few differences. For example, the top <sup>fi</sup>ve methods in ISR are: secondary data, mathematical modeling, laboratory experiments, surveys, and qualitative research. We <sup>fi</sup>nd these results to be consistent with ISR’s focus and mission.

## 5. Conclusion

This research provides a meta-analytic view of IS research published in Information & Management from 2004 to 2014. Previous such analyses were provided in 2000 and 2007 [4,19]. A four-dimensional framework comprised of topics, research methodologies, research models, and paradigmatic research approaches for classifying research was developed. Using this framework, 645 I&M articles were coded and then analyzed to identify trends and patterns. The most heavily researched topics in I&M during this 11- year period are Electronic Commerce/Business, IS Usage/Adoption, and Knowledge Management. Survey is the dominant research methodology in IS research throughout the entire period. Other methodologies are used less often; but case study, qualitative research, secondary data, and content analysis have seen a rising trend. The positivist approach is the dominant paradigmatic approach used in I&M.

For comparing and benchmarking purposes, we also compared the I&M results with the European Journal of Information Systems and the Management Information Systems Quarterly. There were signi<sup>fi</sup>cant topical differences re<sup>fl</sup>ecting the interests of the various authors and nationalities. A striking observation that EJIS authors more often employ the interpretive approach and qualitative methodologies.

Our study continues the cumulative tradition in meta-analytic research. It contributes to the literature by providing an important overview of IS research in an important journal and comparing it with two other leading journals. As pointed out in the previous section, bene<sup>fi</sup>ciaries of our research include all stakeholders of a journal: readers, authors, editors and reviewers. The self-introspection provided in this study could lead to improvements in all aspects of research published in this journal.
