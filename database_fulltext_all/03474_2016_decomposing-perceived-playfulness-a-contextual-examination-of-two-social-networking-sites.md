# Decomposing Perceived Playfulness: A contextual examination of two social networking sites

## Abstract

Social networking sites (SNSs) are focused on providing playful services for users that will keep them engaged without losing their interest. Perceived playfulness is derived from the user’s subjective evaluation of their interaction with technology and is represented through the sub-dimensions: concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. Results show that there is a positive association between perceived playfulness and their attitude toward using the technology. However, the existing studies do not provide a clear direction about conceptualizing and measuring the sub-dimensions of perceived playfulness. The lack of conclusive evidence, despite the IS literature, shows the need for a more empirical studies investigating the actual effect of the distinct sub-dimensions of perceived playfulness across various contexts. This study develops a context-specific model by decomposing playfulness to completely identify the extent to which specific dimensions are influencing the user’s attitude and further clarify their relative importance. An empirical study was conducted with a total sample size of 551 participants, from two different types of SNSs, to test the context-specific model. The results showed that the relative importance of the sub-dimensions is significantly different between the two SNSs. The findings make the relationship between perceived playfulness and the user’s attitude more context sensitive and provide a useful guide to formulating greater user experience in constructing SNS’s services.

Keywords: Perceived playfulness, Social networking sites, User acceptance, Context-specific model

## 1. Introduction

The rapid advances in social media and the wide application of social networking sites (SNS) usage have changed the way people interact and communicate. The population of social media users has increased to 1.5 billion, and more than 80 percent of them are online community users [15]. Attracting an even larger number of committed users has become a significant issue for administrators. A sufficient number of members with a high participation rate can promote ongoing social interaction to attract potential users and yield user-generated content to sustain the entire community [27, 96]. Users are not only drawn to technology for the expected benefits [6], but also for playful experience [95]. Hence, various behavioral models have been established to study the role of perceived playfulness in the user acceptance of the SNS [26, 39, 40, 67, 72].

IS literature has emerged that offers ambiguous definitions and inconsistent measures about perceived playfulness, resulting in the research findings being difficult to compare and interpret [1, 37]. Most previous works have not clearly conceptualized playfulness as a state of mind or an individual trait in the context of the Internet [37, 66], and the measurement of perceived playfulness has been structured in many different ways such as a single-dimensional construct or a multifaceted construct [41] (See Appendix A). This issue demands that researchers reconcile the measures of perceived playfulness presented by previous studies. Perceived playfulness represents a short-lived subjective experience that resides between a user and an information technology (IT) in which it is temporarily unconstrained and easily modified [95]. We argue that perceived playfulness is represented from the individual’s subjective experience of the interaction with a SNS; therefore, its attribute is a playful state which is inherently represented by concentration, enjoyment and curiosity.

Perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity theoretically represent three correlated yet distinct dimensions. Enjoyment is an emotional construct, whereas the dimensions of concentration and curiosity represent cognitive functions [88]. Based on Csikszeentmihalyi’s flow theory [16], perceived concentration is defined as an individual’s temporal patterning of optimal experience with system usage. Perceived enjoyment is system-dependent and refers to the extent to which the activity of using a specific system is perceived to be enjoyable [77]. Perceived curiosity is often embedded in the aspect of physical reality and is induced by an external stimulus [47, 49]. Without distinguishing these dimensions from one another, the empirical findings would conclude that perceived playfulness, in general, produces biased results. We define perceived playfulness as a superordinate construct given it involves more than one single theoretical dimension. The concept of perceived playfulness will be explicated through an examination of the sub-dimensions. Simply combining concentration each dimension.

Using a SNS is a voluntary behavior with significant influences from interpersonal effects. The Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) explicitly considers the effect of individual attitude as well as social influence and has been applied to different contexts to predict user acceptance of technology. The high predictive validity of the TRA mode serves as an effective model to develop a contextspecific one [29]. Research to date has expanded perceived playfulness into different Internet contexts to successfully predict a user’s attitude [39, 40, 43, 57, 67]. We incorporate perceived playfulness as a context-specific construct to serve as the antecedent of individual attitude in the SNS context.

A SNS is characterized by reduced anonymity and increased offline-to-online social interactions [21, 39]. Empirical results show that the dimensionality of playfulness may not be represented together in explaining a user’s attitude but have an insignificant factor structure across different contexts [1, 40, 57, 79, 89]. Further, the meanings behind a user’s attitude may be similar across different SNSs, but its manifestation may vary in different SNSs. Following Hong et al. [29], the decomposition approach is applied to a study where a certain part of the dimension of perceived playfulness could be considered as more influential when individuals decide to use a particular SNS. This approach allows differential effects of perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity to be apparent without sacrificing theoretical rationalization [7, 29] and make the relationship between perceived playfulness and individual attitude clearer.

A theoretical justification of the attribute of playfulness is necessary because previous works that have studied playfulness have found it difficult to explain its effect across different contexts. Perceived playfulness is a state of mind that will change according to the context of the SNS; the test

of the interaction effect between perceived playfulness and the different types of SNSs provides evidence to explain the inconsistent effects of perceived playfulness regarding a user’s attitude in previous studies and, in turn, enhance the explanatory power of perceived playfulness to specific context. In this study, the reconceptualization of playfulness outlines the scope of its measurements and resolves the conflicting perspectives on measuring it in the IS literature. The empirical results suggest that perceived playfulness may not be estimated in the absence of a dimension such as perceived concentration, enjoyment or curiosity. Compared to the influence of extrinsic motivational factors, our findings successfully prove that perceived playfulness directly accounts for the context distinguishing effects on individual attitude. These findings can be used by administrators in light of the general characteristics of SNSs for constituting greater user acceptance.

We detail the research method, sample collection, a survey with two famous SNSs and then report the research findings. Finally, we discuss the implications of the findings and provide suggestions for academics and associated practitioners.

## 2. Theoretical Background

## 2.1 Perceived playfulness

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) has been proposed to study user acceptance of the utilitarian system from the extrinsic motivational perspectives [17]. The intrinsic motivations often direct behavioral intention more strongly than the extrinsic ones do [84]. Moon and Kim [53] introduced perceived playfulness as an intrinsic motivational factor to influence user acceptance of the World-Wide-Web (WWW). They discovered that when a user perceived playfulness with the WWW the resulting perception would make them concentrate on, and be curious about, the interaction, and would make them feel that such interaction was enjoyable. They successfully combined playfulness

#

with the TAM model and provided insights for the IS community in understanding how playfulness influences a user’s attitude and intention. Their critical findings constitute theoretical support concerning the role of intrinsic factors having a more powerful effect than extrinsic ones. Based on their work, Lin et al.[46] integrated perceived playfulness into the Expectation-Confirmation (ECT) model to explain why people continually use a particular Web portal. A significant relationship between perceived playfulness and satisfaction supported the extended ECT model, showing that perceived playfulness contributed significantly to a users’ intention to reuse a Web portal. They concluded that perceived playfulness is a vital consideration in the design of WWW systems.

Ahn et al. [1] investigated the effect of playfulness on user acceptance of online retailing, and probed the antecedents of playfulness from the perspectives of design quality. Drawing from the IS success model, they identified three Web features: information, system and service quality as considerably influencing the extent of perceived playfulness. They corroborated the findings of previous work that playfulness is one of the salient beliefs in constructing user attitude and intention to use in the Internet context. Chang [9] contributes to the current understanding by empirically investigating the fit between an online auction task and an agent’s technology toward a user’s perceived playfulness on a Website. The results suggested that the Task-Technology Fit (TTF) was significantly related to perceived playfulness. In addition to studying the antecedents and consequences of perceived playfulness, a portion of IS literature has detailed data investigating whether perceived playfulness would have a detrimental effect on a user’s attitude and use intention towards SNSs. The findings showed that the more playfulness they perceived in the IT interaction process, the more likely they would be to use the Website [26, 39, 40, 67, 72].

Although perceived playfulness has been incorporated into different theoretical models, it is often measured from different perspectives in the IS literature, giving rise to some variation in the application of it. We have summarized previous studies entailing the use of it in different contexts (see Appendix A). Many studies have developed different scales for playfulness reflecting the unique aspects of their analysis. Shang et al. [66] adopted cognitive absorption to conceptualize it which they assert is composed of temporal dissociation, focused immersion, enjoyment, control and curiosity.

#

Terzis and Economides [82] conceptualized perceived playfulness with the dimensions of enjoyment and curiosity but overlooked that of concentration. Lin et al. [46] however, used only one dimension, that of concentration, to represent it, omitting the dimensions of enjoyment and curiosity. They viewed playfulness as an individual state and argued that the user would sense different levels of it from different angles when visiting a Web portal. Hsu and Chiu [32] studied the role of perceived playfulness in the context of electronic services. The measures of it were largely addressed according to the degree of enjoyment, failing to consider the dimension of concentration and curiosity. This approach was extensively employed in other previous studies which used only the degree of emotional pleasure to measure perceived playfulness (e.g. [37, 39, 48, 67]). Yet, Turel, Serenko and Bontis [85] reminded researchers that playfulness is dependent on the degree of positive emotional gain and thus serves as a higher level concept of enjoyment. The IS literature shows a lack of understanding of playfulness despite the widespread recognition of the criticality of it.

Research often produces ambiguous definitions of playfulness due to differences among the studies in their treatment, context and measurement instruments. The empirical findings in previous studies have not fully supported the consistency of the dimensionality of perceived playfulness under different contexts. The concept of perceived playfulness itself is a function either as an individual trait [65, 86] or an induced state [48]. For example, Ahn et al. [1] defined perceived playfulness as a shortterm system-specific state and used concentration, enjoyment and curiosity to investigate the effect of it in explaining Web-based online retailing usage. The empirical results indicated that sub-dimensions were loaded distinctly within perceived playfulness. Tan and Chou [78, 79] adopted the same measures and tested the relationship between service quality and compatibility in the context of mobile information and entertainment services. The factor structure of the sub-dimensions converged imperfectly such that perceived concentration had a low correlation with enjoyment and curiosity. The interdependence among sub-dimensions is inconsistent and may not occur at the same time [40, 57, 89], but none of the prior studies could explain the observed discrepancy.

## 2.2 The reconceptualization of perceived playfulness

To address the concerns above, we have clarified the attributes of perceived playfulness in the context of SNSs. Webster et al. [90] characterize playfulness as a motivational characteristic of individuals (trait) or a subjective characteristic of an experience (state). The difference is that a trait is a stable characteristic of an individual that is relatively invariant to situational stimuli. The trait of playfulness means it would not have interaction with the situational factors and is less likely to change at various points in time [90]. They explain that the trait of playfulness is a relatively enduring tendency to interact playfully with technology (system-independence), while the state of playfulness is a temporary state with the technology (system-dependence) [37, 90]. We argue that perceived playfulness suits a temporal evaluation since it emerges from the interaction between the user and the particular technology [22, 37, 95]. The attribute of perceived playfulness corresponds to a playful state; the users configure their evaluation based upon their interaction with the SNS which involves a series of positive or negative usage experiences. The positive interaction between users and the SNS shapes them into a state of “feeling good” and they become absorbed in their online activities. As a result, perceived playfulness is a playful state of the individual’s subjective experience with the interaction with a SNS.

The majority of the IS literature on the conceptualization of playfulness is based on the flow theory. We characterize the relationship between the flow experience and the dimensions of playfulness at Figure 1. The reconceptualization of perceived playfulness at the center of the Venn diagram is our major contribution to the flow theory. Flow is expected to occur when individuals adequate capabilities to engage in such activity [16, 41, 62, 71]. Based on the flow theory, when people are engaged in the state of flow, their attention will be focused on the current activity and they will filter out irrelevant perceptions, causing them to totally concentrate on the activity [16, 41, 55]. The relationship between playfulness and concentration is according to the central argument of the flow theory such that the playful experience is represented through a high degree of concentration on the activity due to undivided attention to a stimulus. Individuals entering an optimal state of playful experience will be completely engaged in the activity so that almost nothing will distract them from it. Thus, we conceptualize that an individual who is experiencing a playful state in interaction with a SNS will exhibit concentration.

Another important positive feeling from the flow experience that will be produced is enjoyment [41, 54]. A strong flow experience contributes to the way that individuals perceive enjoyment because they reach the desirability of optimal experience resulting in a positive effect toward such activity [37, 54]. A specific feature of playful experience is that the individual will respond with pleasure toward the activity especially when they perceive the interaction with it is positive [16]. On the other hand, if the interaction is negative (i.e. a family crisis), the individual will not perceive such an activity as fun or enjoyable [51, 55]. Assuming using a SNS is an enjoyable activity; enjoyment is the emotional outcome that results from the playful experience. We apply this logic to the conceptualization of playfulness; when one concludes the interaction with a SNS is playful, enjoyment is the emotional outcome of it.

To completely engage in an activity, one must possess an enthusiastic interest so s/he can continue to explore new information without an awareness of the present moment and enjoy the experience utterly [55]. Curiosity is embedded in the mind and it will be stimulated when the individual becomes absorbed in an activity [49]. Playful experience takes place in the interaction with a SNS causing the individuals to act in ways that allow their thoughts and experiences to develop more clearly, thereby promoting a curiosity for specific information. Surprises and new activities will cause people to be more focused and their senses heightened, partly because the less familiar they are with a new activity the longer it may take for them to reorganize the new information in a way that they can understand it [49]. According to the flow theory, curiosity is complementary to concentration and enjoyment but it has been under-represented in prior studies [53, 55, 71]. When an individual is engaged in an activity s/he is very interested in disclosing more details and becomes an active seeker of new information without consciously being aware of how the time was spent. If such activity can lead to greater exploration and discovery, the activity is expected to deepen the enjoyment [54]. In this case, we conceptualize that an individual who is experiencing a playful state in the interaction with a SNS will become increasingly curious.

By reviewing previous studies, we acknowledge that the measurements of perceived playfulness in previous studies are ill-defined by including inconsistent dimensions. Some treat perceived playfulness as a single-dimensional construct, whereas others treat it as a multifaceted construct. We view that perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity theoretically represent three interdependent yet distinct dimensions. The common variance shared by each of the three dimensions is defined as perceived playfulness. Perceived playfulness is a superordinate construct that is manifested by including perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. Following this, if any one of the dimensions is absent, then it does not capture the overall meaning of perceived playfulness.

## 2.3 Professional Technology Temple and Facebook

SNSs can be classified by their shared characteristics and the purposes of their registered members. Previous studies have outlined the different types and activities utilized by the members. However, a particular SNS may fall into more than one definition, or may overlap with others in terms of its characteristics and functionalities. The differences are based on how the users perceive and use the SNSs. The two SNSs, Professional Technology Temple (PTT) and Facebook, were selected for comparative study because they are very popular in Taiwan and have different characteristics and functionalities.

PTT is the largest bulletin board system in Taiwan with more than one million registered users. Facebook’s popularity has grown over recent years, and have about 14 million active Taiwanese users in 2014 (the whole population in Taiwan is 23 million). PTT with the same intention of Reddit, which is a message board system where users offer up-to-date information and in-depth discussions, and the stream of content is curated by the users. PTT contains more than twenty thousand different billboard topics, more than twenty thousand new online posts, and five hundred thousand article commendations and criticisms (a limited test-based response such as upvote/downvote comments in Reddit) are published daily. While PTT users are anonymous, and do not need to create a profile to reveal their real identity, they are more focused on the content and are less likely to care about “who you are” (i.e., Hubermanet al. [35]). Similar with Reddit, with the safety of anonymity in online discussions, users have more freedom to think more open-mindedly, critically, or with more curiosity than they would offline, and with less social pressure, it is safer for them to express their own views [14].

Facebook provides users with a sense that their friends are easy to reach and identify if they want to disclose personal feelings and emotions in order to build a relationship or offer family support. A user should have an authentic identity on Facebook, and invite his or her friends to join the network by inviting them in real life, one individual at a time, and by importing a list of contacts from other Steinfield and Lampe [21] reported that most users use Facebook to maintain existing offline relationships. The tendency to form a personal social network on Facebook is largely started with shared social networks (similarity, familiarity and past friendship), which keeps a sense of perceived closeness and contributes to users’ perceptions of the SNS as an intimate space [20, 36].

We recognize that both perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are different contextual features in PTT and Facebook. Perceived usefulness is considered as a salient contextual feature in

#

Facebook. Perceived usefulness is defined as the degree to which an individual believes that using a SNS can effectively fulfill his/her personal requirements for information seeking or social relationships. Facebook has the ability to effectively provide customized content to each user. Facebook users can adjust what they want to see on their wall by filtering out the information that they no longer want and selectively following information sources that they think are personally interesting. This design increases customization to arrange their updates for the specific audiences with whom they enjoy and choose to discuss and decide what information sources they want to follow.

Perceived ease of use is considered a salient contextual feature in PTT. Perceived ease of use is defined as the degree to which an individual believes that using a SNS is effortless. The information shared on the PPT billboards is freely accessible and public to all users so topics are extensively discussed by heterogeneous individuals, thus the content can not be specifically customized for a user’s specific needs and interests. One will be more likely to believe that advantages result from using PTT when s/he could easily start a discussion on a specific topic or express his/her viewpoint on an existing topic. However, PPT is a bulletin board system and the interface is text-based. Users need to understand how to use keyboard commands to perform functions from one topic to the next or to response and enter a message. Thus, only when a user perceives that using PTT is easy and convenient, will s/he will further develop a tendency to perceive PTT as useful.

Although perceived playfulness and perceived ease of use are not mutually exclusive, perceived usefulness is made more salient by the presence of customized content in Facebook, since it allows users to salient in $\mathrm { P T T , }$ because achieving the expected benefits is dependent on whether the user can perceive an efficient way of interacting with the system. Ridings and Gefen [63] indicated that different types of online communities will behave differently and have their own characteristics. The unfolding playful state is subjected by the interaction between an individual’s experience and the given environmental feature [53]. The users will respond according to the characteristics of the SNS and experience different depths of interaction.

## 3. Research Model and Hypotheses

We utilize Hong’s contextual-theorizing framework to develop a context-specific model for SNS context. First, we select TRA to guide the development of a context-specific model. Based upon the TRA model [3], intention is an individual’s conception formed by directing his/her attitude toward the behavior and the normative influence [3]. Attitude is an overall evaluation of a certain stimulus, which guides individual behavior toward that stimulus [3, 95]. Normative influence has been regarded as a critical factor that can enhance a user’s acceptance of online interactive technology [30, 31]. This study includes social norms, which is the normative influence of one individual upon his or her peers, as a positive driver of attitude and intention. As TRA is a well-established model that has been validated in different contexts and the use of SNS is a voluntary behavior, TRA is suitable to capture individual use behavior. Secondly, TRA model is contextualized to the SNS context. We identify playfulness as a context-specific construct for individual attitude. We treat perceived ease of use (PEOU) and perceived usefulness (PU) as control variables given that Moon and Kim’s study [53] has already shown that these two factors will influence an individual’s attitude toward WWW acceptance. Lastly, as noted earlier, we conceptualize perceived playfulness is manifested by including perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity and further propose that perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity as the salient context-specific factors for attitude.

The hypotheses development in this study is focused on modeling the context-specific factors and examination of the interaction between the context-specific factors and the contextual features. First, to formulate a context-sensitive version of a user’s attitude, we decompose perceived playfulness into perceived concentration, enjoyment, and curiosity in order to understand the differential effects on a user’s attitude (i.e. H1a-c). These context-specific factors are included in the refined TRA model. Secondly, we examine the interaction between the context-specific factors and the contextual features (i.e. H2a-c). The types of SNSs act as moderators to explain the interaction effect of perceived playfulness on individual attitude. A multi-group analysis using users from PPT and Facebook permits this study to explain the different effects of context-specific factors on users’ attitudes. The first dimension within perceived playfulness is concentration. A reasonable attention span is a requirement for experiencing flow [16]. Webster et al. [91] denoted that the state of flow is a specific form of human-computer interaction, and will be influenced by the characteristics of the IT. Smith and Sivakumar [73] proposed that the optimal state will be moderated by the Website characteristic because it has the ability to virtually provide convenience functions to customers, which in turn reduce the cost of gathering further information and make them engaged in such activities. An argument can be made for the relationship between perceived ease of use and perceived concentration because systems that are difficult to use are more likely to interrupt user’s concentration [33]. Given that perceived ease of use is the salient contextual feature in PTT, the current users will only consider using PPT when it is effort-free to them. This contextual feature not only reduces the perceived effort of searching for information but also increases goal-relevant stimuli to support users effortlessly engaged in an activity [1, 50]. Rettie [62] further stated that “attention was more focused in a goaldirected activity”. In this context, individual’s experiences seamlessly unfold from moment to moment and enter a state of focused concentration on the activity [55]. Based on these arguments, the effect of perceived concentration on attitude is expected to be moderated by the different types of SNSs. As for decomposing individual attitude into three distinct dimensions, the hypothesis can be restated as follows:

H1a: Perceived concentration on a SNS will have a positive influence on attitude.

H2a: The effect of perceived concentration on attitude will be moderated by the type of SNS, such that the effect of it on attitude will be higher on PTT than on Facebook

The second dimension within perceived playfulness is enjoyment. The findings of prior literature support the view that the influence of perceived enjoyment in relationship to intended use will be moderated by using different devices. Xu et al. [93] stated that using high-hedonic technologies will enhance the influence of perceived enjoyment on intended use. Further, users will perceive higher enjoyment when they use ICT-based services which are characterized by hedonic benefits, and perceive less enjoyment when they use services which are characterized by utilitarian benefits [56]. Given that user can customize the audience and subscribe to their preferred updates in Facebook, the current users should perceive that Facebook can help them to effectively achieve the expected benefits. The customized function can make interface more interactive to the users, thereby increasing the perceived enjoyment [19]. Further, enjoyment is likely to increase with customization, because users can be better able to experience the content they can manipulate [19]. Empirical results indicated that when users are capable of making their own choices in online services, they will evaluate such experiences positively [19]. Most importantly, this positive experience will provide greater enjoyment to the user because they may enjoy the increased independence and flexibility in making their own aesthetic decisions [18]. Based on these arguments, the effect of perceived enjoyment on attitude is expected to be moderated by the different types of SNSs. As for decomposing individual attitude into three distinct dimensions, the hypothesis can be restated as follows:

H1b: Perceived enjoyment of a SNS will have a positive influence on attitude.

H2b: The effect of perceived enjoyment on attitude will be moderated by the type of SNS, such that the effect of it will be lower on PTT than on Facebook.

The third dimension within perceived playfulness is curiosity. Curiosity reflects part of a transitory state in which users are influenced by the character of Web environments [34]. Smock and Holt [74] affirmed that the discrepancy between the cognitive structure and the environmental-event processes constitutes the motivational basis for curiosity. Smock and Holt [74] confirmed that different types of novelties arouse differential curiosity. The same finding was also confirmed in the users’ imaginations and capture their curiosity. The state of individual curiosity varies frequently and considerably and is influenced by the presence of different human-computer interactions [4]. A user’s desire to explore and discover the unknown in a system is expected by influencing his/her perceived ease of use of the system. If the same level of actual effort invested, curiosity will be piqued, as in the case of an easy to use condition, where users could more efficiently discover new information. Perceived ease of use is the contextual feature in PTT because effort on their part is minimal. This implies that when users have higher levels of perceived ease of use, it will lead to lowered perceptions of effort in exploration, which in turn, will likely induce more desire to discover and learn. In such a condition, one has lower perceptions of the effort it will take to satisfy their knowledge, and to more pleasurably explore what s/he wants to know without a lot of effort. Based on these arguments, the effect of perceived curiosity on attitude is expected to be moderated by the different types of SNSs. As for decomposing individual attitude into three distinct dimensions, the hypothesis can be restated as follows:

## H1c: Perceived curiosity on a SNS will have a positive influence on attitude.

H2c: The effect of perceived curiosity on attitude will be moderated by the type of SNS, such that the effect of it will be higher on PTT than on Facebook.

Attitude refers to the positive feeling about a particular behavior. When more preferences toward using a SNS are perceived by the users, the higher the behavioral intention generated [3]. Social norms provides the extent to which the user perceives that his or her acquaintances/friends approve of their use of a particular technology [3]. Empirical studies have found that social influence positively influences both individual attitude and intended use [31]. The effects and degrees of social factors will have varying impacts upon influencing a user’s intention when s/he uses different ICT-based services [56]. They may use a SNS to strengthen their social ties, by joining a group of close friends who share common interests [21]. Participants use Facebook as a platform to solidify existing offline relationships [21]. It is reasonable to expect that social norms is a more important driver for Facebook than for PTT.

The relative lack of social presence on PTT minimizes the role of social norms. Those who are aware of high levels of social norms tend to be more involved with their social network and get together more frequently with their friends [21, 31]. In light of the characteristics exhibited by this phenomenon, we would expect the relationship between social norms and attitude to be greater for Facebook, than for PTT. Likewise, the relationship between social norms and intended usage would be greater in the former than in the latter. The hypothesis can be restated as follows:

## H3: Social norms will have a positive influence on attitude.

H4: The effect of social norms on attitude will be moderated by the type of SNS, such that the effect of social norms on attitude will be greater with Facebook than with PTT.

H5: Social norms will have a positive influence on intended use.

H6: The effect of social norms on intended use will be moderated by the type of SNS, such that the effect of social norms on intended use will be greater with Facebook than with PTT.

H7: Attitude will have a positive influence on intended use.

## 4. Research method

## 4.1. Measures

All of the measurement items were adapted from previous studies and modified to fit the context. The measurement of perceived playfulness consists of nine items which can be summarized into three dimensions: concentration, enjoyment and curiosity [53]. Perceived playfulness was measured as the degree to which an individual perceives concentration, enjoyment and curiosity toward using a particular SNS. Concentration is the extent to which an individual pays attention to the SNS interactions. Enjoyment is the extent to which an individual perceives interest in the SNS interactions. Curiosity is the extent to which an individual’s curiosity is aroused during SNS interactions.

Attitude was measured as the degree to which an individual experiences positive or negative feelings toward using a given SNS [3]. Social norms was measured as the extent to which an individual perceives social persuasion from his or her friends or prior adopters [3, 5]. Intended use was measured as the degree to which an individual intends to use a given SNS [87]. In terms of the two control variables, perceived ease of use was addressed by measuring the degree to which an individual believes that using a SNS is effortless [17]. Perceived usefulness was addressed by measuring the degree to which an individual believes that using a SNS will fulfill personal requirements for information and social relationships [17].

All of the measurement items used in this study were subjected to pretesting and pilot-testing using experts and relevant samples. Participators involved in the pretest and pilot-test were not included in the main sample. This study invited two MIS professionals and four MIS Ph.D. students to evaluate the proper use of the measurement items and the translation issue in the pre-test stage. In order to pursue the content validity, unclear and wordy sentences were refined based on their feedback. Twenty master’s degree students who had had experience in participating in SNSs were engaged in the pilot-test. According to their suggestions, we worked to eliminate redundant phrases and some of the ambiguous items were modified to improve the overall clarity. All measurement items are shown in Appendix B and seven-point Likert scales ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree” were incorporated.

## 4.2. Data collection

This study targeted users who had joined PTT and Facebook. PTT is the largest bulletin board system in Taiwan, and Facebook is the most popular SNS for users to interact with their friends [21]. We released our Web-based questionnaire to PTT and Facebook where registered members could access the hyperlink to answer our online questionnaire. The Web-based questionnaire started with an introduction page in which we explained the purpose of this study and the benefits of the results to SNSs. At the end of the introduction page, we provided contact information for respondents to ask any questions they might have prior to filling in the questionnaire.

This study offered financial incentives for respondents upon completion of the survey. (Participants were given the opportunity to win ten \$500NT (\$16US) convenience store gift vouchers). Their registered account, IP address and email were recorded in the log file to prevent the possibility of multiple submissions. After we disqualified 44 incomplete questionnaires, the remaining 551 valid samples available for data analysis consisted of 358 respondents from PTT and 193 from Facebook. Descriptive details are presented in Table 1. The homogeneity test was applied to the demographic data between the two SNSs. The chi-square test showed there was no significant difference between the PTT and Facebook respondents at 95% confidence level in terms of gender,

age, education and social networking usage experience (chi<sub>‐</sub>square $( \mathrm { x } ^ { 2 } )$ ranged from 0.006 to 5.469),

which suggested our sample characteristics are equally distributed across the two sites.

We compared the characteristics of the respondents to known characteristics of the population to provide an indication of a possible non-response bias. Most respondents were between 21-30 years old, and the gender proportions showed males slightly exceeded females. The distribution pattern of the respondents’ gender and age were both close to the known characteristics of Taiwanese online community users [83], implying that our sample was a fair representation. We further examined how respondents might differ from non-respondents by comparing early to late ones on the constructs we measured. The t-test analysis indicated no significant differences so our samples were not different from those not included in this study (t-value ranged from -0.170 to 1.245).

## 5. Results

For the data analysis, XLSTAT 2010-Partial Least Squares Path Modeling (PLSPM) software was utilized. This is a component-based path modeling software for explaining construct relationships. Along with bootstrap re-sampling techniques, it is used to obtain parameter estimates and confidence intervals. First of all we conducted a principal component analysis (PCA) with varimax rotations to demonstrate the distinctions among the sub-dimensions. The results indicated that three dimensions emerged with no serious cross-loadings present (see Appendix C). Prior to the test measurement model, Harman’s one-factor analysis had been applied to detect a common method bias. If there is a substantial common method bias, one general factor will account for the majority of the covariance among the measurement items [59]. The Harman’ analysis extracted five factors to explain the overall variance in eight constructs; the first factor only accounted for 41.12% of the total variance, showing no dominant one. Further, this study respectively performed a CFA test and a latent method variable test for detecting a common method bias on all of the measurement items. First, we conducted the eight-factors CFA model and then compared its chi-square with a one-dimensional method variable which jointly consisted of 24 items. The results (see Appendix D) demonstrated that the eight-factors CFA did fit the data significantly better than the one-dimensional method variable

model did $( \triangle \mathrm { x } ^ { 2 } = 2 1 4 7 . 6 6 , \mathrm { d . f . } = 2 8 , \mathrm { p } { < } 0 . 0 0 1 )$ . Therefore, a common method bias was not a serious

## 5.1. Measurement model

There are two analytical procedures in PLS methodology. First, examining convergent validity and discriminant validity are required for the measurement model. Secondly, estimating the structural path and the explanatory power are required for the structural model. In addition, reliability is a measurement of internal consistency which is used to assess whether the measurement items within the respective constructs have an equal contribution. The results are presented in Table 2. All constructs have adequate reliability, as the composite reliability and Cronbach's alpha are both above the recommended level of 0.7 [23].

To demonstrate convergent validity, each item is considered acceptable if the loading above 0.7 within their associated constructs is significant [24]. In addition, each construct has to have an average variance extracted (AVE) higher than 0.5. When accessing convergent validity, one of the four items measuring perceived usefulness was lower than 0.7. Hence, we excluded this item from further analysis. As seen in the results presented in Table 3, the remaining items were significantly loaded above 0.7 in their associated constructs, and the AVE in each construct was higher than the recommended threshold of 0.5. Examining the square root of AVE with the correlation between the given construct with the other constructs proved discriminant validity [12]. The results are presented in Table 4. The square root of the AVE for each construct was greater than the correlations between them. Consequently, the results from the above analytical procedures concluded that the measurement items have sufficient convergent validity and discriminant validity.

An independent two-sample t-test is used to compare the mean scores of PU and PEOU for PTT and Facebook. The results indicated that there was a statistically significant difference between the mean scores of PU and PEOU for PTT and Facebook along with the 95% confidence intervals. Specifically, PTT has a statistically significantly higher mean score on PEOU (5.73) than Facebook (5.59), whereas Facebook has a statistically significantly higher mean score on PU (5.39) than PTT (5.21). The results supported that PEOU is the more salient factor than PU in presenting the contextual feature of PTT, and PU is the more salient factor than PEOU in presenting the contextual feature of Facebook.

## 5.2. Structural model

Since convergent and discriminant validity have been established for the measurement model, we followed the guidelines proposed by Wright et al. [92] to structure the test of the superordinate construct. The standardized latent variable scores for perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity were used as indicators of perceived playfulness. We thus created the superordinate construct and incorporated it with the TRA model to test the hypotheses by using the whole sample. The t-value of each path was estimated through bootstrap 1,000 re-sampling.

The path coefficient of each hypothesis and the significance levels are presented in Figure 3. Overall, all structural paths were significant and the model explained 58 percent of the variance in attitude (the explanatory power > 0.8) and 57 percent of the variance in intended use (the explanatory power $> 0 . 8 )$ , indicating that the model fitted well with the data. Regarding the superordinate construct, the path coefficients between the first-order dimensions, perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity, and the superordinate construct were 0.803, 0.906 and 0.886 respectively. These results showed justification for the use of the superordinate construct since it demonstrates a good fit with the data [60, 92]. The results also indicated that perceived playfulness was positively related to attitude $( \beta = 0 . 5 5 7 , \ p < 0 . 0 1 )$ . Further, social norms was positively related to both attitude (β $= 0 . 1 5 9 , \mathtt { p } < 0 . 0 1 )$ and intended use $( \beta = 0 . 1 4 0 , \ p < 0 . 0 1 )$ . Finally, attitude was positively related to intended use $( \beta = 0 . 6 6 5 , \ p < 0 . 0 1 )$ . The results demonstrated that TRA model is suitable to be used as a general theory to SNS context, and the effect of context-specific factor, perceived playfulness, is identified in the TRA model.

Since this study considered perceived playfulness as a context-specific construct for individual attitude, we decomposed individual attitude into perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. This decomposition approach overcomes the drawback of monolithic structures of general beliefs and clarifies the relationships between perceived playfulness and individual attitude [29]. All of these dimensions could therefore be modeled as direct predictors of individual attitude. The results at Figure 4 indicated that perceived concentration $( \beta = 0 . 1 4 9 , \ p < 0 . 0 1 )$ , perceived enjoyment $( \beta = 0 . 2 0 5 , \rho <$ 0.01) and perceived curiosity $( \beta = 0 . 2 8 6 , \ p < 0 . 0 1 )$ were positively significant with attitude. In summary, the results suggest that hypotheses 1a, 1b and 1c are all supported. Consequently, attitude toward using a SNS was apparently influenced by perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. Further, the effect of social norms was positively significant with attitude. Hypothesis 3 is therefore supported. Lastly, hypotheses 5 and 7 are both supported in the structural model. Overall, the results indicated that the effects of perceived enjoyment and curiosity on attitude were greater than either perceived concentration or social norms.

## 5.3. Multi-group analysis

#

The multi-group test was adopted to further diagnose the equality of the paths between PTT and Facebook with regard to perceived concentration, enjoyment, curiosity and social norms on attitude, as well as the effect of social norms on intended use. The multi-group test is a suitable approach for examining the moderating effect in the case of nominal moderating variables [2, 8]. Because we proposed different types of SNSs as a nominal moderating variable and intended to compare th equality of the hypothesized paths, we conducted multi-group analysis and tested for differenc between PTT and Facebook by means of the XLSTAT 2010-PLSPM multi-group analysis.

We followed the procedures for multi-group PLS analysis as defined by Chin et al. [13]. The test of measurement invariance is required prior to examining the structural paths between the constructs of interest across the different groups [52]. Measurement invariance refers to the test of the lack of variance between the items used in the context of the two groups [11, 75]. Configural invariance is used to test whether the same basic factor model can hold for the two different groups [11, 75]. Metric invariance is used to test the extent to which the loadings between the factors and the items are equivalent across groups [11, 75]. Configural and metric invariance were assessed in this study to ensure the items exhibited adequate equivalence across the two SNSs. The configural invariance model exhibited a good model fit (chi-square (x<sup>2</sup>) = 950.72; df = 448; CFI = 0.939; RMSEA = 0.045), showing that the factor structure was equivalent. The metric invariance model did not fit significantly worse than the configural invariance model $( \triangle \mathrm { x } ^ { 2 } = 2 0 ; \mathrm { d f } = 1 6 ; \mathrm { p } = 0 . 2 2 )$ , implying that the item loadings were equal for each group. These analyses showed that measurement invariance was obtained across both groups.

A comparison of structural paths was then conducted in two steps. First, the structural paths of the proposed models were analyzed separately. All structural paths were significant for PTT while two were insignificant for Facebook. Second, by taking the structural paths and the standard error from the PLS analysis results, we calculated the adapted t-test for the difference in perceived concentration, enjoyment, curiosity and social norms [13]. If the difference between the structural paths was significant, it could be recognized that this structural path was significantly different across the two SNSs [8, 13, 61]. The results of the multi-group test on each structural path are shown in Figure 6 and Figure 7.

The results in Figure 5 supported that there was a difference in the relationship between perceived playfulness and individual attitude (Difference = 0.137, t = 1.652, p < 0.1). The effect of social norms on intended use (Difference = 0.285, t = 3.823, p < 0.01) showed a significant difference lending support only for H6. In the PPT group, the effect of PEOU on attitude was significant but the effect of PU on attitude was insignificant. In the Facebook group, the effect of PU on attitude was significant but the effect of PEOU on attitude was insignificant<sup>1</sup>. The results in Figure 6 supported the fact that perceived concentration, enjoyment, curiosity were three distinct yet related dimensions and that each one of them has a different effect on individual attitude in the two SNSs. The effect of perceived concentration on attitude was significantly higher on PTT (Difference = 0.216, t = 2.987, p = 0.003). The effect of perceived enjoyment on attitude was significantly greater on Facebook (Difference= 0.296, t = 2.972, $\mathsf { p } = 0 . 0 0 3 )$ , while the effect of perceived curiosity on attitude was significantly greater on PTT (Difference= 0.227, t = 2.521, p = 0.012). These results supported H2a, H2b and H2c and indicated that there were significant differences in the influences among concentration, enjoyment, curiosity and the user’s attitude.

## 6. Discussion

The results provide a clear understanding of the role of perceived playfulness in explaining the attitude toward using SNSs. We summarized the contextual findings of perceived playfulness in the

#

Table 4. In the review of IS literature, little attention has been paid to the conceptualization of perceived playfulness in the context of SNS. According to finding (1), our evidence shows that perceived playfulness has a more significant effect on attitude than either perceived usefulness or ease of use. It has proven to be profoundly involved in the architecture of attitude toward using SNSs. Generally, the SNS interfaces are becoming more user-friendly and require only limited experience to benefit to users. Therefore, in this unique context, providing a high level of ease-of-use and usefulness may not largely increase users’ positive attitudes as much as a focus on delivering a playful experience to them.

The effect of perceived playfulness on attitude is distinct between PTT and Facebook. Unlike the supposition of traditional IS literature that views playfulness as being system independent, based on the finding (2), we discovered that perceived playfulness is not a fixed structure, but undergoes significant changes as shown by the comparison between PTT and Facebook. Further, finding (3) represents constituent dimensions of attitude and reveals the actual effects of perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity on attitude. Curiosity is crucial as it reflects a desire to close the knowledge gap in the context of SNS. The explosion of new information available on SNS has sparked users’ interests and made them want to explore further. They are free to know and learn, and thus develop an increasingly positive attitude toward using SNS. The possible interactions between perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity and the contextual features are elucidated in the finding (4). The results indicate that the formation of a positive attitude toward using PTT and Facebook does not refer to the same sub-dimensions of perceived playfulness. In summary, the three sub-dimensions, concentration, enjoyment and curiosity all have an influence on a user’s attitude but the relative importance of them is inconsistent across the different types of SNSs. This reiterates the statement from Moon and Kim [53], which implies that sub-dimensions are not always harmonious in a user’s mind; suggesting that one dimension alone cannot truly reflect one’s playful experience with technology.

## 7. Implications

## 7.1. Implications for research

According to the extent of our knowledge, this study is the first to empirically test the effect of the sub-dimensions of perceived playfulness and compare their relative importance across different contexts. Our findings have several implications for existing IS research. First, previous studies exhibit some variation with respect to the definition, application and measurement of perceived playfulness [95]. It is being considered either as a trait or the state of an individual without a clear direction. Without investigating the effect of perceived playfulness between two types of SNSs, there has been little consensus on how it should be defined in the IS literature. Results show that the effect

#

of perceived playfulness on attitude is divergent from Facebook to PTT. Since state and trait have very different assumptions, it is necessary to first delineate perceived playfulness as a state or a trait before the incorporation of playfulness into an individual behavioral model. Perceived playfulness should be regarded as a state of mind in the context of a SNS and will change according to different stimuli (cf. [95]). This implies that the effect of concentration, enjoyment and curiosity on a user’s attitude can be influenced by different situational factors. The findings will be of value to IS literature in closing the ambiguous definition of perceived playfulness to enhance its reliability and validity via an empirical study.

Secondly, we have extended the IS literature on the actual effect of sub-dimensions within perceived playfulness. Using the three sub-dimensions together to present playful experience suggests some progress in IS research. Our results imply that to adopt only a single dimension would significantly misinterpret the effect of perceived playfulness on an individual’s attitude. For example, the relationship between playfulness and attitude could be underestimated if one adopts concentration to surrogate the overall playfulness on Facebook, but the relationship would be overestimated in the presence of the information seeking process. To avoid such a possible bias, researchers should realize that playfulness is not the mere presence of one dimension but is comprised of multi-faceted dimensions. Based on the flow theory, we developed the construct of perceived playfulness and the way that we proposed to operationalize it as a superordinate construct has been successfully supported by the empirical findings [60, 92]. A superordinate construct of perceived playfulness does not explain less variance in a user’s attitude toward SNS than does its individual dimensions. Hence, perceived playfulness acts as a superordinate construct behind the three distinct but related first-order constructs. The key here is that concentration, enjoyment and curiosity should all be achieved for the IT interaction to be characterized as perceived playfulness. This is important, since IS researchers need to share a common understanding of the multi-faceted dimensions of perceived playfulness if they are extensively using it in the context of SNSs.

Thirdly, viewing perceived playfulness as an emotional pleasure is a common perspective shared by a number of previous researchers [37]. Results highlight the fact that enjoyment is essential for the

#

prediction of a user’s attitude, enabling us to understand why most previous studies have only adopted perceived enjoyment as a surrogate for perceived playfulness, yielding similar results in different contexts. However, according to the flow theory, Moon and Kim’s notion [53] of perceived playfulness refers to the subjective evaluation of the WWW through the representation of concentration, enjoyment and curiosity in the human-IT interaction process; it goes beyond enjoyment to also include concentration and curiosity. As a flow state it is a self-justifying experience and may not only result from intrinsically motivated activities (autotelic activities) but also from extrinsically motivated ones (work and exams) [51, 55]. It is possible that one becomes focused in a working meeting but experiences negative emotions which, in turn, results in distress and inhibits the presence of enjoyable feelings. SNSs are different to other forms of Internet usage; users are intertwined with their participation in intrinsically and extrinsically motivated activities with heterogeneous friends. To treat perceived playfulness as a single dimension of enjoyment apart from a multi-faceted construct requires the theoretical justifications about the activity undertaken by the individual in the SNS (e.g. [88]). In the absence of such justifications, IS research will continue to offer fragmented perspectives and contradictory findings.

Fourthly, our findings formulate a context-specific model to examine the user acceptance of SNSs. Perceived playfulness contributes to the distinguishing effect to explain an individual’s attitude. The general model of TRA by only including perceived useful and perceived ease of use may not fully capture the characteristics of SNSs. Our results highlight the importance of considering perceived playfulness in SNS acceptance research in order to make the general model more context sensitive. A context-specific version of attitude is developed in this study; it presents the actual effects of the sub-dimensions of perceived playfulness and breaks down the monolithic structures of a user’s attitude toward SNSs to ensure that his or her attitude is refereeing different context-specific factors on either PTT or Facebook. The decomposed model shows an alternative view of the relationship between perceived playfulness and a user’s attitude, and the theoretical relationship is becoming more readily understood.

#

Fifthly, the interaction effect explored in this study helps explain the inconsistent effects of perceived playfulness regarding a user’s attitude in previous studies, such as some studies find it has a large influence on attitude in the outcome-specific context (i.e. IPTV and SkypeOut) [45, 68] whereas others find it has a small influence on attitude in the effort-specific context (i.e. Online tax filing) [32]. Perceived playfulness is a strong predictor of attitude, particularly when it is measured by using the concept of enjoyment in the outcome-specific context where perceived usefulness is a salient contextual feature as users evaluate the system in terms of how it can effectively provide the expected entertaining content and pleasure experience. In this case, “enjoyable experience” becomes a user’s major expectation regarding the eventual consequences of interacting with the system. The enjoyment concept can therefore, better capture how the system helps users achieve he pleasure-oriented outcomes, which in turn formulate positive attitude. In contrast, in the effo context where perceived ease of use is a salient contextual feature, users evaluate the system in terms of how easily the system can be used and how many courses of actions are required to achieve the expected outcome. In this case, “effortlessness” becomes as a user’s major expectation regarding the interaction with the system. Thus, the enjoyment concept, of perceived playfulness, can only capture a small degree of variance regarding how the system helps users achieve the pleasure-oriented outcomes by building positive attitude to use the system.

Lastly, we focus on the interaction between the different types of SNSs and perceived playfulness, and the results confirm that investigating the interaction between context-specific factors and the different types of SNSs can better explain how a user’s attitude is positively formed. The results indirectly respond to Hong et al’s work [29] that calls for attention in the understanding of the interaction between the context-specific factors and the domain-specific factor. The findings provide a stable set of context-specific factors in regard to the formation of a positive attitude which can be applied to different SNS settings. This mitigates difficulties that have occurred in establishing a set of context-specific factors to attitude. By focusing on these factors, the explanatory power of perceived playfulness can therefore be increased.

## 7.2. Implications for practitioners

#

SNS administrators should develop their own strategies to increase the number of committed users. An effective marketing strategy would attract online users’ attention and keep them coming back for more. The administrator should firstly assess the orientation of the SNS and promote the corresponding marketing activities that would encourage users to engage in playful activity. Using a self-assessment method should enable them to understand if the current operational model fits the needs and whether further applications could be implemented in order to increase the user’s concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. Overall, provoking curiosity is the most significant dimension behind the use of a SNS, because it encourages users to explore additional information, thereby closing their information gap which in turn increases their usage.

Facebook should look carefully at the role of playfulness and social norms. Greater user acceptance is critical to revenue because the more the user spends time involved with their services the more exposure there is to potential marketing. The findings provide some principles for the design of Facebook to correspond to user tastes. When a potential Facebook user experiences social influence from peers, he or she is likely to form a sense of intimacy with their family and friends through Facebook and with such a feeling they will be more likely to share information with others. The trend that designers should focus their attention is the developing of interactive applications. By using hedonic functions with friends, the users will create strong ties amongst other users and their family and friends. In addition, the like/share buttons of a particular online article or discussion, designers should enable the individual to express a personal interest publicly and bring the related information back to their network. However, not all information is disclosed because users’ friends can decide who their audiences are and what information is shown on their pages, and thus partly limit the visibility of divisive information on the network. Perhaps one thing Facebook could do is to provide users with an anonymous list including different information sources (Website, blog, and Fan page) that are commonly shared by friends. They could follow the particular information source proactively instead of receiving new information, depending on the connections made by their associates. This design would enable customization for users to access diverse but unique information generated by the common interests of the network.

#

As for PTT, the next, most important step is to quickly develop new and stimulating information to improve the user’s positive attitude. The design should have more emphasis on the ability to update content, which can fulfill the need of users who have a strong desire for information. Further, customized widgets offering something new when they access the SNS could directly inspire their curiosity to activity research the past. Another important point is that with greater content management, a user’s concentration would be improved to optimize the browsing behavior. As social norms plays a significant role by indirectly and directly influencing usage, administrators should begin to provide system functionalities for sharing newsletters with peers. This may be a way to market the SNS to friends-of-friends and enable the user to perceive that they are connected by the same topics. Second, PTT should initiate new features to facilitate the bi-directional communication among a group of registered users, and further, allow them to use this feature to share group discussions with their unregistered friends. This could be valuable to PTT in seeking strategies for increasing peer influence and capturing the attention of potential users in group discussions.

## 7.1. Implications for future studies and limitations

The way perceived playfulness is manifested varies and requires different sub-dimensions particularly in regard to the use of various modern technologies. The empirical findings demonstrate that these three distinct dimensions do not explain a user’s attitude to the same degree. Because the experience of IT interaction appears in different ways, researchers have had difficulty capturing its comprehensive meaning with the existing measures. The reason for the influence of concentration, enjoyment and curiosity on a user’s attitude in different SNSs results from the variance of the subjective evaluation in terms of the particular characteristics, feelings, objectives and functionaries of each SNS. Measuring perceived playfulness addresses, in particular, the appraisals during the interaction process with a specific SNS. The development of perceived playfulness should take this distinction into account and define the measures accordingly. To fully reflect perceived playfulness in its various contexts, future study should explore more diverse sub-dimensions and develop the related measures albeit with caution.

#

Perceived playfulness is referred to as the subjective evaluation of a specific interaction with technology during the explorative process. In this case, the evaluation process focused on the interaction with a particular IT. Serenko and Turel [65] proposed that playfulness will change with the constant use of new technology or in a new playful computing environment, thus leading the user’s experience to change. In other words, the playful state would be accurate for the current usage but playfulness might be carefully reexamined in future research as that stable trait in playfulness that may not be able to be generalized to different online human-IT interactions; otherwise, perceived playfulness could tend to be manipulated as system-specific and environment-specific in measurement [65].

In spite of making several contributions to the existing understanding of perceived playfulness, some limitations in this study need to be addressed. It is cross-sectional in nature and uses a convenience sample. Although PTT and Facebook are representative of SNSs, the scope in this study is a narrowly-defined segment of the entire online population. Concentrating on their characteristics, we distinguished between PTT and Facebook. However, some other online SNSs which are motivated by transactional interests have not been included in this study. An effort to generalize to other population segments is necessary and the explanation of our findings should be executed with caution.

Surely there are many context-specific constructs that could be added that precede the individual attitude or use intention in the context of SNSs. Future research could take this opportunity and develop a comprehensive model to refine our context-specific model. Lastly, this study was not conducted in an experimental environment where we had the ability to control external variables. In a controlled environment, researchers can utilize observational methods to exert varying amounts of a user’s playfulness when he or she is involved in new technology or a new playful computing environment. Such interpretation requires future IS research to gauge whether the perceived playfulness can be influenced by different situational factors over time.

## 8. Conclusion

#

The positive attitude of the user toward a SNS is driven by the strength of their playfulness. This study deliberates how the influence of different SNSs changes the effect of sub-dimensions and discusses what is the best practice for marketing PTT and Facebook. The results show that each dimension can coexist to explain a user’s attitude to a different degree. In general, perceived curiosity expresses the determinate role to facilitate a positive attitude toward SNS usage. In terms of PTT perceived concentration and curiosity are the compelling factors to enable a user to develop a positiv attitude, while perceived enjoyment is vital for Facebook. While in practice resources may be limited, SNS administrators and designers should endeavor to apply our results. Our findings serve to demonstrate that different types of SNSs can facilitate the effect of sub-dimensions in different ways, and the universal perspective of psychological traits may not be the best conclusion for perceived playfulness.

The degree to which playfulness is generated in the interaction process tends to be associated with the targeted technology. The results make a response to the current IS literature by empirically testing the attribute of perceived playfulness. This study concludes that perceived playfulness is an individual state in the context of a SNS and clarifies the ambiguous definitions and confounded measurements of it in the IS literature. It is anticipated that the use of a superordinate construct would fully capture the complex structure underlying perceived playfulness. This reconceptualization reconciles the apparently inconsistent views of it. We advise that the neglect of using multiple dimensions to reflect perceived playfulness has resulted in both theoretical and operational issues.

Our findings identify the role of perceived playfulness in the field of SNSs and the decomposed model further provides useful advice to SNS researchers and practitioners. The interaction between the types of SNSs and sub-dimensions provides a better understanding of how to encourage a user’s attitude and how to develop a context-specific model for both PTT and Facebook. More research is required based on the flow theory to add contributions to the body of knowledge in the reconceptualization of playfulness. A practical view of the our study exhibits that knowing about the differences among the effects of sub-dimension and social norms can enable participators to design system features supporting SNSs and thus fit members’ needs more specifically.

## APPENDIX C. Additional data analysis

The results shown in Table C-1 indicated that all measurement items loaded on their respective constructs above 0.6 level were higher than any of the cross-loadings on each construct, representing adequate convergent and discriminant validity [25]. Because several items slightly cross-load on more than one construct, and the correlation coefficient between perceived enjoyment and curiosity was the set of underlying dimensions. The perceived playfulness items were then subjected to EFA and the results at Table C-2 showed that the loadings for all items exceeded 0.5, no item had a loading of higher than 0.5 on two or more dimensions.

Although EFA is useful in exploring factors in the development of measure, it assumes that each item’s measurement errors are unrelated which limits its ability to analyze the issue of item crossloadings [25]. We acknowledged that two items (PP6 and PP7) may have had cross-loadings threatening discriminant validity. Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) takes each item’s error correlation into account and reveals more information to diagnose cross-loadings. The three dimensions structure was examined by a CFA using a paired construct test to demonstrate discriminant validity. In addition, the correlation coefficient between attitude and intended use was 0.741 in this study, which may raise a concern regarding discriminant validity. Therefore, we also included those two constructs into paired construct tests.

We conducted a Chi-square difference test for testing the discriminant validity between perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity [64]. The discriminant validity of two constructs can be established by calculating the difference of the chi-square statistics for the constrained and unconstrained measurement models [64]. We fixed the correlation between two factors at 1 in the constrained model and freed the correlation in the unconstrained model. The results at Table C-3 indicated that the unconstrained model was significantly superior to the constrained model, affirming good discriminant validity between perceived concentration, enjoyment and curiosity. Further, a two construct solution between attitude and intended use provided a better model fit, and discriminant validity between attitude and intended use was therefore supported.
