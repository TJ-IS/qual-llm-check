# Enterprise social networking usage as a moderator of the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity: A multilevel study

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Enterprise social networking Challenge stressors Hindrance stressors Employee creativity

## A B S T R A C T

Enterprise social networking (ESN) techniques have been widely adopted by organizations to provide a platform for public communication among employees. Based on the job demand–control–support model and communication visibility theory, this study investigates how the relationships between the perceived work stressors of employees (i.e., challenge and hindrance stressors) and their creativity are moderated by team task- and relationship-oriented ESN usage. We used the hierarchical linear model to test this multilevel model. Results of a survey of 260 employees in 72 groups indicate that two ESN usage types diferentially moderate the relationship between work stressors and emplovee creativity. Specifically. task-oriented ESN usage positively moderates the efects of the two types of stressors on employee creativity, whereas relationship-oriented ESN usage negatively moderates the relationship between hindrance stressors and employee creativity. Theoretical and practical implications are also discussed.

## 1. Introduction

Employee creativity is considered as a critical source of organiza tional competitiveness [28,81,86]; however, promoting such creativity “also translate into increasing levels of work stressors such as high job demands” [65, p. 141]. The existing literature has empirically examined the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity [9]. However, the findings of these studies have been challenged by the emergence of a new group of information technologies (IT) in the workplace, especially enterprise social networking (ESN) [13,16,26,85]. ESN, such as Yammer and Jive, has been widely adopted by organizations to support team members in communicating and collaborating with each other [75]. The prevalence of ESN is not only changing workplace communication and coordination but also providing considerable opportunities and challenges in stress management and creativity development [26,45,84]. Thus, scholars have indicated that a team’s various usage of ESN may influence the outcomes of team members perceiving diferent work stressors; however, such an efect may not be equal among members [15]. Accordingly, they have questioned the relationship between the perceived work stressors of employees and their creativity to consider under team ESN usage conditions.

Previous studies have extensively explored the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity. However, they still had several limitations [4,9,62]. First, previous studies have typically conceptualized work stressors as a one-dimensional concept, whereas work stressors involve both positive and negative elements [79]. Collapsing such positive and negative elements into one concept may eliminate their potentially diferential efects [11]. A study that investigates how diferent types of work stressors, such as challenge and hindrance stressors, afect employee creativity in a single model will extend our understanding of this domain. Second, prior research has largely neglected the contextual efects of the new workplace condition reconstructed by IT on the stressors–creativity linkage. Some scholars have explored contingencies, such as the self-regulatory [65] and personality traits of employees [9] and organizational climate [62], to scrutinize the stressors–creativity link [65,88]. However, their findings sufer a lack of IT contextualization, thereby limiting our understanding of the digital conditions under which work stressors afect employee creativity.

Given the inconsistent findings, promoting creativity among stressed employees under specific contexts presents a considerable challenge to managers. To improve the creativity of employees, managers realized that changes in the workplace such as team’s ESN support must occur to fit the stressor management of each member. For example, a team’s ESN usage could help team members solve problems by increasing their access to information and their ability to communicate with other members [18]. However, diferent usages could cause diferent reactions in team members and require them to adjust in various ways [70]. In this view, some scholars have indicated the special role of various ESN usages in the workplace. For example [15], reported that two facets of IT support $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ personal assistance and resources support) could be associated with less stress and strain. Certain scholars have further proposed that using ESN for tasks may fa cilitate professional knowledge acquisition, distribution, and transformation through message transparency, whereas using ESN for socialization can help maintain and develop interpersonal relationships through network translucence $[ 6 , 4 5 , 5 1 ]$ ]. However, few studies have empirically tested the diferent moderating roles of various ESN usages in the stressors–creativity relationship.

To address the above gaps, the current study aims to explore the moderating role of team ESN usage on the relationship between the perceived work stressors of employees and their creativity based on the job demand–control–support model (JDCS) [34] and communication visibility theory [45]. Specifically, JDCS argues that employee performance is directly determined by their job demands, which refers to the “psychological stressors involved in accomplishing the workload” [37, p. 291]. Accordingly, we propose that work stressors could be directly associated with employee creativity. To specify the association, we further adopt the challenge − hindrance stressor framework introduced by [11]. Challenge stressors are “good” stressors that can be easily managed; overcoming these stressors could provide employees with opportunities for growth [11]. By contrast, hindrance stressors are commonly appraised as “bad” stressors; they are usually dificult to manage and can hinder individual growth [11]. We propose that these two work stressors could afect employee creativity in various ways.

Furthermore, JDCS argued that “job control and social support interact with job stressors to reduce levels of employee strain” [14], p. 1]. This finding indicates that the stressors–creativity linkage should be leveraged by both job control and social support. In this view, we categorize team ESN usage into task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages [51] and propose that these two could separately incur job control and social support in a team based on communication visibility theory. This theory indicated that ESN usage could increase message transparency and enhance network translucence [45]. From this per spective, task-oriented ESN usage typically focuses on completing tasks and assisting employees to control their tasks with increased message transparency, whereas relationship-oriented ESN usage emphasizes maintaining and developing social relationships and provides social support for employees with enhanced network translucence. To test the proposed model, the current study uses a data set obtained from teams in various industries in China. This data set is used to develop a mul tilevel model that examines how the task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages of a team shape the influence of the perceived work stressors of employees on their creativity.

## 2. Theoretical background and literature review

## 2.1. Job demand–control–support model and communication visibility theory

Scholars have increasingly emphasized the research on the potential moderators of the influence of work stressors on employee outcomes $[ 9 , 6 2 , 6 5 , 8 8 ]$ . The notions of control and support have been extensively investigated as moderators of the stressor–outcome relationship, and it is depicted by JDCS [37]. The JDCS specifies three essential job char acteristics, namely job demands, job control, and social support, which can vary in a workplace setting and influence individual attitudinal and behavioral reactions [29,37,52]. Based on this model, job demands reflect the psychological stressors involved in accomplishing workload demands, dealing with time pressures, and handling job-related personal conflict that employees perceived in the workplace [12]. Such demands have been widely proposed as the significant factors influencing employee outcomes. Demands were treated as individual-level constructs because they were viewed by employees as opportunities or obstacles for individual creativity in this study.

Furthermore, JDCS emphasizes the moderating roles of job control and social support in the relationship between work stressors and employee outcomes [29]. Job control specifically reflects the extent to which employees can control their tasks and general work activities [29]. Social support refers to the overall level of helpful social interaction available on the tasks from supervisors and colleagues [36]. The JDCS proposes that job control and social support could help employees leverage the efect of stressful demands on their outcome [34,35]. For example, job control may leverage the efect of stressful demands on employee outcome because it enables employees to decide how and when to perform their work tasks or duties [4]. Social support could moderate the efect of stressful demands on employee outcome by achieving emotional and practical support from supervisors and colleagues.

Previous research has applied JDCS to explore the interaction efect among job demands, job control, and social support [29]. However, few studies have specified job control and social support in a certain context, such as the IT context. These two job characteristics have also been tested in both group and individual level assessments [17,41,53,80]. For example, Langfred [41] explored the efect of autonomy (i.e., work control) at individual and group levels on group cohesiveness and Wang, Liu, Zhan and Shi [80] have tested the group-level moderation of social support on the relationship between family–work conflict and individual alcohol use. Following these research, job control and social support were treated as group-level constructs in the IT contingent because such IT-enabled job control and social support depend on other group members’ behavior.

[45] Proposed communication visibility theory to explore the job control and social support roles of ESN in a team. This theory argues that transparent communication within a team enables employees to improve their awareness of who knows what and who knows whom [45]. Thus, ESN could ensure message transparency and network translucence in the team, which are necessary for the job control and social support of employees [10,19,45]. Specifically, ESN ensures message transparency in the team by enabling previously created and published content to remain visible and permanently accessible [78]. Under this condition, employees in a team with high ESN usage would find it easier to seek and collect useful knowledge and experience with transparent messages and to approach appropriate members to ask for knowledge [45]. Thus, team ESN usage could allow employees to have increased options to rearrange their work schedule and to decide how to accomplish creative tasks with rich knowledge and experience. In short, team ESN usage gives employees more decision authority to forge ahead with their tasks. What’s more, message transparency in team increases ESN users’ belief and ability to identify, understand, and ask for the specific skills he/she needs [44,46]. Thus, team ESN usage may improve employees’ skill discretion, which reflects the opportunity to utilize specific job skills at work [20]. Skill discretion and decision authority are two components of job control so that task-oriented ESN usage gives expression to employees’ control of their job.

Meanwhile, ESN ensures network translucence by visualizing the behaviors, preferences, and communication networks of team members [72], thereby facilitating them to achieve social support. In brief, the feature of ESN, network translucence, provides a primary way that users of ESN build, enhance, and maintain their social relationship [23]. Scholars have shown that network translucence can increase employee engagement as users learn about the personal and professional contexts of their colleagues and, thus, have both the impetus and useful information with which to approach to relationships development [67,68,82]. Network translucence also enables ESN users to expand the range of members through reactivate dormant ties and maintain a set of “latent ties” [23,49,58]. The establishment of a “connection” with someone through ESN suggests that the user is interested in initiating or reinforcing a social relationship. Connections among peers often produce a form of homophily, which has also been associated with stronger network ties among virtual teams and improved providing social support through ESN [87]. This paper therefore suggested employees could benefit from the translucent social interaction through ESN and achieve social support, including emotional and practical support. In summary, team ESN usage would help employees control their job better and achieve social support from supervisors and colleagues because of message transparency and network translucence in the team [45].

## 2.2. Work stressors

Work stressors are derived from job demands that could evoke stress processes and require adaptive responses [50]. Scholars have in vestigated the efect of work stressors on job satisfaction, turnover, organizational commitment, productivity, and creativity. Empirical evidence supports the negative efects [27] and positive outcomes of work stressors [24]. Some scholars have attributed such mixed findings to the existing one-dimensional conceptualization of work stressors [11,47,50]. The literature indicates that employees could perceive certain work stressors that could be manageable because they regard these stressors as under their control [11]. Thus, such stressors could result in opportunities for growth if employees overcome them. However, some stressors could be perceived as unmanageable, and these stressors could hinder opportunities for employee growth [11].

Accordingly, categorizing work stressors into challenge and hin drance stressors is considered the best approach to understand work stressors [47,50,57]. Challenge stressors stem from job demands that involve workload, time pressure, and responsibility, which could promote mastery, personal growth, and achievement [11,47,83,88]. Hindrance stressors emerge from job demands that involve role ambiguity and job insecurity that potentially constrain personal development and work-related accomplishments [11,47,83,88]. Existing literature indicates that challenge stressors lead to positive outcomes, whereas hindrance stressors are negatively related to similar outcomes [47,59]. Findings on the negative efects of hindrance stressors on employee performance are fairly consistent, but results on the role of challenge stressors are inconsistent [65]. Thus [33], stated, “it is also proposed in most models, either explicitly or implicitly, that the relationship between workplace stressors and such outcomes is moderated by a number of diferent factors (i.e., demographic characteristics, personal traits, the social environment, etc.).”

Table 1 shows that scholars have widely explored the potential moderators in the relationship between work stressors and employee outcomes. Although these studies have helped in understanding the relationship between stressors and outcomes, few studies have considered the new change in the current workplace brought by the emergence of ESN in workplace [16,26]. An increasing number of scholars have realized that the implementation of IT has broad and indirect organizational efects [13,55,69]. [1], for example, presented that IT usage could help improve the knowledge and adaptability of employees [45]. Further indicated that the introduction of ESN into the workplace could facilitate the visibility of communication and coordination within a team. Unfortunately, no study has empirically in vestigated the potentially moderating role of ESN usage in the influ encing mechanism of work stressors on employee creativity.

## 2.3. Enterprise social networking usage

Team ESN usage refers to the extent to which a team implements ESN inside an organization for business or commercial purposes. Many leading firms, such as General Motors, IBM, HP, Microsoft, and Deloitte, implemented ESN to encourage employees to share professional and personal information [43,56]. The allure of these technologies is often seen in an increased ability to foster team communication and collaboration within organizations [45]. Team ESN usage enables team members to “(1) communicate messages with specific coworkers or broadcast messages to everyone in the organization; (2) explicitly indicate or implicitly reveal particular coworkers as communication partners; (3) post, edit, and sort text and files linked to themselves or others; and (4) view the messages, connections, text, and files communicated, posted, edited, and sorted by others in the organization at any time.” [45, p.799]

Although preceding technologies, such as email and video conferencing, have allowed employees to do one of the first three activities, ESN is unique and transformational in supporting these three activities and making them visible at the team level, rather than at the individual level [72,76]. As communication visibility theory indicates, using ESN in a team could facilitate team members to control tasks and achieve social support by message transparency and network translucence [45]. Message transparency in the team could specifically facilitate each ESN user to generate ideas, solve problems, and coordinate tasks [63], whereas network translucence in the team allows each ESN user to demonstrate the social ties with each other, thereby building and maintaining network relationships [6]. In this view, the literature indicates that team ESN usage could be categorized into task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages [40].

Task-oriented ESN usage reflects the extent to which a team applies ESN to plan, clarify, and monitor tasks, such as setting their team’s goals, planning how a task is completed, and deciding on the best course of action when problems arise. Such usage could help realize message transparency in the team, thereby facilitating each user to flexibly arrange work and consequently enhance job control [40,45]. Relationship-orientated ESN usage refers to the extent to which the team implements ESN to build and maintain personal relationships by listening, encouraging, and supporting each other. Such usage could help improve network translucence in the team, thereby enabling each user to easily achieve social support from supervisors and colleagues [6]. This categorization indicates that task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages are not mutually exclusive. However, understanding the various roles of task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages in the workplace, especially their moderating roles in the relationship between the work stressors perceived by employees and their creativity, remains limited.

## 3. Research framework and hypotheses development

In this section, we develop a research model to explain the role of team ESN usage in setting a contextual condition under which employees’ perceived work stressors afect their creativity in multilevel, as depicted in Fig. 1. We applied the JDCS model and stressor literature to propose that challenge stressors are positively related to employee creativity, whereas hindrance stressors are negatively related to employee creativity. Furthermore, we integrated JDCS with communication visibility theory to propose that team task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages could serve as job control and social support to diferently moderate the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity.

## 3.1. Work stressors and employee creativity

As the JDCS model proposed, various job demands may incur different reactions from employees, thereby influencing their creativity [37,52]. In the existing literature, challenge stressors have been widely appraised as employee-perceived manageable stressful demands with the potential for personal growth and goal attainment [11,47,83,88]. When stressors were perceived manageable, they could evoke positive reactions from employees, such as attentiveness, eagerness, and confidence, which lead to active problem-solving [64,79]. Thus, employees’ perceived high challenge stressors, such as responsibility, are more likely to consider creative activities to efectively deal with a task [62]. Therefore, when engaged in challenging tasks, challenge stressed employees would as much as possible try creative solutions, which enhance creativity [8]. Meanwhile, challenge stressors could bring potential benefits for employees, which motivate them to recruit re sources to fully engage in creative tasks and sustain their eforts. These positive reactions have important implications for employee creativity [2,54]. For example, time pressure can push employees to increase ef forts and concentrate on creative tasks, thereby promoting creative thinking. In summary, employees’ perceived challenge stressors could be positively related to their creativity by triggering positive reactions.

Hypothesis 1. Employees’ perceived challenge stressors are positively related to their creativity.

By contrast, hindrance stressors have been defined as stressful demands beyond manageable by employees and might obstruct opportunities for personal benefit and future gain [11,79]. Specifically, when stressors were perceived unmanageable, they would induce negative reactions such as anger, fear, and frustration among employees [25]. For example, employees who feel that their role is ambiguous and that their job is insecure may resort to passive behaviors, such as avoiding work or performing tasks passively on purpose. Under this condition, hindrance-stressed employees would encounter dificulty in exerting any efort on creative tasks. Meanwhile, hindrance stressors may hinder opportunities for employee growth [11], which makes employee withdraw from the current situation. Such negative motivation may prevent employees from developing and using creative thinking. For example, employees who faced job insecurity would experience difi culty increasing their eforts and concentrating on creative tasks, thereby preventing creative thinking. In summary, employees’ perceived hindrance stressors, which trigger their negative motivations, may be negatively related to their creativity.

Hypothesis 2. Employees’ perceived hindrance stressors are negatively related to their creativity.

## 3.2. Moderating role of team task-oriented ESN usage

Based on JDCS, job control could help employees acquire necessary job resources, which enable them to manage and actively cope with stressful demands [29]. Communication visibility theory further in dicates that team task-oriented ESN usage could provide ESN-enabled job control to help employees acquire information resources due to the message transparency in the team [45]. In this view, we propose that team task-oriented ESN usage could help employees leverage their perceived challenge stressors to improve creativity. First, challenge stressed employees were motivated to engage in creative tasks because they perceived these tasks to be manageable [11,47,83,88]. Team task oriented ESN usage could show all issues, such as plans, schedules, changes, and statuses of creative tasks, thereby realizing message transparency in the team [45,63]. Such message transparency facilitates challenge stressed employees to acquire important information to manage these tasks and encourages them to contribute to teamwork and to provide creative suggestions to solve team problems. Thus, in a team with low task-oriented ESN usage, challenge stressed employees may assume they have low job resources to manage their jobs and become less likely to engage in complex problem solving and creativity to meet stressful demands. By contrast, in a team with high task-oriented ESN usage, where messages are transparent, challenge-stressed employees would regard such usage as a platform through which job resources are achieved to manage task and to engage in creativity.

Second, challenge-stressed employees were motivated to engage in creative tasks because they perceived that they can achieve potential benefits [47,50,57]. Message transparency brought by team task-oriented ESN usage allows employees to acquire and evaluate colleagues messages, including task plans and updates, requests for help, and summaries of past tasks, when they want to conduct creative tasks [40,45]. Message transparency would help challenge stressed employees obtain insightful understanding about the potential benefits of creative tasks, such as promotion or personal development [45]. In summary, in a team with high task-oriented ESN usage, challenge stressed employees would have a clear understanding of stressful demands, thereby enhancing their motivation to invest time and eforts to undertake creative activities [3].

Hypothesis 3a. Team task-oriented ESN usage could strengthen the positive relationship between the perceived challenge stressors of employees and their creativity.

For hindrance stressors, the reason for their lesser influence on employee creativity in teams with higher task-oriented ESN usage is the same as that for challenge stressors: the greater the message transparency, the more job control is present for addressing stressful demands. For hindrance stressed employees, their motivation of engaging in creative tasks would be limited because they perceived such tasks as unmanageable [11,47,83,88]. In teams low in task-oriented ESN usage, the absence of message transparency and the associated job control may increase uncertainty about the appropriate means to engage in creative tasks. To avoid role ambiguity and job insecurity, hindrance-stressed employees tend to avoid creative tasks in the team.

By contrast, in teams high in task-oriented ESN usage, the details of creative tasks posted by colleagues would enable employees to imitate efective means of addressing stressful demands. Message transparency in a team may stimulate or at least make hindrance-stressed employees worry less about role ambiguity and job insecurity when they engage in creative tasks [63]. Under this condition, hindrance-stressed employees would face less dispositional barriers in trying new approaches and easily clarify uncertainties by team discussion and problem solving [29]. In summary, team task-oriented ESN usage that facilitates job control could diminish the negative consequences of employees’ perceived hindrance stressors on their creativity because such usage realizes message transparency and encourages team members to discuss diferent approaches.

Hypothesis 3b. Team task-oriented ESN usage could weaken the negative relationship between the perceived hindrance stressors of employees and their creativity.

## 3.3. Moderating role of team relationship-oriented ESN usage

According to JDCS, social support could help stressed employees enhance positive emotional reactions to cope with stressful demands [29,36]. Communication visibility theory further indicates that team relationship-oriented ESN usage could act as the ESN-enabled social support to enable employees acquire emotional and practical support based on network translucence in the team [6,45]. In this perspective, we expect that challenge stressors will improve employee creativity. However, this potential can be further released when the team has higher relationship-oriented ESN usage. First, team relationship-oriented ESN usage could make all relationships between team members visible, thereby leading to network translucence in the team [45]. Such network translucence could facilitate challenge-stressed employees to achieve emotional and practical support, such as encouragement, approval, recommendation, and comments from supervisors and colleagues in order to manage creative tasks. Both emotional and practical support would further motivate them to engage in teamwork and to share creative ideas. Thus, in a team with low relationship-oriented ESN usage, challenge-stressed employees may assume they cannot achieve social support to manage creative tasks; thus, they become less likely to engage in such tasks. However, in a team with high task-oriented ESN usage, network translucence makes challenge-stressed employees re gard such usage as an efective approach to achieve social support from supervisors and colleagues in order to manage creative tasks; thus, they tend to engage in such tasks.

Second, network translucence caused by team relationship-oriented ESN usage allows challenge-stressed employees to realize the potential benefits of engaging in creative tasks. In teams with higher relationshiporiented ESN usage, challenge-stressed employees would have a higher feeling of being connected to others and a higher sense of belongingness, thereby facilitating them to realize the potential benefits of stressful demands [71,77]. Enhancing the emotional energy of employees such as vigor and enthusiasm is beneficial to challenge demand, thereby motivating them to engage in creative processes. In this view, in a team with high task-oriented ESN usage, challenge-stressed employees tend to perceive their team members to be facilitative of their eforts to be creative and feel more comfortable directing their attention and efforts toward creative activities.

Hypothesis 4a. Team relationship-oriented ESN usage could strengthen the positive relationship between the perceived challenge stressors of employees and their creativity.

We expect that the negative influence of hindrance stressors on employee creativity would be attenuated because of network translucence. Hindrance stressors lead to individually focused cognitions and mental separation from team knowledge structures because negative emotions and motivations are induced [27,50]. In teams low in relationship-oriented ESN usage, the absence of network translucence in the team and the associated social support may increase the frustration of hindrance stressed employees on ways of handling creative tasks. Thus, these employees would hesitate to engage in creative tasks in the team because of their worry about role ambiguity and job insecurity.

By contrast, in teams high in relationship-oriented ESN usage, network translucence in the team may facilitate hindrance stressed employees to seek help, support, and encouragement from others in order to validate the appropriateness of their emotions and reduce their fear and anxiety [66], thereby bufing the negative efect of hindrance stressors on creativity. Specifically, relationship-oriented ESN usage could help employees easily and rapidly identify someone to turn to for support and help within a translucent network. The ESN-enabled social support not only provides employees with encouragement and assistance, it also conveys expectations of creativity [2]. For example, network translucence could help set some expert employees as positive role models to other members for how they might revaluate hindering situations, thereby weakening the perception that they are threatening [48]. In sum, relationship-oriented ESN usage that facilitates social support could diminish the negative relationship between hindrance stressors and employee creativity because such usage realizes network translucence and motivates team members to engage in creative tasks.

Hypothesis 4b. Team relationship-oriented ESN usage could weaken the negative relationship between the perceived hindrance stressors of employees and their creativity.

## 4. Research methods

## 4.1. Sample and data collection

A questionnaire survey was conducted to test our hypotheses. We identified organizations that allow employees to apply ESN. These organizations are located in eastern China, which is one of the most developed regions in the country. An increasing number of organizations are adopting social networking in their routine work. We invited 100 organizations to participate in the survey, and 36 agreed. We collaborated with the department managers of these organizations. We introduced the research topic to all potential respondents and assured them of data confidentiality. We examined functional departments because such departments (1) formed their smallest functional unit, (2) reported directly to the same supervisor, and (3) worked together on a permanent basis.

Data collection was accomplished through e-mail and web-based survey. We received 340 responses from employees in 96 departments, which is equivalent to a 44.10% response rate. We eliminated departments with fewer than three completed questionnaires. Our final sample consisted of 260 individuals from 72 departments. The size of departments ranged from 3 to 9 members, and the average size was 3.61 members. Table 2 shows the demographic information of the participants.

We tested potential nonresponse bias in the current study by comparing key measures of the first and final 25% of the respondents. No significant diferences were observed between these two groups, which indicate that nonresponse bias was not relevant.

## 4.2. Measures

Measurement was developed based on previously validated measures in related literature. To ensure the face and content validity of the questionnaire, we invited three researchers from information systems and human resources to conduct a review. Revisions were made based on their comments and suggestions. Revisions included clarifying certain words of several items, changing the format of the questionnaire, and re-sequencing the questions. We translated the original English version instrument into Chinese following the translation committee approach suggested by [73]; this approach was adopted because the research was conducted in China. A committee translated the original English questionnaire into Chinese and then translated it back to English, thereby ensuring the equivalence of meaning between the English and Chinese versions.

The scales used to measure work stressors, team ESN usage, and emplovee creativity are shown in the Appendix A. The items used to measure challenge and hindrance stressors were adapted from [11]. All items were scored using a response scale, in which “1″ means “no stress” and “7″ means “a great deal of stress.” An example of the 6-item challenge stressors scale was “The volume of work that must be accomplished in the allotted time.” One item in the four-item hindrance stressor scale was “The lack of job security I have.”

We measured task- and relationship-oriented ESN usage using the items developed based on [30,40], and [51]. The items were scored using a Likert response scale, in which “1″ means “strongly disagree” and “7″ means “strongly agree.” An example of the 10-item scale for task-oriented ESN usage is “Deciding how to go about our team’s work.” An example of the eight-item relationship-oriented ESN usage scale is “Encouraging team members when they’re upset.”

We measured employee creativity by using the three-item scale reported by [21]. The items were scored using a Likert response scale, in which “1″ means “strongly disagree” and “7″ means “strongly agree.” A sample item is “I believe I am usually very creative in my solutions to work problems.”

Employee creativity may be afected by certain group or individual characteristics. We included two group-level control variables, namely group history and group size, as well as four individual-level control variables, namely gender, age, education level, and department.

## 4.3. Validation of multilevel data structure

Perceptual data were collected from a single source at one point in time. Thus, common method bias might be an issue in this study. We applied Harmon’s one-factor test to assess common method bias. Results showed that all items could be categorized into five constructs with eigenvalues greater than 1.0, thereby accounting for 77.02% of variance. The first construct did not account for majority of the variance (32.76%), which suggests that common method bias was not a serious issue in our study.

This study included individual- and group-level constructs. For group-level constructs (task-oriented ESN usage and relationship oriented ESN usage), we assessed within- and between-group homogeneity to determine if aggregation is viable within naturally occurring groups [5]. First, we assessed within-group homogeneity using r<sub>wg j( )</sub> statistic. According to [32], an $r _ { w g ( j ) }$ equal to or greater than 0.70 indicates suficient within-group agreement. The result indicates that task- and relationship-oriented ESN usages were normally distributed, and their $r _ { w g ( j ) }$ means were 0.75 and 0.77, respectively.

Second, we computed the variance and associated infraclass correlation coeficients (ICCs) to assess homogeneity. ICC (1) indicates the proportion of variance explained by group membership, whereas ICC (2) estimates the reliability of the means [61]. If ICC (2) is greater than or equal to $0 . 7 0 ,$ we can assume that group means are reliably diferent. We use the formulas reported in [5], in which ICCs for team task-oriented ESN usage were given as ICC (1) = 0.22 and ICC (2) = 0.51; ICCs for team relationship-oriented ESN usage were ICC (1) = 0.12 and ICC (2) = 0.35. All other indices supported aggregation. A small ICC (2) value could be attributed to small group sizes, whereas a subpar ICC (2) score was compensated for by a high $r _ { w g } .$

To provide a comprehensive test of a multilevel data structure, we applied an advanced analytical technique called multilevel factor analysis [22]. We used a five-step procedure [22] to assess data structure. First, we used LISREL to perform confirmatory factor analysis on the constructs of challenge and hindrance stressors, task- and relationshiporiented ESN usages, and employee creativity. We constructed a model with these five factors, and the fit indices were acceptable (five-factor model: χ2 = 629.26, df = 2242, p < 0.01, RMSEA = 0.079, CFI = 0.96, IFI = 0.96, and NFI = 0.94). The model fit with five factors was compared with the fit of four potential alternatives. The first alternative model was a four-factor model that collapsed challenge and hindrance stressors into one stressor (four-factor model: χ2 = 1158.67, df = 246, p < 0.01, RMSEA = 0.120, CFI = 0.92, IFI = 0.92, and NFI = 0.90). The second alternative was a three-factor model that collapsed taskand relationship-oriented ESN usage into one ESN usage (three-factor model: χ2 = 2396.10, df = 249, p < 0.01, RMSEA = 0.182, CFI = 0.83, IFI = 0.83, and NFI = 0.81). We then tested a two-factor model by collapsing all moderators and independent variable in one factor (two-factor model: χ2 = 4332.47, df = 251, p < 0.01, RMSEA = 0.251, CFI = 0.70, IFI = 0.71, and NFI = 0.69) and a one-factor model (one-factor model: χ2 = 4915.73, df = 252, p < 0.01, RMSEA = 0.267, CFI = 0.66, IFI = 0.67, and NFI = 0.65). Data modeled at the individual level demonstrated that the five-factor model provided good fit that was better fit than those of the other models.

Table 3 shows the mean values, standard deviations, and correlations among variables. One interconstruct correlation was higher than the benchmark of 0.60. Furthermore, we conducted multicollinearity test, which showed that the highest variance inflation factor (VIF) was 1.641 and the lowest tolerance value was 0.610. The VIF values were all less than 10, and the tolerance values were all higher than 0.10. Therefore, multicollinearity was not a significant problem in our study.

## 5. Discussion

The present study develops a multilevel model in which the teamlevel contextual factors of ESN usage moderate the efects of individuallevel relationship between work stressors and employee creativity. We explored the diferent influences of challenge stressors and hindrance stressors on employee creativity and the various moderating roles of task-oriented ESN usage and relationship-oriented ESN. Theoretical and practical implications are outlined below.

## 5.1. Theoretical implications

As the JDCS proposes, work stressors stem from stressful job de mands and play a critical role in afecting employee outcomes [37,52]. Despite extensive previous research from this perspective, the nature of the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity remains equivocal. Previous studies rooted in JDCS have indicated a negative relationship between stressful job demands and employee outcomes. Transcending the traditional conceptualization of work stressors as a unidimensional construct, the current study distinguishes challenge and hindrance stressors in the IT-supported workplace context. Our findings include three primary contributions to extant literature.

First, the current study provides insightful understanding on work stressors research by resolving inconsistent findings regarding the relationship between work stressors and employee creativity. Specifically, JDCS mainly indicate the negative outcomes of stressful demands. However, [11] and [79] pointed out that the existing literature has largely ignored the positive efects of work stressors; thus, they represent an oversimplification of the context in which stressors operate. By applying the challenge-hindrance stressors framework [11], we deviate from the unidimensional conceptualization of work stressors by examining the diferential roles of challenge and hindrance stressors in afecting employee creativity. Our findings confirm that challenge stressors are positively related to employee creativity, whereas hindrance stressors are negatively related to employee creativity. These findings enrich the stressors–creativity literature by addressing the call for “diferent stress stimuli may work through distinct mechanisms to diferentially afect creativity” [9, p2].

Second, the current study developed and tested an IT contingent view of work stressors by accounting for the moderating roles of taskand relationship-oriented ESN usages. By combining the JDCS model and communication visibility theory, the present study expands our understanding on the moderating efect of two types of ESN usage by exploring the job control and social support role of ESN in a team. The findings present that task-oriented ESN usage could act as the positive job control to strengthen the positive relationship between challenge stressors and employee creativity, as well as weaken the negative relationship between hindrance stressors and employee creativity. These findings are consistent with the perspective of JDCS, which proposes the positive role of job control in leveraging the influence of job demands [4,29]. Meanwhile, the findings extend communication visibility theory to the stressors literature and enrich the JDCS literature; that is, task-oriented ESN usage could serve as an important job control factor through its provision of message transparence.

However, our findings present that relationship-oriented ESN usage cannot leverage the positive relationship between challenge stressors and employee creativity, whereas it strengthens the negative relation ship between hindrance stressors and employee creativity. These findings are inconsistent with our hypotheses and the perspective of JDCS and communication visibility theory. The findings indicate the negative role of relationship-oriented ESN usage in the workplace. Thus, al though relationship-oriented ESN usage could result in social support for employees, it could also make employees lose their focus, thereby possibly eliminating the positive role of relationship-oriented ESN usage [74]. For example, personal communication posts on ESN may attract employee attention and promote frequent browsing and checking of nonwork-related information and communication. Such a form of distraction tend to limit the employees’ attention on creative tasks, which, in turn, leads them to exert reduced efort and persistence at work, thereby decreasing the positive role of social support that they attained from relationship-oriented ESN usage.

Thus, our findings indicate that for challenge-stressed employees, the social support achieved from relationship-oriented ESN usage is not their prior concern when they engaged in creative tasks because of the potential negative efects. However, for hindrance-stressed employees, the negative efects of relationship-oriented ESN usage became domi nant compared to their achieved social support. These findings extend our understanding about the controversy of using ESN in the workplace [13,55]. Thus, relationship-oriented ESN usage could increase interruption and decrease productivity, but it is only efective for hindrance-stressed employees. By contrast, task-oriented ESN usage would not interrupt employees and could increase their creativity, regardless of whether employees are challenge- or hindrance-stressed. The present study helps us understand whether ESN actually provides value to organizations and extends our understanding about the role of ESN usage in the workplace.

Finally, the current study ofers additional empirical insights be yond JDCS and communication visibility theory. Although JDCS proposed the moderating role job control and social support in the influ encing mechanism of job demand, few studies have specified the control and support in the IT context. By investigating the moderating role of task- and relationship-oriented ESN usage, we integrate the perspective of communication visibility theory with JDCS, which not only enriches the JDCS but also extends communication visibility theory to the stress literature.

## 5.2. Practical implications

Our findings ofer several implications for managers to apply ESN to inspire creativity among stressed employees. First, we suggest that managers be aware of the need to distinguish between challenge and hindrance stressors when promoting employee creativity. Not all stressful demands lead to poor performance. Managers could help employees perceive the responsibility and workload of their job demands when they conduct creative tasks. Meanwhile, we suggest that managers be cautious about hindrance stressors that prevent employees from proposing creative thinking. Specifically, managers should do their best to clarify the role of each employee and decrease the confusion about each role relative to the team. This clarification would motivate employees to engage in creative tasks, which then improve their creativity. Furthermore, managers should be cautious in threatening employees when promoting them to engage in creative tasks. Such threats would make employees perceive high job insecurity, thereby negatively influencing their creativity.

Second, our findings indicate that the efect of work stressors de pends on team ESN usage. Managers should adapt their stress sources to reflect the implementation of ESN. Thus, managers should focus and promote team task-oriented ESN usage, which helps all employees achieve job control and addresses the challenge of stressful demands. For example, managers could digitalize task-related processes and encourage employees to use ESN to plan, clarify, and monitor team tasks. Meanwhile, managers can arrange ESN training to help employees to learn how to use ESN to address their tasks and better use it to control their job. However, managers should control the relationship-oriented ESN usage in the team. Although team relationship-oriented ESN usage may provide social support for stressed employees, such support cannot help them efectively address stressful demands. Managers should set the rules for relationship-oriented ESN usage in the team and train employees to focus on acquiring social support from such usage.

## 5.3. Limitations and future research

The contributions of the findings of this study should be considered in light of its limitations. First, although this study applied multilevel data to test the hypotheses, the cross-sectional nature of the data prevented us from testing the causal relationship in our model. A long itudinal study would enrich our understanding by ofering information on the causal relationships between independent and dependent vari ables.

Second, this study investigated departments in Chinese firms, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. However, China’s high guanxi orientation and collectivist culture may diferentiate the role of relationship- and task-oriented ESN usages in teamwork compared with that in Western countries. Thus, verifying whether the findings on ESN usage, stressors, and creativity in Western countries remain valid in the Chinese context is significant. Although our study provides only a snapshot of ESN usage, stressors, and creativity in China, our findings help justify and extend the JDCS and communication visibility theory, which was established in the context of ESN usage, stressors, and creativity, to the context of an emerging economy such as China.

Third, the constructs of employee creativity and ESN usage are measured by the perception of individual respondents, which are in herently subjective. This approach potentially creates incongruence between perceived and actual employee creativity and ESN usage. Future research should examine actual employee creativity and ESN usage by using objective data or collecting data from multiple in formants to ensure the robustness of results.
