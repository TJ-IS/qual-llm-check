## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Crowdfunding Lottery option Promotion instrument Propensity score matching Cannibalization efect

## A B S T R A C T

In recent years, some leading crowdfunding platforms initiated a new promotion instrument, the lottery option, to fundraisers. To gain insights into whether it is a wise decision or a harmful choice to promote crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery, we explore its impacts on crowdfunding performance using a unique dataset collected from Zhongchou.com. Based on our proposed multidimensional evaluation framework, we find that the lottery option is beneficial to the participation performance of crowdfunding campaigns. However, its impacts on the fundraising performance of crowdfunding campaigns are sophisticated. Furthermore, it has a cannibalization efect on the potential backers’ selection of existing types of options.

## 1. Introduction

The design of the reward scheme in a crowdfunding campaign, which refers to a deliberate setup of the reward scheme’s disposition to facilitate the crowdfunding performance for achieving the objectives set by the fundraisers, is one of the most prevailing research topics in the crowdfunding research [1–6]. In recent years, the prosperous development of online crowdfunding has attracted more diversified fundraisers than early start-ups. For example, crowdfunding campaigns start to be used as a novel marketing tool by well-established organi zations [7–10]. Besides, competitions amongst crowdfunding campaigns are becoming increasingly intense. Fundraisers make eforts such as personalized recommendation [11,12], e-coupon, and price discount [13] to involve more backers and motivate them to contribute more.

The importance of investigating the impacts of the reward scheme design on crowdfunding performance is further signified by the in novative use of crowdfunding to accomplish the objectives of involving backers and collecting funds. In order to gain insights into the details of such impacts, we propose a multidimensional evaluation framework for crowdfunding performance. In accordance with fundraisers’ marketing and financing motivations, the framework evaluates crowdfunding performance in terms of participation performance and fundraising performance. For participation performance, we examine the variety and volume of backers’ funding behavior. For fundraising performance, we examine the efectiveness and eficiency of fundraising and the success of crowdfunding campaigns. Participation performance and fundraising performance are highly correlated. Fundraisers are free to complete either or both of them, with a carefully designed reward scheme.

Based on our systematic review of the extant literature on crowdfunding, we found that most existing studies on the reward scheme design focus on the definite yield disposition of the reward option and their impacts on the fundraising performance of crowdfunding campaigns, which will strengthen our understanding of the optimal portfolio of the definition yield disposition of the reward option [1–3,5,6]. However, to achieve greater success and more sustainable develop ment, crowdfunding platform owners are motivated to put forward new features on reward scheme design to engage more fundraisers and backers and facilitate deals among them. Under such circumstances, some leading crowdfunding platforms in China took the initiative to provide a new campaign-level promotion instrument that adds stochastic yield disposition to the reward scheme, namely the lottery option.

The introduction of the lottery option significantly changes the landscape of the research on the reward scheme design because of the fact that the fundamental assumption of the extant literature on the reward scheme design, the definite yield disposition, has been challenged by the latest practices from the field. It consequently brings an interesting and novel research question that is worthy to pursue: what are the impacts of the lottery option’s impacts on crowdfunding performance? The underlying logic of establishing this research question is three-fold.

First, the research on the impacts of the lottery option extends the existing theoretical understanding of the reward scheme disposition and its impacts on crowdfunding performance. The lottery option is similar to sweepstakes, which is a stochastic-based promotion strategy for enhancing consumers’ engagements and contributions to business activities [14]. The sweepstakes strategy has been widely adopted in industry practice and investigated in the marketing literature [15–19]. Examination of the lottery option in crowdfunding complements the extant literature on reward scheme design by taking a stochastic yield perspective. It extends the scope of the examination on the reward scheme design with the aim of identifying the appropriate strategy for attracting backers and enhancing fundraising.

Second, we not only examine the lottery option’s impacts on fun draising performance but also investigate its impacts on participation performance. As far as we know, such an investigation has not been conducted before. Compared with the growing industrial eforts in crowdfunding promotion, there is a dearth of dedicated eforts from the academic on investigating relevant research topics. Only a few studies have taken into account the efect of promotional eforts in crowdfunding [20,21]. However, they did not investigate the impacts of the lottery option, nor shed light on its impacts on both the fundraising performance and participation performance [2]. Our study fills the research vacuum, thus providing significant contributions to theory and practice.

Third, in addition to investigate the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns, we also examine its impacts on the performance of existing option types: the reward option and donation option. By this way, we gain insights into the more finegrained influence of the new option type. Therefore, not only crowd funding platform owners and fundraisers but also scholars will have a deeper understanding of the impacts of the new promotional instrument. As far as we know, our study is the pioneer one in exploring the impacts of reward scheme characteristics on crowdfunding performance at the option type level.

To address our research question, we collected a large-scale dataset from a leading crowdfunding platform (www.zhongchou.com) in China and conducted a series of econometric analyses. Our results show that, as expected, the lottery option has significant positive impacts on the variety and volume of participation performance. However, its impacts on fundraising performance are sophisticated. On the one hand, the lottery option is beneficial to increase the amount of funds raised. On the other hand, it has a detrimental efect on the time to achieve crowdfunding success and the success of crowdfunding campaigns. Moreover, we found the inclusion of a lottery option in the reward scheme has a cannibalization efect on the potential backers’ selection of other options in a reward scheme [22].

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. First, we ofer a de tailed discussion of the reward scheme of crowdfunding campaigns and the lottery option. Then, we develop the research hypotheses based on the literature on crowdfunding and sales promotion. After that, we present the variable definitions and summary statistics. This is followed by the empirical model and results. Finally, we conclude this study with the discussion on the findings, theoretical implications, practical implications, and limitations.

## 2. Research contexts

## 2.1. The reward scheme of crowdfunding campaigns

When fundraisers intend to initiate a new crowdfunding campaign, they are asked to submit information about it in accordance with the requirements of the crowdfunding platform. Among the information provided, a reward scheme is a critical part [6]. The reward scheme contains the available options of a crowdfunding campaign. For each option, the fundraiser should specify its name, description, price, and reward to backers as well as the time of reward delivery. The reward can be a physical one such as a pre-sale product or a virtual one such as an email of gratitude message.

Diferent crowdfunding platforms impose diferent rules on the backing for the options in the reward scheme of crowdfunding campaigns. Some crowdfunding platforms such as KickStarter regulate that a backer can only back a copy of an option in the reward scheme of a crowdfunding campaign. Others such as Zhongchou.com allow a backer to back multiple copies of multiple options in the reward scheme of a crowdfunding campaign.

Most crowdfunding platforms provide two types of options in their reward scheme: the reward option and the donation option. In crowdfunding platforms such as KickStarter and Zhongchou.com, the reward option is the dominant option type. For the reward option, there is a specific price for each option. Backers will receive material or virtual reward in return for their support. Crowdfunding campaigns usually ofer a tiered, bundled, or hybrid system of the reward options. For the donation option, there is no specific price. Backers are free to pay any amount of money. They will receive no material return or just a letter of thanks for their support. In most cases, there is just one donation option in each crowdfunding campaign. In Zhongchou.com, the platform provides a default donation option for each crowdfunding campaign and three suggested backing amounts for the donation option. Furthermore, backers of the donation option will receive no return for their support.

## 2.2. The lottery option

The lottery option is newly introduced by leading crowdfunding platforms in China, including Zhongchou.com [2]. It is similar to the reward option except that backers of the lottery option have a very small chance of winning the ofered prize. In Zhongchou.com, only one lottery option could be included in the reward scheme of a crowd funding campaign.

When setting a lottery option, the fundraisers should specify its price, reward, the number of rewards, and frequency of drawing a prize. It is noteworthy that the price of the lottery option is fairly low in most cases. For most crowdfunding campaigns, the lottery option’s price is as low as 1 RMB (USD 0.15). Meanwhile, the prize is usually the same as the focal product or service in the reward scheme. The number of prizes can be one or multiple. If the number of prizes is multiple, the fundraisers must specify the frequency of drawing a prize. The frequency of drawing a prize is specified in forms of “Draw a lucky one from every n backers,” where n refers to the frequency of drawing a prize. If the number of prizes is one, there is no need to specify the frequency of drawing a prize.

The lottery option in crowdfunding campaigns takes the form of the two-stage lottery [23]. In the first stage, backers are awarded the lottery numbers after their backing. In the second stage, the prize numbers are automatically drawn by the crowdfunding platform using predefined rules after the successful end of the crowdfunding campaign. The delayed resolution of uncertainty in the two-stage lottery is believed to be more preferable to an immediate resolution in the ordinary lottery, especially for risk-seeking shoppers and products and categories that attract a variety-seeking mind-set [17,23].

As mentioned earlier, there is no limitation on how a backer can back the options in the reward scheme of a crowdfunding campaign in Zhongchou.com. A backer can freely back the donation option, lottery option, and reward option in multiple transactions. In each transaction, he/she can back any number of copies of an option.

## 3. Literature review and hypotheses

## 3.1. The influence factors of the success of crowdfunding campaigns

The most concerned issue for all crowdfunding stakeholders is how to achieve crowdfunding success in all-or-nothing mode. A crowdfunding campaign is succeeded when the amount of funds raised reaches the fundraising goal before the end of its fundraising period [21]. If a crowdfunding campaign fails to raise enough funds by the end of its fundraising period, the fundraiser will get nothing, and the backers will get the refund but sufer from the opportunity cost. Meanwhile, because most crowdfunding platforms make a profit by charging a ratio of the funds raised by successful campaigns, they will also get nothing from failed campaigns.

Extensive research eforts have been made to explore various factors that have an influence on crowdfunding success. Scholars have focused on the characteristics of crowdfunding campaigns [4,21,24], eWOM [24], textual information [4,21,25], and geographical factors [21,26–28], etc. Others have paid attention to the impacts of the fun draisers’ social network and social capital [21,29,30], the fundraisers gender [31], and the dynamics of backers’ behavior [8,32].

In addition, there are a few studies that have considered the efect of promotional eforts. For instance, Mollick verified the efect of the appearance on the homepage of online crowdfunding platforms in helping crowdfunding campaigns reach their funding goal [21], and Lu et al. demonstrated the strong correlation between the number of backers and the volume of promotional activities for crowdfunding campaigns that launch promotional activities in social media within their cam paign duration [20].

## 3.2. Evaluation framework of crowdfunding performance

In the early days of online crowdfunding, fundraisers initiate crowdfunding campaigns to obtain funds. Their most concerned issues include whether a crowdfunding campaign can collect enough funds, how long does a crowdfunding campaign collect enough funds, and how a crowdfunding campaign can collect as many funds as possible. With the burgeoning of online crowdfunding, the intentions of fun draisers begin to diversify. Except for serving as an appealing financing instrument, it has become an increasingly popular marketing tool. For the fundraisers with marketing motivations, their most concerned issue is how many backers will be involved in their crowdfunding campaigns and be aware of their products and services through crowdfunding participation [7].

Based on the above considerations, we propose a multidimensional evaluation framework that is discussed in the introduction and is elaborated in Table 1. The framework evaluates crowdfunding performance in terms of participation performance and fundraising performance, which is in accordance with fundraisers’ marketing and financial motivations. In addition, some measures in the framework are also indicators of crowdfunding campaigns’ popularity and participation risk. As far as we know, there is no study that has proposed such a framework with which we can have a better understanding of crowd funding performance from the perspective of fundraisers’ motivations.

## 3.2.1. Participation performance

For participation performance, the framework includes (1) the number of backers, which measures the variety of backers, and (2) the number of copies, which measures the volume of backers’ participation. A backer can back multiple transactions for a crowdfunding campaign. He/she can also back multiple copies for a single option in one transaction in Zhongchou.com. Therefore, the number of backers will not be larger than the number of copies. Despite their slight diferences in quantity, the two measures are highly correlated.

The variety of participation performance is described using the number of backers, i.e., the number of unique backers of a crowdfunding campaign. It indicates how many backers have been involved in a crowdfunding campaign. The measure is especially important for the fundraisers who launch crowdfunding campaigns for marketing purposes. Although the number of backers is not displayed on the profile pages or listing pages of crowdfunding campaigns, the scale of it can be inferred from the number of copies.

The volume of participation performance is described using the number of copies. The measure is important because it indicates the popularity of crowdfunding campaigns. It is widely investigated and verified that popularity information has salient impacts on consumers’ decision and behavior in many online contexts, which will bring the prominent efect of herding and strengthen the sales performance [32–35]. Thus, it is reasonable to conclude that popularity information will have a similar efect in online crowdfunding context and influence the fundraising performance. In Zhongchou.com, the number of copie is prominently displayed on both the listing pages of crowdfunding platforms and the profile pages of crowdfunding campaigns.

## 3.2.2. Fundraising performance

For fundraising performance, the framework includes (1) the amount of funds raised, which measures fundraising efectiveness, (2) the time to achieve crowdfunding success, which measures fundraising eficiency, and (3) whether the crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed, which measures fundraising success.

Fundraising efectiveness is described using the amount of funds raised. It is crucial for the fundraisers who launch crowdfunding campaigns for financing purposes. Similar to the number of copies, it is also prominently displayed on the listing pages of crowdfunding platforms and the profile pages of crowdfunding campaigns. The measure is regarded as a key popularity indicator of crowdfunding campaigns, which will influence other measures of crowdfunding performance, such as the number of copies. The amount of funds raised is the key concern of the fundraisers and backers because a crowdfunding campaign is successful if and only if the amount of funds raised by it reaches the fundraising goal. Even when the amount of funds raised exceeds the fundraising goal, more funds are preferable.

Fundraising eficiency is described using the time to achieve crowdfunding success. It is important because prior studies have demonstrated that the early success of crowdfunding campaigns is im portant to their ultimate success [36]. Given two similar crowdfunding campaigns, the one that raises the expected amount of funds earlier is usually regarded as more attractive. The time to achieve crowdfunding success is neither displayed on listing pages or profile pages of crowdfunding campaigns nor can it be easily inferred from any displayed information. However, backers can perceive it through the joint analysis of the fundraising goal, the amount of funds raised, the fundraising percentage, and the time remaining until the end of the crowdfunding campaigns.

Success is described using whether the crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed. It is the fundamental measure of fundraising performance because all crowdfunding stakeholders including the fundraisers, backers, and crowdfunding platform owners could get what they want under the premise that it is no less than their fundraising goal in all-or-nothing mode. In particular, the measure is equally important for the fundraisers with financing and marketing motivations. Moreover, it is also a key indicator of participation risk for backers. As it is well-known, backers’ participation in successful crowdfunding campaigns is risk-free. However, their participation in the crowdfunding campaigns that have not achieved crowdfunding success will bear the opportunity cost. Although backers will get a refund, they cannot obtain the desired products or services after long waiting for the end of the crowdfunding campaign if it fails to collect enough funds.

## 3.3. Hypotheses

Based on our proposed multidimensional evaluation framework, we investigate the lottery option’s impacts on crowdfunding performance from two perspectives. First, we examine the lottery option’s impacts on the participation performance and fundraising performance of crowdfunding campaigns. The results ofer us a better understanding of how the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will influence diferent aspects of the performance of crowdfunding campaigns. Next, we delve into the lottery option’s impacts on the participation performance and fundraising performance of the existing option types in cluding the reward option and donation option. The results provide us with the more fine-grained impacts that the new option type has on crowdfunding performance. Consequently, we are capable of having some ideas of how the impacts take place.

For the sake of clarity in the following discussion, we hereby provide the definitions of potential backers and backers. Potential backers are the users who have visited the profile page of a crowdfunding campaign but have not backed any option in its reward scheme. Backers are the users who have backed at least an option in the reward scheme.

Fig. 1 presents an overview of the hypotheses in our study. We posit five hypotheses about the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns and two hypotheses about its impacts on the performance of existing option types. The former intends to in vestigate how the lottery option will influence the participation per formance and fundraising performance of crowdfunding campaigns. The latter aims to examine how the lottery option will influence the participation performance and fundraising performance of the reward option and donation option.

In consideration of the participation performance, we posit that the lottery option is beneficial to the variety and volume of participation performance. First of all, the lottery option usually has a significantly lower price than the reward options [2]. For instance, backers can get the opportunity to win the prize by just contributing 1 RMB (USD 0.15) to back the lottery option in many crowdfunding campaigns. The low price of the lottery option is quite attractive to many undecided or hesitant potential backers who are interested in a crowdfunding campaign but do not intend to participate in it through backing the reward option or donation option. Moreover, the low price is an important factor that will directly trigger impulse buying in both ofline retail environments and online contexts [37–40]. In this regard, the lottery option provides an additional avenue that allows a large portion of potential backers to participate in crowdfunding campaigns.

Second, the lottery option introduces the possibilities of having excess profits to backers. For a conventional lottery option, it could yield a large amount of payback that may worth over hundreds of times of the cost that backers paid for. Consequently, the lottery option acts as an efective promotion tool. Its role in online crowdfunding is quite similar to what its counterpart of sweepstakes plays in sales promotion [16,17]. Because backers are considered to be more risk-seeking and variety-seeking than consumers, the efect of the lottery option in online crowdfunding is more prominent than the sweepstakes in sales promotion. As a result, many potential backers are expected to be motivated to become backers by backing the lottery option.

Third, the lottery option can bring fun for backers in online crowdfunding as what various forms of lotteries do [54]. In contrast to the extrinsic motivation of reward hunting, the intrinsic motivation of fun is also an important motivation to participate in many online contexts [41,42,43–45]. Therefore, the lottery option can ofer additional value to the backers who are not interested in getting prizes. Consequently, it can increase the crowdfunding campaigns’ attractiveness to potential backers with intrinsic motivations.

In summary, the lottery option provides potential backers with the opportunity to gain high-value prize and fun at a low cost. Thus, it facilitates the achievement of fundraising goal by enlarging the potential backers’ base [46]. Furthermore, the increase in the number of backers will cause an increase in the number of copies ultimately. Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

H1. The lottery option has a positive impact on the variety of participation performance.

H2. The lottery option has a positive impact on the volume of participation performance.

Fig. 1. An overview of the hypotheses.

As mentioned earlier, more potential backers will become backers through backing the lottery option. However, it is worth noting that the lottery option is a campaign level promotional instrument, not a platform level promotional instrument. There are no significant visual cues for the lottery option on the homepage and listing pages in Zhongchou.com. That is, users are not aware of whether there is a lottery option in the reward scheme of a crowdfunding campaign until they visit its profile page and become the potential backers. Thus, users who are merely interested in backing the lottery option but not interested in pledging a crowdfunding campaign will not be attracted to the profile page of the crowdfunding campaign by any visual cues of the lottery option on the homepage and listing pages of the crowdfunding platform. Consequently, the number of potential backers for a crowdfunding campaign will not increase just because there is a lottery option in the reward scheme.

In summary, the lottery option has a two-sided efect on the amount of funds raised by crowdfunding campaigns. On the one hand, backers who are merely attracted by the lottery option add new funds. However, it is worth noting that the amount of funds collected from these backers will be limited because of the low price of the lottery option in most crowdfunding campaigns. On the other hand, the features of the lottery option we mentioned above also make it attractive to the potential backers who will back the reward option or donation option if there is no lottery option.

In consideration of the prominent price gap between the lottery option and the reward option, the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will induce a significant reduction in the amount of funds raised by the reward option even if there are only a few potential backers who intend to back the reward option but ultimately choose the lottery option. Similarly, it will also induce a reduction in the amount of funds raised by the donation option. Moreover, the cannibalization efect of the lottery option to the reward option and donation option in the amount of funds raised is supposed to be even larger in consideration of the prominent herding efect in online contexts [47]. Specifically, the option level backing decisions of preceding backers will influence that of subsequent backers. Eventually, the overall impact of the lottery option on the amount of funds raised depends on the strength of the two opposite impacts.

Based on the considerations above, we believe that the reduction induced by the decrease of the amount of funds raised by the reward option is the dominant aspect. Thus, we posit that the lottery option will have a negative impact on the amount of funds raised. Moreover, the time to achieve crowdfunding success will be longer for crowd funding campaigns with the lottery option. Consequently, the lottery option will be detrimental to the success of crowdfunding campaigns. Thus, we propose the following hypotheses:

H3. The lottery option has a negative impact on the efectiveness of fundraising performance.

H4. The lottery option has a negative impact on the eficiency of fundraising performance.

H5. The lottery option has a negative impact on the success of fundraising performance.

For the purpose of gaining insight into the lottery option’s impacts on existing option types, we decompose its impacts on the volume of participation performance and the efectiveness of fundraising performance by the existing option types of the reward option and donation option.

To facilitate the development of hypotheses, we illustrate the impacts of the lottery option on the participation performance and fundraising performance of the reward option and donation option in Fig. 2. As discussed previously, the lottery option will not change the number of potential backers of a crowdfunding campaign because it is a campaign level promotional instrument. We divide the potential backers of a crowdfunding campaign without the lottery option into three groups by their backing intentions: the potential backers who intend to back the reward option, the potential backers who intend to back the donation option, and the potential backers who do not intend to back any option.

![](/api/attachments/NSAMQW9Q/fulltext/images/11834eb7d5b8cdfd4639c8915192ece5609ba6987b120832d88eb11a254e6168.jpg)  
Fig. 2. The lottery option’s impacts on existing option types.

When the lottery option is included in the reward scheme, there are potential backers who will change their intentions. On the one hand, some potential backers who do not intend to back any option will become the backers of the lottery option. On the other hand, some potential backers who intend to back the reward option or donation option will change their minds and turn to back the lottery option. Therefore, the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will decrease the number of backers and the amount of funds raised by the reward option and donation option. In other words, the lottery option will be detrimental to the variety of participation performance and the efectiveness of fundraising performance for both the reward option and donation option. Thus, we posit the following hypotheses:

H6. The lottery option has negative impacts on the volume of participation performance for existing option types.

H7. The lottery option has negative impacts on the efectiveness of fundraising performance for existing option types.

## 4. Data and variables

## 4.1. Data

We collected the data from Zhongchou.com, which is one of the largest crowdfunding platforms in China, using a self-developed web crawler. The platform supports reward-based and charitable crowdfunding in all-or-nothing mode [21]. Zhongchou.com ofers six categories of crowdfunding campaigns: commonwealth, publishing, agriculture, art, entertainment, and others. Crowdfunding campaigns in the category of the commonwealth are charitable, whereas the crowdfunding campaigns in the last five categories are reward-based.

Since its inception in April 2013, this platform has supported more than 21,000 crowdfunding campaigns as of July 22, 2016. The first crowdfunding campaign with the platform defined lottery option started to raise funds on November 12, 2015. There are 5,530 crowd funding campaigns that have been initiated and completed as of July 22, 2016. Within these crowdfunding campaigns, 1,396 are successful. The success rate is 25.24%. Of the 5,530 crowdfunding campaigns, 1,961 ofer the lottery option, resulting in a success rate of 35.46%. Specifically, for the 4,666 reward-based crowdfunding campaigns, the success rate is 20.10% and the rate of crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option is 38%; for the 864 charitable crowdfunding campaigns, the success rate is 53.01% and the rate of crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option is 21.76%. It is clear that the lottery option is more popular in reward-based crowdfunding campaigns, whereas charitable crowdfunding campaigns have a higher success rate.

## 4.2. Variables

Definitions and descriptive statistics of the dependent variables, independent variable, and control variables are presented in Table 2.

## 4.2.1. Dependent variables and independent variable

The dependent variables and the independent variable are developed in the following way. We have three groups of dependent variables. The first group of dependent variables consists of the number of backers, the number of copies, the amount of funds raised, and the time to achieve crowdfunding success, and whether a crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed. They describe the participation performance and fundraising performance of crowdfunding campaigns. The second and third groups of dependent variables include the number of copies and the amount of funds raised by the reward option and donation option, respectively, which describe the participation performance and fundraising performance of the two types of options. Except for whether a crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed, we use the logarithmic form of the other dependent variables due to their skewed nature. We code whether a crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed (1 and 0, respectively). The independent variable is whether a lottery option is ofered in the reward scheme of a crowdfunding campaign.

## 4.2.2. Control variable

Based on the literature on crowdfunding and sales promotion, we choose five characteristics of crowdfunding campaigns and three characteristics of fundraisers as the control variables. The five campaign characteristics include the number of options in the reward scheme, the fundraising goal, the duration, the number of images displayed on the profile page, and whether there is a video clip displayed on the profile page. For the number of images displayed on the profile page, we use the logarithmic form. The three fundraiser’s characteristics include the number of completed crowdfunding campaigns that fundraiser has launched before initiating the current one, the rate of success for the completed crowdfunding campaigns that fundraiser of the project has launched before initiating the current one, and the authentication type of the fundraiser. For the first two variables, we use their logarithmic form.

## 5. Empirical model and results

## 5.1. Propensity score matching (PSM)

Self-selection bias may arise if the fundraisers of crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option are systematically diferent from the fundraisers of crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option in their decisions on whether to include the lottery option. To attenuate the influence of self-selection bias, we apply propensity score matching, a commonly used approach for correcting such bias [48–50]. We con struct a sample that includes both the crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option and an equal number of crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option using propensity score matching based on the one-to-one nearest-neighbor matching (without caliper and replace ment) algorithm.

We compute the propensity score using the characteristics of crowdfunding campaigns and fundraisers. The characteristics of crowdfunding campaign include: (1) whether the crowdfunding campaign is a charitable one or a reward-based one; (2) the number of options in the reward scheme; and (3) the natural logarithm of the fundraising goal. The characteristics of fundraisers include: (1) the natural logarithm of the number of completed crowdfunding campaigns that the fundraiser has launched before initiating the current one; (2) the natural logarithm of the success rate of the completed crowdfunding campaigns that the fundraiser has launched before initiating the current one; (3) whether the fundraiser has set a personalized avatar; and (4) whether the fundraiser has set a personalized nickname. We believe that the set of covariates is comprehensive and informative, such that the covariates influence the treatment assignment but are not afected by the treatment.

## 5.2. Econometric model specification

To investigate the lottery option’s impacts on the dependent variables, we use ordinary least squares (OLS) regression and logistic regression on the matched samples. For the dependent variable of whether a crowdfunding campaign is successful or failed (Success ), we use logistic regression:

$$
\operatorname{Logit} \left(S u c c e s s _ {i}\right) = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} H a s L o t t e r y _ {i} + \gamma \alpha_ {i} + \delta \varphi_ {i} + \varepsilon_ {i}\tag{1}
$$

For other dependent variables, we use OLS regression:

$$
Y _ {i} = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} H a s L o t t e r y _ {i} + \gamma \alpha_ {i} + \delta \varphi_ {i} + \varepsilon_ {i}\tag{2}
$$

where Y denotes two vectors of dependent variables. The first vector of dependent variables is used to investigate the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns. It includes the natural logarithm of the number of backers (lnBackerNum ), the number of copies (lnCopyNum ), the amount of funds raised (lnFundAmount<sub>i</sub>), and the time to achieve crowdfunding success (lnSuccessDays ). The second vector of dependent variables is used to investigate the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of the reward option. It includes the natural logarithm of the number of copies (lnRCopyNum ) and the amount of funds raised (lnRFundAmount ) for the reward option. The third vector of dependent variables is used to investigate the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of the donation option. It includes the natural logarithm of the number of copies (lnDCopyNum ) and the amount of funds raised (lnDFundAmount<sub>i</sub>) for the donation option. HasLottery is a binary variable indicating whether the lottery option is included in the reward scheme.

For control variables, we include a set of campaign characteristics and a set of fundraiser characteristics. <sub>i</sub> denotes the set of campaign characteristics including the number of options in the reward scheme (OptionNum ), the natural logarithm of the fundraising goal (lnFundGoal ), the duration (Duration ), the natural logarithm of the number of images presented on the profile page (lnImageNum ), and whether there is a video clip presented on the profile page (HasVideo ). In addition, we control for unobserved heterogeneity across diferent campaign categories and geographical locations with categorical dummies and province dummies, respectively. To capture the possibi lity that crowdfunding campaigns are launched more in certain year, month, or day of week, we include dummy variables for the year, month, and day of the week of the crowdfunding campaigns’ start time.

## 5.4. Robustness checks

In this section, we examine the robustness of our major findings with a series of tests. First of all, we estimate a seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) model to check the robustness of our findings in consideration of the significant correlation among the dependent vari ables. Second, we change the estimators of PSM to kernel matching to test the robustness of the results with regard to the matching estimators. Then, in order to check the robustness of our findings in respect of the outliers, we exclude the crowdfunding campaigns of which the fun draising goal or the amount of funds raised is extremely large. Next, we confirm the robustness of our findings with regard to the alternative performance measures of crowdfunding campaigns and existing option types. After that, we conduct an analysis of collinearity by calculating the variance inflation factors (VIFs) associated with the independent variable and control variables. Finally, we have a discussion on the counterintuitive results of the lottery option’s impacts on the efectiveness measure and success measure of crowdfunding campaigns.

## 5.4.1. Seemingly unrelated regression

To account for the dependent variables except for the measures for the eficiency and success of fundraising performance, we estimate a SUR model to check the robustness of our findings for H1˜H3 and H6˜H7 [51,52]. Although the eficiency measure of fundraising performance is significantly correlated with other crowdfunding performance measures, we do not include it because it is missing for failed campaigns.

Tables A1 and A2 present the estimation results of the SUR model. Table A1 presents the estimation results for the impacts of the lottery option on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns, which are consistent with that of the OLS regression for H1˜H3. Table A2 presents the estimation results for the impacts of the lottery option on the performance of existing option types, which are also consistent with that of the OLS regression for H6 and H7. In summary, our findings are robust in consideration of the significant correlation among the dependent variables.

## 5.4.2. Kernel matching

To check the robustness of our findings regarding matching estimator of PSM, we apply kernel matching as the matching estimator when performing PSM and conduct the regression analysis again based on the matched sample. In contrast to one-to-one nearly neighbor matching, kernel matching is nonparametric. It uses weighted averages of the sample in the control group to construct the counterfactual outcome. Therefore, kernel matching is supposed to be able to lower the estimation variance because more information is used in the estimation process [48].

Tables A3 and A4 present the estimation results after kernel matching. Table A13 presents the estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns after kernel matching. Table A4 presents the estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of existing option types after kernel matching. The results are consistent with that after one-to-one nearly neighbor matching. Thus, our findings are robust in regard to the matching estimators of propensity score matching.

## 5.4.3. Outlier exclusion

To check the robustness of our findings in respect of the outliers, we exclude the crowdfunding campaigns of which the fundraising goal or the amount of funds raised is extremely large. In consideration of the fundraising goal, we treat those crowdfunding campaigns of which the fundraising goal is larger than 500,000 RMB as the outliers. After excluding the 65 outliers, we have 5,465 samples in the dataset for analysis. Then, we perform PSM and apply one-to-one nearly neighbor matching as the matching estimator. After that, we conduct regression analysis again based on the matched sample. Tables A5 and A6 present the estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns and the existing option types, respectively. The results are consistent with our main results. In consideration of the amount of funds raised, we take those crowdfunding campaigns of which the amount of funds raised is larger than 500,000 RMB as the outliers. After excluding the 4 outliers, we have 5,526 samples in the dataset for analysis. We perform the same matching and conduct the same regression analyses again. Tables A7 and A8 present the new estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns and the existing option types, respectively. The results are also consistent with our main results. Thus, our findings are robust in regard to the outliers.

5.4.4. Alternative measures for the performance of crowdfunding campaigns

Aside from the measures in the multidimensional evaluation framework, there are two alternative measures for the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns: the number of transactions and the fundraising percentage.

The number of transactions is the alternative measure for the volume of participation performance. As discussed earlier, a backer can not only initiate multiple transactions for a crowdfunding campaign but also back multiple copies for a single option in one transaction in Zhongchou.com. Thus, the number of transactions will be no less than the number of backers and no larger than the number of copies. That is, the three measures are highly and positively correlated.

The fundraising percentage is the alternative measure for the effectiveness of fundraising performance. It is the ratio of the amount of funds raised to the fundraising goal. Similar to the number of copies and the amount of funds raised, the measure is also prominently displayed on both the listing pages of crowdfunding platforms and the profile pages of crowdfunding campaigns. The fundraising percentage is regarded as an important indicator of the crowdfunding campaigns’ popularity and the backers’ participation risk. As the indicator of crowdfunding campaigns’ popularity, it will significantly influence other performance measures including the number of copies and the amount of funds raised. As the indicator of backers’ participation risk, it is more specific than the success of crowdfunding campaigns. In general, backers’ participation is risky when the fundraising percentage of a crowdfunding campaign is less than 100%. In particular, the risk of backers’ participation is large when the fundraising percentage is small. It is even larger when the fundraising percentage is small and the crowdfunding campaign is approaching its ending.

To test the lottery option’s impacts on the two alternative performance measures of crowdfunding campaigns, we run the re gression analysis defined by Eq. (2) using the two measures as the dependent variables.

Column (1) of Table A9 reports the estimation result of the lottery option’s impact on the number of transactions, which shows that the lottery option has a significant positive impact on the number of transactions. That is, the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will increase the number of transactions. On average, crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option will have 46.6% more transactions than crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option. The result is consistent with the lottery option’s impact on the volume measure of participation performance.

Column (2) of Table A9 reports the estimation result of the lottery option’s impact on the fundraising percentage, which shows that the lottery option has a negative but not significant impact on the fundraising percentage. That is, the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will decrease the fundraising percentage. On average, crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option will have 8% lower fundraising percentage than crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option. The result is consistent with the lottery option’s impact on the success measure of fundraising performance. However, it is inconsistent with the lottery option’s impact on the amount of funds raised. This inconsistency can be explained by the t-test results of the fundraising goal between the crowdfunding campaigns with and without the lottery option.

As presented in Table A10, the mean of the fundraising goal for crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option is larger than the mean of the fundraising goal for crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option. The relationship remains both before and after propensity score matching. Moreover, the results provide a clear illustration of the seeming inconsistency between the lottery option’s impacts on the effectiveness and success measures of the fundraising performance fo crowdfunding campaigns. That is, crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option usually have higher fundraising goal than crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option. Thus, although the crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option can collect more funds, they have a lower fundraising percentage and less possibility of achieving successful fundraising.

## 5.4.5. Alternative measures for the performance of existing option types campaigns

Similar to the situation discussed in the previous section, there is an alternative measure for the efectiveness of the fundraising performance for existing option types: the fundraising percentage. The fundraising percentage for the reward/donation option is the ratio of the amount of funds raised by the reward/donation option to the fundraising goal. As mentioned earlier, when the lottery option is included, some potential backers who intend to back the reward option or donation option will change their intentions and turn to back the lottery option. Consequently, both the number of backers and the amount of funds raised by the reward option and donation option will decrease because of the cannibalization efect that the lottery option imposes on the potential backers’ selection of the reward option and donation option. Thus, we expect that the lottery option will have negative impacts on the fundraising percentage of the reward option and donation option.

Columns (3) and (4) of Table A6 report the estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the fundraising percentage of the reward option and donation option, respectively, which show that the lottery option has significant negative impacts on the fundraising percentage of the two types of options. That is, the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will decrease the fundraising percentage of the reward option and donation option. On average, the reward option and donation option in crowdfunding campaigns with the lottery option will have 17.1% and 9.5% lower fundraising percentage than the reward option and donation option in the crowdfunding campaigns without the lottery option. The results are consistent with the lottery option’s impacts on the efectiveness measure of the fundraising performance for existing option types.

## 5.4.7. The counterintuitive results of the impacts on the measures of crowdfunding performance

To get a deeper understanding of the counterintuitive results of the lottery option’s impacts on the efectiveness measure and success measure of crowdfunding campaigns, we conduct a quantile regression analysis on the matched sample generated by the PSM approach that applies one-to-one nearly neighbor matching as the matching estimator. We take the fundraising percentage as the dependent variable of quantile regression. Table A12 presents the quantiles of the fundraising percentage of crowdfunding campaigns. Table A13 presents the estimation results of the lottery option’s impacts on the fundraising percentage of crowdfunding campaigns by quantile regression.

As depicted in Fig. 4, it is interesting that the lottery option’s impact on the fundraising percentage changes with the quantiles. When the quantile is small, the impact is positive and significant. With the increase of the quantile, the impact changes to negative but not significant first, and to negative and significant later, and to negative but not significant finally. Overall, the lottery option poses a negative but not significant impact on the fundraising percentage, which is presented in column (2) of Table A9.

Based on the analysis above, we propose an explanation of the seemingly conflicting results of the lottery option’s impacts on the effectiveness measure and success measure of crowdfunding campaigns. Although the inclusion of the lottery option helps to increase the amount of funds raised, its impact on the fundraising percentage is complicated. When the fundraising percentage is low, the impact is positive. That is, the inclusion of the lottery option does help to increase the fundraising percentage. However, the increase is not enough to turn the crowdfunding campaigns that would otherwise fail into successful crowdfunding campaigns. When the fundraising percentage is high, the impact is negative. That is, the inclusion of the lottery option will decrease the fundraising percentage. As a result, the inclusion of the lottery option is detrimental to the success of crowdfunding campaigns.

## 6. Discussion and contributions

## 6.1. Discussion of findings

The data analysis results of our proposed multidimensional framework provide strong support to the hypotheses testing. We also performed a series of tests to assess the robustness of our analysis results. The results demonstrate that our findings are reliable. In particular, we not only evaluate the lottery option’s impacts on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns but also gain insight into its impacts on the performance of existing option types. In summary, our research has several interesting findings as follows.

First of all, we find that the lottery option has strong positive and significant impacts on the variety and volume of the participation performance for crowdfunding campaigns. This finding is consistent with our expectations. The results suggest that the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme makes crowdfunding as a desirable marketing tool. Thus, it is a wise decision for the fundraisers who in tend to use crowdfunding for marketing purposes.

## 6.2. Theoretical contributions

Owing to the unique findings, this paper contributes to online crowdfunding literature by ofering insights into how the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme will influence crowdfunding performance. In particular, this paper provides three-fold theoretical implications to the literature on crowdfunding.

First of all, this paper provides a salient extension of the theoretical understanding of the reward scheme design in the online crowdfunding context. As the reward scheme is the only source where backers get information about their contributions and returns, the design of it provides a direct and fundamental modality of shifting crowdfunding performance. Prior studies on the reward scheme design mainly adopted the assumption that the reward scheme disposition design, such as the reward option, should be built upon the information of definite yield. With the appropriation of crowdfunding as a novel marketing tool in the recent online crowdfunding marketplace, it is imperative to examine any potential reward scheme designs that go beyond the classical definition and their impacts on crowdfunding performance. Our study fills the gap by examining a general type of extension on the reward scheme design with the consideration of the stochastic yield information. We contextualize our research to the scenario in which a typical reward scheme disposition with stochastic yield information is provided: the lottery option. Our study shows that the lottery option would significantly influence the performance of crowdfunding campaigns. In this regard, our study sheds light on extending the fundamental principle of reward scheme design with the consideration of the possible stochastic yield information embedded in the reward scheme disposition.

Second, this paper contributes to the extant literature by providing a granular examination of the crowdfunding performance from both the fundraising and participation perspective. The context of developing a crowdfunding campaign has been significantly shifted in recent years from a pure approach for fundraising to a dynamic tool for either raising money, or advertising, or both. The phenomenon in the conventional online crowdfunding market implies that the examination on a single aspect of performance for crowdfunding campaigns may not provide a well understanding of the initiatives of crowdfunding. To address this theoretical concern, we anchored on the extant literature of online crowdfunding and developed a multidimensional evaluation framework to examine crowdfunding performance systematically. The utilization of a multidimensional evaluation framework further ad vances our understanding of the impacts of the lottery option.

Third, our paper contributes to the theoretical understanding of the inter-related efects amongst the diferent types of options in the reward scheme. We provide implications to the reward option design by con textualizing the inter-related efects between the lottery option, reward option, and donation option. In this regard, we ofer a fine-grained picture of the lottery option’s impacts on the reward option and donation option. Our study shows that the lottery option has a cannibalization efect on the utilization of the two types of options. Therefore, not only crowdfunding platform owners and fundraisers but also scholars can have a deeper understanding of the impacts of the new promotional instrument. As far as we know, our study is the pioneer one in exploring the impacts of reward scheme characteristics on crowdfunding performance at the option type level.

## 6.3. Practical implications

Aside from theoretical contributions, our paper also has some important implications for practitioners. The findings of our study suggest that the inclusion of the lottery option could help fundraisers attract a large amount of backers. At the same time, it can also help increase the amount of funds raised. The results pose an interesting tradeof fo including the lottery option in the reward scheme. Overall, the fun draisers should exercise care in making their decision regarding whe ther to include the lottery option in terms of their motivations to launch crowdfunding campaigns and confidence in achieving crowdfunding success. In particular, if the primary motivation is marketing, the in clusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme of their crowd funding campaigns is supposed to be a wise decision because it does help to involve much more backers. To enjoy the benefits brought by the lottery option, however, the fundraisers need to be well-prepared on the negative consequences including a long-time to achieve crowdfunding success and the reduced success likelihood of their crowdfunding campaigns. In contrast, if the fundraisers’ primary motivation is financing, whether to include the lottery option in the reward scheme of their crowdfunding campaigns is also a tough or harmful choice. It is because although the lottery option will help to increase the amount of funds raised, it will also decrease the amount of funds raised and the possibility of achieving crowdfunding success.

## 6.4. Limitations

Although our research has highlighted several notable findings, we acknowledge some limitations. First, the dataset used in our study is collected from a major Chinese crowdfunding platform that supports reward-based and charitable campaigns in all-or-nothing mode. Thus, the findings of this paper may sufer the limitation on the generalizability to crowdfunding platforms of diferent size, in diferent countries, with diferent crowdfunding mechanisms, and support different types of campaigns etc. To address this concern, future works can use datasets collected from other crowdfunding platforms. Second, our study is based on the analysis of cross-sectional data, which have inherent limitations in exploring the causal relations compared with panel data. The results will be more persuasive if the empirical analysis is made with panel data. Finally, our work only uncovers the impacts of the inclusion of the lottery option in the reward scheme on crowdfunding performance. Future studies may delve deeply into how specific design features of the lottery option will influence the backers’ decisions and the performance of crowdfunding campaigns.

## 7. Conclusions

With the burgeoning of online crowdfunding across the world, the reward scheme design of crowdfunding campaigns is attracting increasing attention from the practitioners and scholars because the reward scheme is the only source where the backers get information about their contributions and returns. To achieve greater success and more sustainable development, owners of crowdfunding platforms are motivated to put forward new features on reward scheme design to engage more fundraisers and backers and facilitate the deals among them. The lottery option is a new campaign-level promotion instrument that adds stochastic yield disposition to the reward scheme. It is expected that the lottery option will boost crowdfunding performance as its sweepstakes counterpart does in sales promotion. However, the results of our analysis show that despite the use of the lottery option is a wise decision for some fundraisers, it is a harmful choice for others.

We find the evidence for the positive and negative impacts of the lottery option using a unique dataset from Zhongchou.com. The im plications of the lottery option for the performance of crowdfunding campaigns and existing option types are more sophisticated than what the crowdfunding platform owners and fundraisers expect. Our findings are of practical importance for them to understand how the lottery option really works and properly apply it to meet their specific goals. Our work provides a salient extension on the theoretical understanding of the reward scheme design in the online crowdfunding context. It not only contributes to the extant literature by providing a granular examination of the crowdfunding performance from both the fundraising and participation perspective but also adds to the theoretical under standing of the inter-related efects amongst the diferent types of options in the reward scheme.

His articles have appeared in leading information systems conferences and journals such as Information & Management, Decision Support Systems, and Information Systems Frontiers. His current research interests in clude crowdsourcing, online personalization, and digital commerce.

Mengxiang Li is an assistant professor of Information Systems at the Department of Finance and Decision Sciences, School of Business, Hong Kong Baptist University. He received his Ph.D. degree in Information Systems from the City University of Hong Kong. He has coauthored in leading information systems journals such as MIS Quarterly, Journal of Management Information Systems, Information & Management, Decision Support Systems, and International Journal of Electronic Commerce. His articles appeared in premium information system conferences such as the International Conference on Information Systems and the Pacific Asia Conference on Information Systems. His current research interests include the business information systems and analytics, usercentered technology design, digital commerce, innovation and IT implementation, and NeuroIS.
