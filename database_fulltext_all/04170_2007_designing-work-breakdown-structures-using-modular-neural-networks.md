# Designing work breakdown structures using modular neural networks

S. Alireza Hashemi Golpayegani <sup>⁎</sup>, Bahram Emamizadeh

Department of Industrial and System Engineering, Amirkabir University of Technology, Hafez Ave., No. 424, Tehran, Iran

Received 30 March 2007; accepted 30 March 2007

Available online 6 April 2007

## Abstract

In this paper, a framework which employs neural networks to plan the work breakdown structure of projects has been introduced. Using the proposed framework, a modular neural network has been developed to plan the structures of a limited project domain. The main concepts of the Andishevaran Methodology of Project Management (AMPM), including project control work breakdown structure (PCWBS), functional work breakdown structure (FWBS) and relational work breakdown structure (RWBS), have used to form the outputs of the model and its modules. The nature of projects, which have been represented by a limited set of attributes, are considered as the main inputs of the model. The independency from project domains is the main advantage of the proposed framework. The framework has been tested on a sample domain, and results showed that the planned work breakdown structures and activities have satisfied the expectations with different levels of validity. Therefore the model outputs could be considered as the primary plan of project structures which could be improved by some modifications. © 2007 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Project management; Project activity planning; Neural network; Work breakdown structure; Complex relationship; Knowledge modeling

## 1. Introduction

Project planning is one of the important steps in the process of project management, which impacts many of the steps to follow. The main goal of project planning is to develop the project work plan, considering various aspects. The project work plan could be used to predict the situation of project during its lifecycle, and enables the control of its progress trend [6].

The project planning could be divided to several steps including activity planning, activity sequencing, resource assignment, scheduling, and cache flow determination [9]. As the project activities are the base for other stages, the completeness and correctness of activity planning is more important. There has been a lot of research on developing network-based planning methods and project management techniques, assuming that the activities and network structure are readily available for the project manager [1]. Most of the researches were mainly about developing mathematical models for time scheduling and resource assignment, which should be done after the planning of work breakdown structure (WBS), and the activities of project.

Despite of its importance and profound effects, there are a few researches about the methodologies or tools to develop the appropriate work breakdown structure for a given project. Most of the related works are only usable in special conditions and for very special kinds of projects.

Then the planner should apply trial and error and unknown methods, to find the work breakdown structure and activities of a given project, which could increase the total risk. Results of Tavarez' reviews [20], shows that most of the related researches were mainly based on tree subject categories including “project modeling”, “project evaluation” and “project scheduling and monitoring”. He noted that most of these researches were about developing mathematical models in “resource assignment”, or “project scheduling” for different kind of projects. Meanwhile only some simple activity and network-based modeling techniques were stated, and there were not any procedures or even prescriptions about how to recognize the activities of projects. Lee and Kim [14] have worked on the case and constrained based project planning for apartment constructions, which is one of the key researches in this area. They have developed a system named FASTRACK-APT, inspired by the use of previous cases and the human expert project planner for planning new projects. They have stated that the system, has drastically reduced the time and effort required for initial project planning, and improved the quality and completeness of the generated plans. They have selected the domain of apartment construction projects, because it has relatively more structured and available knowledge, which indicates that the methodology is only useable for domains which have structured knowledge and available data. Zhang and Maher [27] have used a “Case-Based Reasoning” (CBR) method [2] in the structural design of buildings. They have mentioned that as a design model, CBR is intuitively appealing because much of the design knowledge comes through the experience of multiple, individual design situations. They have issued that many domains, which planning knowledge are difficult to acquire and might not be applicable objectively, knowledge-based techniques such as case-based paradigm can provide a model for the acquisition and reuse of specific planning knowledge because previous cases contain much valuable knowledge in themselves. Wilhelm and Smith [23] have proposed a process planner which employs an integrated expert system and neural network approach. The planner has been used for planning the process of metal furniture assembly, welding and painting. They have used some rules and neural network models, to allow the generalization capability, for planning the process of new products. Recently some emphasis has been placed on implementing neural network technology, in the development of automated process planning systems [4,22]. Knapp and Wang [12] formulated an approach for the automated acquisition of process selection and within-feature process sequencing knowledge using neural networks. The authors have cited that time consuming, costly and error prone knowledge acquisition process, inability to change in manufacturing practice and technology, brittle nature (i.e., with sharp boundaries of application) and the inability to generalize from past experience to handle new cases, are the main reasons about why the use of neural networks is a preferable alternative in creating expert systems for process planning. It is believed that human project planners will have more problems in generating the work breakdown structure, activities and network plan of projects, without referring to previous cases. The main reason for this is the huge amount and very complex of required knowledge [14]. Therefore it is expected that project planners will try to search within previous project plans, and modify the most similar, to generate the work breakdown structure and activities of the current project.

From the view of decision making, the planning of project work breakdown structure and activities is a decision problem which should be solved by the project planner. Therefore some kind of decision support system could be applied to manage the knowledge of previous decisions and use them to support the planning of future projects. As the knowledge seems to be very complex, the system should be some kind of hybrid or intelligent decision support systems [21], which has been discussed and developed for some other decision problems [15,19,25,16].

This paper is going to introduce a process which shows how the work breakdown structure (WBS) and the activities of projects could be developed, with the aid of neural networks. The outcome of the proposed process is a modular neural network, which has been implemented to plan the structures and the activities of a sample project domain. The paper has been organized in several sections. The basic definitions and main concepts, including project structures, master structures of project domains and project attributes were discussed in Sections 2–4. General definition, capabilities and some basic details about neural networks has been discussed in Section 5. Section 6, contains the details about the proposed methodology and the results of its implementation, in a selected sample project domain. Finally, in Section 7 some further discussions and the overall paper conclusions were presented.

## 2. Project structures

The “AVEC Methodology of Project Management” (AMPM) is one of the recent methodologies for planning projects [7]. The main concern of this methodology is to offer some special tools and techniques, to ease the planning of project work breakdown structure and activities. We have assumed that the title of any project could be illustrated by the combination of a “Master

Function” (MF), and a “Master Deliverable” (MD). For example the combination of “Construction” and “Gas Production Complex” which results “Construction of Gas Production Complex”, could be considered as the title of a project. According to AMPM, any project “Work Breakdown Structure” (WBS), consists of three different structures including “Project Control Work Breakdown Structure” (PCWBS), “Functional Work Breakdown Structure” (FWBS), and “Relational Work Breakdown Structure” (RWBS). As these structures have been used to form the outputs of the proposed patterns of neural networks, they have been described in the following sub sections.

## 2.1. Project Control Work Breakdown Structure (PCWBS)

The “Project Control Work Breakdown Structure” (PCWBS) is the hierarchical structure which shows the overall components of project main deliverable or subject. The project deliverable is usually a product, a service or a combination of them. Therefore the PCWBS demonstrates the project scope of work, which contains all the subjects or deliverables that should be considered to complete the project. All the components of any PCWBS should be the name of “things” or “subjects”, and the use of “functions” are invalid. Fig. 1, illustrates the PCWBS for the project of “Construction of Gas Production Complex” as an example.

In this regard, one of the main questions in project planning stage is, how the best project control work breakdown structure (PCWBS) could be recognized among the possible structures? It seems that the answer of this question is not clear and needs further analysis and researches. A very rough answer is that each PCWBS is formed under the certain project circumstances which have been recognized and handled by the planner. Therefore the identification of these circumstances and their relationships to the structure of the best PCWBS could be a good strategy in finding the answer of this question.

## 2.2. Functional Work Breakdown Structure (FWBS)

The “Functional Work Breakdown Structure” (FWBS) is the second project structure, which shows the scope of functions and operations that should be performed to achieve the main subject or deliverable of project. With respect to definition, the FWBS can be considered as the hierarchical structure of project main operation. Fig. 2, shows the sample functional work breakdown structure (FWBS) which has been developed for the project of “Construction of Gas Production Complex”.

Again the main question is how the best functional work breakdown structure of a specific project could be recognized among the possible structures? Similar to the PCWBS, this question could not be answered easily. But it is expected that the best form of the FWBS is strongly related to the PCWBS and the circumstances of project.

Reviews show that, still there are not analytical and certain methods which could result the best process to achieve project deliverables yet. This problem will become more complex, if we assume that the operations of one process could be affected by the operations of other processes. Therefore the relationships between the project characteristics and the best form of FWBS are still unknown. Previous experiences show that, the design of project FWBS, is a knowledge dependent task.

## 2.3. Relational Work Breakdown Structure (RWBS)

The “Relational Work Breakdown Structure” or the RWBS is the third project structure, which represents the relationships between the components of PCWBS and FWBS. In other word, the project RWBS shows what components of the FWBS should be performed to obtain each component of the PCWBS. The RWBS should be presented by a matrix, where the components of the PCWBS are considered as the rows, and the components of the FWBS as the columns. If any part of the FWBS was in the process of achieving a certain part of PCWBS, the cell which is located in the intersection of the related row and column, should be assigned to (1), otherwise to zero. Consequently, the RWBS is a matrix with a limited number of rows and columns, and its cells are assigned by binary values (zeros or ones).

According to AMPM, the titles of project activities are considered to be the combination of operations which should be the parts of project FWBS, and deliverables (or subjects) which should be the parts of project PCWBS. As the master operation and the master deliverable (or subject) of project, have been combined to form the project title, it is reasonable that each combination of their components, which are known as project activities, are considered as the components of project title. ## 3. Project domain master structures

## 3.1. Matrix representation of project structures

We have seen that while PCWBS and FWBS are represented by hierarchical structures, the RWBS is represented by a matrix. Since the matrix form is more suitable to be used as the outputs of neural networks, we have implemented the equivalent adjacency matrices of PCWBS and FWBS to show them in the outputs. In general, let $G =$ (V; E) be an undirected graph with vertices $V { = } ( \nu _ { 1 } { , } . . . \nu _ { n } )$ and edge set $E .$ The adjacency matrix $A = [ a _ { i j } ]$ of $G$ is the $n \times n$ symmetric matrix in which $a _ { i j } = 1$ if and only if i≠j and $\nu _ { i }$ is adjacent to $\nu _ { j } ,$ which means there is an edge between $\nu _ { i }$ and $\nu _ { j }$ [8]. If the hierarchical structures of PCWBS and FWBS are assumed as tree graphs, then their components can be considered as vertices, and the relationships between the components as edges. These matrices will be square and each of their cells can only be evaluated by (0) or (1).

## 4. Project attributes

Project attributes are a limited set of variables which describes the characteristics of projects. The set of project attributes should be selected in such a way that could represent most of its nature. In this regard, we have defined two major sets of project attributes. The first is the general set of attributes, which is applicable in describing projects regardless of their kind. At least, the project main operation and the project main subject (or deliverable), are the members of this set. Also the total amount of time, total amount of budget, maximum amount of expected risk, maximum number of decom position layers, maximum number of operations and deliverables which could be considered in decomposing projects, are some other members of this set. The second is, the specific set of attributes, which are mostly describes the main subject (or deliverable) and main operation of project. Since it is possible to use different items as the main subject (or deliverable), and main operation of project, different set of specific attributes should be defined to describe them.

If the set of subjects (or deliverables) which has been used as the main subject of domain projects is shown by ${ \cal D } D = \{ p d _ { 1 } , p d _ { 2 } , . . . , p d _ { k } \} , ( 0 < k < M )$ and the total number of domain projects by n, each $p d _ { k }$ could be described by a specific set of attributes which is defined by $\mathrm { P D A } _ { p d _ { k } } = \{ d a _ { j _ { p d _ { k } } } \} ( 0 < k < M , 0 < j _ { p d _ { k } } < L )$ There-<sup>¼ f gð Þ</sup>fore the master set of specific attributes, which belongs to the selected domain of projects, can be defined by $\mathrm { S P A S } = \cup ^ { p d _ { k } } \mathrm { P D A } _ { p d _ { k } }$ . As each $\mathrm { P D A } _ { p d _ { k } }$ is a subset of the SPAS, the master set of specific attributes can be applied to describe the main subjects (or deliverables) of domain projects. In this regard, if any member of the SPAS, was not applicable in describing a project, it should be assigned to a predefined NULL value. Since this could be happened for each of the specific attributes, all of them should have a NULL value, which must be defined with respect to the kind and range of their assignable values.

## 5. Overview on neural networks

Artificial neural networks are composed of interconnected, usually adaptive elements, which are intended to respond to stimulus in a manner not unlike the human nervous system [10,13]. Perhaps the most attractive property of these structures is the ability to learn associations between and patterns within data. By using neural networks, there are several important resulting attributes. First, it is not necessary to develop difficult algorithms, statistical models and knowledgebased rules which can be extracted from data, and the inherent parallelism speeds processing [17]. Another important advantage is the ANNs ability to generalize data and produce outputs for previously unseen inputs [24]. The most fundamental aspect of ANNs is their use of simple interconnected processing elements, which correspond loosely to neurons and synaptic connections in the brain. The strength of the connecting links determines the functionality of the ANN as a whole. The strength of each link is determined as the ANN learns. The method employed in training the ANN may depend upon the ANN architecture, the intended purpose of the ANN and even the resources available in developing the system. The method we use, supervised learning implies that there is prior knowledge of the system to be modeled in the form of input–output pairs. There are almost as many structural and strategic variations of ANNs as there are applications [3]. In a feed-forward network there are no connecting arcs feeding back to upstream (input) neurons. Input patterns are propagated through the ANN to produce an output pattern that will initially be different than the desired output. The training process consists of adjusting the weight values to produce desired outputs.

Back propagation is the most commonly used method for supervised training of multilayer feed forward networks [18]. It has been shown that multi-layered feedforward ANNs with one hidden layer can uniformly approximate any continuous function to any desired degree of accuracy given an adequate number of neurons in the hidden layer and the correct interconnection weights [5]. There are a number of pitfalls involved in empirically determining the number of hidden neurons. Judging which network is best does not rely on fool-proof methods. Furthermore, knowing when training can stop is not precise [3]. In summary, the characteristics of ANNs that make them seem ideal are their ability to learn relationships between inputs and outputs and to generalize those relationships to previously unseen data [11].

If one elects to employ neural networks rather than more traditional methods, i.e. regression, several important questions must be answered:

1. What is an appropriate architecture for the problem at hand?

2. For a given architecture, how do we know when to stop training?

3. How do we select from among candidate ANN models?

Unfortunately, there is no way to positively answer these questions and we must resort to heuristic methods to arrive at acceptable answers. Ultimately, in the absence of methods for rigorously structuring a neural network for a given problem, one must rely on experience and experimentation. We intend to use artificial neural networks (ANNs) to model the relationships between

project attributes and work breakdown structures. The main reason for this is the need for reusing the unknown, complex and implicit planning knowledge which the project planning experts have employed. As this relation is very complex, we found that it could not be represented by the formal or classical methods of knowledge representation completely and correctly. From the other side, a great amount of historical data about the work breakdown structures and activity plans which have been implemented in previously executed domain of projects is available. Therefore we have decided to employ the neural networks to extract the knowledge of relationships between project attributes and the configuration of work breakdown structures, using the available domain of data. We expect that the proposed model of neural network could be able to generalize the absorbed knowledge to find the suitable form of work breakdown structures for other projects inside the selected domain.

## Step 6: Designing the structure of neural network model

In this step, the structure of the proposed neural network which should be implemented for learning the relationships between projects attributes and their structures should be designed. In this regard, we have used a modular neural network (MNN) which is consisted of five different modules. The main reason of using modular neural networks in regard of other network structures is that the elements of project PCWBS and FWBS are considered to be the inputs of the RWBS module. Also the structure of PCWBS, FWBS are considered to be completed at least in two different stages which are related to each other. Therefore we need to use a specific neural network module to complete each stage and then relate them to each other to complete the final configuration of the project PCWBS, FWBS and RWBS. Fig. 4, shows the entire structure of our proposed neural model.

Each module is contained by a separate neural network which has a different configuration, and should be able to complete some stages of inferring projects structures and activities from their attributes. The master vector of attributes, which has been resulted from the selected domain of projects, is considered as the main input of the model. Since the project attributes are supposed to affect the outputs of each module, they have been considered, as the entire or a section of module inputs. The matrices of master domain structures, which have been obtained from the selected domain of projects, have been considered as the outputs of the proposed model. As it has been discussed, these matrices can represent the appropriate structures and activities for the project which has been described in the model inputs. By considering matrices of master domain structures as the model outputs, it must be noticed that each of their cells should be considered as an individual output for the entire model.

As it has been shown in Fig. 4, the inputs of the PCWBS1 and the FWBS1 modules are only consisted of project attributes. They should find what components might be implemented, in the body of appropriate PCWBS and FWBS, with respect to the project attributes. Therefore the outputs of these modules (i.e. P-Vector and F-Vector) are two column vectors which their rows are linked to the individual members of subject and operation domains concluded from the selected domain of projects. Therefore according to the results of step (3), the P-Vector and the F-Vector will be single column vectors which have 966 and 182 rows. If any row evaluates by 1, it will mean that the related deliverable or operation can be employed in the body of PCWBS or FWBS. Therefore, the outputs of PCWBS1 and FWBS1 modules are the quantities of P-Vector and F-Vector rows. After that the P-Vector and the master vector of project attributes are considered as the inputs of the PCWBS2 module. Concurrently the F-Vector and the master vector of project attributes are assumed as the inputs of the FWBS2 module. These modules will find how the recognized components of PCWBS (P-Vector) and FWBS (F-Vector) should be configured and leveled with respect to the project attributes. These configurations will be attained by determining their identical adjacency matrices, which should be achieved by evaluating the cells of MPCWBS and MFWBS matrices. Therefore the quantity of MPCWBS and MFWBS cells are the outputs of the PCWBS2 and the FWBS2 modules. At the same time, P-Vector, F-Vector and the master vector of project attributes are assumed as the inputs of RWBS module. The task of this module is to find how the components of PCWBS and FWBS should be related to form the activities of the specified project, with respect to its attributes. These relationships should be determined by finding the appropriate RWBS for the project, which must be represented by the MPRWBS matrix. Therefore the cells of the MRWBS are considered as the outputs of RWBS module.

Step 9: Testing and validating the neural network

In this step, the neural network modules should be tested and evaluated by the test project patterns. Two groups of test projects have been considered in the test patterns. The first was the “interpolation test projects”, which the combination of their main operation and main deliverable makes similar project titles to the training projects, but the values of their other attributes are different. The second group was the “extrapolation test projects”, which the combination of their main operation and main deliverable, makes quietly different project titles with respect to the training projects And the values of their other attributes could be different. Therefore we have entered attributes of test projects to the inputs of modules and their outputs, including the MPCWBS,

MFWBS and MRWBS matrices, were calculated with respect to the specified relations between them. As the outputs of modules, are continuous and can be evaluated between 0 and 1, we have modified them with respect to the learning threshold parameter. The learning threshold is a training parameter which defines the output error range that could be equaled to zero [26]. Therefore the outputs which are greater than the value of learning threshold can be assigned to 1, and the others to 0. The test outputs should be validated, by comparing the calculated and the real MPCWBS, MFWBS and MRWBS matrices. The calculated matrices are resulted during the tests, and real matrices are the equivalent adjacency or relationship matrices which indicate to the real structures of test projects. As the matrices of domain structures should be compared by the values of their cells, three criteria have been considered for better judgments. If we define $a _ { i j } = 1$ as any nonzero cell of calculated matrices, we should answer three validation questions:

2) Possible: It means that the related subject or operation to the row or column is not a part of the real project structures, or is not a piece of their components, but they could be valid with respect to the real structures of similar projects, or the judgment of experts.

3) Wrong: It means that the related subject or operation can not to be a part of project structures, or it can not be considered as a part of their components.

## 7. Conclusions

We have shown that how the neural networks could be utilized to recognize structures of projects. We have proposed a distinct process with specific steps and procedures for this purpose. We have developed a neural network with 5 modules, where each of them contained a separated neural network, and has to perform a distinguished task. The outputs of some modules were connected to the inputs of others, to enable the inference of project structures with respect to a certain sequence. Regarding the test results, it could be concluded that the proposed neural network, has satisfied the expectations about recognizing the components and configuration of project structures, within the selected domain of projects.

The main reason for this is the relatively good capability of model modules in recognizing the valid and possible components and their relationships in project structures. Therefore the proposed model can support the planner to build the primary plan of project structures and activities, with sufficient degree of validity which could be refined with further manual modifications. We insist that the quality of primary project structures could be improved by training the modules with larger amounts of data within the selected domain.

As the project structures (PCWBS, FWBS and RWBS), and the matrices of master domain structures (MPCWBS, MFWBS and MRWBS), are general concepts and independent from the kind or the title of project, it can be concluded that the main advantage of the proposed methodology of modeling is that it can be applied in every limited domain of projects, regardless of their kind. Also the methodology will be more accurate and efficient, if the domain projects have used more shared components, in the body of their structures. But if the selected domain of projects expands and contains more kinds and titles of projects, we expect that the size of master domain structures, and also the size of master set (or vector) of attributes increase. Therefore a bigger size of neural networks should be employed. This will make the designing and the training process of the neural network modules to become more complex and time consuming, which could be the main disadvantage of the proposed modeling technique. Also we will need more amount of data to train the modules (especially the RWBS, PCWBS2 and FWBS2) for achieving an acceptable level of validity in their outputs. We can be certain that if the projects of the selected domain, have more shared components in the body of their structures, the size of the neural network will increase with lower ratio. The generalization capability of the model is highly dependent to the quality and quantity of training data. The training data should be sufficient and contain more experiences about using various items as the components of different structures, for the projects which could be defined within the selected domain. But nobody can be guaranteed about having access to the data in all project domains, which is another disadvantage of proposed modeling technique.

In this regard, using fuzzy values to evaluate the cells of output matrices, and a layer by layer paradigm in finding the components of project structures, especially the PCWBS and FWBS, could be the subjects of further researches to extend the presented paradigm in planning the project structures. Also finding and using better methods for representing the value of project attributes and project structures, and using alternative neural network structures instead of multi layer perceptron (MLP), could be the potential subjects of further researches, to refine the presented methodology and model pattern.
