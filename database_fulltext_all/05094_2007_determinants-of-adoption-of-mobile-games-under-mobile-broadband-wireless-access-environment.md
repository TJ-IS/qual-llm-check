# Determinants of adoption of mobile games under mobile broadband wireless access environment

Imsook Ha <sup>\*</sup>, Youngseog Yoon, Munkee Choi

School of IT management, Information and Communications University, 119 Munjiro, Youseong-gu, Daejeon 305-714, Republic of Korea Received 20 November 2004; received in revised form 9 December 2006; accepted 8 January 2007 Available online 21 January 2007

## Abstract

Rapid advancements in technology and changes in cost have made the game industry a profitable area. Recently, Mobile Broadband Wireless Access technology-based (MBWA) games have been introduced into the marketplace. They are superior to current mobile games in terms of transmission speed and price and to older online games that have limited mobility. Since MBWA technologies, such as High Speed Downlink Packet Access (HSDPA), Wireless Broadband (WiBro), and Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access (WiMAX) have been developed, customers can enjoy games whenever and wherever they like. We decided to analyze the factors that influence potential users’ adoption of MBWA games using an extension of TAM. Most previous game-related studies lack a comprehensive approach to both technological and psychological aspects of game adoption and do not reflect a variety of consumer preferences. We therefore extended TAM to include an emotion variable and measured the moderating effects of gender, age, and prior experience on game adoption. Overall, the results revealed that the effect of perceived enjoymen was very important but that usefulness did not influence an individual’s attitude. In addition, we also find that age can be key moderator of game acceptance.

## 1. Introduction

The game industry is growing as IT and the web develops. Its strong potential lies in the availability of a ‘‘one-source, multiuser’’ environment. Games started as arcade games or become available as video games and as the game gained popularity, its content was transferred to other platforms, such as online games (where tickets are purchased through a network of computer terminals located at retail outlets—including lotto, keno and number games) and mobile games as processing speed and graphic processing ability of PCs developed. This market has been expanding very quickly, driven by increasing numbers of broadband infrastructure users, advancement in graphic engines, and the introduction of 3D games.

Mobile games are played on PDAs, cellular phone, or portable game device. As 2.5G and 3G services have spread, the sophistication of the games has increased. Because many mobile games use cell phones, they are more accessible, mobile, portable, and convenient than other game platforms. They allow users to enjoy games without interruption and their accessibility has attracted many to use mobile games. However, existing games have some limitations: the interface, which is simple (because most cell phone interfaces are optimized for making phone calls), limited storage capacity and data speed (which are insufficient for large-scale mobile games especially those with 3D visuals), and a relatively expensive service charge.

MBWA games, including HSDPA, WiBro, and WiMAX, are wireless IP network based; they can provide high-speed games with fast transmission, mobility, space availability, terminals and economy regardless of the device with which they are played. Thus MBWA resolves problems of mobility, price burden and network speed and service providers will have to adopt a business strategy that favors user adoption of games. In addition, it is important to determine factors important to users and providing them and identify problems and remove them to increase accessibility to games [21]. We therefore decided to analyze users’ intentions to play MBWA games; i.e., investigate the factors that influence potential users’ adoption of MBWA games.

In many prior studies, the main foci were technical and psychological aspects. Studies of effective methods to improve game technology included three-dimensional rendering of graphics and sound to make the game more realistic; and algorithms to make artificial intelligence control characters more effectively. In studying psychological aspects, Sweetser and Wyeth [18] suggested a model that included player enjoyment. Choi et al. [5] analyzed gamers’ preferences and important design elements using four factors: interest, curiosity, control, and concentration. However, all studies have overlooked major psychological influences. Therefore, we added material reflect the emotional aspect of games to TAM. Other influences depend on individual or group differences. Therefore, we considered gender, age, and prior experience as moderator variables on user characteristics, dividing the population into subgroups according to each moderator.

## 2. Theoretical background

Perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived ease of use (PEU) have been shown to be factors in explaining user acceptance behavior toward using a system (Fig. 1) [7].

In the adoption model of games, Hsu and Lu [11] examined customer intention to use online games. They incorporated social influence and flow experience as belief-related constructs to predict users’ acceptance of online games. Their study empirically showed that PU did not motivate users to play online games, but directly affected user attitude. They also showed that social influences, including perceived critical mass and social norms, significantly, directly, and separately, affected attitudes and intentions. Consequently, PEU was demonstrated to play a dominant role when people play online games. Kwang and Kim [14] investigated the factors influencing the usage and acceptance of mobile games and showed that PU and ease of use were the major determinants for mobile game users. Four factors (ease of use, self-expression, visibility, and innovativeness) were empirically shown to determine the level of PU. In addition, innovativeness and facilitating conditions were found to affect ease of use directly.

Researchers have also been interested in the moderating effect of experience, voluntariness, gender, and age on the relationship between the independent and dependent variables. Venkatesh and Davis [22] showed that age and gender were key modifiers of the individual’s activity. Such differences were also shown to influence wireless internet adoption [12]. Several studies have investigsted experience as a moderator but called it ‘‘prior experience’’ [19], ‘‘past usage’’ [1], or ‘‘habit’’ [20]. but most studies have used period and frequency of use as their experience index, thus dealing more with quantity than quality. Behavioral intentions of experienced and inexperienced users at a computing resources center have been studied and the influence factors were surveyed; some variables influenced outcomes. The inexperienced group was more affected by the behavioral intentions of PU, subjective norm, and PEU than the experienced group. Influence of Perceived Behavior Control affected the inexperienced more than the experienced. Potential customers considered perceived enjoyment as the most important among all significant factors that affected their attitude and intention toward T-commerce [25]. They compared the factors influencing T-commerce by experienced and inexperienced users and showed that PE had the greatest impact on attitude among experienced users, and that attitude had the greatest influence on intention. For inexperienced users, perceived enjoyment and PU positively affected their attitude, and that attitude and subjective norms had an impact on intention.

Based on this work, our study concentrated on three moderators: gender, age, and prior experience. This provided two subgroups for each moderator: (1) male or female; (2) younger or older; and (3) expert or Novice.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

Prior research models do not adapt easily to the field of MBWA games: PU and PEU are not able to explain consumers’ adoption of MBWA games, because games are intended to provide entertainment, enjoyment, and relaxation and these outcomes are totally different from the normal purpose of IS which attempts to increase performance. Thus, we added flow experience, perceived enjoyment, and perceived attractiveness as factors that provide pleasure and fun. Moreover, we considered the financial sacrifice of purchasing a game service. Therefore, in terms of MBWA games, we extended TAM by adding seven constructs, as depicted in Fig. 2.

## 3.1. Research hypotheses

We first verified the hypotheses related to PU and PEU. Prior studies had shown that PEU was directly or indirectly related to behavioral intention. However, we needed to refine the definition of PEU because we were considering an entertainment-oriented service. In this context, PEU is extended to include the user’s belief that playing games would be effortless, from the viewpoint of playing the game as an entertainment-oriented service. Therefore, PEU was expected to have a positive effect on attitude and PU in the context of the game. As in previous studies, we wished to demonstrate a positive relationship between PEU and attitude. We therefore stated the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1. PEU positively affects PU.

Hypothesis 2. PEU positively affects user attitude toward playing MBWA games.

In our study, PU was augmented to involve the extent to which the user believed that playing MBWA games would fulfill his or her purpose without regard to time and space. As previous research has demonstrated the relationship between the original PU and attitude, we have established Hypothesis 3.

Hypothesis 3. PU positively affects attitude toward playing MBWA games.

## 3.2. Hypotheses about emotional aspects

Literature about games generally focuses on technology to support or implement it rather than on players’ emotional aspects. However, a game may be of good technical quality, but upplayed if potential users do not enjoy it. We therefore generated hypotheses related to emotional aspects such as perceived enjoyment, flow experience, perceived attractiveness, and perceived sacrifices.

## 3.2.1. Perceived enjoyment

This is the extent to which the activity is perceived to be enjoyable, apart from any performance consequences. Heijden et al. [9] added it to a TAM for ecommerce and demonstrated that it influenced consumer attitude and intention toward personal website adoption. The positive relationship between playfulness and a player’s loyalty to a game was examined and validated in a previous study [13]. Games must, of course, provide customers with enjoyment, as part of their basic nature. Thus a TAM for game systems mustincorporate enjoyment, including interest, pleasure, and fun, as an influence on attitude. This led to Hypothesis 4:

Hypothesis 4. Perceived enjoyment positively affects attitude toward playing MBWA games.

Heijden et al. also showed that PEU positively affected enjoyment. He found that ease of use contributed to enjoyment. Mobile games certainly try to provide pleasure, enjoyment, and relaxation but they can be considered a ‘‘time-killer’’, though sometimes they serve an educational purpose. Thus, the following two hypotheses were proposed.

Hypothesis 5. PEU positively affects enjoyment.

Hypothesis 6. Perceived enjoyment positively affects usefulness.

## 3.2.2. Flow experience

Flow theory, introduced by Csikszentmihalyi [6], was defined as ‘‘the holistic experience that people feel when they act with total involvement.’’ When people are in the flow state, they become totally involved in their activity and unable to recognize changes in their surroundings. Specifically, they lose their self-consciousness, concentrating only on their ongoing activity. The key element of flow is that it is an end in itself—the activity must be intrinsically rewarding and autotelic. This applies to games because people play them for the experience, as there is no external reward. Flow is characterized by: (1) a sense of playfulness; (2) a feeling of being in control; (3) concentration and highfocused attention; (4) mental enjoyment of the activity for its own sake; (5) a distorted sense of time; and (6) a match between the challenge at hand and one’s skill. Therefore, enjoyment is an important factor in flow experience [10]. Thus, Hypothesis 7 was established:

Hypothesis 7. Perceived enjoyment positively affects flow experience.

Immediate feedback, commensurate challenges, skills, and clear goals are precursors to flow. Thus, we can posit that if a game is easy to play, an individual can easily fall into the flow experience. Many studies [15,16] have succeeded in explaining human behavior by applying flow theory. Currently, flow theory is used in IT and relative services because it describes mental phenomena well. A variety of studies such as those of Hsu and Lu have attempted to find determinants influencing online game adoption. They raise the possibility of a positive relationship not only between flow experience and attitude, but also between flow and intention, by extending TAM with this construct into the game field.

To verify these relationships in the field of MBWA games, we therefore posited Hypotheses 8 and 9.

Hypothesis 8. PEU positively affects flow experience.

Hypothesis 9. Flow experience positively affects attitude toward playing MBWA games.

## 3.2.3. Perceived attractiveness

Many authors have emphasized the importance of visual and acoustic attractiveness in games. White [23] suggested that the aural and visual properties of a game can provide an impression of activity and fun, encourage initial participation, and stimulate further play. Wolfson and Case [24] investigated the effects of sound and color on response to a computer game. We therefore felt it was important to investigate the attractiveness of MBWA games by dividing it into the two parts: visual and acoustic attractiveness. We assumed that such attractiveness affects attitude and that there will be a positive relationship between perceived attractiveness and perceived enjoyment because physical attractiveness may make an individual enjoy the experience. Two more hypotheses were made:

Hypothesis 10. Perceived attractiveness positively affects attitude toward playing mobile games.

Hypothesis 11. Perceived attractiveness positively affects perceived enjoyment.

## 3.2.4. Perceived lower sacrifices

Previous studies have shown a negative relationship between price and perceived quality [8,17]. Chen et al. [3] demonstrated that online consumers were price sensitive. Javenpaa and Todd noted that many consumers expected lower prices because of lower set up costs, lower cost per customer contact, and the lower maintenance cost of virtual stores; the higher the price perceived, the lower the value perceived. A user’s perceived sacrifice involved: financial and mental sacrifices. Specifically, consumers evaluated the quality of a service or product by considering the total cost of purchasing it. [26] Furthermore, consumers evaluated the quality of a product or its value in relation to the price paid. Perceived monetary and nonmonetary costs constituted perceived sacrifice. Perceived non-monetary costs included such items as the time and effort required to purchase the product.

We defined measurement variables as the degree of one’s perception of lower monetary sacrifice in terms of Internet access cost and the game service usage fee. We posited that the relative price of games in the MBWA environment was not greater than that of mobile games in the 2.5G or 3G environments. Because MBWA providers promised that the imposed network usage fee through the wireless Internet under the MBWA environment would not exceed that of 2.5G or 3G environments, we posited that potential consumers would pay less in the future to download games and play multiplayer games. Assuming that the costs will decrease faster than those for current mobile games, the extent of the effect this decrease will have on attitude and intention is important. Thus, we made the hypothesis:

Hypothesis 12. Perceived lower sacrifice positively affects attitude toward playing mobile games.

## 4. Testing and results

We decided to use a questionnaire to collect data and therefore first had to check its reliability and validity.

All the questionnaires were proven to have reliability and validity. We used the value of Cronbach’s alpha for identifying the reliability of the questionnaires, and conducted factor analysis for convergent validity. All the questionnaires used in this survey have been validated in previous studies. Each item of the questionnaire was measured on a 7-point Likert scale with the end points of ‘‘strongly agree (7)’’ and ‘‘strongly disagree (1)’’. The majority of the sample substantially matched current Internet users. To investigate the moderating effect of user heterogeneity, we conducted sample segmentation based on the

## 4.3. Hypothesis testing

## 4.3.1. Results of structure model for overall data

The analysis results of the SEM are depicted in Fig. 3 and the results of the hypothesis were shown in Table 5: all but two hypotheses are significant (there were no direct effects of PU and perceived lower sacrifices on attitude).

The hypothesized positive relationship from PEU to PU and attitude was supported. Hypotheses 1 and 2 cannot be rejected. The coefficient path from PEU to PU and attitude was 0.395 $( p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ and 0.228 $( p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ This inarguable finding implies that potential gamers’ believed that the ease of use of an MBWA game would be a critical factor in future wireless environments. However, Hypothesis 3 was rejected. Thus the solid role of PU cannot be observed in our study, and this finding contradicts many previous TAM studies, with the exception of Hsu and Lu’s, who had shown that PU did not motivate users to play online games, but it did directly affect their attitude. Although MBWA games have more developed game infrastructure than online games, the character of the games may not be completely changed.

The purpose of a game is to provide enjoyment, playfulness, and relaxation. Perceived enjoyment $( \beta =$ 0.462, $p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ , flow experience $( \beta = 0 . 2 2 2$ $p <$ 0.001), and perceived attractiveness $( \beta = 1 7 2$ $p <$ 0.001) all influenced users’ attitude toward playing mobile games. Thus, Hypotheses 4, 9 and 10 could not be rejected. As shown in Table 5, the most significant determinant having a positive attitude was perceived enjoyment, which implied that attitude was mainly influenced by perceived enjoyment. It was inferred that the establishment of a positive attitude was critically influenced by enjoyment.

## 5. Conclusion and implications

The purpose of our study was to examine factors that influence user adoption of MBWA games, and thus to explain to developers how to increase acceptance of MBWA-based games in the marketplace. Based on previous studies, we extended TAM focusing on recognized emotional variables, that are fundamental factors of game acceptance. In addition, we examined group differences in terms of adjustment variables (such as gender, age, and prior experience). The following strategies should help improve the MBWA game market:

First, perceived enjoyment is the variable with the greatest effect on intention to play MBWA games. This implies that psychological rather than technical factors are most important in the game industry. Developing content with creative and solid storylines should be pursued to provide more fun. The new broadband infrastructures will enhance the network function of MBWA games, and the environment will be able to provide games whenever and wherever players wish.

Second, PEU should be considered important as a prerequisite of enjoyment. Developing a game that is easy to use technically is key to increasing fun.

Although new digital products emphasize performance improvement, product developers should focus on maximizing simplicity.

Fourth, acceptance was influenced by age. PEU was a more important factor for older people in providing enjoyment and flow experience. Similarly, PEU was more important among women than men for enjoyment. In addition, giving players as many chances as possible to be familiar with the games is needed before commercialization of MBWA games to keep them from being uncomfortable when using mobile devices for accessing games. Promotion of mobile devices and increasing game experience opportunities through various channels will be necessary. Fifth, younger people and experts in prior games are more influenced by enjoyment than older people pt novices. Perceived attractiveness had a greater influence on younger people. Lastly, the MBWA game market is slow-growing compared with other platform games. Thus, market participants should continue to find ways to improve gamers perceptions of their products.

He holds a Ph.D. in Operations Research from North Carolina State University, USA, M.S. from Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology and BS from Seoul National University, Korea. His research interests

include IT network service and business, network and system architecture, and performance analysis.
