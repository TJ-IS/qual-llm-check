Keywords: Inter-organizational information systems (IOS) Traditional communication media (TCM) Transaction information Management information Inter-organizational relationships

## A B S T R A C T

I developed a framework that can identify types of inter-organizational relationships. Various kinds of information exchanged between trading partners were grouped into two broad types: transactional and managerial. I then examined the effects of inter-organizational information systems (IOS) and traditional communication media (TCM) on the amount of information exchanged. Stepwise regression analyses showed that the use of value chains was the most significant factor influencing the exchange of both types of information. In examining the impact of the information exchanged to improve production performance, it appeared that different kinds of information influenced different aspects. Depending on the usage level of the media and the types of information exchanged, four categories of interorganizational relationships and their particular characteristics were defined: traditional links for coordination, strategic alliances, electronic links for coordination, and virtual organizations.

## 1. Introduction

Rapidly changing business environments require information to be communicated among trading partners across company borders. The exchange of information is vital to ensure coordination and control of activities between firms and various communication media have been used: among these, face-to-face meetings have often been proposed to be best because of the richness of information conveyed [14].

However, inter-organizational information systems (IOS) have been replacing traditional communication media (TCM). IOS are value chains that extend beyond traditional enterprise boundaries [13]. The resulting different types of inter-organizational relationships are hybrids. Strategic alliances, partnerships and network organizations are possible, but no framework has been developed to explain both the use of IOS and the flow of information between trading partners.

Although many firms actively utilize IOS to communicate business information to trading partners, the kinds of information exchanged are not well known or effectively transmitted. Mediarichness theory (MRT) sees e-mail as a relatively lean medium that does not effectively convey rich information, whereas face-to-face meetings are the richest. Of the various types of information exchanged, some kinds may be easily and effectively communicated by IOS but others may require face-to-face meetings. Therefore, IOS cannot completely replace TCM.

Many authors have suggested that the flow of information between trading firms has a positive effect on organizational performance. In manufacturing firms, the exchange affects production performance. Its dimensions are generally classified into cost reduction, quality improvement, increased flexibility, and supply dependability [1].

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. The flow of information between trading firms

Ordering and delivery information are almost completely automated in major national and international organizations. Often, inventory, cost, and some accounting information is communicated with trading partners to allow them to optimize events across organizational boundaries; indeed, financial information exchange helps firms assess their financial rewards and risks [8].

When manufacturing firms try to reduce product costs through collaborative efforts, they must understand each other’s cost and product related information [6]. Without this information, the information asymmetry between buyer and supplier may be aggravated. Buyer and supplier do not understand each other’s decision-making and the buyer may impose products or parts specifications that unnecessarily increase costs to the supplier.

## 2.2. IOS and TCM

IOS information is exchanged via e-mail, the Internet, video conferencing, etc. [2]. Generally, TCM can be considered to include telephone, facsimiles, letters and face-to-face meetings. MRT states that communication media differ in the richness of information processed by them [7]. Information richness makes it easier for a recipient to understand or use the information. This depends on the feedback capability, communication channel characteristics, language mechanisms, interface, and personal focus provided. The more a medium incorporates these characteristics, the richer it is. In general, oral media are believed to be richer than written media. Furthermore, synchronous media are believed to be richer than asynchronous ones. Thus, in categorizing media from the most rich to the least, it is assumed to be faceto-face meetings, video conferencing, telephone, letters, e-mail, facsimiles, etc.

According to MRT, rich media enable people to interpret and reach agreements about difficult and complex issues, while lean media are appropriate for communicating routine activities. Issues requiring debate and discussion are more difficult to resolve via a lean medium such as e-mail because it does not support a wide range of communication cues (such as bodylanguage or facial expression) in real time. Media with low richness can be effective in processing well-understood messages and standard data.

Purchase and supply transactions are generally routine business activities but management information is susceptible to multiple interpretations, often needing discussion and debate between trading partners. Therefore, the research question is: Are different communication media needed for effective information exchange between trading partners?

## 2.3. The flow of information and production performance

Frequent and speedy information exchange through IOS gives rise to competitive advantage, such as faster decision analysis and shorter response time, close control and coordination of business activities, and resulting lower costs. These contribute to an increase in internal and inter-organizational efficiency.

In manufacturing firms, the goals that can be attained through the use of good production systems entail maintaining low cost, improved quality, increased flexibility, and a highly dependable supply chain. The exchange and sharing of information between trading manufacturing firms can have a direct effect on its performance. Thus, the second research question was: How does different information exchanged between trading partners affect production performance?

## 3. Research method

## 3.1. Study sample

Data were drawn from a survey of the current status of IOS in Korean manufacturing firms. 500 organizations were randomly selected from a population of about 1000 firms listed on the Korean stock market. First, the chief factory managers of the selected firms were contacted to ask for their participation. Initially, 137 organizations responded to the request; however, only 120 of them were finally included in the study. Questionnaires were administered to the participating firms. Only chief factory managers were asked to respond, since they well understood their use of IOS and their firm’s performance. The survey was conducted during a 4-month period between October 2004 and January 2005.

To test for non-response bias, the final sample was partitioned into two groups: early and late responders. Non-response was then examined using a t-test, which showed no significant difference between the two groups as far as the number of employees in the firm (t = -1.3, p = 0.18), its sales volume (t = -0.7, p = 0.47), total assets (t = -1.1, p = 0.24) and value chain usage (t = 0.8, p = 0.41). ## 3.2. Measurements

## 3.2.1. Transaction and management information

Sixteen question items based on the research of Cooper and Slagmulder [6], Hakansson and Lind [8], and Vijayasarathy and Robey [15] were utilized to measure transaction and management information exchanged. In 16 types of information, the levels of the amount exchanged with major trading or related firms were measured. The question items for transactional data included types of information about the order, delivery, receipts, transportation, production planning, production progress, inventory status, and liquidation. The questionnaires for managerial data consisted of cost, quality, product markets, profitability, manufacturing technology, new product development, product selling, and consulting. Respondents were asked to indicate the amount of information that was exchanged with trading or related firms on a seven-point Likert-type scale, anchored by ‘no information’ and ‘very large amount of information’.

## 3.2.2. IOS and TCM

The degree of usage of each medium in the exchange of information was measured on a seven-point Likert-type scale that ranged from ‘never use’ to ‘highly use’.

## 3.2.3. Production performance

Using the 19 questionnaire items developed by Agarwal [1] and Vickery et al. [16], the degree of improvement in production performance relative to competitors in the last 3 years was measured by using a seven-point Likert-type scale that ranged from ‘not improved, worse’ to ‘highly improved’. The five items to measure flexibility included new product introduction, speed in changing product volume, speed in developing new products, speed in making product changeover, and R&D efforts. The four items for supply dependability were lead time of orders, delivery reliability, production lead time, and response to customer requirements. The six items for quality comprised product performance and reliability, product durability, conformance to specifications, design and engineering quality, product features, and customers’ perception of quality. The four items for cost included unit production, material, labor and overhead costs.

## 4.2. The effects of IOS and TCM

To investigate the impact of IOS and TCM on the amount of information exchanged, stepwise regression analyses were performed. In Table 5, the results of these analyses are presented. Apparently, the use of IOS is the first significant factor that positively influenced the amount of transaction information exchanged. Therefore, considering that video conferencing is almost never used in Korean manufacturing firms [12], these results confirmed that the use of lean IOS is a major influence factor on the amount of transaction information exchanged.

In terms of management information, Table 6 shows the stepwise regression analysis results. From these, it is obvious that lean IOS was still the most influential factor on the amount of information exchanged. Such results are contrary to our expectation that the use of rich media would improve the exchange of management information. However, if the exchange of management information involved ambiguity and discussion, rich media was required.

According to the interpretive perspective, richness or leanness is not an inherent property of e-mail, but a function of mutual understanding. This perspective notes that a person is not merely a passive receptacle, but an intelligent being in a shared social context who can transform lean words and cues into an understanding of the writer’s intent. Under a high degree of mutual understanding, e-mail also can convey rich information. Thus, in this study, the differentiation between lean and rich was based on the inherent characteristics of the medium as well as the degree of mutual understanding of the parties irrespective of the types of medium.

## 4.3. The effect of the information exchanged

To examine the impact of information exchanged on production performance, multiple regression analyses were made. The results of these are presented in Table 7. In terms of the quality and dependability of supply, the effects of the two types of information were significant and positive. Thus, it could be concluded that the communication of the two types of information contributed to quality improvement and a high dependability of supply.

However, the two types of information had no significant impact on increased flexibility. When cost reduction was considered, only the impact of transaction information was significant and positive. Thus, the exchange of transaction information can lead to a reduction of purchase or supply-related administrative costs that contribute mainly to production overhead. Hence, different types of information exchanged between trading partners affect different dimensions of organizational performance.

## 5. A framework for inter-organizational relationships

The economic approach to inter-organizational relationships emphasizes economic ways of attaining benefits or gains through the formation and use of inter-firm relationships. The social and behavioral aspects of interacting firms provide a different view where power, reciprocity, learning, trust, and dependence directly and indirectly influence the formation and types of relationships through the use of an IT-based network. Both views employed the use of IOS and the exchange of information as the two main indicators that explain the formation and development of interfirm relationships [5].

In our study, we found that transaction and management information enhanced inter-organizational business activities, and both IOS and TCM were actively utilized. Thus, the configuration of inter-organizational relationships can be viewed from the perspectives of types of information exchanged, and the usage level of the IOS or TCM.

## 5.1. Types of information

Coordination in buyer and supplier relations monitors the purchase or supply activities of the partners. Cooperation, however, focuses on the process by which groups and organizations come together, interact, and form relationships for mutual gain or benefit. Strategic collaboration implies the willingness of partners to pursue mutually compatible benefits in the alliance, rather than to act opportunistically.

The exchange and sharing of information provide efficient coordination or cooperation in inter-organizational relationship. Trading firms coordinate their processes in the supply-chain, thereby making them adjust their activities to promote mutual advantages. Transaction information, such as inventory, production planning and transportation, is exchanged between trading partners.

## 5.2. The usage level of IOS and TCM

IT-based inter-organizational relationships are strikingly different from those based on TCM. Cost of information exchange is reduced and the business processes are tightly coupled in longterm relationships. TCM can be considered as traditional interorganizational systems. They support traditional inter-firm relationships. However, through IOS, firms can develop electronic relationships. Thus, inter-firm relationships can be grouped into two categories; traditional and electronic.

## 5.3. Types of inter-organizational relationships

The framework for inter-firm relationships shown in Fig. 1 has four forms of relationships.

## 5.3.1. Traditional links for coordination

Coordination links can be formed through discrete market transactions (contracts that represent relatively short-term bargaining relationships between highly autonomous buyers and sellers); they are designed to facilitate efficient transfer of goods or services. Arm’s-length market and spot market relationships belong to traditional coordination links. In both, a minimum amount of information exchange is needed. Under such relationships, price may act as the main coordinating devices. Buyers and sellers have only a sales-to-purchasing interface.

## 5.3.2. Strategic alliances

These are broad ranging relationships that encompass joint ventures, franchises, joint R&D, joint marketing, long-term supply arrangements and outsourcing. They allow firms to secure resources and technology at a lower risk than corporate acquisitions. Through strategic relationships, complementary, but scarce resources or capabilities of the firms are combined, and unique new products, services, or technologies are created. Strategic supply and outsourcing are also strategic alliances.

## 5.3.3. Electronic links for coordination

The coordination of supply-chain activities between trading firms involves IOS, which reduce coordination costs in transactions of goods or services between firms. In an electronic market, there are suppliers and buyers who interact to supply and purchase products. However, electronic partnerships exist in a bilateral setting, having a dyadic relationship between a supplier and a customer [3]; the relationships between customer and supplier firms become tightly coupled and continue for longer periods.

## 5.3.4. Virtual organizations

Virtual organizations rely on electronic networks to exchange information quickly inside and beyond the firm’s boundaries. This allows strategic partnerships to use networks to exploit thei complementary knowledge and capability in cooperating on joint product development and R&D. Firms in virtual organizations can access and obtain key resources including know-how that is not jointly owned but a need to purchase them.

Kraemer and Dedrick [10] pointed out that Cisco’s virtual organization is composed of resellers, service specialists (providing network integration and operations), and manufacturers (providing most of Cisco’s production capacity). Cisco used the Internet and IT to integrate external partners into providing effective operations and customer service. Cisco referred to these Internetbased virtual organizations as their global networked business model.

Japan Airlines’ (JAL) virtual organizations provide the basis for their strategic partnerships that use their complementary knowledge and capabilities [4]. JAL used their computer reservation system as the base of their value chain. The system includes upstream firms that provide products and services as well as downstream firms that sell and distribute JAL’s end products. The result was that JAL and its partners could better understand customer needs and jointly reduce new product development time.

The inter-firm relationships of a firm seem to take various forms. One firm may use traditional links for coordination, while another may prefer an electronic partnership.

## 5.4. Characteristics of inter-organizational relationships

The level of trust, control mechanisms, and business strategy depend on the type of inter-organizational relationship. In traditional links, the products or services tend to be nonspecific, and may be transacted with many different firms; the customer may measure and evaluate the quality and timeliness of suppliers’ deliverables. If the performance is deemed to be unsatisfactory, another firm can be substituted without high switching costs. Thus, the level of trust between partners is relatively low.

Strategic alliances always involve incomplete contracts, as it is not possible to develop contracts that specify all potential outcomes of their interactions. To mitigate risk and uncertainty, strategic alliances require behavior control focusing on trust. Electronic links allow frequent, quick, and timely sharing of information between trading firms resulting in a moderate or high degree of trust between partners [9,11]. This results in a sharing of common values and culture between the parties.

In virtual organizations, strategic collaboration depends on high levels of mutual trust to encourage the continuation and growth of a successful relationship. Thus, to ensure strategic relationships between involved firms, social control should be employed and utilized.

In electronic links, through the use of IOS, various economic factors such as cost reduction, greater accuracy and quicker response can be attained. Virtual organizations use strategic cooperation among networked firms and promote joint strategic goals, such as cost leadership, and product and market differentiation.

## 5.5. Empirical evidence of the four types

Using cluster analysis, sample firms were classified according to the amount of information exchanged and the usage level of IOS or TCM. This provided groupings of companies that are similar in terms of the amount of information exchanged and their use of IOS and TCM. We performed two cluster analyses: one for types of traditional links and electronic links, and a second for strategic alliances and virtual organizations. We used the hierarchical agglomerative method to form clusters because it generates nonoverlapping clusters. For the sorting or linkage rules, Ward’s method was chosen.

## 5.5.1. Types of traditional links and electronic links

Based on the values of IOS, TCM, face-to-face meetings and transaction information, a cluster analysis was performed to find two clusters of organizations: Traditional and electronic links. In addition, the mean scores of IOS, TCM, face-to-face meetings and transaction information were calculated for each cluster. A critical issue was to determine the optimal number of clusters. While there are formal decision rules to guide this process, heuristics are commonly used. A formal approach in determining the most appropriate number of clusters is to examine the distance coefficient. This is presented in Table 8. The points at which the distance coefficient suddenly jumps indicate suitable stages for analysis.

In Table 8, the distance coefficient increases substantially at three points—between the sixth and seventh clusters, between the third and fourth clusters, and between the first and second clusters. This implied that the seven-cluster, four-cluster and twocluster solutions could be appropriate points for analysis. Considering that four variables were utilized in the cluster analysis, the four-cluster result provides suitable data to examine the variations in IOS, TCM, and transaction information. ## 6. Conclusion

The first research question was whether the effectiveness of different communication media depended on the types of information exchanged. Based on the MRT, we expected that IOS was suitable for transaction information, and rich TCM was most appropriate for exchange of management information. The results of our analyses showed that the usage of IOS was the first significant influence factor and face-to-face meetings were the second, depending on the amount of transaction information exchanged.

However, the results suggested that the most significant factor on the exchange of management information is a lean IOS such as e-mail. This implies that IOS can be effectively utilized for the exchange of both transaction and management information. IOS can be used as either lean or rich media depending on the levels of mutual understanding of the message sender and receiver.

The second research question was whether or how different kinds of information exchanged influenced production performance. The results indicated that transaction and management information have a positive impact on quality improvement and a high dependability of supply. Only transaction information had a significant and positive effect on cost reduction. However, the two types of information did not significantly increase flexibility.

Our study suggested four types of inter-organizational relationships: traditional links for coordination, strategic alliances, electronic links for coordination, and virtual organizations. The framework can be utilized to identify a particular firm’s relationships with others. If the type of inter-firm relationships is ascertained, various mechanisms can be investigated and proposed. IOS also function as a control mechanism in inter-organizational relationships.
