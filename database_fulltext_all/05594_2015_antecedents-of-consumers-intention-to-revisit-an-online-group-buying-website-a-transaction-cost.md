# Antecedents for consumers’ intention to revisit an online group-buying website: a transaction cost perspective

## Abstract:

Consumers’ revisit is essential for online group-buying (OGB) websites to enhance business benefits. From the perspective of transaction cost economics (TCE), this study identifies three context-specific TCE factors, i.e., unpredictability, trust, and personalization specificity, that can directly affect consumers’ revisit intention. Moreover, the proposed direct effects are arguably contingent upon the type of consumers (from direct versus indirect visiting channels). Our research model is empirically validated via an online survey. The results support the hypotheses that unpredictability and trust significantly influence consumers’ revisit intentions. In addition, the results indicate that the visiting channel moderates the effects of the transaction cost factors. Specifically, the unpredictability has stronger negative effects on revisit intention for indirect visitors, while the positive effects of personalization and trust become stronger for direct visitors.

Keywords: Online group-buying; Revisit intention; Transaction cost; Visiting channel

## Antecedents for consumers’ intention to revisit an online group-buying website: a transaction cost perspective

## 1. Introduction:

Online Group-Buying (OGB) is a business model which aggregates the buyers power to gain lower prices [40]. Originated by Mercata.com and Accompany.com in 1999, OGB has experienced rapid growth and evolved into a huge e-commerce market. For example, according to the IBISWorld report in 2013 [33], the OGB market generated about \$2.9 billion in revenue in America in 2012. In China, according to the China Online Group-buying Data Monitoring Report, the sales volume of OGB in China peaked to about \$3.3 billion in 2012 [9, 10]. However, due to the intense competition of the past two years, many OGB practitioners have encountered a downturn in customer flow, and many of them have quit this market<sup>1</sup>.

On traditional e-commerce websites, vendors provide a wide range of products, and consumers accordingly have many choices with regard to the products they would like to purchase. Considering the intensive competition in the past two decades, every surviving traditional e-commerce website has had a relatively steady customer flow. Furthermore, with accumulated experience of interacting with a number of specific websites in the past, most consumers often choose to revisit and purchase products from specific websites. However, different from traditional e-commerce websites, OGB websites provide a very limited range of products, which often exist on the website for a relatively short interval, and they thus can satisfy fewer customers needs. Usually, it is only after continually revisiting an OGB website that a customer is able to obtain an optimal choice and conduct a final purchase. Particularly, as a new and growing form of e-commerce, OGB websites remain relatively unknown to many potential customers, while the market is intensively competitive (e.g., hundreds of OGB websites competing in the China market [9]). In other words, a customer faces many potential choices with regard to conducting online group buying, and a consumer’s revisit thus has become the predictor of the potential purchase. To this end, how to attract customers to continually revisit a specific OGB website has become a critical concern to both practitioners and scholars.

Despite the significance of customer revisit, prior literature has paid little attention to this critical phenomenon, and some research gaps remain to be fulfilled. First, a major stream of the study on revisits is related to customer satisfaction and emotions in marketing and tourism literature [6, 31, 37, 43]. Although antecedents such as satisfaction and attitude may also be influential in the OGB context, they are too general, and we have little knowledge regarding context-specific antecedents which can provide us with more closely scrutinized theoretical and practical meanings. Second, prior IS literature has mainly drawn upon classic IS theories (e.g., TAM and the flow theory) to identify antecedents for consumers’ adoption of OGB [24, 54, 76, 85, 91], while the transactional nature of OGB has not been theoretically considered. In other words, as OGB involves the buyer-seller transactional dyads, it is necessary to identify some transactional antecedents so as to assist OGB practitioners in further developing their businesses. Third, in this newly emergent market, consumers are able to visit a certain OGB website through different channels. However, we have little knowledge on whether the alternative channels can actually affect consumers’ revisit behaviour.

Drawing upon transaction cost economics (TCE) [21, 22, 53], we conceptualized customers’ perceived unpredictability as the factor that represented uncertainty, while personalization specificity and trust specificity were conceptualized as factors that represented asset specificity. It is proposed that these antecedents can directly affect customers’ revisit intention. Further, based on the TCE, it is argued that customer type (i.e., direct visit customers versus indirect visit customers) can moderate the relationships between the proposed TCE factors (i.e., unpredictability of the product show, personalization specificity and trust) and customers’ revisit intentions. To test the proposed research hypotheses, an online survey was conducted, and the results support most of the proposed hypotheses.

This paper can potentially contribute to previous literature in several ways. First, the major contribution of this paper is the extension of the TCE to the research field of customer revisits. Particularly while in a well-known IS study, Liang and Huang [53] tailored the TCE to study customer behaviour in e-commerce, we extend their research framework by investigating consumers’ revisit behaviour. In general, our research effort adds to the generalizability of TCE, and provides future research on customer revisits with an insightful theoretical lens. Second, this study particularly contributes to OGB customer revisit literature by investigating the effects of the

#

critical yet greatly neglected factor, i.e., of alternative visiting channels (which are conceptualized as customer type in this study). In fact, our study further extends the study of Liang and Huang [53] by justifying that the relationships in their research framework is not static but contingent upon customer type, i.e., customers from different visiting channels. Third, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study attempting to investigate context-specific antecedents for customers’ revisiting of OGB websites, which can add to our knowledge on our focal phenomenon.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. OGB and OGB revisit

In general, OGB is a business model that aggregates buyers’ power to gain wholesale prices [40], and is different from traditional e-commerce in many aspects (see Table 1). First, compared with traditional e-commerce, OGB often provides customers with low relevance but limited types of products in a relatively short time-frame [10, 17, 55, 87]. Second, different from traditional e-commerce websites, which mainly retail products [35], OGB usually adopts the discount business strategy [2, 12, 35, 40] and guarantees higher bargaining power for buyers [35, 40, 50], in such a way that they can purchase the desired products at lower prices [2, 12, 35, 40]. Third, traditional e-commerce does not limit the transaction volume at a certain price, while an OGB transaction can only be permitted when the number of purchases is greater than the criteria set by the seller [39, 56, 95]. Besides, while most online consumers are familiar with traditional e-commerce and often visit the target websites directly, many consumers are not that familiar with OGB, and they often turn to navigational

## portals to visit an OGB website [10, 17].

Following prior research [31, 58, 59], consumers’ revisit intention in this study refers to customers’ perception of the likelihood of their revisiting the target online group-buying website in the future. Although previous studies have attempted to investigate consumer revisit behaviour, most of them have focused on general factors such as consumer satisfaction and emotions [6, 31, 37, 43, 72] and thus are unable to provide us with context-specific implications regarding consumer revisiting in the OGB context. Besides, prior IS literature draws upon traditional theories such as TAM, the flow theory, and the social exchange theory to identify the antecedents of OGB adoption [13, 24, 54, 76, 85, 91]. However, the transactional nature of OGB, which is an important perspective to understanding the buyer-seller relationships, has not been fully explored. Thus, we attempt to scrutinize the transactional characteristics of OGB websites, to gain insights into some more context-specific antecedents (i.e., considering the specific features of OGB websites) so as to provide us with a more comprehensive understanding of the OGB context.

## 2.2. TCE in e-commerce

TCE theoretically explains why a transaction subject chooses a particular transaction partner instead of others [15, 53, 80, 88]. According to TCE, a buyer is one who would like to transact with a seller who can minimize the transaction costs [15, 53]. The transaction cost is mainly derived from uncertainty and specificity [21, unexpected outcome and asymmetry of information [53, 65, 88], asset specificity describes “durable investments that are undertaken in support of particular transactions, the opportunity cost of which investment is much lower in the best alternative uses or by alternative users” ([53], p.32). Particularly in the e-commerce context, uncertainty reflects the inability to predict relevant issues due to information asymmetry, while asset specificity refers to the time, effort, or capital invested in a particular transaction [21, 22, 53, 83].

From the consumer’s perspective, TCE factors have been found to be critical predictors of an individual’s acceptance of and preference for e-commerce [21, 22, 53], as are the purchase and repurchase transactions, and loyalty to the transaction partner [44, 83, 92]. For instance, Liang and Huang developed a research model based on TCE to explore consumers’ acceptance of the electronic market [53]. The results indicate that TCE factors (i.e., uncertainty and asset specificity) determine consumers’ choice of online shopping. Devaraj et al. integrated TCE and TAM to capture consumers’ satisfaction toward e-commerce channels and their channel preferences [21]. In an empirical study, Devaraj et al. found that asset specificity and uncertainty are associated with channel preferences [22]. Based on the TCE, Teo and Yu developed a model to investigate consumers’ online buying behavior, and the results indicated that consumers’ willingness to buy online is determined by TCE factors such as uncertainty, frequency, and trust [83]. Yen et al. integrated TCE and the expectancy confirmation theory to explore a bidder’s repurchase intention in online auctions [92]. Moreover, in the online travel market, the TCE factors were found to significantly influence consumer satisfaction and loyalty [44].

## 2.4. OGB visit channels

In general, customers attempting to access alternative sellers are likely to have different levels of sensitivity to transaction costs with each seller. Specifically, consumers with more alternative sellers may be more sensitive to the negative effects of the transaction costs, and hence the transaction partnership with a current seller is relatively fragile [67, 74]. In the OGB context, different ways of visiting [49], i.e., buyers engaging in direct visiting (e.g., typing in homepage address, clicking pre-stored bookmarks) or indirect visiting (e.g., OGB navigation portals, search engines), can provide customers with alternative websites at different levels.

Specifically, the direct visiting of a channel refers to the direct ways of visiting an OGB website. For instance, users can directly gain access by typing in the homepage address of an OGB website such as Groupon, Livingsocial, or use their pre-stored links. The consumer engaging in direct visiting of a channel is termed a

#

direct visitor. Besides making possible the direct visiting of a channel, the OGB market also provides indirect ways of promoting an OGB business such as through navigation portals, online advertising, search engines, etc. For instance, Tuan800.com and Yipit.com, both of which are OGB navigation websites, collect various group-buying products provided by affiliated OGB websites. Customers can find some interesting exhibited products and gain access to an independent OGB website through the product links. The customer, who visits an OGB website through an indirect channel, is known as an indirect visitor.

In the OGB context, compared with direct visitors, consumers from an indirect search engine of an OGB website can help in reducing the transaction costs in the alternative evaluation stage, such as search costs, or comparison costs. Thus, visitors will be more sensitive to the negative effects of the transaction costs [67, 74]. Prior research has found that consumers’ direct revisits are a good sign of loyalty, providing great value to businesses [63, 79], and a consumer’s direct revisit behavior significantly increases repurchase decisions [58]. Additionally, it is also suggested that the number of visitors acquired through other websites or search engines is positively related to financial performance [27].

Generally, consumers from indirect channels can easily access more alternative websites that provide the desired products. However, such buyers tend to be unfamiliar with the target website and are more concerned about the uncertainty of transacting with it, and thus are less likely to spend more time and effort on appropriating the usage of the website. To this end, it is believed that different types of consumers (from direct or indirect channels) may respond differently to transaction costs (with a specific OGB website), and this will in turn have different effects on consumers visiting different channels.

## 3. Research model

Drawing upon transaction cost economics (TCE) [21, 22, 53], we propose a research model (see Figure 1). Customers’ perceived unpredictability was conceptualized as the factor that represented uncertainty, and personalization specificity and trust were conceptualized as factors that represented asset specificity. It is proposed that these antecedents can directly affect customers’ revisit intentions (i.e., H1, H2a, and H2b). Moreover, it is proposed that that customer type (i.e., customers from direct vs. indirect visiting channel) can moderate the relationships between the proposed TCE factors (i.e., unpredictability, personalization specificity and trust) and customers’ revisit intention (i.e., H3a, H3b, and H3c).

## 3.1. TCE antecedents in the OGB context

Generally, revisit behavior can be considered as a choice between the target OGB website and other OGB websites [31, 58, 59]. To this end, transaction cost economics, which explains why a transaction subject chooses a particular transaction partner instead of others [15, 53, 80, 88], can be employed as the theoretical base, and a seller who can ensure a significantly low transaction cost can “lock in”, and thus facilitate the revisit of the target consumers [3, 5, 88].

As discussed previously, a transaction cost is derived mainly from uncertainty and asset specificity [21, 22, 53]. The characteristics of OGB provide some cues that product range, consumers are unable to predict which product(s) will be presented on the next visit (uncertainty cue). With respect to the asset specificity cues, buyers may have already expended time and effort in personalizing the use (e.g., personalizing the location, and preferences to reduce unrelated product information), and already gained some experience with a certain OGB website that minimizes the threat of information asymmetry (e.g., low price, low quality concerns). In the following paragraphs, we will specifically conceptualize these cues as theoretical constructs in the OGB context.

## 3.1.1. Uncertainty

Uncertainty refers to the cost associated with the unexpected outcome and information asymmetry [53, 88]. It includes product uncertainty and process uncertainty [53]. Product uncertainty refers to the buyer’s difficulty in predicting the outcome of a transaction due to the information asymmetry related to the product per se; and process uncertainty describes the buyer’s perception of a lack of complete confidence in the transaction process. While it should be acknowledged that product uncertainty may also be a critical issue with regard to consumer acceptance and revisit effect of process uncertainty, which involves new and unique issues on consumer revisits in OGB.

From the consumers’ perspective, OGB is a kind of e-commerce website which is subject to continuous change. Particularly, one product will only be displayed for a short period. For a particular website, new products are transacted daily, and a website often does not consistently provide the same products or the same kinds of products. These unpredictable provisions can result in higher search costs, because buyers need to pay for each effort to browse and seek products. Consistent with the TCE framework of Liang and Huang [53] , this unpredictable process is derived from the transaction process (i.e., product searching) and is an important form of process uncertainty. In the current study, we define such uncertainty as unpredictability, which refers to the extent to which a buyer believes that the current product on sale in the target OGB website is unpredictable. Obviously, the unpredictable feature of the

OGB may increase the perceived information asymmetry and lead to transaction costs.

In this study, unpredictability indicates that consumers cannot accurately predict whether the desired products will be provided on the next visit. The higher the unpredictability is, the lower the possibility of consumers’ needs being fulfilled, and thus the less the likelihood that the consumers will revisit the focal website. Besides, high unpredictability can result in search costs and cognitive costs which accordingly impede consumers from progressing to the next transaction process, i.e., revisiting the target OGB website. Based on the preceding arguments, we thus hypothesize:

H1: Unpredictability is negatively related to revisit intention.

## 3.1.2. Asset specificity

Asset specificity refers to the durable investments of the transaction and transaction partner [53]. In general, OGB users do have investments in a specific OGB website. There are hundreds of OGB websites; each of them has its own features in terms of design or services. OGB users need to expend time and effort on familiarizing themselves with and personalizing the website so as to lower the transaction costs. For example, most of the OGB websites provide regional deals and the products are not specific to certain categories nor closely related. It will be easier to find the target deals if consumers customize the references and personalize the exhibition formats of the products. Otherwise, they would have to deal with many less related deals or redundant information. Such time and effort expended to modify a website is described as personalization specific investment [5, 42]. Obviously, personalization specificity is specific to the target website, and can potentially assist consumers to reduce the cost of future visits to that specific OGB website [48, 52].

Moreover, the vast variety of available OGB websites and the features of OGB (e.g., limited provision range each visit and inconstant provisions) result in the consumers frequently encountering unfamiliar sellers. At the same time, OGB also offers discount selling, while the market has less credible websites. Therefore, building trust is a critical issue for the OGB business [47, 66, 75]. The transaction perception for a website [11, 16, 45]. Due to the information asymmetry, trust is a means of alleviating information deficiency and decreasing the transaction costs [7, 8, 65]. Based on TCE, these specific assets are basically sunk costs which will be lost if a buyer switches to other websites [5, 53, 88]. From this point of view, trust can be conceptualized as a kind of asset specificity.

According to the TCE, these asset specificities are basically sunk costs and will be lost if a consumer switches to other websites [5, 53, 88]. Therefore, a lock-in effect is built by these asset specificities [3, 5, 86, 88]. Specifically, personalization specificity can reduce the transaction cost of reuse [48, 52] and causes the user to favor the target website. With respect to trust, due to information asymmetry in OGB, trust is a means of alleviating information deficiency, and suggested to be the most important risk reducing strategy [7, 8], and trust thus reduces the transaction cost [65]. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H2a: Trust is positively related to revisit intention.

## 3.2. Effects of consumer type (direct visitors versus indirect visitors)

Consumers visiting different channels are likely to behave differently. Generally, when consumers perceive higher transaction costs at an OGB website, they are more likely to switch to other alternatives and are unlikely to revisit. Compared to direct channel OGB visitors, users visiting indirect channels can easily obtain alternative websites, and the transaction partnership is thus relatively weak [67, 74]. If they perceive a higher transaction cost with an OGB website, they are more likely to switch to other alternatives and would not choose to revisit.

When consumers are faced with many alternatives, they tend to overestimate the probability of potential losses or costs, even if the probability of such losses is quite low [65]. Therefore, if indirect visitors find it difficult to know what is on sale on a specific website on the next visit (i.e., the unpredictability is high), they may rely more on the agent to find a target transaction partner to reduce the transaction cost instead of revisiting that OGB website. Besides, indirect visitors are less likely to have an overview of the target transaction partner. They need to collect more detailed product information [20] and may be more concerned about the uncertainty (i.e., unpredictability) that whether they can obtain the desired product if they choose to revisit a website. In this case, they suffer high transaction costs (e.g., cognitive costs) and thus the revisit intention tends to be low. Thus, the unpredictability will lead to higher transaction costs for indirect visitors compared to direct visitors. Therefore, it is proposed that:

#

H3a: The effect of unpredictability on revisit intention is stronger for indirect visitors than for direct visitors.

It is believed that the effect of asset specificity is also contingent upon consumer type. Indirect visitors get access to the target OGB website through the products exhibited by third parties and they can easily find other similar options. Therefore, they may not want to invest much asset specificities (e.g., time and effort) to a certain transaction partner [74]. Thus, they are more sensitive to transaction cost and will keep away from the transaction with high asset specificity requirement [67, 74]. On the other hand, indirect visitors keep the opportunities to switch for other alternatives [64, 84]. They rely more on the third parties to reduce the transaction costs instead of carefully personalizing a certain website. Accordingly, they are less likely to value the time and effort spent on modifying and personalizing a website [74], which is the potential source of building the lock-in effect. In this case, the effect of personalization specificity will have a weak effect on indirect visitors. Moreover, compared with direct visitors, the indirect visitors are more likely to be oriented toward the product, and they are less likely to build a stable relationship with a transaction partner [64, 84]. Toward this end, they are less likely to strongly value the trust they have in the target website. Therefore, we propose:

H3b: The effect of trust on revisit intention is stronger for direct visitors than for indirect visitors.

H3c: The effect of personalization specificity on revisit intention is stronger for direct visitors than for indirect visitors.

## 4.2. Measures

Where possible, existing validated scales were adapted to the OGB context; while some new items were developed for the construct unpredictability based on prior relevant research. All the measures for constructs in the research model were based on a seven-point Likert scale, ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree”. Specifically, consumer revisit was measured by behavioral intention [94] using the instruments from Hausman and Siekpe [32] and Jones et al [36]. Personalization specificity was measured using the items in Kim and Son [42], and trust was measured by the scales in Kim et al. [41]. The instruments for unpredictability were developed based on Devarah et al. [21] and Dimoka et al. [23]. The items for each construct are presented in Appendix I.

## 6. Discussion and implications

## 6.1 Main effects

In this study, we investigated the effects of unpredictability, personalization specificity, trust and visiting channels on consumers’ OGB revisit intentions. The findings confirm that transaction cost factors indeed influence consumers’ revisit intentions to an OGB website, and thus suggest that transaction cost is an insightful theoretical lens to investigate consumer revisits in the OGB context.

The current study contextualized the transaction cost theory and identified the specific transaction cost factors in the OGB context. Specifically, we contextualized the uncertainty and asset specificity [21, 22, 53] in terms of unpredictability, personalization specificity, and trust. As we predicted, the unique type of uncertainty (i.e., unpredictability) was indeed negatively related to revisit intention. This demonstrates that OGB operators should carefully design a website to reduce unpredictability. For instance, Juhuasuan.com has introduced the announcement of the following day’s products since 2012 and also provided fixed time group-buying (e.g., at 10 am daily). Such strategies may reduce unpredictability to some extent.

Different from our expectations, personalization specificity is unable to significantly affect revisit intention. A possible explanation might be that the effects of personalization specificity are likely to be contingent on the consumer involved (which will be described in detail subsequently).

Moreover, previous literature has shown that perceived risk damages consumers’ OGB intention [91, 97] and there are also studies verifying that trust is a key factor in OGB. For instance, Pi et al. [66], Shiau and Luo [75], Ng [61], and Ku [47] all demonstrated that trust significantly influences consumers’ group-buying intention. Consistent with these studies, this study investigates customer trust from another perspective (i.e., the transaction cost perspective) and finds that consumers’ trust of a target website can motivate consumers to revisit that target OGB website.

## 6.2. Effects of visiting channels

In attempting to extend existing customer revisit literature, we investigated the effects of visiting channels, which is critical in OGB but as yet understudied. As is observed in Table 5, unpredictability has a stronger negative effect for indirect visitors, while trust and personalization specificity have stronger positive effects for direct visitors. Specifically, as shown in Figure 3a, unpredictability has negative effects on revisit intention in both of the two subgroups (i.e., direct visitors and indirect visitors), with the indirect visitors seen to be more concerned about the unpredictability. This implies that website operators should take heed that uncertainty reduces the number of available strategies for OGB users, especially for indirect visitors.

## 6.3. Theoretical implications

Several theoretical implications can be drawn from this study. First, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate context-specific antecedents for customers’ revisiting of OGB websites, which can add to our knowledge of the OGB context. Most of previous OGB literature has focused on general constructs such as attitude, satisfaction, and usefulness [24, 47, 75, 91] to investigate consumers adoption and post adoption behaviour, and revisiting is always suggested as being related to customer satisfaction and emotions [6, 31, 37, 43, 46, 72]. Our study move a step further and attempt to investigate context-specific factors. By scrutinizing the characteristics of OGB websites, we both theoretically and empirically justify that consumer revisiting can be particularly affected by context-specific antecedents.

Second, the current study extends existing knowledge of OGB adoption and makes contribution to TCE. Specially, TCE has been extensively employed to explain customers’ purchase behavior in e-commerce [e.g., 53, 65]. Its ability to shed light on customers’ revisit behavior, particularly in the context of OGB has yet been formally tested. This study both theoretically and empirically demonstrate that TCE is effective in explaining users’ revisit behaviour. Thus, this study extends the generalizability of TCE to the research field of customer revisit behavior and also provides an insightful theoretical lens for future research.

Third, in order to further the exploration of the competitive OGB market, the current study particularly contributes to OGB customer revisit literature by investigating the effects of the critical but greatly understudied factor, i.e., customer type. Prior literature has shown that consumers behave differently through multiple channels in terms of information searching, page view, etc [20, 49]. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is yet a study that has investigated the underlying mechanisms, especially in the OGB context. Our inclusion of the visiting channels also extends the TCE framework of Liang and Huang [53] by justifying the contingent effects. Specially, indirect visitors are more vulnerable to uncertainty (i.e., unpredictability), while direct visitors benefit more from asset specificity (i.e., personalization and trust).

## 6.4. Practical implications

Our study explored the effects of transactional characteristics on consumers’ revisit intentions. The results provide insightful practical implications for OGB operators. First, we propose that practitioners consider the strategies to reduce consumers’ perceptions of unpredictability, such as pre-announcements of forthcoming products on offer in the near future. For instance, pre-announcements strategies could be used on OGB website home-page, email subscriptions, and mobile short messages, etc. Actually Juhuasuan.com has adopted this pre-announcements strategy and attracted many consumers to revisit. In addition, considering that indirect visitors are more sensitive to unpredictability, OGB practitioners are advised to develop the uncertainty reducing strategies for all OGB users, especially for indirect visitors. Thus, it may be helpful to provide fixed time strategy (e.g., provide new deals at a fixed time everyday) and various pre-announcement strategy (e.g., provide the announcement on product pages or OGB portals) so as to reduce the perceived unpredictability of indirect visitors.

Second, managers of OGB websites should try to carefully design their websites so as to provide specific and personalized service, and thus reduce the transaction costs, especially for direct visitors. For instance, customized home-page based on users’ preference or location could be helpful to reduce the searching cost. In addition, OGB operators need to attempt closer collaboration with their suppliers, and to carefully control the product quality and service quality so as to build customer trust [11, 19, 62, 71]. Thus, the resulting personalization specificity and trust will help in building a lock-in effect to the visitors’ usage and stimulate revisit behavior. Furthermore, the moderating effects of consumer type (i.e., direct versus indirect visitors) indicate that OGB operators should identify consumers’ visiting channels and provide customized services to different consumers accordingly, so as to minimize the transaction costs, and thus maintain customer flow. For instance, considering that direct visitors value a website’s personalization design, OGB operators are well-advised to develop well-designed personalized services to maintain these users.

## 7. Limitations and future research

Despite contributing fresh insights to OGB literature, there are some limitations to this study. First, we utilized an online self-reported survey. Thus, self-selection bias might be a major limitation of the results [81, 90]. Although the test for common method bias did alleviate this bias to some extent [60], it could not be fully eliminated. Second, there are two types of users in the OGB context: suppliers and consumers. The OGB website plays an intermediary role with them. Our focus was only on the consumer’s perspective. Future studies will benefit by focusing on the seller’s perspective in order to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the OGB market. Third, the sample of the survey was limited to the China context, which may result in the issue of generalizability. For instance, when considering that group buying is a kind of group behavior, it is likely that cultural differences in China and west countries (e.g., collectivism versus individualism) can potentially influence consumers’ revisit behavior. Thus, future research is urged to collect more data, and replicate the study in other (or larger) context and/or explore the effects of regional or

#

cultural factors. Fourth, while the consumers are the objective of transaction on OGB, the current study mainly focused on the technology (i.e., the features of OGB), and some potentially critical factors that can illustrate the impacts of consumers per se (such as consumers’ occupation, and their status in the social network) has not been involved in current study. Furthermore, the monetary benefits are one of the advantages of OGB. Although our research focused on consumers revisit behavior within the OGB market, there are still some differences among websites due to the variance of their bargaining power. Future research could further enrich our understanding of OGB revisit by either exploring the implications of the current study and design some prototypes or market strategies, or involving more factors related to consumers per se. Finally, this study focused mainly on consumers’ revisit intentions. Although intention is suggested as one major predictor of behavior [25], it would also be interesting to explore consumers’ actual revisits. In future, researchers could collect more objective data to further explore OGB users’ behavior.
