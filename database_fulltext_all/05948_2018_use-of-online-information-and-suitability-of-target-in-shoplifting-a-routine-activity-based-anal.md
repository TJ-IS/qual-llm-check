## Abstract

Shoplifting is the largest contributor to inventory depletion in the US retail sector. To effectively mitigate and prevent such criminal activity, one needs to understand the shoplifter’s perspectives on the suitability of the retail products targeted for shoplifting. Extending Routine Activity Theory (RAT) from criminology literature to include usefulness of online information, we analyze shoplifters’ perceptions regarding future target suitability by considering a retail item’s value, inertia, visibility, and accessibility (VIVA). We also examine how online information about a target’s disposal and guardianship can influence shoplifters’ decisions. In this paper, the Partial Least Squares (PLS) method was used to analyze data collected in the Western New York area over a one-year period. The results show positive effects of value and reverse inertia on target suitability. Interestingly, the relationship between target suitability and the usefulness of online information about post-shoplifting disposal activity was negative. Implications for future research and practical applications for shoplifting prevention are discussed.

Keywords: Routine Activity Theory (RAT), Online information, Target suitability, Shoplifting

## 1. Introduction

The Internet has changed consumer shopping habits (Donner et al. 2015). Just as this technological change has revolutionized the conduct of legitimate shoppers, it has also had an impact on the planning and decision making processes for shoplifters (Bossler and Holt 2009; Newman and Clarke 2013). Information about commercial products is now widely available on the Internet, and thus, rational decisions about a product and its desirability can now be based on such information (Kim et al. 2008). Since shoplifters are generally presumed to be rational individuals (Carroll and Weaver 1986; Cornish and Clarke 2003; Smith and Clarke 2015), we suggest that information (gathered from a retailer’s website, for instance) can shape their decisions when considering potential targets for shoplifting or theft.

In this paper, we draw upon criminology literature, specifically Routine Activity Theory (RAT) (Cohen and Felson 1979) as the basis for informing theory and applying it to the shoplifting domain. RAT focuses on environmental factors that affect the likelihood that a crime will occur and according to traditional RAT, criminal activities can be explained by examining offenders’ routine activities. Using the Internet to provide information about consumer products has become a routine activity in modern society, and therefore the analysis of information technology and its relationship to shoplifting certainly falls within the ambit of RAT in order to trace the sources of crime. In addition, RAT proposes that three core factors – a motivated offender, the perceived suitability of a target, and the lack of capable guardianship (i.e. store owners, employees, security guards, etc.) – should converge in time and space for crimes to occur (Felson 1994). The Internet has facilitated various information gathering methods; thus, it can be argued that before the actual shoplifting event, the offender and the target have already converged virtually (Bossler and Holt 2009; Hinduja and Patchin 2008). In the case of shoplifting, we argue that the shoplifter’s initial convergence with a target product happens online while gathering information to ascertain its suitability for theft, and potentially gather information about the guardianship of the product.

Since the use of the Internet has created an environment where potential shoplifters and potentially suitable targets can virtually converge, this virtual convergence must be translated into a physical and temporal convergence for an actual crime to occur (Cohen and Felson 1979). This is

Third, for the act of shoplifting itself, we go beyond the current literature that primarily focuses on forefront considerations like the actual act of theft. Instead, we examine the back-end of the act and also study how online information about the disposability of a retail item factors into the decision-making processes around shoplifting. We, therefore, develop the concept of usefulness of online information about guardianship and post shoplifting activities. In short, extending RAT, we articulate constructs to capture the usefulness of information acquisition for post-shoplifting disposal activities.

## 2. Literature Review

Shoplifting is commonly defined as the act of stealing merchandise offered for sale in a retail store (Hayes 2007) and is a category of crime all over the world (Kuang et al. 2017; Szumigala and Savage 2017). According to Hollinger and Davis (2013, it is the second major source of inventory depletion among American retailers. The 26th annual retail theft survey (Hayes 2014) has established that retail losses in the U.S. are approximately \$44.2 billion annually and roughly 30-40% of these total annual retail losses are caused by shoplifting. Thus, this data suggests that shoplifting as a phenomenon is not effectively being addressed in retail-industry practice (Jacques et al. 2015; Lasky et al. 2017).

Alongside the increase in shoplifting, information channels that can provide potentially-valuable information to shoplifters are also increasing. For instance, Craigslist, online forums, retailers’ websites, eBay, Amazon, etc., may be sources used for gathering information about a target, methods of disposal or resale of the target item for shoplifting, as well as detailed product related information including price, weight, and/or size. Moreover, advertising websites such as Craigslist can provide information about an item’s popularity and resale value. While the literature examines the modus operandi of actual shoplifting, very few studies have taken the initiative to examine the planning and thought process involved in the perpetration of shoplifting. Most studies that have examined this phenomenon have only examined the pre-and actual shoplifting motivations. Seldom have the post-shoplifting motivations been studied. The examination of such motivations could direct law enforcement officers to identify the demand for stolen products and help close fencing (i.e., dealing in stolen goods) operations in their jurisdictions.

## Online Information and Target Suitability

The literature is scant regarding the use of online information by potential offenders about the suitability of target in shoplifting. Research has shown that online offenders are also offenders off-line (Donner et al. 2015). Donner et al. (2015) in their study of college students also found most offenders choose their targets online and carry out their offending in the physical world. Other areas of research suggest that heavy users of the Internet are the preferred targets of online offenders (Bossler and Holt 2009; Hinduja and Patchin 2008; Reyns 2013) and in bullying research, it has been found that victims who are more exposed on the Internet or are heavy users of the Internet have become more prone to victimization (Bossler and Holt 2009; Hinduja and Patchin 2008). This means that offenders use information about their targets obtained online when choosing who to target.

## Online Information and Guardianship

Literature about the uses of the Internet to obtain guardianship related information for the purposes of shoplifting decisions is sparse.

Therefore, we draw inspiration from the physical world where several researchers have examined anti-shoplifting measures and found them to be ineffective (Jacques et al. 2015; Johns et al. 2017; Lasky et al. 2017; Reyns et al. 2016). Some literature has found that shoplifters use anti-shoplifting measures in order to perpetrate their crimes. For example, Lasky et al. (2017) found that mirrors installed by retailers to monitor activities within the store have been used by shoplifters to monitor the presence of guardians as well as a means of successfully conducting the shoplifting activity. In short, perpetrators use monitoring devices to instead monitor the movement of the guardians (i.e., store owners, employees, security guards, etc.). This information about the guardians and how they work have been used by the offenders to perpetrate crimes. The supposed crime prevention tool has turned into a perpetration support

## 3. Theory and Hypotheses

Since apprehended offenders have already committed the shoplifting crime, it would be instructive to understand offenders’ perceptions about the future suitability of the target for (repeat) shoplifting. Below we detail the hypotheses related to future target suitability.

## Extending Routine Activity Theory (RAT)

RAT proposes that criminal activity occurs when ordinary activities of motivated offenders and targets meet and there is a lack of capable guardianship (Cohen and Felson 1979). Offenders find suitable targets desirable because they offer the highest rewards (Cromwell and Olson 2004; Wright and Decker 1994). Research on RAT suggests that the suitability of target is shaped by four factors: Value, Inertia, Visibility, and Accessibility (VIVA) (Leukfeldt and Yar 2016; Wang et al. 2015). The meeting of the target and the offender happens first in virtual space where decisions to shoplift are finalized. This virtual meeting is also the impetus for the shoplifter to situate himself or herself in the physical space. In this paper, we suggest that future target suitability for the shoplifters would be impacted, not only by the intrinsic properties of the target but also online information regarding disposal of the shoplifted item.

## Value, Inertia, Visibility, Access (VIVA)

As mentioned above, earlier research has claimed that target suitability is determined by VIVA (Leukfeldt and Yar 2016; Wang et al. 2015). We argue that information about these factors could be readily available to the motivated offender in the virtual world. Potential offenders may find online information that can establish these properties for the suitability of the target.

## Value

Value is an important factor that can lead to higher target suitability for theft (Wang et al. 2015). The value of a target reflects the potential gain that an offender can possibly obtain by engaging in a criminal

activity (Bernasco 2006). In a study by Moore (1984), it was found that the financial benefit of shoplifting was the greatest motivation for shoplifters. Similarly, Yaniv (2009) outlined the economic dynamics of shoplifting and found value to be a prime motivation. From these studies, it can be surmised that if shoplifters perceive an item to be of higher value, it is more suited to be a target for theft. Therefore, we propose:

H1: There is a positive relationship between the value of the item and its future target suitability.

## Reverse (R) Inertia

Inertia of the target is related to the mobility of the target (Cohen and Felson 1979). Heavy and large items are difficult to move as compared to light and small items (Felson 1996). For instance, stealing a 60-inch TV is more difficult than stealing a pocket-sized digital camera. The inertia of the target could also refer to devices that can help immobilize the target. For instance, Johns et al. (2017) found that protective display fixtures could significantly prevent shoplifting because of the restrained mobility of the object. Smith and Clarke (2015) considered removability of the product as one of the attributes of most often shoplifted items. In this paper, we use the term “reverse inertia (R inertia)” of the target to describe the ease of shoplifting of the target. We propose: H2: There is a positive relationship between item (R) inertia and its future target suitability.

## Visibility

Target visibility refers to the degree that people are aware of the existence of the target and are aware of its whereabouts (Cohen and Felson 1979; Felson and Clarke 1998). For instance, when an item is displayed at a highly visible location, shoplifters will consider that as a more suitable target. According to Felson and Clarke (1998), when the perceived visibility of the target is high, motivated offenders would likely find it more intriguing and therefore have a greater probability of being stolen. Therefore: H3: There is a positive relationship between visibility of an item and its future target suitability. Accessibility

Accessibility of the target results from being able to easily reach the target and exit from the location of the crime (Beavon et al. 1994; Leukfeldt and Yar 2016). Because of the high accessibility of a

In the case of burglary, if the targeted house is located in a place that is easy to reach and get away from, the suitability of this house as a target would be high. Therefore, target accessibility may involve knowing about the approach to the target inside the store, as well as the path to a quick exit from the store itself. According to RAT, motivated criminals evaluate their challenges by looking for the presence of guardians before engaging in criminal actions (Carroll 1978; Cornish and Clarke 2014; Felson 1986). With regard to physical security measurements, the presence of police, guards, security cameras, mirrors, alarm devices are considered to be prohibitory factors for conducting criminal activities (Tseloni et al. defeating security systems (Jacques et al. 2015; Johns et al. 2017; Lasky et al. 2017). For instance, potential shoplifters may be conducting surveillance of anti-shoplifting devices in order to evade detection and apprehension by guardians.

Given that, shoplifter’s knowledge about guardianship would enable the offender to evade such guardianship, it becomes important to follow the clues about the relationship of shoplifting and antishoplifting devices. We argue that motivated offenders, as far as shoplifting is concerned, would try to reduce the probability of being caught by guardians. In this case greater online information about guardianship would enable offenders to be on the watch for them. We argue that having more information about guardianship surrounding the target would enable motivated offenders to defeat or avoid the security systems (Jacques et al. 2015; Johns et al. 2017; Lasky et al. 2017). Hence, an increase in useful information about guardianship would increase the future target suitability. We suggest that: H5: There is a positive relationship between perceived usefulness of online sources for guardian information and future target suitability.

## Usefulness of online sources for information about post shoplifting disposal activity

RAT has evolved from simply examining a target’s suitability from an individual’s perception about its intrinsic properties to the impact of such environmental factors such as access, visibility and in general online information to form an impression about the suitability of the target. We argue that getting information about those environmental elements plays a vital role in identifying suitability of a target for shoplifting. Since potential shoplifters want to have early involvement in the process, they need to search over the Internet to understand whether demand is available for the item and whether it is possible to dispose the item easily for a reasonable monetary value. Online sources such as retailer’s websites, Craigslist, among others, can provide information and therefore, could motivate potential offenders to identify a target as suitable. In order to measure the usefulness of online sources for information about post-shoplifting disposal activity, we created a second order formative construct by combining the measures of the usefulness of online information sources to dispose the item after shoplifting. We consider the usefulness of information regarding sale or disposal as well as a target’s value and inertia being key to the post-shoplifting information activity. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

H6: There is a positive relationship between the perceived usefulness of online sources for information about disposal activity and future target suitability.

## 5. Data Collection

In a 1992 study by Carolin (1992), apprehended shoplifters were immediately questioned in order to frame their (respondents’) answers in the context of their current infraction. Similarly, drug addicts of the Arrestee Drug Abuse Monitoring (ADAM) study were surveyed immediately after being apprehended to capture their self-reported views about their current drug offense (Peters et al. 2015). Following this literature, each survey was administered immediately after the time of arrest to make sure that the respondents would contextualize their answers to the questions related to their current shoplifting infraction. This procedure alleviates the problem of associating questions in the survey to their current offense rather than previous offenses. In addition, this immediacy of the administration of the surveys helps in alleviating problems of recall.

In order to collect the actual shoplifters’ responses, an Institutional Review Board (IRB) approved questionnaire was distributed to apprehended shoplifting suspects in a county detention facility in the Western New York. Most of the shoplifters were apprehended in the largest mall in Western New York. Because of the characteristics and vulnerability of the respondents, IRB did not allow police officers to distribute the surveys. Instead, we hired and trained undergraduate students to be at the detention facility for 10 hours a week and distribute the surveys. When we handed in the survey, we provided clear instructions about the questionnaire as well as alerted respondents “If you need someone to read the survey to you orally and/or for your responses to be recorded in writing by the researcher, please inform the person that handed you this survey.” Survey instructions are provided in appendix B.

We distributed 117 surveys from May 2015 to May 2016, out of which 72 respondents completed the survey. Therefore, the response rate was 61%. However, only 57 responses out of 72 were useable for analysis resulting in a 48.7% response rate. (The police did not allow pens or large pencils to be given to the suspects, since there was an apprehension that such artifacts could be used as a weapon by a suspect. So only crayons or small pencils less than 3 inches long were allowed to be given to the respondents for filling out the surveys).

## 7. Discussion and contribution

In this study, we applied RAT to the context of shoplifting while considering the role of online information systems throughout the shoplifting decision-making process. The purpose of this research was to predict the future target suitability not only from the mainstream measures of suitability factors (perceived value, (R)inertia, visibility, and accessibility) of the target but also by understanding the offenders’ perception of usefulness of online information for post shoplifting activities. This research contributes in a major way to shoplifting and information support literature because of the dearth of literature that has studied actual shoplifters. This is the gap that was the impetus for Reyns et al. (2016), Lasky et al. (2017) and Jacques et al. (2015) to conduct simulation studies to study the phenomenon from the “eyes of the shoplifter.” This study has provided greater evidence to the claim by Hayes (2007) that shoplifting requires some communication processes pre and post shoplifting both in choosing the target and disposing of the target. Also, the findings explains the role of information communications

Shoplifters’ Perceptions of Future Target Suitability

technology and Internet in a shoplifter’s decision-making processes in assessing the suitability of targets for theft.

Results revealed that the shoplifters in our sample found that value of the item and ease of shoplifting were the most important factors (among the VIVA components) when considering an item as a suitable target in the future. By empirically testing the relationships in the model, we could expand the boundary of original RAT that only focused on current target suitability, to the future target suitability context. This was possible since we surveyed actual shoplifters after they had committed the crime. Since everything was still fresh in their minds, the elements of poor recall and memory decay was minimized.

The empirical results showed an interesting relationship - that there was a negative relationship (significant) between usefulness of online information sources for post-shoplifting disposal activity and future target suitability. This means that the more useful the online information regarding disposal of a particular item, the less the item will be considered as a suitable target. This finding is important and instructive. One possible explanation is the equal inform tion opportunities these online market places provide, make it also a source of information for law enforcement or retail industries to search for stolen items. In other words, there is no information asymmetry between the offender and police. This availability of information will make police officers also well-informed and, thus, reduce the future target suitability for the offender. Likewise, because the product moves heavily on the black market, offenders might be led to think that they could be trapped in the disposal. There might be some expectations that the product would be more heavily monitored and surveilled in the online market or the fence market.

Further, in terms of control variables we included gender and level of education, both variables were statistically significant in relation to future target suitability. Results indicate that male and educated people have higher perception about target suitability than others.

We also tested the relationship between usefulness of information regarding guardianship and future target suitability that turned out as insignificant. This means that information about guardianship is not much of a concern for future target suitability. This finding is congruent with other findings that suggest motivated offenders are not really deterred by most forms of retail store guardianships (Jacques et

## 8. Conclusion

In the case of shoplifting, we argue that the shoplifter’s initial convergence with a target product happens online while gathering information to ascertain its suitability for theft, and potentially gather information about the guardianship of the product. This paper used RAT to develop a predictive model for future target suitability, taking into account the online information-seeking activities used to plan the disposal of shoplifted items.

We tested our model using 57 survey responses from actual shoplifters who had been caught by police. Analysis of this data demonstrated that the perceived value of the item and the relative ease of taking and disposing of the item are important factors, in identifying future target suitability. In addition, we expanded on the literature and showed that online sources such as retailers’ websites, Craigslist, etc. provide useful information for the post-shoplifting phase. Therefore, shoplifters can gain information

Shoplifters’ Perceptions of Future Target Suitability

about the trade value of a target, the ease of selling it, and the evaluation of online advertisements to assess the possibility of selling the item. However, we found a negative relationship between usefulness of online information activities for post-shoplifting disposal and the future target suitability. Perhaps, future research could continue to refine the conceptualization of this factor to fully explain this unexpected finding in the research.

As with many other studies, this study has limitations. The number of samples was small due to the difficulty of surveying actual shoplifters/ Research in this area can rarely catch or capture a huge proportion of actual shoplifters (Farrington 1999). This small sample size is typical in shoplifting research. For example, (Caputo and King 2011) published a qualitative study using 12 samples of women shoplifters. Further, in Farrington’s (1999) review of studies involving shoplifting, he estimated that less than 1 percent of total shoplifters are actually apprehended. Hence the sample captured by the survey may not be representative of the actual shoplifting population.

There are some avenues for further investigation. For instance, one can examine the effect of perceived deterrence on the relationship between VIVA and future target suitability. In addition, more research on both criminal and non-criminal social network memberships for offenders will provide us with more understanding of the process by which target search, or disposal of the target search happens. There are multiple opportunities where this research could be expanded. For example, would the type and crimes.

Despite the increasing investments of business in anti-shoplifting devices, shoplifting has not been alleviated. This means that the proper target for intervention should not be store but the demand for these stolen goods. As noted earlier, the study of shoplifting has been scant in the criminology literature. As the classic works of Klockars (1974), Steffensmeier (1986) and Goodman et al. (2005) suggest, professional thieves gain much from the shoplifting and that fences (dealers in stolen goods) are instrumental to the continuing involvement in shoplifting. This current research could be expanded to examine the effects of the proliferation of individual e-commerce sites such as Ebay, Craigslist,

The implications of how online and develop future models of target selection in such crimes as terrorism, human trafficking, drug trafficking or even some organized crime activities where information on the Internet can be collated in order to perpetrate a physical crime. There is so much now that we know about consumerism and online information but we have not fully explored the implications of these information in the criminal world. This is just the beginning.

## Research Highlights

 This research focuses on understanding the shoplifter’s perspectives on the suitability of targeted retail products for shoplifting.

 The study extends Routine Activity Theory (RAT) to include usefulness of online information

The study analyzes shoplifter’s perceptions regarding future target suitability as a function of retail product’s value, (reverse) inertia, visibility and accessibility as well as online information about disposal of a shoplifted target and guardianship.

 Findings show positive effects of value and reverse inertia on suitability of target, negative relationship between target suitability and usefulness of online information about post shoplifting disposal activity.

 The use of information technology in carrying out a physically bounded activity is investigated.
