# A configurational model of reward-based crowdfunding project characteristics and operational approaches to delivery performance

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Reward-based crowdfunding Project characteristics Operational approach Delivery performance Qualitative Comparative Analysis

## A B S T R A C T

Reward-based crowdfunding projects promise funders various kinds of rewards for contributing to their funding goals. After successful funding, the funders expect the project owners to deliver the promised rewards within the specified delivery time. Contrary to crowdfunding research based on conventional symmetric thinking, this research investigates the crowdfunded projects using asymmetric analytics to identify diferent configurationa paths to delivery performance. Using data from the Kickstarter crowdfunding platform. Oualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA) was conducted for the antecedent factors, including project venture status, promised lead time, and sourcing as well as production approaches. The findings indicate that the configurational models, with high consistency and coverage, are suficient antecedent conditions for on-time and late delivery. Further, the results confirm that a single antecedent condition cannot solely produce an outcome but should be incorporated with other conditions to produce an intended outcome. The findings ofer insights into the setting of realistic promised lead times that can be combined with other antecedent conditions to achieve on-time delivery. This research supports the complexity theory and extends its application to crowdfunding research by emphasizing that the approaches for on-time reward delivery performance are unique and not mirror opposites of those applied in late reward delivery performance. Further implications are drawn for crowdfunding stakeholders.

## 1. Introduction

Crowdfunding is a new Internet-based financial model that supports entrepreneurs who intend to raise funds for their projects from a rela tively large number of individuals without standard financial intermediaries [1]. There are various crowdfunding models such as donation-based, lending-based, equity-based, and reward-based. Among these models, the most popular and frequently applied is reward-based crowdfunding [2]. Reward-based crowdfunding (RBCF) is a new form of entrepreneurial finance in which investors do not receive cash returns from the project venture but rather receive non-monetary rewards for their investments [3]. Due to the popularity of this model, exploring its dynamics would be of much interest to stakeholders and academics. Hence, this research focuses on RBCF.

Currently, crowdfunding research focuses on success factors [4–9] and the motivations for sponsors [10–13]. In the project implementa tion phase, research has focused on the factors that are responsible for delivery delays [2,14–18]. Crowdfunding literature has emphasized the need for projects to have an operational plan for the delivery of promised rewards to reduce the challenges in the implementation phase, and to improve on delivery performance [17–19]. An operational plan is an approach to be applied for sourcing and production of promised rewards. However, the choice of an operational approach to improve delivery performance depends on certain essential project characteristics. Based on the literature on crowdfunding success, thi study considers the essential project characteristics (i.e., project venture status, promised lead time) which are deemed to influence delivery performance. The combinatory efect of the operational approaches for sourcing and production, project venture status, and the promised lead time would either have a positive or negative efect on the delivery performance. It can be inferred that there exists a need for multiple operational configurations to be pursued for projects with diferent venture status in order to achieve the desired delivery performance outcome. Besides, there is a need for configurations that can support crowdfunding projects in setting realistic promised lead times for on time delivery. This is because diferent operational approaches, coupled with a project venture status (i.e., a newly created venture or an existing startup venture), require specific time frames to be able to complete the entire process of reward delivery. For instance, an already existing project venture with an established supply network would need a shorter promised lead time than a newly created project venture with no established supply network. Therefore, understanding such config urations would help RBCF projects to reduce the challenges in the im plementation phase and achieve on-time delivery. However, in crowdfunding literature, little evidence exists on such configuration research.

As a contribution to this gap, this study applies a fuzzy set-Qualitative Comparative Analysis (set-QCA), a set-membership analy tical technique to explore the configurations of project venture status, promised lead time, sourcing approaches (i.e., proactive sourcing, postcampaign sourcing), and production approaches (i.e., in-house production, outsourced production) that lead to on-time or late delivery performance. Furthermore, such configurations seek to serve as the basis for setting appropriate promised lead times for the various categories of ventures that utilize RBCF. The choice of this method is based on the idea that relations are usually better understood through settheoretic relations rather than correlations [21,22]. This analytical approach, grounded in set and complexity theories as well as Boolean algebra, allows for a detailed analysis of how antecedent conditions contribute to an outcome in question.

The findings indicate that the configurational models, consisting of antecedent conditions with high consistency and coverage, are suficient to predict on-time and late delivery. The results also indicate the combinatory conditions for which an antecedent has a positive efect on the outcome as well as the combinatory condition for when an antecedent has a negative influence on the outcome in a multiple path set. Therefore, the models confirm that a single antecedent condition cannot solely produce an outcome but should be incorporated with other conditions to produce an outcome. Furthermore, the findings suggest that antecedent conditions for on-time delivery are unique compared to the ones for late delivery. Lastly, the models ofer insights into the setting of appropriate or realistic promised lead times that can be combined with other antecedent conditions to achieve on-time delivery.

The sections are organized as follows. Section 2 is a literature review on crowdfunding. Section 3 is on the development of set-theoretical hypotheses. The presentation and explanation of data collection follow in Section 4. The fifth section presents the results, followed by a discussion of the findings and the conclusion in Section 6.

## 2. Literature review

This study applies a QCA of the configuration models in crowd funding projects while Multiple Regression Analysis (MRA) has been applied in similar previous research. Thus, this section provides a review of the related crowdfunding literature and compares QCA to MRA through existing research.

In crowdfunding research, reward delivery performance is an essential measure of RBCF project implementation success. This is because the timely delivery of rewards contributes to sponsor satisfaction, among other factors, as established through this kind of research [12]. Further, crowdfunding literature suggests that one of the most important motivations for funders to invest in a project is the promised reward [15.23]. A recent study has established that potential funders have concerns about the founders' credibility to produce and deliver the promised products as specified in the crowdfunding campaigns [19]. Hence, reward delivery performance would have a long-term efect on funders' reinvestment and loyalty in RBCF. A project's delivery perfor mance may either be on-time or late when compared to the agreed scheduled date. Despite the importance of delivery performance in RBCF, late delivery is a common phenomenon among crowdfunded projects [2]. Consequently, crowdfunding research, in the project im plementation phase, have resorted to symmetric analytics and used various regression models to identify certain factors that account fo late delivery among crowdfunded projects [2,15–17]. Mollick [2] established that a project's size, increased expectation of highly popular projects, and the amount of funding a project receives predict the degree of delay. In addition, Hauge and Chimahusky [15] established that a project's goal and the team are the causes of late delivery. Likewise, Mollick and Kuppuswamy [16] established that a large funding goal and more money raised than expected decrease the chances of on-time reward delivery. Mollick [17] reported on crowdfunded projects' delivery rates and revealed that a delay occurs due to the size of a project goal and the degree of overfunding in a project.

Existing literature on crowdfunding has also suggested the need for a project implementation plan to improve delivery performance $[ 1 7 , 1 9 ]$ . Mollick [17] advised that because the majority of projects promise and deliver concrete products as rewards, it is necessary for projects to have a manufacturing strategy before the start of the project. He [17] further recommended that having a complete project schedule before the launch of the campaign would reduce or avoid delays. Courtney et al. [19] also proposed that project founders should have an operational plan for the delivery of promised rewards. The inference from this research suggests the need for operational approaches for both sourcing and production of rewards. This study argues that op erational approaches and a project's venture status influence the setting of promised lead time for the delivery of rewards. Therefore, the combinatory efect of these project characteristics would form antecedent conditions with multiple paths to the desired performance outcome and ultimately assist projects in setting a realistic promised lead time suitable for each configuration.

Currently, research on project delivery performance has emphasized less on the antecedent roles of project venture status, promised lead time, and operational approaches of sourcing and production, and mostly adopted symmetric methods of MRA [15–17]. Even though MRA, which focuses on the net efect, is classy to contemplate, it cannot show how the complex combinatory antecedent conditions of the in dependent variables (project venture status, promised lead time, sourcing approaches, production approaches) would contribute to the dependent variable (on-time or late delivery). A symmetric method tests the association of two or more items to measure the same construct (e.g., seeking high coeficient alphas for a test). Further, based on an underlying assumption on the sampling and distribution of data, MRA, which asks “what factor,” holding all other factors constant at an individual factor's average, will increase or decrease the likelihood of an outcome [20]. MRA uses $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ to predict individual ability in a model rather than group behavior [26]. Furthermore, statistical correlation analysis which relies on matrix algebra application describes the size and path of a direct relationship between two or more variables [24]. Consequently, it ignores other alternative causal conditions $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ antecedents) to an outcome as can be displayed by fuzzy set-QCA configurational models.

The fuzzy set-QCA approach ofers more insight into the study's dataset than the use of other conventional symmetric methods, such as MRA [20]. The causal asymmetric principle, with its foundation from complexity theory, suggests that a study with a focus on causes of one outcome $( \mathbf { e . g . }$ , acceptance) often tells very little about the causes of another outcome $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e } } . \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g } } . ,$ , rejection) [24,25]. For instance, in an exploratory study of crowdfunding dynamics, Mollick [2] revealed that project size and the increased expectation of highly popular projects have a substantial adverse net efect on the projects and lead to late delivery. However, Mollick [2] did not address how the seemingly negative project size and increased expectation was overcome to achieve the 24.9% rate of projects with on-time delivery, thus, what combination of antecedent conditions of the crowdfunded projects led to on-time delivery? Studies on crowdfunding success have established that the outcome of a funding success is not the mirrored opposite of a funding failure [8,9]. Therefore, separate asymmetric models for both outcomes are necessary for research to ofer more insight into a particular phenomenon. Likewise, in the implementation phase, diferent factors identified in existing research as the causes of late delivery performance would not be mirrored opposites to on-time delivery performance.

In this study, QCA models have been applied to support the essential tenets of complexity theory [28,32]. The first tenet states that a single antecedent is necessary but insuficient to predict a high or low outcome condition. The second tenet, the approach principle, states that a complex condition of two or more simple conditions is adequate for a consistently high score of an outcome condition. Moreover, the third tenet, the equifinality concept, illustrates that there are diferent but equally valid ways to reach the same outcome depending on a specific context, a much-observed phenomenon which the conventional social science methods have not captured [28]. Based on the equifinality principle, fuzzy set-QCA configurational models establish the antecedent conditions that represent diferent causal paths to achieve an outcome [21,27,28]. Further, the central principle of tenet four states that an approach leading to the second outcome (e.g., on-time delivery) is unique and not the mirror opposite of one leading to a diferent outcome (e.g., late delivery).

Furthermore, tenet five indicates that an individual attribute in an approach can contribute positively or negatively to a specific outcome, depending on the absence or presence of other attributes in the ap proach. The sixth tenet also states that for an outcome score, a given approach should have a coverage that is less than 1.00, which suggests that exceptions exist for all the approaches. Besides, the homogeneity of causal efects is not presumed; on the contrary, a given condition may, combined with other varied ones, sometimes act in favor of or against the outcome. Thus, within a given model's outcome, an antecedent could be absent or present as well as interact with the other conditions either positively or negatively. The absence or presence suggests the essential or non-essential role in the model's outcome, whereas a positive or negative indicates the extent of impact on the outcome. Therefore, an antecedent condition is not expected to be symmetrical—instead, causal asymmetry is assumed, implying that the presence and the absence of the outcome, respectively, may require different explanations, which makes the asymmetric analytics more informative than the symmetric methods in management research [24,26,29]. Thus, it brings out a social reality and supports complex social science thinking than other statistical methods, such as MRA and Structural Equation Model (SEM) [24,30].

A set-theoretic hypothesis in the QCA is a case-oriented approach that examines relationships between antecedent or causal conditions and an outcome. It is based on the assumption that additivity is no longer assumed, meaning every single cause has its own separate, in dependent impact on the outcome, and is abandoned and replaced by the assumption that conjunctural causation is at work, which mean that one condition cannot solely produce an outcome [20]. Further, the set-theoretic and complexity approaches examine the nonlinear, and non-probabilistic influence of the combined variables on an outcome [20,55]. The test of set-theoretic hypothesis is based on the “INUS condition, which is defined as an insuficient (i.e., it cannot produce the outcome by itself) but non-redundant (i.e., it is necessary for all the configurations) part of an unnecessary but suficient condition [56].” The application of a fuzzy set-QCA model, by this study, combines quantitative and qualitative data by using a unified and systematic analytical approach, and reports on empirical models that include both positive and negative antecedent conditions that are necessary and suficient for the outcome [24,31]. This paper focuses on the project implementation stage of RBCF. It explores the interactions of project venture status, promised lead time, and sourcing approaches as antecedent conditions which form configurations with multiple paths to ontime and late delivery performance.

## 3. A configurational model of reward delivery performance

This sub-section proposes set-theoretic hypotheses based on related literature on crowdfunding and operations management. Based on the propositions, a configurational model is put forth to assist in answering the research question.

## 3.1. Project venture status

Previous studies suggest that sourcing and production approaches difer from one venture to another due to factors such as the type of venture, funding constraints, demand, and agreed customer service level [33,34]. Similarly, a crowdfunding project may either belong to an existing or newly created venture during a crowdfunding campaign. This status is likely to influence the operational approach on sourcing and production of rewards. For instance, in an existing venture with supply chain partners, sourcing would probably be initiated before the beginning of a campaign, whereas in a newly created venture, a successful funding campaign would be the prerequisite of deciding on the appropriate approaches for sourcing and production. An existing ven ture with certain resources may prefer in-house production or the incorporation of outsourced production as part of its approach. Likewise, a newly created project venture would consider outsourcing the production function as a better option to fulfill promised rewards on time due to limited production facilities. Besides, a project's venture status would influence the setting of promised lead time. A newly created venture with few or no supply chain partners would set a longer lead time to enable them to find partners after the funding phase. An existing venture with established supply partners would set a short lead time due to information availability and short time intervals in the flow of goods from upstream to downstream. The preceding discussion indicates the influential role of a project's venture status in the choice of the other operational approaches and the setting of promised lead time. Thus, we suggest that a project's venture status would be part of the antecedent conditions to achieve a specific delivery performance. Hence, we formulate the first set-theoretical hypothesis as follows:

H1. A crowdfunding project's venture status would serve as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal conditions to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

## 3.2. Promised lead time

Lead time is the order delivery period and the accumulation of waiting time for loading and warehouse operations by a venture or supplier to fulfill a customer order [35,36]. The existing literature found a direct relationship between lead time, operational management, and delivery performance [37]. In RBCF, promised lead time is a period given by a project founder as the stipulated duration for the delivery of promised rewards after successful funded. The promised lead time reflects on multiple factors such as the project venture status, resource availability, the nature of the supply chain network, and crowdfunding experience. For instance, a project from an existing venture with crowdfunding experience would rely on available in formation to set a realistic lead time than a counterpart from a newly created venture with less or no information. Consequently, the promised lead time in RBCF exhibits the level of preparedness toward delivery. A shorter lead time could suggest adequate preparation, whereas a long lead time could mean that the project founder prefer using the campaign outcome to make an informed decision on the appropriate and eficient operational approaches to achieve on-time delivery.

Accordingly, Bretschneider and Leimeister [38] established that the scheduled delivery time, which is similar to other project campaign attributes, ofers an informative cue about the project's preparedness, hence, influences funders' investment decisions. An inference from the above suggests that the promised lead time would determine the number of rewards a project is expected to deliver, and with the interaction of other antecedent conditions, would either result in on-time or late delivery performance. However, there is no clear evidence in current crowdfunding literature on the antecedent role of promised lead time on delivery performance [2,15–17]. Therefore, this research argues that the promised lead time would be part of the antecedent conditions to achieve a specific delivery performance. Hence, the second set-theoretic hypothesis is proposed as follows:

H2. A crowdfunding project's promised lead time would serve as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal condition to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

## 3.3. Sourcing approach

Sourcing approach entails the selection of suppliers, the design of a supply contract, product design collaboration, and procurement and delivery of materials as well as other supplies [39]. The type of sourcing approach, as mostly referred in literature, is internal or external sourcing from home, market, or third countries for components and the final product [40]. It could also be a single, sole, multiple, or hybrid type of sourcing [41]. These approaches are essential tools for better organizational performance depending on the type of industry [42]. Moreover, industry diference may have an impact on the appropriateness of a particular sourcing approach with its corresponding level of performance [43]. Thus, the diverse working environment of ventures may require a diferent specific operational approach [44]. For example, regarding the type of sourcing, the military sector often uses a single sourcing approach for security reasons. The automotive industry also relies more on single sourcing. Further, healthcare institutions usually go for multiple sourcing to secure an inventory of essential supplies to avoid the loss of human lives [44].

Likewise, in initiating any project that has demand uncertainties with varieties of deliverables tied to specific timelines, “the time” to choose an appropriate sourcing approach is vital. In the RBCF model, a project founder could choose to identify a source for supply before the number of rewards is established (i.e., proactive sourcing) to support early take-of or he or she would wait and confirm the exact project demand before sourcing for supplies (i.e., post-campaign). Chakraborty and Swinney [45] observed that the fixed funding mechanism in RBCF allows founders to develop a sourcing plan that is contingent on the funding goal. The authors have stressed that it is essential for crowdfunding projects to have appropriate operational management to support crowdfunding delivery owing to the technical challenges and unforeseen obstacles involved in product development and the manufacturing process. Based on the above discussion, this research proposes that a crowdfunding project's sourcing approach (i.e., proactive and post-campaign sourcing) would constitute an essential part of a project's operations management and would, therefore, serve as an antecedent condition to a specific delivery performance outcome. Consequently, we formulate the next two set-theoretic hypotheses on sourcing approaches as follows:

H3a. A crowdfunding project's proactive sourcing approach would serve as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal conditions to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

H3b. A crowdfunding project's post-campaign sourcing approach would serve as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal conditions to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

## 3.4. Production approach

The fixed funding mechanism allows creators to develop production plans contingent on the level of demand. Chakraborty and Swinney [45] suggested that the technical challenges and unforeseen obstacles involved in projects' reward development and manufacturing require an efective and eficient operational management approach to achieve the desired delivery performance. They also ofer the view that a high-type experience creator would opt for better production technology or supply chain partners for better delivery performance than a counterpart with minor experience. Mollick and Kuppuswamy [16] have also advised that projects need to have a manufacturing strategy before the launch of a campaign. Furthermore, projects can pursue either in-house production or outsourced production. In-house production requires the availability of internal resources. Besides, it works well when smaller quantities of customers' orders exist and economies of scale as well as cost reduction cannot be achieved through an outsourcing approach [46]. Further, a project venture may enjoy the benefits of in-house production if it has full operational control. However, customer dissatisfaction may arise if internal capacity cannot adequately meet quality expectation and the demand level. Venkatesan [47] defined outsourcing as a management strategy of transferring non-core functions to specialist, eficient, and external providers.

Usually, outsourced products are resource intensive and available from niche market suppliers with proven technology and skills. Further, outsourcing requires less managerial supervision, with clear contractual accountabilities, and the certainty of demand [48]. Moreover, outsourcing of the production function would reduce operational costs in the short term, free up assets, and provide access to proven technical and commercial world-class practices [49]. However, lack of control, overdependence on external providers, and increased complexity in the supply chain put the outsourcing of the production function at risk [46]. These could afect the extent of the responsiveness to customers' orders and delivery performance if not appropriately managed. Both approaches could be taken into consideration based on the project venture status and the promised lead time. Thus, a new venture with few resources would prefer to outsource the production function, while a project from an existing venture would probably produce certain aspects of the product in-house and outsource some non-core components. Additionally, the duration of the promised lead time would determine the production approach to meet such timelines. Accordingly, this research argues that the two production approaches would serve as antecedent conditions and interact with the other project characteristics in multiple paths to produce a specific delivery performance outcome. Therefore, we formulate the last two set-theoretic hypotheses on the production approaches as follows:

H4a. A crowdfunding project's in-house production approach would serve as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal conditions to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

H4b. A crowdfunding project's outsourced production approach would act as an antecedent condition and can be combined with other causal conditions to achieve on-time or late delivery performance.

## 4. Methods

## 4.1. Data collection

The confirmation of the above configurational model is from data collected on crowdfunded projects campaigns in eight (8) categories on the Kickstarter crowdfunding platform (https://www.kickstarter.com) from 2010 to 2016. The idea behind the selection of the project categories is that not all projects promise physical rewards which require sourcing and production processes. Consequently, considering these categories of projects, this research builds a sample for the scope based on the project characteristics of interest. Further, it is imperative to note that the sampling, which was based on the features, does not generate severe bias because of the suficient variations among them as will be indicated later in the results' presentation.

Data on the independent and dependent variables were extracted from the projects' crowdfunding campaign homepages on the Kickstarter platform. On the platform, a project founder pitches a project with descriptions that include the funding goal, types of rewards, estimated delivery dates, and product descriptions/videos. Other information include a project's venture profile, crowdfunding experience, anticipated risk and challenges, operational approaches on sourcing and production of rewards delivery, reward delivery updates, and a project community. Kickstarter works on the “all-or-nothing funding principle,” where a project is successful when it meets its funding goal and receives the total amount pledged at the end of the campaign. Project implementation begins after the funding period. In this phase, a project is expected to deliver all promised rewards within the specified scheduled time. A project is considered to have an on-time delivery status if all funders receive rewards before or on the last date stipulated to be the end of delivery. Further, a project is considered to have a late delivery status if there are outstanding rewards that need to be delivered after the end of the date stipulated to be the end of delivery. All such information is accessible in the updates section of an individual project on Kickstarter.com. In this research, the unit of analysis comprises of projects that had completed or were supposed to have completed the delivery of rewards during the data gathering period. This unit of analysis ensures that our dataset contains only those projects with clear performance outcomes, that is either on-time or late delivery.

## 4.2. Predictive variables

The identification of the types of sourcing and production approaches in the risk and challenges as well as the projects' update sections on Kickstarter.com was extracted manually due to the small textual content. Subsequently, we explored the statements that demonstrated any form of operational approaches put in place to reduce the identified risks and ensure the timely delivery of rewards. These raw textual documents were subjected to the coding process described below to arrive at a project's operational approaches.

In order to achieve consistency in the coding, the research team agreed on coding parameters which consisted of category names, rules, and the definition for assigning codes as suggested by Weber [51]. The research team then coded a sample of the raw data to validate the agreed parameters for the coding. Verification of the consistency level was performed through an inter-coder agreement within the research team. The team resolved all doubts and minor problems concerning categories, rules, and definitions. Subsequently, the team looked for the expression of statements that had patterns of the operational approach as suggested by Smith [52], and used inductive and deductive reasoning approaches to identify categories from the patterns. The patterns identified from the first open coding were grouped into families, followed by superfamilies as the last phase of the data coding. This coding resulted in four categories of themes as illustrated in Table 1. In order to recheck our coding consistency and resolve conflicts, one of the research team members, who is familiar with content analysis and was not involved in the first coding process for objectivity, assessed the final coding against the agreed parameters. The few identified inconsistencies were collectively resolved through a discussion. Besides, other quantitative and qualitative data on the promised lead time and the year of a project venture establishment were all retrieved to de termine venture status as either an existing or newly created startup during the crowdfunding campaign.

## 5.3. Configurational models

In this research, the use of a fuzzy set assisted in maintaining more details of the data with continuous values [20]. We followed the QCA technique applied in some studies [30,53] to analyze the truth table, and identified the necessary as well as suficient conditions to help explore antecedent or causal conditions that exhibit the same outcome. The above was followed by a computation of the parameters of fit (i.e., consistency and coverage), respecification of cases (identification of combinations), and antecedent or causal conditions for the outcomes. In order to allow for the transformation of qualitative information to quantitative values, fuzzy set-membership scores were generated for the variables, followed by the calibration of the data to produce values ranging from 0 to 1 [27]. We, subsequently, specified three qualitative anchors for the calibration process: the threshold for a full membership fuzzy score was 0.95, for a non-membership was 0.05, and the crossover point was 0.5 [27]. The calculations of the calibration for the membership scores were performed using the fuzzy set score in the QCA package in R. The data were condensed into a “Truth Table” to indicate all possible configurations of the diferent antecedent or causal condi tions that were thought to afect on-time or late reward delivery performance [32,54]. The “truth table” analysis assessed which antecedent or causal conditions (if any) are individually necessary or suficient for achieving the outcome. An assessment to confirm whether all the lo gical configurations have empirical cases, based on the existing the ories, was also conducted. The QCA configurational models explain the combinations of the antecedent or causal conditions for on-time or late reward delivery performance.

The test and confirmation of each set-theoretic hypothesis are based on the configurational models which consist of each hypothesized variable and other causal conditions, with their consistencies and coverage values within the recommended parameters. In the fuzzy set QCA, a model is informative, and it predicts an outcome when the consistency value is above 0.74 with a coverage not exceeding 0.65, indicating that a subset relation exists and supporting the existence of a suficient condition [27]. Further, the raw and solution coverage determine the empirical relevance of a solution and measure the degree to which the configurations explain the outcome. The unique coverage measures the proportion of memberships in the outcome or the percentage of cases explained by an individual configuration [52]. The high coverage value indicates that a configuration model explains a large proportion of on-time or late delivery performance [21]. Based on the coverage and consistency parameters above, a hypothesized variable is confirmed as an antecedent or causal condition when it forms part of any of the configurational models that predict the outcomes.

In Tables 10 and 11, all the configuration models have consistency values exceeding 0.74 and coverages not exceeding 0.65, indicating that the antecedents forming the configurations are suficient condi tions to cause on-time and late delivery performance outcomes [27]. ## 6. Discussions, research implications, and conclusions

## 6.1. Discussions and research implications

Contrary to existing empirical studies based on symmetric analysis, this research explores a crowdfunding project's delivery performance by the use of asymmetrical analytics. The models in Table 10 consist of diferent paths with unique approaches to achieve on-time delivery. More importantly, they depict the relevance of promised lead time as the basis for achieving on-time delivery. Model one offers some insight into newly created ventures, seeking crowdfunding. It is essential to set long promised lead times to support post-campaign sourcing and the outsourcing of the production function. This approach is necessary because newly created ventures usually do not have established and reliable supply chain partners to support the delivery of rewards within a short lead time. Model one would be more applicable to project founders who are skeptical about the number of project subscriptions and would prefer to use a post-campaign sourcing approach after the number of rewards is known. For post-campaign sourcing, a collaborative efort between the projects and their supply partners is required to be able to deliver rewards within the promised lead time. Further, the adverse efect of a short lead time in model two of Table 10 can be reduced with a proactive sourcing approach specifically for strategic and bottleneck components which are crucial for the production of the rewards.

In-house production would also support a short lead time due to the control of significant aspects of the processes as compared to outsourced production. The proactive sourcing approach coupled with inhouse production would serve as a contingency plan as suggested by Chakraborty and Swinney [45] that crowdfunding projects should have such a plan to reduce the challenges and other obstacles involved in the reward delivery process. In addition, it answers the call in the study by Hauge and Chimahusky [15] that more reward levels necessitate careful project planning. Model two, which does not have the presence of outsourced production, supports existing entrepreneurial literature that some entrepreneurs lack the resources to purchase and manage an outsourced arrangement to be able to realize the associated benefits [48,57]. Therefore, not every project creator would feature it in their operational approach toward on-time delivery. Model two further suggests that with or without outsourced production, an on-time reward delivery is feasible depending on the extent of risk exposure, production capacity, and the venture management decision. The above assertion is supported by the literature that discovered that outsourcing is beneficial to operational performance in high-risk scenarios and disincentive in a low-risk scenario with high production capacity [58].

Model three also ofers an understanding that newly created ven tures can promise a long lead time when they have the capacity and resources to pursue in-house production to reduce the adverse efect of the outsourced production approach. Thus, for some ventures, one approach alone would not be feasible for implementation, probably due to the lack of infrastructure and full capacity. This model ofers flexibility in an operational approach toward on-time delivery. It falls in line with existing operations management and supply chain literature which suggest that flexibility in an operations decision yields better performance [59,60]. Besides, aligning outsourced production with other operational approaches, such as proactive sourcing and in-house production, also supports management literature that the performance of outsourcing yields better results when aligned with another operational approach [29,59]. In comparing configuration models one and three, it is reasonable to suggest that model three, which contains outsourced production in addition to the other four variables that form model one in Table 10, ofers more flexibility in the operational ap proach on how to achieve on-time delivery as compared to model one. Furthermore, an existing study finds that outsourcing is a common practice in entrepreneurial ventures which lack facilities for in-house production but with the objectives to achieve cost reduction, an increment in production output, and product and service quality improvement [61]. Therefore, it is not surprising to see outsourced production appearing in three of the four configurational models to achieve on-time delivery in both existing and newly created entrepreneurial ventures.

Model four has a significant implication to crowdfunding projects with existing venture status. The positive efect of proactive sourcing would reduce the adverse efect of a short lead time and an outsourced production function. Furthermore, the proactive sourcing approach would assist in the due diligent work for the selection of capable and competent suppliers and contract manufacturers. The model also supports the importance of a manufacturing plan or strategy before the launch of a funding campaign [16]. All the four configurational models depict the essential role of a project's venture status in the setting of a promised lead time and the choice of an appropriate operational approach to achieve on-time delivery. They also support previous research findings that an operational approach difers from one venture to another [33]. These models, with high consistency and coverage values, are more useful and implementable in setting realistic promised lead times for funding campaigns, and in efective and eficient project delivery.

The findings on the paths to late delivery performance difer from previous crowdfunding research findings on the causes of late delivery [2,16,17]. The four models suggest that the absence of either proactive sourcing or outsourced production is among the antecedent conditions for late delivery. Specifically, newly created ventures that decide to produce all rewards in-house but do not begin the sourcing process before the start of the campaign, would deliver late. This finding is in line with the outcome of the interview reported in CNNMoney that manufacturing challenges are among the primary causes of a project's late delivery [14]. Furthermore, models one and three indicate that the adverse efect of in-house production in both ventures would result in late delivery. The high consistency and coverage values make the models critical paths to late delivery. The models confirm that apart from the level and amount of funding received, project category and size as established in previous crowdfunding studies as the causes of late delivery, promised lead time, project venture status, sourcing, and production approaches are found to be the antecedent conditions for late delivery performance, in this study.

Theoretically, this research presents insights into the nature of the complex interactions among crowdfunding operational approaches, promised lead time, and venture status. This research also provides several insights into the crowdfunding phenomenon, while also con tributing to the growing body of literature on projects' reward delivery performance. Through the analysis, the antecedent conditions to a project's reward delivery performance were examined and verified, and the applicability of complexity theory was extended to crowdfunding. Further, as Woodside [29] advocates that complexity theory underpins testing theory beyond a symmetric viewpoint, the results of this research support the call to develop and test the major tenets of the theory as indicated in Section 2.

Practically, this research ofers some advantages to several stake holders in crowdfunding. First, the configurational models would benefit crowdfunding platform operators and funders in terms of providing for them a useful idea for a project's reward delivery performance. Second, the results of this research equip crowdfunding project creators with an idea of setting a realistic promised lead time that would syn chronize with a particular operational approach. Third, a knowledge of the configurational models would assist prospective crowdfunding project creators to plan an optimal approach toward successful campaign implementation. Last, it is vital for potential crowdfunding projects to note that the ventures in which their projects originate from should be considered in the selection of operational approaches and in the setting of promised lead times.

Besides, project creators should embrace proactive sourcing for the strategic and bottleneck components of the promised rewards to ensure timely delivery in the implementation phase. Conversely, post-campaign sourcing can be used to procure leverage items to take advantage of cost minimization. Crowdfunding platforms should encourage project founders to make adequate preparation toward the sourcing and production of funders' rewards before the funding phase begins. Advanced preparation may promote on-time reward delivery and fur ther boost crowd confidence in this emerging scheme of entrepreneurial financing. The implications of this research are limited to the rewardbased model on Kickstarter crowdfunding platforms without considering the other crowdfunding platforms. Consequently, future research can be conducted by applying a comparative study design of diferent crowdfunding platforms. This research further recommends that crowdfunding platform operators and entrepreneurs should comprehend the various antecedents approaches toward both on-time and late reward delivery.

## 6.2. Conclusions

Based on the crowdfunded proiects' data on Kickstarter.com. this research investigates the antecedent role of project venture status, promised lead time, and sourcing as well as production approaches in the achievement of on-time or late delivery performance through asymmetric analytics. It centers on the literature of crowdfunding and operations management to propose antecedent conditions for the out comes. The results of the cross-tabulation analysis confirm that both positive and negative performances to the main relationship occur be tween the independent and dependent variables. With the asymmetric analytics, an understanding of the configurations of the antecedent conditions which lead to on-time or late reward delivery performance contributes to crowdfunding research. This research has established the essential role of promised lead time and project venture status as the basis for the choice of an appropriate operational approach that has a high likelihood of achieving on-time delivery.

The use of QCA in this research gives more information on a project's reward delivery performance compared to previous research with symmetric analysis. Apart from showing the relationship between the independent and dependent variables just as in symmetric analysis, QCA results indicate interrelationships among multiple antecedent conditions that lead to on-time and late delivery performance. This research is among the few crowdfunding research to apply asymmetrical analytics and demonstrate the significance of applying such analytics in constructing new results. It supports the tenets of complexity theory with its application in the crowdfunding context.
