# Determinants of customer acceptance of multi-service network: An implication for IP-based technologies

Dong Hee Shin

Department of Interaction Science, Sung Kyung Kwan University, 53 Myeongnyun-dong 3-ga, Jongno-gu, Seoul 110-745, South Korea

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Article history: Received 23 May 2006 Received in revised form 23 September 2007 Accepted 2 May 2008 Available online 14 January 2009

Keywords: IP-based technologies IPTV Technology adoption Perceived values and characteristics Technology acceptance model

## A B S T R A C T

Using a modified TAM as a conceptual framework and SEM for analysis, we determined the factors influencing the adoption of Internet Protocol Television by surveying 320 consumers. The modifications involved new constructs that incorporated user-perceived control and security in the model. The perceived quality of the content and system were found to have a significant effect on perceived usefulness and perceived playfulness. In addition, perceived control was found to have a significant effect on both extrinsic and intrinsic motivation, A significant relationship was also found between consumerperceived security and intention. Also consumers regarded price as a main driver for switching TV services. We discuss the significance of this observation in the context of the emerging trend of technology convergence and importance in acquiring customer preference and other strategies.

## 1. Introduction

Internet Protocol Television (IPTV) has emerged as a multiservice network for carrying broadband services. Despite its innovative functionalities and convenience, it is still not a major threat to older technologies. Its evolution has had to focus on overcoming four obstacles:

1. Bandwidth limitations, because simultaneous video streams, especially in an expanding HDTV environment, has demanded high bandwidth.

2. Cable operators, broadcasters, phone companies, and the telecommunication industry have provided major competition.

3. The carriers have had to consider how to achieve highperformance, stable operations and inter-operations of integrated parts in the network, in order to ensure high-quality service.

4. Customers have had to be convinced that it is in their interest to adopt IPTV, because it offers better features, such as content, price, interoperability, search, etc.

Consequently, industries are now focusing on users’ motivations and experiences: how they feel about new technologies and how they use the technology. User acceptance is tied directly to user experience, which is in turn related to motivation.

From the user acceptance perspective, we took a micro-based approach focusing on customers’ motivations in order to identify forces that drove the development of IPTV services. Diffusion of new technologies resulted from a series of individual decisions to begin using them; such decisions were often the result of a comparison of possible benefits against the uncertain costs of adoption. Understanding the factors affecting this choice was thus essential.

We thus decided to develop a theoretical and empirical analysis that explained factors influencing potential user adoption of IPTV.

## 2. Overview of today’s IPTV

IPTV is the set of multimedia services, such as television, video, audio, text, graphics, and data delivered over IP-based networks managed to provide the required level of quality of service, security, interactivity and reliability. Digital television service is delivered using IP over a network infrastructure, which includes delivery by a broadband connection. For residential customers, IPTV is provided in a form of Video-on-Demand and can be bundled with Internet services such as VoIP and Web access. The commercial bundling of IPTV, VoIP, and Internet access is called as Triple Play service. Recently, Quadruple Play was introduced by adding mobility to Triple Play.

Market research firms expect that global IPTV subscribers will grow to 53 million by 2009 in the USA, and that in the meantime service providers will rapidly increase the amount of IPTV content and transport equipment. Most telecom broadband network operators are planning to offer IPTV. While it is at different stages of product definition, field trials, or early deployments, it is apparent that IPTV is poised to become the framework of incremental revenues of fiber deployment. In addition to the quest for better revenue, rapid progress in IPTV has been fueled by the competition between telcos, cable operators, and new network entrants of home entertainment.

While most developed countries have deployed IPTV, the world’s leading markets for IPTV are the U.S., South Korea, Hong Kong, Japan, Italy, and Belgium. In the U.S., big telcos like SBC Communications, BellSouth, and Verizon have already started launching IPTV systems, but their strategies vary. For example, Verizon plans to adopt a hybrid model combining traditional cable and IPTV technologies while SBC and BellSouth want to launch fullfledged IPTV networks. One global driver of IPTV is Microsoft, which has been pushing its Microsoft TV IPTV Edition software in hopes of dominating the nascent IPTV market. Microsoft has been trying to break into the TV business for years with limited success. South Korea is one of the most advanced broadband markets, with availability and subscriber levels for high-speed DSL broadband that lead the world. The competitive atmosphere is also becoming tense as operators try to gain a place in the market.

By deploying IPTV services, providers can derive additional revenue through their subscriber access networks in less time, at lower cost, and from a larger market. The service providers can bundle their services based on Internet protocols. In addition, the IPTV platform gives providers an open platform for delivering advanced entertainment services with the dual benefit of responding to the cable threat with a superior offering, while opening revenue opportunities not available on cable TV platforms.

However, IPTV also has several potential problems and challenges: the most serious concern is security of the content, services, and infrastructure. Unlike traditional television services, IPTV is not a closed system and is subject to threats from hackers, etc.

## 3. Theoretical background

## 3.1. Customers’ attitude toward converged multi-service network

TAM is an adaptation of the theory of reasoned action (TRA) specifically tailored to model user acceptance of IT. To provide an explanation and prediction of the determinants of IPTV adoption, our motivational model used two constructs: intrinsic and extrinsic motivation. Intrinsic motivation can be defined, per Teo et al. [16] as the performance of an activity for no apparent reinforcement other than the process of performing the activity per se. In contrast, extrinsic motivation was seen as the performance of an activity because it was perceived as instrumental in achieving valued outcomes distinct from the activity itself. Perceived usefulness can be an example of extrinsic motivation, while perceived fun, playfulness, and enjoyment are intrinsic. Shin [15] developed a more comprehensive version to reflect mobile Internet context. His model employed perceived playfulness, content quality, system quality, Internet experience, and perceived price level, in addition to perceived usefulness and ease of use.

Cconvergence is the synergistic combination of voice (and telephony features), data (and productivity applications), and video on a single network. These technologies are now able to share resources and interact, creating new efficiencies. Nysveen et al. [12] investigated the consumers’ intention to use mobile Internet service and examined three factors affecting usage intention: motivational and attitudinal influence, and perceived control. Fogelgren-Pedersen [4] found that connection stability and geographic coverage were two of the significant variables of perceived relative advantage in wireless broadband. Also Yu et al. [19] found that the perceived enhanced utility of mobile services was a strong value motivating use of t-commerce. While these studies found significant perceived variables, they still did not find variables specific to emerging technologies.

The question remained: what peculiar variables in convergence technologies were different from those in other services? We needed to understand user behavior associated with multiple, synchronous, ad hoc, and ubiquitous connections. One of the distinctive features of convergence was that it offered different content (voice, texts, video, broadcasts, etc.) in a simplified, convergent, and computer-mediated system that enabled individuals to interact, play, communicate, collaborate, and share information in many different ways.

However, many consumers are concerned with security issues, and providers have begun enhancing system security. The model should therefore incorporate new factors to improve its explanatory utility [10]. Our study therefore proposed motivational variables specific to IPTV from a content and conduit framework. Reflecting the most distinctive competitive advantage of controllability off IPTV, perceived control was included as an antecedent variable affecting both motivations. In addition, perceived security was included as a factor directly affecting intention.

## 3.1.1. Theory of reasoned action

TRA assumes that a person’s performance of a specified behavior is determined by his or her behavioral intention to perform it, and that this, in turn, is jointly determined by the person’s attitudes and subjective norms. The best predictor of behavior is intention, which is the cognitive representation of a person’s readiness to perform a behavior and is considered to be its immediate antecedent.

Accordingly, attitude toward behavior was defined as an individual’s positive or negative feeling about performing the target behavior while subjective norm referred to a person’s perception that most people who were important to him or her think that he or she should or should not perform the behavior in question. In addition, a person’s attitude toward a behavior is determined by the salient beliefs and evaluations.

H1. Attitude is positively related to the intention to use IPTV.

## 3.1.2. Perceived usefulness

Perceived usefulness has generally been found to have a stronger influence than perceived ease of use [7]. In our study, perceived usefulness was the degree to which a person believed that a particular system would enhance his or her job performance. It also therefore meant that the technology could be used advantageously.

H2. Perceived usefulness is positively related to intention to use IPTV.

H3. Perceived usefulness is positively related to attitude toward IPTV.

## 3.1.3. Perceived playfulness

A number of studies have investigated the effect of perceived playfulness on computer usage. A research project analyzing the antecedents of perceived playfulness showed that speed, content, variety and focused attention were the most important factors. Lessons learned from human computer interaction, acceptance and usage surveys could be adapted to IPTV.

Venkatesh [17] conceptualized enjoyment as an antecedent of ease of use; its effect increased as users gained more experience with the system. Nysveen [11] included perceived enjoyment as an additional attribute of technology and determined the effect of perceived enjoyment on usage intention for four different mobile services [11]. Most recently, Shin found that perceived enjoyment strongly influenced the use of portable Internet for entertainment purposes. However, the specific effect of enjoyment on attitude has been overlooked in convergent technologies.

Since TV is an entertainment medium, IPTV can be seen as a hedonic system, because it offers entertaining content and information services. Therefore, we hypothesized that customers seek hedonic IPTV services to satisfy their amusement need.

H4. Perceived playfulness is positively related to intention to adopt IPTV.

H5. Perceived playfulness is positively related to attitude toward IPTV.

## 3.1.4. Perceived content quality

DeLone and McLean [3] argued that information quality was important in building successful IS. Lin and Lu [9] developed it as part of system quality and argued that its variables were useful predictors of user behavior. As systems became complex, perceived content quality seemed more important than perceived information quality. Beyah et al. [2] measured perceived content quality and other constructs in assessing Web-based systems. They found that perceived content quality was a significant determinant in their use. Shin applied perceived content quality to his model of mobile Internet service.

We used content quality as a possible factor in IPTV use and adoption. We hypothesized that content quality had a positive impact on attitude, because better content could make individuals feel that IPTV was more enjoyable and playful.

H6. Perceived content quality is positively related to perceived playfulness.

H7. Perceived content quality is positively related to attitude toward IPTV.

## 3.1.5. Perceived quality of service

System quality is particularly important in IPTV service, because it requires real-time data transmission. Thus, IPTV is sensitive to packet loss or delays causing picture break-up or loss.

Lin and Lu examined system quality, response time, and system accessibility as IS qualities. They argued that these three variables were predictors of perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness. IS quality can be comprehensively assessed through system and information quality. On the basis of these findings, the following hypotheses were made.

H8. Perceived quality of service is positively related to perceived usefulness.

H9. Perceived quality of service is positively related to attitude toward IPTV.

## 3.1.6. Perceived security

We explored the effect of users’ perceived security on intention to use. As IPTV can provide users with various security-sensitive functions, security concerns have become a major issue. IPTV content streams – like VoIP – are subject to spoofing, spamming, content theft, and other types of hacking. In contrast to the analog world, IPTV encryption takes place at the software level, and hacking into a system can merely require downloading malicious code. Thus perceived IPTV security vulnerabilities have become barriers to adoption. Given that IPTV will integrate e- and tcommerce eventually, the perceived security measures in ecommerce must be applied to IPTV.

H10. Perceived security is positively related to intention to use IPTV.

## 3.1.7. Perceived cost

We added perceived cost as a variable in developing the intention to use IPTV. In developing behavioral intention, customers compare the benefit of the service with its cost. The large and diverse array of IPTV services offer a number of unique benefits, but will a large number of consumers embrace the technology?

The cost of using IPTV includes the initial investment in devices plus subscription initiation charges and then monthly payments, etc. The behavioral intention of customers is thus influenced by their valuation of the service, which is related to the perceived cost [18]. Obviously, this is one of the critical factors in developing behavioral intention (Fig. 1).

H11. Perceived cost is negatively related to the intention to use IPTV.

## 3.1.8. Perceived control

As IPTV provides users with an unprecedented level of control over services, it seemed appropriate to add perceived control in our study. Personal control is an individual’s beliefs, at a given point in time, in his or her ability to effect a change. In the IPTV context, this may reflect the degree to which an individual believes that a situation is controllable and that users can enjoy any services and content at any time. Perceived control is thus related to a sense of readiness, availability, and accessibility: users’ perceive that they are emotionally connected with the world, its resources, and people via IPTV. With this perceived control, users may have increased self-efficacy in using IPTV [1]. We therefore proposed two hypotheses.

H12. Perceived control has a significant influence in perceived usefulness of IPTV.

H13. Perceived control has a significant influence in perceived playfulness of IPTV.

## 4. Method

Before conducting the main survey, we performed a pretest and a pilot study to validate the instrument. The pretest was undertaken to examine test–retest reliability and construct reliability. Thirty undergraduate students, majoring in IS, participated in the pretest during a 2-week interval. Cronbach’s a-test was employed to identify poor item-to-total correlation measure items. After eliminating the measure items that failed in either the test, the pretest, or the alpha test, the remaining measure items were tested for their Cronbach’s a. The scores ranged between ranged between 0.72 and 0.91, suggesting acceptable construct reliability.

The first round of the survey was conducted during July and May 2006. As a result, new variables were added and a revised questionnaire was created. A second round consisted of telephone interviews with residents during August and September 2007 using a local marking survey institute. Because the same company conducted the two survey rounds, data collection was expedited with the help of local telco, who was preparing to provide IPTV in the near future. In the survey, interviewers placed calls to residential households whose telephone numbers were generated from a computerized random-digit dialing procedure; non-responses were repeated up to five times. The telephone survey asked respondents whether they had had experience with IPTV-related services (Triple Services, WebTV, streaming content, Live TV, Video-on-Demand, Time Shift TV, TV gaming, interactive TV, digital video recording, or any kind of IPenabled convergence services), and the survey continued only with those who had had experience with at least one. The interviewers asked the respondents if they would adopt IPTV, with step-wise factors.

The final sample consisted of 320 respondents. Characteristics of sample data reflected the general features of early adopters. The ratio of males to females was 53.3% to 46.7%. Age groups consisted of teens (20.6%), 20s (34.9%), 30s (14.7%), and over 40s (9.8%); occupational groups consisted of students (30.1%), white-collar workers (28.5%), blue-collar workers (9.1%), self-employed workers (11.9%), and others (20.4%).

LISREL 8.54 was used for factor analysis, reliability analysis, and the structural equation modeling.

## 4.1. Measurement

The questionnaires were developed by selecting items and questions from the literature; the list of items is shown in

Appendix A. The questions were carefully restated to reflect the characteristics of IPTV. A pilot test for measures was then conducted and the wording of items was reviewed and modified, based on the outcome.

Participants in the test were asked to signify their agreement with a set of statements using a 7-point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Measures of behavioral intention to use were adapted from previous studies.

To address enjoyment, we used a 4-item scale developed by Nysveen et al. The items were entertainment, relaxation, excitement, and fun-seeking gratification. Perceived usefulness was measured with six items from Davis; work, measuring the extent to which a person believed that IPTV was capable of being used advantageously and providing positive expected outcomes. Perceived content quality and perceived quality of service were measured with the items used by Lin and Lu for mobile Internet service. The measures of items of perceived cost were adapted from Liao and Cheung [8]. To address the elements of perceived security, we used items developed by Yenisey et al. [20] and the measure used by Salibury et al. [13]. The measures used to assess perceived control were taken from the self-efficacy of Schwarzer [14].

## 5. Data analysis

## 5.1. Discriminant and convergent validity

Discriminant validity was assessed to ensure that constructs differed from each other. As per Fornell and Larcker [5], the correlations between items in any two constructs should be lower than the square root of the average variance shared by items within a construct. The square root of the variance shared between a construct and its items was greater than the correlations between the construct and any other construct in the model, satisfying this criteria. The results therefore confirmed that our instrument encompassed satisfactory construct validity (Table 1).

Fornell and Larcker also suggested that the average variance extracted from a construct should exceed 0.5. As shown in Table 2, all constructs exceed this criterion. Thus, the proposed constructs demonstrated convergent validity on all three metrics. A further test of convergent validity was conducted using PLS, following Gefen and Straub [6]. According to this, convergent validity is satisfied when the t-values of the outer PLS model loadings are above 1.96. This criterion was met for all construct items in our model.

## 7. Findings

Motivated by a need to understand the underlying drivers of customer adoption of IPTV, our research incorporated a motivational perspective, and postulated a new integrated model as the key factors affecting customer adoption of IPTV. For this new model, new constructs were employed, and additional motivations were introduced: intrinsic motivations, including perceived content quality and perceived playfulness; and extrinsic motivations, including perceived usefulness and perceived quality of service. Perceived control was included as an antecedent variable to the intrinsic and extrinsic motivations.

The measurement model was strongly confirmed with adequate convergent and discriminant validity with respect to the measurement of all the measurement of all the variables in our model. Overall, the results showed that the model demonstrated good predictive power and explained behavioral intentions to use IPTV. The structural model showed a good fit of the data, and most path coefficients in the model were found to be statistically significant.

The results regarding intention also highlighted implications for IPTV providers with regard to the development of new services over IPTV to increase users’ intentions to use the services. Perceived content and system quality were influential in predicting the intention to use IPTV. Perceived playfulness can be viewed as a specific utility/aspect of content, based on what is received and what is given; and perceived usefulness can be seen as an aspect of the specific conduit transmitting such content, based on our findings that perceived playfulness and perceived usefulness were mediated by perceived content and system quality.

The challenging question is: what will the killer application be for the next-generation Internet? This question is being aggressively pursued by a number of industries attempting to integrate the content and conduit of convergence technologies. Certainly, IPTV providers should put significant resources into developing and delivering high-quality content, such as timely, up-to-date, and personalized services. In addition, they should offer a stable system by which users can enjoy services via a new platform.

It can be seen that IPTV is a tool for both usefulness and enjoyment to users. IPTV-based technologies allow real-time distribution of digital content with lower costs; thus, IPTV is useful, and IPTV allows users to enjoy various hedonic applications such as online-games and podcasting. This implies that IPTV becomes an integrative platform for work and entertainment. This implies an IPTV business question: what services should be developed and how they are delivered to customers? IPTV providers may have to accommodate both work- and entertainment-related services.

In addition, IPTV service will be significantly dependent on the system security. This, along with perceived quality in terms of system and content, sheds light on important practical implications for the industry. Dedicated IPTV networks may have to be built so that end-to-end quality of service can be guaranteed. As IPTV provides multiple services, consumers are adding major use of Internet services in their daily lives. Providers must be offer sufficiently sophisticated security (authentication and encryption, etc.) to ensure that user privacy is not breached.

## 8. Conclusion

The expectations toward the IPTV business rose mainly because of the gradual removal of technical barriers. Today, IPTV is considered a huge business opportunity; but there are diverging forecasts. On one side, IPTV will succeed in imposing itself as a leading technology in the multimedia market. On the other side, IPTV carries many big challenges for carriers in terms of technology, market, and customers.

We investigated the issues from a user perspective and derived industrial implications of the IPTV determinants and found that customers expected IPTV to be a mechanism for enjoying content and innovative services while offering a stable connection for the services. Also IPTV is compelling because of its interactivity and convenience: it can completely alter the way that information, communications, and media services are consumed at home, moving the TV experience from a passive to active, two-way communications experience, where every user can be unique. A subscriber may choose to receive only some channels and pay only for the content that he or she consumes. Users can also use services such as instant messaging a friend about a favorite show while both are watching it at their separate homes.

## 9. Limitations

Although our study provided insights into the factors affecting IPTV intention, the results should be treated with caution. First, the findings reflected only limited aspects of user experience of IPTV, which is still in its early stages of evolution. A second limitation concerns external validity. All the survey respondents lived in a specific region and the sample was drawn from a single domestic market, which limits the generalization of our findings. Finally, because IPTV is not yet a mainstream phenomenon, responses from customers were based on expected experience on future IPbased services. Thus, the findings may raise questions such as (1) how seriously did respondent think about the prospective IPTV services? and (2) to what extent are the samples representative of the actual population engaging in IP-based services? Therefore, although our scales could be useful, they have not been shown to be generalizable.
