## 1 INTRODUCTION

The trend of Internet of Things (IoT) has swept the world, and its related applications have led to the development of new business models, boosted innovative activities, and driven the transformation of industries across the world (French & Shim, 2016; Joseph et al., 2017; Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017). Gartner, an international research and advisory organization, reported that the number of things networked globally reached 6.4 billion in 2016, representing an increase of 30 percent from that in 2015. By 2020, this number, as discussed above, is predicted to grow to 20.8 billion. In terms of economic efficiency, the service expenditure driven by the IoT in 2016 was USD 235 billion, representing an increase of 22 percent compared with that in 2015 (Gartner, 2015). In the future, enterprises will transmit massive business information through the Internet, wireless networks, and cloud services in an IoT environment. Enterprise information systems will also generate and collect business transaction data by operating an intelligent device to enhance the processing, analysis, and application of big data (Barnaghi et al., 2013; Perera et al., 2014).

The rapid growth of the number of networked things in an IoT environment will bring considerable new business opportunities, but it will also generate many challenges (Almeida et al., 2015; French & Shim, 2016; Ouaddah et al., 2017). The leading cause of this development arises when innovative technology is widely adopted, and new risk management problems will emerge. These include the misuse of unauthorized business transactions, the failure to store transaction records, hacker attacks on IoT device/information systems, leakage of customer information, and other issues that will pose growing threats to modern enterprises (Boyd & Crawford, 2012; Madakam et al., 2015). Simon Segars, CEO of ARM Holdings, reported that, when companies embrace the IoT, they simultaneously encounter some unprecedented information security challenges (ARM, 2017). For instance, in 2016, hackers used the Mirai/DDoS malware (e.g., BASHLITE, Linux. Darlloz, Remaiten, and Linux.Wifatch) to attack unprotected IoT devices. Specifically, these attacks generally cause the improper operation of enterprise information systems and website failure (TechCrunch, 2016). Therefore, IoT devices and related facilities connected in the enterprise information systems must be continuously managed and monitored in response to potential threats.

In a study on 123 multinational corporations in the United States, Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) has been emphasized as a new paradigm of management (Beasley et al., 2005). Beasley et al. (2005) deduced that the development of ERM aims to increase the ability of the board of directors and senior management to monitor the portfolio of risks faced by companies. Sicari et al. (2015) have inferred that the IoT is characterized by its various technologies, which are applied frequently in multiple industries and business service activities. Therefore, measures such as access control, authentication, and confidentiality are all essential issues to consider while incorporating IoT risk management into a business strategy. Furthermore, in reviewing the IoT business model frameworks, Chan (2015) asserted that traditional business models are inadequate for competitors’ collaboration and cross-industry alliances in the IoT ecosystem. However, business operators must tackle the changing situation of organizations and establish a sound risk management checklist and, thus, a reliable internal control framework, policy, and mechanism to achieve the purpose of corporate IT governance. The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA) highlighted the crucial role of enterprise management in identifying and addressing potential risks at all levels of an organization. Management must implement necessary actions and establish a set of appropriate evaluation and audit systems. The acknowledgment of the potential effects of risk and their possible occurrence is needed (IIA, 2013; Nuijten et al., 2018) to reduce the risk incidents within the IoT context.

Enterprises may have existing frameworks and standards for internal control such as the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations (COSO), the Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies (COBIT), and the principles and guidelines of risk management included in ISO 31000. However, the potential new threats and risks in the IoT environment are very different from the past decade. If enterprises continue to carry out internal control by using their existing audit mechanisms or information security architectures, they will have a high likelihood of failing to remedy these new threats (Weber, 2010; Gubbi et al., 2013). In an increasingly complex IoT environment, through the implementation of appropriate risk management and internal control mechanisms, companies may more easily cope with changes and challenges using the emerging technologies to reduce operational risks, thereby enhancing their physical capabilities and increasing their competitiveness (Debreceny & Gray, 2013; Ives et al., 2016; French & Shim, 2016).

In practice, IoT governance in an ICT workplace has been recognized as a critical challenge to the risk management of the enterprises. Despite this issue, the risk factors of the enterprise internal control in an IoT environment and the relevant issues have not been investigated thoroughly. As a result, this study aims to strengthen IT governance research through an in-depth investigation of risk factors in an IoT environment according to practical and academic needs. Specifically, this work has two main objectives: (1) to identify the critical risk factors that influence the governance of enterprise internal control in an IoT environment and (2) to classify the risk factors and study their importance in an IoT environment. Discovering the critical risk factors and establishing a clear and valid risk management checklist should be the first tasks of enterprise internal control in performing IT governance in an IoT environment. This research provides a theoretical background by reviewing prior studies on IT governance in an IoT environment and the three-layer DCM (i.e., Device, Connection, and Management) architecture of the IoT and then investigating the framework of enterprise internal control and risk classification. The research method and design, data analysis, and discussion of the results is also presented. Finally, the implications for academia and practice management, as well as research limitations, have also been illustrated for IoT governance.

## 2 LITERATURE REVIEW

## 2.1 IT Governance in an IoT Environment

In recent years, the number of emerging technologies and their related applications has been rapidly rising in the context of the IoT. The techniques of the IoT have broadly been applied throughout the global service market. These technologies aim to deliver realtime messages via a common platform through wired networks, wireless networks (i.e., Bluetooth, RFID, and infrared detectors), GPS, or cloud computing methods, with the objective of understanding and providing warnings, communicating, and/or monitoring business operations (Babar et al., 2010). For instance, the application of a global supply chain network provides highly diverse services and involves extremely complicated types of IT, people, and associated business processes. For this reason, new problems related to IoT network security and privacy, risk management, and internal control that threaten the governance of enterprises occur continuously (Weber, 2010; Chen et al., 2014; French & Shim, 2016).

The purpose of IT governance is to verify whether an enterprise adopts an effective mechanism for IT applications to fulfill the mission of an organization and ensure that the organization’s strategic objectives have been achieved. Broadbent and Weill (1997) argued that IT governance concerns those entities entitled to make major decisions, those with input, and those accountable for implementing such decisions. It is to maintain the consistency between IT and business objectives, promote business development, maximize operational revenue, ensure the rational use of IT resources, and properly manage the IT-related risks (Xue et al., 2008; Debreceny & Gray, 2013). In general, IT governance focuses mainly on the role of information technology in the organization. The design of decision rights, organizational structures, IT functions, and other support and operational units are deemed as the essential elements of IT governance, reflecting the reality for IT governance success. To this end, considering how to acquire the maximum benefit from IT and performing the functional control of risk management may enhance the long-term success of an enterprise (Debreceny, 2013; Merhout & O'Toole, 2015). The IoT environment is an Internet-connected network, and it provides the relevant stakeholders to communicate or exchange information among IoT devices using the wired or wireless network. The people, things, IoT facilities, and frameworks involved are complicated and diverse. The governance issues continue to emerge as a critical issue and require a timely resolution.

For analyzing the problems in the IoT environment and recognizing current research trends, this study reviews articles from academic and professional journals in various fields on emerging IoT subjects, and further synthesizes these articles into several areas to provide the needed directions for the research development. The integrated areas are the issues of strategy and governance, security and privacy, modeling and architecture, IoT innovation, theoretical foundation, value co-creation, and risk management and internal control of the IoT. IoT strategy and governance. The critical role of implementing governance in the IoT is to consider the completeness of control mechanisms, such as establishing the principles of privacy, security, ethics, and competition to enforce the rights of citizens and consumers and protect their data. Therefore, how to adequately address the issue of IoT strategy and governance has become a significant challenge in the IoT environment (Roman et al., 2010; Sicari et al., 2015; Weber, 2010). According to the reviewed literature in this regard, Gubbi et al. (2013) targeted their study in the vision, architecture, and directions of the IoT. This aforementioned research presented a cloud-centric idea for worldwide implementation. Additionally, the development of the IoT business models and analysis on how to enhance customer services are increasing (Chan, 2015; Ives et al., 2016). The risk and impacts that emerge with the adoption of IoT and the embedded future of supply chains have been continuously studied (Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017). With so many potential risks associated with ubiquitous IoT facilities, how to improve the organization's ability, by establishing governance regulations, has also been an essential topic in the subject area of strategy and governance (Almeida et al., 2015; Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017; Trequattrini et al., 2016).

Security and privacy. Security and privacy have been the premier theme for conducting computer security research. To understand the research situation in this area, several papers in the IoT area have been reviewed to obtain the preliminary findings. Roman et al. (2013) provided an explicit analysis of the security challenges of different IoT strategies. They include identity and authentication, access control and protocol, network security, privacy, trust management, governance, and fault tolerance. Moreover, their study concluded that the decentralized and heterogeneous nature of the distributed approach increases the complexity of most security mechanisms. Sicari et al. (2015) corroborated that the IoT is characterized by its different technologies that provide innovative services in various application domains. Flexible infrastructure is needed to manage security and privacy risks in such a dynamic environment. The nature of privacy and security problems are frequently associated with IoT implementation. Therefore, performing the rigorous security validity checks, authentication procedures, and data verification will be part of the critical foundation in these relevant applications (Almeida et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2014; Weber, 2010).

Modeling and architecture. The deployment of the IoT has disrupted information security management in the financial, manufacturing, technology, agriculture, and other industry organizations. These organizations are struggling to transform from providing traditional products to delivering smart products and related outcomes. Scholars (Chan, 2015; Chen et al., 2014; Hsu & Lin, 2016) pointed out that the biggest challenge in realizing the IoT is not in terms of technology but instead of business value and services. For example, the issues of adopting instant information filtering techniques, analyzing various data sources, and providing instant services have commonly been the main concerns for the enterprise to bring out the value of the IoT and secure competitive advantages (Madakam et al., 2015; Perera et al., 2014). Gubbi et al. (2013) presented a cloud-centric vision and stimulated the motivation for worldwide IoT implementation. The roadmap of relevant critical technological developments and applications could provide an indispensable guide to the success of IoT technology. A new information framework for creating a smart city through the IoT was also discussed by Jin et al. (2014). Their research illustrates an original method with a cloud-based architecture for existing operations that can be adapted to the enhancement and delivery of essential city services. Chan (2015) examined IoT business models and proposed a framework that integrates the IoT strategy category, tactics, and value chain elements. Using case studies in Hong Kong and Mainland China, this study mentioned above summarized a four-layer architecture to provide a direct target advertising platform for merchants. In line with the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), and a critical review of related articles, Al-Momani et al. (2016) developed a conceptual model of the IoT services adoption. This model provides important references for model building and a theoretical foundation to investigate the relationships among security, privacy, and behavioral intention. The research on the business models and architectures of the IoT are also prominent topics that have been heavily discussed over the last several years.

IoT innovation. In response to the global market demand and development trend, IoT innovation has become a new alternative for enterprises to deploy actively. For example, the concepts of smart city and smart transportation, smart healthcare, smart retail, and smart home can all bring potential business opportunities for enterprises and are worthy of investing in research and innovation (French & Shim, 2016; Joseph et al., 2017; Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017). French and Shim (2016) discussed the digital revolution of the IoT and examined the fifth-generation (5G) network and its impacts on big data, particularly, the adoption of smart technology. Joseph et al. (2017) targeted insights from social media Twitter analytics to explore significant themes like business concerns. Their findings show that the scope of applications, security, emerging smart technologies, and manufacturing are the growing innovations in IoT. Further, Majeed and Rupasinghe (2017) studied global supply chains for Industry 4.0. Through an investigation on an enterprise resource planning system and the use of RFID technology and business application programming interface (BAPI) technology in SAP®, they provided several constructive suggestions for the improvement and innovation of the inbound and outbound operations. More studies in this subject area also provide valuable context information that can be used as a reference for generating innovative ideas in the adoption and application of IoT (Chen et al., 2014; Madakam et al., 2015; Trequattrini et al., 2016).

Theoretical foundation. In general, the theoretical basis of the IoT can be divided into technical and application aspects. In other words, the IoT theory can be investigated from the phase of "machine-to-machine" information exchange and that of the "machineto-man" applications service. In terms of technology, several studies are focusing on sensing devices technology, network standards, and communication architecture (Chen et al., 2014; Madakam et al., 2015; Jin et al., 2014). However, researchers noted that since common standards are lacking in the world and the IoT technologies vary from vendor to vendor, there is a strong need to continuously develop and establish relevant communication standards and protocols for better global governance. In terms of applications, there are also some studies focusing on business applications and services in practice (Al-Momani et al., 2016; Hsu & Lin, 2016; Joseph et al., 2017). Through a study of modeling the adoption of IoT services, Al-Momani et al. (2016) reviewed critical literature based on TAM and the UTAUT. They proposed a conceptual framework for investigating the behavioral intention to use IoT. Hsu and Lin (2016) proposed a theoretical model and empirically evaluated it using survey data collected from 508 users to explore the perceptions of IoT service. Their research finding indicates that the influences of network characteristics (i.e., perceived critical mass, compatibility, and complementarity) play a significant role in affecting consumers' perception of usage benefits and adoption. Furthermore, the same study uncovered that behavioral research on IoT services seems to be receiving much attention in recent years (i.e., the exploration of eliminating privacy risks, provider liability, and privacy-preserving mechanisms).

Value co-creation. Researchers have become more interested in learning about IoT, big data, and cloud computing because they realize that the co-creation value of related applications will bring a great benefit to enterprises (French & Shim, 2016; Jin et al., 2016; Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017). For example, enhancing the customer service through the IoT devices, platforms, and data streams analysis and continuing to invest research energy in the IoT profitable business model are important issues in this regard (Chan, 2015; Ives et al., 2016; Perera et al., 2014). However, to better understand consumer’s behavior, intention of continued use, and views on related applications, several studies on network externalities have been undertaken, and a significant effect concerning the application of information privacy has also been taken into consideration (Hsu & Lin, 2016; Joseph et al., 2017; Roman et al., 2013).

Risk management and internal control. In recent years, the IoT interconnect devices have been rapidly deployed in organizations around the world. In the absence of adequate security controls and measures, the applied network will become a springboard for hacking. Therefore, studying the issues of risk management, internal control, and auditing mechanisms in the IoT environment is essential to improve and enhance business operations. Weber (2010) argued that IoT technology has a significant impact on the security and privacy of the involved stakeholders. Furthermore, some problems such as legal challenges, facts about RFID use scenarios, security concerns, and privacy issues cannot be ignored. To this end, problems mentioned above must be addressed in the context of IoT internal control (Sicari et al., 2015). Moreover, to understand the resistance influences that affect the success of the IoT innovation, Trequattrini et al. (2016) investigated the main types of sector-based resistance in the football industry. Their study suggested that the risks of general resistance and widespread skepticism delay the process of adopting IoT instruments, and consequently, it affects the successful implementation of IoT innovation.

This study confirms that the existing literature is divergent, and the IS research investigating the IoT risk management for enterprise internal control and governance is somewhat limited. Scholars (Jin et al., 2014; Almeida et al., 2015) have argued that the existence of an IT risk in introducing or applying emerging technology to enterprises poses a potential threat and causes damage to valuable assets. Frameworks about the different levels of ICT architecture or various types of corporate operational risks should be adjusted and developed to ensure the effective and efficient use of IT in an IoT environment. IT governance must be followed and thoroughly performed in a specific operating application and environment, including artificial intelligence, financial technology, and IoT emerging technologies. The identification of the risk factors of the IoT and the classification of its attributes and importance for enterprise internal control are keys to performing effective risk management under IT governance (Spira & Page, 2003; Beasley et al., 2005; Krishnan, 2005; Babar et al., 2010; Almeida et al., 2015).

## 2.2 Three-layer DCM Architecture of the IoT

The IoT has accelerated the rapid development of industries across the world. The application of related devices and technologies not only changed business processes and their innovation model but also amplified the obtained benefits and improved services on business operations, for example, intelligent scheduling control and material inventory activities performed in the manufacturing sector; and healthcare services, medical institutions interacting with residents, patients, medical staff, and various medical equipment rendered in the healthcare industry. Consequently, risk management issues in the IoT environment have become a new subject to investigate enterprise IT governance. However, in considering the security of an emerging IoT environment, past risk management frameworks or internal control specifications have been unable to meet the IT application service needed by IoT and have difficulty in helping enterprises to implement sound monitoring mechanisms. Several studies verified that technical standards should focus on conventional vertical integration management and integrate all sensors, networks, and applications in the IoT network (Zhou, 2013; Madakam et al., 2015). Furthermore, in response to the gradually expanding IoT applications, services, and facilities, placing a focus on changes in risk management and developing a sound audit control framework is necessary.

The IoT links different networks and various sensors, actuators, and computing systems to deliver messages or provide intelligence services within the society (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2013). Several standard-setting organizations and industrial organizations have been actively developing different architectures to support communication networks and IoT applications. For example, Global ICT Standardization Forum for India [GISFI], the China Communications Standards Association [CCSA], the European Telecommunications Standards Institute [ETSI], and IEEE P2413. The IoT reference architecture of GISFI is an ICT standard that evolved in India and is used to meet regional requirements. The four-layer framework (i.e., comprising a device, gateway, service platform, and application) provides an ICT bridge to the globalization of Indian achievements (GISFI, 2017). The CCSA is a standard, professional organization in China, whose technology framework covers the broadband popularization, speed enhancement, power supply, emergency, and M2M communications (Chen et al., 2014). This proposed framework provides an open and general IoT architecture, including an application layer, network and service layers, and a sensing layer. The architecture mentioned above integrated solutions obtained from an interoperability working group on sensor networks, an electric tag standards technical committee, and others. To this end, the multilevel application and development of the IoT urged the ETSI to propose an architecture for IoTbased applications to meet the challenges in the service management field (Gubbi et al., 2013; Zhou, 2013).

Recently, IEEE P2413 has been promoting cross-domain interaction to aid system interoperability and functional compatibility and further facilitate the growth of the IoT market (IEEE, 2015). The IEEE P2413 framework differs from other structures that have confined to specific applications. The primary goal of the IEEE P2413 working group is to boost the growth of the IoT market by improving system compatibility, interoperability, and functional exchangeability to facilitate cross-domain interaction and platform unification. The organization further provides some other purposes including (1) to identify a framework for covering the requirements of different IoT applications; (2) to render a more transparent system structure for conducting the system’s benchmarking, safety, and security assessment; (3) to lower the industry fragmentation and create numerous multi-stakeholder activities worldwide; and (4) to leverage the existing body of work.

The IEEE P2413 has the advantage of adopting a unified approach that facilitates the development of IoT systems to reduce the industry fragmentation and create a critical mass of multi-stakeholder activities worldwide (IEEE, 2017). Furthermore, the IEEE P2413 is currently considered a three-tiered architecture that comprises the device, connection, and management layers. It is the critical framework to cover the architectural needs of various IoT application domains such as healthcare, media, logistics, mobility/transportation, manufacturing, energy, retail, and home and building (IEEE, 2015). Thus, the IEEE P2413 has been adopted in this research as an essential basis for framing and synthesizing the critical risk factors under IoT governance. The first tier, the device layer or the sensing layer, is an architecture designed to identify objects and gather information. It is used to control technology and the application of local and ad hoc sensor networks, embedded middleware, sensors, and actuators. For instance, it comprises sensors (i.e., RFID, reader-writer, and RFID volume label), smart objects, intelligent terminals (i.e., tablets, computers, and smartphones), data storage volume labels, sonar, radio, receiver, label reader, and ubiquitous terminal devices and facilities. The second tier, the connection layer or the network layer, is an architecture of wired and wireless networks. This architecture supports machine-types communication, edge middleware, and pervasive networks. Specifically, this layer covers private networks, the Internet, network management systems, mobile communications, satellite signals, and cloud computing platforms, among others. These networks discussed earlier are mainly designed to process and send information from the device layer. The third tier, the management layer or the application layer, is mostly responsible for the vertical application and server-side middleware data management. Furthermore, this layer supervises a set of applications or smart solutions designed to meet user needs in the IoT. This application layer has been commonly applied to logistics monitoring, pollution monitoring, telemedicine, smart grids, data-driven farming, and smart transportation, among others.

In the IoT context, many enterprises have adopted the ubiquitous communication equipment, network systems, digital media, and other emerging technologies in recent years to establish their new business model. Most of them are operating under an IoT environment with a highly developed informatization, including various network systems, communication equipment, and digital mediums, to establish a business and data communication network. For this reason, the added values of the three-tiered DCM architecture are that it effectively reduces the communication overheads (Sethi & Sarangi, 2017) and functions as an essential architecture to quickly strengthen the risk management and internal control of enterprises (Zhou, 2013).

## 2.3 Framework for Enterprise Internal Control and Risk Classification

The framework of enterprise internal control in an IoT environment is a management architecture and interactive process that usually involves various stakeholders (i.e., senior managers, IT managers, and auditors). Such a framework aims to control a series of risk activities in enterprise operation and project plans implemented. Through the cooperation of the board of directors, management levels, and all employees, the reliable, reasonable, and complete policies, laws, and rules have been established, thereby enabling an enterprise to achieve its set targets (Pathak, 2005; Feng et al., 2009). Drawing from studies on ERM, Scholars (Beasley et al., 2005; Sutton et al., 2008) asserted that an excellent internal control should consider the various risks that an organization faces in different departments or operational stages and provide an effective management mechanism to prevent the occurrence of risks in all cases. Therefore, to identify critical risk factors and types, the researchers of this present study reviewed the manuals, standards, and literature in the fields of IT governance regarding internal control and risk management.

In 1992, the US COSO issued an internal control and integration framework, and in 1994, several contents were supplemented to address the problems of internal control and ERM. The original framework can be divided into five control dimensions: (1) control environment, (2) risk assessments, (3) control activities, (4) information and communication, and (5) monitoring activities. To fulfill the demand of the enterprises for modernization, the COSO released a new version of the Internal Control-Integrated Framework in May 2013, which replaced the old framework used for over 20 years. Recently, based on five COSO elements, the ERM framework for corporate risk management included eight constructs: internal environment, objective setting, event identification, risk assessment, risk response, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring (COSO, 2017a; COSO, 2017b). Since its release, the COSO ERM framework has been gradually approved by the formulation authorities and has become the source of international general internal control guidelines (Azzali & Mazza, 2013; Martin et al., 2014).

However, COBIT is a business framework that provides significant advantages to organizations that recognize the need for internal control over their information and system management. Since 1996, COBIT has been periodically updated to meet the current needs and remain relevant for the efficient governance and management of IT enterprises. COBIT 5 is the fifth version of an IT framework used to control the management and governance of the IT released by the Information Systems Audit and Control Association (ISACA) in 2012. COBIT 5 empowers organizations to improve their performance by optimizing their information management technology. Moreover, COBIT 5 is a leading and comprehensive framework that provides a generally accepted set of control objectives to business managers, auditors, and IT professionals in all types of industries. The globally recognized COBIT 5 Framework, which helps ensure effective enterprise governance of information and technology (EGIT), has been recently updated to COBIT 2019 with new information and guidance—facilitating easier, tailored implementation. Under the guidance of its globally recognized practices, analytical tools, and models, enterprises can effectively address their business issues and perform IT asset governance (De Haes et al., 2013; ISACA, 2015; Astuti et al., 2017).

Although both COSO and COBIT provide substantial guidance for ERM, internal control, and fraud auditing, these two frameworks still have several drawbacks. For instance, the COSO focuses too much on financial reports and disregards corporate risk from the overall and strategic level, thereby failing to consider different industries and management approaches and provide more detailed guidance on specific operations at various development stages (Rochette, 2009; Daud et al., 2010). The International Risk Management Institute (IRMI, 2017) highlighted that the matrices of the COSO ERM are challenging to conduct the reviews properly, may create an unstable quality, and lack an adequate list of controls (IRMI, 2017). Some scholars have argued that COBIT has several sustained limitations. For example, “the lack of emphasis on the organization’s attitude toward sustainability”; “IT policies surrounding sourcing, use, and disposal of IT assets fail to account for sustainability”; and “the lack of emphasis on the application of sustainable IT policies in handling an organization’s daily operations” (Moeller et al., 2013; Merhout & O’Toole, 2015, pp. 2-3). Specifically, the rapidly changing technology and innovative digital business model often always lead to many potential risks and information security problems, and this is because the emerging technology is outgrowing

the existing business ecosystems.

In this situation, IT assets have become one of the most valuable assets of enterprises. When these assets are affected by hacking, denial of service (DoS), data leakage, cybercrime, and other threats that occur from the Internet, the problem of risk management and information security has been considered an important matter. It will seriously affect the operational performance of an organization (Brender & Markov, 2013; Saleh & Alfantookh, 2011; Soomro et al., 2016). According to the earlier arguments, if the company failed to implement IT governance, it may be due to its lack of the identification of risk factors at a different level, and it is not having an appropriate internal control framework to monitor and manage potential risks (Almeida et al., 2015; Brender & Markov, 2013; Martin et al., 2014). For this reason, organizations must demand a proper internal control framework to deal with various risks in today’s IoT business environment.

Risk factors can usually be discussed in two aspects, including general enterprise and IT risks. As for general enterprise risks, Miller (1992) developed a framework for integrated risk management in international business. This framework includes four specific risk types, which are general environmental uncertainties, industry uncertainties, operating uncertainties, and the risk of organizational responses to changes. This abovementioned study explicitly considered the guidelines related to alternative business strategies and provided an important basis for a comprehensive assessment of uncertainty risks. On the contrary, Kaplan and Mikes (2012) suggested a new risk framework that allows executives to manage risks through an alternative rule-based approach for controlling non-preventable risks. The structure includes three broad types of hazards, and they are preventable risks, strategy risks, and external risks. Scholars suggested that each category of risk requires different management strategies and methods to enhance control. Furthermore, in research into the perceptions of e-business risk by UK small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), Grant et al. (2014) categorized the risk into eight classifications such as strategic risks; reputation and customer expectations risks; branding risks; security threats (i.e., external and internal security threats); legal and tax risks; leadership, cultural, and staffing risks; outsourcing and dependency risks; and technology risks. Their work provides a careful consideration of e-business risk control for enterprises. In short, for the control of general enterprise risk, broadly identifying all aspects of risk factors can help enterprises reduce potential risks and improve performance, which is more conducive to the enterprise's sustainable development (Aloini et al., 2007; Bromiley & Rau, 2016; The Economist, 2008, 2007).

As for IT risks, in the new economic environment, enterprises are facing a significant challenge of electronic transformation. Promoting IT risk management has become a major issue in achieving corporate goals. Thus, in response to changes in the industrial environment, companies are more aware of the technological risks and hence make appropriate responses to ensure the achievement of electronic transformation projects. Many leading journals and studies have invested in exploring this topic. For example, Alhawari et al. (2012) investigated the knowledge-based risk management framework for information technology projects. Brender and Markov (2013) studied on risk perception and risk management in cloud computing. Wang et al. (2015) probed into the issues on insider threats of attack-proneness of information systems applications, and the issue regarding practical access control for sensor networks in the context of the IoT (Li et al., 2016). Notably, these studies help us to understand the IT risks in various situations. Furthermore, many internationally influential publications and standards are also raising relevant discussions on this concern. For example, British Standards Institution (BSI) presented its standards for industries—BS ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management and ISO 31000 for risk management (ISO 31000, 2009; ISO/IEC 27001, 2013). Several articles exploring various ways to better manage the major risks in Harvard Business Review (Bromiley & Rau, 2016; Davenport, 1998; Disparte, 2016; Kaplan & Mikes, 2012); and some other studies published in the Business Horizons addressed the issue of the challenges and solutions for implementing the enterprise risk management (Fraser & Simkins, 2016; Hess & Cottrell Jr, 2016; Lee & Shin, 2018).

The characteristics of an IoT environment are generally the presence of multiple agents connecting various users, services, and devices among different networks. Roman et al. (2013) noted that security management in the IoT environment is still in its infancy. In the future, it is still necessary to invest much work and effort into analyzing the existing challenges and developing possible protection mechanisms; especially in the fields of risk management, information security, privacy, and trust in IoT (Sicari et al., 2015; Trequattrini et al., 2016). Moreover, general risks and IT risks are inseparable in modern enterprise operations. ISACA links IT risks with business-oriented implications. Scholars (Zhang et al., 2014; Al-Ayyoub et al., 2015; Giannakis & Louis, 2016) have shown that a multi-agent-based infrastructure is vital in realizing the flexible cooperation and service composition among IoT devices, and it is fundamental in performing the relevant request tasks in an IoT environment. Being an innovation of business strategy (Joseph et al., 2017), the adoption of emerging technologies (French & Shim, 2016) and the value cocreation (Majeed & Rupasinghe, 2017) must be built on an architecture that proves both safe and stable. To this end, the extensive enterprise risks and the concept of three-layer DCM architecture can be considered for the framework developed to ensure the dynamic monitoring in an IoT environment (IEEE, 2015, pp. 10-11). IoT risk management, in particular, is often directed at the internal control focusing on special events, including the probability of occurrence of the risk, the scale, the determination of the response mechanism, and the method of developing the monitoring process. Based on the abovementioned relevant literature on risk management, information security, and the context of IoT, this study initially identifies 84 risk factors and divides them into eight risk categories including environment, process, decision-making, operation, authorization, data processing and information technology, moral, and finance (please see Appendix A for details). These eight types of risk classifications can be regarded as the key aspects and elements of observing potential risks when the internal control measures have been taken into account.

## 3 RESEARCH METHOD AND DESIGN

This study adopts a qualitative research approach to explore critical risk factors in the IoT environment since qualitative research is a commonly used research approach in the fields of social science and education. This research method has many advantages including exploration through nature; inductive analysis; comprehensive review; qualitative data utilization; individual contact and in-depth understanding; typical and particular cases used as research orientation; and flexible research design (Cooper & Schindler, 2001; Roche et al., 2014; Seawright & Gerring, 2008). Compared with quantitative research, qualitative research provides more in-depth, targeted, and complementary observations through the analysis of small samples. Hence, qualitative research can not only provide a comprehensive view of the research topic and related comments but also promote the generation of information or knowledge for further case research.

However, the research method also has certain disadvantages that may occur, such as when the provided information and plan for studying are insufficient or when researchers fail to put forward sufficient evidence data. The research will be viewed as too subjective and hence reducing the reliability and validity of the results. Therefore, proposing effective research strategies is notable for improving the quality of qualitative research (Seawright & Gerring, 2008). In carefully considering the characteristics of the qualitative research approach, this study adopts Gowin’s Vee knowledge map (see Figure 2) as the foundation for research and development.

The above-mentioned knowledge map is an active learning strategy and knowledge management tool, the core concept of which is to establish a knowledge system based on the problem (Gowin, 1981; Novak & Gowin, 1984; Novak, 1990). The application of this framework in the field of academic and qualitative studies has attracted increasing attention because it acts as an essential tool in developing knowledge management and promoting knowledge exchange (Gowin, 1981; Novak & Gowin, 1984; Novak, 1998, 2002). Based on the theoretical foundation of Gowin’s Vee knowledge map, the strategy of this research can be divided into two major aspects, and they are theoretical and methodological. In the theoretical aspect, this study reviews the important literature to better understand the general concepts, principles, rules, and theories of IT governance in the IoT environment (i.e., the IT governance in an IoT environment, three-layer DCM architecture of the IoT, and framework of enterprise internal control and risk classification). Thus, in this stage, the research concepts and the reviewed literature can be used as the prototype development for the classification of IoT critical risk factors. In the methodological aspect, it usually uses for data gathering, transformations, interpretation, and value claims according to the needs and applications of the research context. The purpose of this aspect is to expand and deepen the knowledge formed by the research. Accordingly, the Delphi method is applied to revise and confirm the classification of IoT critical risk factors.

The Delphi method is an expert methodology and has commonly been used in the collection of specialist knowledge. This method sends the questions individually to seek solutions from experts by communicating with and consulting them, given the intuitive judgment of expert experience (Rudner & Schaefer, 2000; Hanafin, 2004; Chang, 2005). Surprisingly, the Delphi method has been widely applied in qualitative research science. In short, this method has four main features, including an expert panel that provides opinions, an anonymous manner, repeated questionnaires, and descriptive statistical purposes to present the group opinions (Linstone & Turoff, 1975; Whitman, 1990). In other words, the Delphi method is an integrated research method that combines quantitative and qualitative characteristics. It has the advantage of enabling the researcher to set a panel of selected experts in the process of investigation and inducing experts professional domain knowledge, experience, and opinions in an anonymous way, thereby establishing the consensus of the expert panel and resolving complex issues (Dalkey & Helmer, 1963; Hill & Fowles, 1975; Chang, 2005). However, despite its strengths, the possibility of low response rates, multiple rounds of discussions, and maintaining strong feedback remain to be major challenges for the Delphi method. Hsu and Sandford (2007) affirmed that, when researchers use the Delphi method, their disciplinary competence should include being highly trained in the field of research issues to ensure that they are qualified for the task. Okoli and Pawlowski (2004) deduced that the Delphi study is a group decision mechanism requiring qualified experts with a deep understanding of the issues. The earlier discussion implies that providing complete guidance and comprehensive research process design is needed to conduct a rigorous Delphi approach (Dalkey & Helmer, 1963; Whitman, 1990; Sumsion 1998; Okoli & Pawlowski, 2004; Chang, 2005).

After the scrupulous adoption of Gowin’s Vee knowledge map and application of Delphi expert questionnaire, this study intends to not only resolve the difficulties resulting from face-to-face discussions or group meetings but also enable experts to express their professional opinions in the least affected situation when classifying and verifying the critical risk factors in an IoT environment with the limited number of samples (Okoli & Pawlowski, 2004; Chang, 2005). In sum, the added value of Gowin’s Vee knowledge map and the domain expert panel of the Delphi survey provide a rigorous qualitative approach to fulfill the needs for obtaining consistent feedbacks in classifying critical risk factors and the level of importance.

This research then uses the statistical software SPSS to analyze the feedback information gathered from the Delphi expert questionnaires. The test statistic values include average, quartile deviation, and content validity ratio (CVR). First, the average value is used to observe the importance of critical risk factors. By using a measure of the five-point Likert scale, the importance level of risk factors can obtain from experts feedback opinions. The range of the measuring scale is from 5 to 1, with 5 being very important and 1 being very unimportant. A high average value indicates that a factor can gain additional attention from experts, whereas a low average value suggests that the specific factor will not be paid any more attention.

Second, quartile deviation has been used for observing the decentralized case and degree of consistency in the collected data. Unlike the standard deviation, quartile deviation has a characteristic of robust statistics, which means it is unlikely to be affected by extreme values. More explicitly, through the observation of abnormal values, quartile deviation can identify whether any deviation exists between the decentralized case and judgment result of the various variables in the statistical data. If the value of the quartile deviation is smaller than or equal to 0.6, it represents a high consistency of the experts opinions on the critical risk factors. If the quartile deviation is between 0.6 and 1, then consistency is medium, and if the quartile deviation is larger than 1, it shows that no consistency exists (Holden and Wedman, 1993; Hair et al., 2006; Murry and Hammons, 1995). Based on this rule, this study utilizes the quartile deviation to test the degree of consistency of the expert opinions on the critical risk factors.

Third, content validity ratio (CVR) is the statistical value used to quantify content validity; it is a quantitative method to evaluate content validity (Lawshe, 1975; Churchill, 1979; Hair et al., 2006). In this study, to screen out the improper risk items, the options “Belong” and “Not Belong” were added in the questionnaire items so that experts can determine the appropriateness of the category corresponding to the risk factor. The CVR values are normally between 1 and -1, and when this value is 1 or larger than the suggested threshold value, it means that the risk factor should be put in the corresponding risk category. Since two rounds of expert survey were conducted to judge the risk factors, and the numbers of participant experts were 24 and 23, respectively, the CVR must be higher than the threshold value 0.42 (Lawshe, 1975).

After the Delphi experts survey and statistical testing, one of the 84 risk factors was considered inappropriate (i.e., 1.D.2 Personnel health problems) and has been deleted. Finally, a total of 83 critical risk factors have been obtained and classified into eight types of general risk categories, including environmental, process, decision-making, operational, authorization, data processing and IT, moral, and financial factors. The summarized result of the data analysis and detailed statistical information by the general risk category in an IoT environment is shown in Appendix B. Moreover, it is worthwhile mentioning that risk management is a process involving various IoT devices, technologies, applications, and monitoring by different stakeholders (i.e., IT managers, auditors, and senior managers). ## 5 DISCUSSIONS

The IoT creates a vast network of “things” through information-sensing devices. Following its evolutional and developmental trends, the application of this technology will spread to various industries to expand the scope of services and improve operational efficiency. Consequently, to strengthen the foundation of a comprehensive connection, reliable transmission, and intelligent processing for the enterprise, focusing on the critical risk factors under the IoT environment and adopting the proper control policy has constituted to be an essential issue that should not be ignored (Chen et al., 2014). The result of the Delphi survey generated several vital findings in Appendix B. This study highlights the top three risk factors for different risk categories in Table 4. It has shown and illustrated the impact of these factors based on the characteristics of the three-tiered IoT architecture (i.e., Device, Connect, and Management). Finally, based on the opinions of different expert panels (i.e., IT, Auditing, and Risk management), the importance of risk categories provided in Table 5 will also be discussed.

## (1) Important factors corresponding to each risk category

Environment risk. Environment risk is referred to as an external risk beyond an enterprise's control. Such risk frequently threatens the operation of the enterprise (Benaroch et al., 2006; Soomro et al., 2016). The research findings show that the top three critical risk factors in the environment risk category are the Connection layer: 1.C.7 Unsafe wireless communication channel (4.58), 1.C.1 Loophole of sharing technology (4.38), and 1.C.4 Performing a malicious detection or scan (4.25). A prior study on a framework for integrated risk management in international business (Miller, 1992) revealed that environmental risk could threaten corporate risk management through the impacts from different sources, such as political strategy, government policy, macroeconomic, social factors, and inherent uncertainties. The IoT is virtually a connected Internet environment, and its architecture seeks to aggregate and transmit information through applications, networking and data communications, and a sensing layer to meet the enterprise’s demand. Following the three-tiered architecture of IoT for the IEEE P2413 (IEEE, 2015, p.11), the connection layer controls different sensing components to share the sensed data through wireless (i.e., RFID and Wi-Fi) communication device. Therefore, focusing on the risk control of wireless communication security and loophole/vulnerabilities, the regular monitoring of malware detection, and virus scanning for loopholes will help to ensure the accomplishment of the desired purpose of ERM in the IoT environment (Chen et al., 2014; Vikas et al., 2015).

Process risk. Process risk usually occurs during the company’s operations. As time goes by and activities become more complex, the incidence of these risks will gradually increase (Olson & Wu, 2015). This study uncovers that three factors cannot be ignored in process risk, namely, Management layer: 2.M.1 Invasion of privacy (4.67 on average), and Device layers: 2.D.2 Message eavesdropping (4.67 on average) and 2.D.1 Invasion of privacy (location privacy) (4.58 on average). IIA (2004) issued the COSO ERM framework and disclosed that the control of process risk includes the risk of operations, empowerment, information processing and technology, integrity, and financial risk. However, despite the attention to traditional process risks, this study confirms that the management layer risk factor “invasion of privacy” and the device layer risk factors “Invasion of privacy (location privacy)” and “Message eavesdropping” are more relevant and cannot be neglected. Under these considerations, organizations should propose the specific measures such as implementing personal data protection, monitoring the use of IP locations, and strengthening the firewall rule settings to ensure that risks have been

Decision-making risk. This study confirms that two risk factors appear at the connection layer and are sequenced in terms of their importance as follows: 3.C.1 Problems concerning auditing and searching (4.00) followed by 3.C.2 Long-term and permanent outage (3.38). Decision-making is about making the choices and choosing among a series of options to do or not to do something. Such risks are related to information collection, plan selection, and action in the decision-making process. The source of reliable information and confirmation becomes an important basis for reliable decision-making and further reduces operational, financial, or strategic risks (IIA, 2004; Kaplan & Mikes, 2012). Furthermore, choices available for the decision are usually limited by social, technical, commercial, security, and environmental needs and goals. Decision-makers should identify whether the information is from a source associated with internal or external risk (i.e., medical malpractice, finance, or strategy), ensure the security of IoT facilities, and perform appropriate auditing and verification to avoid and reduce the risk of making a wrong decision. Also, this study uncovers that the operation of the wireless communication network of IoT facilities is highly dependent on the supply of electrical power and telecommunications services. Generally speaking, massive-scale power outage (i.e., earthquake or flood) commonly leads to the failure of wired and wireless networks and consequently impact the performance of the information system in an enterprise. These risk factors should be followed and avoided by the execution of appropriate remedial measures in the management of decision-making and the IoT security control (Bromiley & Rau, 2016).

Operational risk. Operational risk is one type of preventable hazards. Kaplan and Mikes (2012) contended that avoidable risks are related to internal events (i.e., financial and operational risks, and the illegal or unethical behavior of staff), all of which are risks that can prevent in advance. The result of the Delphi analysis in this study confirms that most operational risks have classified in the Connection layer: 4.C.5 Conflict between customer’s solidification procedure (Rigorous procedure) and Cloud environment (4.17),

4.C.4 Economic interruption service (3.88), and 4.C.2 The behavior of the other tenants causing the company to suffer damage (3.87). This study infers that with the rapid development of cloud operating systems and low-cost considerations, the need for enterprises to adopt cloud commercial suite software in developing the integrated service platforms has become a gradual new trend (Zhou, 2013; Almeida et al., 2015). Cloud services are closely related to the network environment; they present unlimited business opportunities and benefits. However, generally owing to its inflexible and rigorous operational function, it lacks agile utility in supporting the complex operational and trading activities, thereby making the transaction process unable to meet the needs of users and customers (Ali et al., 2017). For this reason, the company should pay more attention to avoid the operational risks that the IoT may bring since adopting cloud services and applications. Scholars (Hyun et al., 2018) suggested that the cloud service environment should dynamically update its network security functions to reduce the system operating costs. Specifically, enterprises should frequently check if the security controller is functioning correctly and whether the communication network provides realtime and secure mechanisms. By doing so, it can effectively prevent any risk events from occurring, thereby improving the stability and security of the cloud environment. Furthermore, given the cloud leasing behavior and the purchase of new component functions, the system maintenance cost continues to rise. In the end, stopping cloud leasing services while considering economic costs will also expose companies to the risk of operational losses (McAfee, 2011; Zhou, 2013), and this is simply because cloud services may provide multi-tenant system services. Hence, if cloud infrastructure, platform, or software operation is abused or illegally exploited and involved in malicious access or destruction, it may pose a significant information security risk. In this case, using a locking application for blocking vulnerability attacks, enhancing workload protection for cloud service environments, implementing user environment isolation and resource management are feasible measures necessary to eliminate operational risks in the IoT environment (Hyun et al., 2018; Li et al., 2016; Soomro et al., 2016).

Authorization risk. This risk usually occurs when an enterprise or agents entrusted individuals or organizations (i.e., clients, suppliers, or manufacturers) conduct the legally binding but unauthorized transactions. Such risks occur remarkably in the IoT environment (Kaplan & Mikes, 2012; Wang et al., 2015). For example, many websites have an open authorization mechanism that allows users to perform identity authentication. However, the potential weakness of open licensing also poses a substantial risk to users' security and privacy. This study finds out that the first three risk factors are distributed at the connection layer and are sequenced by importance as follows: 5.C.1 Termination or failure of cloud services (4.71), 5.C.2 Cloud service providers are merged (4.50), and 5.C.3 Privilege escalation (4.25) accordingly. Authorization and supervision represent the fundamental aspects of the internal control of enterprises. In practice, authorization can roughly divide into operational authorization and technical authorization. Operational authorization is the proper regulation and supervision of the employees’ or stakeholders' behavior during their operations, such as competency, documents, procurement, and the scope of exercise of personal dispatch. However, technical authorization is used to regulate the access of employees and stakeholders to the information system and to change the program for ensuring the security of the information system and minimizing the risk of personal privacy leakage (McAfee, 2011; Kaplan & Mikes, 2012; Pieket Weeserik & Spruit, 2018). The relevant scope and limitations of authorization should be clearly defined and communicated appropriately to guarantee compliance from all employees. Specifically, due to the operational authorization problems of “Termination or failure of cloud services” caused by “Cloud service providers are merged” and the technical authorization problem of “User privilege escalation,” these certification and control activities become necessary in the IoT environment.

Data processing and IT risk. Information and professional skills are the lifeline of enterprise computerization. For this reason, data processing and IT risks are essential for digital companies. The justification is that such risks can easily lead to threats to the information security of transactions, processes, customers, and organizations. The results of analyzing risks factor in this category are the Connection layer: 6.C.18 Network outages (4.79), Management layer: 6.M.4 Need new professional skills (4.75), and Connection layer: 6.C.7 Data recovery and reliability (4.71) accordingly. Since the IoT is an extremely complex network environment, to avoid irreparable damage caused by network interruption, the technical capabilities of information personnel and related backup measures are the keys to reducing data processing and IT risks (Sicari et al., 2015; Soomro et al., 2016). Therefore, appropriate governance, such as new professional skills and management mechanisms, should be adopted for the prevention of network outages and ensuring the reliability of data recovery. Thereby to safeguard the security of data in the organization and its embedded information system to prevent the occurrence of such risks (Miller, 1992; Kaplan & Mikes, 2012; Grant et al., 2014).

Moral risk. As to this category, this study identifies the following three critical factors, and they include in the Connection layer: 7.C.2 Cloud service provider malicious misrepresentations (4.58) and 7.C.3 Management interface compromises (4.50); and in the Management layer: 7.M.2 Hostile staff (4.17). Moral risk is one of the problems often encountered in corporate management. It usually occurs when a party participating in the contract changes their behavior, fails to comply with the contractual content, and harms the interests of others for their ends (Cloninger, 1983; Miller, 1992). Specifically, those who take part in economic activities commonly engage in selfish behaviors that are not conducive to others' success in the process of pursuing their best interests or conveniences. Disparte (2016) noted that “bad things happen in the dark.” In other words, when people take risks but do not bear the adverse effects of risky behavior immediately, moral risks will be likely to occur. These risk types have usually hidden in remote areas, less regulated business units, and understaffed teams. Besides, inconsistent incentives allow employees to turn a blind eye to unethical behavior, and this act may also cause risks (Cloninger, 1983). As a result, to prevent such risks, the fundamental measure is to hold every employee in all processes be responsible for the consequences of their mistakes or negligences. However, perform the necessary control to avoid the situation of "cloud service provider malicious misrepresentations, management interface compromises, and hostile staff" is also important to reduce the moral risks that occurred in the IoT environment.

Finance risk. This risk refers to the loss of assets or the imbalance among financing, transferring of daily capital, and distribution of working capital, which may lead to a crisis in the operation of an enterprise (Davenport, 1998; Kaplan & Mikes, 2012; Miller, 1992). This study notes that the top three significant risk factors of the IoT occur in the Device layer, and these factors are 8.D.3 Equipment is stolen (4.13), 8.D.1 Physical attack (3.88), and 8.D.2 Maintenance cost for equipment (3.54). In terms of financial risk, the most important factors are also at the level of “Device” and this is simply because the sensorrelated equipment (i.e., smart bracelet, scanner, and RFID) is a critical component that connects both the internal network and the Internet to maintain the smooth operation of the enterprise. Therefore, it is crucial to protect these devices from being stolen or damaged since the maintenance of the failed devices is very costly. However, establishing IoT equipment management inventory, identifying equipment uses and network segments, standardizing storage locations and management approach, and setting up equipment renewal deadlines can not only effectively eliminate the malpractices of equipment management but also improve the effectiveness of risk management (ISO/IEC 27001, 2013; Chen et al., 2014; ISO 31000, 2018).

## (2) Views of different expert panels on the importance of each risk category

In a study of an actor-observer asymmetry perspective, Nuijten et al. (2018) reported that both IT managers and IT auditors are involved in the activity of IT-related risk control such as assessing, monitoring, and reporting IT risks. Nevertheless, this does not certainly mean that they share the same views in terms of enterprise risk management. With the integrating of many hardware, software, sensors, system platforms, and users, the environment of the IoT has become more sophisticated, diverse, and hence received increasing attention in the fields of IT/IS, accounting, and auditing. Thus, it is helpful to discuss risk controls from different experts' perspectives to enforce the security of related services and the environment (Ammar et al., 2018; Omitola & Wills, 2018). Based on the result of the Delphi survey provided in Table 5, this study discusses the feedback opinions from the experts upon the importance of each risk category.

First, in the view of all expert panels, data analysis determined that process risks are the most important aspect among the risk categories, followed by those of moral and data processing and information technology (IT). The process management generally involves the design, internal audit, and risk management of business processes and internal control systems, among others (Grabski et al., 2001). In terms of internal control, the more concentrated and the longer the process, the higher the risk ratio, especially in a complex IoT environment (Trequattrini et al., 2016). The control process aims to strengthen the internal audit mechanism to mitigate data processing risks and fraud; in particular, cloud based business service innovation and applications tend to be more important (Ali et al., 2017). Therefore, in risk management of IoT at the application layer, it is essential to strictly prevent the leakage of business information to ensure that stakeholders’ privacy is protected and IoT facilities can operate smoothly across the network.

Second, IT experts also opined that process risks are the most significant risks affecting the business, followed by data processing and IT risks, and authorization risks. From the viewpoint of IT experts, enterprises have applied advanced knowledge and technologies in managing the production, manufacturing, supply, and marketing sectors. However, they have failed to mitigate risks caused by using IT. For instance, the contents of a message are pirated, faked, bribed, or tampered with because of the differences in IT activities and norms (Bulgurcu et al., 2010; Omitola & Wills, 2018). Thus, cultivating a secured IoT operating environment necessitates enterprises to strengthen management levels at the networking and data communications layer to avoid additional transaction losses caused by frequent network outages. Enterprises should also ensure a successful implementation of data recovery and reliability after network outages. Furthermore, in terms of the management layer, technicians’ skills should be enhanced, particularly in immediately responding to IoT application risks. Authorization risks may refer to all risks that appear when enterprises temporarily allow their customers, suppliers, or providers to have the same authority to make decisions in manufacturing and marketing through transfer (Li et al., 2016; Ratnasingham, 1998; Kim et al., 2008). The most common causes may include risks in outsourcing contracts, authorization, and certification. For the IoT environment with wired and wireless network architectures, the connection layer, which comprises private networks, the Internet, mobile communications network, RFID, satellite signals, and cloud computing platforms, is mainly designed for processing and sending data and information from the sensing device layer. Thus, for the mitigation of the possible risks caused by improper authorization, enterprises should implement an effective monitoring and audit management of all possible risks for such situations as the termination or expiration of cloud services, merging of cloud service providers, and escalation of privileges.

Third, auditors emphasized that moral risks are the most important, followed by process and authorization risks. Moral risks refer to improper practices resulting in illicit profits obtained by any internal staff members, as well as infringement of the rights and interests of the majority of shareholders and investors (Cloninger, 1983; Gaynor & Gertler, 1995). An interconnected network comprising sensors, actuators, and devices, and the network is built in the IoT environment (Gubbi et al., 2013). Therefore, to protect enterprises from the unnecessary operational risks and significant losses caused by the unethical behavior of employees, the cloud service management of IoT should be strengthened at the networking and data communications layer. This strategy will avoid the malicious misrepresentations of cloud service providers or internal staff members and management interface bugs effectively, thereby protecting enterprises from the potential risks and crises.

Fourth, risk management experts ranked process risk and moral risk as first and second, respectively, among the risk categories and should be carefully controlled in the IoT environment. Decision-making risks have been identified by risk management experts as important items in the governance of IoT networking and data communications, which is slightly inconsistent with the views of other experts. Decision-making risks refer to all risks wherein decision-making activities cannot achieve the intended objective (Kaplan & Mikes, 2012; Miller, 1992). These risks also indicate the possible outcomes caused by subjective or objective factors, as well as the uncertainties of such factors, including the processes that are inevitably faced while gathering information, selecting programs, and taking actions (Bromiley & Rau, 2016; Camerer & Weber, 1992). Thus, enterprises should focus more on internal audit and evidence collection when faced with a new IoT environment to prevent unnecessary losses caused by poor decisions. Enterprises should also avoid the permanent or long-term outages in case of any damage to the system and/or any of its components. Additionally, the potential risks of unreliable data in the IoT environment can confuse and even mislead the decision-makers. Therefore, improving the management of data quality, providing effective verification methods, and strengthening the identity verification of information providers are all critical strategies for enterprises in conducting internal control and auditing (Krishnan, 2005; Nuijten et al., 2018).

## 6 CONCLUSIONS

By adopting a qualitative research approach and using Gowin’s Vee knowledge map, this study has made several contributions. First, this research comprehensively reviews related literature on IT governance in an IoT environment, three-layer DCM architecture, and frameworks of internal control and risk classification. In the referenced literature, the prototype of a risk factor checklist was created. The derivative approach and list of risk factors can be used by other applications such as internal control of unmanned vehicles, drones, and healthcare monitoring networks. Second, this research adopts the Delphi questionnaire survey, and by doing so, the defined risk factors have been classified through the feedback of experts' opinions, and their importance is ranked. The DCM rated list will no doubt provide valuable insight for the IT practitioners, platform developers, and industrial experts to stimulate and conduct further analysis and study. Also, the general risk factors under IoT governance is designed and tested carefully. Through the statistical examination, the confirmed list has high reliability and validity, which can further provide organizations and agencies an essential basis for risk management. The last contribution, this research can be used as a reference for the companies and industries to establish proper guidance, policy, and regulations to protect their information assets. Furthermore, in the new generation of IoT governance practice, the related factors can also be regarded as key measurement items for enterprises in conducting internal control and auditing.

## 6.1 Implications for Academia

This study uses Gowin’s Vee knowledge map as a strategy to address the limitations of qualitative research through a set of strict procedures. Under the systematic guidance of this method, an in-depth investigation is conducted, starting from the formulation of a theoretical framework. A prototype version of the Delphi questionnaire has been developed sequentially from the concepts of the reviewed literature, theory, and framework (Fox, 2007; Gowin, 1981; Novak & Gowin, 1984). A panel of experts in the fields of IT, auditing, and risk management was engaged in testing, provide feedback for, and revise the critical risk factors (Dalkey & Helmer, 1963; Hanafin, 2004; Okoli & Pawlowski, 2004). The research process in investigating IoT critical risk factors and the Delphi research method applied in this study can be used as a reference for the future development of organizational risk auditing and internal control mechanisms. The discussion of risk factors in different fields has also provided research implications for further model development. Notably, the approach concerning the categorized risk factors can also be an academic inspiration for further research in emerging areas. It may initiate a new revenue for the courses and curricular design to complement the traditional data communication, eBusiness, and information security courses by integrating the knowledge and concept of IT governance with IoT together.

## 6.2 Implications for IoT Governance

The IT governance of enterprises has focused mostly on the risk management and implementation of the internal control audit system. In an increasingly complex IoT environment, the applications of related technologies (e.g., cloud computing, mobile instruments, RFID, and wireless networks) have proliferated in the global service market. In the face of the rapid change and technical innovations of the IoT environment, enterprises should not only establish their own competitive business model but also construct the risk-oriented security protection mechanism necessary to guarantee their digital future (Xue et al., 2008; Babar et al., 2010; Yoo, 2010; Debreceny & Gray, 2013; Nuijten et al., 2018).

In this research, several experts (i.e., experts in IT and risk management) suggests that IoT-related activities should be able to be transmitted and connected in a series of business operations through professional connections among all segments. Therefore, process risk management is vital for an enterprise to maintain its security in the IoT environment. Meanwhile, moral risk management cannot be ignored either. Steps should be taken to prevent internal staff from committing fraud, such as illicit buying and selling, creating fake accounts, conducting illegal practices, seeking illegal interests, and infringing on the benefits of investors and shareholders. Internal staff with malicious intentions can also leak data or hide operational events through application-level or network-level cloud services. These malicious acts are important moral risk management items that must be addressed. Accordingly, this study indicates that enterprises should employ moral risk management in addition to paying additional attention to risk control of the flow of data transmission and receipt. By doing so, the performance of enterprises in internal control and auditing can be further enhanced.

The application of IoT-related technologies brings up the concern of risk auditing in internal control for enterprises. However, the question of whether subsequently expected enterprise risk can be controlled effectively under the existing policies requires a further exploration and investigation. In practice, few standards or audit mechanisms that can provide the necessary audits to the business. Under such demand, this study identifies eight categories of critical risk factors: environment, process, decision-making, operation, authorization, data processing and IT, moral, and financial. These factors are explored based on a literature review and further classified in line with the three-layer DCM architecture of IoT (management, connection, and device) to constitute the reference for enterprises to execute internal control. These results can no doubt assist the enterprises in discovering potential risks in an IoT environment and in performing adequate safety supervision of internal control for IoT governance. In the future, IT governance may evolve into the data governance area, given the widespread development and increasing attention to Big Data and Business Intelligence. To this end, how to implement data governance in a broader sense to manage the complex IoT environment and applications proves to be beneficial and promising.

## 6.3 Limitations and Future Research

This research may have four limitations. First, using a qualitative research method, this study defines eight categories of 83 IoT risk factors. Under the guidance of Gowin’s Vee knowledge map, while the research process strives to be objective and tends to be a rigorous study, there is still a lack of further refinement of these factors. Thus, more indepth research may be needed to further verify and revise the correctness of risk factors through case interviews or empirical surveys. Second, the field of IoT applications is vast and may include the following areas such as industrial automation, military antiterrorism, urban transportation to daily family life. Moreover, the risk factors obtained from this research are critical and visible. However, the panel of Delphi experts invited to take part in this study is only from three areas, namely, IT, auditing, and risk management. It is recommended that the source of the expert domains such as finance, operational, and intelligent logistics can be expanded in the future to verify more suitable risk factors and make it more practically applicable. Third, with the rapid growth of emerging technologies, the IoT environment has become more complex and difficult to govern. In addition to the critical risk factors defined in this study, there are still more potential factors not explored and understood, such as Blockchain technology, artificial intelligence, and the application of other financial technologies. These emerging areas are undoubtedly important research directions for future investigation. Fourth, this study has investigated risk factors but does not offer a feasible plan for enterprises to execute an auditing mechanism or implement a control method. Accordingly, further research has been recommended for the establishment of internal control and auditing system for risk management to ensure the auditing mechanism being fully enabled to have a successful implementation.
