# The efects of business systems leveraging on supply chain performance: Process innovation and uncertainty as moderators

<sup>a</sup> Department of Business Administration, National Cheng Kung University, No. 1 University Road, Tainan City 701, Taiwan <sup>b</sup> Department of Health-business Administration, Fooyin University, No. 151 Jinxue Rd., Daliao Dist., Kaohsiung City 83102, Taiwan

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Business systems leveraging (BSL) Supply chain performance Information sharing Process innovation Uncertainty

## A B S T R A C T

Rooted in the profit-seeking motives of social exchange theory, this study aims to investigate the factors that facilitate leverages of firm’s business systems and their sharing of information with group members, as well as the impacts of business systems leveraging (BSL) on information sharing and supply chain performance. Simultaneously, the moderating efects of uncertainty and the degree of process innovation on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance are also estimated. The analytical results indicated that proactive technological orientation, information technology (IT) connectivity, supply chain member pressure, and member relationship quality encourage firms to combine their business systems with those of other business partners. Interestingly, in a situation where there is high demand and an uncertain supply, the impact of BSL on supply chain performance is low, while the moderating efect of process innovation on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance is significant. Finally, it is suggested that firms implementing BSL work to upgrade their technology maintain tight relationships with supply chain partners and create innovation in all aspects of their business processes in order to survive in the highly competitive IT environment.

## 1. Introduction

The rapid development of information technology (IT) has increased information digitalization, thus reshaping the traditional supply chain model. According to Deloitte [1], the digital transformation of supply chains and the use of big data in supply chain management (SCM) have increased in the number of products, which has led to more service customization, faster deliveries, real-time transparency, global scale, cost competition in terms of fulfillment, and increased sustainability eforts. Therefore, firms are facing greater competition and market uncertainty, leading to both opportunities and challenges [2]. To in crease firm’s competitiveness, seize opportunities, and cope with these challenges, firms have changed their business strategies, cooperating with members in the supply chain to obtain overall profits instead of acquiring limited individual profits. The mutually competitive relations among firms are, thus, gradually being replaced by closer, more cooperative relations. Prior studies have pointed out that profit-orienta tion and a desire for sustainability are causing firms to integrate their business systems and share information/knowledge to maintain close, long-term cooperative partnerships [3]. Moreover, coordinating and integrating of business systems among firms not only adds value to the products and services ofered to consumers but also leads to maximization of firm’s profits ([4–6]). System integration also makes it possible to monitor the production performance of suppliers and, thus, ensures the quality of the products supplied [7]. Therefore, BSL with supply chain members is essential to enhance competitive advantage in the current highly digitalized business environment.

A firm’s business system can be considered to be an entire business operational system that includes an enterprise resource planning (ERP) system, a vendor-managed inventory (VMI) system, a supply chain (SC) system, an information system (IS), and an inter-organizational (IO) system. When implementing BSL among firms, more business activities can be conducted in an integrated, electronic environment than would be possible through a single application [8]. However, in order to integrate a business system among firms, some important factors must be considered. For instance, a firm’s internal factors, such as its internal IT and network connection, must be up to date and operational [9]. External factors such as the environment of competitors [10] and the closeness of the relationship between firms and their business partners [8.11] must also be taken into account

Some studies have adopted the technology–organizational–envir onment model to discuss firm’s IT adoption, in which technological factors include technology compatibility, readiness, and usefulness [10,12]; organizational factors include organizational culture, organizational structure, management support, financial commitment, and firm’s size [13,14], and environmental factors include competitive pressure and regulatory support [10]. However, the current study argues that technological factors such as organizational factors and environmental factors are inter-organizational factors. Because firm’s IT capability is a necessary capability that should be in place when moving forward with leveraging a business system, it can be considered to be a part of organizational readiness related to facilitating BSL [13]. This study suggests that when firms actively adopt new IT applications in their business operations, they are demonstrating management support, financial commitment, and the presence of digital information structures. In such cases, a firm’s internal network and communication structure should have the capability of supporting its decision to implement BSL. Thus, this study proposes proactive technology orientation, IT connectivities, and internal communication as organizational factors that facilitate BLS. In addition, firms implementing BSL change the buyer–supplier relationship into a partner relationship. Therefore, this study considers external (enviromental) factors, such as supply chain member pressure and member relationship quality, as inter-organizational factors that motivate firms to implement BSL.

Moreover, information processing theory (IPT) suggested that or ganizations need more information to cope with environmental uncertainty, which is related to changing customer demand, unstable suppliers, unpredictable actions of competitors, and the complexity of inter-organizational activities in the supply chain ([5,6]). Firms’ information processing needs must fit their information processing capacity and strategy planning in order to improve performance [15]. In the context of business systems leveraging (BSL), all business partners need the same information for forecasting, which means that new in formation related to business chain practices must be shared frequently [16]. The information, therefore, must be available to all business partners. The current study posits that when firms implement BSL, the quality and content of information sharing must increase among business partners and ultimately foster supply chain performance.

Some studies have mentioned the benefits and efectiveness of business system integration, including reducing inter-enterprise supply chain information sharing costs [17] and improving the eficiency of SCM [18]. However, previous studies have mostly discussed BSL-related issues through conceptual discussions [19,20], literature reviews [21], or case studies [22], and few have emphasized the factors that facilitate BSL [8]. Thus, there are insuficient empirical studies discussing such facilitators. In addition, studies related to BSL [8] or IS integration [23] suggest that BSL or IS integration lead to improved firm’s benefits/performance. However, this study argues that when firms leverage their business systems, information sharing activities among firms will be increased because more new information must be combined and shared among partners, which is similar to the research concept of Maiga et al. [24] but seldom discussed in the BSL context.

Furthermore, information sharing, information processes, and system integration among supply chain partners have long been considered to be the key factors in successful SCM ([5,25,6]). Prior literature indicates that process innovation in new product development enhances firm’s performance [26,27] and that management of uncertainty is an important issue in current dynamic IT and digital busi ness environment [15,28]. However, these issues are seldom mentioned in the BSL context and. thus. should be further discussed [29.30].

Based on the above discussions and to fill the research gaps, the main objectives of the study include first developing a conceptual framework to understand the factors facilitating BSL and testing the efects of organizational factors (proactive technological orientation, IT con nectivity, and internal communication) and inter-organizational factors (supply chain member pressure and partner relationship quality) on BSL. The second objective is to examine the relationship between BSL and information sharing and ensure the direct efects of BSL and information sharing on supply chain performance. The last objective is to examine the moderating roles of process innovation and uncertainty on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance.

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. Business systems leveraging (BSL)

Mutually beneficial cooperation enables companies to maximize profits and minimize costs through business integration [31]. Business integration involves the integration of hardware, software, and negotiated standards that permit data and information access and exchange. BSL has a similar concept to that of the Keirestu system in Japanese companies, where the resources of the key members are centralized to reduce transaction costs, increase coordination and innovation, lower opportunism, and yield more stable production [32]. Companies that combine or conjoin business systems, either partially or totally, broaden their information capabilities and allow the support of more business functions and processes than is the case for companies with disjointed applications [33]. Iyer et al. [34] further noted that, based on the process integration and real-time information, firms can reduce in ventory and associated costs and, thus, achieve supply chain integration. Supply chain integration can, therefore, be defined as the coordination and integration of supply chain processess between a company and its partners in a supply chain [35].

BSL extends the scope of supply chain integration. It broadly leverages the business functions related to integration of inventories, processes, procurement/order, and distribution [8]. BSL can, thus, be seen as the degree to which companies combine their business systems and use them to carry out orders, information exchanges, customer services, and business processes. In practice, before companies leverage their business systems, they are likely to develop islands of automation to limit the scope and scale of their information capabilities and organizational impact. As Chengalur-Smith et al. [8] mentioned, the major distinction between systems integration and BSL is that systems integration may result in a short-term competitive advantage, while BSL leads to a sustainable competitive advantage.

## 2.2. The antecedents of BSL

Chengalur-Smith et al. [8] adopted the concept of relational concurrence to discuss the antecedents of BSL although this can only be used to understand the level of shared business interests between suppliers and customers, as reflected in the amount of cross investing, satisfaction of mutual needs, and the importance placed on the customer–supplier relationship. The success of BSL depends heavily on how an organization can eficiently fulfill the corporate networking requirements of its partners and clients and how it can continue to augment its capabilities through vertical or horizontal business integrations to enhance its sustainable competitive advantages. Such integrations have been examined in the literature using various factors, both within and across organizations (e.g., [36]). The various organizational and inter-organizational factors that supply chain firms choose in order to leverage their supply chain business systems are, thus, examined in this study, as explained in more detail below.

## Organizational factors

Organizational factors are closely related to the reasons firms move forward to implement BSL, such as the degree of readiness of their IT capability, the IT connectivity network, and internal communication Because BSL requires a significant amount of technological knowledge and extensive resources, organizations should actively adopt new technology to support their needs [13]. In addition, Liu et al. [37] suggested that organizational IT components must be well-connected to facilitate the integration of data sources within an organization in order for firms to be able to communicate and exchange information with their partners. The degree to which organizations achieve efective internal communication also plays a key role in facilitating BSL. Jacobs et al. [38] indicated that good internal communication creates an atmosphere of respect between employees and organizations, which leads to open relationships, trust, and mutual understanding. This study, thus, defines organizational factors as the reasons why firms are willing to leverage their business systems, where proactive technological orientation, IT connectivity, and internal communication are adopted as the organizational factors that may facilitate the implementation of BSL. Each of these is introduced as follows.

Proactive technological orientation is described by Grover [39] as “concerning how sophisticated the organization’s orientation to tech nology is.” A proactive technological orientation includes engaging in strategic IS planning, recognizing the integral role of IT, having a strong IT internal infrastructure, having aggressive management willing to take risks, and having a proactive technology policy. Ho et al. [40] indicated that technology orientation is a key to develope firm’s competitive advantage, especially in the case of new product innovation. A firm that has a proactive technological orientation is more likely to be characterized by discovery, variation, and innovation, to pay more attention to exploratory learning, and to be willing to take risks [41]. This study adopts Grover‘s [39] assertion to depict the cause–efect relation between proactive technological orientation and BSL. Proactive tech nological orientation is defined as the level of sophistication of an organization’s orientation toward technology, including strategic IS planning, a complete IT infrastructure, and a proactive attitude toward new technology adoption and risk.

IT connectivity can be defined as the ability of every technology component to connect and interact with any other exterior and interior component in the environment of a firm [42]. Dubey et al. [43] suggested that a well-connected technological resource enables efective information sharing in the supply chain environment. That is, firm’s IT connectivity capability enables firms to exchange knowledge eficiently with their partners in the supply chain, which allows such firms to transfer and recombine knowledge across functional units [37]. Russell and Hoag [44] indicated that connectivity capability enables firms to connect to a network of other organizations and to forge new relationships to select business partners who are best suited for the task under consideration. Thus, when a firm’s IT has the ability to connect with internal and external technology in the business environment, the firm may have more opportunity to expand its business, which, in turn, makes it more likely that the firm will implement BSL.

Internal communication as defined by Jacobs et al. [38] is the exchange of information and decisions among the employees of a firm to build trusting relationships and to create mutual understanding. Good internal communication enables employees to understand the firm’s goals and policies and helps them understand their merits, which contributes to a positive internal relationship by facilitating participa tion in decision-making and building and maintaining relationships [38]. Moreover, studies have suggested that a good internal communication mechanism enables firm’s departments to interact easily and conveniently within the internal network, which promotes mutual un derstanding and develops organizational cohesiveness [45,46]. This can result in easier access and a stronger inclination to request support from other members in the case of decision-making related to IT adoption. Therefore, it is posited in the current study that a firm’s internal communication enables the firm’s members to work together, have the same vision, and to gain mutual understanding, which, in turn, facilitates the firm moving forward to implement BSL.

## Inter-organizational factors

Inter-organizational factors are related to the inter-relationships among firms in the supply chain environment. Smith et al. [47] suggested that the collaboration processes among supply chain partners involve the principle of creating a flexible supply chain through the presence of collaborative relationships that facilitate a mutual decisionmaking process directed toward achieving common goals across partners. Such collaborative relationships aim to acquire desired outcomes from social interactions by maximizing rewards and minimizing costs [48]. To obtain the maximum benefits, close inter-organizational relationships become extremely important for supply chain members [49]. Thomas et al. [48] further indicated that pressure from group members has a potential influence on relational exchange and collaborative behavior because firms will have less competitive advantage if they do not integrate their business systems into the chain system. Thus, here, inter-orgnizational factors are defined as the reasons that firms are motivated to implement BSL, and supply chain member pressure and member relationship quality are adopted as factors that facilitate BSL implementation.

Supply chain member pressure is viewed as coercive pressure from supply chain partners [50] or the industry itself [51], which comprise two of the inter-organizational factors that may influence firms to integrate their supply chain business systems. Coercive pressure is an important external motivation for trading partners to start the development of IOS [52]. For example, Wu and Chuang [53] argued that peer pressure is one of the important attributes of collaborative structure in the difusion of e-SCM. This study defines supply chain member pressure as all pressure in terms of technology from supply chain members, including competitors and cooperation partners, as in Patterson et al. [51]. The firm initiates the adoption of a new supply chain technology, which puts pressure on or entices other firms in the supply chain to adopt it in order to achieve the same standardization of data formats that can improve coordination and communication among these organizations [54]. Patterson et al. [51] concluded that a firm’s decision to adopt electronic data interchange (EDI) is based on what its business partners are doing. For example, in a supply chain, two firms may integrate their business systems, which leads to standardized data formats and better information sharing. Other partner firms will then be likely to use the same business system in order to streamline transactions and improve inter-firm communication.

Member relationship quality has been heavily discussed in terms of its importance in B2B [55] and B2C business environments [56]. Jelodar et al. [57] indicated that satisfaction, commitment, and trust are the common attributes that can be used to evaluate relationship quality. In the B2B context, relationship quality reflects the overall depth and closeness of inter-organizational relationships and the overall business climate, as well as the degree to which the business relationships meet the needs and expectations of the parties involved [55]. High-quality relationships between business partners can lead to mutual benefits and reciprocity as a result of the exchange of business knowledge in a dynamic business environment [58]. In the context of the supply chain relationship, high-quality relationships between partners can facilitate trust and mutual understanding between partners [59], where firms believe their partners will not engage in opportunism related to the shared information. In such circumstances, firms will find it easier to connect their business systems to those of other partners. Accordingly, the study defines member relationship quality as a favorable relationship among supply chain members that indicates trust, commitment, and satisfaction [55,58,59].

## 2.3. Information sharing

The purposes of information sharing not only include the sharing of information but also the sharing of reciprocity benefits between two parties [60]. It is a significant factor leading to tight coordination that has been greatly facilitated by advances in IT [61]. Information sharing refers to efective and eficient flows of information between an enterprise and its partners in a supply chain [62]. Such information includes predictions and production schedules, as reflected in inventory drawdown rates, as well as inventory data at upstream locations [8].

Following prior studies, in this study, information sharing is defined as the level of good-quality information flow between an enterprise and its partners in a supply chain [62].

Information sharing is a key to success with developing an SC system because it reduces the bullwhip efect and improves firm’s performance [63]. Zhou and Benton [28] found that firm’s performance was improved when more information was exchanged with customers and suppliers via EDI. Information quality measures the degree to which the information exchanged between two organizations fits the needs of the organizations. In this study, information quality is defined as the degree of accuracy and availability of informational content. Zhou and Benton [28] mentioned that information quality is an important determinant of the usefulness of an IS and used nine features to measure this: accuracy, availability, timeliness, internal connectivity, external connectivity, completeness, relevance, accessibility, and frequency of updating of information. According to the current research background, we use the following seven characteristics to measure in formation quality: accuracy, availability, timeliness, frequency of updating, completeness, relevance, and accessibility. Companies that share information with each other have increased visibility, and thus they are able to coordinate replenishment more efectively and streamline the flow of goods and services [16].

## 2.4. Supply chain performance

Prior studies have discussed diferent measurements of supply chain performance based on the evaluation of SCM, including tangible (e.g., cost) and intangible (e.g., capacity utilization) factors [35,62]. Beamon [64] used resources, output, and flexibility to measure supply chain performance. Martin and Patterson [65] adopted three elements to measure preformance in the supply chain: inventory, cycle time, and finance. Supply chain performance can be measured by inventory in vestment, throughput eficiency at the delivery and service levels, and supplier performance, as well as cost. In this study, supply chain performance is defined as the evaluation of SCM, with a focus on the efects of information sharing and BSL in the supply chain.

To measure the efects of BSL and information sharing on supply chain performance, we adopt three key elements from Beamon [64]: resources, output, and flexibility. Resources generally measure the minimum requirements or a composite eficiency of resource perfor mance, including inventory levels, personnel requirements, equipment utilization, energy usage, and cost. Output is measured by customer responsiveness/satisfaction, quality, and final production quantity [64]. Flexibility indicates how well a firm reacts to uncertainty. It can be divided into two types: range flexibility and response flexibility. Range flexibility is defined as to what extent an operation can be changed, while response flexibility is defined as the ease with which this can be done.

## 2.5. Moderators: process innovation and uncertainty

Process innovation refers to elements that are new to the organization, industry, and subunits in a company. Langley et al. [66] defined process innovation as the use of tools, devices, and technologies that can influence the relationship between inputs and outputs. Parthasarthy and Hammond [67] further claimed that outputs can be positively af fected when the integration level of the innovation process increases with the degree of inputs, so process innovation will moderate the “inputs–outputs” relationship. Therefore, in this study, it is assumed that process innovation will moderate the relationship between BSL (input) and supply chain performance (output). Moreover, this study focuses on the supply chain process, and thus, three types of processes are used to measure supply chain innovation: the inventory process, the product manufacturing process, and the customer response process. In addition, the number of innovation activities and type of innovation orientation of firms are also assessed in this study.

Pesamaa et al. [68] mentioned that learning orientation should be considered to understand efective innovation that leads to competitive service delivery. Successful SCM requires the introduction of new processes and the re-engineering of existing ones to facilitate eficient and efective flows of information among supply chain partners [69]. In supply chains, both suppliers and customers usually face a dynamic situation, thus leading to an uncertain environment. Vijayasarathy [69] assessed such uncertainty in terms of manufacturer demands, competitor strategies, and the number of competitors. Early end-user in volvement is an important way to fill key information gaps in joint process development projects and thus serves as a vital activity for reducing uncertainty [70].

Davis [71] suggested that there are three diferent types of uncertainty in a supply chain: demand uncertainty, supply uncertainty, and technological uncertainty. Demand uncertainty includes unknown or unpredictable variations in both the quantity and timing of demand in a supply chain [29]. Supply uncertainty refers to an unpredictable variation in the quantity and timing of the supply in a supply chain, including the switching and adjustment costs related to changing outputs or product specifications. Technological uncertainty is related to technological change, and when this occurs rapidly, the firms in a supply chain must be able to carry out fast, efective information sharing. The integration of business systems is also more important when technology is changing rapidly [29].

Germain et al. [30] discussed supply chain variability, organizational structure, and performance, and their results suggested that demand unpredictability is the main factor in overall uncertainty and that this increases when demand is more dificult to monitor and forecast. Fynes et al. [29] defined demand uncertainty as unknown or unpredictable variations in the quantity and timing of demand, while supply uncertainty was defined as the unpredictable nature of the quantity and timing of supply. Finally, technological uncertainty is the rate of technological change. Therefore, in this study, Davis’ (1993) uncertainty dimension is used, which is based on demand uncertainty, supply uncertainty, and technological uncertainty.

## 3. Conceptual model

The objective of this study is to verify the importance of organiza tional factors (i.e., proactive technological orientation, IT connectivity, and internal communication), and inter-organizational factors (such as supply chain member pressure and member relationship quality) with regard to BSL. Firms will be more likely to leverage their business systems when they have a proactive technological orientation, higher IT connectivity, greater internal communication, higher supply chain member pressure, and high member relationship quality. After firms have managed to successfully leverage their business systems, information sharing, including information quality and content, is expected to be increased, and thus to improve supply chain performance (i.e., resources, output, and flexibility performance). In addition, uncertainty and process innovation are treated as moderators, which are assumed to have positive moderating efects on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance. ## 3.1. Hypotheses development

## 3.1.1. Organizational factors and BSL (H1)

Grover [39] verified that a proactive technological orientation is a strong facilitator of a customer-based inter-organizational system (CIOS) and argued that a proactive IT role and the willingness of top management to take risks are important predictors of adopting CIOS. Mohamed et al. [36] also found that a proactive technological orientation is a factor contributing to IT success. Zhou and Li [72] in dicated that technology orientation has a positive efect on technologybased innovations. Thus, firms guided by a technology orientation accumulate rich technological knowledge stores through past experi ence and processes, such as heavy investments in R&D, rapid acquisition of new technologies, and the collection of up-to-date technological information. Therefore, technology orientation increases adaptive capabilities.

When a firm has a proactive technological orientation, it will be more likely to have contact with new technology, will have more managerial support in adopting new technology, and will also have more comprehensive strategic system planning [73]. Such companies will be more willing to take risks related to adopting new technologies. In practice, adopting BSL is similar to the adoption of new technology and integrating business systems with others, so firms with a more proactive technological orientation are more likely to increase the extent of supply chain BSL. Moreover, in the supply chain context, it is posited here that firms with a proactive technological orientation are commonly using new technology in routine operations. This not only increases their compatibility with the business systems of their supply chain partners but also less time is required to cope with integrated market information and business knowledge. Based on this line of discussion, proactive technological orientation firms have higher degree of readiness for BSL. Hence, the following hypothesis is presented:

H1a. Proactive technological orientation has a positive efect on the extent of BSL.

According to Dubey et al. [43], high IT connectivity can be regarded as a firm’s ability to gather and share information through the use of information communication technology systems (ICTs), which allow firms to contribute to the supply chain network to share business knowledge. Good ability also means the firm’s system is compatible with the system network of other firms, which helps the firm span organizational boundaries and facilitates collaboration with channel partners [37]. In addition, Brandon‐Jones et al., [74] pointed out that the connectivity of technological resources enables firms to make decisions more successfully and improves coordination with business partners. Thus, it is posited here that when a firm has a high degree of readiness in terms of IT infrastracture and good ability to connect with other firms, the firm will be more likely to facilitate BSL implementation because it can make rapid adjustments to its system and business strategies when faced with a changing business environment. In addi tion, when a firm integrates its business systems with its formal com petitors and becomes partners with them, information can be obtained more easily, and the scope of that information is wider, which benefits the business chain. As a result, it can be predicted that when firms have a high degree of IT connectivity, they will be more likely to increase the extent of BSL. Therefore, the following hypothesis is developed.

## H1b. IT connectivity has a positive efect on the extent of BSL.

To intergrate a firm’s system with its business partners, a solid in ternal communication mechanism is also a prerequisite. Zsidisin et al. [46] suggested that an internal communication climate facilitates cooperation, information sharing, cohesiveness, and coordination among diferent business functions. Indeed, a high level of internal communication helps avoid misunderstanding and improves problem solving and goal attainment [38]. It can, thus, be predicted that when a firm has a high internal communication mechanism, it not only increases internal coordination and reduces misunderstandings but also helps firm’s managers to communicate with the members when the firm decides to implement BSL. Under such circumstances, the new decision can be transmited to all members, and their feedback can be received more easily and rapidly. When the communication has occurred, the members will be expected to be more likely to support the decision, and as a result, the BSL implementation will be smoother and more efective. Moreover, internal communication also means that internal in formation is integrated and flows smoothly across the firm, which also benefits to the firm in terms of the extent of BSL. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H1c. Internal communication has a positive efect on the extent of BSL.

## 3.1.2. Inter-organizational factors and BSL (H2)

Prior studies have indicated that competitive pressure is an important element encouraging firms to efectively move across various stages of new technology adoption [75]. Wu and Chuang [53] suggested that when major competitors adopt e-SCM successfully, this will put more pressure on competitors and force them to adopt the new technology, particularly if it is related to their supply chain operations, which are closely tied to those of trading partners. Indeed, coercive pressure has been identified as a important driver for the adoption of EDI and e-SCM on the part of organizations [76].

In the supply chain context, the members of the chain can engage in horizontal collaboration that includes competitors and other business partners [77]. When other members in the supply chain integrate their business systems, firms will feel pressure if they do not also do so because they will become isolated and unable to share business information with other members. Under such circumstances, the firms will be keen to upgrade their supply chain technology using BSL in order to meet other partners’ requirements, to improve the mutual efficiency of supply chain functions, and to avoid having to replace supply chain partners. In addition, Pan et al. [76] suggested that dominant companies can force dependent firms to adopt specific ISs they are in favor of within the institutional environment because they control access to limited, important resources.

In practice, firms may feel pressure when competitors adopt a new technology or integrate systems to operate supply chain functions, which causes managers to feel that their competitors have lower costs, more eficient processes, and faster information flow. In this context, companies will be more willing to integrate their business systems with those of their supply chain partners to enhance firm competitive advantages. Therefore, this study presents the following hypotheses:

H2a. Supply chain member pressure has a positive efect on the extent of BSL.

Hoppner et al. [58] indicated that a high quality inter-firm relationship is built on a foundation of reciprocity, which can lead to greater collaboration efectiveness and eficiency. Relationship quality in the B2B context is an overall evaluation between business partners, where a favorable relationship enables partners to engage in long-term cooperation and conflict resolution [55]. Moreover, Jelodar et al. [57] suggested that proactive relationship management can facilitate and have direct impacts on project’s success and performance. That is, when a firm has developed a good relationship with its business partners, showing mutual trust and honesty, their business collaboration will be long-lasting and have no boundaries, which will ultimately improve overall performance.

Based on the above discussion, it is posited that a favorable relationship among members of the supply chain motivates firms to implement BSL. Because a high quality relationship indicates trust, commitment, and satisfaction among members in the supply chain, the firms in the chain will feel more comfortable sharing business information and will be more willing to develop reciprocal relationships with other members. Zhang et al. [56] pointed out that relationship quality also exists in the B2B context and that it facilitates customer loyalty. Because firms implement BSL to collaborate closely with other supply chain members, higher member relationship quality fosters a firm’s decision to implement BSL. This not only benefits the firm but also benefits the entire supply chain as a whole.

H2b. A favorable member relationship quality among supply chain members has a positive effect on the extent of BSL.

## 3.1.3. BSL and information sharing (H3)

According to the IPT, firms need more information to cope with uncertain business environments [15]; therefore, firms are encouraged to share information with business partners to increase competitive advantage. However, Du et al. [78] argued that information sharing at the organizational level requires maintaining the security of companies financial and strategic information as their current partners might once have been, or may later become, competitors. Therefore, organizations are cautious about the information they share, and to what extent and to whom it should be divulged. Whether a firm shares information or not depends on the levels of trust and commitment within the part nership [78].

In the context of BSL, supply chain members are a firm’s formal business partners, and firms have developed mutual trust among the members. This favorable condition may motivate a firm to leverage its business systems with these members to carry out business cooperation and transaction more closely. In addition, when firms have decided to leverage their business systems into the SC system, it is indicated that they are eager to cooperate with other members in the supply chain to obtain greater benefits. Maiga et al. [24] indicated that a close collaborative relationship between partners increases quality and improves cost performance. That is, when firm members work closely with the partners who have implemented BSL, information sharing activities, such as frequency of sharing and information quality, are expected to increase. This is because all business information will be recombined, will flow across the system, and will be shared with all members in the supply chain. [5] [6],) suggested that implementation of new IT facil itates communication among supply chain partners. Therefore, BSL not only facilitates sharing of information frequently but also increases information quality. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H3. BSL has a positive efect on information sharing among supply chain members.

## 3.1.4. Information sharing and supply chain performance (H4)

Information sharing is essential in a supply chain. Companies should, thus, not only make their own demand forecasts, inventory replenishment decisions, and place orders with suppliers but should also inform suppliers of their forecast requirements [79] [5]. suggested that one of the purposes of information sharing among business partners is to exchange and communicate ideas among partners and executive supply chain activities. Prior studies have indicated that information sharing among business partners has an impact on supply chain performance. For instance, Eng (2004) argued that information sharing not only advances the ability to control SCM, but also reduces transaction costs. Chang and Wong [17] suggested that the flexibility of product specifications can be enhanced by information sharing, such as that occurring in the BSL process. Kochan et al. [63] indicated that information sharing improves supply chain performance by reducing the bullwhip efect. Similarly, Chang et al. [62] found that information sharing between an enterprise and its partners is positively related to supply chain performance.

In the context of BSL, it is, thus, posited that BSL boosts recombination of business information among supply chain partners. Therefore, the information that is shared among the partners is no longer only production and inventory information, but also includes strategic planning, marketing activities, product knowledge, etc. In such cases, information quality will be improved, and quantity will be increased, which will, in turn, help chain partners make more accurate decisions and will shorten the time required to respond to changes in the market. Huang et al. [80] pointed out that Walmart shared weekly sales information with its suppliers, which enhanced coordination and mitigated the bullwhip efect along the supply chain. The sharing of more accurate information leads to greater postitve efects on the supply chain [17]. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4. Information sharing has a positive efect on supply chain performance.

## 3.1.5. BSL and supply chain performance (H5)

The timely and accurate flow of data is necessary for successful supply chain operations [33,69]. BSL not only facilitates and enchances information exchanges but also improves processes and product avail ability, resulting in lower inventory and labor costs, higher returns on investment, and greater flexibility in reacting to the market [8]. Com bining business systems, thus, improves overall supply chain perfor mance. Maiga et al. [24] mentioned that external IS integration enables firms to work closely and to exchange more information and business strategies, which ultimately fosters improved supply chain performance. Vijayasarathy [69] indicated that the use of technology in a supply chain has a positive influence on supply chain performance. In the supply chain context, firms that combine their business systems to execute their supply chains can coordinate their internal and external supply chain activities, leading to lower inventory, less obsolescence, and lower transportation costs, thus improving overall productivity [81] and leading to operational and financial benefits. Similarly, Chengalur-Smith et al. [8] posited that BSL is positively related to the firm’s performance. Therefore, if companies engage in BSL to a greater extent, they are more likely to have better supply chain performance, as stated in the following hypothesis.

H5. BSL has a positive efect on supply chain performance.

## 3.1.6. Moderators: process innovation and uncertainty (H6˜H7)

Firm’s innovation capability is an important element in firm’s performance [82], so more innovative firms should, thus, exhibit better performance than less innovative ones. Prajogo and Ahmed [27] showed that there is a positive relationship between innovation and performance, while Ilker and Birdogan [26] argued that process innovation is directly and positively associated with firm’s performance. According to Chengalur-Smith et al. [8] and Ilker and Birdogan [26], companies that have achieved better supply chain performance under a high degree of BSL and process innovation are more likely to have improved firm’s performance. Thus, when firms work on improving process innovation, they will find it easier to upgrade their supply chain processes, such as business systems, manufacturing processes, logistic processes, and ordering processes.

Based on the above discussion, it is posited that firms with high levels of process innovation will be more likely to implement BSL and exhibit better supply chain performance. On the other hand, with low levels of process innovation, firms will be less competitive not only in terms of infrastructure but also in terms of information formatting. Ultimately, such firms will be isolated and lack competitiveness in the business market. Vijayasarathy [69] suggested the adoption of process innovation as a moderator between technology use in the supply chain and supply chain performance and noted that technology use has to be coupled with investments in process improvements to ensure a gain in supply chain performance. Therefore, it can be expected that process innovation will have a positive moderating efect on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, which can be stated as follows.

H6. Process innovation has a positive moderating efect on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance.

IT facilitates the linking of firms to system networks, such as SC systems, to share information and cope with the uncertainty of the business environment [25]. Vijayasarathy [69] mentioned that the relationship between technology use in the supply chain and supply chain performance is moderated by uncertainty. Resource dependence theory suggests that the times of greater uncertainty foster stronger relationships among firms to draw resources from partners and maintain performance [29]. Transaction cost theory also supports the idea that systems integration or quasi-integration may result from uncertainty in the environment [83]. Fynes et al. [29] suggested that greater uncertainty in terms of demand, supply, and technology leads to a stronger relationship between supply chain relationship quality and supply chain performance.

Under conditions of high demand uncertainty, firms must respond to the uncertainty promptly and make adjustments to their operations accordingly [29]. Thus, implemention of BSL is useful and valuable for firms because it enables them to cope with uncertainty and, as a result, improves supply chain performance. In other words, BSL is more likely to support supply chain activities under volatile demand conditions than under stable demand conditions. Furthermore, supply uncertainty occurs in the form of manufacturing downtime, quality and yield problems, order-entry errors, forecast inaccuracies, or logistical mal functioning [29]. BSL can benefit firms because it can help them forsee problems and derive solutions earlier. Technological uncertainty includes whether or not opportunities arising from technological changes and new product ideas can be identified and exploited [84]. Pesamaa et al. [68] found a moderation of the impacts of risk-taking, creativity, competitor benchmarking orientation, and environmental opportunities on innovativeness. However, companies that are able to successfully engage in BSL are more likely to improve in an environment with high technological uncertainty. As noted above, the following hypothesis is, thus, proposed.

H7. Demand, supply, and technology uncertainty have positive moderating efects on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance.

## 4. Research design and results

This study was a quantitative research design; all construct definitions and hypotheses were proposed based on the prior literature (see Appendix A). A paper-based questionnaire was developed for the field survey, where people who work in departments such as procurement, production, and inventory were eligible to participate in the survey. The respondents were initially asked about their familiarity with the supply chain situation at the firm in which they work, and when the respondents answered “normal” or “unfamiliar,” they were eliminated. Consequently, the respondents were asked to rate all the research scales based on their experience with their firm’s situation. All scales were based on a seven-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree), except for the information content sharing scale, which ranged from 1 (never) to 7 (once a day). The last section comprised demographic variables, including gender, age, seniority, position, department, company role in the supply chain, business scope of the company, and company scales by number of employees, industry, and total revenue. The questionnaire items are listed in Appendix B.

A pilot test was conducted to ensure the internal consistency and reliability of the scales; item-to-total correlation (>.50) and Cronbach’s alpha (>.70) were computed. A total of 35 respondents participated, including 26 middle-to-senior level managers and 9 MBA and PhD students. As a result, all item-to-total (.516˜.944) and Cronbach’s alpha values (.705˜.953) were above the threshold, except for the DU1 item to-total, which was slightly lower (.476); however, the item was retained after some wording adjustments were made. Moreover, because the respondents commented that the information content items were too similar, 3 items were eliminated to avoid redundance, including ISC1, ISC2, and ISC4. There were a total of 77 items in the final questionnaire pool, including one reversed item.

The formal survey was conducted using a paper-based questionnaire. A total of 1000 questionnaires were distributed to firms that had adopted an SC system, as selected from the list of “Taiwan’s Top 5000 firms.” As a result, 228 questionnaires were collected, and 24 were deemed invalid and filtered based on respondent familiarity with the SC system and the reversed item. The remaining 204 questionnaires were included in the formal data analysis. Almost 70% of the respondents were male. Approximately, 43% of the respondents were aged between 20 and 30 years old. Overall, the respondents tended to be aged below 40 years old. Furthermore, 60.3% of respondents had worked less than 5 years in their production and operation departments of (22%) or in R&D (16%). The demographic details for the respondents are shown in Table 1.

In the formal data analysis, the mean value and standard deviation were computed for all items, followed by an item-to-total correlation (>.50) to ensure internal consistency and reliability using SPSS17.0, and a factor analysis for convergent and discriminant validity was conducted using SmartPLS2.0. As a result, all item-to-total correlation coeficients were above the threshold; however, 8 items (SO4, IN1, IN2, MP1, OP8, FP5, PI1, and PI3) were eliminated because of cross-loading between two constructs. After these eliminations, all criteria were satisfied. Table 2 demonstrates the final results for the means, standard deviation, item-to-total correlation, and factor loadings, and Appendix C shows the cross-loading.

In terms of convergent and discriminant validity, the average variance extracted (AVE > .50), composite reliability (CR > .70), and Cronbach’s alpha (>.70) were computed. Table 3 shows that the AVE ranged from .675˜.891; the CR values were .901˜.964, and the Cronbach’s alpha was .815˜.956, which satisfied the requirements and de monstrated high convergent validity. Moreover, a discriminant analysis was conducted to ensure that one construct could be distinguished from any other construct. Table 3 also shows the square root of the AVE, which was greater than the correlation values between two constructs. These results also satisfied the criteria.

Furthermore, because the study comprised self-reported data from a single source, a Harmon one-factor test was conducted to ensure the efects of common method bias (CMV). As a result, the first extracted factor accounted for 37.7% of the variance in the data (<50%). In addition, the highest correlation coeficient between two constructs was .738 (OP↔PR), which was far from the threshold of .90. Therefore, overall, the potential efect of CMV was not a major concern.

## 5. Findings and discussion

## 5.1. Organizational factors and BSL (H1)

H1a was supported, which was consistent with the previous discussion of Grover [39]Zhou and Li [72], and Haro‐Domínguez et al. [73]. Proactive technological orientation is one of the prerequisite factors that encourages firms to adopt new technology and, as a result, increases adaptive capability in a competitive business environment. The study claims that a strong IT internal infrastructure, engagement in strategic IS planning, proactive technological policies, and aggressive management enable firms to combine their business systems in order to operate a supply chain. Thus, a proactive technologically orientated firm is more willing to adopt new technology, find new ways to improve processes for supply chain partners, and ultimately leverage its business systems with collaborative supply chain members.

Consistent with the ideas of Dubey et al. [43] and [37], H1b suggests that IT connectivity facilitates BSL implementation. That is, firms with high IT connectivity have higher readiness in terms of IT capability, which facilitates conjoining of business systems to the supply chain. In other words, when firms tend to combine their business systems, this makes the systems more visible to other business partners and increases interactions with both the business partners and the market environment. When the interaction experiences are smooth and favorable, the firms also feel that it is easier and more comfortable to implement BSL. Therefore, IT connectivity is a critical factor when two companies are trying to combine their business systems to leverage the capabilities of supply chain members.

Surprisingly, H1c was not supported by the study. The results went against the concepts presented in Jacobs et al. [38] and Zsidisin et al. [46] suggesting that high levels of internal communication foster cooperation, cohesiveness, and coordination among diferent business functions. However, in practice, BSL is diferent from internal IT integration across a firm’s departments. In fact, it is a form of external IT integration among business partners. The current study suggests that a solid internal communication mechanism promotes mutual trust and understanding within a firm, which helps support internal IT integra tion [46]. However, implementation of BSL is another story. Member of firms are typically limited to using email or internal networking sites for communication, to convey information, and receive work orders, but in the case of BSL, they need more knowledge and skills to deal with the information exchanges among partners related to operating the business system. Moreover, a firm’s internal communication system and business systems may be operated independently, which develop based on the firm’s needs. If this is the situation, system compatibility may be another issue that needs to be discussed in future studies.

## 5.2. Inter-organizational factor and BSL (H2)

Consistent with previous studies [53,76], H2a was supported. Firms are eager to work closely with business partners to avoid being replaced by other competitors. Companies integrate their business systems to obtain the mutual benefits derived from cooperation. Collaborative companies normally use the same systems in trading and information sharing in order to reduce transaction costs and gain competitive advantage. Therefore, it is suggested that when other partners have integrated their business systems into the supply chain, the focal firm will feel pressure to follow in order to obtain the same competitive advantage as that of the other partners; otherwise, the firm will be isolated and may be eliminated by the market.

H2b was also supported as consistent with the concepts of Hoppner et al. [58]Jelodar et al. [57], and Zhang et al. [56]. Developing a trust filled, honest, and committed relationship among business partners is important for long-term business cooperation. High relationship quality among business partners means they will not engage in opportunism in the business collaboration and will, therefore, feel more relaxed about conjoining their business systems with the supply chain for further collaboration. This study confirms that long-term, trusting relationships among firms are a key driver of electronic integration.

## 5.3. BSL and information sharing (H3)

H3 was supported. This result was similar to that discussed in Du et al. [78]Maiga et al. [24], and [5]. BSL enables firms to collaborate closely with their business partners and recombines the information in the supply chain. Therefore, BSL not only increases information quality through sharing but also increases the flow of information across the chain, which enables firms to share on-time information more frequently with partners and to quickly respond to market changes. Moreover, with a high level of systems integration, firms have a standard operating procedure to share information, thus reducing paperwork and communication expenses among partners. Thus, BSL increases information sharing among the members of a supply chain.

## 5.4. Information sharing and supply chain performance (H4)

Increases in information sharing quality and frequency enable firms to improve supply chain performance. Thus, H4 was supported. The results were similar to those of Chang et al. [62]Kochan et al. [63], and Chang and Wong [17]. Sharing manufacturing and replenishment schedule information among collaborating firms can improve supply chain activity coordination, material requirements, planning roles, and the assumption of responsibilities [8]. When more information is shared among firms, suppliers, and customers, it increases accessibility, accuracy, the relevence to the supply chain, and the frequency of up dating, leading to better and faster decisions.

## 5.5. BSL and supply chain performance (H5)

H5 was supported, which was consistent with the discussions in Chengalur-Smith et al. [8]Maiga et al. [24], and Vijayasarathy [69]. When firms use BSL to execute their supply chains, they can coordinate their internal and external supply chain activities and derive important operational and financial business benefits. Moreover, BSL enables firms to recombine market information and obtain real-time information with regard to customer demand, inventory, new product development, production, and finance, thus leading to better performance. Thus, BSL has direct impact on supply chain performance.

## 5.6. Moderating efects of process innovation and uncertainty (H6 & H7)

This study found support for positive moderating efects of process innovation on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance (H6), which is consistent with the results of Vijayasarathy [69]. The result implies that firms should concentrate on developing new processes and/or re-engineering existing processes in their supply chains because the positive impact of BSL on supply chain performance often requires new processes to be established or existing processes to be re-engineered in order to facilitate more eficient and efective information and production flows among supply chain partners.

Moreover, the post-hoc analysis showed that uncertainty (H7) was partially supported by the study, which was slightly diferent from the results of Vijayasarathy [69]. In practice, demand and supply uncertainty are caused by delayed supplier deliveries, fluctuations in market demand, and product planning adjustments. Uncertainty will spread through the supply chain in the form of amplification of order or demand variability, which may lead to an excess of safety stock, increased logistical costs, and ineficient usage of resources. Demand and supply uncertainty will lead to a phenomenon called the “bullwhip efect,” in which the variability of an upstream member’s demand is greater than that of a downstream member [63]. Although BSL can enhance information sharing and reduce some uncertainty, companies experiencing high demand and supply uncertainty will have poorer supply chain performance. Technology is now changing too fast to predict, and companies, thus, are sufering from greater technological uncertainty. Even though BSL can enhance information sharing among partners, it cannot overcome the uncertainty associated with rapid technological changes. This may explain why demand and supply uncertainty have a negative moderating efect on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, while technological uncertainty has no moderating efect.

## 6. Conclusions and recommendations

While some studies have reviewed the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, little is known about why companies are willing to adopt BSL and what the benefits of doing so are. This study examined the antecedents, consequences, and outcomes of BSL adoption in relation to supply chains. Organizational and inter-organiza tional factors were treated as the antecedents for facilitating BSL. BSL was hypothesized to enhance information sharing and supply chain performance, while process innovation and uncertainty were expected to moderate the relation between BSL and performance. The theoretical and managerial implications as well as limitations and future research directions based on this work are presented as follows.

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

First, this study used BSL to represent the combination of busines systems. In Chengalur-Smith et al. [8], BSL only referred to the GE

Operations Global VMI (GEOPS) and only the relationship between BSL and business performance was examined. This study extended the range to cover all enterprise business systems, including the SC systems, ISs, and so on. It was found that BSL could improve both information sharing and supply chain performance. Therefore, companies should work to combine business systems with their supply chain partners in order to enjoy the benefits that are available from doing so. Moreover, a field survey and empirical testing of the research model were adopted in this study, which distinguishes it from previous studies [20–22]. Our findings provide a foundation for future research and enrich the related literature, thus extending examinations of the antecedents and benefits of BSL on firm’s performance.

Second, three organizational factors (proactive technological orientation, IT connectivity, and internal communication) and two interorganizational factors (supply chain member pressure and member relationship quality) were proposed in the current study as antecedents of BSL implementation. These factors have not previously been discussed in the BSL context. For instance, proactive technological orientation is discussed in CIOS [39], IT connectivity has been discussed in the supply chain [43], internal communication has been discussed in terms of IT integration [38], supply chain member pressure has been discussed in IOS [52], and member relationship quality has been discussed in the B2B business environment [55]. The current work showed that only internal communication has no efect on BSL implementation. Thus, this study provides some further understanding of the organizational and inter-organizational factors that enable and facilitate BSL implementation, which also enriches the related literature. Future research can further discuss these factors to estimate the impacts of systems integration or technology development in diferent contexts.

Third, this study suggests that BSL fosters information sharing among business partners, which distinguishes it from the study of Chengalur-Smith et al. [8], which examined both information sharing and BSL-related business benefits. Their study argued that BSL leads to further integration of business systems with the supply chain that triggers more collaboration with business partners. Therefore, a lot of new information will be recombined and shared. The scale for rating information sharing is also diferent between the current study (quality and frequency) and previous studies (information to be shared). Moreover, the Sobel test further demonstrated that information sharing plays a partial mediating role between the relations of BSL to supply chain performance. This result also provided some insights to future study and enriched the related literature.

Last, the use of process innovation in this study as a moderator between BSL and supply chain performance is also a new approach, unlike previous studies examining the direct efect of BSL on firm’s performance [26,27]. The study results show that process innovation has a moderating efect on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, which creates a new direction for future investigations. In addition, many previous studies used uncertainty as a moderator (e.g. [69],), for which the current work found some new results. This study showed that demand and supply uncertainty have a moderating efect on the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, while no significant moderating efect was found for technological uncertainty.

## 6.2. Managerial implications

From the aspects of management, first, this study confirms that proactive technological orientation and IT connectivity are critical factors for implementing BSL. Thus, firm’s managers are suggested to pay attention to forming teams intended to manage technology, to difuse new technology across their departments, to enhance their IT infrastructure, and to establish a proactive technology policy. Firms should also demonstrate their readiness to cope with changes in the dynamic business environment. Even if a firm’s scope is small, it still needs aggressive management with regard to technological development, as this will enable the firm to have greater opportunities to leverage its business systems with supply chain partners and thus enhance competitive advantage.

Second, supply chain member pressure and relationship quality exhibited a positive relationship with the extent of BSL. This study suggests that firms should maintain or strengthen their relationships with their supply chain partners through engaging in supply chain conferences and holding business or entertainment activities. This could be a method to understand other members’ methods for doing business. Companies should also form teams to manage their relationships with partners, while maintaining good product quality, providing great customer service, and being honest with supply chain partners to increase their credibility. Consequently, companies that build high le vels of trust, faith, and commitment among their supply chain partners will achieve a greater extent of BSL and, thus, better supply chain performance.

Third, this study found that process innovation positively moderates the relationship between BSL and supply chain performance, leading to the following suggestions for improving company utility and eficiency with regard to BSL: Companies should frequently upgrade or renew their business processes, such as manufacturing processes, logistical processes, and so on. By doing so, process operations will be more efficient, the chances of innovation will rise, and costs will be reduced. The utility of BSL will increase, leading to better supply chain perfor mance.

Finally, this study suggests that companies should work to decrease demand uncertainty through carrying out market surveys, purchasing related information from market research companies, or using business systems to track sales, profits, and inventory turnover. With regard to supply uncertainty, companies can obtain more real-time information about their supply chain partners through BSL. In addition, companies should select suppliers carefully and should continuously monitor the quality of the goods provided by suppliers, because such actions can reduce supply uncertainty.

## 6.3. Limitations and directions for future research

Some research limitations are worth mentioning. First, the respondents were mostly staf (57%) ranging in age 20–30 years (43%). Although, a filter item was set by asking about their familiarity with the supply chain situation in their firms, future studies should further limit the respondents’ seniority and work experience in the firm to obtain more accurate study results. Moreover, it is also suggested that data could be collected more evenly at each management level in order to get more varied opinions.

Second, the study limits the number of organizational factors (proactive technological orientation, IT connectivity, and internal communication) and inter-organizational factors (supply chain member pressure and member relationship quality). Future studies should consider other factors, such as system compatibility [10,12], organizational culture [14], or management support [13] to lead to a more comprehensive discussion of the related issues.

Finally, dificulties were encountered in the definition of proces innovation because new processes are frequently created and updated. Future researchers may, thus, consider interviewing employees in order to obtain their opinions about process innovation, as this may then lead to a more accurate definition of this term.
