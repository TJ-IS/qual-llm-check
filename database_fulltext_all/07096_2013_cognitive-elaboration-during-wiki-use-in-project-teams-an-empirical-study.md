# Cognitive elaboration during wiki use in project teams: An empirical study

Yixiang Zhang <sup>a,</sup>⁎, Yulin Fang <sup>b</sup>, Kwok-Kee Wei <sup>b</sup>, Wei He <sup>c</sup>

<sup>a</sup> School of Management and Economics, Beijing Institute of Technology, Beijing, PR China

## a r t i c l e i n f o

Keywords: Knowledge integration Cognitive elaboration Task involvement Critical norm Process accountability Task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity

## a b s t r a c t

Increasing research efforts attempt to understand how wikis can be used to improve team performance. Previous studies have mainly focused on the effect of the quantity of wiki use on wiki content quality in wiki-based communities. Our study focuses on the quality aspect of wiki use in a team context. We develop a construct, cognitive elaboration during wiki use, and explore its nomological network in the team context. Integrating literature on wiki and distributed cognition, we propose that cognitive elaboration during wiki use in<sup>fl</sup>uences team performance (i.e., wiki content quality) through knowledge integration among team members. We likewise identify its team-based antecedents, including task involvement, critical norm, task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, time pressure, and process accountability by drawing on the motivated information processing literature. Furthermore, we empirically test the research model using multiple-source survey data collected from 46 wiki-based student project teams. The theoretical and practical implications of our <sup>fi</sup>ndings are subsequently discussed.

## 1. Introduction

Contemporary teams use collaborative technologies to enhance group work and achieve superior team performance [2]. Wiki, an emerging Web 2.0 collaborative technology that enables members to engage in collective editing and knowledge co-creation [32], is deployed as a popular information technology (IT) tool in supporting a variety of collective content building [32]. Numerous enterprises, such as Adobe, IBM, and Sun Microsystems, have been increasingly using wikis to facilitate collective content building [11]. However, the deployment of wikis in teams does not guarantee that such teams can build satisfactory wiki content to serve team goals. Given the crucial role of wiki in supporting a variety of content building, exerting greater research efforts is important to understand how wikis can be used to collectively build high-quality content.

Literature review indicates that our existing knowledge of wiki use and performance has been contextualized mainly in wiki-based communities (e.g., Wikipedia). For example, according to Wilkinson and Huberman [55], high-quality Wikipedia articles can be attributed to a larger number of edits. For Kittur and Kraut [28], the number of editors improves Wikipedia article quality. Although previous literature improves our knowledge of wiki, the main focus was on the quantity of wiki use to explain performance. Thus, a deeper understanding of the underlying factors of wiki-usage behavior that ultimately contributes to the effective content building using wiki is necessary. We propose that studying the quality aspect of wiki use provides a more in-depth explanation of the quality of wiki content building by a team. We believe that previous studies have neglected this important issue.

The main objective of this study is to <sup>fi</sup>ll in the research gap by investigating the quality aspect of wiki use. We speci<sup>fi</sup>cally address such aspect by developing a construct called cognitive elaboration during wiki use and examining its antecedents and team performance consequences (i.e., wiki content quality). In this paper, we identify wiki content quality as the major indicator of team performance in wiki-based project teams that use wiki for collective content building, as such endeavor is the de facto practice in these teams [32]. Wiki content quality refers to the quality of knowledge manifested in the form of wiki content that team members collectively contribute. Cognitive elaboration re<sup>fl</sup>ects the mental efforts that people spend in processing relevant information [10,37]. We propose that the level of cognitive elaboration expended in using wikis would in<sup>fl</sup>uence wiki content quality in teams using wiki for collective content building. We speci<sup>fi</sup>cally propose that cognitive elaboration during wiki use affects wiki content quality by enabling knowledge integration among team members. Furthermore, we explore its team-based antecedents by drawing on the motivated information processing literature from social psychology. In developing this construct and exploring its nomological network in the team context, our study contributes to literature by uncovering an important aspect of wiki use in the team setting.

In the following sections, we review the relevant literature on wikis, after which we provide the theoretical background of the study. We then present the research model and develop the hypotheses. Thereafter, we describe the research methodology, analyze the data, and report the hypotheses testing results. Finally, we discuss our research <sup>fi</sup>ndings and its contribution to research and practice.

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. Wiki research

Wikis represent a new collaborative technology that has been increasingly used to achieve various business goals [33]. A wiki is de-<sup>fi</sup>ned as “a set of linked Web pages created incrementally by a group of collaborating users” [53]. Two major technological features distinguish a wiki from other IT tools. First, unlike other widely used collaborative technologies, wiki is distinct in its ability to support “open editing” [26], a function through which everyone can concurrently edit the same wiki page. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, each edit of a wiki page may improve the content to a certain extent, and any inaccurate edits can be followed up or corrected by other users. Over time, the crowd can improve the content of wiki pages, an effect viewed as the “wisdom of crowds” [46]. Second, wiki possesses the function of “edit preservation” [26], that is, each edit to a wiki page is recorded in a history page. As such, visiting previous edits or rolling back to any previous versions is easy [26]. Each wiki page has a history page that contains its history of edits, including who edited what and when.

As an emerging form of collaborative technology, wiki has been widely used in supporting collective content building works [33], thereby giving rise to the need to develop a better understanding of wiki use and its performance outcomes in a collective setting. After carefully reviewing the existing wiki literature, we determine that prior research mainly studied the relationship between the quantity of edits and the quality of Wikipedia articles [55]. The quantity of edits can positively in<sup>fl</sup>uence article quality; however, “edit wars” may occur if editors have many con<sup>fl</sup>icts and do not communicate well [55]. Edit wars may generate large quantity of wiki edits, but improved article quality does not necessarily follow.

This paper focuses on wiki-based teams that use wiki technology as a primary IT platform for collective content building. Such wiki-based teams differ from wiki communities in two important ways. First, only a limited number of people in a team (authorized users) can view and edit the wiki content. Second, teams using wiki to accomplish shared tasks have a relatively clear goal and timeline: they may need to <sup>fi</sup>nish their content building before a deadline. In this case, they may not have as much time as in public wiki scenarios to allow a large number of content iterations. Unlike Wikipedia where errors can be corrected through continuous iterations, errors in team-based wikis, if any, should be corrected before the designated deadline. Such limitation requires team members to be more careful and exert greater efforts to produce high quality edits when editing the wiki. If team members make numerous edits without thoughtful consideration, then these low quality and mindless edits may eventually damage their team performance.

Thus, we postulate that the quality aspect of wiki use plays an important role in in<sup>fl</sup>uencing wiki content quality of such wiki-based teams. For example, team members may have different or unique opinions toward the same issues, leading them to add different content on the same wiki page. If they ignore the writings of others, the resulting wiki content would be chaotic and incoherent, re<sup>fl</sup>ecting fragmented pieces of different writings of people. However, if they insist on their own opinions and delete the input of others without deliberate evaluation, then such disregard might hinder effective content building. Thus, people should exert more cognitive efforts to understand the current wiki content in any improvement attempt and to achieve effective content co-creation. Team members can then determine the best means to add their own understanding in improving the wiki, based on their understanding of the current wiki content.

Unfortunately, existing literature on wiki-based communities or teams has not yet systematically investigated the quality aspect of wiki use; this is an important research gap to address. In the following section, we conceptualize this quality aspect of wiki use by developing the notion of cognitive elaboration and appropriating it in the context of team-based wiki use of collective content building.

## 2.2. Motivated information processing theory

Cognitive elaboration refers to the extent to which people think about and cognitively process issue-relevant information [10,37]. This concept is a construct that re<sup>fl</sup>ects the varying degrees of cognitive efforts that people exert when processing received information. Individuals have a high level of cognitive elaboration if they thoughtfully process and scrutinize different pieces of relevant information to understand and assimilate it, and if they try to draw conclusions based on the systematic processing and relational consideration of all relevant information [13]. On the contrary, a low level of cognitive elaboration occurs if people super<sup>fi</sup>cially process information, such as ignoring relevant information or merely accepting information without cognitive processing.

While cognitive elaboration appears to focus on the individual cognitive process, the motivated information processing literature [12,13,16,17], which originates from the dual-process model [8], provides a useful lens for us to understand this construct in an interpersonal context. Drawing on the concept of cognitive elaboration, the motivated information processing literature focuses on studying how information is processed and exchanged between two individuals in negotiations to achieve mutual agreement [16]. This type of interpersonal behavior is in<sup>fl</sup>uenced by cognitive limitations and biases, e.g., the negotiators could assume that their own thoughts and those of others are quite different [16]. These cognitive limitations and biases can hinder the achievement of integrative result. The premise underlying this line of research is that the greater care an individual exerts in exchanging and processing relevant information received from the other party, the more likely he or she will overcome human cognitive limitations and biases. In turn, this idea means that one will practice a higher level of cognitive elaboration that integrates the interests of both parties [16]. Such cognitive elaboration on the information exchanged within the dyad is underpinned by the “analytic and comprehensive treatment of judgment-relevant information” [8, p. 74]. Although different from negotiation, teamwork involves information processing and cognitive elaboration. Realizing that groups are information processors, researchers have recently begun to apply motivated information processing in a team context [13,18].

## 2.3. Cognitive elaboration during wiki use

Building on the notion of cognitive elaboration, we use the concept of cognitive elaboration during wiki use to determine the inherent cognitive effort that team members exert in elaborating and processing an issue-relevant information using wiki. The notion of cognitive elaboration during wiki use re<sup>fl</sup>ects the extent to which team members engage in cognitive elaboration using the wiki platform. Team members with high cognitive elaboration during wiki use would spend greater efforts in understanding and evaluating the current content presented in the wiki platform, determining their merits and shortcomings, and then considering ways to resolve shortcomings and improve the content using the wiki platform. On the contrary, members with low cognitive elaboration during wiki use may merely make their own edits without critically considering how these would relate to the existing wiki content.

Team members do not automatically engage in cognitive elaboration when working on wiki for collective content building. Moreover, the importance of cognitive elaboration during wiki use would not be clear unless we examine its relationship with wiki content quality. Thus, examining team-based factors that can in<sup>fl</sup>uence cognitive elaboration during wiki use is crucial, as well as the extent and process by which cognitive elaboration during wiki use affects wiki content quality. In the next section, we draw on the theoretical background discussed above to develop a research model that accounts for situational antecedents to a team's cognitive elaboration during wiki use and its performance consequence.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

Fig. 1 presents the research model. First, cognitive elaboration during wiki use is hypothesized to in<sup>fl</sup>uence wiki content quality through the mediation of team knowledge integration. Second, based on the literature review, process accountability, task involvement, time pressure, task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, and critical norm are hypothesized to in<sup>fl</sup>uence cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

## 3.1. Consequence of cognitive elaboration during wiki use

As discussed earlier, the primary outcome identi<sup>fi</sup>ed in the motivated information processing literature is the achievement of integrative agreement on the negotiated terms [16]. However, literature does not provide an adequate explanation of mechanisms through which such integrative outcomes are collectively achieved. We refer to literature on distributed cognition advanced by Boland and Tenkasi [6] and draw on the notion of knowledge integration<sup>1</sup> [1] to explain how this integrative outcome is achieved. We argue that cognitive elaboration during wiki use can in<sup>fl</sup>uence wiki content quality through knowledge integration, which is “the synthesis of individuals' specialized knowledge into situation-speci<sup>fi</sup>c systemic knowledge” [1]. In the team context, knowledge integration refers to the synthesis of individually held information by team members, which is relevant to their collective task.

Boland and Tenkasi [6] suggested that the ability of individuals to create strong perspectives (i.e., perspective making) and the ability to consider the perspectives of others (i.e., perspective taking) are required to achieve a high level of knowledge integration in any group of people, such as an organization. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, organizations are distributed communities of knowing, wherein the focal knowledge domain is developed and reinforced through the process of perspective making and taking, an iterative process of developing knowledge and strengthening the knowledge domain within the communities [6]. Thus, to synthesize and reinforce the knowledge that a community possesses (i.e., knowledge integration), community members must not only re<sup>fl</sup>ect on, rationally analyze, and revise their own knowledge to <sup>fi</sup>t into the context; they should also know and analyze the knowledge of others, and combine external knowledge with their own to enrich and strengthen their knowledge domains. All these efforts pertaining to knowledge integration require individuals to engage in a deep cognitive elaboration on relevant information. Prior research has implied the importance of cognitive elaboration in in<sup>fl</sup>uencing knowledge integration by referring to the “thoughtful consideration” of knowledge [42] as an enabler to the process, by which people “appreciate and synergistically utilize” knowledge [6].

The role of cognitive elaboration in affecting knowledge integration can be appropriated in a team context. A team can be viewed as a collective distributed cognitive processing units, in which individual members autonomously create and exchange interpretations with other team members to achieve the team goal, based on the comprehensive understanding of their own interpretations and those of others (i.e., a form of knowledge integration) [7]. In this process, team members must create their own perspectives (e.g., express their own understanding and opinion), exchange ideas, and understand the perspectives of others. Individually held information can be gradually integrated through this cyclic process, ultimately leading to a joint solution that combines the effort and understanding of multiple team members [7].

In the context of wiki-based teams, members can greatly facilitate their cognitive elaboration of relevant knowledge by leveraging the distinct wiki features discussed earlier to help them create their own perspectives and consider those of their teammates. By using wiki, team members can examine, discuss, and correct certain individually held knowledge and understanding that may be problematic when appropriated to the speci<sup>fi</sup>c project context. They may also use wiki to keep track of and resolve con<sup>fl</sup>icts among the sets of knowledge that different members hold. When team members engage in a high level of cognitive elaboration through careful processing and analysis of wiki content, they are more likely to understand the content, <sup>fi</sup>nd similarities and differences among their perspectives and those of their team members, spot possible errors, address existing con<sup>fl</sup>icts, and eventually integrate their own knowledge with that contributed by other members. All these processes can be explicated through the wiki platform. Thus, we believe that cognitive elaboration during wiki use, which facilitates explicit perspective making and perspective taking, positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences knowledge integration.

Knowledge integration, a process essential for the successful accomplishment of content building, can, in turn, improve wiki content quality because it helps leverage and synthesize dispersed knowledge [48]. A reasonable argument is that the integration of knowledge of team members can contribute to content quality on the wiki platform, achieved through an effective synthesis and consolidation of the knowledge shared and contributed by different team members. Previous studies have presented that knowledge integration can contribute to a superior team performance [25,42,48]. Taken together, we have the following hypothesis:

H1. Cognitive elaboration during wiki use positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences content quality through the mediation of knowledge integration.

## 3.2. Antecedents of cognitive elaboration during wiki use

The motivated information processing literature has examined factors in<sup>fl</sup>uencing the motivation of individuals to engage in information processing [13,16]. Based on their <sup>fi</sup>ndings, the extent to which individuals are motivated to engage in cognitive elaboration of issue-relevant information can be triggered by situational cues (i.e., contextual cues that individuals encounter) [13,16]. Through a thorough review of the motivated information processing literature in the interpersonal context, <sup>fi</sup>ve situational cues that can be extended to the team context are identi<sup>fi</sup>ed as follows: task involvement, process accountability, time pressure, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. These <sup>fi</sup>ve factors re<sup>fl</sup>ect the following key project team characteristics: time characteristic (time pressure), project member characteristic (task involvement), work process (process accountability and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity), and group norm (critical norm). These aspects may be seen as encompassing major dimensions of group work that relates to motivated information processing [13,44].

## 3.2.1. Task involvement

Team members work together to achieve goals by accomplishing a series of collective tasks. Thus, task involvement that captures the involvement of team members toward their team task is an important factor that may in<sup>fl</sup>uence the cognitive elaboration of team members. Task involvement is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as the extent to which team members are involved with the group task, as well as view it as important and interesting [57]. The effect of such involvement on cognitive elaboration is discussed, but not tested, in the motivated information processing literature. Previous literature provided several supporting ideas for the impact of involvement on individual cognitive elaboration of information. For example, according to Park and Lee [36], individuals tend to engage in a thoughtful processing of product information when they have a high product involvement. According to psychological research, individuals judge the credibility of another individual based on the processing of more information when they have a high task involvement [40]. Furthermore, De Dreu et al. [16] argued that people are more motivated to process information when they are highly involved with the task.

Extending this concept into the team context, we believe that the task involvement of a team can promote its cognitive elaboration during wiki use. When team members are highly involved with the group task, they are more likely to have a strong desire to be accurate in completing the task. Team members need to consider all perspectives, collect more task-related knowledge, and engage in deep and thoughtful cognitive elaboration of relevant information to achieve the said goal. They can accomplish these tasks using the wiki technology to facilitate their cognitive processing effort. Highly involved team members tend to exert more effort and energy in cognitive elaboration to ful<sup>fi</sup>ll their inner desire for accuracy in the project, and therefore, are more likely to engage wiki (and its functions) to facilitate their cognitive elaboration process. Thus, a high level of task involvement is expected to lead to a high-level cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

H2. Task involvement positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

## 3.2.2. Process accountability

Once teams are built and tasks are assigned, managers may set rules to monitor the group work process. Process accountability, de<sup>fi</sup>ned as the expectation that one may be called on to explain and justify his/ her working processes [30], re<sup>fl</sup>ects an important team rule. This factor is included to examine whether it has an effect on the current wiki team context. With process accountability, group work and decision-making processes are observed and evaluated by others. Moreover, members may be requested to explain and justify their decisions [30]. Under high process accountability conditions, team members are more likely to be evaluated by others and be asked to justify their decisions. As a consequence, team members are more likely to become subject to potential criticisms (e.g., they may be criticized when they cannot explain or justify their work and decision-making processes) [15,30]. In this situation, team members are more likely to engage in thoughtful cognitive elaboration, consider more decision alternatives, and gain an accurate understanding of the issue to better justify their work processes and avoid potential criticisms [15,45]. By contrast, when process accountability is low and team members are not required to justify their decision-making and work processes, they are more likely to jump to conclusions without thoughtful consideration regarding relevant information [17].

Through laboratory experiments, previous studies determined that process accountability increases the thoughtful information processing of an individual in the context of negotiation [15,17] and the thoughtful information processing of the group in group decision making [44]. In the context of wiki-based teams, process accountability is considered able to motivate team members to engage in cognitive elaboration using the wiki platform. Therefore, we hypothesize that process accountability in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

H3. Process accountability positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

## 3.2.3. Time pressure

Timeline is another important parameter de<sup>fi</sup>ning team tasks, particularly for project-based teams. During a work process, teams may work under time pressure to <sup>fi</sup>nish team tasks. Time pressure can in-<sup>fl</sup>uence team processes and outcomes [50]. Through experiments, researchers have likewise determined that time pressure can reduce thoughtful information processing [12,52].

We believe that the time pressure that a team faces can reduce cognitive elaboration during wiki use within the team. With high time pressure, team members are less likely to engage in cognitive elaboration, which is time-consuming and requires numerous mental activities. Instead, team members tend to be close-minded and jump to conclusions using heuristics because they think doing so saves time and <sup>fi</sup>nishes the task quickly [12,52]. Thus, we expect that the extent of using wiki for cognitive elaboration would be severely reduced in this situation. However, under low time pressure, team members may feel that they have suf<sup>fi</sup>cient time to conduct a project. In this case, they are more likely to take time and engage in cognitive elaboration by leveraging wiki technology. Therefore, we arrive at the following hypothesis:

H4. Time pressure negatively in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

## 3.2.4. Critical norm

Group norm is important in a team context [23]. This concept pertains to “a standard or rule that is accepted by members of the group as applying to themselves and other group members, prescribing appropriate thought and behavior within the group” [39]. Critical norm is a team norm that values and supports deviance and critical thoughts [39], serving as an important regulatory function that can guide individual behavior in small groups. In a team with high critical norm, independence and critical thoughts are promoted and valued. Team members likewise tend to access and validate information critically and independently based on certain objective standards [39].

In a team with a high critical norm, team members tend to critically and rationally evaluate information that other members provide [39]. Similarly, when working on a project using wiki, team members under high critical norm are more likely to think critically, appreciate and evaluate the knowledge that others share, as well as analyze and assimilate such knowledge based on thoughtful information processing. As such, they are more likely to leverage wiki technology to facilitate their cognitive elaboration process. Thus, critical norm is hypothesized to positively in<sup>fl</sup>uence cognitive elaboration during team work.

H5. Critical norm positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during team work.

## 3.2.5. Task reflexivity

Task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as “the extent to which team members overtly re<sup>fl</sup>ect upon the group's objectives, strategies, and processes, and adapt them to current or anticipated endogenous or environmental circumstances” [54]. The concept can capture the self-monitoring behavior through which team members conduct their work process and attempt to achieve their goals. Teams with high task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity often review their objectives during the work process, and members are open to using other means to improve the team operation. Through an experiment, van Ginkel et al. [51] determined that team re<sup>fl</sup>exivity can increase the understanding of team members of the importance of information elaboration in group decision making. The better team members understand the importance of cognitive elaboration, the more likely they would engage in cognitive elaboration. Similarly, albeit without empirical veri<sup>fi</sup>cation, De Dreu [13] argues that task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity can motivate team members to systematically process information in teams, hence resulting in increased cognitive elaboration. In the wiki context, we likewise propose that teams tend to use wiki to review the work in progress, thoughtfully re<sup>fl</sup>ect and carefully improve the work if they exhibit a high level of task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

H6. Task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during team work.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Instrument development

The survey method was used to collect data for model testing. The instruments for measuring the latent variables in the model were <sup>fi</sup>rst developed. Where available and to enhance validity, the measurement instruments of the constructs were adapted from previous literature. Appendix A presents the measurement items and their sources. An initial item pool containing relevant items used in literature was generated through literature review. The items were adapted to our research context by modifying the questions. To validate the adapted survey items, individual meetings with university colleagues and Ph.D. students were held to discuss the appropriateness of and possible ambiguities in the questionnaire items, as well as the appearance and layout of the questionnaire. Based on the feedback, a revised questionnaire was developed, printed, and sent to <sup>fi</sup>ve students for pre-testing.

Knowledge integration was measured using items from Tiwana and McLean [48]. Cognitive elaboration during wiki use was measured using the items adapted from De Dreu [12] after revising the wordings to <sup>fi</sup>t the current research context. Items related to time pressure were also adapted from De Dreu [12]. Items related to critical norm were adopted from Postmes et al. [39], whereas those measuring process accountability were developed based on several studies [14,22,44,58] that conducted laboratory experiments and provided manipulation check questions to measure process accountability. Process accountability measures were developed based on these manipulation check questions. Items measuring task involvement were adopted from the personal involvement inventory of Zaichkowsky [57], and items measuring task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity were adopted from the work of Tjosvold, Tang, and West [49].

## 4.2. Empirical setting

Data were collected from students enrolled in a large university in Hong Kong. A total of 202 business school undergraduate students attended a course on business applications of information technologies. To accomplish a team project that would contribute to a good portion of their <sup>fi</sup>nal course grade, the students were randomly grouped, with each group having four to six team members. Individual differences and pre-existing social relationships between individuals, which might compound the explanation of knowledge integration, were naturally controlled by random assignment.

This context is appropriate for our study for several reasons. First, to obtain superior project performance, the team members needed to develop the required professional knowledge on their own, leverage specialized knowledge from other members, and integrate these properly. The teams were requested, to investigate the IT applications of a speci<sup>fi</sup>ed organization (e.g., a university, a <sup>fi</sup>rm, or other organizations). They were required to choose an organization and one of its IT applications, and then evaluate the effectiveness of that speci<sup>fi</sup>c IT application (e.g., the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts or possible problems associated with the IT application). They were further requested to propose possible solutions to the problems they have identi<sup>fi</sup>ed. To ful<sup>fi</sup>ll the individual course requirement, each student was supposed to develop expertise in a certain aspect related to the project, such as insights to the focal organization, related business domain, or speci<sup>fi</sup>c technology, and then contribute to the team using such expertise.

Second, these students were required to build their team report on a wiki platform. Each team has a wiki site where they can openly co-edit their team's wiki pages. Each wiki page has a history page, which allows the function of “edit preservation.” A tutorial regarding its usage was provided to the students to improve their familiarity with the wiki system after the group tasks have been assigned. Private wikis<sup>2</sup> for each group were created before the tutorial. Their lecturer has access (e.g., viewing and editing) to all wikis of all teams. All teams were provided the same wiki environment to control technological variance on cognitive elaboration.

Third, although we randomized the demographic and experiential factors for each team, and the team assignment was the same for everyone, the key variables in our study were expected to vary. Specifically, the team members were provided high autonomy in terms of determining work division and their approach to the project. Thus, the situation cues they would experience, such as task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, critical norm, task involvement, and time pressure as a function of remaining time over the remaining amount of work to be completed, were likewise expected to vary.

## 4.3. Data collection

Survey data at two different times and objective team performance data at the third time point were collected to test the research model. Given the project speci<sup>fi</sup>cation mentioned in the previous section, the lecturer divided the project period into three stages. Teams were asked to submit a project deliverable in each stage. Two surveys were conducted at two different times. At the end of the 3rd week after the wiki training tutorial, teams completed and submitted their <sup>fi</sup>rst progress report. The <sup>fi</sup>rst survey was then conducted to collect data on task involvement, time pressure, process accountability, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. This process provided members with suf<sup>fi</sup>cient time to develop a stable perception of team-level situational cues. Three weeks after the <sup>fi</sup>rst survey, teams submitted the second progress report, and the second survey was conducted to collect data on cognitive elaboration during wiki use and knowledge integration. This temporal design is also consistent with the causality chain that we used to interpret the subjects under investigation, enabling causality reasoning that the situational cues motivate information-processing mechanism in a team [20].

Printed questionnaires were sent to all the students taking the course. Before the survey, these students were informed that their <sup>fi</sup>lled-in questionnaires would be kept con<sup>fi</sup>dential and would only be used as an aggregate base for the research. To motivate participation in the survey, the students were told that those who would participate in both rounds of surveys would be given <sup>fi</sup>ve bonus course marks. In each survey, 202 printed questionnaires were distributed. A total of 138 usable samples were collected from the students who participated in both surveys, resulting in an overall response rate of 68%. The respondents belonged to 46 different teams, comprising a group-level sample size comparable with those reported in previous team-level studies [25,42]. In addition, it exceeds the minimal “<sup>fi</sup>ve-times” rule of thumb offered by Tabachnick and Fidell [47] (in our model, <sup>fi</sup>ve paths direct to “wiki-cognitive elaboration”, requiring 25 samples to estimate). Although Chin [9] suggested <sup>fi</sup>ve-times as a weak rule of thumb, and 10-times would be stronger, they similarly provided an extreme example of Wold [56], who analyzed 27 variables using two latent constructs with only 10 cases.

On average, the valid sample of each team is three, with a standard deviation of 0.82. With respect of gender, 45.7% of them are female and 49.3% are male. Gender information of the remaining 5% is missing. With respect to year in the undergraduate program, 67.4% of them are in <sup>fi</sup>rst year, 24.6 of them are in second year, 1.5% are in the third year, and 0.7% are in fourth year. Information on the remaining 5.8% is missing.

Objective data on the quality of wiki content were obtained from the tutor's rating. After the students submitted their <sup>fi</sup>nal reports, the tutor evaluated and scored the report of each team. The rating criteria were based on the quality, creativity, consistency, and completeness of the team report. The mean value of team <sup>fi</sup>nal performance rating is 12.1 (out of the full mark of 15), with a standard deviation of 1.27. We controlled for the number of edits in the research model by counting the number of history pages, with each history page representing one speci<sup>fi</sup>c version of edits (i.e., an average of 40 edits per team).

## 5. Data analysis and results

Data were analyzed in three steps. First, analyses were conducted to justify data aggregation from the individual to the group level. Second, the measurement model was examined. Third, the structural model was assessed to test the research hypotheses. Partial least squares (PLS) is a powerful component-based method widely used in literature. In this paper, we used SmartPLS [41].

## 5.1. Data aggregation and multicollinearity

Individual-level data were aggregated to enable group-level analysis. Key indexes, including $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } } ,$ ICC(1) and ICC(2), were used to support data aggregation for the shared group-level constructs (i.e., knowledge integration, task involvement, time pressure, process accountability, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity) [29]. Shared team constructs “originate in experiences, attitudes, perceptions, values, cognitions, or behaviors that are held in common by the members of a team” [29]. Cognitive elaboration during wiki use was conceptualized as a con<sup>fi</sup>gural team construct that would re<sup>fl</sup>ect and “capture the array, pattern, or variability of individual characteristics within a team.” [29]. Individual team members may not have similar perceptions in con<sup>fi</sup>gural team constructs. Thus, the three indexes are not necessary to justify its aggregation.

Developed by James, Demaree, and Wolf, $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } }$ is a widely used within-group agreement index [24] that re<sup>fl</sup>ects the extent to which the data obtained from unit members represent a consensus within the unit (e.g., a team) [29]. The $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } }$ value is calculated by comparing the observed group variance to random variance. Using the method proposed by James and colleagues, $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } }$ values were calculated for the variables in each team. The following average values were calculated: knowledge integration (0.96), process accountability (0.95), task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity (0.92), critical norm (0.91), time pressure (0.82), and task involvement (0.85). All values are above the recommended value of 0.7 [29].

Aside from $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } } ,$ other researchers have also suggested the use of interclass correlations, namely, ICC(1) and ICC(2), to justify data aggregation [5]. ICC(1) re<sup>fl</sup>ects the proportion of total variance of a construct that is explained by the group membership, whereas ICC(2) re<sup>fl</sup>ects the reliability of group means. Both ICC(1) and ICC(2) can be calculated from the one-way random effect ANOVA model, with each variable as the dependent variable and group membership as the independent variable. Using the method outlined by Bliese [5], the following ICC(1) and ICC(2) values of the shared group variables were calculated: knowledge integration (0.23, 0.54), task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity (0.26, 0.58), time pressure (0.18, 0.47), critical norm (0.22, 0.53), process accountability (0.24, 0.56), and task involvement (0.36, 0.70). These ICC(1) and ICC(2) values are comparable with those reported in previous studies [31,43]. In addition, all the F-tests are signi<sup>fi</sup>cant (p b 0.05). In summary, the $\Gamma _ { \mathrm { W g } } ,$ ICC(1), and ICC(2) values support the data aggregation employed in this paper.

Multicollinearity among the <sup>fi</sup>ve antecedent variables was examined using the variance in<sup>fl</sup>ation factor (VIF) method. The VIF values range from 1.169 to 1.849, which are lower than the necessary threshold value [34]. Thus, multicollinearity is unlikely to be an issue in this study. We also tested the potential common method bias (CMB) issue between cognitive elaboration during wiki use and knowledge integration that are collected and tested at the same time. Harman's single-factor test [38] was used to test CMB. A principal comments analysis without rotation was performed on all the seven items, and two factors with eigenvalues larger than 1 were extracted, which indicates that common method bias is likewise of no major concern in this paper.

## 5.2. Measurement model

The measurement model was <sup>fi</sup>rst assessed to establish reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity of the key constructs. Construct reliability can be assessed by Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability. The recommended threshold value is 0.7. As shown in Table 1, all Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability values are greater than 0.7, which con<sup>fi</sup>rms the reliability of the measurement model used in this paper. The measurement model exhibits convergent validity. First, as shown in Table 1, the average variance (AVE) extracted from the constructs exceeds the recommended threshold value of 0.5 [21]. Second, all items load signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly on their corresponding constructs, with all loadings reaching values above 0.7. The measurement model likewise exhibits discriminant validity. The square root of the AVE for the latent variables is larger than its correlation with the other constructs (see Table 2). Besides, the cross-loadings are lower than the item loadings on their corresponding constructs. Thus, all the tests pass the necessary thresholds to establish the reliability and validity of the measurements.

## 5.3. Structural model

Fig. 2 presents the results of the structural model analysis. The model explains 21.7% of the variance in wiki content quality, 30.5% of the variance in knowledge integration, and 42.7% of the variance in cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

To test the mediating role of knowledge integration, we followed Baron and Kenny's approach [4]. Although the direct effect of cognitive elaboration during wiki use on content quality is not signi<sup>fi</sup>cant, the full mediation effect can still be established if the relationship between cognitive elaboration during wiki use and knowledge integration, as well as that between knowledge integration and content quality, are signi<sup>fi</sup>cant [27]. Considering that both relationships are signi<sup>fi</sup>cant (β = 0.54, p b 0.001, and β = 0.45, p b 0.01), Hypothesis 1 is supported. The hypotheses on the antecedents of cognitive elaboration during wiki use were similarly tested. Hypothesis 2, which states that task involvement in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use, is con<sup>fi</sup>rmed (β = 0.25, p b 0.05). The positive effect of the critical norm on cognitive elaboration during wiki use is likewise supported (β = 0.31, p b 0.05), thereby con<sup>fi</sup>rming Hypothesis 5. Hypothesis 6, which states that task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity in<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration during wiki use, is supported $( \beta = 0 . 3 0 , \rho < 0 . 0 5 )$ . However, we did not <sup>fi</sup>nd a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant impact on process accountability and time pressure on cognitive elaboration during wiki use. Thus, Hypotheses 3 and 4 are not supported. We also conducted mediation effect test, and found that cognitive elaboration during wiki use fully mediates the effect of task involvement on knowledge integration; in addition, it partially mediates the effect of critical norm and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity on knowledge integration. The control variable (number of edits) similarly shows interesting results and does not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly in<sup>fl</sup>uence knowledge integration and wiki content quality. The effects of team size on knowledge integration and wiki content quality are likewise not signi<sup>fi</sup>cant.

## 6. Discussion and implications

## 6.1. Discussion of results

The results of our study yield several interesting <sup>fi</sup>ndings. First, the current work helps us understand how a team's cognitive elaboration during wiki use affects the quality of its collective content building using the wiki platform. The results indicate that cognitive elaboration during wiki use does not directly in<sup>fl</sup>uence wiki content quality; instead, it in<sup>fl</sup>uences wiki content quality indirectly through the full mediation of knowledge integration. The number of edits does not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly in<sup>fl</sup>uence knowledge integration and wiki content quality. These results concur with our prior argument that it is the quality (cognitive elaboration during wiki use) aspect of wiki editing that matters to wiki content quality.

Second, we <sup>fi</sup>nd that cognitive elaboration during wiki use is positively associated with several key situation-speci<sup>fi</sup>c variables, including task involvement, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. Based on these <sup>fi</sup>ndings, when teams have high task involvement, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, they are more likely to achieve high level of cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

However, our study fails to <sup>fi</sup>nd a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant relationship between process accountability and cognitive elaboration during wiki use. This observation is not consistent with those reported in previous studies that have identi<sup>fi</sup>ed signi<sup>fi</sup>cant relationships [17,44]. The mixed results may be explained by the fact that the teams in the current study were not formally required to justify and explain their work processes as a mandatory component of the project requirements. In other words, the teams had great autonomy in managing their work. Although some teams might have felt the need to justify their work processes, they were not obligated to engage in cognitive elaboration. Teams with high process accountability might have chosen their believed “correct” work process. Thus, the work process they adopted may not highlight cognitive elaboration. In addition, no peer-review mechanism was adopted in this research, and the <sup>fi</sup>nal grade of individual students on the project would not count peer review. This setting may undermine the potential effect of process accountability, which might partially explain the insigni<sup>fi</sup>cant in<sup>fl</sup>uence of process accountability on cognitive elaboration during wiki use. Future research should consider the effect of peer review.

Meanwhile, the impact of time pressure on cognitive elaboration during wiki use is not supported. Our results show that time pressure does not in<sup>fl</sup>uence cognitive elaboration during wiki use, which is in consistent with the <sup>fi</sup>ndings in prior research that time pressure can reduce people's systematic information processing in the laboratory [12]. This observation may be explained by the fact that the team pro jects were relatively lengthy tasks, and team members had high autonomy in managing their time. Hence, time pressure in real life teams may not be so salient. This result differs from that reported in the context of laboratory experiments, wherein time is rather limited. Another possible explanation is the changes of time pressure perceived by team members. Time pressure measured in the <sup>fi</sup>rst survey might differ from that felt in the second survey, when cognitive elaboration was measured. However, we tested the relationship using the time pressure data collected in the second survey, and likewise found no signi<sup>fi</sup>cant impact on cognitive elaboration during wiki use. Thus, future research may be conducted to further examine the impact of time pressure on cognitive elaboration.

## 6.2. Theoretical implications

Our study provides several important contributions to literature. First, this study adds to our limited understanding of how wikis can be used for improving collective content building by developing the new construct of cognitive elaboration during wiki use and examining its impact on wiki content quality. This contribution is particularly highlighted by our theorizing effort to appropriate cognitive elaboration to the wiki context, which greatly advances wiki literature that simply focuses on the quantity aspect of wiki usage (e.g., edit and editor numbers) [28,55] and the effect of group composition and task con<sup>fl</sup>icts [3].

Second, this research adds to the understanding of the mechanism through which cognitive elaboration during wiki use impacts the wiki content quality of a team, by discovering the mediation of knowledge integration. This <sup>fi</sup>nding is important particularly in light of the result that without knowledge integration, cognitive elaboration during wiki use does not have a direct and immediate effect on content quality. Our study thus highlights the indispensable role of knowledge integration in translating the cognitive effort of team members to collective outcomes. In addition, our <sup>fi</sup>nding contributes to the team knowledge integration literature by providing a new antecedent (cognitive elaboration during wiki use), which complements previous studies on team knowledge integration using other perspectives, such as social capital [42,48], formal interventions [35], expertise availability, and heterogeneity [48]. Our study adds to this knowledge base by focusing on the quality aspect of knowledge work in teams.

Third, we make a novel contribution to IS literature by drawing on the motivated information processing theory [13,15,16] to identify the team-based situational antecedents of cognitive elaboration during wiki use. This theory, along with its situation-speci<sup>fi</sup>c antecedents, is relatively new in the IS <sup>fi</sup>eld. The explanatory power of the research model demonstrates the value of using this theory to predict cognitive elaboration during wiki use.

Finally, this research also contributes to motivated information processing theory, which originates from an interpersonal context, by extending its applicability to the team context. Most of previous studies used laboratory experiments to investigate information elaboration in the interpersonal context [12,17]. Our study extends its generalizability, with adaptation, to the team context.

## 6.3. Practical implications

Our study advises managers that the quality of team's wiki content can be improved by promoting knowledge integration and cognitive elaboration during wiki use. To achieve this, managers are advised to improve the task involvement, task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, and critical norm of team members. For instance, to increase their involvement, team members can be informed of the importance of their projects (e.g., how much pro<sup>fi</sup>t the project may bring in and how it is related to the strategic goals of their organization). The team leader or manager should periodically review the team's objectives and work processes to facilitate building task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, based on the evaluation of and re<sup>fl</sup>ections on past works, the leader or manager could correspondingly adapt the work processes to <sup>fi</sup>t the team goals. This adaptation can promote task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity, which in turn, promotes cognitive elaboration during wiki use. Finally, team managers could likewise implement rules that encourage and value critical thinking. This policy may enhance the critical norm of a team, which ultimately in-<sup>fl</sup>uences cognitive elaboration.

## 6.4. Limitations and future research

Several limitations and future research directions should be considered. First, student teams in a university were used as sources of data. Differences exist between student teams and professional teams in actual <sup>fi</sup>rms. Hence, caution must be observed when generalizing the present <sup>fi</sup>ndings to other contexts, such as wiki teams in real <sup>fi</sup>rms. Student teams were chosen because they allowed us to set up wikis for them and study their knowledge integration using this platform. In addition, in using student teams, collecting data at different times was relatively easy. The process also allowed us to employ causality reasoning. Future research may sample work teams in actual <sup>fi</sup>rms to test the research model.

Second, our data were collected from Hong Kong. Caution must be observed when generalizing the results to other areas or countries with different cultural traits. Eastern culture (e.g., Chinese culture) is characterized by collectivism, whereas Western culture (e.g., American culture) is characterized by individualism [19]. Cultural differences may in<sup>fl</sup>uence several relationships in our research model. For example, the impact of critical norm on cognitive elaboration during wiki use is possibly stronger for teams in China compared with those in America. Future research can study teams in Western countries and assess the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of cultural differences on the relationships.

Third, the distinct features of wiki technology were not integrated in our research model. Future research can consider incorporating such features in the research model and study how wikis can improve teamwork performance. Finally, variances may exist in wiki-based teams. For example, wiki-based teams could be a regular or a shortterm group work, as well as may or may not have a clear and rigid incentive system. Future research can examine our research model in different contexts.

## 7. Conclusion

Previous studies arming at explaining wiki content quality mostly focused on quantity aspect of wiki use in the context of wiki-based communities (e.g., Wikipedia), and failed to study the quality aspect of wiki use. In this paper, we develop a concept of cognitive elaboration during wiki use to study the quality aspect of wiki participation with regard to the content quality of team wiki. We argue that knowledge integration mediates the impact of cognitive elaboration during wiki use on wiki content quality. By adapting relevant situation-speci<sup>fi</sup>c antecedents to the team context, we also study its antecedents based on motivated information processing theory. The results of this paper indicate that cognitive elaboration during wiki use positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences wiki content quality through the mediating role of team knowledge integration. Furthermore, cognitive elaboration is positively associated with a team's task involvement, critical norm, and task re<sup>fl</sup>exivity. However, process accountability and time pressure do not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly impact cognitive elaboration during wiki use. Overall, the current <sup>fi</sup>ndings contribute to our understanding of how wikis can be used to achieve superior collective content building by project teams. We achieved this by focusing on the quality aspect of wiki participation by advancing a theory of cognitive elaboration during wiki use. This study likewise contributes to practice by providing suggestions on how to improve wiki team performance.
