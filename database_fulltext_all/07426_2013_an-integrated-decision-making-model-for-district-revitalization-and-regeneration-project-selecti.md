# An integrated decision making model for district revitalization and regeneration project selection

## a b s t r a c t

The main purpose of this research is to propose an effective hybrid process for evaluating district development directions concerning district revitalization and regeneration (DRAR) prospects along with simultaneous positive and negative conflict criteria and their interdependence Accordingly, the fuzzy Delphi method (FDM) the interpretive structural modeling (ISM), and the analytic network process (ANP) with bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks (BOCR) are integrated to construct a project selection model regarding the DRAR. A real case in Jiufen in Taiwan is studied using the proposed model to evaluate four feasible development directions, and the results verify the applicability of the novel MCDM method. This hybrid process can not only transform complex interaction of district reviving factors into simple quantitative evaluation, but its result can also be guidance for determining future development direction

## 1. Introduction

The contemporary urban development and management often focuses on innovative construction and district reviving simultaneously. Especially, with the consideration of local characteristics and cultural values, the new and progressive development can expand expedi tiously. A number of studies [14,15,22,46,47] show that manifesting local values and identities and creating renaissance in developed districts have become signi<sup>fi</sup>cant issues.

A district's sustainable development should not simply focus on external demolition and construction, but should also stress on manifesting internal local values and identities (revitalization), and further create renaissance and competitiveness of local development (regeneration). Consequently, the core of district revitalization and regeneration (DRAR) should reveal provincialism and continuity and stimulate new life and competitiveness [8,14,48]. Nevertheless, there are many complex in<sup>fl</sup>uence factors involved for DRAR, and the DRAR project evaluation and selection problems are multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) problems in nature. Since a suitable decision-making model is important for evaluating and selecting project alternatives, numerous methodologies have been developed and proposed over the last few decades [10].

Like many multiple criteria decision-making (MCDM) problems in real world, there are usually several tangible and intangible elements and favorable and unfavorable concerns that must be considered at the same time for DRAR project evaluation [54,80]. Complicated interdependence may exist in the problem with simultaneous interaction of positive or negative impacts, such as criteria in bene<sup>fi</sup>ts versus those in costs, and criteria in opportunities versus those in risks. It would simply make perplexity for DRAR project selection and decision.

In order to tackle the above-mentioned inevitable problem effectively, this paper proposed a systematic approach that integrates the fuzzy Delphi method (FDM), the interpretive structural modeling (ISM) and the analytic network process (ANP) with the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks (the BOCR merits). The FDM is a methodical procedure for evoking expert group opinion to sift the critical criteria from multitudinous possible impact factors [10,27,56]. The ISM is applied to clarify interdependencies among evaluation criteria [1,37,71]. The ANP with BOCR, proposed by Saaty [51], is a mathematically-based MCDM tool to deal with interdependent and multi-attribute problems. Especially, it can solve the positive and negative impacts of a problem simultaneously, and it has been applied in some recent works with outstanding outcomes [12,28,53].

In Taiwan, due to dense population with small land area and diverse immigrant societies, the district development has been saturated and possessed of the characteristics of various historical cultures. District reviving is highly related to the physical construction of traditional urban renewal [68]. Accordingly, developing arts and cultures, injecting commercial activities, and advancing tourisms and recreations have been the major development types in recent years. Nevertheless, local characteristics were usually neglected, and the resulted developments had a very high similarity and lack of uniqueness. In order to <sup>fi</sup>ll the vacancy, this paper studies Jiufen, a district with self-characteristics in Taiwan, to <sup>fi</sup>nd critical criteria of DRAR and construct an evaluation model to help the district in selecting the most suitable revitalization and regeneration project.

The objective of this paper is to propose a methodology for DRAR project selection problems that have interdependence property and simultaneous positive and negative impacts among evaluation criteria. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, this paper demonstrates how an integrated FDM, ISM and ANP with BOCR model can be used as an aid in DRAR project selection problems. An evaluation framework for project selection of DRAR is proposed, and the related importance weights of the critical criteria and alternatives are calculated. The results show that the model can transform complex positive or negative impacts and interrelationship into simple quantitative values for objective and effective evaluation. The empirical results also can be the consultation and guidance for practical project development selection in the future. This paper is organized as follows. DRAR and relevant project selection works are reviewed in Section 2. The integrated MCDM methodology is presented in Section 3. The proposed methodology is applied to a real case in Section 4. Discussions and conclusions are provided in the last section.

## 2. DRAR and project selection

In today's rapidly changing society and environment, revitalization and regeneration as a major source of competitive advantage for district development is widely accepted by practitioners, governments and academics [11,47,61,66]. The ideas of revitalization and regeneration have meant an introduction of broader ideas of environmental sustainability containing social dimension and localization targets [55].

Numerous previous works [14,16,42,48] have indicated that the aims of revitalization and regeneration are to manifest local environmental identities, promote inhabitant emotions, enhance regional belongingness, and stimulate local industrial animation and economic bene<sup>fi</sup>ts. In contrast with such developments and tendencies, Mese [43] proposed the concept of revitalization planning, which uses regional resources to change current situation and to leap toward new objectives. Yang [76] presented the local revitalization approach, which adopts integrated local development strategies to promote district socio-economic revivi<sup>fi</sup>cation and to infuse new vitality into the area. Kuo et al. [30] de<sup>fi</sup>ned district revitalization as integrating and manipulating the inherent characteristic resources of district to promote localized industrial development and life quality, and further to highlight the existent orientation and value of district.

In addition to the implementation of the above-mentioned concepts, benign interaction among inhabitants, professionals, and governments is necessary to advance DRAR [31]. In fact, the DRAR project is a series of actions designed to accomplish DRAR goals such as improvement in economic, physical, social, and environmental conditions of an area that has been subject to change [78]. In the DRAR problem, there exist many complex in<sup>fl</sup>uence factors such as planning and operating, existing culture patterns, inhabitant' consciousness, living interaction, and so on, and these factors have simultaneous positive and negative impacts. For example, Wagner et al. [64] stated that a successful local revitalization planning consists of strong public leadership, well-focused planning notions, ability to respond to traumatic events, existing community characteristics, and good relationship among levels of government. Besides, the culture-led regeneration also can be deployed by locales to advance a new development and also to liven up a decayed local area [20]. Wang [65] proposed that a culture-led regeneration should be shaped distinctively by conjoining the heritage conservation of archaic buildings, the reinforced widespread use of cultural symbols, and urban regime. Other relevant studies can refer to Aravot [3], Çevik et al. [8], Helleman and Wassenberg [24], Raco [47], Wang and Wu [69], and Wedding and Crawford-Brown [74], etc. To sum up, a DRAR project is a systematic program developed to reconstruct the district spatial structure, improve its infrastructure, and foster its natural functions [78]. Moreover, it has special characteristics such as high risks, long time return, and large proportion of intangible costs and bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, and these make the process of decisions very dif<sup>fi</sup>cult and the direction of the oncoming development very uncertain. Because the revitalization and regeneration of a district involves many complex situations and in-<sup>fl</sup>uence factors, the DRAR project evaluation and selection is a MCDM problem.

In general, the issues related to project evaluation and project selection have been discussed in various <sup>fi</sup>elds such as quality management, research and development, environmental management, and district development. Decision makers have to gather information from various sources about relevant alternatives, and evaluate these alternatives against each other or some set of criteria through appropriate methods [6,33]. Different methods have been proposed to help make good project selection decisions [10]. The existing methods for project evaluation and selection range from single criterion cost/bene<sup>fi</sup>t analysis to multiple criteria scoring models and ranking methods, subjective committee evaluation [75], or mathematical programming. Overall, the methodologies frequently used in the past include ranking technique [7], balanced scorecard [4], analytic hierarchy process (AHP) [13,26], analytic network process [23,63], technique for order preference by similarity to ideal solution (TOPSIS) [45], data envelopment analysis (DEA) [29], multivariable analysis method [40], dynamic programming [32], goal programming [5], etc. These studies unanimously demonstrate the necessity and importance for a careful assessment of different development directions to overcome the dif<sup>fi</sup>culties of project selection.

A review of current related DRAR models and evaluation methods shows that most past works applied economic concept, environmental impact assessment (EIA) and multiple criteria decision making (MCDM). For example, Mazzanti [41] adopted the microeconomic concept to treat cultural heritages as multi-attribute goods, and applied conjoint analysis techniques to perform the evaluation. Dutta et al. [16] based on the economic point of view and adopted the contingent valuation method (CVM). Tobit regression model was used to understand the determinants of the willingness to pay, and the results were used to quantify the total economic value (TEV) of the heritage sites. EIA has been applied abundantly in many cities and areas in Europe [57]. For example, Tweed and Sutherland [60] applied EIA in the sustainable development of historical areas by integrating the satisfaction of human needs and different people's perceptions of and attitudes to urban historical areas. MCDM has been used in many DRAR works. Aravot [3] used post occupancy evaluation (POE) to generalize the values of the environmental characteristics, and applied statistics and Logit to analyze the quality and suitability of local revitalization. Thomas [58] used geographic information system (GIS) to develop the evaluation criteria for area development, and applied decision support systems (DSS) to evaluate the new developments. Doratli et al. [15] used strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats (SWOT) to determine the key criteria of historic urban quarters and devised an appropriate revitalization strategy. Other recent works include Zavadskas and Antucheviciene [79] and Dutta and Husain [17].

Based on the above analysis, we can see that most of current models and evaluation methods have some de<sup>fi</sup>ciencies. First, the types of evaluation criteria can be very limited, for example, only based on the economic aspect, or very subjective. Even for an objective categorization such as SWOT analysis, the results of the key criteria under the four aspects cannot be integrated together to provide a <sup>fi</sup>nal ranking of the alternatives. Second, the selection of criteria is generally based on literature review, and there is no systematic scheme for the selecting process. Third, the positive and negative impacts [50] and the interrelationships [73] among the factors or criteria are not considered. In addition, the above-mentioned methods cannot be applied to problems that need to consider resource feasibility, optimization requirement, or criteria interdependences. Therefore, an integrated decision making model is proposed in this paper to tackle the above problems. ANP, a general form of AHP, can adequately incorporate interdependent relations among components, represent and analyze interactions, and synthesize their mutual effects using a single logical procedure [10,67]. In addition to solving interdependent problems effectively, the BOCR merits, proposed by Saaty [51], can appropriately conduct simultaneous favorable and unfavorable concerns of problems. Consequently, the advantage of the ANP with BOCR over other MCDMs is the former's ability to combine mutual dependence as well as opposing impact criteria, especially when the subjective evaluations of different individuals are important for project selection.

To summarize, the proposed model has the following attributes. First, the criteria are categorized under the BOCR merits so that both the positive and negative impacts of each alternative can be assessed and an overall rating of the alternative can be generated. Second, numerous criteria can be listed under each merit, and FDM can be applied to achieve a consensus among experts in the selection of the important criteria. Third, while most other methods neglect the interrelationship among the criteria and assume independence among them, ISM is used to understand the interrelationship so that a better evaluation can be carried out later. Finally, the evaluation model using ANP with BOCR can not only integrate the <sup>fi</sup>rst three attributes together, but can also provide an objective process for evaluating and ranking the alternatives by incorporating the expertise of the professionals.

## 3.1. Fuzzy Delphi method (FDM)

Since its conceptual development by Dalkey and Helmer in 1963, the Delphi method has been used in a wide range of research applications. Despite its merits, the method has ambiguity and uncertainty problems in survey questions and responses [9,56,75]. The incorporation of fuzzy set theory with Delphi method is one of the approaches to tackle the problems [10,37]. Murray et al. [44] <sup>fi</sup>rst applied the fuzzy theory to the traditional Delphi method. Ishikawa et al. [27] employed the cumulative frequency distribution function and the fuzzy integration to integrate experts' estimation into fuzzy numbers, and utilized the “gray zone”, the intersection of the fuzzy numbers, to develop the Max–Min FDM and the FDM via fuzzy integration (FDMFI).

This paper is based on the FDM developed by Ishikawa et al. [27] to <sup>fi</sup>nd the critical factors, and the procedures for executing the method are as follows [27,68]:

Step 1 Construct a table of cumulative frequency distribution, with $F _ { i } ^ { 1 } ( { \bf x } )$ being a function that denotes the period of realization with an extremely high degree of possibility, and $F _ { \mathrm { i } } ^ { 2 } ( \mathbf { x } )$ being a function that denotes the period of non-realization with an extremely high degree of possibility.

## 3.2. Interpretive structural modeling (ISM)

Interpretive structural modeling (ISM), proposed by War<sup>fi</sup>eld $[ 7 1 , 7 2 ]$ , is often used to provide fundamental understanding of complex situations and to put together a course of action for solving a problem [37]. The method helps impose order and direction on the complexity of relationships among elements of a system. It is a suitable modeling technique for analyzing the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of one variable on other variables [1]. In this paper, ISM is applied to understand the interaction among criteria. Due to the fact that there may be an interrelationship among the criteria, ISM is applied to determine whether the interrelationship exists or not. Based on the results of ISM, an ANP with BOCR questionnaire can be prepared so that the questionnaire will not be unnecessarily lengthy. That is, if there is no relationship from one criterion to the other based on the ISM, a pairwise comparison question about the impact of the former criterion to the latter criterion will not be prepared. Thus, the length of the ANP with BOCR questionnaire can be reduced substantially.

The procedures of the binary matrix manipulation of ISM are as follows [25,37,70]:

Step 1 Prepare a questionnaire to understand the relationship among the criteria. A question such as “Does criterion e affect criterion $e _ { i } ? ^ { \mathfrak { n } }$ is asked. If the answer is yes, then $\pi _ { i j } = 1 ;$ ; otherwise, $\pi _ { i j } = 0 .$

Step 2 Establish relation matrix which shows the relationship among the criteria. The general form of the relation matrix D can be presented as follows:

$$
D = \begin{array}{c} e _ {1} \\ e _ {2} \\ \vdots \\ e _ {\mathrm{n}} \end{array} \left[ \begin{array}{c c c c} e _ {1} & e _ {2} & \ldots & e _ {\mathrm{n}} \\ 0 & \pi_ {1 2} & \ldots & \pi_ {1 n} \\ \pi_ {2 1} & 0 & \ldots & \pi_ {2 n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \pi_ {m 1} & \pi_ {m 2} & \ldots & 0 \end{array} \right]
$$

where $\pi _ { \mathrm { i j } }$ denotes the relation between the ith row and jth column criteria, if criterion $\mathsf { e } _ { \mathrm { i } }$ affects criterion $\mathrm { e _ { j } , }$ then $\pi _ { \mathrm { i j } } = 1$ otherwise $\pi _ { \mathrm { i j } } = 0$

Step 3 Calculate the reachability matrix as follows:

$$
M = D + I\tag{1}
$$

$$
M ^ {*} = M ^ {k} = M ^ {k + 1} k > 1\tag{2}
$$

where I is the identity matrix, k denotes the powers, and M<sup>∗</sup> is the reachability matrix. The reachability matrix is under the operators of the Boolean multiplication and addition (i.e., $1 \times 0 =$ $0 \times 1 = 0 , 1 + 0 = 0 + 1 = 1 )$

Step 4 Calculate the reachability set and the priority set, respectively.

$$
R (t _ {i}) = \left\{e _ {i} \mid m _ {j i} ^ {*} = 1 \right\}\tag{3}
$$

$$
A (t _ {i}) = \left\{e _ {i} \mid m _ {i j} ^ {*} = 1 \right\}\tag{4}
$$

Step 5 Determine the levels and relationships between the criteria by Eq. (5). The relationships of all the criteria and the network structure can be constructed.

## 3.3. Analytic network process (ANP) with BOCR

The bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks (the BOCR merits) is a concept of the analytic network process (ANP), proposed by Saaty [50]. It is a simple, mathematically based multi-criteria decisionmaking tool to deal with complex and multi-attribute problems. The ANP with BOCR has been applied in some works [10,21,38,59,62], and it can effectively solve the positive and negative impacts of problems at the same time. Under the BOCR, a network can consist of four sub-networks: bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks. Under bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and opportunities (risks and costs), pairwise comparison questions ask which alternative is most pro<sup>fi</sup>table or has the best chance (riskiest or costliest) under each criterion. Therefore, while the best alternative gets the highest priority for bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and opportunities subnet, the worst alternative gets the highest priority for costs and risks. Then, the weights of the alternatives under each sub-network can be calculated, and these weights are further combined to get a single outcome for each alternative [28,36].

The steps of ANP with BOCR are summarized as follows [19,34– 36,51]:

Step 1 Decompose the problem into a framework with a control hierarchy and a BOCR network. The control hierarchy contains the objective of the problem, strategic criteria, and the four merits, bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C), and risks (R). The BOCR network aims to achieve the goal with simultaneous consideration of the four merits. Under each merit, there are criteria and alternatives. The framework is shown in Fig. 3.

Step 2 Determine the priorities of the strategic criteria in the control hierarchy. The nine-point scale is applied to obtain pairwise comparison results of the importance of strategic criteria toward achieving the overall objective [49]. Arithmetic mean method is applied to aggregate experts' opinions.

Step 3 Based on the BOCR network, pairwise compare the criteria with respect to the same upper level merit, the interdependence among the criteria, and the performance of alternatives under each criterion by the nine-point scale.

Step 4 Calculate the relative priorities of the criteria with respect to the same upper level merit, the relative priorities of the interdependence among the criteria, and the relative priorities of the performance of alternatives under each criterion. The maximum eigenvalue, $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } ,$ and the eigenvector, w, for the matrix [49] is:

$$
\mathbf {A} ^ {*} \cdot w = \lambda_ {\max} \cdot w\tag{6}
$$

Check the consistency property of the matrix. The consistency index (CI) and consistency ratio (CR) are de<sup>fi</sup>ned as [49]:

$$
C I = \frac {\lambda_ {\max} - n}{n - 1}\tag{7}
$$

$$
C R = \frac {C I}{R I}\tag{8}
$$

where n is the number of factors being compared in the matrix, and RI is random index, the average consistency index of randomly generated pairwise comparison matrix of similar size [49]. When a CR value exceeds the threshold of 0.1, an inconsistent judgment is indicated, and the experts would need to revise the original values in the pairwise comparison matrix.

Step 5 Form a supermatrix for each sub-network by the ANP, which is proposed by Saaty [49]. Calculate the priority $( B _ { i } , O _ { i } , C _ { i } , R _ { i } )$ of alternative i under each merit.

Step 6 Determine the importance of bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks with respect to each strategic criterion. A <sup>fi</sup>ve-step scale is used, and the values of each scale is assigned to be very high, 0.42; high, 0.26; medium, 0.16; low, 0.10; and very low, 0.06 [19,51]. Arithmetic mean method is applied to aggregate experts' opinions.

Step 7 Determine the priorities $( b , o , c , r )$ of the merits. Calculate the priority of a merit by multiplying the priority of the respective strategic criterion from Step 2 with the score of a merit on each strategic criterion from Step 6, and summing up the calculated values for the merit.

Step 8 Calculate overall priorities of the alternatives by synthesizing the priority $( B _ { i } , O _ { i } , C _ { i } , R _ { i } )$ of each alternative under each merit from Step 5 with corresponding priorities of the merits (b, o, $c , r )$ from Step 7. There are several ways to combine the scores of each alternative under B, O, C and R [52].

## 4. Case study

The major goal of this research is to build a novel hybrid MCDM model for DRAR project selection. The research design contains three phases: select the critical criteria, determine the interdependences among the criteria, and evaluate and rank the alternatives.

## 4.1. Select the critical criteria

Based on extensive literature reviews of both international and domestic works, interview with relevant professional experts, consideration of local environmental nature and provincial developmental demands, this research <sup>fi</sup>rst generalized 30 possible impact factors (PIFs) for the DRAR project under the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C), and risks (R) merits. The experts were asked again to examine the appropriateness of these PIFS. In order to achieve the validity and practicality of evaluation, the PIFs are simple, understandable, and representative. There are 9, 7, 7, and 7 PIFs under the B, O, C, R merits, respectively (listed in Table 1).

Next, the FDM was applied to extract critical success criteria from the candidate list [68]. An anonymous FDM questionnaire was prepared, and 15 experts were invited to contribute their expertise to evaluate the importance of each PIF. These experts include architects, professional urban planners, urban designers, community constructers, and local development planners. They have general knowledge and expertise in the DRAR in Taiwan. A convergence of their opinions was obtained, and the 13 most decisive factors were extracted. Researchers usually select proper criteria according to the needs of the study while using FDM [56]. Basically, the threshold value is determined by the subjective conception of the decision makers, and it will directly in<sup>fl</sup>uence the number of factors screened out [18]. Hence, there is no simple way or universal rule to decide the threshold value [39], and it is not necessary to conduct the sensitivity analysis on the threshold settings. In this research, according to the practical requirements, the threshold values were subjectively set at 6.6, 7.1, 6.7, and 6.8 for the four merits B, O, C, and R, respectively. Based on the opinions of the experts and the authors, the threshold values were set arbitrarily so that at least 3 criteria were selected from each merit. The results are shown in Table 1, and the factors shaded in gray are selected.

The 13 extracted factors for the DRAR project under bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C), and risks (R) are described here. Under bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), the criteria are: external utilities of district regeneration $\left( \mathrm { E U D } ; \mathrm { C } _ { 1 } \right)$ , revitalization of district industries (RDI; $C _ { 2 } ) _ { }$ , and improvement of living environment (ILE; $C _ { 3 } ) .$ . The criteria under opportunities (O) are linkage of open space network system (LOS; C ), guidance of government planning $( \mathrm { G G P } ; \mathbb { C } _ { 5 } ) ,$ , and place marketing and strategy management (PMS; $C _ { 6 } )$ . The criteria under costs (C) are: ecology and landscape resource $\left( \mathrm { E L R } ; \mathrm { C } _ { 7 } \right)$ , conservation of cultural and historical prospect (CCH; $\mathsf { C } _ { 8 } )$ , expenditure of physical construction (EPC; C ), and integration and management of local resource $( \mathrm { I M L } ; { \mathrm { C } } _ { 1 0 } )$ . The criteria under risks (R) are: negative impact of government policy $\left( \mathrm { N I G } ; \mathrm { C } _ { 1 1 } \right)$ , ignorance of local residents' equity (IRE; $C _ { 1 2 } ) ,$ , and loss of existing district value and identity $( \mathrm { L E V } ; \mathbf { C } _ { 1 3 } ) .$ . In order to evaluate the performance of DRAR projects, this research constructed a BOCR network framework using the above-mentioned 13 critical success criteria.

## 4.3. Evaluation model and alternatives ranking

The evaluation model contains two parts, a control hierarchy and a BOCR network, as shown in Fig 5. During the decision process, bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C) and risks (R) may not have equal importance, and the control hierarchy aims to determine the priorities of these four merits. To achieve the goal of selecting the most suitable district revitalization and regeneration project, three strategic criteria must be considered: (1) to animate district assets effectively (represented as SC ), (2) to manifest and sustain local activities (SC ), and (3) to stimulate district sustainable development $\left( \mathsf { S C } _ { 3 } \right)$ . In achieving these strategic criteria, not only may some bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and opportunities be acquired, but some costs and risks may also be of result. Therefore, the priorities of bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C), and risks (R) can be calculated by solving the control hierarchy.

In the second part of the model, the BOCR network aims to calculate the priorities of the alternatives under each merit. While a district revitalization and regeneration project is being implemented, both positive and negative impacts will and may occur. Therefore, the network contains four sub-networks, i.e. bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (B), opportunities (O), costs (C), and risks (R) sub-networks. The results from FDM and ISM are used to construct these four sub-networks, as shown in Fig. 5 [68]. The expected performance of each project can be evaluated under each merit, and an overall performance of each project can be calculated afterwards. This comprehensive model can provide a systematic process for experts to evaluate both the positive and negative aspects of each project and to understand the interrelationship among the criteria. The expected performance of each project can be obtained quantitatively and justi<sup>fi</sup>ably.

The subject of the empirical study was Jiufen, which is located in the northeast of Taiwan. A mountain town in the Ruifang District of New Taipei City, Jiufen is encircled by mountains and on the sea front, and it possesses charming natural landscapes. Because of frequent wind and rain, the majority of buildings were built with stone walls and asphalt felts on roofs, and this becomes a special local image. In general, the fascinating district is shaped with three intersections. The four longitudinal and cross roads connect the mountainside settlement and thread the gold routes together for tourists. The longitudinal road is Shuqi Road, and the three cross roads from north to south are namely Qiche Road, Qingbian Road and Jishan Street. In addition, the buildings are surrounded by hills on one side and the heights of buildings are strewn at random. The paths run zigzag over the area and interact with dwellings and stores. All of these forms the whole settlement with self-style and interest. Formerly, Jiufen was renowned for its mining industry, and the entire area has developed from ostentatiously prosperousness to degeneration with the rise and decline of mining.

Nowadays, the town is a famous tourist attraction with plentiful mountain-town landscapes, mining museums, <sup>fi</sup>lm shooting, art works, localization af<sup>fl</sup>uence commercial activities, traditional settlement buildings and space formation, and distinct local arti<sup>fi</sup>cial scenery. However, the town is facing the predicament about how to select and determine an appropriate development direction for DRAR while preserving the existing resource features and living modes. There are four projects (denote $\mathsf { A } _ { 1 } - \mathsf { A } _ { 4 } )$ under evaluation for DRAR development: (1) mixeduse of residence and commerce $\left( \mathsf { A } _ { 1 } \right)$ , museums of local culture and tradition $\left( \mathsf { A } _ { 2 } \right)$ , local industrial and commercial circles $\left( \mathsf { A } _ { 3 } \right)$ , and development of tourist and recreational activities $\left( \mathsf { A } _ { 4 } \right)$

Based on the proposed model and experts' opinions, the performance of the four projects could be generated. In the project evaluation model, we invited <sup>fi</sup>ve experts to evaluate the district revitalization and regeneration projects in Jiufen. Unlike the 15 experts who have general knowledge and expertise in the DRAR in Taiwan but do not necessarily need to know Jiufen well, the experts in this stage possess DRAR professional knowledge and knew the historical development, regionalism and development demand of Jiufen well. Some of them had also participated in the planning and design of the district before. Their backgrounds include scholars in DRAR, policy planners for the government planning and development, professional planners, and architects. In the <sup>fi</sup>rst part of the model, the experts were asked to evaluate the priorities of bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs, and risks. After the consistency property of the pairwise comparison matrix was examined [49], <sup>fi</sup>ve experts' opinions were used for further analysis. For instance, a pairwise comparison matrix of expert 1 was formed (Table 3) to evaluate the three strategic criteria (SC). Since both consistency index (C.I.) and consistency ratio (C.R.) were lower than the threshold, the consistency test was passed. The arithmetic mean method was applied to synthesize experts' opinions. The integrated priorities of experts for the strategic criteria were calculated and were 0.5832, 0.1894 and 0.2273 for $\mathsf { S C } _ { 1 }$ ${ \mathsf { S C } } _ { 2 }$ and ${ \mathsf { S C } } _ { 3 } ,$ respectively (shown in Table 4). Therefore, the most important strategic criterion, based on the experts' opinions, is to animate district assets effectively (SC ). Strategic criteria are the sub-goals for achieving the ultimate goal, and such results indicate that experts believe that the most direct impact of DRAR should be the ef<sup>fi</sup>cient improvement of existing assets for revitalization and regeneration.

## 5. Conclusions

An integrated FDM, ISM and ANP evaluation model is constructed in this research for project selection of district revitalization and regeneration. Because human decision making process involves many complex in<sup>fl</sup>uence factors, which have simultaneous interaction of positive and negative impacts, the proposed model can help decision makers in the development project selection process by considering the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs and risks (BOCR) perspectives.

The implementation of an evaluation model on a case study is often done to examine the feasibility of the model [2]. In this study, the proposed integrated model was applied in a case study in Jiufen, Taiwan, to examine its practicality. The evaluation results are fed back to local residents and government policy planners, and the results are rather consistent with their opinions. This shows that this quantitative method is well constructed and has the ability to facilitate the evaluation process and generate appropriate solutions.

By applying the proposed model, decision makers in the district development can base on the results to examine the expected performance of the projects on various criteria, and can select the most appropriate project of revitalization and regeneration in the future. The network of ANP with BOCR is constructed based on literature review and interview with experts in the <sup>fi</sup>eld, and there are 13 extracted critical criteria under bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs and risks. In addition, the interdependent relationship among criteria also can be clari<sup>fi</sup>ed.

Based on the proposed model, the priorities for the four possible projects are calculated to generate the <sup>fi</sup>nal ranking. The relative performance (priorities) of each development direction (i.e. alternative) with respect to each criterion is provided distinctly. The results show the relative advantages and disadvantages of these possible development directions in detail, and the information can be used as future improvement references. The result of the empirical study shows that the ranking is identical under different synthesizing methods, and the project of local industrial and commercial circles (alternative $\mathsf { A } _ { 3 } )$ is the best development direction for Jiufen.

In the case study, the most important criteria, in descending order, are guidance of government planning, improvement of living environment, external utilities of district regeneration, and place marketing and strategy management. These four criteria belong to the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and opportunities merits, and this implies that the positive impacts of the development are stressed more in evaluating DRAR projects in Taiwan. In addition, when the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, opportunities, costs and risks merits are considered concurrently, improvement of living environment, guidance of government planning, conservation of cultural and historical prospect, and negative impact of government policy must be emphasized, respectively. The outcomes of these criteria may be important references for determining a suitable project for development.

To sum up, this paper sets up an objective and practicable project selection model for district revitalization and regeneration. The results show that the model can transform complex simultaneous positive and negative impacts/interrelationship and interdependence into simple quantitative values for objective and effective evaluation. The empirical process and results can not only provide an innovative evaluation pattern for district reviving, but can also be guidance for practical project development selection in the future.
