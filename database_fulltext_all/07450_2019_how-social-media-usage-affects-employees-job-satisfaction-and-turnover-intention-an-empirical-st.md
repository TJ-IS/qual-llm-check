# How social media usage a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ job satisfaction and turnover intention: An empirical study in China

Xin Zhang<sup>a</sup>, Liang Ma<sup>a</sup>, Bo Xu<sup>b,⁎</sup>, Feng Xu<sup>b</sup>

<sup>a</sup> Shandong University of Finance and Economics, Jinan, China

<sup>b</sup> School of Management, Fudan University, 670, Guoshun Road, Shanghai 200433, China

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Social media usage Employee engagement Organizational commitment Job satisfaction Turnover intention

## A B S T R A C T

Social media are frequently used in enterprises for both work-related and non-work-related (social) purposes. Drawing on the organizational commitment theory, we developed a research model to explore how di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage a<sup>f</sup>ect employees’ job satisfaction and turnover intention in the Chinese context. Online and o<sup>fl</sup>ine surveys were conducted in China, generating 298 valid responses for analysis. The results suggest that the following: (1) work-related and social-related social media usage positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees organizational commitment through their organizational engagement; (2) social media usage improves job satisfaction and reduces employees’ turnover intention through improving their engagement and organizational commitment; and (3) in the process of social media usage in<sup>fl</sup>uencing employees’ job satisfaction and turnove intention, employees of di<sup>f</sup>erent genders show signi<sup>fi</sup>cant di<sup>f</sup>erences.

## 1. Introduction

The advent of social media such as WeChat, QQ, and Micro-blog has changed the way individuals connect and communicate with each other within organizations [1,2]. Social media usage in organizations can contribute to internal knowledge management, social interaction, and the promotion of <sup>fi</sup>rm performance [3,4]. Although some people think social media in enterprises may also bring negative consequences and question their cost-e<sup>f</sup>ectiveness, more than two-thirds of companies plan to use social media in the next 12 months [5 7].

To date, social media research in enterprises predominantly focused on the following two aspects. First, some scholars have devoted their attention to exploring the in<sup>fl</sup>uential factors of social media usage, mainly including technology factors [1], organizational factors [8,9], environment framework [1], as well as personal factors [10]. Second, some scholars have examined the consequences of social media use in enterprises, including job satisfaction and job performance [3], relationship management [1], information searching, and sharing [11]. Although social media have been studied in enterprises, the following limitations remain: (1) As Parveen et al. [3] stated, it is critical to in vestigate the various purposes of social media usage and its impact on organizational performance. Most of the studies have not segmented social media usage; they have just studied social media use intensity or frequency for work [6,12]. Few scholars have studied social media usage from the perspective of both work and social purposes and explored their e<sup>f</sup>ect in organizations. (2) Some existing research examined the direct e<sup>f</sup>ect of social media usage on job satisfaction and job performance [10,13] but did not show the process or how di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage may have the impact. (3) Although multiple studies have attempted to identify the antecedents and consequences of social media use in enterprises [3,9], most of them treated employees as a homogeneous entity, ignoring potential group di<sup>f</sup>erences in social media usage and its organizational impact [14,15].

To narrow these research gaps, we drew on organizational commitment theory to investigate how di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage a<sup>f</sup>ect employees’ organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and turnover intention in the Chinese context. Thereafter, the model was empirically tested using survey data. Our research provides both theoretical and practical contributions. First, this paper has studied social media usage from the perspective of both work and social purposes and explored its e<sup>f</sup>ect on employees in organization. Second, we contribute to organizational commitment theory by clarifying the relationship between di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media use and organizational commitment and revealing its path process. Third, we also found that gender di<sup>f</sup>erences exist in social media usage’s e<sup>f</sup>ects on employees’ job satisfaction and turnover intention. Moreover, this paper provides practical implications on how to distribute the work between male and female employees; how to improve the engagement, organizational commitment and job satisfaction level; and how to re duce employees’ turnover through social media in enterprises.

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides the literature review. Section 3 describes the research model and develops hypotheses, followed by the method and analyses in Section 4. Section 5 discusses the research <sup>fi</sup>ndings, implications, and limitations.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Social media use in enterprises

The usage of social media in enterprises has attracted wide attention of scholars [6,16]. With regard to the purpose and dimension of social media usage in enterprises, most of the prior studies have been con ducted from a single perspective of social media use [3,9,17,18], or social media use for work [6,10,19]. For example, some articles studied social media use intensity [13] or social media use frequency for work [8]. Tajudeen et al. [1] studied social media use for information search, for marketing, for building customer relationship. And Tajvidi and Karami [20] indicated social media use may enhance a company’s marketing capabilities, thus improving <sup>fi</sup>rm performance. Social media can be used for work-related and social-related purposes by employees. However, few scholars have studied social media usage from the perspective of both work and social purposes and explored its organizational impact. As Parveen et al. [3] stated, di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage might lead to di<sup>f</sup>erent impact on organizational perfor mance. Therefore, it is necessary to study social media usage for both work and social purposes, and examine the results of di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage in organizations.

The review of previous literature on social media usage in en terprises showed that some scholars have explored the in<sup>fl</sup>uential fac tors of social media usage in enterprises [8,10], and other scholars have examined the e<sup>f</sup>ects of social media usage in enterprises [3,11]. In particular, organizational factors such as organizational rules, norms and policies, organizational type and size [8], the number of commu nication sta<sup>f</sup>, communication professionals’ strategic vision [13], personal factors such as perceived utilitarian and hedonic values [10], experience, employees’ perceptions of a workplace related to coworker support, supervisor support, and job-related demands are main factors that a<sup>f</sup>ect social media usage in enterprises [13]. The consequences of social media use in enterprises mainly focused on the impact of social media usage on relationship management [1], information searching and sharing [11], job satisfaction, and job performance [3]. However, the existing research mainly examined the direct e<sup>f</sup>ect of social media usage on job satisfaction and job performance [10,13] and did not reveal how the di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage have e<sup>f</sup>ect on employees. Employees’ satisfaction and turnover have great impact on organizational performance and are mostly concerned by enterprises. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the path process between different purposes of social media use and employees’ job satisfaction and turnover intention.

Finally, although multiple studies have attempted to identify the antecedents and consequences of social media use in enterprises [3,9], most of these studies treated employees as a homogeneous entity, ignoring potential group di<sup>f</sup>erences [14]. Past studies have identi<sup>fi</sup>ed signi<sup>fi</sup>cant di<sup>f</sup>erences in the patterns of IT use by men and women [21,22], suggesting that the outcomes of social media usage in enterprises may also di<sup>f</sup>er between male and female employees. It is important to study gender di<sup>f</sup>erences in employees’ engagement and outcomes of social media usage.

## 2.2. Organizational commitment theory

The concept of organizational commitment was <sup>fi</sup>rst proposed by Becker [30] in 1960, and it was de<sup>fi</sup>ned as a tendency to maintain “activity consistency” resulting from unilateral investment.

Organizational commitment is an important variable to re<sup>fl</sup>ect the important attitude of employees to the organization, and also an important predictor of employee behaviors. Porter et al. [31] indicated that organizational commitment is an emotional dependence of employees on organizations. Later, Meyer and Allen [32] conducted a comprehensive study of the organizational commitment scale, showing that it had at least three forms of commitment: emotional commitment, continuing commitment, and normative commitment, which has been recognized by most scholars. The theoretical model of organizational commitment includes the antecedents and outcome modes of organi zational commitment [33] and the role perception of organizational commitment [34]. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, Steers [33] held that the antecedents of organizational commitment mainly include personal characteristics, job characteristics, and work experience; the outcomes of organizational commitment mainly include retention, attendance, and job performance. The role perception model of organizational commitment believes that the psychological view and exchange view of organizational commitment does not fully consider the related factors of organizational membership or turnover [34]. Thus, a new role perception model is established to explain the retention intention and turnover intention of the core employees.

The existing research on organizational commitment mainly focused on two aspects: one is study of the concept, structure, and measurement, that is, the study of organizational commitment itself [35,36], and the other is research on the antecedents, related, and resulting variables of organizational commitment [37,38], which include job satisfaction, turnover intention, organizational culture, trust, etc. [39–41]. Job satisfaction and turnover intention are closely related to organizational commitment, which provides powerful explanation on employees satisfaction and retention [42]. Commitment is also ex plored largely in the IS domain [43,44]. For example, based on commitment theory, Chen et al. [45] indicated that a<sup>f</sup>ective and continuance commitment are the main drivers of users' content creation behaviors in SNSs, while normative commitment has little in<sup>fl</sup>uence on content creation behaviors. Wasko and Faraj [46] found that users commitment signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly a<sup>f</sup>ects their contribution to an online community. Bateman et al. [47] held that each form of community commitment has a unique impact on each behavior, with need-based commitment predicting thread reading, a<sup>f</sup>ect-based commitment predicting reply posting and moderating behaviors, and obligation-based commitment predicting only moderating behavior. Malhotra and Galletta [48] indicated that users may generate commitment to a system, which positively in<sup>fl</sup>uences their attitude and intention to use the system. Li et al. [49] found that users may have commitment to a website, which drives their stickiness to the website. However, few study explored the relationship between system usage and employees organizational commitment. Bala and Bhagwatwar [50] found that dispositions to job (i.e., job satisfaction and job security) and organization (i.e., organizational commitment and organizational trust) act as a dual role of antecedents and consequences of system use. Based on research on organizational commitment, this study investigates e<sup>f</sup>ect of employees’ social media usage of di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes on the formation of organizational commitment and their job satisfaction and turnover in tention.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

Organizational commitment results from employees’ investment in their role and in the organization [30]. Social media is an e<sup>f</sup>ective means for employees to communicate, cooperate, build, and maintain relationship with each other. With social media, employees would be more engaged in work and organizational activities, which leads to commitment to organization and, consequently, job satisfaction and retention.

## 3.1. Social media usage and employee engagement

Employee engagement is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as a distinct and unique construct that consists of cognitive, emotional, and behavioral components associated with individual role performance [51]. The two most dominant roles for most organizational members are their work role and their role as a member of an organization [52]. Therefore, employee engagement can be divided into job engagement and organizational engagement to re<sup>fl</sup>ect the extent to which an individual is psychologically present in a particular organizational role. A review of the prior research showed that factors such as job crafting [53], job hindrances and job resources [54], job demands [55], intra-organizational social connection [56], social capital, and job satisfaction [57] are considered important in a<sup>f</sup>ecting employee engagement. However, few scholars have examined the relationship between social media usage in enterprises and employee engagement. If there is a positive association between social media usage in enterprises and employee engagement, the role of social media usage in enterprises has greater clarity.

The theory of uses and grati<sup>fi</sup>cations suggests that users choose their own media to meet their speci<sup>fi</sup>c needs. Social media can be used in enterprises for both work-related and nonwork-related (social) purposes. In enterprises in China, social media, such as WeChat, are widely used for work-related purposes, for example setting up meetings with colleagues about work projects and sharing information about organi zational objectives, policies, and procedures. Social media are also used for social-related purposes, for example, setting up social events with co-workers after working hours, making friends within the organiza tion, and <sup>fi</sup>nding people with similar interests. Thus, social media usage in enterprises can be divided into work-related and social-related purposes [58]. Using 421 employees’ survey data, Zoonen et al. [6] found that social media usage for work is positively related to engagement through enhanced accessibility and e<sup>fi</sup>cient communication. This relationship was also proved by Drummond et al. [17] through a case study, which pointed out that Facebook and Twitter in<sup>fl</sup>uence en trepreneurial <sup>fi</sup>rms’ dyadic and network actor engagement. Generally, social media used for work-related and social-related purposes in enterprises might contribute to information accessibility and e<sup>fi</sup>cient communication [3], which will lead to employees’ higher job engagement and organizational engagement [58]. Based on the above, this paper puts forth the following hypotheses:

H1. Work-related social media usage positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ job engagement.

H2. Work-related social media usage positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees organizational engagement.

H3. Social-related social media usage positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ job engagement.

H4. Social-related social media usage positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees organizational engagement.

## 3.2. Employee engagement and organizational commitment

Organizational commitment di<sup>f</sup>ers from engagement in that it refers to a person’s attitude toward and attachment to his or her organization. Engagement is not an attitude but the degree to which an individual is attentive to and absorbed in the performance of his or her role [51]; the former is attitudinal, while the latter is behavioral. Commitment has been operationalized in di<sup>f</sup>erent ways. Meyer and Allen [32] proposed the three-component model, which states that commitment has three di<sup>f</sup>erent components that correspond with different psychological states, including a<sup>f</sup>ective commitment, continuance commitment, and normative commitment. Although the three types of commitment have been demonstrated to be powerful in predicting turnover, some scholars believed that a<sup>f</sup>ective commitment has stronger associations with a wider range of behaviors, compared to normative and continuance commitment, and suggested approaching organizational commitment as a single construct, which would help predicting various behaviors in general [59]. In this study, commitment is conceptualized as a single construct focusing on employee’s attitude to an organization and intention to continue the relationship with the organization.

The relationship between employee engagement and organizational commitment has been studied by many scholars in di<sup>f</sup>erent contexts. For example, by analyzing 352 hospitality employees, Jung and Yoon [60] found that employees’ job engagement positively a<sup>f</sup>ects their organizational commitment. This conclusion was also reached by Paek et al. [61], who provided meta-analytic evidence supporting the positive relation between engagement and organizational commitment. In this context, we also consider that employees’ organizational commit ment will increase with engagement. We suggest the following hypotheses:

H5. Job engagement positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ organizational commitment.

H6. Organizational engagement positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ organizational commitment.

## 3.3. Organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and turnover intention

Job satisfaction is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as the pleasant or positive emotional state resulting from employees’ work or work experience evaluation [62]. There are three main viewpoints about the relationship between organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and turnover intention: First, some scholars have pointed out that job satisfaction a<sup>f</sup>ects organizational commitment and thus employees’ turnover intention [63]; second, organizational commitment has an impact on turnover intention through job satisfaction [64]; and third, job satisfaction and or ganizational commitment are interrelated and both a<sup>f</sup>ect turnover intention [65]. Generally speaking, organizational commitment and job satisfaction are interrelated and exert a mutually related positive in-<sup>fl</sup>uence. When employees are satis<sup>fi</sup>ed with their job, they are often willing to continue to stay in the enterprise; at this time, their organi zational commitment is higher. When they decide to stay in the enterprise, they tend to be hardworking, which contributes to their satisfaction with their work. Empirical studies have con<sup>fi</sup>rmed that, when employees’ organizational commitment increases, their job satisfaction tends to increase [64,66], and employees’ turnover intention will decrease with an increase in their job satisfaction and organizational commitment [65,67]. Based on the above, the current research pro poses the following hypotheses:

H7. Organizational commitment positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ job satisfaction.

H8. Organizational commitment negatively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ turnover intention.

H9. Job satisfaction negatively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ turnover intention.

## 3.4. Employee gender diferences

According to the gender role theory, men and women tend to place di<sup>f</sup>erential importance on work and family roles because of the stereotypes associated with the roles that they occupy [68]. Men tend to place more emphasis on their work role than women, while women may place more emphasis on their family roles [69]. Considerable research has emphasized employee gender di<sup>f</sup>erences in attitude, risk perception, emotion, and behavioral intention [69,70]. However, they have generally been ignored in relation to social media usage in the enterprise domain. To our knowledge, no empirical study has directly addressed gender issues in the study of social media usage in the workplace. According to Akman et al. [71], employees’ gender has a positive impact on the average daily time spent on the use of the Internet for communication/e-mailing /chat and information access/ downloading/entertainment. Selvarajan et al. [69] pointed out that female temporary employees have more positive work-related attitudes toward the client organization. Based on the gender role theory, we hold that di<sup>f</sup>erences exist in employees’ engagement, commitment, and behavioral intention, as men and women tend to place di<sup>f</sup>erential importance on work and family roles. ## 4. Research method and data analyses

## 4.1. Data collection

A questionnaire was used to validate the conceptual model. The items that measured each variable were developed from the prior literature. Those measures are shown in Appendix A. All the items were measured with a seven-point Likert scale ranging from one (strongly disagree/unlikely) to seven (strongly agree/likely). In the <sup>fi</sup>rst part, we gave a brief introduction to the purpose of this study, and then the respondents answered questions about their social media usage, engagement, organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and turnover intention. In the second part, they were asked to provide their demographic characteristics, such as age, gender, and education, and their enterprise-related characteristics, such as the length of time since they entered the company, size of the company, position, department, fre quently used social media, etc.

A small-scale pilot test was conducted with three professors and <sup>fi</sup>ve master students to assess the questionnaire’s logical consistency, ease of understanding, and contextual relevance. From September 4th to September 12th, 2017, the distribution of questionnaires took place in two ways: a paper questionnaire and an electronic questionnaire. First, with the help of the Shandong Electronic Commerce Association, the electronic questionnaire link was issued to the enterprise department managers and human resource managers. They were then asked to forward the questionnaire link to their employees. A total of 194 electronic questionnaires were returned. At the same time, paper questionnaires were distributed in two research and development companies in Shandong Province, China. A total of 187 copies of paper questionnaires were returned (Table 1).

The total number of questionnaires obtained was 381, of which 83 incomplete and unquali<sup>fi</sup>ed questionnaires were deleted. We retained and used 298 valid questionnaires for this study, and the e<sup>f</sup>ective rate was 78.22%. Table 2 presents the descriptive statistics of the respondents, including their gender, age, education, and monthly

personal income.

Meanwhile, we also analyzed the enterprise-related characteristics of the respondents. As shown in Table 3, 31.21% of the respondents had entered the company more than 4 years before, accounting for the largest proportion of the sample, followed by the respondents who had entered the company less than 1 year before (28.52%). With regard to the size of company, 68.79% of the respondents stated that their company had more than 400 employees, representing the largest proportion of the sample. Meanwhile, the largest proportion of the sample reported being general clerks (74.83%), followed by operational managers (13.42%). Furthermore, 39.26% of the respondents worked in technology department, and 21.81% of the respondents were in marketing department. Finally, 92.28% of the respondents stated that they frequently used WeChat, 81.54% frequently used QQ, while 43.29% frequently used Micro-blog.

## 4.2. Measurement model

Partial least squares (PLS), rather than covariance-based structural equation modeling (SEM) was used to analyze the data. As a secondgeneration multivariate technique, PLS2.0 can simultaneously assess the measurement model and the structural model. Compared with covariance-based SEM, PLS requires a relatively small sample size, does not rely on the assumption of a normal distribution, and is more appropriate for exploratory analysis and for handling formative constructs. Thus, PLS was determined to be more suitable for this current research. Following the two-step analytical procedure [72], this research examines the measurement model and the structural model in that order.

The measurement model can be assessed by examining the reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, reliability can be assessed by determining Cronbach’s alpha, the composite reliability (CR), and the average variance extracted (AVE). According to the suggestions of Hair et al. [72], Cronbach’s alpha is acceptable when it exceeds 0.7, CR is acceptable when it exceeds 0.7, and AVE is acceptable when it exceeds 0.5. As shown in Table 4, Cronbach’s alphas for this study range from 0.707 to 0.913, all of which exceed the recommended value of 0.7. The CR ranges from 0.819 to 0.945, exceeding the recommended level of 0.70. The AVE ranges from 0.531 to 0.852, exceeding the recommended level of 0.50. Thus, the measurement constructs have high reliability. The convergent validity can be assessed using the factor loadings. As shown in Table 5, all of the factor loadings are higher than 0.6 at the signi<sup>fi</sup>cance level of p < 0.01, suggesting good convergent validity. Meanwhile, this paper examined the discriminant validity of the measurement items. As shown in Table 4, the square root of each factor’s AVE is larger than its corresponding correlation coe<sup>fi</sup>cients with other factors, indicating adequate discriminant validity.

To make sure that the dataset is free from common method bias, an inspection of Harman’s single-factor with seven constructs (WL, SL, JE, OE, OC, JS, TI) and 26 scale items was conducted. The statistical result in this respect indicated that no single factor was able to emerge; the highest covariance explained by one factor is 34.819%, which is less than the cut-o<sup>f</sup> value of 50%. Besides, common method bias can be assessed by examining the statistical signi<sup>fi</sup>cance of factor loadings of the method factor and comparing the variances of each observed indicator explained by its substantive construct and the method factor.

## 4.4. Moderating efect of gender

The moderating role can be tested by assessing the di<sup>f</sup>erences in the path coe<sup>fi</sup>cients for each subgroup [73]. To examine the di<sup>f</sup>erences in gender, path comparison testing was conducted between two groups. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we divided the sample into two groups: group 1 (male) contained 152 employees and group 2 (female) contained 146 employees. We <sup>fi</sup>rst tested hypotheses H1–H9 separately for the two groups. We then compared the path coe<sup>fi</sup>cients based on the method developed by Keil and Wassenaar [74], which is widely used in the IS context to test the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ects when the moderating variable is categorical [75,76]. The results are shown in Table 7. Based on the results of multigroup comparisons, gender di<sup>f</sup>erences exist; thus, hypothesis H10 was supported.

## 4.5. Mediating efect

We used the widely accepted bootstrap method to test the mediating e<sup>f</sup>ect of employee engagement and organizational commitment [77]. The PROCESS SPSS macro developed by Hayes was used to test mediation [78]. We used both the bias-corrected method and the percentile method to test the mediating e<sup>f</sup>ect, and the con<sup>fi</sup>dence level for the con<sup>fi</sup>dence intervals was 95%. Prebensen and Xie [78] suggested that when the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the statistic (indirect e<sup>f</sup>ect), as measured by the bias-corrected method or the percentile method, does not include zero, then the statistic has an intermediary e<sup>f</sup>ect. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, a partially mediated e<sup>f</sup>ect is present when the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the indirect e<sup>f</sup>ect does not include zero and the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the direct e<sup>f</sup>ect does not include zero. A fully mediated e<sup>f</sup>ect is present when the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the indirect e<sup>f</sup>ect does not include zero and the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the direct e<sup>f</sup>ect includes zero. The results are shown in Table 8.

It was interesting to <sup>fi</sup>nd that the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the indirect e<sup>f</sup>ect does not include zero, while the con<sup>fi</sup>dence interval of the direct e<sup>f</sup>ect does include zero. Thus, employees’ engagement, which includes job engagement and organizational engagement, acts as a full mediator between social media usage (including work-related and social-related) and organizational commitment. Organizational commitment acts as a full mediator between job engagement and job satisfaction. However, organizational commitment acts as a partial mediator between organizational engagement and job satisfaction. Finally, organizational commitment acts as a full mediator between employees’ engagement (job engagement and organizational engagement) and their turnover intention.

## 5. Discussion

This study aimed to investigate how di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes of social media usage a<sup>f</sup>ect employees’ organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and turnover intention in the Chinese context. In particular, social media usage was divided into work-related social media usage and social-related social media usage, and we examined their e<sup>f</sup>ect on employees’ engagement and organizational commitment as well as job satisfaction and turnover intention. It was interesting to <sup>fi</sup>nd that social media usage, which includes work-related social media usage and social-related social media usage, positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ engagement, which includes job engagement and organizational engagement. Moreover, employees’ organizational engagement positively a<sup>f</sup>ects their organizational commitment, while the e<sup>f</sup>ect of employees’ job engagement on their organizational commitment is not signi<sup>fi</sup>cant. Finally, employees’ organizational commitment positively a<sup>f</sup>ects their job satisfaction and negatively a<sup>f</sup>ects their turnover intention. Job satisfaction also negatively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ turnover intention.

## 5.1. Key findings

The <sup>fi</sup>rst interesting <sup>fi</sup>nding was that social media usage for di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes (work and social) positively a<sup>f</sup>ects employees’ engagement. Based on in-depth interviews, Drummond et al. [17] found that social media usage is positively related to engagement. This relationship was also proved by Zoonen et al. [6], who found that social media usage for work is positively related to engagement through enhanced accessi bility and e<sup>fi</sup>cient communication. This paper took a further step and found that both social-related and work-related social media usage in enterprises have a positive impact on employees’ job engagement and organizational engagement.

Second, this study found that employees’ organizational engagement positively a<sup>f</sup>ects their organizational commitment, while the effect of employees’ job engagement on organizational commitment is not signi<sup>fi</sup>cant. Employee engagement can be divided into job engagement and organizational engagement to re<sup>fl</sup>ect the extent to which an individual is psychologically present in a particular organizational role. The results of this paper con<sup>fi</sup>rmed the conclusion by Saks [51], who found that organizational engagement is a much stronger predictor of the outcomes, such as organizational commitment, than job engagement. However, the result showed that the e<sup>f</sup>ect of employees’ job engagement on their organizational commitment is not signi<sup>fi</sup>cant in our context. One of the possible reasons may be that job engagement re<sup>fl</sup>ects employee's input to the work, and organizational engagement re<sup>fl</sup>ects employee's input to the organization. Compared with job engagement, organizational engagement more directly increases bonding between employees and organization; thus, it often has a more obvious in<sup>fl</sup>uence on organizational commitment [51].

Finally, the results of this study suggest that male employees are more likely to generate job and organizational engagement than female employees under the same level of work-related and social-related social media usage. Male employees are more likely to generate organizational commitment through job engagement, while female employees are more likely to generate organizational commitment through organizational engagement. Furthermore, our results show that the in<sup>fl</sup>uence paths of male and female employees’ turnover intention are different. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, male employees are more likely to reduce turnover intention through job satisfaction, while female employees are more likely to reduce turnover intention through organizational commitment.

## 5.3. Practical implications

The results of this research provide some practical insights for enterprise managers and could help them weigh the usage of social media in enterprises. On the one hand, according to our research, social media can be used for di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes in enterprises, and these di<sup>f</sup>erent purposes lead to di<sup>f</sup>erent e<sup>f</sup>ects. Thus, when considering whether to ban social media usage in enterprises, managers should take into account the purpose of social media use. Furthermore, the role of social media should not be ignored as it will contribute to the dimensions of employees’ psychological conditions, such as engagement, commitment, and satisfaction. The results of this research also showed that social media usage in workplace might reduce employees’ turnover intention through employees’ engagement, organizational commitment, as well as job satisfaction. Thus, banning social media usage in the workplace blindly is not a wise move. Actually, social media fundamentally change the way in which people communicate, establish relationships, and share information. Thus, it is wise for enterprises to issue social media usage regulations to strengthen the management of social media usage, for example, issuing regulations to de<sup>fi</sup>ne the ac ceptable purposes of social media usage in the workplace.

On the other hand, enterprises should take into account the gender di<sup>f</sup>erences in the e<sup>f</sup>ect of social media usage. According to the results of this paper, male employees are more likely to generate job and organizational engagement than female employees under the same level of work-related and social-related social media usage. Thus, when considering employee’s engagement with their job and organizations, enterprises should encourage male employees to use social media more for work and social purpose, and assign di<sup>f</sup>erent jobs to them to achieve greater success. In addition, the results show that male employees are more likely to generate organizational commitment through job engagement, while female employees are more likely to generate organizational commitment through organizational engagement. Thus, enterprises should assign female employees more emotional job at or ganizational level and allocate more routine work to male employees. Furthermore, our results show that the in<sup>fl</sup>uence paths of male and female employees’ turnover intention are di<sup>f</sup>erent. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, male employees are more likely to reduce turnover intention through job satisfaction, while female employees are more likely to reduce turnover intention through organizational commitment. Thus, to retain employees, the human resources department of an enterprise should consider giving more emotional care to women employees and paying more attention to male employees’ satisfaction. Thus, this study may increase corporate awareness of social media and enable companies to make better use of social media for <sup>fi</sup>rm performance.

## 5.4. Limitations and future research

Although the <sup>fi</sup>ndings of this study are valid and valuable, the results should be approached with caution for several reasons. First and most importantly, the sample may not represent the whole population of social media users in enterprises. The sample was mainly collected from e-commerce companies and software companies in China. Because of cultural di<sup>f</sup>erences conclusion of this, study may not be generalized to employees from other countries. Second, this study used a survey method, in which data were collected from a single source, employees, by survey. Although we examined common method bias and the results showed that bias was not a major issue, future studies should use quantitative data to actually measure employees’ social media usage. Third, social media are widely used in daily life for both work and social purposes; future study can further subdivide social media usage. Finally, it would also be useful to examine the e<sup>f</sup>ect of social media usage on other aspects, such as employees’ in-role and extra-role performance in enterprises.
