# Market reaction to application service provider (ASP) adoption: An empirical investigation

Bong-Keun Jeong \*, Antonis C. Stylianou

Department of Business Information Systems & Operations Management, The Belk College of Business Administration, University of North Carolina at Charlotte, Charlotte, NC 28223 United States

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Article history: Received 5 June 2008 Received in revised form 20 June 2009 Accepted 29 September 2009 Available online 4 February 2010

Keywords: Application service provider (ASP) adoption Announcement Event study Market value

## A B S T R A C T

We examined the impact of ASP adoption on the market value of the firms and explored contextual factors in the positive abnormal returns. We employed event study methodology to analyze 268 ASP adoption announcements from 1998 to 2007. The results indicated that ASP adoption announcements were associated with positive increases in the market value of the firm. In addition, differences in market returns to ASP adoption were found to depend on strategic adoption intent, firm size, industry, and vendor status. These results provide useful implications for making decisions about whether, when, and how to adopt ASP to maximize the business value.

Published by Elsevier B.V.

## 1. Introduction

With the rapid advance of Internet technologies and the expansion of network infrastructure, the application service provider (ASP) has gained attention as a viable option in delivering software services. An ASP delivers and manages applications and computer-based services from a remote center to multiple customers via the Internet or a VPN [27]. In seeking improvements in IRM, many firms have begun considering its adoption. According to the Gartner Group, Software-As-A-Service (SaaS), which shares common themes with ASP and On-Demand Service, will deliver about 25 percent of all new business software by 2011. International Data Corp. also predicted that healthcare, professional services, banking, financial services, and even local government would soon be using ASP services. From a managerial point of view, the procurement of external software and services via ASP is promising. Benefits include a focus on core competencies, an ability to access a broader range of applications, scalability while maintaining flexibility during business environment changes, and less concern about a lack of in-house expertise (reduced need to attract and retain qualified workers).

However, the adoption rate of ASP services has been increasing at a modest pace. Furthermore, many service providers have failed to acquire a large client base and satisfy customer needs. Such a situation questions the viability of the ASP business model. To address the discrepancy between expected and realized benefits of ASP, some IS researchers have examined issues such as potential benefits and risks, relationships, and key success factors on ASP choice. However, there has been little research that empirically assessed the business impact of ASP adoption.

The purpose of our study was to examine the impact of ASP adoption on the market value of the firms. In particular, by employing an event study methodology, we empirically investigated how investors perceived and evaluated the announcements of ASP adoption. From the efficient market theory, we expected that if the market believes ASP adoption announcements have a positive effect, then the value should be reflected in the market price of the firm’s common stock. In addition to examining the impact of these announcements, we also explored contextual factors, such as firm size, industry sector, and vendor status. ASP is an emerging area where it is hard to capture the full spectrum of impacts, since most tangible and intangible benefits have not yet been proven. Thus, our study can provide an opportunity to understand the practical implications of ASP implementation, which can be helpful in making future ASP-related investment decisions.

## 2. Literature review

We review ASP adoption in two research streams: the literature regarding the ASP phenomenon, then various types of IT investment announcements and their impact on the market value of the firm.

## 2.1. ASP: emergence and evolution

Since the concept of ASP was introduced, it has experienced different levels of adoption success by organizations. In the beginning, the idea of consuming software applications like a utility (e.g., electricity) through the network was discussed by the media and industry analysts. ASP provisioning, mainly driven by start-up companies, was seen as a radical innovation that would change the entire course of software delivery.

However, most early ASPs provided diverse applications on various platforms. Thus, selecting the ASP that offered the best application for specific functions often required outsourcing each application to a different ASP, resulting in compatibility issues. In addition, the uncertainty surrounding the effect on existing business processes and several other problems, such as lack of customization, legitimacy concerns, and the dot.com crash caused failures of many ASPs. There were a few like Salesforce.com that had built a successful business, but generally the ASP industry was deemed a fad.

With enhanced value-added services and new business practices, many ASP firms have now found ways to address the challenges, and provided a second wave of ASP. First, the idea of an ASP aggregator was introduced; the aggregator (e.g., Jamcracker) was a third-party company that combined and offered a number of services or suites of products, usually from a set of partners. Since the aggregator offered a single point of access, management, and service support to its customers, this model was especially useful when there were a significant number of ASPs from which to choose [13]. Another noticeable trend was a shift from pure play start-up companies to alliances or partnerships in the mainstream of IT services. ASPs increasingly merged or were bought by established IT companies, such as Microsoft and IBM, in order to gain legitimacy while avoiding financial burden.

## 2.2. ASP research trends

Early research on ASP focused on describing the prospects of the model, and was exploratory in nature; the main issues typically included origins (selective outsourcing, application hosting, browser-based computing), benefits, risks, challenges and the future of ASP. As an outsourcing option, ASP adoption had potential to fill; the gaps between available and required IT resources and capabilities. One important debate surrounding ASP was the extent to which ASP was different from traditional IT outsourcing. If one considers ASP simply as a derivative of traditional IT outsourcing, the impact of adoption announcements can be understood in the context of IT outsourcing. However, ASP possesses unique characteristics that set it apart from traditional outsourcing. For instance, the ASP business model provides a one-to-many relationship model, where standardized or customized applications are offered to multiple customers across different sites, while traditional outsourcing is more likely to be that the customer has a one-to-one relationship with the supplier and the application is often tailored to the needs of that customer. Since most ASPs invest in IT infrastructure and amortize their investments over a large customer base, they are sensitive to sustaining a critical mass of customers for financial stability [25].

Building on the early work, some researchers attempted to explore strategic drivers and antecedent conditions in making ASP adoption decisions, typically using surveys and interviews to identify key success factors related to the adoption decision; diffusion-of-innovation theory, motivation-maturity model, transaction cost theory, resourced-based view, resource-dependent view, and a knowledge-based view were used to determine ASP choice. For instance, Jayatilaka et al. proposed an integrated model that combined four perspectives to determine significant factors in

ASP adoption. They argued that each theory offered unique insight into ASP choice, but each had its own limitations. Based on their propositions, they found 8 significant factors.

Another research stream analyzed complex relationships between client and provider, and identified various types of ASP arrangements, such as strategic alliance and partnership. For example, based on a social theory perspective, Swinarski et al. investigated whether the quality of a partnership and the client’s power had a significant effect on the service provider’s compliance, cooperation, and commitment; partnership quality and client power were shown to make the service provider comply with the contractual obligations, cooperate, and invest additional resources into the relationship. Susarla et al. developed a conceptual model of satisfaction with ASP. They found, not surprisingly, that satisfaction with ASP was negatively influenced by the disconfirmation effect (discrepancy between the expectation and performance), but positively affected by the perceived provider performance and prior system integration experiences. Research that deals with ASP adoption is presented in Table 1.

Much has been said, but despite an abundance of theoretical perspectives in ASP adoption, apparently little has been done to assess the impact of ASP adoption empirically. We therefore felt that empirical research connecting emerging technology adoption with tangible outcomes was essential. Such a study could link ASP adoption with its benefits and provide a way of making future ASPrelated decisions.

## 2.3. IT investment announcements and their impacts on the market value of the firm

In today’s business environment, investment in IT has become an absolute necessity for organizations. Many companies need IT to attain their strategic goals as well as to improve operational efficiencies. While IT has been the largest capital expenditure for many organizations, determining whether investment in IT indeed contributes to firms’ performance requires an extremely complex undertaking [9]. Much evidence supports the contribution of IT investment to firm performance but some studies suggest little or none. Over the past decade, there have been a large number of studies that have tried to address this problem [10,15].

To provide a different way of measuring business performance, some IS researchers have adopted a stock market valuation approach based on event study methodology, which is an efficient way to capture investors’ overall assessment of a firm’s value. Unlike the resource-based view approach, which uses annual financial measures, the event study measures the stock market’s reaction to unexpected events, such as announcements, to estimate how the event impacts the value of the firm. The underlying assumption is that capital markets are sufficient to evaluate new information about the key event, including IT investment, which can potentially impact the expected future profits of the firm, and that this is reflected in the change of the firm’s stock price. This methodology has been used in monitoring the effect of wide-ranging announcements, such as stock splits, layoff announcements, dividend policies, and key personnel (CEO) changes. Within the IS literature, the impact of unexpected announcements has been studied for IT investment in general [12], outsourcing [21], e-commerce, security vulnerability, knowledge management, IT infrastructure [3], ERP system [14], etc. There are different types of IT investments, but they can be divided into two parts: infrastructure and application investments. The impact of general IT investment on firm performance and the impact of IT infrastructure investments have already been examined, but our study is the first to investigate the impact of IT application investment (through ASP) on the market value of the firm. Also, we consider multiple contextual factors, such as strategic intent, firm size, industry, and vendor status that were seldom considered in previous studies. ## 3. Hypotheses development

## 3.1. Link between ASP adoption announcements and market value

ASP allows organizations to outsource software applications by renting them through the Internet, providing access to software and services on-demand. In addition to the benefits of a low initial cash investment, an ASP provides customers some degree of flexibility through the rental agreement [22]. More importantly, because companies can obtain a broad range of business applications via ASP, they can concentrate on the core competen cies of their businesses in the market. This, coupled with access to advanced technology, can positively affect the organizational performance.

Announcements of ASP adoption are a way for organizations to communicate favorable information to investors and stakeholders by leveraging their new technological innovations. From an option value perspective, an announcement of ASP adoption reflects the commitment of a company to build resources and capabilities for new business practices; it also implies that the firm is planning to reorganize its software procurement procedures to take advantage of new significant efficiencies. Such announcements are expected to position the firm to use new opportunities, thus creating additional benefits.

Thus, we expected that firms announcing ASP adoption were likely to achieve significant strategic and operational advantages in the near future: investors should interpret the announcements positively, resulting in a positive abnormal stock market return. This leads to our first hypothesis:

Hypothesis 1. (H1) ASP adoption announcements will produce positive abnormal shareholder returns.

## 3.1.1. Strategic intent to adopt ASP and market value

Business strategy can be characterized as having two key objectives, operational effectiveness, which attempts to reduce costs and increase productivity and speed, and strategic positioning, which aims at extending the existing market by providing customer access and better market practices. This has been used to link firms’ strategic intents for IT [4,29]. In operations-focused firms, IT is primarily implemented to improve efficiency and effectiveness of the organizational processes, while in marketfocused firms IT is used to enhance their strategic positioning.

Based on previous strategic intent classification schemes, we proposed a 2  2 strategic intent matrix for ASP adoption. As can be seen in Fig. 1, each quadrant represents a different strategic intent in adopting ASP. An Internal Focus represents an acquisition of ASP services to improve internal efficiency. An External Focus attires to provide value-added services to entities such as customers and suppliers through increased connectivity and better collaboration.

However, one could argue that an external focus requires some degree of operational emphasis in order to be successful. To address this issue, we added Dual Focus, which supports both drivers simultaneously; it emphasizes both an internal and external focus, extending the use of ASP beyond operational effectiveness and external connectivity. In general, internally focused services can be characterized as geared towards operational effectiveness, while externally or dual-focused ones emphasize more strategic positioning.

Firms that favor strategic positioning over operational effectiveness should achieve superior levels of firm performance; also previous event studies found that the market reacts more positively to announcement of innovative and transformative investments than non-innovative ones. We hypothesized that the stock market would recognize the potential capabilities of various ASP adoptions based upon their strategic intent, reflecting future benefits in their stock prices. Particularly, we expected that firms announcing the adoption of ASP with either an external or dual focus would achieve a higher positive abnormal stock market return than firms with an internal focus. This lead to:

Hypothesis 2. (H2) Firms announcing the adoption of ASP with either an external or dual focus will produce higher positive abnormal shareholder returns than firms with an internal focus only.

## 3.1.2. Firm size and market value

We also explored how the stock market reactions were affected by firm size. Prior research had not established the relationship between firm size and general IT investment but had shown that firm size affected IT adoption in different ways. On one hand, the literature suggested that large firms had greater resources and thus their announcements produced a greater impact [20]. However, some had argued that the market responded more strongly to information announced by smaller firms. For example, Im et al. found a positive return for smaller firms, but no price change for large firms.

Traditional IT outsourcing is mainly targeted toward large companies since the up-front investments and maintenance costs are substantial [6]. However, the ASP business model tends to attract SMEs who want to focus their resources on building their businesses, rather than in making significant IT investment. Supply-side economies of scale make the ASP model a viable and affordable option for SMEs who have struggled with acquiring sufficient IT resources. Large companies are interested in contracting with an ASP that understands the needs of clients and the impact of the application to the entire business; SMEs, on the other hand, find that the lower up-front investment and range of applications provided are particularly attractive. We hypothesized that, since the stock price of SMEs is more volatile, they announce more valuable information than larger companies, and, having less operating capital, their profitability will be more dramatically affected by ASP adoption. Thus:

Hypothesis 3. (H3) ASP adoption announcements will produce a significantly different level of abnormal shareholder returns for small and medium firms (SMEs) than large firms.

## 3.1.3. Industry sector and market value

Despite the fact that IT has been a major factor in changing business practices, its role differs in industries [17,19]. Especially in information-intensive industries, IT is altering the dynamics of the industry and changing the requirements for competitive success. Thus, it is critical to understand the impact of IT on different industries. In previous studies, it was argued that the financial services industry was likely to have more informationintensive processes than the manufacturing industry. As a result, IT investment may depend on the industry sector. Similarly, different price reactions to ASP adoption across industry sectors can be expected. We focused on two broad industry types, manufacturing and service. Our expectation was that the services industry, which requires more information-intensive capabilities, will be affected to a greater degree than the manufacturing sector for ASP adoption. This lead to:

Hypothesis 4. (H4) ASP adoption announcements will produce a higher level of abnormal shareholder returns for the service industry than the manufacturing industry.

## 3.1.4. ASP vendor status and market value

Finally, we investigated the market reaction to ASP adoption based on ASP vendor status. There are several obstacles to ASP adoption, such as concerns about security, service quality, and even vendor bankruptcy. The quality of the vendor’s services, support, and reliability are important factors in ASP choice [18]. It is generally expected that adopting ASP services from vendors with proven track records and long industry sector experience has a higher chance of securing reliable services and guaranteed performance. Also, leasing software applications from leading ASP vendors is likely to have a greater option value for firms in terms of network externality, if the same technology platform is used by other firms.

Nevertheless, the literature does not strongly support vendor status as important. Hayes et al. investigated market reactions to ERP implementation announcements and found that the stock market showed greater market valuation for firms that announced investment in ERP systems offered by the top two vendors (SAP and PeopleSoft). Another ERP investment study by Ranganathan and Brown found no significant difference, indicating that investors do not respond favorably based on the status of the ERP vendor. We posited that the stock market would realize the potential value of ASP adoption based on the vendor status:

Hypothesis 5. (H5) Announcements of adoption of ASP from market leading vendors will produce a higher level of abnormal shareholder returns than announcements from other vendors.

To take different vendor status into account, service provider information was collected from the ASPnews website (http:// www.aspnews.com). This provides the list of leading ASP providers on a monthly basis since 2001. Their main criteria for inclusion in the list of top ASP providers are that they:

\- Have the provision of network-based applications and/or services as their core business.

\- Have a substantial and active end-user customer base for those services.

\- Be able to demonstrate proven revenue streams.

\- Be innovators in the online delivery of software-based services.

\- Be recognized as a leader by others within the industry.

We compared vendor names in the announcements with the top provider lists, and if the vendor was recognized on the lists between 2001 and 2007, it was classified as a Market leading service provider; otherwise, it was categorized as a Non-leading service provider.

## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Sample selection procedure

Firms considered in this study were the constituents of the S&P LargeCap 500, MidCap 400, and SmallCap 600 as of March, 2007 (extracted from http://www.standardandpoors.com). Information on ASP adoption announcements were collected using a full text search of news sources (e.g., PR Newswire, Business Wire) within the Lexis-Nexis academic search engine for a ten-year span from January 1998 to December 2007. A data set of 1500 companies over this period allowed us to capture any significant market trends and to ensure normality assumptions due to large sample sizes. The daily returns of the individual firms were retrieved from the Center for Research on Security Prices (CRSP) database. We examined the daily abnormal returns (AR) over a 200-day estimation period (the period before the event was used to estimate the regression parameters). The indices used for the market return regression were from the S&P 500 Index (OEX).

Following the search string structure, our search keyword contained the noun of the technology, the company name, and the company ticker symbol. In addition, we included the names of major stock exchanges to eliminate firms that were not traded on major security exchanges. The search keywords for announcements thus contained (ASP OR application service provider OR software as a service OR on demand service), AND (NYSE OR NASDAQ OR AMEX OR OTC) AND (S&P firm name OR ticker symbol). Even with this search restriction, the database still provided thousands of announcement articles. We employed a step-by-step process to select announcements to be included in our sample. First, titles were reviewed to determine whether the articles were related to specific ASP adoption announcements. If there was more than one title repeating the same ASP adoption announcement, the earliest article was used. After this initial title screening, all announcements were read to confirm the nature of the announcement and only those that dealt with ASP adoption were retained for further screening.

The remaining announcements were then further examined; we only retained announcements for publicly traded firms in major security exchanges and whose stock market data were available in the CRSP database. For each firm, we searched other significant events such as: acquisitions, earning announcements, dividends, stock splits, law suits, and other financial events that could create confounding effects with ASP adoption announcements. Any announcements made on a day with concurrent confounding events were dropped. Daily returns of each individual firm were then collected from the CRSP database. As a result of all screening processes, the final data sample consisted of 268 announcements (the initial sample size was 271 but three additional extreme outliers were eliminated during a descriptive statistics analysis).

## 4.2. Data analysis

In our study, we defined the event window as three days; 1 day from the event (day 0) for the following reasons. First, by narrowing the event window, we could reduce the possibility of confounding effects in the dataset. Since many organizations in our study were market leaders likely to have frequent news announcements, the use of a short event window assured capturing an abnormal return due to the event rather than other effects. Also, using a long event window reduces the power of the test statistic, $Z _ { t }$

Event-study methodology is based on the assumption that capital markets are sufficiently efficient to evaluate the impact of new information on expected future profits of the firm. We used the market model to obtain the estimated abnormal returns: a linear relationship is assumed between the daily return for a firm and the return of market portfolio of its stocks

$$
R _ {i, t} = \alpha_ {i} + \beta_ {i} R _ {m, t} + \varepsilon_ {i, t}\tag{1}
$$

where $R _ { i , t }$ is the return on the share price of firm i on day $t , R _ { m , t }$ is the rate of return on the market portfolio of stocks (S&P 500 index) on day $t , \alpha _ { i }$ is the intercept term, $\beta _ { i }$ is the systematic risk of stock i, $\varepsilon _ { i }$ is an error term.

Using the parameter values estimated from Eq. (1), the abnormal return (AR) which represents the difference between the actual return and the expected return was calculated for each firm i during the event window t, as:

$$
\mathrm{AR} _ {i, t} = R _ {i, t} - \left(\alpha_ {i} + \beta_ {i} R _ {m, t}\right)\tag{2}
$$

where $\alpha _ { i } , \beta _ { i }$ are the ordinary least square (OLS) parameter estimates obtained from the regression of $R _ { i , t }$ on $R _ { m , t }$ over an estimation period (T) preceding the event, 200 days prior to the event, $\mathsf { A R } _ { i , t }$ is the rate of return on the stock after being adjusted by subtracting the expected return from the actual return.

If the difference was statistically significant, it indicated an abnormal or excess return. The abnormal return for the event window was then standardized (SAR) [dividing by its standard deviation]:

$$
\mathrm{SAR} _ {i, t} = \frac {\mathrm{AR} _ {i , t}}{\mathrm{SD} _ {i , t}}\tag{3}
$$

where

$$
\mathrm{SD} _ {i, t} = \left(S _ {i} ^ {2} \times \left[ 1 + \frac {1}{T} (R _ {m, t} - R _ {m}) ^ {2} / \sum (R _ {m, t} - R _ {m}) ^ {2} \right]\right) ^ {1 / 2}
$$

$S _ { i }$ is the residual variance from the market model as computed for firm $i , R _ { m }$ is the mean return on the market portfolio during the estimation period, T is the number of days in the estimation period (200 days in our study).

The standardized abnormal returns (SAR) can then be cumulated over the event window k, to sum a measure of the cumulative abnormal return (CAR) for each firm

$$
\mathrm{CAR} _ {i} = \left(\frac {1}{k ^ {1 / 2}}\right) \sum_ {t = 1} \mathrm{SAR} _ {i, t}\tag{4}
$$

Following Dardan and Stylianou, the values of CAR are assumed to be independent, normal, and identically distributed. As a result, these values were identically distributed variables when dividing CAR by its standard deviation, which is $\left[ ( T - 2 ) / ( T - 4 ) \right] ^ { 1 / 2 }$ . Then the average standardized cumulative abnormal return (ASCAR) across n firms over the event window is:

$$
\mathrm{ASCAR} _ {i} = \frac {1}{n} \times \frac {1}{[ (T - 2) / (T - 4) ] ^ {1 / 2}} \times \sum_ {i = 1} \mathrm{CAR} _ {i, t}\tag{5}
$$

And the Z-value for the average standardized cumulative abnormal return is:

$$
Z = \mathrm{ASCAR} _ {t} \times n ^ {1 / 2}\tag{6}
$$

## 5. Analysis results

5.1. Effect of ASP adoption announcements on the market value of the firm

Table 4 presents a summary of cumulative abnormal returns (CAR) and average standardized cumulative abnormal returns (ASCAR) for the 268 ASP adoption announcements, and the test for significance of the effect, including both an overall effect over the 3-day event window and the effects on three individual days.

Hypothesis 1 was supported. The overall ASCAR over the 3-day event window of all firms making adoption announcements was found to be significant (ASCAR = 0.12%, Z = 1.70, p < 0.05). In particular, a positive abnormal return was found on the day of the announcement. This indicated that investors or shareholders reacted favorably to firms’ ASP adoption initiatives.

## 5.2. Effect of strategic adoption intent on the market value of the firm

The sample was divided into two groups: internal focused, and external/dual focused. Table 5 summarizes the ASCAR for each group both over the 3-day event window and on each of the individual days. The overall ASCAR for internal focus was found to be insignificant. ## 6. Discussion

## 6.1. Does ASP adoption provide benefits to firms?

Although the advantages of ASP seem to be compelling, the adoption rate is disappointingly low. Our results suggested that, overall ASP adoption announcements were positively associated with increases in market valuation of firms and thus create value for shareholders. This finding was consistent with prior IT investment research. The positive abnormal returns to ASP adoption announcements reflected investors’ growing confidence in the business model as it continues to mature. While it does not need to show excess return for an investment to be worthwhile, showing a positive market reaction following ASP adoption can be valuable to many companies who are deferring their investment decision.

## 6.2. Should the cost savings be the main purpose of ASP adoption?

Studies have shown that the primary purpose of adopting ASP is cost savings associated with external procurement of software and services. However, we showed that ASP adoption was less likely to provide sustainable benefits when firms focused on internal use. IT becomes a commodity that makes it more difficult for firms to sustain competitive advantages gained through IT investment. Unless they are extremely difficult to duplicate or provide significant value-added benefits, competitors will quickly implement similar initiatives, thus making the process a competitive necessity. Given the ease of acquiring services and the short implementation time period, ASP adoption is less likely to provide sustainable benefits when firms use it internally. But when firms announce the adoption of ASP services focusing on gaining sustained profit by providing value-added service to customers and better collaboration with suppliers, investors react more positively.

## 6.3. Does ASP provide benefits to SMEs?

ASPs target small and medium size firms by providing applications that they normally cannot afford. Contrary to our expectation, our results indicated that large firms enjoyed significant positive returns to their ASP adoption announcements while SMEs did not. Existing IT infrastructure can often be complimentary with ASP services. For instance, CRM and supply chain management can be hosted by an ASP. However, a company will enjoy full benefits when information is integrated with internal IS, requiring a significant investment in IT infrastructure, and large companies usually have better access to such resources. The combined effect of firm size and strategic intent provides further insight into ASP adoption. As shown in Fig. 2, when large firms announce adoption of ASP with their focus on external/dual drivers, investors perceive these initiatives more positively, which results in a significant positive abnormal return. This indicates that when adopting ASP as a complimentary function and exploiting opportunities to improve critical aspects of firm performance, firms maximize the returns of their investment in ASP.

## 6.4. Does ASP adoption provide the same benefits to all industries?

A significant industry difference was found in the overall abnormal returns to ASP adoption announcements. Further finer granular analysis reveals a significant positive abnormal return to announcements for firms in the information technology, industrial, and health care industry segments. One plausible explanation is that the fast-changing nature of these industry segments requires high levels of employee competencies in these segments. Since the quality of people is the underlying driving force of continuing business growth and competitive advantages, senior management as well as shareholders pay special attention to human resource capabilities through employee training and development programs. This is perhaps the reason why we were able to find an unusually high number of announcements in the information technology and health care industry who adopted diverse employee training programs through ASP. The market tends to perceive announcements in these segments more positively.

## 6.5. How about the choice of a vendor?

The announcements of ASP adoption from market leading providers seemed to produce superior outcomes than those from those of non-leading vendors. This reflects the importance of vendor experience and knowledge. As reliable services and guaranteed performance are the important issues of the ASP choice, the market seems to perceive a proven track record and long industry sector knowledge and experience as particularly important.

## 7. Conclusion

We sought to examine the impact of ASP adoption announcements on the market value of the firms and explore contextual factors in the positive abnormal returns. In analyzing a sample of 268 ASP adoption announcements during a 10-year period from 1998, the results of our event study indicated that ASP adoption announcements were associated with positive increases in the market value of the firms, indicating that investors or shareholders perceived ASP initiatives favorably with significant future benefits.

We also investigated whether strategic adoption intent, firm size, industry sector, and vendor status made a difference in market returns of these firms. The analysis showed that when firms announced the adoption of ASP services in order to gain sustained profits by providing value-added service to customers and better collaboration among suppliers, investors perceived and react positively to the initiative. With respect to the firm size effect, it was found that large firms had statistically significant positive abnormal returns, while ASP adoption did not increase the market value of small and medium size firms. In particular, the market perceived the adoption of ASP positively when large firms announced their adoption of ASP with a focus on external/dual drivers. Our analysis also supported the difference in market returns between the manufacturing and service sectors. Finer granular analysis of industry sectors revealed a significant positive abnormal return for IT savvy firms in the IT and health care segments. Similarly, we found a statistical significance for firms announcing ASP adoption through market leading vendors.

Our findings provide useful implications for practitioners. First, the positive impact of ASP adoption announcements on a firm’s market value can be particularly beneficial for companies who plan to make future ASP-related decisions and companies who defer the adoption of ASP due to its current risks. We also help by providing a better understanding of the impact of firms’ investments in ASP by delineating strategic adoption intent, industry, firm size, and vendor status effects on the investment announcements. However, firms should be cautious in determining the type of investments, since the strategic intent of new investment may be evaluated differently by the market. Similarly, the firm size and industry difference in market returns imply that it is necessary to make investment decisions in sync with firm-specific factors as well as its industry characteristics.

There are several limitations of the study that should be recognized. First, care must be exercised in generalizing the findings, since we were unable to provide any causal relationship; this is an inherent limitation of an event study. We represent one way of addressing the impact of ASP adoption, based on a stockmarket based approach. Second, although a thorough screening process was employed to control for confounding variables, all confounding effects cannot be completely eliminated. Lastly, traditional IT event studies (including ours) have relied on a parametric analysis of abnormal returns and their significance. As utilized in a few previous studies [3,8], incorporation of semiparametric and non-parametric event study analysis can be useful in verifying and/or strengthening the findings. Further, other models such as the raw return model, the Fama-French threefactor models, or a custom factor model can be employed to estimate and test abnormal returns.
