# Retail Strategies for E-Tailers in Live Streaming Commerce: When Does Influencer Marketing Work?

Guangyu Li,<sup>1</sup> Guofang Nan,<sup>2</sup> Ronghui Wang,<sup>3</sup> Giri Kumar Tayi,<sup>4</sup> Dahui Li<sup>5</sup> <sup>1</sup>College of Management and Economics, Tianjin University, China, guangyu\_li@tju.edu.cn <sup>2</sup>International Business School, Hainan University, China, gfnan@hainanu.edu.cn <sup>3</sup>College of Management and Economics, Tianjin University, China, ronghuiwang@tju.edu.cn <sup>4</sup>School of Business, State University of New York at Albany, USA, GTayi@uamail.albany.edu <sup>5</sup>College of Business, University of Colorado Colorado Springs, USA, dli@uccs.edu

## Abstract

Retailers are increasingly embracing influencer marketing channels and live broadcast services to boost product sales. While influencer marketing channels offer the potential to attract a large consumer base, they also present challenges such as demand cannibalization and high costs associated with influencers. This paper develops a stylized model to investigate how the introduction of an influencer marketing channel can enhance a retailer’s profits and social welfare by considering two scenarios: one where the retailer does not provide live streaming services through its own online channel, and one where it does. We find that, in both scenarios, the retailer can benefit from the influencer marketing channel when the channel’s information value is high or when product complexity is relatively low. Additionally, under certain conditions, the retailer’s own live broadcast service can either substitute or complement the influencer marketing channel to improve profits. Furthermore, the retailer might be willing to sacrifice short-term profits from introducing the influencer marketing channel in the initial sales period to achieve higher long-term profits. Finally, there is significant consistency in the impact of the influencer marketing channel on the retailer’s profits and social welfare if consumers are not involved in product return. On the contrary, if product return caused by impulsive consumption is considered, the introduction of the influencer marketing channel may cause a decline in consumer surplus and social welfare.

Keywords: Live Streaming E-Commerce, Influencer Marketing, Multichannel Retailing, Two-Period Retailing

## 1 Introduction

Over the past decade, the live streaming industry has experienced rapid development due to advances and innovations in essential components such as live streaming platforms, streamers, and multichannel network institutions. Live streaming has been integrated with e-commerce to create a mainstream marketing and selling model known as live streaming e-commerce. Following the outbreak of the COVID-19 epidemic in

2020, consumer dependence on online shopping surged, and the new consumption pattern driven by live broadcast marketing demonstrated significant strength and vitality during the epidemic prevention and control period. Several websites, including Amazon and YouTube, have incorporated live streaming into their platforms to offer live streaming e-commerce services. Retailers can market and sell products either in their own live broadcast rooms or through influencer marketing channels on Amazon Live, YouTube Live, and LazLive.

Compared to traditional online (TO) channels, live streaming has transformed e-commerce platforms from merely displaying text and graphic descriptions of products to fostering more meaningful engagement and interactions with consumers (Fei et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2019; Xue et al., 2020).

To engage in this new type of e-commerce, a retailer has two options for incorporating live streaming ecommerce into its distribution channels. The first option is the influencer marketing (IM) channel, where the retailer collaborates with professional marketing influencers to sell products in the influencers’ live broadcast rooms. The second option is for the retailer to add its own independent live broadcast service to the TO channel, allowing it to sell products in its own live broadcast rooms without partnering with influencers. To distinguish between these two scenarios, we refer to the TO channel with an independent live broadcast service as the TL channel.

If the retailer opts for the first option and introduces the IM channel, it may reach more consumers who follow influencers. These influencers can be individuals, groups, or even virtual avatars who have built a network of followers on social media and are regarded as digital opinion leaders with significant social influence (Leung et al., 2022). Compared to the TO channel, the IM channel allows the retailer to sell products in influencers’ live broadcast rooms. Influencers can provide more comprehensive product information to consumers by showcasing product details, personally trying products, and answering questions in real time. The engaging process in the IM channel provides information value to help reduce purchase uncertainty and misfit cost between the product and the consumer’s preferences, meaning that the consumer purchasing a product through the IM channel can obtain valuable information to address their concerns about the product. Furthermore, influencers excel at creating an engaging atmosphere in their live broadcast rooms, warming up consumers, and fostering an active and enthusiastic purchasing environment. This results in a more pleasant purchasing experience for consumers compared to the TO channel. In other words, the IM channel can provide consumers with purchasing experience value, which is influenced by factors such as the influencer’s level of influence, the scale of their followers, real-time interaction with consumers, and their professional level of live streaming marketing.

However, there are also costs for retailers when cooperating with influencers, who usually charge high marketing service fees. Influencers offer different pricing strategies for retailers to choose from when planning to sell goods in the IM channel, such as “fixed link fee + commission,” “pure commission,” and “fixed link fee related to sales + commission.” This study focuses on the “fixed link fee + commission” model, which has been widely accepted by retailers and influencers (Chen, 2021). Under the “fixed link fee + commission” agreement, the retailer pays a fixed link fee to the influencer to have their products sold in the influencer’s live broadcast room. This fee is not based on product sales but is determined by the influencer’s level of influence. In China, fixed link fees range from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of yuan, depending on whether the influencer has moderate or significant influence. Additionally, the influencer charges a commission proportional to product sales as compensation for selling the retailer’s products.

Due to the high fixed link fee and commission, introducing the IM channel might not necessarily increase the overall profits of the retailer (Cao et al., 2016). Consequently, some retailers may choose to operate the TL channel and provide live broadcast services in their own live broadcast rooms. With advanced live streaming technologies gradually lowering the entry barrier for retailers, a live streaming service added to the TL channel can also allow consumers to understand product details through personal trials by streamers and real-time interactions in the retailer’s own live broadcast room, similar to the IM channel.

The retailer needs to be cautious when deciding whether to introduce the IM channel or to build its own live streaming service in the TL channel. This paper explores the impacts of introducing the IM channel on the retailer’s profits and social welfare. Specifically, we aim to address the following research questions: (1) For the retailer operating the TO channel without live streaming service or the TL channel with live streaming service, under what conditions should the IM channel be introduced? (2) Are there any interactive effects between the retailer’s own live broadcast service of the TL channel and the IM channel in improving the retailer’s profits? (3) When the retailer considers long-term profits, is the optimal channel strategy the same as when the retailer considers short-term profits? (4) How does the retailer’s channel strategy affect consumer surplus and social welfare when product return caused by impulsive consumption is considered?

To address these questions, our analysis proceeds through four phases. First, we examine a scenario where the retailer sells products exclusively through the existing TO channel. In this phase, we characterize the demand allocation across distribution channels to determine whether the retailer should introduce the IM channel and assess its impact on social welfare (Chen et al., 2008). Second, we focus on a scenario where the retailer offers a live streaming service via the TL channel, exploring the retailer’s optimal decisions when operating the TL channel. Third, we compare the two scenarios to analyze how the interaction between the IM channel and the live streaming service in the TL channel influences the retailer’s profits. Finally, we extend the basic model in two directions: to explore the retailer’s optimal channel strategy when considering long-term sales profit as the decision-making goal, and to account for product return in the IM channel due to impulsive consumption.

Our analysis uncovers the following results. First, regardless of whether the live streaming service is offered, the retailer can benefit from introducing the IM channel under two conditions: (1) when the information value of the IM channel is high, or (2) when product complexity is low. In the first condition, the information value of the IM channel plays a crucial role. When the IM channel provides high-quality information that significantly enhances the consumer’s understanding of the product, it can lead to better purchasing decisions. This high information value can reduce the probability of the purchased product not matching consumers preferences, making them more likely to purchase the product. As a result, the retailer can realize an increase in sales and profits. Additionally, the enhanced information value can improve consumer satisfaction, leading to positive word-of-mouth and repeat purchases, further boosting the retailer’s profits.

In the second condition, product complexity is critical because a simple product can be easily promoted and marketed via the IM channel. When the product is less complex, already familiar to consumers, or when the retailer decides to cooperate with an influencer who has previously promoted the product through live streaming, the fixed link fee for working with an influencer of a given influence level can be lower. Additionally, the cost increase for collaborating with a higher- vs. lower-level influencer can also be lower because the product is less complex to promote. This scenario offers two advantages for the retailer. First, by collaborating with an influencer of a given influence level at a lower cost due to low (vs. high) product complexity, the retailer can better manage costs and predict marketing expenses. This allows for more accurate budgeting and financial planning while ensuring that consumers receive the same level of shopping experience value. Second, the retailer can achieve a higher return on investment; while low product complexity controls costs, the potential for increased sales through collaborating with an influencer with high reach and engagement remains high. For instance, the beauty brand Perfect Diary quickly improved brand exposure and product sales in a short time by paying the fixed link fee and cooperating with well-known influencers. <sup>1</sup> By leveraging influencer marketing, the brand conveyed value and established an emotional connection with fans, allowing Perfect Diary to quickly build strong word-of-mouth and consumer loyalty. This suggests a high value associated with the cost of influencer marketing, which is a concrete manifestation of the success of cost management.

Second, the relationship between the retailer’s live streaming service and the IM channel may vary depending on specific conditions. On the one hand, if the information value advantage of the IM channel over the retailer’s live streaming service in the TO channel is not significantly high, if product complexity is relatively high, and if the retailer’s cost of implementing live streaming is relatively low, then the retailer’s live streaming service and the IM channel may act as substitutes. In a scenario where the additional benefits provided by the IM channel in terms of product information and consumer engagement are not substantially greater than what the retailer’s own live streaming service can offer, consumers may not perceive a significant difference in the quality of information or the overall shopping experience between the two channels. In addition, by balancing the high cost of the fixed link fee and the low cost of implementing its own live streaming, the retailer can choose to invest in its own live streaming capabilities instead of relying on influencers. This substitution effect allows the retailer to achieve similar or even better results in terms of consumer engagement and sales while also improving its profit margins and fostering stronger customer relationships.

On the other hand, if the IM channel offers a significantly higher information value than the retailer’s live streaming service, if product complexity is relatively low, and if the retailer’s live streaming cost is relatively low, then the two channels can complement each other. The IM channel can attract a broad audience and provide high-quality information that enhances the consumer’s purchasing experience while charging a lower fee to the retailer. This allows the retailer to maintain direct interaction with consumers, build trust and loyalty, and encourage repeat purchases and longterm customer relationships without needing to invest significantly in building the live streaming platform.

Third, retailers considering the long-term impact of the IM channel will be more inclined to adopt it. One of the primary reasons for this is the potential for sustained growth and profitability. Influencers often have a loyal and engaged following, which can translate into a steady stream of customers for the retailer. By leveraging the influencer’s established network, retailers can reach a wider audience and build brand awareness more effectively than through traditional marketing channels. In the long term, the IM channel can help retailers establish a strong brand presence in the market. The retailer also stays relevant and competitive in its competition with rivals through collaboration with influencers. The cost factor is also important because the fixed link fee and commission structure may be more predictable and manageable for the retailer compared to other marketing expenses.

Finally, there is a notable consistency between the impact of the IM channel on the retailer’s profits and its effect on social welfare when consumers are not involved in product return behaviors. However, when consumers in the IM channel choose to return products due to impulse purchasing, the IM channel may reduce consumer surplus and social welfare. Specifically, the retailer’s decision to introduce the IM channel to maximize profits also positively contributes to social welfare. When a retailer decides to introduce the IM channel, it is primarily motivated by the potential to maximize its profits. This decision is based on the ability of influencers to reach a large audience, provide engaging content, and drive sales through their established networks. This profit-driven decision also has broader implications for social welfare. If consumers are not involved in product return behaviors, the IM channel provides consumers with information value and purchasing experience value, reducing their purchase uncertainty while enhancing their shopping experience. Therefore, it can improve consumer surplus to some extent. Nevertheless, the heightened purchasing atmosphere in influencers’ live streaming rooms may encourage consumers to engage in excessive consumption. Unwise purchasing decisions could further lead to product return, which would harm the rights and interests of some consumers, resulting in a decline in consumer surplus and social welfare.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides a review of the literature. In Section 3, we present our basic model setup. Section 4 analyzes the optimal channel strategy of the retailer in two scenarios and examines the interaction between the TL channel and the IM channel. In Section 5, we extend the basic model and explore the optimal channel strategy for the retailer when considering long-term profit and product return, respectively. Finally, Section 6 presents the results and addresses implications and areas for future research.

## 2 Literature Review

Three streams of literature are related to our study: live streaming e-commerce, online retailing, and multichannel price differentiation.

First, prior studies of live streaming e-commerce have focused on the impact of live streaming on consumer purchases and how retailers should adjust their live streaming strategies to effectively promote online sales (Ang et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2017). For example, Chen and Chen (2017) posited that influencers play the role of key opinion leaders whose endorsement of products may reduce a consumer’s perceived risk. Sun et al. (2019) revealed that visibility affordance, metavoicing affordance, and guidance shopping affordance impact a consumer’s purchasing willingness through live streaming. Wongkitrungrueng and Assarut (2020)

argued that symbolic value, utilitarianism, and hedonic value have direct or indirect impacts on consumer engagement and that online retailers should build strategies to enhance consumer engagement. Guan (2021) pointed out that influencers are professionals who are able to create a lively atmosphere in their live broadcast rooms so that the IM channel can enhance a consumer’s shopping experience and bring additional utility to the consumer. Lu and Chen (2021) applied signaling theory to study how retailers adjust their live streaming methods to decrease perceived uncertainty and increase trust among product-centered consumers and social interaction-centered consumers.

Wies et al. (2023) found an inverted U-shaped relationship between the number of an influencer’s followers and the followers’ engagement with the sponsored content provided by the influencer. Leung et al. (2022), based on a communication model, showed that influencer activity, follower-brand fit, and the positivity of the influencer’s posts have inverted Ushaped moderating effects on IM effectiveness. Tan et al. (2019) found that real-time chats can improve online retail turnover. In addition, several other studies predicted the future development of live streaming ecommerce. Ding et al. (2020) suggested that retailers should work on improving the product’s intrinsic value, and that influencers should strive to develop the ability to be professional marketers and improve the quality of live streaming content. Luo and Sima (2020) posited that because consumer trust and satisfaction are the core foundations that lead to long-term consumption, influencers should focus their efforts on keeping consumer interests and maintaining customer loyalty.

Most of these studies examined the impact of live streaming e-commerce on consumer behavior using a variety of empirical methods such as descriptive analysis, text analysis, correlation analysis, or natural experiments (Sun et al., 2020). However, these studies did not provide guidance for retailers and influencers on how to develop optimal strategies in pricing products and generating profits. The current paper formulates a stylized model framework to analyze the actual causal relationship between an IM channel and the consumer’s purchase decision.

Second, given the rapid development of e-commerce, online retailing has received considerable attention from the research community (Bar-Gill & Reichman, 2020; Chiang et al., 2003; Gao & Su, 2017; Luo et al., 2021; Tsay & Agrawal, 2004). King et al. (2004) suggested that retailers would be forced into a dual-channel strategy as they compete with their rivals over time. Yoo and Lee (2011) revealed that adding an internet outlet may harm the retailer under certain market conditions, and that the online channel does not always lead to enhanced consumer surplus. Cattani et al. (2006) showed that retailers do not necessarily view a direct channel as harmful or intensifying competition, but rather as a market segmentation that helps achieve a win-win situation for retailers and manufacturers. Li et al. (2018) creatively extended the channel strategy of multichannel retailing to the field of digital products. Many other studies on channel management examined the integration or separation of the online and offline channels. In addition, other studies have investigated the diffusion and pricing strategies of digital products and services (Bandyopadhyay & Cheng, 2006; Cheng & Koehler, 2003; Li et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2011).

Unlike most previous studies of online retailing that focus on the integration or separation of online and offline channels, our study examines the impact of the new IM channel on retailers operating in the TO channel, which is more relevant to Pan et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2023). However, when exploring the retailer’s optimal distribution channel strategy in the era of live streaming e-commerce, both Pan et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2023) considered only one form of live streaming e-commerce. Specifically, Pan et al. (2022) considered the retailer’s own live streaming services to study the impact of live streaming on e-commerce, while Wang et al. (2023) only considered the IM channel. In contrast, we distinguish live streaming e-commerce in terms of the IM channel and the live streaming service implemented by the retailer in the TO channel, which demonstrates the integration of live streaming and ecommerce in a more comprehensive manner. This study explores the channel strategy of a live streaming retailer in two scenarios, which can fill the gaps in this area of research.

Third, our study is also related to the literature on multichannel price differentiation. There is a rich body of literature on price differentiation between online and offline channels (Granados et al., 2012; Luo et al., 2020; Stylianou et al., 2005). Wolk and Ebling (2010) found that multichannel retailers who are increasingly engaged in channel-based price differentiation increase their profits, and price differentiation mostly occurs among big companies with market power that can separate markets. Focusing on expert service, Akçura and Ozdemir (2017) showed that consumer surplus can increase if a monopolist charges different prices for face-to-face and online services. Wu et al. (2020) proposed a randomized pricing strategy for omnichannel retailers, where retailers offer fixed prices in offline channels while discounts are randomly provided in online channels according to preset probabilities. The results show that the randomized pricing strategy can benefit both retailers and manufacturers.

In contrast to the above literature focusing on price differentiation between online and offline channels, the current paper examines price differentiation between the TO channel and the IM channel adopted by the retailer, which is more relevant to the following research. Liu and Liu (2021) and Hsu (2019) found that a favorable price is a significant factor in encouraging consumers to purchase through an IM channel. Luo et al. (2021) revealed that material rewards, such as coupons or price discounts that a consumer obtains in the influencer’s live broadcast room, can enhance the consumer’s willingness to purchase. Wang et al. (2022) note that many consumers without clear shopping goals place orders in the influencer’s live broadcast room at significantly low prices.

Based on the above research on price differentiation of live streaming e-commerce, this paper assumes that the retailer will set different product prices for the existing TO channel and the newly introduced IM channel, which is consistent with the pricing strategy of retailers in practice. In contrast to prior studies exploring the stimulating effect of differentiated pricing on consumer purchase behavior via qualitative analysis, the current research further provides quantitative pricing guidance for retailers through establishing economic models.

## 3 Model

Consider that a retailer sells products through a TO (TL) channel, such as Amazon. We explore the retailer’s optimal channel strategy of whether to add an IM channel, which involves paying an influencer to promote the retailer’s products in the influencer’s live broadcast room. Therefore, the retailer has two distribution channel strategies. When the retailer decides not to introduce the IM channel but sells products through the TO (TL) channel, the strategy is denoted as strategy T. Conversely, when products are sold through both the TO (TL) channel and the IM channel, the strategy is referred to as strategy TI. Next, we will explore two scenarios in which the retailer operates the TO or TL channel, respectively.

Scenario 1: The retailer operates the TO channel without live broadcast service. In this scenario where the retailer only operates the TO channel and does not provide its own live broadcast service, a consumer receives the utility of intrinsic value ?? (where $v \leq 1 )$ (Chen & Chen, 2017; McWilliams, 2012; Pun et al., 2020) after purchasing a product at price $p _ { o }$ Additionally, due to purchase uncertainty, there is a possibility that the product will not fully match the consumer’s preferences. We denote purchase uncertainty as ??, where $0 < \rho < 1$ . This means that the probability of the product not matching the consumer’s preferences is ??, while the probability of the product matching the consumer’s preferences is $1 - \rho$ . If the product perfectly matches the consumer’s preferences, the consumer can completely enjoy the intrinsic value ?? of the product. Otherwise, if the product does not match the consumer’s preferences, the consumer needs to bear a certain misfit cost. Consumers with a unit mass are allowed to have heterogeneous preferences for products. We assume that the distance between a consumer and the product x is a random variable, uniformly distributed along a unit line. The closer the value of x is to 0, the better the product fits the consumer’s preferences. If the product does not perfectly match the consumer’s preferences, the consumer bears a misfit cost. For simplicity, we normalize the unit misfit cost in the TO channel to 1.

Influencers in their own live broadcast rooms have more comprehensive and accurate product information. They can interact with consumers in real time, showcasing various product details through personal trials and strategic explanations. Therefore, consumers in the IM channel can obtain the information value, denoted as $\theta _ { I } .$ Through the real-time interactive process, consumers can gain a more intuitive, true, and comprehensive understanding of the product (Zhou & Duan, 2016). This helps reduce consumers’ misunderstanding about the product, especially regarding whether it matches their preferences (Hajli, 2015; Lu & Chen, 2021; Pan et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2023). Consequently, the probability of the purchased product not matching the consumer’s preferences can be reduced (Chen et al., 2021; Zhou & Duan, 2016; Peng et al., 2023). In other words, consumers can obtain the information value, which reduces purchase uncertainty and thereby lowers the expected misfit cost. As a result, consumers’ purchasing intention can be enhanced. Consequently, the information value $\theta _ { I }$ captures the effect of influencer marketing on reducing purchase uncertainty and misfit cost. Therefore, the possibility of the product obtained through the IM channel not fully matching the consumer’s preferences is $( 1 - \theta _ { I } ) \rho$ , where $\theta _ { I } < 1$ The higher the information value of the IM channel, the more efficient the IM channel is in reducing purchase uncertainty. Similar to the TO channel, consumers purchasing products that do not match their preferences need to bear the misfit cost.

In addition, influencers usually receive training in live marketing and are skilled at creating a lively atmosphere in their live streaming rooms. This can prompt consumers who have no clear shopping intentions to make unplanned purchases. In other words, the influence of an influencer can bring purchasing experience value to consumers who may make impulsive purchases through the IM channel (Peng et al.,, 2023). We use ?? to denote the purchasing experience value that the consumer derives from interacting with the influencer, which is related to the influence level of the influencer, the scale of the influencer’s followers, and the influencer’s prestige.

However, three factors may impact a consumer’s decision to purchase a particular retailer’s products in the IM channel. First, the influencer may only promote the retailer’s products at designated times, limiting access for some consumers. This means that the dissemination range of the retailer’s products in the IM channel is restricted. Second, the influencer’s designated times may be scheduled for a much later date, causing consumers who need to buy products immediately to give up on the IM channel. Third, because the schedule of the influencer’s live broadcast is usually fixed, time-constrained consumers may not be able to engage in the live broadcast. Collectively, these negative impacts can be summarized as participation cost ℎ . We assume that consumers do not engage in channel-switching behavior, which means that after bearing the participation cost ℎ to watch the influencer’s live streaming and obtain the information value $\theta _ { I }$ and purchasing experience value $\delta ,$ consumers will remain in the IM channel to make a purchase and will not switch to the TO channel. On the one hand, the purchase behavior is often instant, leading consumers to be more inclined to complete the purchase directly via the IM channel after watching the live broadcast, thereby saving time and energy. On the other hand, the social contact and purchasing experience value that consumers obtain from influencers may be difficult to replicate when switching to the TO channel. Therefore, the assumption of no channel switching is reasonable.

Consumers choose the purchasing channel based on expected utility maximization. According to the above analysis, a consumer’s expected utility function of purchasing through the TO channel or the IM channel in Scenario 1 can be expressed as follows:

$$
E (u _ {o}) = \rho (v - p _ {o} - x) + (1 - \rho) (v - p _ {o})
$$

Scenario 2: The retailer operates the TL channel with live broadcast service. Advances in live streaming technologies have gradually enabled retailers to offer their live broadcast services through their own channels. In this scenario, the retailer’s live broadcast service is typically carried out by multiple streamers employed by the retailer. Additionally, the retailer provides recorded broadcasts for consumers who miss the live sessions. Consequently, consumers can gain a more specific and comprehensive understanding of a product by watching the live or recorded broadcast before making a purchase in the TL channel. Consequently, almost all consumers who buy products through the TL channel can obtain the information value to reduce purchase uncertainty and misfit cost. The information value of the TL channel is denoted as $\theta _ { L } ,$ where $\theta _ { L } < 1$ . The higher the information value, the more efficient the retailer’s live broadcast is in reducing purchase uncertainty and misfit cost.

The timeline of the game is as follows. In Stage 1, the retailer decides whether to introduce the IM channel and, if introduced, selects the influencer who can provide consumers with a certain level of purchasing experience. In Stage 2, if the IM channel is introduced, the influencer determines the commission fee for each product, and the retailer determines the unit product price in each distribution channel. If the IM channel is not introduced, the retailer determines the unit product price for the TO channel. In Stage 3, the consumer makes their purchase decision and chooses where to buy. Scenario 1: The retailer operates the TO channel without live broadcast service. First, we analyze the situation where the retailer operates the TO channel only. The marginal consumer is indifferent between purchasing and not purchasing the product, which is denoted as $x _ { O }$ . By setting the utility $E ( u _ { O } )$ to zero, the demand for the product is $\begin{array} { r } { d _ { O } ^ { T } = \frac { v - p _ { O } ^ { I } } { \rho } } \end{array}$ , as shown in Figure 2a.

$$

The retailer’s optimal price and profits are $\begin{array} { r } { p _ { O } ^ { T * } = \frac { v } { 2 } } \end{array}$ and $\begin{array} { r } { \pi _ { R } ^ { T * } = \frac { v ^ { 2 } } { 4 \rho } . } \end{array}$

If the IM channel is introduced, the retailer will need to pay the influencer a fixed link fee as an admission ticket to showcase its products in the influencer’s live broadcast room. The fixed link fee is positively correlated with both the product complexity (β) and the purchasing experience value (δ) that the influencer provides to consumers. Following previous literature (Ji et al., 2019; Liu & Liu, 2021), we assume that the fixed link fee the retailer needs to pay by cooperating with the influencer is $\frac { \beta \delta ^ { 2 } } { 2 }$ , where the coefficient of the quadratic function β is widely used in the literature related to retailing (Ofek et al., 2011; Yan et al., 2018).

In the current study, product complexity (β) indicates how difficult or easy it is for consumers to comprehend the product’s functionality, benefits, or usage via the IM channel. Products with low complexity are straightforward, simple to understand, and easy to use, leading to faster adoption and greater appeal. Conversely, highly complex products may require more effort to educate consumers or demonstrate their value. For instance, products that consumers are familiar with, such as pure water and Coca-Cola, have low complexity, whereas newly released precision devices have high complexity. The lower the value $\mathrm { o f } \beta ,$ the lower the fixed link fee for collaborating with influencers of a given influence level. Additionally, the cost increment for working with a higher- vs. lower-level influencer is lower.

## 5 Extension

In the previous section, we examined the optimal channel and pricing strategy of the retailer for a single sales period. In practice, introducing the IM channel can increase the exposure and popularity of the retailer’s product, enhancing the consumer’s perceived value in subsequent sales periods. This increased exposure may lead to higher potential sales in the long run. Therefore, the retailer might adopt the IM channel at the initial stage of selling products, even if it does not immediately improve profits in the current sales period. In other words, the retailer may be willing to sacrifice short-term sales to build high awareness of its products and achieve long-term profits. Additionally, impulse purchases driven by the IM channel are not considered in the basic model. Consumers, motivated by the purchasing experience value, may make impulsive buying decisions. However, once they receive the products, this experience value diminishes when they use the products. Consequently, the post-purchase utility for IM channel consumers is lower than the utility at the time of purchase, which can lead to product return.

In this section, we relax the two assumptions to explore the retailer’s optimal channel strategy in two scenarios. First, we consider the situation where the retailer aims to maximize total profit over two sales periods. Second, we examine the case where consumers in the IM channel may return products due to impulsive purchases.

## 6 Conclusion

The advent of live streaming e-commerce—the IM channel and the TO channel with live broadcast service—has created new means for retailers to sell products to consumers. EMarketer reported that global e-commerce sales and retail revenues were projected to exceed 5 trillion USD for the first time in 2022 and would exceed 7 trillion in 2025 USD, signifying the great potential of live streaming e-commerce. <sup>12</sup> Nevertheless, adding an IM channel requires the retailer to pay high fees to the influencer and may lead to demand cannibalization among existing channels (Cao et al., 2016; Luo et al., 2020). Therefore, retailers should carefully evaluate the benefits and costs of the IM channel in their decision-making.

Our research focuses on the impact of adding the IM channel on the retailer’s profits, providing decision support for the retailer. For Scenario 1, where products are sold through the TO channel without live broadcast service, we examine the effect of the IM channel on consumer purchase decisions. We analytically derive the conditions under which it is optimal for the retailer to introduce the IM channel and analyze the impact of the retailer’s optimal channel strategy on social welfare (Fei et al., 2021). Additionally, we explore Scenario 2, where consumers who purchase products through the TL channel are provided with live broadcast service. We identify the outcomes of the interaction between the retailer’s own live broadcast service and the IM channel by comparing the two scenarios. Finally, we extend the basic model of a single sales period to a two sales period model to analyze the influence of the longtail effect of the IM channel on product promotion and the retailer’s channel strategy.

Our paper highlights several interesting and nontrivial findings. First, regardless of whether the retailer provides the live streaming service on its own, the retailer can benefit from introducing the IM channel when it provides high information value or when product complexity is relatively low. Essentially, the IM channel can enhance consumer engagement and sales, making it a valuable addition to the retailer’s strategy, regardless of its existing live streaming efforts. By leveraging the IM channel’s strengths, the retailer can attract more consumers, increase product demand, and ultimately boost profits, even when considering the costs associated with the IM channel.

Second, if the information value advantage of the IM channel is not significantly higher compared to the retailer’s TL channel, if product complexity is relatively high, and if the cost for the retailer to implement its own live streaming service is relatively low, then the retailer’s live streaming service and the IM channel can substitute for each other. In this scenario, the retailer can choose to provide its own live streaming service instead of relying on the IM channel, thereby avoiding the high fixed link fees and leveraging its own cost-effective live streaming capabilities to attract consumers and boost sales. This substitution is particularly advantageous when the retailer’s live streaming service can offer comparable information value to the IM channel. By doing so, the retailer can maintain control over the streaming content, reduce dependency on external influencers, and optimize its overall profitability by minimizing additional costs associated with the IM channel.

Conversely, if the information value of the IM channel is significantly higher than that of the retailer’s live streaming service, if product complexity is relatively low, and if the cost of the retailer’s live streaming service is relatively low, the retailer’s live streaming service and the IM channel can mutually enhance the retailer’s profits. In this scenario, the IM channel’s superior information value attracts more consumers, increasing overall demand. The retailer’s live streaming service can complement this by providing additional engagement and interaction opportunities. This synergy between the IM channel and the retailer’s live streaming service creates a more robust and appealing shopping experience, leading to higher sales and profitability. By leveraging both channels effectively, the retailer can maximize its reach and capitalize on the strengths of each platform, ultimately achieving greater financial success and market presence.

Third, if the long-term impact of the IM channel is considered, the retailer will be more inclined to introduce the IM channel. In other words, a retailer may be willing to forgo short-term profit in the current sales period to introduce the IM channel for higher long-term gains. Although the retailer might experience a dip in short-term profits during the current sales period due to the initial costs and adjustments required to implement the IM channel, the long-term benefits from IM outweigh these initial setbacks. By prioritizing longterm gains, the retailer can build a stronger, more loyal customer base, which will ultimately result in higher overall profits from introducing the IM channel. In essence, the retailer will be willing to sacrifice immediate financial returns to invest in an IM strategy that promises substantial long-term rewards. This forward-thinking approach demonstrates the retailer’s commitment to sustainable growth in the IM channel by focusing on the long-term advantages of IM.

Finally, there is significant consistency regarding the impact of the IM channel on the retailer’s profits and social welfare if consumers do not engage in product return; however, introducing the IM channel may harm consumer surplus and social welfare if consumers in the IM channel choose to return products due to impulse consumption. This means that a retailer’s decision to introduce the IM channel based on maximizing its own profits has a positive effect on social welfare if product return is not considered. The introduction of the IM channel not only enhances the retailer’s profitability but also benefits consumers and influencers. Consumers gain from increased product availability and improved purchasing experiences, while influencers profit from their promotional activities. This alignment of interests creates a win-win situation, where the retailer’s profitdriven decision simultaneously contributes to overall social welfare. However, if product return is taken into consideration, the warm purchasing atmosphere in the live streaming rooms of influencers may drive consumers to make impulsive purchases. This could lead to product return, which would negatively impact the rights of some consumers, ultimately causing a decrease in consumer surplus and social welfare.

This study offers several theoretical contributions. First, it examines the role of the IM channel at various stages of a retailer’s technological maturity, particularly when the retailer has different levels of live streaming technology capabilities. Initially, live streaming ecommerce was only possible through the IM channel. However, as live streaming technologies and the industry have matured, some retailers have begun to offer independent live broadcast services to consumers. This study is one of the first to examine how the IM channel interacts with two different stages of the retailer’s technology capability, TO and TL, and investigate the optimal channel strategies for retailers at different stages of development to introduce IM. The findings provide robust contextual support to the existing knowledge about the roles of live streaming and the IM channel. We highlight the strategic decisions retailers must make as they evolve in their technology capabilities, emphasizing the importance of adapting to technological advancements to maintain a competitive edge and enhance customer engagement. This comprehensive analysis of two different technological stages enriches our understanding of how retailers can effectively leverage both IM and live streaming channels to maximize their businesses.

Second, unlike previous literature on live e-commerce that only considers either the IM channel or the live streaming service of the TL channel, this study is one of the first to comprehensively consider two different simultaneous ways for a retailer to realize live ecommerce. By exploring the interactions between these two channels, we provide insights for online retailers to reap higher profits by making strategic choices when implementing live streaming e-commerce. This dualchannel perspective allows us to understand how the IM channel and the TL channel can complement each other, leading to more robust and effective strategic choices in the live e-commerce era. Our study highlights the importance of considering multiple channels and their interactions to fully leverage the potential of live streaming e-commerce.

Finally, this research delineates the distinct characteristics of the TL channel live broadcast service and the IM channel. Specifically, the TL channel primarily offers utilitarian value (information value), while the IM channel provides both utilitarian value (information value) and hedonic value (purchasing experience value). This dual-value approach allows us to more accurately capture the channel preferences of consumers with varying values and preferences towards the two methods of live e-commerce. To the best of our knowledge, ours is among the first studies to consider both types of values in the relevant live streaming and IM literature. This novel perspective helps us explore how the balance between utilitarian and hedonic values influences consumer behavior and satisfaction in different live streaming and IM e-commerce settings. Understanding the interplay between these values can help in designing more effective marketing strategies that cater to diverse consumer segments.

We also provide the following managerial insights and practical contributions. First, we offer channel strategy guidance for traditional online retailers, considering both the fixed link fee and the additional utility of influencer marketing. Second, we demonstrate that in the live streaming e-commerce era, rational channel decisions by retailers can not only increase their own profits and those of influencers but also enhance consumer surplus to a certain extent. This explores the positive impacts of influencer marketing on all participating parties from a holistic societal perspective. Retailers should consider the IM channel not only for their own benefits but for societal benefits as well, calling on retailers to pay attention to the greater social responsibilities of their businesses. Third, by exploring two scenarios—whether or not the retailer provides live streaming service for consumers—and studying the interaction between the IM channel and the live streaming service of the TL channel, this research provides decision guidance for retailers to achieve the highest profit at the lowest cost. Fourth, by understanding different consumer values and preferences, retailers can tailor their strategies to better meet the needs and desires of their customers. For instance, consumers who prioritize information and efficiency may prefer the TL channel live broadcast service, while those who seek a more engaging and enjoyable shopping experience may gravitate towards the IM channel. This nuanced understanding of consumer behavior can enable retailers to optimize the use of both channels, thereby enhancing customer satisfaction and driving higher sales. Finally, our research suggests that retailers may be willing to forgo short-term profits to introduce the IM channel, anticipating higher long-term returns. Besides singleperiod sales, this research also explores the optimal channel strategy for retailers with multiperiod sales, offering decision guidance for those considering longterm sales in the live e-commerce era.

There are several directions in which to extend our research. First, this study focuses on the “fixed link fee + commission” model in the collaboration between the retailer and the influencer. Because a number of retailers have suffered losses after introducing the IM channel with a high fixed link fee, the “pure commission” model is gradually becoming popular as a cooperation model. Future research could analyze cases in which the influencer only charges commissions on sales. Second, we have only considered utilitarian value and hedonic value from the consumer’s perspective in our models. Future studies could model other types of consumer value, such as social value and emotional value. Future research could also investigate how different consumer demographics respond to these values and how retailers can optimize their channel strategies to maximize engagement and sales. Third, this study explores the retailer’s optimal channel strategy based on the assumption that consumers do not engage in channelswitching behavior. It is more likely that consumers will gather product information through different channels and finally decide on the purchase channel. Consequently, future research could relax this assumption and analyze how the retailer’s optimal channel strategy would be impacted if consumers are involved in channel-switching behavior, for example, by first gaining product information through the IM channel and then making a purchase through the TO channel. Fourth, the two-period sales extension in this study only considers the situation in which the retailer introduces the IM channel in the first period, with the long-term impact of the IM channel being characterized by its positive promotional effect on the subsequent sales of the TO channel. In reality, retailers may continue to cooperate with influencers in the second period. Therefore, exploring the optimal channel strategy for retailers in each period of long-term sales is also a potential direction for future research. Finally, collaborating with influencers is a complex decision influenced by numerous factors, such as selecting the right influencer based on the composition and interactions within their follower network and determining which products to promote. These aspects also present promising avenues for future research.

Her papers have appeared in European Journal of Operational Research, IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management, and Journal of the Operational Research Society. She has also presented her work at the Pre-ICIS Workshop on e-Business and the China Summer Workshop on Information Management.

Giri Kumar Tayi is a professor of management science and information systems at the State University of New York at Albany. His research and teaching interests are interdisciplinary and span the fields of information systems, operations management and operations research. He has published over 70 refereed journal articles. Many of the articles appear in top-tier academic journals. He serves on the editorial boards of top-tier academic journals such as Information Systems Research, Production and Operations Management, Journal of the Association for Information Systems, and Decision Sciences

Dahui Li is the J. Daniel Couger Endowed Chair Professor of Information Systems at College of Business, University of Colorado Colorado Springs. He received his PhD from Texas Tech University. His research interests focus on behavioral decision making, information security management, platform strategy, technology innovation. He has published in several journals, such as Management Science, Journal of the Association for information systems, Information & Management, Decision Support Systems, and Decision Sciences.
