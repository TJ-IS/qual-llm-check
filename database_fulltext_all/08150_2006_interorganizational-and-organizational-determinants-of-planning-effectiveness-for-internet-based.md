# Interorganizational and organizational determinants of planning effectiveness for Internet-based interorganizational systems

Hsiu-Fen Lin <sup>\*</sup>

Department of Information Management, St. John’s University, 499 Sec. 4, Tam King Road Tamsui, Taipei, Taiwan, ROC Received 21 January 2004; received in revised form 11 July 2005; accepted 30 October 2005 Available online 2 December 2005

## Abstract

Internet-based interorganizational systems (IIOS) planning is based on a portfolio of systems that integrate organizational and interorganizational processes and assist a firm in realizing its objectives. However, very limited empirical research has been performed to evaluate the factors that influence IIOS planning effectiveness. Research from trading partnerships, organizational theory, and the literature were used to identify two interorganizational variables (competitive pressure and trading partner readiness) and three organizational variables (top management support, organizational centralization, and technology competence) that are important in planning success. This study then examined the influence of these five variables on IIOS planning effectiveness. Data gathered from 202 CIOs in large Taiwanese organizations were employed to test the relationships between the research model constructs using a structural equation modeling (SEM) approach; the study provided strong support for the hypotheses: certain factors related to interorganizational and organizational factors were found to influence IIOS planning effectiveness. The implications of this finding are discussed. <sup>#</sup> 2005 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved

Keywords: Internet-based interorganizational systems planning; Planning effectiveness; Interorganizational factors; Organizational factors; Structural equation modeling (SEM)

## 1. Introduction

Internet-based interorganizational systems (IIOS), such as extranets, virtual corporation, electronic data interchange (EDI), and business-to-business (B2B) electronic commerce, have significantly influenced business operations and the exchange of business information between trading partners. The importance of planning for IIOS is widely recognized. It provides a business direction for Internet-based IS applications, coordinates the efforts of trading partners, and solves technical and non-technical issues. IIOS planning involves identifying a portfolio of IIOS applications that integrate all processes and allow an organization to enhance linkages between trading partners along the supply chain. Thus, the planning process crosses organizational boundaries; thus companies and their trading partners determine its effectiveness.

IS researchers have examined the planning of IIOS to support interorganizational activity, and various models have been proposed for Internet-based electronic marketplaces [38,41,55]. IIOS require that all participating firms coordinate their efforts and cooperate. There may be complex business relationships between the firms and trading partners, resulting in a number of social and political factors that influence the planning [18,23]. Since the planning of IIOS involves cooperation, various aspects of competitive business environments and trading partnerships have a significant influence on the process [54]. Furthermore, IIOS involve substantial internal operations and efforts and have significant impact. Numerous researchers have therefore indicated that organizational factors (such as management involvement and commitment, organizational structure, and technology readiness) may be expected to influence the planning [19].

The purpose of this study was to examine the influence of interorganizational factors (competitive pressure and trading partner readiness) and organizational factors (top management support, organizational centralization, and technology competence) on IIOS planning effectiveness (planning alignment, improvement in planning capability, and fulfillment of planning objectives).

## 2. Background and research questions

IIOS planning is an important exercise, because corporate success depends on its successful execution [5,27]. The advent of new technologies, such as Internet-based systems, makes the challenge of aligning IS with business more significant and difficult than before [49]. Additionally, involvement in IIOS planning has many benefits; for example, IIOS planning can help an organization use IIOS to implement existing business objectives [10], assess the influence of corporate IIOS investments on existing business activity, and define new business strategies, technological policies, and approaches [25]. However, IIOS planning requires the organization to solve certain problems, such as ensuring adequate knowledge of IIOS planning, convincing top management to approve the process and commit to implementing plans, assessing the current IIOS application portfolio, and adjusting IIOS plans to reflect environmental change [56].

The competitive dynamics of an industry and the way that a firm competes significantly affects the firm’s IS implementation success [7]. Numerous studies have examined the influence of competitive pressure on the use of Internet-based systems for competitive advantage [46,48,59]. Chi et al. [13] also argued that firms evaluate their external business opportunities and threats by assessing their achievement of strategic IS planning objectives. Additionally, much of the research on the influence of trading partners on technology adoption has focused on the implementation of EDI [14,36] and the Internet-based electronic marketplaces [17,43]. For similar reasons, competitive necessity and interorganizational relationships may contribute to successful IIOS planning and implementation.

Notwithstanding the importance of the interorganizational factors, the complete range of systems planning in such environments cannot be realized unless IIOS is integrated with internal IS applications. This planning enables organizations to focus on setting business directions and organizational redesign. The strategybased framework also has been expanded to include environmental, organizational, and technological factors [16,58]. Since IIOS planning is performed in an organizational context, organizational characteristics may significantly influence its success [32,52]. Hence the key objectives of this study are to investigate two research questions:

(1) What is the relationship between interorganizational factors and IIOS planning effectiveness?

(2) What is the relationship between organizational factors and IIOS planning effectiveness?

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

To examine the two questions, a research model was developed; it is displayed in Fig. 1. The model was based on trading partnerships, organizational theory, and IIOS planning literatures [20,47,50]. The model included a set of five antecedent factors (two interorganizational and three organizational) that were expected to influence IIOS planning effectiveness. IIOS planning effectiveness was characterized by a threedimensional construct: planning alignment, improvement in planning capability, and fulfillment of planning objectives.

## 3.1. Relationships among the sub-dimensions planning effectiveness

Planning for IIOS extends internal systems planning by adding Internet-based technology, diverse business strategies, and cooperative arrangements on planning requirements. Based on the research framework of Segars and Grover [50], this study modeled IIOS planning effectiveness as a three-dimensional construct, involving planning alignment, improvement in planning capability and fulfillment of planning objectives. Planning alignment measures the closeness of the IIOS and business strategies. Closeness facilitates acquisition and deployment of Internet-based systems that are congruent with competitive needs. It must also aid in allowing adaptation of the planning process over time to make sure that it remains effective under unexpected environmental and organizational change. Thus, fulfillment of planning objectives is a multifaceted concept that depends on the organizational objectives [29], including increased competitiveness and operational performance, enhanced trading partner relations, and improved information management for strategic planning [15].

Alignment between business and IS objectives makes the firm more adaptive [35]. Segars and Grover [50] also felt that alignment between IS and corporate strategies influenced organizational planning ability. Indeed, increased alignment between business and IS planning reduces IS planning problems and increases the contribution of IS to organizational performance [28,57].

This study conceptualizes the IIOS planning capability in terms of its ability in planning interorganizational systems to analyze IIOS and its associated technologies, and enhance business operations and management performance [31,51]. Moreover, previous studies found a positive relationship between planning capability and IS contribution to business objectives [60]. Similarly, this study expected that effective IIOS planning would provide the organization with a greater IIOS contribution to organizational performance. The following hypotheses are therefore formulated:

H1. Planning alignment positively affects the improvement in IIOS planning capability.

H2. Planning alignment positively affects the fulfillment of IIOS planning objectives.

H3. Improvement in planning capability positively affects the fulfillment of IIOS planning objectives.

## 3.2. Interorganizational factors

Competitive pressure is defined here as the pressure that results from a threat of losing competitive advantage, forcing firms to plan for new business and IS strategies [1]. Corporate e-commerce initiatives and plans may be considered to provide a competitive weapon for coping with competitive environments [42]. Firms that are first-movers in deploying IIOS have tended to derive greater advantage. Thus, the firm has a greater incentive to align IIOS and business strategies and improve their organizational planning capability. The following hypotheses were thus formulated:

H4a. Greater competitive pressure positively affects the IIOS planning alignment.

H4b. Greater competitive pressure positively affects the improvement in IIOS planning capability.

Some IS studies have recognized the significance of trading partner readiness in successful implementing an IIOS. For example, Marshall and Mckay [38] showed that the benefits perceived by trading partners, and the anticipated advantage that IIOS can provide trading partners, significantly influences IIOS planning. Simatupang et al. [53] argued that greater trading partner expertise would provide firms with an external reason for implementing and using IIOS linkages. Additionally, Segars and Grover [50] indicated that partnerships among functional managers and business partners were crucial to the alignment and success of strategic IS planning. Consequently, the following hypotheses were formulated:

## 3.3. Organizational factors

Top management support is important, of course [45]. Numerous studies have found it essential in creating a supportive climate and providing sufficient resources [12]. Moreover, Kearns and Lederer [28] proposed that top management participation encourages other functional managers to join in the process and thus it elicits their knowledge of business processes. Consequently, this study expected that top management support could help and lead to effective IIOS planning. The following hypotheses were therefore formulated:

H6a. Top management support positively affects the IIOS planning alignment.

H6b. Top management support positively affects the improvement in IIOS planning capability.

Organizational centralization is decision-making power concentrated in the hands of a few individuals. Research on IS planning has indicated that there have been problems in achieving internal consistency and effective implementation if decision-making process are excessively centralized. Moreover, Wang and Tai [60] examined the phenomenon of decreasing utilization of needed skills and knowledge in IS planning due to increasing organizational centralization, and found that they hindered improvements in the IS planning process. The following hypotheses were then formulated:

H7a. Organizational centralization negatively affects the IIOS planning alignment.

H7b. Organizational centralization negatively affects the improvement in IIOS planning capability.

The technical competence of a firm is a strong enabler of IS planning success [9,40]. Consistent with previous studies [6,39], this study defined technology competence to consist of IT infrastructure and IT human resources, where IT infrastructure referred to technologies that enable IIOS-related businesses and where IT human resources referred to employees with the knowledge and skills needed to implement IIOS-related applications. IT infrastructure provides a platform for building IIOS, and technical know-how provides the Internet-based skills for developing IIOS applications [37]. Therefore, firms with sufficient technological competence have stronger incentives than firms that lack technology competence to achieve IIOS planning alignment and pursue planning capabilities. The following hypotheses were therefore formulated:

H8a. Greater technology competence positively affects the IIOS planning alignment.

H8b. Greater technology competence positively affects the improvement in IIOS planning capability.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Sample and data collection

The study population consisted of CIOs (chief information officers) in Taiwanese firms. Heijden [22] used CIOs as respondents because of their ability to answer questions related to IIOS planning. A draft questionnaire was pilot tested by three MIS professors to ensure that the content and wording were free of problems. Five CIOs then examined the revised questionnaire; they were given the questionnaire and asked to examine it for meaningfulness, relevance, and clarity.

The sample frame was selected from the 2003 Common Wealth directory of the 1000 largest firms in Taiwan. However, this list did not contain information on IS department. Consequently, to ensure that CIOs received the questionnaire and maximize the response rate, two research assistants spent 2 weeks telephoning these 1000 firms; they asked the target firms whether they had formal IS departments and asked for the name of the CIO or most senior IS executive. Firms with no formal IS departments were removed from the list. This process produced a sample of 820 firms in several different industries. The final questionnaires were mailed to the 820 CIOs in the summer of 2003. A cover letter explaining the study objectives and a stamped return envelope were enclosed. Follow-up letters were sent approximately 3 weeks after the initial mailing.

Of the 820 distributed, 202 completed and usable questionnaires were returned, representing a response rate of 24.6%. All respondents were CIOs who had worked in the IS field for an average of 13.1 years. Respondent’s industries included manufacturing (31.2%), banking/finance/insurance (22.3%), computer/communication (27.2%), and the remainder in other areas such as real estate, construction, health and transportation. 32.7% of the firms had IS departments with fewer than 10 employees, 32.6% had between 10 and 50 employees, 10.4% had between 50 and 100 employees, and 24.3% had over 100 employees.

## 4.2. Operationalization of the variables

All constructs were measured using multiple items; they were measured using a five-point Likert-type scale (ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree). The appendix lists the survey items. IIOS planning effectiveness was measured using threedimensions:

1. A four-item measure modified from Segars and Grover [50] that focused on transformation of the business strategy set to the IIOS strategy and a linkage of IIOS objectives with business objectives to measure the planning alignment.

2. A five-item measure taken from Segars and Grover [50] was used to determine the ability of the IIOS planning to continuously improve support of organizational functions, defined improvement in planning capability; this was modified to assess improvement in planning capability.

3. Four items, derived from the items proposed by Lederer et al. [32] to assess fulfillment of planning objectives through key planning objectives.

Interorganizational and organizational variables were:

1. Competitive pressure, the degree of pressure exerted by competitors on the IIOS implementation decision, assessed by using two items based on guidelines provided by Premkumar and Ramamurthy [44].

2. Trading partner readiness, measured using four items derived from Chwelos et al. [14]; these measure partner willingness and ability to use IIOS.

3. Top management support, assessed by using five items derived from Basu et al. [4].

4. Organizational centralization, measured using a fouritem scale adapted from King and Sabherwal [30]; it assessed the extent to which decision-making responsibility is centralized at the top level of management.

5. Technology competence assessed using three items derived from Zhu and Kraemer [61] to evaluate the technology capability in supporting the IIOS initiatives.

## 5. Results

The SEM approach was used to validate the model. This was chosen because of its ability to test casual relationships between constructs with multiple measurement items [26]. Several researchers have proposed a two-stage model-building process [21,24]. Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was used to examine the reliability and validity of the measurement model, and the structural model was analyzed to test the associations’ hypotheses.

## 5.1. Measuring reliability and validity

The research instrument used CFA. Table 1 summarized the results of convergent validity, the degree to which multiple attempts to measure the same concept are in agreement, for constructs by examining the factor loading within each construct and composite reliability [2]. The factor loading for all items exceeds the recommended level of 0.6. All composite reliabilities measures of constructs exceed the recommended level of 0.7 [3]. Moreover, the correlations of potentially overlapping constructs were used to assess discriminant validity. No pair of measures had correlations exceeding the criterion (0.9 and above), suggested by Hair et al. [21], as shown in Table 2, implying that no multicollinearity existed among the various constructs. The results of the tests for unidimensionality, reliability, and convergent validity and discriminant validity provided evidence of the internal and external validity of the scales used in the study.

## 6. Discussion

## 6.1. Relationship among sub-dimensions of planning effectiveness

This study found support for the hypothesis that IIOS planning alignment positively influences improvement in planning capability. This is consistent with the expectation that IIOS planning alignment is a prerequisite for improvement in planning capability. The results further indicate that IIOS planning alignment and planning capability were positively correlated with fulfillment of planning objectives. As Lee [34] noted, effective IIOS planning helps organizations cope with the competitive environment, but also enhances organizational performance.

## 6.2. Effects of interorganizational factors

The hypothesis that the emergence of competitive pressure is a key influence on both IIOS planning alignment and planning capability was supported. Moreover, trading partner readiness has emerged as a key influence on IIOS planning alignment and improvement in IIOS planning capability. In situations where greater mutual dependence between firms and their trading partners is expected, firms are likely to gain an improved understanding of their trading partners needs and of external opportunities and threats. This will significantly help the firm to achieve its IIOS planning effectiveness.

## 6.3. Effects of organizational factors

Top management support is needed for good IIOS planning alignment and improvement in the planning capability. This is consistent with the fact that lack of top management support and understanding has even caused major IS planning failures [33]. Furthermore, this study found support for the hypothesis that organizational centralization significantly and negatively impacts planning alignment. However, this study did not support the hypothesis that centralization had negatively effects on planning capability. The results further indicated that technology competence correlated positively with IIOS planning alignment and improvement in IIOS planning capability. Consequently, providing sufficient technology resources are critical for firms to pursue effective IIOS planning.

## 7. Conclusion

This study developed a research model that drew upon trading partnerships, organizational theory, and IIOS planning literatures to examine the influences of interorganizational and organizational factors on IIOS planning effectiveness.

## 7.2. Limitations

Several limitations to the work should be addressed. First, there is likely to be a biased response by CIOs in favor of IIOS planning objectives, since the data was collected from a single informant. Second, since the subjects were only in Taiwan and thus findings may involve cultural differences. Third, numerous other factors affect IIOS planning, and this study did not test all of them. Fourth, there were only 202 completed questionnaires, and this is a somewhat small number. Finally, the sample population only included large enterprises in Taiwan and these have greater IIOS maturity than do SMEs; thus the analytical results may have limited generalizability.

## Appendix A. Questionnaire items

## IIOS planning alignment

PA1: Aligning IIOS strategies with the business strategic plan.

PA2: Adapting IIOS objectives to changing goals of the firm.

PA3: Identifying IIOS-related opportunities to support the strategic direction of the firm.

PA4: Adapting Internet-based technology to strategic change.

## Improvement in IIOS planning capability

PC1: Planning capability in identifying key problem areas was improved.

PC2: Planning capability in identifying new business opportunities was improved.

PC3: Planning capability in anticipating surprises and crises was improved.

PC4: Planning capability in understanding the business and its information needs was improved. PC5: Flexibility to adapt to unanticipated changes was improved.

## Fulfillment of IIOS planning objectives

PO1: Increase competitiveness or create strategic advantage.

PO2: Improve trading partner relations

PO3: Improve information management for strategic planning.

PO4: Increase in efficiency of interorganizational transaction processing.

## Competitive pressure

CP1: My organization experienced competitive pressure to implement IIOS.

CP2: My organization would have experienced a competitive disadvantage if IIOS had not been adopted.

## Trading partner readiness

TPR1: The majority trading partners requested implementation of IIOS.

TPR2: The majority trading partners recommended implementation of IIOS.

TPR3: Trading partners are generally very knowledgeable regarding technical matters.

TPR4: Trading partners contain considerable technical expertise.

## Top management support

TMS1: Top management champions the IIOS planning study.

TMS2: Top management provides feedback and guidance throughout the IIOS planning study.

TMS3: Top management believes the cost of IIOS planning study is a long-term investment.

TMS4: Top management actively participates in IIOS planning study.

TMS5: Top management supports adequate resource allocation for IIOS planning study.

## Organizational centralization

OC1: Extent to which decision-making responsibility concerning capital budgeting is centralized at the highest levels of management.

OC2: Extent to which decision-making responsibility concerning new product introduction is centralized at the highest levels of management.

OC3: Extent to which decision-making responsibility regarding entry into major new markets is centralized at the highest levels of management.

OC4: Extent to which decision-making responsibility regarding pricing of major product lines is centralized at the highest levels of management.

## Technology competence

TC1: Availability of firm technology infrastructure for supporting IIOS-related businesses (e.g. extranet, Internet-based EDI, and B2B e-commerce).

TC2: My organization is dedicated to ensuring that employees are very familiar with Internet-based technology.

TC3: My organization contains a high level of IIOS knowledge.
