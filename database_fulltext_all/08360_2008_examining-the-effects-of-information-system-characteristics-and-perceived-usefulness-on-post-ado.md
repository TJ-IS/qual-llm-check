# Examining the effects of information system characteristics and perceived usefulness on post adoption usage of information systems

Khawaja A. Saeed \*, Sue Abdinnour-Helm <sup>1</sup>

W. Frank Barton School of Business, Wichita State University, 1845 Fairmount, Wichita, KS 67260, USA

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Article history: Available online 21 July 2008

Keywords: TAM (Technology acceptance model) IS success model Post adoption usage Structural equation modeling

## A B S T R A C T

Understanding post adoption behavior has emerged as an important issue in IS research. Organizations have invested in a plethora of Information Systems (IS) and the benefits that can be gained from these systems depend on their usage. Prior research has examined factors that impact users’ intention to continue using the IS. However, comprehending post adoption IS usage behavior has not received much attention. Building on TAM and IS success model, this study proposes that information quality and system integration influence perceived IS usefulness which will then drive post adoption usage of the IS. Post adoption IS usage is conceptualized as a broad concept that includes extended usage and exploratory usage. Data (N = 1032) was collected to test the model, in the context of a web-based student information system that students use to manage their academic work. The results show that at the post adoption stage, perceived IS usefulness is a good predictor of extended usage and exploratory usage. However, surprisingly IS usefulness was found to explain a much larger variance in exploratory usage. Information quality and system integration were found to influence IS usefulness. However, information quality also has a direct affect on extended usage, while system integration directly influenced exploratory usage. Assessment of the moderating role of gender and internet experience on model relationships reveals interesting insights. Implications are drawn for future research and practice.

## 1. Introduction

Organizations have invested in a plethora of Information Systems (IS). As the maturity of IS in organizations has increased, interest in examining IS usage at the post adoption stage has also grown. Research on innovation diffusion offers insights on the different stages (pre and post adoption) of IS implementation within organizations [1]. Jasperson et al. [2] define post adoptive behavior as ‘‘myriad of feature adoption decisions, feature use behaviors, and feature extension behaviors made by an individual user after an IT application has been installed, made accessible to the user, and applied by the user in accomplishing his/her work activities’’. At the post adoption stage, users after experiencing the IS form perceptions based on that experience and develop different interaction patterns with the IS, which depicts their post adoption behavior. This study builds on the technology acceptance model (TAM) and IS success model to examine how user’s post adoption perceptions and experience with the IS shapes their post adoption IS usage behavior.

A large body of research focuses on examining initial IS acceptance and success. Innovation diffusion model, technology acceptance model, theory of planned behavior, and IS success model present various factors that play a role in shaping user’s perceptions, intentions, and behavior towards an IS [3–7]. Recent research emphasizes that user perceptions and IS usage at the post adoption stage is an under examined area and needs more attention [2]. Understanding this issue can provide insights on factors that can be leveraged to promote effective utilization of IS after its initial acceptance.

This study has three objectives. First, we will examine how IS characteristics, namely system integration and information quality, influence perceived IS usefulness at the post adoption stage. Past research demonstrates that perceived IS usefulness is an important user perception that holds its influence both at the pre and post adoption phases (Venkatesh and Davis, [40]; [8,9]). Second, we will look at what role perceived IS usefulness plays in promoting post adoption IS usage by looking at two concepts: extended usage and exploratory usage. Finally, we will examine how gender and internet experience influence the relationships between IS characteristics, perceived IS usefulness, and post adoption IS usage.

## 2. Literature review

At the post adoption stage, IS is infused within the organization and becomes an integral part of the work system. Using the IS in different work contexts can trigger an assessment of the value of IS along with evaluation of its functions and features. Actual interaction with the IS can thus reinforce or change initial user perceptions. Users can even develop new cognitions regarding the IS after interacting with it [10]. Prior studies that examine IS usage from a longitudinal perspective have found that IS usefulness is a critical perception that drives intention to continue using the IS [8,9]. Venkatesh and Davis (2000) found that IS usefulness depicts a consistent impact on future intention to use IS and also mediates the impact of other factors on IS usage intentions. Further, Seddon and Kiew [11] propose IS usefulness as a central success factor in the modified version of the IS success model. Expanding on its nomological network, the authors argue that perceptions regarding the attributes of the IS (system quality and information quality) are likely to influence the users cognition regarding IS usefulness.

The antecedents to IS usefulness in the literature (see Table 1), mainly examine factors such as personality, social influence, and facilitating conditions. Users’ perception regarding the characteristics of the IS as an antecedent to IS usefulness has not been effectively addressed in the literature. Users accumulate substantial use experience after initial acceptance of the IS. Users perceptions regarding various characteristics of the IS should play an important role in their assessment of how well the IS supports their work. Nielson et al. [12] offer extensive perspectives on information quality and system quality as two critical components that constitute the overall IS quality. They argue that information quality and system quality need to be assessed with respect to how helpful these factors are in completion of a particular task. Seddon and Kiew [11] propose that perceptions regarding information quality and system quality are key drivers of IS usefulness. This is based on the premise that IS that offers a higher quality experience will be regarded as useful and may be used more [13].

In addition to assessing user perceptions at the post adoption stage, understanding user behavior is also a critical issue. Assessment of prior research in this area reveals a predominant focus on intention to continue using the IS and intention to explore the IS (see Table 2). Although, IS continuance intention is a critical issue, we believe that understanding post adoption IS usage that captures actual behavior is also important. The user’s behavior may range from minimal use of the IS to total integration of the IS in his her work [14]. Further, users differ on the extent to which they explore new and innovative ways to apply the IS in their work [15]. Saga and Zmud [33] propose expansion, integration, and extension as likely behavioral modes the users may pursue at the post adoption stage. Users can develop a standard interaction approach in using the IS, which limits effective utilization of the arrays of features that the IS has to offer. Extended usage, which cumulatively captures the concept of both frequency and breadth of IS usage, relates to the extent to which the users leverage the different features of the IS. Leidner and Elam [16] in the context of Executive Information Systems (EIS) propose usage as the frequency with which the executives use the EIS. Similarly, Devaraj and Kohli [17] use frequency of accessing reports and records and computer resources for capturing IS usage. Thus, the frequency with which the different features of the IS are used reflects the user’s IS usage preference and is a critical aspect of post adoption usage behavior. We term this behavior at the post adoption stage as extended usage and propose that it subsumes the expansion concept proposed by Saga and Zmud [33].

Users may also embark upon exploring new uses of the IS within their work. Ahuja and Thatcher [18] propose the concept of trying to innovate with IT and define it as the user’s goal of finding new uses of existing workplace information technologies. Experience with an IS can trigger user’s cognitions regarding how it can be used in novel ways within the context of their work. Jasperson et al. [2] emphasize exploration of new uses of the IS as a critical post adoptive behavior that needs to be examined. We term this behavior as exploratory usage, which is similar to the extension concept proposed by Saga and Zmud [33].

Prior research provides guidance on factors that may moderate model relationships. A thorough review of literature on IS usage intentions shows that gender and experience are two moderating variables that influence model relationships consistently across various studies [19]. Gender and experience impact the relationship between IS usefulness and intention to use IS [6,7,19]. Researchers have found that the strength of this relationship is stronger for males and users with higher experience with the IS. Thus, assessing the moderating role of gender and experience at the post adoption phase can provide interesting insights.

Cumulatively, the literature review brings forward four critical perspectives. First, IS usefulness emerges as a critical perception at the post adoption phase that mediates the relationship between exogenous factors and IS usage. Second, limited research examines how perceptions regarding the characteristics of the IS impact IS usefulness. Third, post adoption IS usage emerges as a broad concept that needs to not only address current usage, but also incorporate exploration of how IS can be used to support work. Finally, examining the moderating role of gender and experience at the post adoption stage can offer interesting perspectives. Building on these insights we develop the research model next.

## 3. Research model

TAM and the IS success model are the two theoretic perspectives that contribute towards the development of the research model (see Fig. 1). TAM proposes IS usefulness as a critical perception that holds its influence even at the post adoption phase [8,19]. Further, the IS success model argues that users’ perceptions regarding IS characteristics can influence their perceptions regarding IS usefulness [11,20]. This preposition is based on the premise that users’ experience with the IS provides them with ample opportunity to evaluate how well the IS performs in supporting their work. Thus, assessment of information quality and system integration (an aspect of system quality, which is defined as the extent to which the IS brings together multiple systems and offers access to such systems through a unified interface) through repeated interaction with the IS will influence their perception regarding the utility of the IS.<sup>2</sup> Users’ IS usefulness perception will then shape their post adoption IS usage behavior, which may involve greater use of IS features and/ or exploration of new ways of using the IS. Finally, the research model proposes that gender and internet experience are likely to moderate the model relationships. The next section elaborates on specific hypotheses.

## 4. Hypotheses

## 4.1. Information quality, system integration, and usefulness

Information and system quality are two critical IS characteristics that determine the overall success of an IS. DeLone and McLean [20] and more recently Nielson et al. [12] provide an extensive assessment of these aspects and elaborate on what role these factors play in IS adoption and usage. Quality aspect of the IS characteristics need to be assessed in the context of the users’ task or work environment [12]. At the post adoption stage, actual interaction with the IS shapes the users’ mental representation of its characteristics. Repeated interaction with the IS may also help the users identify gaps between their expectations and the IS capabilities in the context of their work. This iterative process helps the user in formulating perceptions regarding how well the IS characteristics assist in his or her work/task performance. Jasperson et al. [2] argue that experience with using the IS to conduct various tasks can invoke a deeper understanding of its characteristics, which can assist the user in forming cognitions about its overall usefulness.

Users rely on the information accessible through the IS in effectively performing their work. IS that provides bad data can adversely impact a user’s job performance and consequently alter his/her perceptions regarding its usefulness. For example, a user will develop a negative perception regarding the value of an IS if he/she makes a decision based on information that turns out to be inaccurate. On the contrary, IS that provides high quality information will be regarded as useful because it helps the user in making sound decisions and improves his/her work performance. Thus, appreciation of the quality of information generated through the IS is likely to influence the user’s value assessment of the IS. Thus we argue:

H1(a). Information quality will be positively related to IS usefulness.

Users with diverse needs have to rely on IS functionalities and features that reside on different systems. Users have to expend more effort in completing their work if they have to interact with multiple IS. Further, the complexity of work increases because the user has to construct a global view of available IS and comprehend the overall configuration of the functionalities offered by the different IS. If an IS is configured in a way that enables the user to access the required IS functionalities and IS based services through a single interface, it reduces the complexity and the effort expended to access the services. For example, portals are a recent phenomenon that offer a single location through which the user can gain access to diverse services. User’s perception regarding IS usefulness will be strengthened, if the IS unifies the diverse services and makes them accessible at a central location. Thus, we argue that users’ perception regarding the value of the IS will be enhanced if the IS provides access to multiple services through a single integrated interface.

H1(b). System integration will be positively related to IS usefulness.

## 4.2. Usefulness and IS usage

Past research has shown that IS which is deemed to be useful is used more [19]. Previous research has also established the link between relative advantage of the IS and its adoption [21]. However, studies on IS usage propose that usage is multidimensional (Massetti and Zmud, [30]). So, it is important to first comprehend the concept of post adoption IS usage and then assess how perceived IS usefulness may impact it. Our conceptualization of IS usage subsumes two perspectives namely extended usage and exploratory usage. Overall, we argue that after having constructed the perception regarding usefulness of the IS, the user will be more willing to effectively integrate the IS within their work environment as well as experiment with the IS in applying it in novel ways in their work.

Extended usage captures the breadth and frequency of using different IS features and functions. We argue that if the user finds the IS to be useful, he or she will be more inclined to fully examine and use its features and functions. For example, while interacting with the web-based Student Information System (SIS), the IS used in the context of our study, a student may use certain features that support their routine tasks (check their grades, register for classes, etc.). If such an interaction establishes that the SIS is configured to support their academic work well, they may use it to conduct a major portion of their work and increase their frequency of its usage. Thus we propose:

H2(a). IS usefulness will be positively related to extended usage.

Exploratory usage captures active examination of new uses of the IS. The use of SIS in our study was voluntary and administrators regularly added new features to SIS. Thus, students had the opportunity to continuously explore new uses of SIS every time they logged on to the website. For example, students may explore easier ways to use certain features (e.g. shortcuts), explore new features (e.g. accessing the schedule of classes) or do more with existing features (e.g. download grades and track them in a spreadsheet). The possibility of such exploratory behavior is much stronger if the user has established cognitions regarding the usefulness of the website in supporting their tasks. Thus, we argue:

H2(b). IS usefulness will be positively related to exploratory usage.

## 4.3. Mediating role of usefulness

Prior research on IS acceptance and usage argues that IS usefulness is a central belief that mediates the impact of external factors on intention to use the IS. The mediation argument is mainly based on the logic that external factors (i.e. IS characteristics) influence the users’ beliefs about the value of performing a behavior, which then drives behavioral intentions (Wixom and Todd [12]). Thus, we argue:

H3. IS usefulness will mediate the relationship between IS characteristics and IS usage.

## 4.4. Moderating role of gender and internet experience

Past research emphasizes various factors that may act as moderators in examining IS usage phenomenon. Many studies have assessed the role of gender and found that perceptions and usage do differ across gender [22,23]. In the context of computer conferencing it was found that men tend to post information items, while women tend to engage in discussion [24]. Gender schema theory proposes that the difference across gender can result from cognitions related to gender roles (Lynott and McCandless, [38]). Males tend to be more task oriented and may place more value on an IS that helps them in effective task completion (Minton and Schnieder, [39]). Males will tend to expand the use of IS and explore it more if they believe that using the IS has helped them in improving their work. Earlier studies have found that the relationship between IS usefulness and intention to use the IS is stronger in males than females [6,7,22]. However, research regarding the moderating role of gender on the relationship between IS characteristics and IS usefulness has not been addressed in prior literature. It can be argued that task centricity orientation which is more dominant in males will impact this relationship. Males will depict a stronger relationship between IS characteristics and IS usefulness because of their stronger focus on task performance. Thus, we argue that the model relationships will be stronger for males as compared to females.

H4. The model relationships will be moderated by gender, such that the relationships will be stronger for males than females.

Since this study examines a web-based information system, internet experience is deemed as an important moderator. Users that have used the internet for a longer period are likely to have a boarder exposure to web sites. Experience may lead to the formation of a more coherent cognition regarding what characteristics of the IS make it more useful. The users with varying levels of internet experience may weigh IS characteristics differently. This difference can reflect on the formation of their perceptions regarding the IS usefulness. Further, this difference may also manifest itself on post adoption IS usage. Research that examines the moderating role of experience has found that the relationship between relative advantage (a variable similar to IS usefulness) and post adoption IS usage is stronger for user’s with higher level of experience [6,7]. However, it should be noted that prior research examines direct experience with the IS. Internet experience constitutes a different perspective, because it captures the interaction of the user with other systems on the internet and not their direct experience with using a specific website. More experience in interacting with the web-based system does alter users’ cognitions and behavior, because of the commonality in the interaction approach and the features available across web sites.

We argue that model relationships between IS characteristics and IS usefulness will be higher for users with more internet experience. Further, inline with prior findings, we also propose that the relationship between IS usefulness and IS usage will be stronger for users with more extensive internet experience.

H5. The model relationships will be moderated by internet experience, such that the relationships will be stronger for users with higher internet experience than for users with lower internet experience.

## 5. Participants and research methodology

The study was conducted in the context of a web-based Student Information System (SIS). SIS was developed by a university in the Midwest region and offers the students various types of functionalities to support their academic work. Advising officers introduce the SIS to students when they enroll for courses. The usage of SIS is completely voluntary. The tasks that SIS supports could be completed through other means at the time this study was conducted.

Data was collected from the students by administering the questionnaire in different classes. The participation in the research study was completely voluntary and this was emphasized before the distribution of the questionnaire in every class. Subjects provided their approval to participate by signing an informed consent. As an incentive, the students were told that they might be able to win a prize based on a draw to be held at the conclusion of the data collection effort. To maintain subject anonymity, a separate sheet of paper was given to the subjects. On that sheet, subjects provided contact information that was used to distribute the prizes after the completion of the study.

A primary objective in the data collection process was to get representation of students from all the major colleges in the university. Classes were identified in each college and the instructors were contacted to gain approval for administering the questionnaire in their classes. The data collection effort yielded 1032 responses. Table 3 provides the profile of the subjects. Eighty six percent of the subjects had more than 7 years of experience in using the computer and ninety five percent had more than 4 years of internet usage experience (Table 3). The gender distribution was fairly equal with 53% females and 47% males. The average experience of using SIS was two and half years.

## 5.1. Scale development

Pre-existing scales were used for perceived IS usefulness, information quality, and system integration [3,12]. A new instrument was developed and validated for measuring post adoption usage of IS. Table 4 provides the list of items for the constructs. The development of the instrument for post adoption IS usage involved four stages. At the first stage, a thorough review of the literature was undertaken for comprehending the conceptual structure of the construct and subsequent item creation. In the second stage, six people who had been using SIS for some time, participated in a focus group to examine the use of the system within their work environment. The main objective of the focus group was to conduct an initial refinement of the items and further comprehend the concept of post adoption IS usage. The input included information on the overall feature set of SIS, additional features that students may like on SIS, and clarity of the survey and items. These inputs provided by the participants of the focus group were used to modify the items. Next a q-sorting exercise was conducted wherein six judges sorted the items based on definitions provided to them. Modifications were made to the items based on the results of the sorting exercise and the suggestions provided by the judges. The final step involved construct validation based on the data collected on a large-scale.

## 5.2. Construct validity analysis

Agarwal and Prasad [28] suggest using both exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) for assessing construct validity. They argue that EFA falls within the classical approaches for examining construct validity, while CFA is part of the contemporary approaches. Exploratory factor analysis was done by employing principal components extraction with varimax rotation on items consisting of extended usage and exploratory usage. After getting satisfactory results from factor analysis, CALIS procedure in SAS was used for CFA (Table 5). The fit indices for the measurement model were found to be at the acceptable level [25]. Convergent validity was examined through composite reliability. Composite reliability of 0.80 for both extended usage and exploratory usage is above the recommended guideline of 0.70 [25]. Fornell and Larcker [26] suggest that discriminant validity is established if the square root of average variance extracted (AVE) for individual construct is greater than the correlation of that construct with other constructs. Table 6 (diagonal elements show AVE) shows that this condition is met in all cases. Overall, the newly developed measures show excellent psychometric properties.

## 6. Results

The model fit indices show that the data fit the model well. Fig. 2 shows that the indices are above the levels recommended in the literature [25]. Significance of the path loadings provides the results for the hypotheses. The results support H1(a) and (b), showing that system integration (0.30) and information quality (0.58) are significant predictors of user perceptions regarding IS usefulness, explaining 67% of the variation in IS usefulness (Fig. 2). Further, H2(a) and (b) were also supported indicating that IS usefulness positively and significantly relates to extended usage (0.29) and exploratory usage (0.68) of the IS. We found a direct and significant impact of information quality on extended usage (0.30) and system integration on exploratory usage (0.14). Thus, H3 was only partially supported. It is interesting to note that the constructs explain 31% of variance in extended usage, but cause 62% of the variation in exploratory usage. Thus, the model performs much better in explaining exploratory usage behavior at the post adoption phase. Overall, the results establish users’ perception about the IS characteristics as pivotal in driving their IS usefulness perception. Further, if users are convinced that the IS effectively supports their work at the post adoption stage, they not only extend its usage but also experiment with how to apply the IS in novel settings.

IS continuance intention is a critical issue addressed by prior research, but it is also important to comprehend post adoption IS usage behaviors and the factors that drive them. Our study shows that perceived IS usefulness, which has been found to influence initial acceptance of IS, also plays an important role in facilitating post adoption IS usage. Users’ favorable cognitions regarding use performance trigger frequent use of features and functions offered by the IS and exploration of new uses of the IS in the context of academic work. However, the results show a stronger linkage between IS usefulness and exploratory usage. A probable reason for this finding may be that users’ cognition regarding IS usefulness strengthens his or her perceptions about the latent potential of the IS that can be leveraged to gain greater benefits. Good experience with a technology can contribute towards the recognition of its untapped potential and trigger exploratory behavior in terms of probing its new uses and application in new contexts. Users are thus more inclined to explore new uses of the IS in the hopes of further unlocking its potential as compared to expanding the frequency of the features of IS currently in use.

Information quality and system integration jointly contributed towards forming the user’s mental representation regarding the usefulness of the IS. Direct impact of information quality on extended usage and system quality on exploratory usage was also observed. This indicates that usefulness only acts as a partial mediator for these relationships. Thus, these two IS characteristics offer critical levers that can be used to influence the user’s perceptions regarding value of the IS and also drive post adoption IS usage directly. Limited research has focused on examining the characteristics of the IS that are regarded as important in forming overall understanding of the usefulness of the IS in a work environment [13]. This study fills that gap and suggests system integration and information quality as two important factors that alter the IS usefulness perception and facilitate optimal use of the IS.

It is interesting to note that there is direct relationship between information quality and extended usage and system integration and exploratory usage. However, the impact of information quality on exploratory usage and the impact of system integration on extended usage is fully mediated by IS usefulness. These results are contrary to the proposed hypotheses, but are understandable because if the users perceive that the system provides high quality information they will use it more extensively. Further, user’s perception about the ability of the system to provide access to multiple services through a single interface drives exploratory behavior. The availability of diverse services can create conditions more amenable to applying the IS in new ways. So contrary to what has been proposed in prior research, it is important that the future studies in this area, explicitly assess the direct influence of external factors on post adoption IS usage.

Analysis was conducted to examine structural invariance of the research model across gender (male vs. female) and internet experience (low vs. high) to test H4 and H5 (Figs. 3–6). The model was rerun but constrained so that structural paths were equal across the baseline model and contingent models. For example, in case of gender the path loadings (structural paths) obtained from running the baseline model (complete dataset) were used to fix the path loadings for the female group (female dataset) and the male group (male dataset). Chi square difference test was used to assess structural invariance. The model for the female group was found to be significantly (p-value < 0.05) different from the baseline model. However, no significant difference was observed between the baseline model and the male group. After variance is detected across the models, the next step is to assess the cause for the variance. Since the cause of variance is path loadings, model fits are compared through chi square difference test again. However, this is done by constraining individual path loadings one at a time, while allowing the free estimation of the other path loadings. For example, the path loading of information quality to usefulness is fixed and all other path loadings are freely estimated.

The chi squares of the constrained models are compared with the chi square of the model for the female group. The results show that the linkage between information quality and usefulness is the main cause of the structural variance across the baseline model and the female group (p-value < 0.05). We also compared the female and male groups following the same procedure. The chi square difference test showed that the models are significantly different (p-value < 0.05). Similar to what was found earlier, the main cause for the variance across the groups was the linkage between information quality and IS usefulness. Thus, the results ascertain that the information quality exhibits a stronger relationship with IS usefulness for the female group. Thus, the results do not support H4 which argued for stronger model relationships for the male group. On the contrary, females were found to rely more on quality of information offered by the website in judging the value of the IS.

Structural invariance was also examined across groups with varying internet experience. The chi square difference tests revealed that the group with high internet experience was significantly different from the baseline model (p-value < 0.05). The high internet experience group was also found to be significantly different from the low internet experience group (p-value < 0.05). The relationships between information quality, system integration, and IS usefulness were found to be the main cause for the structural variance across the high and low internet experience groups. The group with high internet experience placed more value on information quality characteristic in forming there IS usefulness perceptions. Further, this group seems to place less value on system integration as compared to the low internet group in forming their IS usefulness perceptions. Other model relationships were invariant across the structural models. Thus, H5 was only partially supported.

Overall, the analysis for the moderating role of gender and internet experience shows no support for H4 and only partial support for H5. We observe that these contingencies influence model relationships. The stronger influence of IS usefulness on post adoption IS usage, which has been found in earlier studies, was not observed. Path loadings of the linkages between IS usefulness, extended usage, and exploratory usage were similar across users with varying internet experience. Users with different internet experience incorporate information quality and system integration differently in forming their perception about the usefulness of the IS. Users with more internet experience value accessibility to various resources, but put more weight on the quality of information offered by the web-based system in forming their assessment regarding the overall usefulness of the IS. On the contrary, users with less internet experience are more balanced regarding information quality and system integration in forming IS usefulness perceptions.

In summation, the results of the study offer four unique contributions. First, the results of the overall model testing show that information quality and system integration are two IS characteristics that alter the IS usefulness perceptions and can promote different usage patterns (extended usage and exploratory usage). Further, IS usefulness perceptions are a better predictor of exploratory usage as compared to extended usage. Second, the direct influence of information quality on extended usage and system integration on exploratory usage is an interesting insight.

## 7. Implication for research and practice

The study opens up various avenues for future research. First, the study proposes and tests post adoption IS usage as a multidimensional concept. Researchers can further develop the conceptualization offered in this study. It would be interesting to investigate whether post adoption IS usage can be modeled as a generic concept or needs to be examined from a specific IS perspective. Jasperson et al. [2] emphasized that post adoption IS usage needs to be captured at the function or feature level. Although, such an approach may increase the efficacy of measurement, it can also limit the application of the measurement approach in other situations. Further, feature level usage is task specific and not all individuals perform all tasks. Thus, it would be interesting to assess the efficacy of various approaches to capturing and measuring IS usage at the post adoption stage.

The study also highlights information quality and system integration as directly impacting the different dimensions of post adoption IS usage. Most studies that examine usefulness at the preadoption phase propose that IS usefulness is a central belief and the impact of other beliefs and perceptions filters through it. However, the results of our study show that this assertion may not hold in all cases at the post adoption stage. Future studies should examine the direct impact of exogenous variables on post adoption IS intentions and usage. Recent studies offer elaborate perspectives on IS characteristics and future studies can draw on these perspectives to examine more holistic models on the role of IS characteristics at the post adoption stage. Further, our study found IS usefulness to be an equally good predictor of extended usage and exploratory usage across gender, contrary to earlier studies that have found IS usefulness as a stronger predictor of usage intention for males. This issue needs to be examined further. Finally, the asymmetric impact of information quality and system integration on IS usefulness across gender and internet experience, suggests that it would be interesting to examine how the effect of other perceptions on IS usefulness may also differ across these contingencies.

The study offers various implications for IS managers. First, it offers a comprehensive approach towards understanding the post adoption IS usage phenomenon. Usage of the IS is an important barometer of success. The measurement approach offered in the study can be used by managers to better evaluate post adoption usage of IS. The results also provide insights into two characteristics of the IS that play a pivotal role in user’s assessment of how well the IS can support their tasks. Promotion of these two IS characteristics across the user community can provide positive results. Further, such promotion can be targeted to different gender groups and groups that have different level of internet experience. Depending on which aspect of post adoption IS usage is given priority, the results offer guidance to managers on which IS characteristics need to be promoted and actively incorporated in the IS.

Better utilization of the IS, that is currently in use, has become an important issue in most organizations. IS Managers can use the results of this study to devise strategies on how to effectively promote the IS in their organization. IS implementation literature proposes effective usage of IS as one of the success factors. This study not only enhances the understanding of IS managers regarding how to assess success at the post adoption phase, but also provides insights on what characteristics of the IS can increase the likelihood of that success.

The study also has some limitations. First, the measurement of extended usage is specific to the web-based Student Information System. This approach has limited applicability in the context of other IS. However, features available in other IS can be used to capture the breadth of feature level usage. Further, we used a twoitem measure to capture information quality and system integration. The measures were based on prior work and due to the results of the factor analysis we had to drop one item for each factor. Even though this is a concern, prior studies on IS implementation and adoption such as Bagozzi et al. [27] and Venkatesh and Davis [40] have used two items to measures user perceptions such as job relevance and output quality.

## 8. Conclusion

The study offers insights into user perceptions and behavior with respect to an IS at the post adoption stage. In line with prior studies, we found that IS usefulness is a critical perception that impacts post adoption IS usage. Further, we found that information quality and system integration are two important characteristics of the IS that contribute towards the formation of the overall assessment of the value of the IS, but also directly influence certain usage behaviors. The maturation of IS in organizations has increased the importance of better utilization of the IS and consequently understanding its drivers. We hope that future research can build on the findings of this study and offer greater insights into the concept and antecedents of post adoption usage of IS.
