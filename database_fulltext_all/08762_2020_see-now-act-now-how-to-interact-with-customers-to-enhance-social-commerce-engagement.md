## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Live interaction Live broadcasting Customer engagement Perceived risk Psychological distance

## A B S T R A C T

Although live social commerce apps have emerged as a marketing channel recently, live social commerce, as a relatively new phenomenon, has attracted little attention. The authors aim to explore the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement based on the stimulus–organism–response paradigm and the moder ating efect of susceptibility to informative influence. The findings show that live interactions (i.e., personali zation, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, perceived control) positively afect perceived usefulness and negatively impact perceived risk and psychological distance, promoting social commerce engagement. The results provide theoretical insights on live interactions and social e-commerce and contribute managerial im plications for practitioners.

## 1. Introduction

Although social commerce has become an important focused field and hot topic for scholars and practitioners, live broadcasting social ecommerce, a relatively novel business model, allows dynamic interaction between live anchors and consumers and promotes consumers to engage in active social commerce [1,2]. Difering from the traditional ecommerce model, the distinct advantage of live social commerce is the interaction between anchors and interested customers by the virtual face-to-face communication way based on live broadcasting. During the Double 11 shopping festival in 2017, users spent 12.778 million hours residing in live broadcasting rooms, which raised the total sales by 2818 % over the same period in 2016 [3]. The gross merchandise volume (GMV) (turnover) of the social commerce app (SCA), Mogujie, was 16.978 billion RMB in 2018, with an increase of 21.5 % over the previous year, and the number of mobile MAU (monthly active users) on broadcasted live of Mogujie continued to grow by 43.6 % in 2018 [4]. During the Double 12 shopping festival in 2018, more than 70,000 online live broadcasts of Taobao live contributed to a 160 % increase over the last year [5]. Taobao live realized a turnover of about 14.93 billion dollars in 2018 increased by 400 %, and over 600,000 varieties of commodities were sold via the app [6].

Live social shopping is seen as a novel marketing model consisting of “live broadcast + social + e-commerce”, in which consumers can participate in live broadcasting activities through mobile SCAs, and then the anchors can interact with customers and show various details of their commodities to consumers by means of strategic explanation, even trialing or fitting products in person, so that consumers can more truly and intuitively understand the information of commodities and can share social commerce information with their friends or families. The active and passive participation of users can be completed through the real-time interaction between anchors and consumers as well as the real-time interaction between consumers, and eventually the commodity transformation will be realized. This is because live social ecommerce can handle the three tricky problems unsolved all along in traditional e-commerce context: (1) the absence of pleasurable experiences rooted in the impersonal nature of traditional web-based shopping; (2) the poor interaction between e-retailers and consumers increases the risk perception on products; and (3) insuficient display of commodity information and embellished pictures about products provided by e-retailers. An interesting exemplar of live SCAs is Mogujie, a leading live social commerce platform in China, which combines live streaming with e-commerce and permits anchors to promote their products by live broadcasting.

How should live broadcasting anchors interact with customers to promote social commerce engagement (SCE) and to maximize the transaction chance? How do live broadcasting anchors display their products to reduce the risk perception of customers and enhance their behavioral engagement? How does susceptibility to informative influence of consumers afect their behavioral engagement? These issues are very critical in both theory and practice for scholars and practitioners, but no empirical research so far has examined the live interactions and their efects on social commerce engagement.

Because the success of live social commerce relies on live interac tions between anchors and customers and customer engagement [7–9], it is essential to explore the operationalization of live interactions and the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement. Previous studies show that interaction can be divided based on the three perspectives: technological feature, user perception, and process approach [10]. While prior literature on online interaction has mainly focused on machine interaction from the technological features perspective [11–16] and interpersonal interaction between individuals and organization from the user perception perspective [17–19], few studies explore live interactions among objects from the process approach perception, including consumer–consumer interaction and seller–consumer interaction in social commerce context [20]. Tajvidi et al. [20] argued that consumer–seller interactions and consumer–consumer interactions have a positive efect on social support on social commerce sites and then impact the intention to co-create brand value. Wang et al. [21] suggested that interactivity has a positive efect on perceived utilitarian value of marketing messages and positively afects hedonic value, and then positively impacts the intention to repost. However, little is known about how live interactions between anchors and consumers or among consumers impact social commerce engagement. Consistent with this line of research, this study aims to explore the definition and operationalization of live interactions and how live interactions enhance customers’ social commerce engagement.

Previous studies on online computer-mediated communication (CMC) and media ofer theoretical foundations for our research from the perspectives of (1) choice and (2) performance of media and tech. nology [22]. The literature has found that richness and interaction are conceptually distinct [23]. Richness involves the amount of conveyed and received content information [23]. but interaction involves the extent to which media (e.g., Mogujie app in our study) will make users exert influences on the content or form [11]. Existing studies have fo cused mainly on media richness to explore the efect of perceived in teractive richness based on Social Presence Theory [22-25]. Media richness refers to the relative ability of a communication channel to deliver messages including rich information and is a set of obiective features including multiple cues, language variety, immediate feedback, and personal focus, which determine a media's capability to convey the richness of information [26]. However, these features are not suitable for our context of social e-commerce based on live broadcasting. In our study, live interactions are the extent to which anchors, consumers, or more communication parties can act on each other in a real-time way, on the communication medium, and on the messages and the degree to which such influences are synchronized in live broadcasting e-com merce. Our study focused on live interactions between anchors and/or consumers to efectively influence each other on the content or form and then enhance users’ social commerce engagement rather than only information richness of Mogujie app. First, existing research on the choice of CMC technology used media richness theory (MRT), focusing on employing media richness and social presence (media character istics) [24], to predict the adoption of CMC media. Media richness re fers to the relative ability of a communication channel to deliver mes sages including rich information [26]. Rice [27] argued that information richness is tightly bound to social presence. Watjatrakul & Barikdar [24] found that media richness has a positive impact on social presence and then influences the attitude toward using e-mail via per ceived usefulness. Klein [28] pointed out that media richness positively impacts telepresence. Kim et al. [29] noted that media richness positively afects co-presence. Second, studies on performance of CMC technologies, including the CMC interaction model [30] and the media synchronicity theory [31], emphasize how technologies afect the per formance of communication and work, Most research studies on CMo media have employed MRT to forecast the adoption of media rathe than performance [22]. However, this research is concerned with how live interactions in social e-commerce can improve the performance outcomes of live broadcasts (i.e., social commerce engagement) in the mobile live broadcasting app (Mogujie). We aim to contribute to the CMC technology literature by this study because few studies have in vestigated communication performance of media [22,31]. Therefore, our study focuses on how to improve communication performance (i.e., social commerce engagement) of live interactions on CMC media (the second perspective) based on the CMC interaction model [30] and Media Synchronicity Theory [31].

This study ofers several interesting insights. First, live interactions (i.e., personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, and perceived control) positively afect perceived usefulness, negatively impact perceived risk and psychological distance, and then lead to social commerce engagement. Second, susceptibility to informative influence moderates the efect of live interactions (i.e., personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, and perceived control) on social commerce engagement.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we provide a literature review about social commerce and live interactions employed in this study. Then, Section 3 presents the related hypothesis and proposes the research model based on the SOR paradigm. Section 4 presents the research methodology. Next, we report the results of data analysis in Section 5. Finally, we discuss the theoretical and practical implications and limitations of this study in Section 6.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Live interactions

Interaction (Interactivity) as “the degree to which two or more communication parties can act on each other, on the communication medium, and on the messages and the degree to which such influences are synchronized”. Interaction is regarded as the key factor for successful communication, web design, and mobile commerce in CMC [32], which is the essential factor afecting behavioral outcomes. Despite increasing interest in social interaction communications, an exact definition and dimensionality of interaction (interactivity) varies widely but still has a dispute [33]. However, the common concept of interaction includes a sense of relation among users and promotes information available to each other. Burton & Soboleva [17] identified two types of interactivity, namely, interpersonal interactivity and machine interactivity [34], saw interpersonal interactivity as communications between individuals and organization and regarded machine interactivity as “the extent to which users can participate in modify the messages they receive” [11].

First, some researchers attaching the importance to technological features define interaction as machine interactivity, which refers to technological outcome produced by employing the technology [11] or resulting from the role of the technology to promote users easier to interact with each other and to manipulate the content [34]. Yim et al. [19] insisted that interactivity refers to the perception that users can control social media to change the form and content of the media [35]. With advanced technology with the possibility to generate high-level interactivity, users with participation motivation may experience interactivity. The existing study showed that interactive image tech nology can enhance user engagement [16]. For customer control, consumers interact with electronic commerce platforms individually and have little control, while consumers on social commerce platforms or apps are allowed to create content [36]. For instance, by using a smartphone with a touch screen in shopping online, a consumer, who zooms in or out the pictures of products displayed by online stores, will experience a high level of interaction because the information about details and content of products is manipulated.

Second, other scholars argued that interactions involve users’ sub jective perceptions concentrating on individual characteristics that enhance interaction [18,19]. For example, Newhagen et al. [18] discovered that users with the motivation to participate in interactive media will experience interactivity. The interpersonal interactivity is created by establishing a technologically efective delivery process to boost users’ subjective decision-making to participate [19]. Zhang et al. [8] founded that consumers can get informational and emotional value from interactions in s-commerce and have to share valuable informa tion, and interactivity positively afects social support and flow. Kang et al. [32] discovered that interactivity positively impacts afective in volvement and cognitive involvement with mobile LBS retail apps. Zhang et al. [37] contended that interactivity positively afects relationship quality. By interactions, consumers can provide social sup port to others [7,8].

In the traditional e-commerce setting, interactivity mainly focuses on consumer–machine interactivity [11], but the main emphasis of interactivity has shifted to the interactions between anchors and customers or among customers in the live social e-commerce context, owing to technological advances in mobile commerce. For instance, by using a smartphone with a touch screen, a consumer who may have good experience in live shopping will sense a high level of live interaction because (1) the real-time feedback from anchors is provided, including anchors’ answering questions and dispelling doubts about products in time and showing its unique selling points from customers in live rooms, and (2) consumers can interact with other consumers to share the shopping knowledge and experience by viewing comments, sending barrages, and receiving feedback in real time. In other words, diferent from the studies on interactivity, the content, subjects, fre quency, etc., of interaction have changed in live social e-commerce context as shown in Table 1, so it is essential to investigate the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement in live broadcasting social e-commerce context.

Therefore, this study defined live interactions as the degree to which anchors, consumers, or more communication parties can act on each other in a real-time way, on the communication medium, and on the messages and the degree to which such influences are synchronized in live broadcasting e-commerce.

## 2.2. Live broadcasting in social commerce

Live broadcasting or live streaming in social commerce is a novel means that records and streams in real-time. Previous studies on live broadcasting (live streaming) have mainly focused on: (1) the factors afecting purchase intention, including trustworthiness and attractive ness of Wanghong [38], live content (vs. Wanghong vs. self)-product fit [38], social status display [39], IT afordance (visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping) [40], user interface design, and gift-giving feature [41]; (2) the factors impacting watching intention, such as in teractivity, humor appeal and sex appeal [39], entertainment, and social interaction [42]; (3) the factors influencing broadcasting intention, such as social distance, challenge stressors, and hindrance stressor [43]; (4) the motivation of viewers and streamers to employ live broadcasting, including humor appeal and sex appeal [39], entertainment, social interaction [42], and social motivations [44]; (5) psychological mechanism, including immersion and presence [40]; and (6) the efect of perceived values (utilitarian, hedonic, and symbolic value) on customer engagement [45,46]. Park & Lin [38] discovered that live content positively afects utilitarian and hedonic attitude and self-product fit positively impacts intention to buy. Sun et al. [40] argued that immersion and presence are positively associated with purchase intention. Hou et al. [39] found that sex appeal, humor appeal, and interactivity positively impact continuous watching intention, and social status display enhances consumption intention to virtual gifts in live streaming. Chen & Lin [42] noted that entertainment and social interaction have positive efects on watching intention toward a live-stream. Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut [45] pointed out that symbolic value positively afects customer engagement and customer trust in products, and that trust in sellers mediates the efect of utilitarian and hedonic value on customer engagement. Hilvert-Bruce et al. [44] believed that entertainment, social interaction, and external support have positive efects on time spent in watching Twitch. Zhou et al. [43] also proposed that challenge stressors and hindrance stressors of users are positively related to broadcast intention.

While these researchers have explored the efect of live broadcasting (live streaming) on purchase intention [38,41], broadcasting intention [43], and watching intention [39,42], it is until unclear how live interactions influence social commerce engagement in social e-commerce based on mobile live broadcasting apps and its psychological mechanism.

## 2.3. Social commerce

Social commerce is often regarded as a novel form of electronic commerce [47–49], with mainly two essential elements: social media and business activities [7,50]. Lee et al. [51] argued that social commerce refers to an online mediated application, which combines Web 2.0 technologies with interactive platforms in a business environment. Social e-commerce is changing the way people think about e-commerce by combining social media with e-commerce [7], which has attracted much attention from IS and e-commerce scholars and has become an important field [9].

Social commerce is the use of social media for business transactions and activities that are promoted principally by social interactions and user contributions [47,52,53]. Curty & Zhang [48] contended that social commerce refers to any commerce activities based on social media and social websites. Huang & Benyoucef [54] addressed that social commerce is an online commercial application, employing social media and Web 2.0 with social interaction and user-generated content to help consumers to shop online and communities. Kucukcay & Benyoucef [55] referred to mobile social commerce as the set of e-commerce activities conducting in the mobile setting and improved by usergenerated content. Hew et al. [56] proposed that the goals of mobile social commerce are employing it not only for doing business, but also for marketing and promotions. Ng et al. [57] defined social commerce as how to attract potential customers and to disseminate information.

## 2.4. Social commerce engagement

Engagement has received increasing and generous attention across diferent fields including brand marketing [59,60], organizational behaviors [61,62], and service management. It has been regarded as a vital factor to predict and explain consumer behavior outcomes, such as repurchase intention, and brand loyalty. Although engagement has popularized in related studies over the past decade as a precursor of consumer purchase and brand love [60], the conception of engagement has transpired in social commerce literature only recently [9,63–66]. Engagement suggests a connection between two parties [67,68], in cluding a consumer, a product, a brand, an organization, or a community [63,64,66–69], and is a durable relationship and interdependence between them [70] in which two parties play an active role [71]. Mendes De Leon et al. [72] pointed out that social engagement refers to the extent to which people participate in diferent kinds of social activities. Consumers are engaged with a party (e.g. a brand), which will help to nurture a strong psychological connection that promotes customer repurchase behavior, recommendation intention, and a long-lasting relationship with the party [73].

According to the insights obtained from Table 2 and previous research on customer engagement [9,64], we argue that social commerce engagement refers to the behavioral manifestations that represent the level of users’ investment, participation, and eforts in social commerce activities [9].

## 2.5. Susceptibility to informational influence

According to the interpersonal influence theory [74], susceptibility to informational influence (SII) refers to the process in which a con sumer's attitudes, beliefs, and behaviors are impacted by others [75], such as friends, anchors, and families in our study. The degree of SII varies across individuals. Bearden et al. [74] found that a person with high SII is impacted by others, but a person with low SII is less possible to be influenced. Clark & Goldsmith [76] argued that people with high SII may place a high value on information from others to make right and informed decisions, who are risk-takers and prefer to try novel products. Hofmann & Broekhuizen [77] discovered that SII declines transaction frequency in an investment context. Jie et al. [75] further pointed out that individuals with high SII will regard them as dependent and informational, but those with low SII will construe them as independent and special. Therefore, susceptibility to informative influence is addressed in our study because (1) consumers need to collect the useful information or cues about products to facilitate their decisions before shopping by interacting with anchors, other consumers and live broadcasting apps in social commerce in a real-time way, and (2) behavioral engagement of consumers may be afected by the circle of friends in live social e-commerce context, so SII may change the outcomes of live interactions.

## 2.6. The stimuli–organism–response framework

The SOR framework is extensively employed to explore the efect of environmental stimuli on consumer responses [8,78,79]. In the light of the SOR model, environmental stimulus (S) impacts consumers’ internal state (O), and then subsequently influences their responses (R) toward the store in online shopping context. Previous studies have applied the SOR model to the online retail context and conceptualized stimuli as environmental factors, including website design factors and social factors [8]. The internal states involve cognitive and emotional states of users, such as their perceptions, experiences, and evaluations [80]. The responses represent individual behavior, including active or passive participation behaviors and online communication [81]. This study employs the SOR model as a frame and overall theory for two reasons. First, the model has been adopted extensively in many literatures on online shopping behavior [82]. Second, the SOR model ofers a structured manner to test the impact of diferent live interactions as online environment stimuli on users’ virtual experiences and, in turn, social commerce engagement on a live social e-commerce app. The studies on the social and live aspects of the online environment are increasing in social e-commerce websites or apps [79].

We use the SOR model and adopt live consumer–machine interaction factors and interpersonal interaction factors, from the perspective of the interactions between anchors and consumers or among consumers, as the environmental stimuli (S). Given the diferent perspectives of these researches, many factors have been used to assess the cognitive and emotional state (O), such as perceived usefulness, perceived risk and perceived psychological distance, and impacts social commerce engagement (R), such as liking, sharing, and recommending. By structuring a causal relation among stimulus, organism, and response, this ofers a systematic framework to trace the impact led by live interactions.

## 2.6.1. Live interactions as environmental stimuli (S)

The existing studies focusing on environmental psychology research classified online interactions into consumer–machine interaction and consumer–consumer interaction [16,20,79,83,84] in social commerce context, but few studies explore the efect of seller–consumer interaction [85]. Initial evidence is given that interactions can impact the cognitive and afective state distinctly being conditional on the characteristics of interactions. For example. Zhang et al. [8] emploved the SOR model to demonstrate the efect of personalization (consumer–machine interaction) on virtual experience and then social commerce intention. Live social e-commerce involves the application of SCAs to conduct consumer–machine interaction and consumer–consumer interaction, communication, and user-generated content and assist customers in online shopping to make better purchasing decisions and to promote their buying experience [86]. Therefore, SCAs help to facilitate machine interaction and interpersonal interaction, and frequent interactions will improve physical attraction and interpersonal attraction of websites [9,79]. Kang et al. [49] found that customers who relate interaction to mobile retail apps argue that employing the apps is fun, appealing, and important. As we focus on live interactions (including consumer–machine interaction factors and consumer (anchors)–consumer interaction factors), virtual experience, and social commerce engagement, our study adopts five interactions factors, namely, personalization, responsiveness, perceived control, mutuality proposed by Wu [87] and Zhao et al. [88], and entertainment from Luarn et al. [89] and Hsieh & Tseng [90].

## 2.6.2. The cognitive and afective factors as states of the organism (O)

The S-O-R framework demonstrates that the efect of environmental stimuli on customer behavioral responses will be mediated through virtual experiences [8,79]. Previous studies suggest that the impact of live interactions can be explored from cognitive and afective perspectives. The first perspective involves cognitive reactions from the stimuli including social presence [85], perceived usefulness [83], and flow experience [14,79]. The second perspective involves afective reactions from stimulus including trust [83] and perceived hedonic value [21]. However, there is a lack of studies focusing on the cognitive state of consumers, including perceived risk, and on the afective state of consumers, such as psychological distance, especially in live social ecommerce. To picture the cognitive and afective states, this study draws on three factors that have been demonstrated as having a significant efect on social commerce engagement [21,91,92]. Moreover, previous studies indicate that live interaction features may afect these factors [21,88,93,94]. The three factors are perceived usefulness, perceived risk, and psychological distance. Consistent with the existing studies, this study utilizes perceived usefulness and perceived risk to examine the cognitive state, while psychological distance is employed to measure the afective state.

First, perceived usefulness has been a core concept in the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). In the social commerce context, previous studies show that perceived usefulness of social commerce websites (apps) or information has a positive efect on consumers’ online behaviors, such as social usage intention, website stickiness, intention to repost, participation intention, and purchasing intention [21,25,95,96]. Note that perceived ease of use is an underlying determinant to measure and explain the use of an information system in the TAM model, which represents its eficiency [97]. Since this study is to explore how live social interactions in SCAs can be made more efective, we reduced the scope of examination to perceived usefulness according to the study of Friedrich et al. [83].

Second, perceived risk is a critical factor to interpret behavioral intention of mobile commerce users [85], which involves the uncertainty during shopping and the negative outcomes after purchasing [98]. Before consumers make purchasing decisions, they often search product information to learn more about products and assess it to reduce the risk conception. In live interactions on SCAs, the greatest advantage of live broadcast interactions is that consumers can interact with anchors, other consumers, and products by live broadcasting to suficiently obtain commodity information and unbeautified pictures about products provided by e-retailers, which can reduce their risk conception about products. Previous studies show that perceived risk has a significant negative efect on behavioral intention [92,99]. For instance, Wu & Wang [99] and Kim et al. [100] proposed that cost and perceived risk have a negative efect on behavioral intention of mobile commerce users. Natarajan et al. [92] found that perceived risk negatively impacts the intention to use mobile shopping applications. Sig nificant negative efects of perceived risk on consumers’ behaviors have been found, including usage intention and purchasing intention in social commerce context [85,96], but few studies neither investigate how to decline it in live social commerce nor demonstrate its efect on experiential outcomes. Since a goal of this study is to explore how to reduce consumers’ online risk conception and to test a more holistic consumer reaction to live interactions in SCAs, perceived risk is often conceptualized as a single construct [85]. This study is to examine how live interactions impact social commerce engagement through cognitive factors, so we conceptualize perceived risk as a single construct.

Third, psychological distance is a determinant in the success of live interactions in live social e-commerce context and is the key factor for studying diferent behaviors in many fields of social psychology, given that consumers, other consumers, and anchors are spatially and temporally separated and that products can only be experienced virtually [83,101]. Significant negative efects of psychological distance on consumers' behaviors have been found, such as retailer trustworthiness [102]. In this study, psychological distance focuses on the subjective distance as the extent to which a consumer lacks connection with a retailer or a product [103,104]. From the perspective of interpersonal psychology, reducing psychological distance can decline consumers self-defense mechanisms, and provide the feelings that are real, sincere and honest, more open, and trusting [102]. Furthermore, psychological distance is a critical factor in social commerce, but few studies investigate neither how to decline it in live social commerce nor demonstrate its efect on experiential outcomes [94]. How to measure it in live SCA users has not been investigated [94]. Since one goal of this study is to explore a more holistic consumer reaction to live interactions in SCAs, psychological distance is often conceptualized as a single construct [105]. This study is to examine how live interactions impact social commerce engagement through afective factors, so we conceptualize psychological distance as a single construct to measure the afective state of the organism.

## 2.6.3. Social commerce engagement as response (R)

Social commerce engagement is a key factor for SCAs to promote business value [9]. The more investment and participation from consumers a SCA obtains from consumers, the higher is the possibility that a SCA generates sales transactions [106]. According to the insights obtained from Table 2 and previous research on customer engagement [9,64], we argue that social commerce engagement refers to the behavioral manifestations that represent the level of users’ investment, participation, and eforts in live broadcasting social commerce activities [9]. In this study, we planned to focus on analyzing social commerce engagement by exploring the behavioral manifestations, which includes (1) active participation in our study, such as raising questions about products in details and seeking advice from anchors by barrages in live rooms, sharing or reposting commercial information to other consumers on social media platforms, participating diferent kinds of fun or interesting activities, subscribing to anchors and liking for them and (2) passive participation, such as reading online reviews and watching anchors’ display and explanation on their products. This study focuses on the above participation behaviors as indirect commercial transactions rather than purchasing behaviors. By distinctly separating the concept of social commerce engagement from that of purchasing behaviors, we could restrict ourselves to reflecting consumers’ behavioral responses as represented by the SOR framework [83].

## 3. Research hypotheses

## 3.1. The stimuli–organism–response framework

The SOR framework is extensively employed to explore the efect of environmental stimuli on consumer responses [8,78,79]. In the light of the SOR model, environmental stimuli (S) impacts consumers’ internal states (O), which, in turn, influence their responses (R) toward the store in an online shopping context. Previous studies have applied the SOR model to the online retail context and conceptualized stimuli as environmental factors, including website design factors and social factor [8]. The internal states involve cognitive and emotional states of users, such as their perceptions, experiences, and evaluations [80]. The responses represent individual behavior, including active or passive participation behaviors and online communication [81]. This study employs the SOR model as a frame and overall theory for two reasons. First, the model has been adopted extensively in many literatures on online shopping behavior [82]. Second, the SOR model ofers a structured manner to test the impact of diferent live interactions as online environment stimuli on users’ virtual experiences and, in turn, social commerce engagement on a live social e-commerce app. The studies on the social and live aspects of online environment are increasing in social e-commerce websites or apps [79].

We use the SOR model and adopt live consumer–machine interaction factors and interpersonal interaction factors, from the perspective of the interactions between anchors and consumers or among consumers, as the environmental stimuli (S), including personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, perceived control. Given the diferent perspectives of these researches, many factors have been used to assess the cognitive and emotional state (O), such as perceived usefulness, perceived risk, and perceived distance, and impact socia commerce engagement (R), such as liking, sharing, and recommending. By structuring a causal relation among stimulus, organism, and re sponse, this ofers a systematic framework to trace the impact led by live interactions.

Kumar & Benbasat [123] defined personalized as the customer's perception that social media can adapt content to meet his or her preferences. In social commerce, personalization strategies can provide users with tailored product or service information in line with their needs, preferences, and social habits [8], which allow customers to more efectively gather information about products, encourage customers to engage in more eficient message-information processing, and improve perceived usefulness in shopping experiences and purchasing decision [14,124]. Personalization is more efective in attracting consumers' attention, and helping them to better accept marketing activities. In live rooms, provided with personalization information interaction, consumers can assess the usefulness of marketing information in terms of meeting their needs, including deeply understanding products function, usage methods, and purchasing goods [21]. H1a. Personalization positively afects perceived usefulness.

The MRT proposed by Daft & Lengel [127] postulates that three aspects afect a medium's capacity for information richness: (1) the ability to display many kinds of cues, including facial expression, text message, and body gesture; (2) instantaneous feedback, such as the immediacy of response to a query; and (3) language variety, including employing diferent language symbols [90]. According to MRT [127], media richness shows the media’s ability to convey messages with rich information. In live s-commerce apps, live anchors can conduct quick and efective responses to a query or request from customers in live rooms, including trying on clothes, displaying the details of products and its unique selling points in person, which provides rich and useful information for customers to facilitate their purchase decision-making and causes consumers to perceive that information and apps are useful. H1b. Responsiveness positively afects perceived usefulness.

Prior empirical research into interactivity has examined seeking entertainment, or satisfying entertainment gratifications such as hedonic, playfulness, and enjoyment. Pöyry et al. [140] discovered that entertainment has a positive efect on browsing and participating behavior. Sharma & Crossler [108] argued that perceived enjoyment positively afects perceived usefulness of social commerce. Entertainment or perceived enjoyment positively impacts perceived usefulness [92]. Therefore, in live s-commerce apps, live anchors conduct online activities to interact with customers, including initiating a prize draw in promotions, communicating with anchors in the face-to-face way, and employing the interesting and useful function of some technology (e.g., virtual reality) to display products, which induce consumers to participate in social commerce activities and facilitate consumers’ purchase decision-making. H1c. Entertainment positively afects perceived usefulness.

Mutuality refers to the extent to which consumers can get help, advice or information from other consumers in terms of product knowledge and shopping experience on the same s-commerce platforms [143]. On live s-commerce apps, reciprocating motivation of customers promotes them to share useful and valuable shopping information with customers out of obligation [144]. Therefore, we posit:

H1d. Mutuality positively afects perceived usefulness.

Liu [145] defined perceived control as the users' perception of their ability to voluntarily decide where to go, with whom to converse, what features to use, and how to use the contents of websites. Previous studies show that perceived control is the core component of consumer–machine interaction [146]. Joines et al. [147] discovered that perceived control enhances consumers’ purchasing shopping behavior. Yoo et al. [148] found that perceived control positively impacts perceived utilitarian value of products. Based on the literature, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H1e. Perceived control has a positive efect on perceived usefulness.

## 3.3. The mediating role of psychological distance

Psychological distance is the key factor for studying diferent behaviors in many fields of social psychology, and the individual per ceives it between the object and the self as the egocentric reference point [177]. Psychological distance refers to the extent to which consumers feel a connection with a retailer and the information about familiar brands may help customers lower psychological distance and uncertainty [102]. Short ening psychological distance will reduce individuals’ sense of crisis and danger, psychological barrier and psychological mechanism of self-defense, and will promote their feelings of reality, openness and trust [102,180,181]. Hernández-Ortega [182] pointed out that social psychological distance positively afects purchase intention and mediates the efect of online consumer review aspects on the receiver's responses. On live s-commerce apps, live anchors are able to provide with customized, enjoyable, and useful marketing activities or information for customers to reduce their uncertainty and risk perception about products during social shopping, and customers can interact with other customers by browsing online reviews or chatting in live rooms to obtain more useful details about quality of products, which will help to reduce risk perception about products, shorten perceived distance between consumers and products or anchors, and then improve customers’ social commerce engagement.

## 3.5. The moderating efect of susceptibility to informative influence

According to the interpersonal influence theory [74], customers with high SII tend to conform to others’ suggestions and lead to obtain a sense of security when shopping. They may insist that others, including friends and anchors, will ofer them believable and valuable information on products and this helps them to avoid risk or loss through purchasing the products. Consistent with the findings of Bickart & Schindler [188], customers with high SII possess the capability to draw their conclusions on product attributes and quality, based on comments and suggestions from other savvy consumers and anchors. In contrast, customers with low SII may search product-related information by researching other customers’ reviews after purchasing and search product descriptions, promotion activities, and other information from anchors or e-retailors before making their purchasing decisions [75]. They will be afected by online information from eWOM, third parties, and manufacturers/retailers in terms of purchase intention.

Accordingly, customers with high susceptibility to informative influence in social commerce will be likely to conduct systematic information processing and enhance the efect of live interactions. Previous studies in marketing have demonstrated strong support for the efect of SII on consumer behaviors [189]. Motivated by the aforementioned, we further predict that the efect of live interactions (i.e., personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, perceived control) between anchors and customers or among customers on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence. Therefore, we propose that

H10a. The efect of personalization on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence.

H10b. The efect of responsiveness on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence.

H10c. The efect of entertainment on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence.

H10d. The efect of mutuality on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence.

H10e. The efect of perceived control on social commerce engagement is stronger for customers with higher susceptibility to informative influence.

## 4.3. Measures

Reliable multiple-item scales used in this study were adapted based on a comprehensive review of relevant literature and were made minor wording changes to fit the current live SCAs context. Most of the multiple-item scales in our study have been previously validated in prior studies of the social commerce and information system field. Table 3 shows definitions of all the constructs adopted in this study. Table 4 shows the measures adopted in this research. All items were measured using a 7-point Likert scale from “1 = strongly disagree” to “7 = strongly agree”. The study employed the translation-back-translation method to check and guarantee the consistency of the original English scales and the Chinese scales.

Owing to not randomly assigning participants in our study, the possibility of any systematic individual diferences might be increased. So, we adopted some control variables to measure users’ characteristics on live SCAs, including gender, age, education, incomes, and access frequency.

## 5.3. Common method bias

Because the data were collected from one online questionnaire and were self-reported for all of the variables, a threat of common method variance may be present [199]. First, to control for common method variance (CMV), this paper employed the method suggested by Podsakof & Organ [200] to examine whether common method bias (CMB) did exist for this study. A Harman's single-factor test was employed to check the extent to the method variance in the data, and any single factor would explain lower than 50 % of the variance in a principal component factor analysis with no rotation. The largest factor accounted for 46.436 %, being below the 50 % threshold, and the results indicated that no single component accounted for most of the variance and the CMB was not a threat in our study.

Second, we related all items to a common method factor and conducted a single-factor test employing CFA by using the method of Malhotra et al. [201]. The results showed that the fit of the single-factor model was poorly fitted $( \chi ^ { 2 } = 3 6 1 4 . 6 0 8 , \mathrm { d f } = 5 2 7 , \chi ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { d f } = 6 . 8 5 9 , \mathrm { G F I }$ $= 0 . 5 8 3 , \mathrm { A G F I } = 0 . 5 2 9 , \mathrm { C F I } = 0 . 6 8 7 , \mathrm { T L I } = 0 . 6 6 7 , \mathrm { R M S E A } = 0 . 1 2 5 , $ SRMR = 0.082), which indicated that CMV was not a serious problem in this study [201,202].

Third, we followed the method of comparing the results of CFA from Podsakof et al. [199] to examine CMV. The single factor model had a significantly larger χ<sup>2</sup>-value $( \Delta \chi ^ { 2 } = 3 , 6 1 4 . 6 0 8 - 6 3 5 . 6 6 7 = 2 , 9 7 8 . 9 4 1 ;$ $\Delta \mathrm { d } \mathrm { f } = 5 2 7 - 4 8 2 = 4 5 ; \Delta \chi ^ { 2 } = 2 , 9 7 8 . 9 4 1 > \chi ^ { 2 } \left( \alpha = 0 . 0 5 , \mathrm { d } \mathrm { f } = 4 5 \right) =$ 61.656) than the multiple factor model, which indicated that the CMB was not a serious problem in our study.

## 5.5. Gender diferences

The majority of Mogujie users are female in the last two years. For example, according to the data from www.qimai.cn, 87.80 % of Mogujie users were female in 2018 [194]; according to mUserTracker (2019), the demographics of Mogujie users shows that 89.78 were female in 2019 [195]. Among the 377 participants, 89.7 % of the participants were female in our study. ## 6. Discussion and conclusion

This study attempted to answer the central research question, “How to interact with customers to enhance social commerce engagement?” In a live SCAs context, live anchors not only can display their products in person, but also need to interact with customers to introduce the details of products, including unique selling points and usage method of products, and to answer consumers’ questions in the actual process of purchase decision-making. Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of interactions [85,88,211,212]. The growth of live social commerce relies on the interactions between anchors and customers, but little is known that how live anchors should interact with customers to promote them to engage social commerce. Little is known about how live anchors interact with customers to maximize transaction chance

To achieve this target, this paper examined the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement, the moderating efect of SII from the interpersonal susceptibility perspective, and the mediating roles of perceived usefulness, perceived risk, and psychological distance.

First, live interactions have positive efects on perceived usefulness. (1) Personalization positively afects perceived usefulness, which is consistent with prior studies [8,21,123]. Tam & Ho [121] found that personalization can decline users’ information screening costs and boost their decision-making quality efectively, and Kumar & Benbasat [123] discovered that personalization of a website can enhance perceived usefulness. Zhang et al. [8] pointed out that personalization of a web site positively influences flow. The possible explanation is that personalization is more efective in attracting consumers' attention, dis playing more useful information about products or services by live anchors in person and in time, and helping them to better accept marketing activities. (2) Responsiveness positively impacts perceived usefulness. (3) Entertainment positively afects perceived usefulness, which is consistent with the conclusion of Sharma & Crossler [108] and Natarajan et al. [92]. Cvijikj & Michahelles [213] indicated that entertaining function is antecedents of likes, comments, and shares ratios. The results are consistent with Crocker & Canevello [144], who argued that reciprocating motivation encourages consumers to share useful and valuable shopping information with others out of obligation. (5) Perceived control is positively associated with perceived usefulness. The possible explanation is that high perceived control promotes consumers to search useful information to make purchasing decision.

Second, live interaction characteristics (features), including personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, and perceived control, have negative efects on perceived risk, respectively. To begin with, psychological distance had the largest negative impact on social commerce engagement. Furthermore, perceived control had the greatest efect on perceived risk. However, personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, and mutuality have less negative efects on perceived risk. Last but not least, entertainment has the largest positive efect on perceived usefulness. As the hygiene factors afecting the performance of live interactions on Mogujie, personalization, responsiveness, mutuality, and perceived control have less efects on perceived usefulness.

(1) Personalization negatively afects perceived risk, which is consistent with prior studies [122,151–153]. Tam & Ho [122] argued that personalization can generate relevant and better-customized recommendations to closely match individual preferences and needs, cut down consumers’ information researching cost, and en hance the eficiency and quality of social shopping decisions. And this will reduce customers’ risk perceptions concerning negative consequences due to improper seller conduct [151–153]. The finding further indicates that live anchors can implement personalization interaction to develop better-customized recommendations to closely match customers’ preferences and needs, which will decline their uncertain or risk perceptions about products in the process of live shopping.

(2) Responsiveness negatively impacts perceived risk. The findings are consistent with the conclusion of Ahn et al. [125] that consumers can receive quick and efective responses from customer service staf or sellers to decline the uncertainty perception about products. The possible explanations are: 1) Diferent from the temporal and special separations between consumers and e-retailers in CMC in traditional e-commerce, anchors can help customers acquire information about important cues in interpersonal communication to reduce the uncertainty about products. 2) On Mogujie apps, live anchors can answer customers’ questions and requests by using barrages about products in the “face-to-face” communication way, and dispel doubts about the quality of products by displaying the details of products including trying on clothes in person. This provides first-time feedback and efective responses for customers, which conveys rich and real information about products and brands and helps consumers reduce uncertainty or perceived risk in purchase decision-making.

(3) Entertainment negatively afects perceived risk. Consistent with the conclusion of Zhou [156] that CashU presents risks when consumers shopping online, consumers are motivated to trust mobile payments when they will feel that they are fun and enjoying challenging activities [157]. This indicates that on Mogujie apps, live anchors can employ some fun second-kill promotions, and display their products (the styles of clothes or the layout of commodity houses) in details, and customers may feel that they seem to be able to touch them, which helps to reduce their uncertain or risk perception about products.

(4) Mutuality has a negative efect on perceived risk, which is consistent with previous studies [85,126]. Reciprocating motivation promotes social exchange among customers and between anchors and customers and encourages them to share useful and valuable shopping information with other customers out of obligation [144], which will reduce their uncertain and risk conception about pro ducts.

(5) Perceived control has a negative efect on perceived risk. As an active consumer–machine interaction, perceived control facilitates consumers to obtain more useful knowledge, cues, or information about the quality of interested products, including product details, quality, and usage method, which will ofer useful and important information for consumers/viewers to reduce product uncertainty and perceived risk.

Third, live interaction characteristics have negative efects on psychological distance. Personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, mutuality, and perceived control negatively impact psychological distance. And, perceived control (PC→ PD: −0.400) has a stronger impact than either entertainment (EN→ PD: −0.099; Δχ2(1) = 7.781, p < 0.05) or mutuality (MU→ PD: −0.131; Δχ2(1) = 6.384, p < 0.05) on psychological distance.

(1) Personalization negatively impacts psychological distance. Personalization content on social apps will articulate one con sumer’s personal preferences and needs [149]. Edwards et al. [102] further suggested that personalized information about information brand and retailers may reduce customers’ uncertainty and reduce psychological distance. Therefore, consistent with the findings of Liang et al. [7], high personalization promotes customers to feel that live anchors care for them and value them, which can invoke feelings of connection and intimacy by anchors using personalization marketing activities, induce feelings of belongingness and warmth and enhance feelings of closeness, and then reduce psychological distance between customers and anchors or products in s-commerce apps.

(2) Responsiveness has a negative efect on psychological distance. Lim et al. [94] argued that inhabited space negatively impacts psychological distance in a social streaming video system that promotes users to perceive interacting between them in a relevant context. This indicates that, on Mogujie app, live anchors can ofer quick and efective responses to solve problems from customers, which may make customers feel that the anchors are amicable, honest, and reliable. This will shorten psychological distance between customers and live anchors or products.

(3) Entertainment has a negative efect on psychological distance. Hsieh & Tseng [90] suggested that perceived entertainment of interaction positively impacts social connectedness. This is because entertainment can create connections, social relationships, and friendships [166]. This indicates that live anchors can conduct in teresting social activities and fun promotion to interact with cus tomers, which enhances social connectedness between them and consumers, and shrinks psychological distance between interactants.

(4) Mutuality has a negative efect on psychological distance. Consistent with the findings of Zhao et al. [85], consumers can obtain help from other consumers and anchors in terms of knowledge and experiences about products and reciprocating motivation also promotes them to share useful and valuable shopping in formation with customers out of obligation. This enhances the relationship between customers and others or anchors and reduces psychological distance among customers and between customers and products.

(5) Perceived control has a negative efect on psychological distance, which is consistent with the findings of Han & Gershof [171] who discovered that the greater perceived control makes positive targets feel closer. This indicates that consumers can interact more actively by controlling interactive features and obtain real-time responses, so that they perceive targets as being closer in time and space with each other.

Fourth, we found that perceived usefulness has a positive efect on social commerce engagement and mediates the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement. Natarajan et al. [92] demonstrated that perceived usefulness positively influences user intention to use and Williams [118] found that perceived usefulness positively impacts user intention to use. This indicated that live anchors can provide with personalized and useful marketing information for customers, and customers can interact with other customers by browsing online reviews or chatting in live rooms to obtain more useful details about quality of products, which will improve their participating intention in social commerce, including sharing or repost social commerce information to others, participating promotions, liking for anchors and interacting with anchors and other consumers to communicate with each other by sending barrages.

Fifth, perceived risk has a negative efect on social commerce engagement. This is consistent with the findings of Natarajan et al. [92] that perceived risk negatively afects the intention to use mobile shopping applications. This indicates that live anchors can provide with customized, enjoyable, and useful marketing activities or information for customers to reduce their uncertainty and risk perception about products during social shopping, and customers can interact with other customers by browsing online reviews or chatting in live rooms to ob tain more useful details about the quality of products. This will help to reduce risk perception about products, and then will improve social commerce engagement. These findings ofer additional insights into the cognitive mechanisms through which live interactions afect social commerce engagement.

Sixth, psychological distance has a negative efect on social commerce engagement and mediates the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement. These findings provide additional insights into the psychological mechanisms through which live interactions afect social commerce engagement. Hernández-Ortega [182] pointed out that social psychological distance positively afects purchase intention. This indicates that live anchors are able to provide with customized enjoy and useful marketing activities or information for customers during social shopping, and customers can interact with other customers by browsing online reviews or chatting in live rooms to obtain more useful details about quality of products, which will help to enhance the closeness between anchors and customers and to shorten psychological distance between consumers and products, and then improve customers’ social commerce engagement.

Finally, SII moderates the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement, which extends the study of Jie et al. [75] and Shen et al. [9]. The efect of mutuality on social commerce engagement is stronger for users with higher SII in social e-commerce. Perceived control exerted a stronger efect on social commerce engagement for consumers with high SII in social e-commerce. Personalization, responsiveness, and entertainment do not exert a stronger efect on social commerce engagement for users with lower or higher SII.

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

The results of this research ofer some interesting insights into live interactions, interpersonal influence, and social commerce engagement (SCE) on social e-commerce apps for scholars.

First, we target an important problem in live social e-commerce, that is, how to interact with consumers to promote their social commerce engagement, which extends the study of Shen et al. [9] who found that technological attractiveness can enhance social commerce engagement in Douban. With insights obtained from studies on live interactions, this study conceptualizes and measures live interactions from the multidimensional factors perspective and empirically examines the role of live interactions in promoting social commerce engagement. The results show that 75.1 % of the total variance in SCE has been interpreted by five factors. In this respect, we investigate the key problem in live social e-commerce and capture critical points to break the back of a tough problem about engagement. Furthermore, this study strengthens understanding of the emerging phenomenon about live broadcast social commerce and highlights the efect of live interactions in social commerce.

Second, we put forward the new perspective to comprehend how to design an efective SCA to display and communicate the information on products in the virtual face-to-face communication way based on live broadcasting and how to interact with consumers to promote their so cial commerce engagement from the interpersonal interaction perspective. Based on the conception of interaction in human-computer interaction and interpersonal interaction field, a live interaction fra mework is established with five sub-dimensions, i.e., personalization, responsiveness. entertainment. mutuality. and perceived control. This study extends the studies of Wu [214], Lee [170] and Yim et al. [19], and adds entertainment as the fifth factor of live interactions in social commerce to enrich the interaction theory. In this regard, we highlight the multidimensional construct of live interactions and identify their causal mechanism. Moreover, the five sub-dimensions of live interac tions cover the widely explored anchor–consumer interaction factors, consumer–consumer interaction factor, and machine–consumer interaction factor in live social e-commerce context and extend the existing studies of Shen et al. [9] and Cano et al. [16] in IS field. The live interactions framework ofers a novel perspective by distinguishing the efects of diferent kinds of live interactions on social commerce engagement, which can guide further research about live broadcasting social commerce. In our study, entertainment has the largest positive efect on perceived usefulness. This is because as the principal incentive factor, anchors (streamers) on Mogujie app rely chiefly on professional display or products explanations from anchors, and fun interactive activities including second-kill promotion and snatching red envelope activities. to offer useful and satisfactory information. to attract con: sumers, and then to make deals done. As the hygiene factors afecting the performance of live interactions on Mogujie, personalization, responsiveness, mutuality, and perceived control have less efect on perceived usefulness. Furthermore, perceived control had the greatest effect on perceived risk. As an active consumer-machine interaction. perceived control ofers consumers more chance to obtain more virtual touch (haptics) about products and facilitates consumers to obtain more useful cues or information about the quality of interested products, including product details, quality, and usage method, which will pro vide useful and important information for consumers/viewers to reduce product uncertainty and perceived risk. However, personalization, responsiveness, entertainment, and mutuality have less negative efects on perceived risk. Last but not least, psychological distance was the most strongly impacted by perceived control. The possible explanation is that perceived control ofers consumers more chance to obtain more virtual touch (haptics) about products under the condition of high-level product visibility, which has the most efect on declining psychological distance between anchors and consumers or between consumers and products.

Third, this study identified the previously unexplored psychological mechanisms of the efect of live interactions on social commerce engagement that drive decisions in live social commerce, including per ceived usefulness, perceived risk, and psychological distance, from the cognitive state and the afective state, respectively. This explains the unique advantages of live interactions that are diferent from traditional e-commerce interactions, live interactions that can enhance the interactions between e-retailers (in our research, anchors/streamers) and consumers and the display of real and useful commodity informa tion rather than embellished pictures to reduce the risk conception by employing live broadcasting, and promote social interaction to obtain closer psychological distance and higher engagement.

Fourth, our study also made contributions to the literature by shedding novel light into the moderating efect of susceptibility to informative influence (SII) in live social commerce, which extends the scope of this literature to live social commerce domain and the studies of interpersonal influence theory. Susceptibility to informative influ ence was addressed in our study because consumers need to collect the useful information or cues about products before shopping by interacting with anchors, other consumers and live broadcasting apps in social commerce in a real-time way, and it may change the outcomes of live interactions. Previous studies have demonstrated that consumers with high SII are dependent and search information, while consumers with low SII are independent and unique [74,215]. Jie et al. [75] discovered that consumers with high-level SII will be easier to be impacted by online information from an eWOM source rather than that from retailer source when making a decision. However, little is known about how SII afects the processing of the interactive information from dif ferent interactions in live e-social commerce. We found that the efects of mutuality and perceived control on social commerce engagement are stronger for consumers with higher SII in social e-commerce, and that personalization, responsiveness, and entertainment do not exert a stronger effect on social commerce engagement for consumers with lower SII or higher SII, which extends the study of Jie et al. [75]. More importantly, a deeper comprehension of the boundary conditions will bring insight into the moderating efect of SII in social e-commerce. Therefore, the findings reveal some interesting insights into the moderating efect of SII and provide references for future studies.

## 6.2. Practical implications

We also ofer some useful and actionable guidance to live-broadcast social e-commerce practitioners. This study highlights the importance of live interactions in facilitating social commerce engagement. Five types of live interactions addressed the diferent ways that practitioners can apply to promote social commerce engagement.

First, given the importance of personalization, anchors, and social ecommerce managers should provide personalized products for consumers based on their requests and browsing data to deliver personalized products information and reviews for targeted consumers Furthermore, anchors should record viewers’ information, analyze their personalities, tap their demands, and ofer personalized reminders of product usage based on the understanding of fans, which will make fans feel cared for and reduce psychological distance between them and fans. In addition, anchors should have their own personalized and professional style of commodity explanation, which helps them to show products to the greatest extent. The personalized information will help to capture consumers’ attention at the first time and accelerate their purchasing decisions. Besides, developers of live-broadcast appes should think over the design of live-broadcast social e-commerce apps on the intelligent personalized product recommendation system, which can facilitate anchors (streamers) to conduct personalized product recommendation based on artificial intelligence technologies.

Second, responsiveness significantly afects perceived usefulness, risk, and psychological distance. We suggest that live anchors should answer customers’ questions and requests at the first time in live broadcasts or send barrages about products with the “face-to-face” communication way, and employ specialized commodity explanation to dispel users’ doubts about product quality by displaying the details of products including trying on clothes in person. In other words, live anchors can ofer quick and efective responses to solve problems from customers and convey rich information about products and brands, which may make customers feel that the anchors are amicable, honest and reliable and reduce uncertainty or perceived risk in purchase decision-making. This also shortens psychological distance between customers and live anchors or products.

Third, perceived control has the greatest negative efect on perceived risk. We suggest that developers of the live-broadcast app should ofer high-level control options for users to interact with the app or products, which will help users to obtain useful cues or information about product details, such as zooming in or out pictures on products when watching live broadcasts on the app, viewing product details, and adjusting the size of live broadcast window. They should also develop virtual reality (VR) systems and augmented reality (VR) systems to display products in details, and customers may feel that they seem to be able to touch them and this will promote consumers to perceive realism in touch products (e.g., clothes) in stores or look around in physical stores, which helps to reduce their uncertain or risk perception about products. Moreover, some measures can be taken by managers to integrate new technological features to facilitate anchor-consumer interaction and machine–consumer interaction and to improve perceived usefulness of products and apps, such as the use of video explanations about product details from anchors after live broadcasts and enhancing the innovation and application of more interactive tools (e.g., virtual reality (VR), artificial intelligence (AI)) or options.

Fourth, entertainment has the largest positive efect on perceived usefulness. Anchors should encourage consumers to share fun, useful, and valuable shopping information with other potential customers by rewarding them coupons including sharing usefulness comments after purchasing products and sending barrages to answer questions from other consumers before buying, which will provide social support for other consumers and activate them to engage in social shopping. For instance, consumers will review the contents generated and shared by other consumers to collect useful and interesting promotion information or cues about products, which could improve their eficiency of making decisions and the virtual shopping experience.

Fifth, anchors can conduct interesting social activities and fun promotion to interact with customers, which enhances social connectedness between them and consumers, and shrinks psychological distance between consumers and anchors or products. We recommend that anchors should employ some enjoyable and amusing second-kill promotions or some lucky draw activities, and use fun augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) technologies to display their products in details, and promote consumers to perceive realism in touching products, which helps to reduce their uncertain or risk perception about products. Furthermore, anchors can interact with other customers in a humorous and personalized way, which will help to enhance the closeness between anchors and customers and to shorten psychological distance between consumers and products, and then improve customers’ social commerce engagement.

Finally, anchors and SCAs platforms should adopt diferent strategies to reach target customers who vary from one person to another in SII. For consumers with high SII, practitioners (e.g., developers) can ofer more controllable options or buttons to enhance the feeling of user control on watching live broadcasting and browsing details about products, and promote consumers to share useful and valuable shopping information and suggestions with other customers out of obligation or altruistic motivation. Besides, for consumers with low or high SII in live social commerce, practitioners can ofer quick and efective responses to solve problems from customers by professional video-audio interpretations or sending real-time barrages, which may make customers feel that the anchors are amicable, honest, and reliable. Furthermore, anchors should conduct interesting social activities and fun second-kill promotion to interact with customers, encourage consumers to invite their friends to participate in live broadcasts by con ducting cash incentive schemes, and promote consumers to engage in social commerce activities. For example, if a consumer can invite their five friends to watch the live broadcast for one minute within 24 h, an electronic red envelope containing RMB 25 (about \$3.586) will be offered to her or him.

## 6.3. Limitations and future research

This study presents some insights for live interaction characteristics and social commerce engagement, and some limitations can provide directions for future research.

First, this study took Mogujie as the research object and data demonstrated most of the hypotheses. It is possible that the results may not be applicable to other live shopping apps, such as Taobao Live, Tmall, JD.com, and Amazon Live. Further studies could explore the efect of customer motivation on switching live shopping platform.

Second, due to limitations of the data set employed, this study investigated the efect of live interaction characteristics between live anchors and customers on social commerce engagement from the user perception perspective rather than the technological outcome perspective. Future research exploring the efect of live interactions may find that the relationship between live interactions and other behavior intentions (e.g. continuance intention or repurchase intention) is stronger.

Third, we demonstrated that the relationship between live interactions and social commerce engagement, but little is known that how other possible moderating variables, such as consumer innovativeness, and shopping motivation, afect behavioral intention.

Fourth, because our study focuses on how to employ live interactions to improve communication performance of CMC media (the second perspective) based on media synchronicity theory [31] and the CMC interaction model [30], further study may explore the efect of media richness and social presence in live broadcasts on the adoption of live broadcasting apps based on social presence theory and social influence theory (the first perspective).

Last, to keep the model simple and controllable and to focus on the efect of live interactions, other vital factors, such as perceived value and virtual haptic, were not explored in the model. We also recommend future research to extend this line of research by investigating the alternative pathways through which live interactions promote social commerce behavioral intention.
