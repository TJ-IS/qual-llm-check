Keywords: Resource-based view Cloud computing Primary and support use Socio-technical theory Firm performance Value creation

## A B S T R A C T

While cloud computing is touted as a promising information technology advancement, predictions of its value are inconclusive. This research investigates the impact of cloud computing within-firm and across-firm. Drawing on the resource-based view and sociotechnical theory, technical attributes and social attributes of cloud computing are identified to impact firm performance via primary and support use. Results from data collected from 513 firms show varying effects of technical and social attributes in primary use and support use, which help create value and better performance. Such effects also are found to differ between firms in service and manufacturing sectors.

## 1. Introduction

The fast-emerging cloud computing has been touted as one of the most promising information technology (IT) advancements that could fundamentally change how IT solutions are delivered to firms [1]. With the unique characteristics and advantages of cloud computing, firms are expecting to adopt it for improving performance. While sounding promising, the predictions of cloud computing impacts on firm perfor mance were being confounded [2–4]. These lead to the serious consid erations about whether cloud computing should be applied; and thereby. firms often get stuck into a state of indecision.

Different from traditional enterprise IT applications, cloud computing has unique characteristics, such as rapid elasticity, ondemand self-service, and resource pooling [5], which could be applied into different functional activities within firms. Prior literature primar ily focused on one single perspective of IT adoption. Some of the pre vious studies focused on the technical component (i.e. the human-created tools or technologies that carry out organizational work), such as new technologies like business intelligence and analytics [6] and cloud computing [7,8]. Other papers focused on the social component by emphasizing the humans and their relationships [9–11]. We consider unique characteristics of cloud computing to understand the value creation from the technical view. Moreover, due to the lack of existing research on the integration of the social and technical impacts on the cloud computing technology, our current paper examines the relationship between the cloud computing use and value in different firm activities through socio-technical perspectives combined with unique cloud computing characteristics.

In this research, we aim to examine the impact of cloud computing use in-depth through two perspectives: within-firm and across-firm. First, within the firms, we separate cloud computing use into primary use and support use according to Porter’s value chain model [12]: (a) primary use refers to using cloud computing into primary activities. These primary activities include inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service and (b) support use refers to using cloud computing into support activities that includes human re sources, technology, procurement, and firm infrastructure. We leverage on the socio-technical theory (STT) as a theoretical base for identifying the antecedents of cloud computing use, and the resource-based view (RBV) for examining IT value. STT identified two interrelated sub systems in a work system of the organization, including social and technical subsystems [13]. Anecdotal evidence indicates that RBV is a potential framework to investigate the impacts of IT resources on firm performance [14–16]. The resources created by the integration of cloud computing with firm-owned activities are expected to have effects on performance. Thus, we extended RBV to the cloud computing context and examined the influences of cloud computing uses on firm perfor mance via value creation. ## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Unique characteristics of cloud computing and value

Cloud computing is not a completely novel concept, which was evolved based on computing, such as grid computing, cluster computing, and service computing. So, it has some characteristics in common, such as modularization, virtualization, or independence of devices and places [3]. However, cloud computing has some unique characteristics different from traditional IT. These characteristics are the key aspects that distinguish themselves in many aspects. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) indicated the “character istics of cloud computing: on demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, and measured service” ([5], pp.2).

Based on these characteristics, later studies considered two or three characteristics, or expanded the notion of them. Marston et al. [3] tried to encapsulate the unique features from a technological perspective and emphasized on the aspects of dynamic scalability, elasticity, and auto mated self-provision of resources. Gangwar et al. [7] proposed unique advantages of the cloud computing from the technical perspective such as scalability, flexibility, and shared resources. They considered scal ability as the ability of cloud users to scale up the cloud computing infrastructure and resources to meet the new requirements of hardware or software. Cao et al. [2] considered scalability in terms of service and payment options, where the scalability of service can provide an envi ronment conducive for companies. The scalability of payment can reduce the cost structure.

Table 1 summarizes the existing research related to the unique characteristics of cloud computing. Since we focus the cloud computing use on the user-side rather than on the supply-side, we do not consider the resource pooling and measured service. We select three unique characteristics (i.e. on-demand self-service, dynamic scalability, and rapid elasticity) on the user side in this study to fill the gap from existing research on cloud computing by empirically investigating the impacts of these three technical characteristics on cloud computing use. Based on the NIST definition [5], related characteristics are defined as follows: (a) on-demand self-service provides the ability for firms to automatically obtain computing capabilities (such as network storage) to fulfill de mand at any time and any location; (b) dynamic scalability emphasizes the release of firmwide capabilities to scale resources (such as techno logical architecture) dynamically inward commensurate with demand; and (c) rapid responsiveness (rapid elasticity) refers to the capability of quickly and instantly responding outward to customers, which appears to be unlimited. This responding capability is appropriate to any numbers of customers at any time. To clearly distinct elasticity from scalability, we emphasized rapid elasticity on the aspect of responsive ness; therefore, we use the term “rapid responsiveness” instead.

Previous studies investigated several related benefits and business value related to cloud computing such as operational efficiency [4,20], reduction in the cost structure [2,4], new types of products and services $[ 3 , 1 0 ]$ , finance outcomes [21,22], and supply chain integration [2,9]. Majority of the prior research directly evaluated cloud computing adoption as the antecedent of benefits and business value. Mixed results are found for these studies. Several studies discovered positive impacts of cloud computing adoption on business value [21,23]. However, other studies found that cloud computing implementations could lead to the failures of business value $[ 3 , 2 4 ]$ . Some researchers suggested the ne cessity to incorporate organizational internal practice such as related skills competence [21] and managerial capabilities (Garrison et al. 2012, [24]), to ensure the realization of the value.

## 2.2. Cloud computing value and firm performance: Resource-based view

Originated from the strategic management field, the RBV gives a valuable way to consider the relationship between information systems (IS) and firm strategies; and these strategies can help firms to exploit the internal strengths and reduce the level of internal weaknesses through the use of IS [16,25]. This theory suggests that firms can create sus tainable competitive advantages, if they have heterogeneous strategic resources with rare, valuable, and imperfectly imitable attributes ([25, 26]. Cloud computing itself is not a kind of heterogeneous resources since it is available to all competitors. But, the extent of cloud computing use for different firm activities could become heterogeneous resources and create business value for firms. Firms with the high extent of use better integrate cloud computing with firm-owned activities, and further develop value for themselves. When the cloud computing technology is integrated with different firm activities in the value chain, such inte gration becomes heterogeneous resources that are non-substitutable, inimitable, and rare. The resource integration becomes rare resources, indicating that few competitors could create or obtain the same or similar resource integration due to their different business contexts or situations [27]. Then, such integration is usually inimitable because it is particular to specific firms [21]. Moreover, the high extent of cloud computing use provides firms with different and novel ways to produce unique products and services that could not be easily substituted by competitors [23,24].

The RBV and its extensions have been applied broadly in the IS field, and grown popularity among IS researchers. In the IS literature, this theory has been used at the organizational level of analysis in different contexts. Prior studies used the RBV to analyze the relationship between IT-based resources (such as IT-enabled intangibles and IT infrastructure) and firm performance (such as internal and external firm performance and innovation). The summary is presented in Table 2, which shows that studies primarily focused on the effects of adoption or application of IT resources on firm performance [22,28]. Zhu and Kraemer [29] sug gested that the business value is determined by the degree to which IT is utilized into activities of firms. Son et al. [23] extended RBV to exter nally delivered resources and explore the competitive advantages of cloud computing resources. Garrison et al. [30] focused on the inter action of resources between cloud delivery structures and internal IT capabilities in an organization and found the improvement of firm performance by using cloud computing. Moreover, Mitra et al. [21] adapted RBV into the cloud computing context and used it as a theo retical lens to explore the unique capacity of cloud computing and its influences on firm growth. From this view, firms with greater IT use would be more likely to obtain IT benefits and create value.

Thus, the RBV was adopted to study the relationships between the extent of cloud computing uses and firm performance via value creation. Cloud computing value refers to competitive advantages in firms, ob tained by integrating cloud computing and firm activities in the value chain, to exploit opportunities in the market environment and enables a firm to conceive the strategies of implementation to improve effective ness and efficiency [25,26]. Moreover, it gives us the proper theoretical lens to examine how the extent of cloud computing use for different firm activities improves firm performance through the value creation process.

## 2.3. Determinants of cloud computing use: Socio-technical theory

The STT provides a framework for guiding successful organizational changes with regard to new technologies [32]. Trist et al. [33] indicated that the STT is based upon the premise that an organization encompasses both technical and social aspects. This STT indicated that an organiza tion has two interactive subsystems, including the social subsystem and the technical subsystem [13]. The social perspective examines the re lationships among the work-related interactions of focal persons [34]. Moreover, the social component includes individuals or groups from the organizational environment, such as employees, customers, and sup pliers [13,35,36]. The technical perspective could be defined as the techniques, devices, tools, and mechanisms that can be used to improve firm performance by transforming inputs into outputs [13,36].

Applying the STT to the context of cloud computing identifies useful concepts for this study. First, according to the definition of STT, cloud computing systems contain two sub-components (i.e. technical and so cial components). From the technical perspective, elements in the technical system include capabilities for network infrastructures, hard ware, applications, etc. These elements in the cloud computing context refer to on-demand self-service, dynamic scalability, and rapid respon siveness. Besides the technical elements, security concerns and tech nology readiness regarding the technical performance of cloud computing system are regarded as important antecedents in this study. Security concerns, as a type of expectations, are the extent to which users will believe that this technology benefits task performance, and is highly relevant with use [37–39]. Moreover, the more skilled the user of the system, the more flexible and adaptive the work system will be [32]. The level of technology skills and knowledge in firms are measured by technology readiness in this study. Second, from the social perspective, social relationships and socio-environment competitive pressure will impact the cloud computing use at organizations [29,34,36]. Social re lationships in this study refer to the intra-organizational and inter-organizational relationships across a firm, such as the communi cation and collaboration between employees, customers, partners, and suppliers. The socio-environment competitive pressure is regarding the degree of competitive intensity of an organization from its competitors outside.

Finally, STT theory can also elucidate the effects that come with the introduction and adoption of new technologies (i.e. cloud computing) into organizations. The STT has been widely used into many organiza tional situations due to the generality [40]. In particular, this theory can also be used to explain a wide variety of contexts in IS changes [35] and IS innovation (Avgerou and McGrath 2007). The STT “ascribes comparable importance to both the technical component and the social component” ([41], p. 698). We view the technical and social compo nents as antecedents to cloud computing uses and examine the respec tive effects of each component on cloud computing uses. Our research aims at achieving a comprehensive understanding of underlying mech anisms from different perspectives [42]. Thus, we conceptualized the STT into the cloud computing context and adapted the related socio and technical components.

In summary, although there exist some research studies focusing on the cloud computing adoption [7,10], they did not investigate it in-depth by differentiating different types of cloud computing uses. Moreover, from the view of antecedents, they usually considered one single perspective (technical or social perspective) of IT adoption [41]. Prior literature primarily focused on the effect of adopting cloud computing on business value [21,23]. The mixed findings show that emphasizing the cloud computing itself is not sufficient for creating business value. A missing link between cloud computing adoption and business value may exist and needs to be explored. Despite the fact that studies on business issues of cloud computing have received increasing attentions over past years, research on the relationship among cloud computing characteristics, uses, and value from the socio-technical viewpoint remains insufficient. Hence, in this paper, we apply the STT and RBV to investigate the cloud computing use of socio-technical de terminants, and the influences of cloud computing uses on firm perfor mance via value creation.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses development

## 3.1. Research model

Building upon the STT and RBV, Fig. 1 presents a theory-driven research model to study how technical attributes and social attributes impact firm performance via cloud computing uses and value creation. The left-hand side of the research model presents the socio-technical attributes affecting cloud computing uses by applying the STT. The middle part of the model presents the mechanisms about whether pri mary use and support use could generate cloud computing value for firms by applying the RBV.

To contextualize the STT [13,35,36,43], the antecedents in this research are decomposed into two perspectives (i.e. technical-related and social-related). The STT is widely used to examine the effects of new technologies adoptions in organizations [40]. Bostrom and Heinen [13] indicated that the organization has two interactive subsystems including the social subsystem and the social subsystem based on the STT. In the social subsystem, it includess social relationships among employees, as well as outside social environments [35,36]. Under the social perspectives, we identified two related factors, the socio-environment competitive pressure and social relationships to examine whether they will constrain or promote the cloud computing use. The technical subsystem includes technology attributes. Bostrom and Heinen [13], Lyytinen and Newman [35], and Sun et al. [43] described the technical attributes from the perspective of information technologies. In our research, we identified three cloud computing-context factors—the unique characteristics of the cloud computing technology in the technical perspective: dynamic scalability, rapid responsiveness, and on demand self-service—as antecedents of the constructs of cloud computing uses. Moreover, we incorporated two technical factors in general: technology readiness and security concerns about cloud computing, which may have different effects on cloud computing uses from previous studies [10,18]. Based on Porter’s value chain [12], there exist two types of activities in firms in general. Based on our context, we decomposed the cloud computing use into two types: uses in primary activities (i.e. primary uses) and uses in support activ ities (i.e. support uses). Moreover, the RBV [14,25,29] explained the relationships between the IT use and IT value. Grounded on this RBV. the extent to which the cloud computing use may affect the firms’ value and performance are identified by four variables including a cloud computing value variable and three firm performance variables (firm innovation. external integration. and internal operation).

Some researchers use either operational performance or financial performance to study firm performance [9,14,20,44,29]. In our paper, three categories of performance regarding within-firm and across-firm performance, namely, Internal Operation, External Integration, and Firm Innovation have been identified and investigated. Internal Operation re fers to the performance of organizing and managing activities inside a firm, such as structuring its practice, procedures, and processes to gain effectiveness, efficiency, and productivity. Gronroos ¨ and Ojasalo [45] suggested that internal efficiency is related to the cost-effective use of resources. Benefiting from on-demand self-service, firms can consume resources by its special load and reduce its upfront investment and operational costs.

External Integration refers to the performance of operating and structuring activities outside of firms to build external communication and develop relationships, including rapid responding to customers, sharing information with partners, collaborating with partners and so on. As the widespread use of cloud computing in industries, integration has been discussed in the domain of the supply chain management. Different from Enterprise Resource Planning or Electronic Data Inter change, cloud computing enables low costs for sharing information, quickly responding to customers, communicating, and building interfirm relationships [9]. Dynamicity infused by agility in the cloud computing systems escalates the capability of the supply chain, which could enable firms to operate external integration in a highly competi tive market environment. This capability enables a firm to deploy re sources optimally at right time for the right person, and achieves seamless coordination among the members of a supply chain [11].

Innovation is crucial to a firm’s success especially in this dynamic environment. However, since the infrastructure and financial resources are limited, firms should lay on other strategic resources to innovate and enhance their competitive advantages. Cloud computing can low IT barriers to innovation and make new applications and services possible that were impossible before [3].

Firm size and firm age are often applied in IT adoption literature [10, 17,46]. Based on the literature review, firm size and firm age might have confounding impacts on cloud computing value creation. We thus con trol for firm size by using the number of employees and that for firm age by using the years since established in this current study.

## 3.2. Hypotheses development

Based on Porter’s value chain model [12], there are two types of activities in firms—primary activities and support activities. This model has been widely used in prior studies [47–51]. Primary activities include inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service; thus, in this research, the primary use means utilizing cloud computing in primary activities. Support activities include human re sources, procurement, technologies, and firm infrastructure; thus in this research, the support use refers to using cloud computing in support activities.

## 3.2.1. Technical attributes

Technical attributes here describe the technical elements offered by cloud computing, such as capabilities for network infrastructures, hardware, applications (i.e. on-demand self-service, dynamic scalabil ity, and rapid responsiveness), and expectations, skills, and knowledge regarding the technical performance of cloud computing system [32,39, 43].

Unlike traditional information technologies, cloud computing ben efits firms by providing on-demand access and flexible payment ar rangements. It means that firms can consume cloud computing as a service and pay for just like utility computing [4]. On-demand selfservice enables firms to automatically obtain resources and self-provision on the cloud according to their demands at any time or location. This flexible serving way and the pay-as-you-go mode would stimulate firms to use cloud computing in their daily activities. Through cloud computing, firms just should pay for the resources needed and used. Thereby, this flexible way of on-demand self-service reduces the costs for operating any kind of activities. Thus, firms would have impetus to use cloud computing in these value chain activities, such as service, procurement, and sales activities, with large variances in de mands. The above augment results in the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1a. (H1a). The cloud computing characteristic of on-demand self-service is positively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 1b. (H1b). The cloud computing characteristic of on-demand self-service is positively related to support use.

Cloud computing also provides users with massively scalable ser vices. Dynamic scalability emphasizes the release of firm’s capabilities to scale dynamically commensurate with demand, such as architecture and human resources. The massively scalable services offered by cloud computing establish a conducive environment for improving social capital among companies [2]. The firm infrastructure is considered as one of the support activities, referring to an organization’s structure and its management, planning and so on. By using cloud computing, firms can have lower or no upfront investment, such as augment and instal lation of hardware and software [3]. Thus, Firms would have motiva tions to use cloud computing in value chain activities, such as the firm infrastructure and service. The related hypotheses are shown as follows.

Hypothesis 2a. (H2a). The cloud computing characteristic of dynamic scalability is positively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 2b. (H2b). The cloud computing characteristic of dynamic scalability is positively related to support use.

Rapid responsiveness provides firms the capabilities of quickly and instantly responding to their customers or partners, which appear to be unlimited. Firms can update and add related new resources at any time. Considering the marketing and sales activities in primary activities as an example, cloud computing could help firms to fulfill the requirements at any time by improving their response rates, so that firms can seize the opportunities to rapidly target the appropriate consumer groups by using cloud computing. Moreover, firms can utilize cloud computing to provide customer relationship management and supply chain manage ment through close integration among the channels [11]. Benefiting from the rapid responsiveness given by cloud computing services, firms will have incentives to use cloud computing. Thus, the above argument results in the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 3a. (H3a). The cloud computing characteristic of rapid responsiveness is positively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 3b. (H3b). The cloud computing characteristic of rapid responsiveness is positively related to support use.

Technology readiness consists of two aspects: IT structural and ITrelated human resource aspects, which is a critical determinant in the adoption and implementation process of cloud computing [10]. More over, high technology readiness implies that organization is aware of the pros and cons of the current IT infrastructure and likely to enjoy great readiness to implement new IT [10,52]. Hence, the higher technology readiness will lead to the better use of cloud computing. This results in the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 4a. (H4a). Technology readiness is positively related to pri mary use.

Hypothesis 4b. (H4b). Technology readiness is positively related to support use.

Although cloud computing has potential benefits, there has unwill ingness for some firms to implement it, largely due to the security concerns. These concerns include breaches and loss of irreparable data. Security covers system availability, authentication, data privacy, authorization, and so on [53–55]. Most executives are quite concerned about the risks of the cloud, with data security topping the list first from the report of Harvard Business Review in 2016 [56]. Owing to the sharing of computing resources and cloud platforms, cloud computing means firms should input and perform data operations on the platforms from providers [57], and expose to a multi-homing environment, which increase the uncertain security. The value activities in firms, such as the firm infrastructure, technology development, operations, and marketing and sales, are related to firms’ core competitive advantages and crucial to customers/partners interactions. Thus, firms will be quite concerned about the security and risks of cloud on various activities, and the lack of standards and SLA (Service-Level Agreement) will make firms reluctant to use cloud services. This results in the following hypotheses.

Hypothesis 5a. (H5a). The level of security concerns on cloud-computing in a firm is negatively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 5b. (H5b). The level of security concerns on cloud-computing in a firm is negatively related to support use.

## 3.2.2. Social attributes

Social attributes are related with the people (employees) who work in the organizations, the relationships within and between groups (e.g. customers, suppliers) and the forces in the marketplaces in which the organization competes [36]. Socio-environment competitive pressure is regarding the degree of competitive intensity of an organization from its competitors outside [29]. Prior studies highlighted its important role as a strong antecedent and driver of the cloud computing adoption. Oli veira et al. [10] found that under the competitive pressure, cloud computing adoption serves as an essential strategy to compete in the marketplace. Through the use of cloud computing, organizations could benefit from great operational efficiencies and high profits [52]. Thus, socio-environmental competitive pressure outside a firm plays an essential role in promoting the usage of cloud computing into primary activities rather than support activities inside.

Hypothesis 6a. (H6a). Socio-environment competitive pressure is posi tively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 6b. (H6b). Socio-environment competitive pressure is posi tively related to support use.

Social relationships refer to the intra-organizational and interorganizational relationships across a firm, such as the communication and collaboration between employees, customers, partners, and sup pliers. Social relationships are crucial for firms to operate various activities and deliver valuable products in the value chain. Several studies have suggested intra-organizational and inter-organizational behaviors could result in several outcomes such as knowledge reuse, better engagement, and work processes improvement [58]. If employees are fully connected, intra-collaboration is often improved as they could utilize the embedded networks to communicate in their teams closely. If the organizations connect heterogeneous people outside (like customers, suppliers, etc.), inter-organizational relationships would enable em ployees inside organizations to get access to various kinds of informa tion, knowledge, and resources (Reagans et al. 2004). Thus, the stronger social relationships might be the foundations for the more primary use of cloud computing.

Hypothesis 7a. (H7a). Social relationships are positively related to primary use.

Hypothesis 7b. (H7b). Social relationships are positively related to support use.

## 3.2.3. Relationships between cloud computing use and value

Cloud computing is a fast-emerging and powerful technology, and the integration of cloud computing with firm activities could generate competitive advantages for firms. Through the use of cloud computing for different firm activities, firms are likely to create valuable, rare, and imperfectly imitable firm-specific IT capabilities, which lead to strate gically competitive value creation. Moreover, the general IS success model [59] declared a strong relationship between system uses and system impacts. Prior studies have proposed the relationship from the use of new IT to value and empirically found that the greater use of IT generates the higher value ([29,60–62]. Hence, the hypotheses about the relationship between cloud computing uses and value are shown as follows.

Hypothesis 8a. (H8a). Firms with more primary use incur higher cloud computing value.

Hypothesis 8b. (H8b). Firms with more support use incur higher cloud computing value.

## 3.2.4. Cloud computing value with its impacts on firm performance

The cloud computing value can be applied to external integration (i. e. coordinating with partners over the value chain, interacting with the customers), which is particularly evident in the supply chain manage ment. For example, Firms could achieve seamless coordination and close integration from sharing real-time inventory and sales information among the members of a supply chain [11]. The value created by cloud services and computing platforms could improve the performance of the supply chain to allocate resources optimally on demand. Within a short time, the supply chain manager could extend resources through the cloud to gain tremendous capabilities. Thus, Cloud computing helps firms gain competitive advantages to operate external integration in a highly competitive market environment. Based on this, we propose the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 9a. (H9a). Cloud computing value positively correlates with external integration.

The cloud computing value reflects the integration of business agility and IT efficiency [3], which could be deployed and scaled rapidly. In this study, internal operation refers to the cost-effective use of internal re sources, such as process flexibility, procedures improvement, product-time reduction, savings in resource usage, reduction in total execution cost, and improvement in productivity. The cloud computing value could provide firms with significant cost advantages due to the high elasticity and flexibility [4]. This value of cloud computing could provide access for firms to allocate information sources from a resource pooling with minimal efforts and optimal costs. Thus, we propose the hypothesis below.

Hypothesis 9b. (H9b). Cloud computing value positively correlates with internal operation.

Cloud computing is a revolutionized computing paradigm that pro vides an innovative delivery method for traditional IT infrastructure [18]. The cloud computing value enables firms to mutually operate its internal processes and external business in a different way. As a result, new types of products, new patterns of services, and new classes of ap plications are created for firms [3]. The key advantages of cloud computing value, such as lowering IT upfront investment, scaling ser vices easily, and reducing the computing cost, can create opportunities for innovation. Therefore, cloud computing stimulates innovation by enabling firms to concentrate on the core business [10]. Hence, the below hypothesis is proposed.

Hypothesis 9c. (H9c). Cloud computing value positively correlates with firm innovation

3.2.5. Industry effects: differences between service and manufacturing sectors

Prior studies have shown that cloud computing have been applying into different industries, such as the retail industry, high-tech industry, healthcare industry, and education industry [17,18,63]. The critical factors of cloud computing adoptions may be different across industries. For instance, the competitive pressure in firms differs across industries [17,64]. Moreover, previous studies found that innovation characteris tics (i.e. relative advantages) have the opposite effect in different in dustries and are more crucial for firms in manufacturing than for firms in the service industry [10,65]. Hence, this paper aims to shed lights on evaluating the factors impacting on cloud computing uses and value systematically across manufacturing and service sectors. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 10. (H10). The effects of socio-technical determinants on cloud computing use (i.e. primary and support use) and value will differ in service and manufacturing sectors.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Data collection

To test the research model in Fig. 1, we adapt and modify the questionnaires from prior research and conduct a survey to collect data from service and manufacturing firms. The questionnaire used in the current study was modified based on the validated surveys in the exiting literature. Technology readiness consists of structural aspects and specialized human resources [10]; security concerns are about the risks of cloud, such as system availability, data privacy, and data lost [55]. Socio-environment competitive pressure is regarding the competitive in tensity perceived by firms from their competitors outside [29]; social relationships refer to the intra- and inter-firm relationships, such as the communication and collaboration between employees, customers, partners, and suppliers [35,36]. External Integration refers to the per formance of operating and structuring activities outside firms to build external communication and develop relationships [11]. Internal oper ation refers to the cost-effective use of internal resources, such as process flexibility, cost reduction, and improved productivity [29]. Firm inno vation refers to new types of products, applications, and services [3].

Each construct and its items are extracted from the literature and adapted to the cloud computing context. First, in terms of technical at tributes, the three items for technology readiness in our survey are modified from Oliveira et al. [10], which measure the importance of IT in a firm; two items for security concerns, which place emphasis on data, are drawn from Oliveira et al. [10] and Sabi et al. [18]. Second, in terms of cloud computing context, two items in rapid responsiveness are based on the definitions of cloud computing characteristics from Marston et al. [3] and then self-developed. The items for dynamic scalability are derived from Cao et al. [2] and self-developed, which demonstrate the scalable benefits on operations. The items for on-demand self-service are drawn from Maresova et al. [4] and self-developed, which indicate that cloud computing can offer flexible services. Third, being integrated with the STT [13], the items for socio-environmental competitive pressure are modified from Zhu and Kraemer [29], and items for social re lationships are derived from Shani and Sena [36] and Kuegler et al. [66]. Fourth, in terms of firm performance, the three items for firm innovation based on the business perspective are drawn from Marston et al. [3]; the items for the internal operation are adapted from Zhu and Kraemer [29] to the cloud computing context. The items for external integration based on supply chain management are drawn from Yan et al. [11]. Finally, in terms of cloud computing uses, the two items for support use and the other two items for primary use are modified from Porter [12] and Zhu and Kraemer [29]. Grounded on the RBV, the three items for cloud computing value are adapted from Barney [25] into the cloud computing context. Table A1 (in Appendix A) summarizes the oper ationalization of these constructs.

Cloud computing use is measured by two dimensions: variety and depth according to Zhu and Kraemer [29]: (a) the variety refers to the number of cloud computing use for different activities in each type, and (b) the depth refers to the percentage of cloud computing use for each activity. The right-hand side of the research model reveals how firms improve their performance via value creation of cloud computing. Cloud computing value was adapted from Barney [25]. Moreover, to further study firm performance impacts, we classified the impacts on firm per formance into three categories: internal operation (e.g. procedures improvement, product-time reduction, cost-effective use of internal re sources), external integration (e.g. rapid responding to customers, building relationships with partners, collaboration with partners), and firm innovation (e.g. new products research and development, new types of services or business models) [3,11,29].

To ensure the meaning of the questionnaire was the same in different languages, each item in English was translated into Chinese; and in turn, from Chinese to English by a language instructor and reviewed by two pro fessionals in academics in IS research fields. All constructs were measured by a 5-point Likert scale including 5 (strongly agree), 4 (agree), 3 (neutral), 2 (disagree) and 1 (strongly disagree), except the total number of employees, total annual revenues, the average market share in the past three years, and the year since established. These four indicators are all translated into a 5-point Likert scale.

An online version of the questionnaire was made in Qualtrics and emailed to individuals such as CEOs and CIOs in 950 service and manufacturing firms in a variety of regions including Yangtze River Delta in China. ## 4.3. Comparing service and manufacturing sectors

Measurement invariance is necessary before using PLS-SEM multi group analysis (MGA). To assess the measurement invariance, the measurement invariance of composite model (MICOM) procedure is followed. This procedure includes three steps: configural invariance, compositional invariance, and equal mean values/variances [85]. In our research model, service and manufacturing sectors had identical items, data processing, and algorithm setting, which means the configural invariance (step1). In addition, we use SmartPLS 3.0 to analyze the compositional invariance (step2), equal mean values and variances (step3). The results from the three steps are shown in Table 7. The results of configural and compositional invariance (the last column in Table 7) indicate full measurement invariance, and equal mean values and var iances (the last column in Table 7) indicate partial measurement invariance. According to the criteria [85], the results allow us to compare multi-group path coefficients.

## 6. Findings

All hypotheses presented before have been tested for the full sample and two subsamples. The summary of hypotheses testing is listed in Table 10. Several major findings are discussed as follows.

Finding 1 Technology readiness and security concerns are all found significant to affect primary use and support use of cloud computing in firms. However, dynamic scalability has significant impacts only on pri mary use; and on-demand self-service and rapid responsiveness have impacts only on support use.

As shown in Table 10, firms with an established technology infra structure and complete technical competence are willing to use cloud computing in value chain activities. This result is consistent with liter ature that firms with technology readiness have more resources to cover the investment risk of adopting new technologies [10,17]. However, firms with security concerns are reluctant to use a new technology, which is consistent with the previous studies [57,55]. Dynamic scalability from cloud computing characteristics provides firms with scalable capabilities to dynamically commensurate with demands. In this rapid development era, ever-changing requirements increase the importance of dynamic scalability on the primary activities, such as inbound logistics, service, marketing, and sales. The firm infrastructure is considered as one of the support activities, referring to an organiza tion’s structure and its management, planning, and so on. The cloud computing characteristic of on-demand self-service helps firms save costs by decreasing or removing upfront investments, such as augment and installation of hardware and software [3]. Moreover, rapid responsiveness enables firms to adjust their strategy and planning ac tivities according to external environments. Different factors have different influences on cloud computing uses, further leading to signif icant differences in specific resources, capabilities, and advantages that firms gain from cloud computing.

## Finding 2 Cloud computing uses in different types of activities contribute to value creation of cloud computing differently.

As hypothesized before, firms with higher extent uses of cloud computing for primary and support activities are likely to create greater value. In Table 10, the findings show a significant evidence on the linkage from the cloud computing use to the cloud computing value, which is consistent with the results from previous studies [29,44,62].

Moreover, we also discover that the value of cloud computing generated via primary use of cloud computing is higher than that in support use. Prior studies have proposed the missing link from IT use to value and empirically found that the greater use generates the higher value [61,62,29]. In Table 10, H9a and H9b are both supported, sug gesting that higher degrees of use on primary and support activities are related to a greater value. Among them, primary use would have the greatest contribution to cloud computing value creation. Because pri mary and support activities emphasize on different aspects, the value created from cloud computing usages on these two types of activities may be different. Primary activities, strategically including inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service, are identified as “the activities involved in the physical creation of the product and its sale and transfer to the buyer as well as after-sale assistance” ([12], pp.38). However, support activities sustain and sup port the primary activities by providing procurement, technologies, human resources, and firm infrastructures. Cloud computing can be integrated with primary activities and add tremendous value in the value chain. Moreover, real-time inventory and sales information can be shared between channels of a supply chain to obtain close integration and efficient supply chain analytics [11]. In summary, the primary use plays a key role in value creation, thus firms need to pay more attention to the integration of cloud computing with primary activities.

Finding 3 The value created by cloud computing has a significantly positive impact on firm performance, where the impact on the internal operation presents the strongest.

As hypothesized before, the cloud computing oriented-value of firms has positive relationship with firm performance, namely, internal operation, external integration, and innovation, which are all supported in Table 10. As mentioned before, due to the characteristics of cloud computing, firms are able to allocate more resources from resource pools with minimal efforts and optimal costs than traditional IT. Then, the cloud computing value can provide firms with significant cost advan tages due to their high elasticity and flexibility [4]. By integrating cloud resources with firm activities in the value chain, the competitive value provides IT efficiency and business agility for firms to mutually operate its internal process and external business. This finding indicates that cloud computing applied in firms has great potential to improve firm performance.

## 7. Discussions, implications, and conclusions

This research marks an important attempt to incorporate cloud computing characteristics into the determinants of cloud computing uses from socio-technical perspectives. Moreover, the integration of cloud computing with different firm activities is categorized into pri mary use and support use in our research model. We consider such integration is a novel source of value creation to explore the mechanism of cloud computing value creation and further examine their impacts on firm performance based on the RBV. Different from previous studies, this study focuses on the post-adoption stage and investigates the relation ships between the cloud computing use and value based on the STT and RBV. Moreover, we consider unique characteristics of cloud computing to understand the created value (i.e. internal operation, external inte gration, and firm innovation) from the technical view. By comparing the cloud computing value between the service and manufacturing sectors, we contribute to the broader scope on the cloud computing use and value across sectors.

In this study, a research model is developed by integrating the unique characteristics of cloud computing based on the RBV and STT. For cloud computing use, this study takes technical attributes (cloud computing characteristics, technology readiness, and security concerns) and social attributes (socio-environmental competitive pressure and social re lationships) into considerations for studying cloud computing uses and firm performance. Among them, technology readiness and security concerns have significant impacts on cloud computing uses, while there exists a different driver (i.e. socio-environmental competitive pressure) for support uses in the service sector from that in the manufacturing sector. For cloud computing values, this study implies that the cloud computing usage in primary and support activities have effects on cloud computing value, and further impacts on firm performance. Besides, primary use can create more value than support use. These impacts on firm performance also differ across service and manufacturing sectors. With respect to the well-known “IT doesn’t matter” ([89], pp.5–6), this study provides the empirical analysis and evidence to IT value, which implies the paradox of IT value.

Our research has theoretical and practical implications. First, due to the lack of research in combining social attributes with technical attri butes to study firm performance, this research attempts to integrate these three attributes to investigate the impacts of cloud computing uses on firm performance via value creation. Sarker et al. [41] suggested that the STT offers an integrative foundation and captures the essence of the IS discipline. This research serves as an important attempt to apply the STT in the cloud computing post-adoption studies, and emphasizes the technical and social attributes related to the organizations also matter for understanding the relationships between IT use and firm perfor mance in the IS research.

Second, this study integrates the STT with unique cloud computing characteristics to study the cloud computing uses. Upon theoretically investigating traditional contexts and the unique characteristics of cloud computing, we believe that the technical attributes and social attributes are both necessary for studying cloud computing uses. With the application and popularization of cloud computing, firms are concerned about integrating cloud computing with various firm activities to create the competitive value, establish their own core competences, and collaborate with customers, suppliers, and partners in the entire value chain.

Fourth, this study also shows that there exists the difference when applying the proposed model to examine service and manufacturing sectors. By conducting the three-step procedure and using PLS-SEM MGA, we found that there is no significant difference when comparing these two subgroups except that the effect of socio-environmental competitive pressures on support uses significantly differed (p = 0.020) between the service and manufacturing sectors. Further studies could use a larger sample size to analyze the multi-group differences. Under severe competition for customers, this is especially important for service companies to use cloud computing to develop their core competence, and further improve the response rate and service quality. Based on the comparison, we can get a deep understanding of these two subsamples; thus, the appropriate conclusions and increased general ization would be gained. These findings could be the helpful guidelines for firms to use cloud computing and create their competitive advantages.

There are also some practical implications in terms of the cloud services providers, firm-level cloud users, and policy makers. First, the cloud services providers need to assess the level of use intention to target the appropriate user group. As indicated by our empirical findings, effective cloud computing uses rely on certain characteristics of cloud computing, as well as technical attributes and social attributes. The providers can promote their cloud service and stimulate the firms to use cloud computing properly according to their situations.

Second, for firm-level users, this study discovers a path of creating value from using cloud computing. As in the IT world, the cloud computing is a new trend. It is necessary for firms to figure out the pros and cons of cloud computing, as well as the best way to use this tech nology. With regard to the technical attributes, technology readiness matters a great deal to cloud computing use. Managers need to assess the level of current IT environments and related IT manpower to make informed decisions on using cloud computing. Besides, the external environments of organizations also matter in cloud computing uses, especially for service companies. Cloud computing are creating entirely new blueprint for competition. Our empirical results highlight the im pacts of socio-environmental competitive pressure on cloud computing uses in the service sector. In this situation, it will become more impor tant for service companies to use cloud services for better computing capacities and faster response rates.

Third, according to our research, security concerns largely impede for the pursuit of using cloud computing. Because some firms are afraid of handing over their operations to others, especially with the lack of service standards when using cloud computing [3]. Thus, policy makers have the responsibility to set related rules and regulations for services providers, ranging from data access privacy to data authorization. Then, a well-regulated and orderly market environment could be established to accelerate the diffusion of cloud computing.

There still exist some limitations in this study, suggesting some future opportunities of research. First, this study examines the cloud computing use based on the survey data. The future research could collect the accurate cloud computing usage data from a variety of cloud computing systems to conduct simulation and obtain objective results. Second, the current research focuses on the social and technical ante cedents to cloud computing uses. The future research could focus on the effects of socio-technical interaction on producing outcomes. Third, based on the results, we could expect the impacts of firm size on per formance while using cloud computing in firms. Future research should consider the firm characteristics as important factors to provide a clear picture of value creation in cloud computing for different firms. Finally, because cloud computing has been adopted and implemented into different countries, future research could also be conducted to investigate the cloud computing value and firm performance across countries.
