## Introduction

Sponsored by the Association of Information Systems (AIS), the Pacific Asia Conference on Information Systems (PACIS) is the premier annual information systems (IS) conference in the region. It serves to provide a platform to researchers and practitioners to share their research findings and practices in the field of information systems and management.

The theme of PACIS 2012 is ‘‘IS Innovation in Pacific Asia’’ and Ho Chi Minh city, Vietnam was chosen to host the conference. In recent decades, this region has undergone remarkable economic progress and rapid development. Amidst the dynamism of growing change and transformation, organizations and society at large face unique challenges as a result of the diverse economic, political, and sociocultural contexts in this part of the world. Their development experience, therefore, has stood out as an extraordinary example of how information systems and technology are being leveraged in generating new solutions. Meanwhile, as we deliberate on the impacts of such a diverse and dynamic context on the unique application of IS, we should consider deeply the effects of IS innovation on the environment. In other words, it represents an opportunity for the IS researchers and practitioners to think about how information and communication technologies can contribute to the advancement of social, cultural and economic well-being of this region.

After a comprehensive reviewing process, 130 regular papers and 51 research-in-progress papers were accepted for the conference, addressing issues specific to the conference theme as well as general IS research. For this special issue of Information and Management, six research articles from PACIS 2012 are selected. These papers are extended, further reviewed and revised by the authors.

In the first paper of the special issue, Sedera and Dey have explored the concept of user expertise in hyper-competitive markets in their paper ‘‘User Expertise in Contemporary Information Systems: Conceptualization, Measurement and Application’’. Positing that the development of user expertise is a strategic imperative for organizations operating in a dynamic environment, this paper aims to explicate this concept that has been largely neglected. More specifically, the authors conceptualize, operationalize and validate user expertise in contemporary Information Systems (IS) as a formative, multidimensional index. The validation involved three separate studies, including exploratory and confirmatory phases, using data from 244 respondents. With that, this paper makes a contribution by facilitating progression of past research on user competence and efficacy of IS to complex contemporary IS, while at the same time providing a benchmark for organizations to track their user expertise.

The second paper ‘‘Examining the Relationships among E-Government Maturity, Corruption, Economic Prosperity and Environmental Degradation: A Cross-Country Analysis’’ by Krishnan, Teo and Lim, examines the relationships among e-government-maturity, corruption, economic-prosperity and environmental-degradation. Intrigued by the impact of e-government and the nascent stage of such research, the authors design a quantitative study, which makes an innovative use of publicly available archival data, in order to unveil the pivotal role of e-government in managing corruption and eco-efficiency. The study demonstrates a significant relationship between e-government-maturity and corruption, and e-government-maturity, economic-prosperity and environmental-degradation through the mediating effects of corruption. While egovernment-maturity does not contribute to economic-prosperity and environmental-degradation, its value can be realized indirectly via its impacts on corruption, which in turn influences economicprosperity and environmental-degradation. More importantly, the findings of this paper contribute to the theoretical discourse on egovernment impact by identifying the role of e-government and provide indications topractice on enhancing its economic-prosperity and environmental-degradation by managing corruption.

In the third paper by Phang, Zhang and Sutanto titled ‘‘The Influence of User Interaction and Participation in Social Media on the Consumption Intention of Niche Products’’, social media’s role in organization is of central interest. Social media has been hailed as a democratizing force that enables consumers to discuss niche products that were previously ignored by mainstream media. Due to a tendency of consumers to participate in generating reviews and discussing about such products, the authors argued that social media may be particularly apt in promoting niche products. Hence, this study investigates how patterns of user interaction in discussing about a niche cultural product may influence participation level, which in turn enhances its consumption intention. The result shows support to the positive relationship between participation and consumption intention. Furthermore, interaction patterns with high inclusiveness and betweenness centralization may enhance the participation level, but out-degree centralization and core-periphery have a detrimental impact.

The fourth paper ‘‘Investigating Effects of Business Process Orientation on Organizational Innovation Performance’’ by Tang, Pee and Iijima focuses on the effects of business process orientation (BPO) on innovation. Business process orientation supports business process management by reorienting employees’ focus from functional performance to customer value adding. While it is generally believed that BPO improves various aspects of organizational performance, there has been a lack of empirical evidence. By addressing this gap, the authors demonstrate that BPO significantly influences organizational innovation performance and the underlying mechanism for the effect is identified. Interestingly, it is found that process view, or a clear process definition and documentation in an organization, is not sufficient to enhance organizational innovation performance. Cross-functional integration may also need to be carefully managed for innovation. These findings improve current understanding of BPO, and offer managerial suggestions for enhancing innovation.

In the fifth paper of this special issue, Ng conducts a close examination on social commerce. Her paper ‘‘Intention to Purchase on Social Commerce Websites across Cultures: A Cross-regional Study’’ emphasizes culture as a critical element in studying the issue of trust in social commerce. Hence, the theoretical background of trust transference, social interactions, and Hofstede’s cultural dimensions are considered. With the online survey data of participants from two different regions – Latin America and East Asia, the analysis results support the moderating effect of culture and the mediating role of trust in a social network community on the relationship between social interactions and intention to purchase in social commerce environments. In addition, the mediating effect of trust in a social network community is conditional on culture. Empirical study results also suggest that social commerce market penetration in East Asia may have relatively fewer barriers to overcome and be more likely to succeed than in the Latin America region.

The special issue ends with Wang, Huang and Tan’s paper of ‘‘Managing Organisational Identity in E-commerce Industry: An Ambidexterity Perspective’’. An enduring and stable identity is a key for organisations in obtaining long-term success while adapting to the volatile environment for organisational profitability. These on-going paradoxical challenges concerning the management of organisational identity has been left unaddressed in the existing literature. Drawing on the literatures of ambidexterity and organisational identity, and the data from an in-depth case study of the China’s largest online ticketing firm, this paper proposes two theoretical frameworks for examining how organi sations in the e-commerce industry should manage organisational identities by leveraging on four types of ambidextrous balancing modes. With that, the paper contributes to the knowledge of organisational identity and ambidexterity and provides detailed means for practitioners to manage organisational identities at strategic and operational levels.

Dr. Shan-Ling Pan is Deputy Head and Associate Professor in the Department of Information Systems, School of Computing at the National University of Singapore. He received his MBA degree from University of Texas, MA degree from University of London and Ph.D. degree in Industrial and Business Studies from the University of Warwick, UK. Dr. Pan’s primary research focuses on the recursive interaction of organizations and information technology (enterprise systems), with particular emphasis on organizational issues such as work practices, cultures, structures, decision-making, change, strategy implementation that often require qualitative research methods. Specifically, he is interested in understanding the complex issues related to the adoption, implementation and use of enterprise systems within organization. Dr. Pan was a winner of NUS teaching awards (2003, 2004, 2006 and 2009). His teaching interests include Egovernment, Knowledge Management Systems and Organizations, Enterprise Systems, and Case Study Methods. Dr. Pan is serving on the editorial boards of MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, Information and Management, Electronic Commerce and Research and Application, and Communications of AIS.

Dr. Calvin Yunjie Xu is an associate professor at the Department of Information Management and Information System, School of Management, Fudan University, Shanghai, China.
