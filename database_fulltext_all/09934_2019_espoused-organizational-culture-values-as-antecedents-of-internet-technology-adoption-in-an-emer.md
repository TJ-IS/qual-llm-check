## A B S T R A C T

Success of an organization, both in the public and private sectors, depends on increases in eficiency due to acceptance of information technology. This has focused attention on factors that influence individuals’ acceptance and subsequent use of information technologies in organizations using the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT) model. In this research, we investigate the role of espoused organizational culture traits as an antecedent to the UTAUT model. We found that espoused cultural traits influence users acceptance and use of the Internet technology in a government agency in an emerging economy, India.

## 1. Introduction

As the Internet has grown, it has spawned a number of information technologies, such as laptops, tablets, and mobile smart phones, which have been widely adopted by individuals and organizations. Organizations have designed information systems applications using technologies that work across platforms, networks, and computing environments. Adoption of these systems has been studied at the individual, as well as, organizational levels. Models such as the technology acceptance model (TAM) and unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT) have investigated factors that influence individual use [1,2] while other models such as the Theory of Diffusion of Innovation have examined technology adoption at the organizational level [3,4]. In this study, we concentrate on individual adoption of Internet technology in a government agency in India.

After using the TAM to examine individual use, information systems researchers proposed a few variations and extensions to the model [5–7]. These variations as well as some of the earlier models, such as the theory of reasoned action (TRA), and theory of planned behavior (TPB), were then integrated into a single model called the UTAUT [2]. The UTAUT model has been replicated and validated in diferent technological contexts such as use of mobile devices and services [8,9], information kiosks [10], biometrics [11], and community weblogs [12]. Researchers have expanded or enhanced UTAUT by identifying factors that serve as antecedents to the model. Antecedents include personality traits [13], charismatic leadership [14], and local and cultural environments [15], to name a few. While studies have examined the roles of a number of individual factors, research on the role of espoused organizational culture values on information technology adoption or acceptance has been limited.

In this study, we examine the role of espoused organizational culture as an antecedent of Internet technology adoption. Culture has been defined in many ways. Culture refers to shared values and beliefs of individuals within a unit. It is also considered to be the totality of socially transmitted behavior patterns, arts, beliefs, institutions, and all other products of human work and thought characteristic of a community or population [16]. Additional definitions of culture are provided in the theoretical framework section of the paper.

Culture is the central focus of this paper, so it is imperative that we examine the concept in depth, especially the concept of espoused cultural values. Although culture is a group concept, we focus on espoused organizational culture, which refers to organizational culture values that are embraced by individuals. Schein [17], a pioneer in the study of organizational culture, proposed the concept of espoused cultural values. He proposed that culture consists of three layers: artifacts, espoused beliefs and values, and underlying assumptions. Artifacts are visible organizational structures and processes that are hard to decipher. Under the artifact layer are espoused cultural beliefs and values, which are defined as strategic goals and philosophies. Below the espoused cultural values layer are underlying assumptions, which are unconsciously taken for granted beliefs, perceptions, thoughts, and feelings. Cultural values manifested in an individual are espoused cultural values [18], and this is the focus of our research. We found a few examples of espoused culture in information systems literature. Srite and Karahanna [19], in their work on national culture and information technology adoption, propose “One can argue that culture is a collective phenomenon and, thus, irreducible to the individual level of analysis. Nonetheless, culture can only manifest itself through the individual [18], and then be aggregated to the collective. The efect of culture is not homogeneous but rather dependent on the extent to which the in dividual subscribes to various cultural values. As such, assessing each individual’s espoused cultural values is both appropriate and meaningful for predicting individual level behavior” ([19], p. 681).

The context of this study is the adoption of Internet technology in a government agency in an emerging economy, India. First, there are limited number of studies on the adoption of technologies in government organizations. Second, research in this area has concentrated on western or developed economies. There are some studies that have examined information and communication technology (ICT) adoption in a government organization in India [20], and there have been others that have looked at the diferences between e-government implementations in developed and developing countries [21]. Emerging economies face unique challenges in the area of technology adoption. India, like other developing countries, lags behind developed nations because of poor infrastructure and the slow response to the emerging cyber-culture [22–24].

The importance of organizational culture on Internet technology adoption has been highlighted in a number of studies. Teo [25], in their study of organizational characteristics and modes of Internet technology adoption in Singapore, concluded that weak entrepreneurial (organizational) culture and various other organizational characteristics impact the level of Internet adoption. In a diferent study of In ternet adoption, Teo and Pain [26] state that “In order to make Internet adoption seamlessly integrated with its business activities, no matter what level of adoption a firm is at, it should always create a conducive organizational culture…” (p. 90). Another study examined the factors that influence Internet adoption in SMEs in Malaysia. The findings show that manager’s characteristics, perceived benefits, organizational culture, technology competency, and cost of adoption have a significant efect on Internet adoption [27]. There have been other studies that have explored the impact of organizational culture on implementation of other technologies and processes. In a study that utilized the TAM [1], Ciganek et al. [28] examined the role of three dimensions of organizational culture on acceptance of a knowledge management system (KMS). They concluded that process-oriented and open communication system organizational culture significantly influenced factors that led to the acceptance of KMS. Similar studies have examined the role of organization culture on the implementation of lean processes [29] and CRM systems [30]. Review of these studies shows that organizational culture has a significant role to play in the implementation of the Internet and other technologies. This provides the motivation for our study.

Based on our review of literature, we could not identify any study that examined the role of espoused organizational culture values in technology acceptance. Details of the literature review are provided in the theoretical framework section. Most studies in the information systems literature have concentrated on national culture and espoused national culture values. Ours is the first to explore espoused organizational culture values in the context of the acceptance of Internet tech nology. Venkatesh et al. [31] provided a synthesis of the entire body of research on the UTAUT and suggested areas of future research. One of the areas they recommended was the study of organizational factors such as organization culture. They also suggested studies in countries other than the U.S.A. This study addresses a gap in the literature in the areas of espoused organizational culture, technology adoption in the public sector, as well as adoption in a developing country.

Our paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we review literature in organization culture and information technology accep tance. We propose the research model and hypotheses. In the following section, we discuss the research methodology including the sample, data collection, and analysis. We then present our results and discussion, followed by conclusions.

## 2. Theoretical framework

In this section, we propose the theoretical framework for our study. Because espoused organization culture is the focus of this study, we review definitions of culture first. Then, we examine research related to our study. Our study draws on three bodies of information systems literature: culture and information systems, the UTAUT, and research on espoused organizational culture and information systems. We review these three streams of literature. Then, we propose our research model and hypotheses.

## 2.1. Overview of culture

Culture refers to values and beliefs of individuals within a unit. It is also considered to be the totality of socially transmitted behavior patterns, arts, beliefs, institutions, and all other products of human work and thought characteristic of a community or population [16]. There are a number of definitions of culture. Hofstede et al. [32] state “There is no consensus about its definition, but most authors will probably agree on the following characteristics of the organizational/corporate culture construct: it is (1) holistic (2) historically determined, (3) related to anthropological concepts, (4) socially constructed, (5) soft, and (6) dificult to change” (p. 286).

Culture is dependent on the unit of analysis; the unit could be a nation, organization, profession, functional area, or a team. Therefore, depending on the unit, culture can be categorized into diferent types: national culture, organizational culture, professional culture, functional culture, and team culture. A number of studies in the information systems literature have examined the role of national culture.

Organizational culture refers to common values and beliefs shared by individuals within an organization [33]. Schein [17] defined organizational culture as a pattern of shared set of basic assumptions that a group learned as it solved its problems of external adaptation and internal integration.

## 2.2. Literature review: culture and information systems

Culture plays a unique role in technologies such as the Internet and trans-national or global information systems, systems that provide information and services to individuals and organizations in diferent countries, with diferent cultures [19,34]. Studies have explored the efect of cultural diferences on the motivation of analysts and programmers [35], design of information systems [16,36], and technology acceptance [19,37,38].

Myers and Tan [39] agree that the examination of culture is important but observe that studies of national culture that dominate research in information systems are too “simplistic.” They recommend that information systems researchers should go beyond models of national culture. Warkentin et al. [40] emphasized the significance of cultural issues in the design, adoption, and use of IS in organizations. They pointed out the need for IS studies that consider cultural values at the individual level to study relationships between culture and the management of IT. An individual’s work behavior is influenced by various cultural norms, ranging from supranational, national, and organizational to professional cultures. These diferent cultures are not equally predominant for various task behaviors in a work environment. Relative influence of organizational and professional culture may be stronger for behaviors that are task focused and are related to an individual’s competency and practices [41]. Recent studies in the area have looked at culture at the organizational level, which is the focus of this research.

Boynton and Zmud [42] recommended that organizations should evaluate the importance of organizational culture and its impact on information technology planning. Researchers have investigated the role of organizational culture on absorptive capacity and information technology success [43], information technology adoption and difu sion [44], information technology implementation [45,46], information technology infrastructure flexibility [47], and user computer eficacy [48]. Other studies have looked at impact of organizational culture on specific technologies such as KMS [49] and implementation of data warehouses [50].

There have been a few studies that have looked at the impact of organizational culture and information systems. Because culture is a concept used frequently when discussing global firms and countries, it is not surprising that a number of studies of culture have been done in countries other than the U.S.A. Ifinedo [51] examined the interactions between organizational size, culture, structure, and some information technology factors in the context of ERP success. In this study, data were collected from 44 private and industrial firms in Finland and Estonia. Their results showed that organizational size, structure, and culture collectively impact ERP system success. Zefane and McLoughlin [52] studied the role of job satisfaction, communication, and culture on cooperation and stress in the work place. Data for thi study were collected from two departments of a medium-sized ICT company in Australia. The authors found diferent sub-cultures in the two departments. They found that job satisfaction and positive com munication with managers are predictors of feelings of stress and perceived cooperation. In another study, Tarafdar and Vaidya [53] in vestigated the role of organizational factors on the adoption of ecommerce technologies in India. The organizational factors include role of top management, organizational culture, structure, and characteristics of information systems professionals. There is an increased in terest in organizational culture and its impact on information systems and more research is needed in the area.

## 2.3. Literature review: unified theory for the acceptance and use of technology

The TAM [1,2,7] has been widely used in the information systems literature to explain individual adoption of information technology. In the 1990s, a number of studies either extended the model by identifying antecedents to technology acceptance or have replicated and validated the model. The TAM was the basis for a newer integrated model, the UTAUT. Next, we present an overview of the UTAUT model. Then, we review literature that has investigated the role of organizational culture in information systems research.

One of the recent technology acceptance models, UTAUT, synthesized elements across eight well-known technology acceptance models: the TRA, the TAM, the motivational model (MM), the TPB, the combined TAM and TPB, the model of PC utilization (MPCU), the innovation difusion theory (IDT) and the social cognitive theory (SCT). The objective of the UTAUT was to achieve a unified view of user acceptance [2,54,55]. The resulting unified model consists of four core components or determinants of intention and usage (these are described later). The model is a useful tool for managers to assess the likelihood of acceptance of a new technology within an organization. It also helps in understanding factors that drive acceptance of a new technology, so that appropriate features can be designed to facilitate acceptance of new technology by users.

A number of recent studies on technology acceptance have used the UTAUT model. Researchers have examined antecedents to the model, and others have applied the model to diferent contexts and even in diferent countries. ## 2.4. Espoused organizational culture and information systems

Schein [17] proposed a multilayered approach for organizational culture. The outermost layer of culture called artifacts consists of organizational structure and processes. The next inner layer is espoused culture beliefs and values, and the innermost layer consists of underlying assumptions. So why do we concentrate on espoused cultural values? According to Schien, “Though the essence of a group’s culture is its pattern of shared, basic taken-for-granted assumptions, the culture will manifest itself at the level of observable artifacts and shared espoused beliefs and values” ([17], p. 36). Although Schein proposed a model of organizational culture and its three layers, he did not provide a way to quantitatively measure espoused cultural values. For measures of cultural values, we turn to Denison and Mishra’s [74] model for understanding organizational culture. Denison and Mishra’s model emphasizes cultural traits and espoused values associated with efectiveness. They categorize cultural traits and values into four dimensions: adaptability, involvement, mission, and consistency. It is important to note that Denison and Mishra examine espoused cultural values embraced by individuals. We have taken a similar approach in this paper.

Our approach is similar to that used by Srite and Karahanna [19] in which they argue that individuals espouse national cultural traits to varying degrees. They considered national cultural traits such as masculinity/femininity, individual/collectivism, power distance, and uncertainty avoidance but at the individual level of analysis. It is appropriate for us to consider organizational cultural traits similar to the way Srite and Karahanna [19] considered national cultural values. They state that “…since at the national level culture is primarily defined through values [32,75], we suggest that at the individual level of analysis national culture manifests through an individual’s espoused national cultural values. Espoused national cultural values are defined as the degree to which an individual embraces the values of his or her national culture” ([19], p. 681). Hoehle et al. [37], in an approach similar to Srite and Karahanna [19], adopted Hofstede’s five culture values and conceptualized them as cultural values espoused at the in dividual level to understand users’ continued intentions to use mobile social media applications. They argue that an espoused cultural valuebased model that studies the extent to which an individual embraces cultural values is more appropriate for understanding the use of IT at the individual level. Culture is an organizational construct and it influences values of all individuals in the organization. These are the espoused cultural values that we focus on in this study.

There have been a few studies in the information system literature that have used Denison and Mishra’s model of culture. Ahmad et al.’s [65] case study of e-government use in Pakistan demonstrates that inherent cultural values and beliefs in the organization are critical to the adoption of and implementation of new technologies. This study uses four dimensions of Denison and Mishra [74] as constructs to measure the internalized values and shared beliefs of organization employees that allow them to get involved in the adoption and implementation process. Cerne et al. [76] argue that innovative firms with strong cultures have employees that tend to have similar beliefs and behavior patterns as identified by Denison and Mishra [74]. Chen [77], used Denison and Mishra’s constructs to measure “environmental organizational culture.” Their study found environmental organizational culture to be critical to strengthening green organizational identity and to improve green competitive advantage. Fey and Denison [78] applied the Denison and Mishra framework to compare cultures of Russian and America firms arguing that “organization culture is embedded in and shaped by national culture (p. 687).” Based on its use in multiple studies of organization culture, we believe that Denison and Mishra provide us with a validated framework for understanding espoused cultural values and their measurement.

Details of the literature review are provided in the theoretical framework section. Most studies in the information systems literature have concentrated on national culture and espoused national culture values. Ours is the first to explore espoused organizational culture values in the context of the acceptance of Internet technology. In their paper, Venkatesh et al. [31] provided a synthesis of the entire body of research on the UTAUT and suggested areas of future research. One of the areas they recommended was the study of organizational factors such as organization culture. They also suggested studies in countries other than the U.S.A. This study addresses a gap in the literature in the areas of espoused organizational culture, technology adoption in the public sector, as well as adoption in a developing country.

It is important to note here that organizational culture has been operationalized in a number of diferent ways in the research literature. Most have operationalized culture as a second-order construct. Etzioni [79] explained organizational culture by using two dimensions, involvement and participation, and classified organizations into three types - coercive, utilitarian, and normative, based on these two dimensions. Cameron and Quinn [80] used the dimensions of flexibility/ stability and internal/external focus to classify organizations into four types: (1) clan, which has flexibility and internal focus; (2) adhocracy, with flexibility and external focus: (3) hierarchy, with stability and internal focus; and (4) market, which is characterized by stability and external focus. Denison and Mishra [74] identified four traits of organizational culture: involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission at the individual level. We use these four traits as second-order constructs for espoused organizational culture. These constructs are described later in the research model section.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

We have provided a justification for research into the adoption of Internet technology in a governmental organization in a developing country and reviewed literature on models that are used to understand user acceptance and use of information and communication technolo gies. In this section, we present the research model we use for modeling Internet technology use, the UTAUT model, and discuss espoused organizational culture as an antecedent for the model. The validated UTAUT identified four factors that influence use of information technology. These are PE, EE, SI, and FC [55]. We list the definitions provided by Venkatesh et al. [2]. PE is the degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help her to attain gains in job performance. EE is the degree of ease associated with the use of the system. SI is the degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe she should use the new system. FC refer to the degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support use of the system.

Recent studies also suggest that the adoption and use of a new technology in an organization might be influenced by organizational culture [41]. We propose that espoused organizational culture is an antecedent to the UTAUT. Espoused organizational culture is operationalized as a second-order construct that consists of four traits: adaptability, mission, involvement, and consistency [74]. Involvement refers to the extent of participation in the organization. More the involvement of an individual within an organization, greater is the sense of ownership and responsibility. Consistency provides an implicit control system based on internalized values within the organization. It represents the degree of normative integration. Adaptability is a reflection of the norms and beliefs in the organization and provides the capacity for internal change in response to external conditions. Mission trait provides purpose and meaning and long-term vision. Table 2 provides a quick reference to Denison and Mishra’s organizational culture traits. We use these four traits, involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission, as constructs for organizational culture. In this study, we examined the use of Internet technologies in a government agency in a developing country. We believe that these individual traits will impact an individual’s perception of the ease of use and usefulness of the Internet technologies. That is, we propose that organizational culture is an antecedent to the UTAUT. We use the terms Internet technology and system interchangeably in this paper.

In this study, we propose that organizational culture is an important antecedent to the UTAUT. Fig. 1 graphically represents our research model, the UTAUT model. The validated UTAUT identified four factors that influence use of information technology. These are PE, EE, SI, and FC [55]. We have included these four factors in our model.

Balthazard et al. [81] found the constructive organization culture norms positively related to individual employee outcomes including role clarity (the extent to which organizational members know what is expected of them), job satisfaction, and behavioral conformity (the extent to which organizational members are required to think and be have diferently than otherwise would be the case, person/norm conflict). They concluded that “Regardless of professionalism and professed or assumed goal sharing or congruency, organizational members may not behave in ways that promote eficiency and efectiveness if doing so is inconsistent with their reference prevailing culture. … That understanding these cultures in terms of expected behaviors and norms can explain why some organizational units (or the entire organization) exhibit dysfunctional behaviors that are counter to the organization’s expressed values or mission, and which hamper eficiency and efec tiveness” ([81], p. 727). Pakdil and Leonard [29] have studied the effect of organizational culture on implementation of lean processes. Their study has shown that diferent levels of organizational culture have an impact on creativity, control, standardization, and predictable performance outcomes [29]. These measures are all related to EE. Ciganek et al. [28] in their study of organizational culture and its efect on the acceptance of KMS used the TAM. TAM is the precursor to UTAUT, the model we have used in this study. Ciganek et al. [28] found that process-oriented culture has a significant impact on perceived usefulness of the system. Perceived usefulness is the same as the PE measure in the UTAUT. Therefore, we propose that espoused organizational culture will influence PE.

PE is the extent to which individuals think that using information systems or technology will help them with their job performance. This is a reasonable goal. Therefore, an organization’s culture that allows adaptability and consistency and facilitates design/adoption of information systems to align with the organization’s mission should influence the PE of individual member of the organization. Taking this a step further, we could say that the espoused organizational cultural values shown by the individual member of the organization will impact her PE. Therefore, we hypothesize:

## H1. : Espoused organizational culture values will influence PE.

We, as users, expect information systems and technology to be easy to use. It is a reasonable expectation in most organizations. EE is the extent to which an individual perceives a system is easy to use. Such an expectation can be a reflection of an individual's ability to adapt and even be involved with the development and maintenance of the system. Studies have shown that organizational culture has a significant impact on perceived ease of use of KMS [28]. Perceived ease of use is a TAM construct and is equivalent to EE in UTAUT. Therefore, we believe that espoused cultural values such as adaptability and involvement will impact EE. Therefore, we propose:

## H2. : Espoused organizational culture values will influence EE.

SI is the degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe that she should use the system [2]. The root construct of SI is subjective norms [2]. Subjective norms was originally proposed in TAM [1]. Ciganek et al. [28], in their study of acceptance of KMS, found that organizational culture influences subjective norms. Therefore, we believe organizational culture will influence SI. Balthazard et al. [81] found that the organization culture influences behavioral conformity (the extent to which organizational members are required to think and behave diferently than otherwise would be the case, person/norm conflict). Organizational culture will drive these expectations of system use. We expect that espoused organizational cultural values such as involvement and mission will drive such expectations. As the espoused culture exhibits traits of higher involvement and higher mission, there will be an increased expectation of use of the system. Therefore, we state hypothesis 3 as follows:

## H3. : Espoused organizational culture will have a significant impact on SI.

FC refer to the degree to which an individual believes that an organization and technical infrastructure support use of the information system [2]. ## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Sample

This study is aimed at understanding how employees in a government organization, Wildlife Institute of India (WII), use Internet tech nologies, and how can the acceptance and use of these technologies be enhanced equitably across government organizations. WII was established in 1986 as a nonprofit autonomous Institute of the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change of the Government of India. WII’s mission is “… is to nurture the development of wildlife science and promote its application in conservation, in consonants with our cultural and socio-economic milieu”<sup>1</sup> with the aim to provide research, training, and advisory services in wildlife management and nature conservation to oficers in various Indian government services including Indian Administrative Services, Indian Army, and Department of Customs, and to sensitize people at various strata for nature conservation. WII also collaborates with international organizations such as the UNESCO, Food and Agricultural Organization, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Asian countries such as Nepal, Bhutan, China, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam. WII is supported by the Central Government of India (equivalent to the federal government in U.S.A.) in its technology investment to facilitate WII objectives.

## 4.2. Survey design and data collection

A survey questionnaire was used to collect data regarding use of Internet technologies in a government agency in India. The questionnaire consisted of 37 items. In addition to 10 demographic information items, this paper-based questionnaire collected data from individual users of Internet-based technologies on a number of constructs identified in the research model using 27 items. These constructs included espoused organizational culture, PE, EE, FC, SI, behavioral intention to use, and actual use. Earlier UTAUT research had validated measures for each of the constructs, and we decided to include those validated items in our questionnaire. Each of the four organization culture traits, involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission, consists of two validated items taken from Denison and Mishra [74]. We used this questionnaire to collect data from 102 individuals in a government agency. A list of 27 questionnaire items for UTAUT and organization culture constructs used in the study is provided in Appendix 1.

Survey was administered in the English language as the employees working in the chosen government organization conducted all their oficial work in the English language and were deemed proficient in it. Each item in the survey used a 7-point Likert scale, anchored 1= “strongly disagree” and 7= “strongly agree.”

For this study, we designed a survey and then conducted a pilot study with five employees in a department at another government organization in India to test the design eficacy of the survey. Pilot study did not suggest any major changes to the survey research questions except for some minor changes to the language in a few questions. Questionnaire items used are provided in Appendix 1. After finalizing the questions, a survey was conducted at WII in India by distributing paper-based surveys to employees over several days. Out of the 110 surveys that were distributed, a total of 102 completed surveys were returned with a return rate of almost 93%. One of the reasons for this high response rate was that one of the authors was able to spend several weeks at the government agency.

## 5. Results

We used a multistep process to analyze the data. We first checked for multicollinearity. Table 4 shows means, standard deviations, reliability, and correlations between independent and outcome variables. We found that Cronbach alpha values for reliability were all above 0.80, a generally accepted threshold. Correlations between variables did not exceed 0.60. The average variance extracted values for the individual constructs were greater than 0.8 and greater than the correlations with other constructs in our study.

We examined construct validity by conducting a factor analysis to review loadings and cross loadings of the latent variables. While most items loaded on the corresponding factor, there were a couple of items, PE4 and FC4, that did not. Closer examination of the items showed that these measures were not relevant to the constructs. Item PE4 refers to “If I use the Internet, I increase my chances of getting a salary increase or promotion.” Our sample was from a government organization, where salary increases and promotions are primarily based on years of service. Therefore, this item was not relevant to our construct. We dropped it from further analysis and the measurement of PE. Item FC4, “The Internet is not compatible with other systems I use,” was another item that did not load on the factor FC. The item did not provide clarity to the respondent on which other systems the Internet is not compatible with and was, therefore, interpreted diferently by diferent users. We dropped FC4 as an item for FC. The resultant factor loadings are provided in Appendix 2. All items loaded on their corresponding factor. All factor loadings were 0.7 or above except for one, SI2, that was close to 0.6.

We tested our data for signs of common method bias. We applied Harman’s single-factor test and found no evidence of common-method bias due to our use of a single method, a survey, to collect data. Nine factors were extracted from the data with eigenvalue greater than one, and these correspond to the latent variables in this study.

We conducted a partial least squares (PLS) regression analysis to test the hypotheses in our research model. According to Denison and Mishra [74], involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission are dimen sions of organizational culture. So, in our model, we defined espoused organizational culture as a second-order construct. In PLS, we set up organization culture as a formative construct with the four cultural indicators or dimensions. Each dimension was a reflective construct measured by two items [74]. The rest of the model for PLS regression analysis was set up in line with our research model (see Fig. 1). Because espoused organizational culture is a formative construct, the PLS regression results (Fig. 2) show arrows from the cultural indicators involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission to the espoused organizational culture variable. A formative construct refers to an index of a weighted sum of variables. Therefore, the values next to the arrows from cultural indicators to espoused organizational culture in Fig. 2 are the weights for the cultural indicators: involvement (0.288), consistency (0.307), adaptability (0.284), and mission (0.307), and these weights determine the higher order construct, espoused organizational culture.

Results from PLS regression show that most of our hypotheses are supported. Fig. 2 provides results of PLS regression. We first examine the espoused organizational culture variables and their role as antecedents to the UTAUT. We found that espoused organization culture influences PE, EE, SI, and FC. Espoused organization culture has a positive impact on the PE (coeficient = 0.24, R<sup>2</sup> = 0.06, and p-value < 0.01). Therefore, we found support for hypothesis 1. We also found support for hypothesis 2. Our results show that organization culture influences EE (coeficient = 0.32, R<sup>2</sup> = 0.10, and p-value < 0.01). We also found support for hypotheses 3 and 4. Espoused organizational culture influences SI (coeficient = 0.43, $\mathrm { R } ^ { 2 } = 0 . 1 9 )$ and FC (coeficient = 0.49, $\mathrm { R } ^ { 2 } = 0 . 0 2 4 )$ both at the 0.01 level of significance. In summary, all our espoused organizational culture hypotheses, H1 through H4, were supported.

Although we found that espoused organizational culture had a significant impact on the technology acceptance variables such as the PE, EE, SI and FC, we analyzed these relationships further by running individual regressions using the espoused organizational culture dimen sions of involvement, consistency, adaptability, and mission. A summary of our findings with the results of our analysis is provided in Appendix 3. We now examine our results for the UTAUT variables. Hypotheses H5, H6, and H7 examine the influence of the PE, EE, and SI on the behavioral intention to use the system. We found partial support for hypotheses H5 and H7. The PE influences the behavioral intention (p value < 0.1) to use the system but gender does not have a significant moderating efect on the relationship. We found that the SI has a positive influence on the behavioral intention to use the system (pvalue < 0.05), but we did not find a significant moderating impact of gender on this relationship either. Considering all the results, we can say that the UTAUT, which was primarily proposed and tested in a developed country, can also explain information technology acceptance in a developing country such as India.

## 6. Discussion

Our research model drew on organization cultural traits developed by Denison and Mishra [74]. The model was designed to better understand the influence of an individual’s espoused organization cultural traits on the technology acceptance constructs using the UTAUT [2,54,55]. The PLS results indicated that our model incorporating espoused organization cultural values as antecedents to the UTAUT technology factors is supported. This demonstrates that the acceptance of a technology by the individuals in an organization does not take place in a vacuum. Technology acceptance success in a developing country is dependent on the context of the organization, its values, and its culture. Further, our analysis of the dimensions of the espoused organization culture constructs at a more granular level indicates that the culture trait, mission, is significant as an antecedent of the three of the four UTAUT technology acceptance variables. This underscores the need in organizations to ensure that each employee is cognizant of the organization’s mission and values and is an active participant in the actualization of the mission through its alignment with the organiza tion’s policies and procedures.

Our study was conducted in India, a developing country with an emerging technological infrastructure, especially in government and government afiliated organizations, which typically are slower to adopt new technologies. India presents special challenges for organizations considering technology adoption with its variety of languages (the Indian Constitution lists 22 oficial languages), ethnicities, customs, and local cultures. This work provides managers with the incentive to develop strategies to inculcate a coherent organization culture where each employee feels part of the organization mission and is aware of its values. This should help in achieving a higher rate of the technology acceptance.

## 6.1. Theoretical contributions

Prior research (see Table 1) on exploring the antecedents of the UTAUT have focused on perceived business threats and perceived private threats [60] and several antecedents to the mobile services in Taiwan [64]. Another study by Chiu and Hofer [15] tested UTAUT in two culturally diferent market contexts and implied that local and institutional environment influence the technology acceptance. However, their study did not consider any actual culture constructs in the research model and considered culture at the national level using Hofstede’s classification of the two countries. Our study has empirically demonstrated that the espoused organization cultural values play an important role in the acceptance of the Internet technology in organizations in a developing country, India. This research has several important theoretical contributions.

First, our research is among the first to theorize and test the espoused organization cultural values from Denison and Mishra [74] as the antecedents to understand the technology acceptance in organizations. Therefore, an organization introducing new technology should consider how meaningful the organization culture is to its employees. Warkentin et al. [40] pointed out the paucity of research focusing on the cultural issues in the technology. This work then makes an important contribution to the nascent body of research in understanding of the role of cultural issues in the design, adoption, and use of IS in organizations.

Second, this research contributes to the literature that examines the value of using espoused culture in advancing information systems research. Hoehle et al. [37] stated in their paper that early research using culture in information system studies was primarily cross-cultural or country-specific comparisons, which treated culture as a monolithic construct. Srite and Karahana [19] have long called for the need to study espoused culture values. Hoehle et al. [37] reiterate the call for conducting studies that employ espoused cultural values “because they can be influenced by membership in professional, organizational, ethnic, religious, and other social groups besides national culture” ([19], p. 697). Our research addresses that need and contributes to the growing literature on espoused culture traits in information systems research.

Third, our research is in the context of an economically developing country such as India while most of the technology acceptance work has been conducted in the context of economically developed countries. Table 1 provides a summary of the technology acceptance work using the UTAUT model in diferent countries. Weeger et al. [60] had India as one of the countries in their multicountry study. Hoehle et al. [37] also conducted their study in five countries, one of which was India. Emerging economies such as India vary culturally from the developed countries and, therefore, warrant studies focusing specifically on their cultural issues and influence of these cultural issues on the advance ment of IS. Our work addresses this gap and provides a key direction in understanding these issues further.

Finally, this study adds to the body of knowledge of UTAUT and its applications. It confirms that the UTAUT model can be further enriched by including antecedents that provide greater insight into its constructs: PE, EE, SI, and FC.

Overall, our results show that espoused organizational culture is an antecedent to the technology acceptance, and UTAUT is a valid model that can explain the acceptance of technology in a government organization in a developing country.

## 6.2. Implications for practice

Our study also has implications for practice. Managers should pay attention to the role of espoused culture values of individuals within the organization when planning to implement new information technologies. While our research does not diminish the role of the technology artifact in its acceptance, it raises important issues related to the context in which technology is accepted and used. It is imperative that managers review culture traits of people in the organization and see to it that information technologies that are implemented align with those traits. This alignment, which may be part of the change management process in an organization, will help support the success of the technology.

Second, a detailed examination of espoused organizational culture traits shows that the mission dimension of culture has the most impact on factors in the UTAUT model. Therefore, it is of utmost importance that the organizational culture provides purpose and meaning and longterm vision (see definition of the mission trait in Table 2). In addition to the mission trait that influences three of the four technology acceptance variables, PE, EE, and SI, the adaptability trait impacts PE, involvement afects SI, and consistency influences FC. Therefore, for technology acceptance to be successful, a manager should pay attention to all espoused organizational culture traits. In short, our findings inform business executives and managers about the strategic potential of managing organizational culture.

## 7. Conclusion

## 7.1. Summary of result

Our results show that espoused organizational culture values have an impact on the individual’s acceptance and use of Internet technology in a government agency in developing country. This implies that organizational culture should be carefully managed for the successful adoption and difusion of Internet and other technologies. The mission trait of organizational culture is perhaps most important when it comes to technology acceptance. This trait that refers to long-term purpose and vision of an organization positively impacts PE, EE, and SI. Organizations need to provide greater emphasis on identifying its longterm purpose and reason for existence. Adaptability is the organizational culture trait that captures the ability of a firm to make internal changes in response to changes to the external environment. More adaptable the organization, less is the PE. If stability is considered to be the opposite of adaptability, more stable the internal environment of an organization, more is the PE. Overall, organization culture and all its constituent traits need to be managed carefully to enable successful acceptance of information technology.

## 7.2. Limitations and future research

Our study also has its limitations. We have examined the role of espoused organizational culture on the acceptance of one technology within a government agency in an emerging economy, India. We did not test our model for other information technologies and across other government agencies and private companies. Second, the government organization we studied is a small institute within the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. Espoused organizational culture in smaller organizations can be diferent from that in much larger organizations. This could limit the generalizability of our findings. Third, because of the small size of the organization, the sample used was a convenience sample. Fourth, all respondents in this study belonged to a small organization and live in a geographically small area, which could limit the variability of the sample.

We believe our study is a step in the right direction and additional research is needed in this area.

Next, we examine a few areas for future research that could ascertain if the results indeed generalize to other contexts, subjects, and sampling techniques. Replicating this study with a much larger organization that enables randomization of the sample may yield diferent results. Additionally, future research focusing on a geographically distributed large organization could provide additional support for the espoused organization culture component of this research.

Future research on the acceptance of information technology in emerging economies could consider other related Internet technologies and their adoption in organizations. The use of mobile technologies may be an interesting area. Other emerging economies such as Brazil, Russia, China, and South Africa (the other members of BRICS) can be examined to see if the espoused organizational culture has a similar impact on technology acceptance.

Additional research is needed on the influence of espoused organizational culture on the acceptance of other technology artifacts. Each artifact has its own characteristics and further study is needed to determine whether espoused organizational culture traits have a similar impact on technology acceptance as we found in our study. In this study, we examined the role of espoused organizational culture on the acceptance of the Internet within a government agency. Further studies on acceptance of other technologies is needed to check that our findings

are generalizable across artifacts.

We used gender as the moderating variable. Future research should consider additional variables such as age, length of service in the organization, income, location, and nature of the occupation, job performance as additional moderating variables. Variables such as psy chological profile, confidence, and leadership qualities could be used as additional constructs to their influence on the espoused organization culture.

We believe that we have made a valuable contribution to the lit erature in the area of cultural research in information systems. We recommend further research in governmental and non-governmental organizations in diferent countries for inquiry into the acceptance and use of Internet technologies.

We ran separate regressions using the espoused organizational culture dimensions as our independent variables. The table above provides results from regression analysis. Our results show that espoused organizational culture influences information technology adoption. We found support for Hypotheses 1, 2, 3, and 4. Adaptability and mission cultural traits have a significant impact on PE. While mission has a positive influence on PE, adaptability has a negative impact. Only mission has a positive impact on EE. Involvement and mission cultural traits influence SI. The only cultural trait that has an efect on FC is consistency. In short, our results show that espoused organizational culture affects Internet technology adoption, This also supports existing literature in the area of culture and information systems [43,44,46,50].

According to our results espoused cultural values such as adaptability and mission influence PE. Adaptability as a value is a reflection of the norms and beliefs in the organization and provides the capacity for internal change in response to external conditions. Mission provides purpose and meaning as well as long-term vision, These two cultural values are related because they are a reflection of the long-term vision of the organizatior and the flexibility the organization has to attain this vision. EE is only influenced by the mission cultural trait. This seems intuitive because the mission of an organization helps in focusing efort to achieve certain common objectives.

Our results show that espoused cultural values involvement and mission afect SI. SI is the degree to which an individual perceives that important while others believe she should use the new system. Involvement refers to the extent of participation in the organization. More the involvement of an individual within an organization, greater is the sense of ownership and responsibility. Mission, we know, provides purpose and meaning. Because involvement refers to the degree of participation, it seems likely that someone with high involvement will consider what others want and consider i as important. Therefore, this individual will use the system.

The consistency cultural trait impacts FC. Consistency provides an implicit control system based on internalized values within the organization. It represents the degree of normative integration. FC refer to the degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support use of the system. An organization that exhibits a higher consistency cultural trait is likely to have a consistent orga nizational and technical infrastructure in place to support system use. This is confirmed by the results we have above.
