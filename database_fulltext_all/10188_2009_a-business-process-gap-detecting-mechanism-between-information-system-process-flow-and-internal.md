## a b s t r a c t

The information system (IS) has become an important backbone of the modern enterprise, which has caused electronic data generated by the IS to be more easily manipulated and destroyed than hardcopy data. It is therefore important for auditors to assure that the IS is well-controlled and secure. Auditors generally use Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques and Tools (CAATTs) to assist them in auditing the IS, though given the growing complexity of these systems, it is hard to completely perform a control test of the systems. This research aims to develop a mechanism, namely the Business Process Gap Detecting Mechanism (BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism), to automatically detect the business process gap between IS processes and interna control <sup>fl</sup>ows. This study also justi<sup>fi</sup>es the feasibility of BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism by providing a real case study. The result indicates that the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism can assist the case company in resolving data quality problems that have occurred in its ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) system and can also provide additional information for the company.

## 1. Introduction

During the last several decades, the information system (IS) has become the backbone of the modern enterprise because of its ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency and effectiveness. However, electronic data generated by the IS have become easier to change, manipulate, or destroy. These aspects of IS have created an easier way for crime to take place [16]. This fact was quanti<sup>fi</sup>ed in the 2005 Computer Crime and Security Survey, which revealed that losses caused by computer crimes and computer security breaches were \$130,104,542 for the 639 respondents in 2005 [17]. Hence, it is important for auditors to assure that ISs are adequately controlled, secured, and function as intended [39].

Reassuring IS security in the modern business environment requires a complete understanding of the continuous events that comprise the way businesses organize their activities. An important way to attain this understanding is to deconstruct a business into its underlying business processes [5]. It should be noted that the Sarbanes–Oxley (SOX) Act [44] requires both managers and auditors to force <sup>fi</sup>rms to follow their business activities more closely and verify controls over the <sup>fi</sup>rm's <sup>fi</sup>nancial reporting processes [23]. To improve the performance and ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency of business processes, IS activities should be recorded in a log that indicates when and who accesses what data [3]. These logs should be reviewed frequently to ensure that system activities parallel business activities [43].

However, the increasing complexity and variety of IS along with the need to uncover unrevealed information provides auditors with serious challenges. It is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to audit the IS if auditors do not know the data structure and process <sup>fl</sup>ow inside the system. When auditing the IS, either the control test or the substantive test is conventionally required. The control test indicates the illegal actions which have violated internal controls, while the substantive test is performed to test the material misstatements afterwards [40]. In general, auditors use the Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques and Tools (CAATTs) to support them in auditing these systems [11,36,43]. Most CAATTs provide the substantive test of the IS, but it is more effective to review the controls of the systems than to audit the output data [53]. In addition, the CAATTs which only provide the substantive test of the IS may fail to <sup>fi</sup>nd the business process gap between IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The above phenomenon may lead to poor data quality, the mask-up effect, incorrect decision-making, or fraudulence.

Therefore, the control test is essential for the CAATTs to ensure that the business process gap does not exist between IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. In speci<sup>fi</sup>c, both the control test and substantive test should be included to complement each other in the CAATTs. In practice, auditors should not only audit the output data produced after the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow, but audit the activities conducted during the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow itself. For some complex information systems, they need to perform more control tests rather than only substantive tests or control tests in order to reduce the audit risk to an appropriate level [57].

It is doubtless that CAATTs can help auditors to audit the IS in either a static or dynamic way. Although many CAATTs were proposed in the past two decades, they are still in their infancy and possess some limitations and drawbacks. Generalized Audit Software (GAS), Parallel Simulation, Test Data, and Integrated Test Facility (ITF) focus on the output data produced by the IS. They provide the substantive test and risk assessment, and cannot detect the business process gap between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. Although the concept and execution of the control test are not dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to realize, the prototype systems/commercial software which can perform the control test are limited.

An Embedded Audit Module can detect the business process gap between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow by working with the IS. However, it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to construct a general system which can combine with different IS. Additionally, client cooperation is also a disadvantage of Embedded Audit Module [20], as auditees are not always willing to be cooperative with auditors, whose applications affect their IS. In short, because of the complexity of the IS without combining the CAATTs with these systems, it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to conduct the entire control test of the IS.

As a result, this paper aims to propose a mechanism to automatically perform a control test of the IS of <sup>fi</sup>rms. This prototype system is expected to achieve the following objectives:

• automatically identify the internal control for the audit objects;

• automatically identify the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow for the audit objects;

• automatically compare the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow with the IS <sup>fl</sup>ow to detect any differences between these two <sup>fl</sup>ows.

In addition to the aforementioned objectives, this study can also be recognized as an effective development practice which can provide the structured form, formal logic and mathematical algorithm for the whole auditing process and can be used as a reference for future researchers and practitioners.

The purpose of the substantive test is to ensure the integrity and data quality of the output data produced by the IS. Both GAS and Parallel Simulation can perform the substantive test, though GAS is the most frequently used technique of CAATTs [11]. GAS software like Audit Command Language (ACL) and Interactive Data Extraction and Analysis (IDEA) are widely employed by <sup>fi</sup>rms. It has a friendly user interface to extract the data produced by the systems and analyzes the patterns of variables. By constructing another identical IS, Parallel Simulation will compare and verify the data produced by both systems to detect the gap/difference between two systems.

The control test, including risk assessment and compliance tests, is used to ensure IS process <sup>fl</sup>ows. Both Test Data and ITF can perform the control test. Test Data is a technique in which auditors prepare the input data for the auditee's IS and analyze the output results to assess the potential risk of the systems. The ITF is similar to the Test Data, as both ITF and Test Data employ some dummy data as test sets to help auditors analyze the output result of the dummy test data and to detect any potential risk.

The Hybrid Test, like the Embedded Audit Module, can perform both substantive tests and control tests. The Embedded Audit Module combines the audit module with the IS, and can therefore monitor the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and collect data. However, it is less popular due to its dif<sup>fi</sup>culty in getting cooperation of the auditee and in constructing a general system which can combine with different ISs.

## 2.2. Dynamic modeling tools for internal control flow

Internal control is a process that permeates an organization's activities and hence, provides reasonable assurance that the following control objectives are achieved [43]. To this end, internal control is critical to any business organization. Without the ability to ensure the accuracy and reliability of information produced by ISs, it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult for a business organization to survive in a competitive environment [8]. Furthermore, the implementation of ISs and other associated systems performed in business process is not straightforward [19]. For these reasons, it is necessary to assure that the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow conforms to the planned control objective. The design and evaluation of systems of internal control also have become important tasks to auditors. This is because they are charged with responsibility of certifying to the reliability of information produced by ISs [8].

Auditors evaluate information reliability by assessing whether or not the appropriate system controls are in place and under operating effectively [18]. Almost all practicing auditors employ some sort of internal control system, such as questionnaires, narrative descriptions, and <sup>fl</sup>owcharts, in their resulting evaluation process [8]. Utilizing the computer-assisted methods has the unique advantage of bringing the speed, accuracy, and great memory capacity of the computer to carry out the internal control evaluation problem [8,34]. The computerassisted evaluation actually allows the utilization for larger and more complex models. Moreover, it can be much more rigorous than the using of manual methods [8,34].

To complete the assessments of the reliability of system controls, IS auditors <sup>fi</sup>rstly need to understand the IS processes information [18]. In order to realize the system and thus, conduct the control test for an IS, auditors rely heavily on an understanding of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow of the IS. Therefore, it is important to choose a suitable, computer-assisted dynamic modeling tool to keep track of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow of the IS. The most common tools are: Flowchart [21], Event-driven Process Chain (EPC) [56], extended Event-driven Process Chain (eEPC) [25,46], IDEF [7], Uni<sup>fi</sup>ed Modeling Language (UML) [10,45], Data Flow Diagram (DFD) [47,52], Business Process Modeling Notation (BPMN) [49], and Petri-Net [12]. Table 2 classi<sup>fi</sup>es them into three categories: work<sup>fl</sup>ow model, data<sup>fl</sup>ow model, and mathematical model. The classi<sup>fi</sup>cation can focus the modeling tools on the usage.

The purpose of the work<sup>fl</sup>ow model is to depict the dynamic work<sup>fl</sup>ow which has the sequential, iterative, parallel, and conditional properties of the IS. The purpose of the data<sup>fl</sup>ow model is to depict the dynamic data<sup>fl</sup>ow through the processes. The work<sup>fl</sup>ow model and data<sup>fl</sup>ow model can contain the fundamental <sup>fl</sup>ows of the IS, but they cannot provide any simulation and analysis method. Therefore, to simulate and analyze the system, a mathematical model is needed. Mathematical models can depict the IS by using graphical notation and performing dynamic simulation and mathematical analysis on the model.

## 2.3. The mathematical model: Petri-Net

Petri-Net is a mathematical modeling tool which was invented by C. A. Petri (1962) to describe and simulate the concurrent processes of the networks [58]. It provides both graphical description and mathematical analysis capabilities and is widely used in simulating and analyzing the concurrent, asynchronous, distributed, and stochastic systems [28,37,54].

A Petri-Net is de<sup>fi</sup>ned by 5 tuples:

PN = (P, T, F, W, M), where:

• P is the set of places.

• T is the set of transitions.

$ { \mathrm { F } } \subseteq (  { \mathrm { P } } \times  { \mathrm { T } } ) \cup (  { \mathrm { T } } \times  { \mathrm { P } } )$ is the set of arcs.

• W is a function which means the weight of the arc.

• M is the marking vector which means the number of tokens in each place when a Petri Net is static.

When the transitions are <sup>fi</sup>ring, which means the processors, the tasks, or the functions of the systems are executing, the numbers of the tokens will be removed from the input places and are added to the output places according to the weight of the input arc and output arc [2,31]. Then the conditions or the resources of the systems are said to be changed or moved from one to another one, and the marking of the Petri-Net is updated to represent the new state.

## 2.4. Reachability analysis of the Petri-Net

The major strength of the Petri-Net is to analyze the properties of a complicated system or problem in an easy and well-constructed way. In this research, reachability analysis of the Petri-Net is used to detect any business process gap. If a marking $M _ { b }$ is possible to reach from $M _ { a }$ after a sequence of transitions is <sup>fi</sup>red, it is said that $M _ { b }$ can be reached from $M _ { a } .$ The Incidence Matrix and State Equation can analyze the reachability of Petri-Net. The method creates a matrix: Incidence Matrix I based on the Petri-Net. Each row of I is the place and each column of I is the transition. Elements $\pmb { { E } } _ { i , j }$ in I means the number of tokens changed of place j when transition i <sup>fi</sup>red. After creating the Incidence Matrix, it can be performed on the State Equation: $M _ { b } = M _ { a } + I { \times } f i r i n g$ vector to predict the reachability between $M _ { a }$ and $M _ { b } .$ Firing vector is a vector of non-negative integers which maps every transition to its number of occurrences in a <sup>fi</sup>ring sequence [9,50]. This method is easy to implement on computers because of the mathematical formula. Fig. 1 is a simple example of the Incidence Matrix and State Equation. By comparing the vector values $M _ { a }$ and $M _ { b } ,$ the reachability of Petri-Net can detect the difference between two data sets. The example that illustrates this reachability can be seen in the Section of 3.5.

## 3. System architecture

The objective of this research is to develop an automatic mechanism to detect the business process gap between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The mechanism should be automatic in order to support continuous auditing [24,40,41]. The audit object of the automatic mechanism is the business process compliance test, and the audit evidence the automatic mechanism needs includes the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The automatic mechanism has three phases:

The three phases use a simple logical inference method. Phase1 and Phase2 will identify and reconcile the two pieces of audit evidence, while Phase3 is the kernel algorithm of the business process gap-detecting mechanism. This three phase architecture is widely used in other gap detecting architecture approaches.

After Phase3, an analysis report is produced to identify the business process gap between IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The business process gap may exist due to nonstandard actions taken by end users, modi<sup>fi</sup>cation of systems without updating internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow, primary error of the systems, a security <sup>fl</sup>aw of the system, etc. Once the analysis report indicates that the IS has a business process gap, auditors need to determine why the business process gap exists and formulate the audit report. The IS is comprised of the work<sup>fl</sup>ow and data<sup>fl</sup>ow [52]. The eEPC diagram extended the work<sup>fl</sup>ow model EPC (Event-driven Process Chain) by providing it with enhanced resources [46] so that it involves both the work<sup>fl</sup>ow and data<sup>fl</sup>ow. The eEPC has also been widely used. • R is a <sup>fi</sup>nite set of resources.

$\mathsf { D F } \subseteq ( \mathsf { F } \times \mathsf { S } ) \cup ( \mathsf { R } \times \mathsf { F } )$ is a set of data<sup>fl</sup>ow arcs.

There should <sup>fi</sup>rst be an underlying EPC diagram which describes the basic work<sup>fl</sup>ow of the IS. Then, resources and data<sup>fl</sup>ow arcs are added on the EPC diagram where data<sup>fl</sup>ow arcs connect the resources and the functions of EPC. The underlying EPC should follow the requirements of EPC formalized by Aalst [1] to avoid con<sup>fl</sup>ict of the work<sup>fl</sup>ow. The data<sup>fl</sup>ow should also contain some detailed de<sup>fi</sup>nitions about what operations the functions perform on the resources.

De<sup>fi</sup>nition2 de<sup>fi</sup>nes the operations according to the direction of the data<sup>fl</sup>ow. It classi<sup>fi</sup>es the operations into input data<sup>fl</sup>ow operation and output data<sup>fl</sup>ow operation. The details of the input data<sup>fl</sup>ow operation and the output data<sup>fl</sup>ow operation are based on the Data Manipulation Languages (DML) of the database.

De<sup>fi</sup>nition 2. (Operations of Data<sup>fl</sup>ow): Let eEPC=(G, R, DF) and DF=(SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE), where:

• ∀DFp(R × F)→DF= (SELECT),

an input data<sup>fl</sup>ow arc must have a SELECE operation.

• ∀DFp(F×R)→DF=(INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE),

an output data<sup>fl</sup>ow arc must have an INSERT, UPDATE, or DELETE operation.

## 3.1.3. Phase1-3: adjusted Petri-Net of the internal control flow

Petri-Net, which is converted from eEPC, should be adjusted to reconcile with the structure of the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow. The reconciliation of the <sup>fl</sup>ow can reduce the con<sup>fl</sup>icts of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow and IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow when performing the analysis mechanism.

First, the resources which do not affect the major transactions in Petri-Net should be removed. Second, to avoid the ignored data<sup>fl</sup>ow, the weight of the arcs between data<sup>fl</sup>ow places and data<sup>fl</sup>ow transitions are adjusted to equal 2. This means that the data<sup>fl</sup>ow places must require two tokens for <sup>fi</sup>ring the connected data<sup>fl</sup>ow transitions. Once the data<sup>fl</sup>ows are scanned from the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow, one token is put into the corresponding places of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow, so that there are two tokens in the corresponding places and the connected data<sup>fl</sup>ow transitions can <sup>fi</sup>re. The token in data<sup>fl</sup>ow places of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow will not move to other places if a business process gap exists between it and the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow. Third, the transition: RESOURCE UNCONCERNED is added for all resources to reduce the tokens of the resources place in case the resources may become irrelevant later. While executing the SELECT operation of a data<sup>fl</sup>ow, the original token will be reserved in the initial resource place and the new copied token will be moved to other places. At the end of the data<sup>fl</sup>ow, the reserved token may become irrelevant. For example, SALES DOC. HEADER (which can be represented by VBAK) and SALES DOC. ITEM (which can be represented by VBAP) are relevant in SALES process, but irrelevant in the DELIVERY process. Therefore, it is required that the transitions are added for all resources to reduce the reserved token.

## 3.2. Phase2: Identifying the IS process flow for audit object

## 3.2.1. Phase2-1: extracting database log

The second phase identi<sup>fi</sup>es the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow. The IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow has both a logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow and physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow, wherein the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow is physically implemented by the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow [52].

Identifying the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow of IS is more dif<sup>fi</sup>cult than the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow. Various IS have various work<sup>fl</sup>ow log structures, though almost all database management systems (DBMS) have the same database log structure. However, because the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow is physically implemented by the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow, this study can use the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow and extend it to represent the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow. For instance, the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow: NEW SALES ORDER DOCUMENT is physically implemented by the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow: INSERT VBAK and INSERT VBAP. This study supposes that if data<sup>fl</sup>ow of INSERT VBAK and INSERT VBAP is performed, it must be performed by the work<sup>fl</sup>ow: NEW SALES ORDER DOCUMENT. Therefore, the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow (INSERT VBAK and INSERT VBAP) can be extended to the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow (NEW SALES ORDER DOCUMENT). It is then possible to combine the extended work<sup>fl</sup>ow and the data<sup>fl</sup>ow with the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow. The extension of physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow to logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow is implemented in Phase3: Business Process Gap Analysis.

The database log of DBMS, which records how IS use database manipulation language (DML) commands to interact with DBMS, is the result of the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow. Retrieving the database log is easy because DBMS such as Oracle and Microsoft SQL (Structured Query Language) Server can provide the log <sup>fi</sup>le, which records activities run on them in a common structure [35,38,42].

## 3.3. Phase3: business process gap analysis

The <sup>fi</sup>nal phase of the mechanism is business process gap analysis. The mathematical analysis properties of the mathematical model are used to test the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow. This research uses the Incidence Matrix and State Equation of the Petri-Net to test the business process gap between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow.

The Incidence Matrix $I = \lfloor a _ { i j } \rfloor$ and entry of it represents the number of places that the token changed $P _ { j }$ when transition $\pmb { T _ { i } }$ is <sup>fi</sup>ring. And for the Incidence Matrix I, there exists a state equation: $M _ { d } = M _ { d - 1 } + I ^ { T } \sum _ { 1 } ^ { d } U _ { k } .$

$M _ { d }$ and $M _ { d - 1 }$ is the marking of Petri-Net, ${ \pmb I } ^ { T }$ is the transpose matrix of Incidence Matrix I, and $\sum _ { 1 } ^ { d } U _ { k }$ is an i×1 Transition Firing Matrix which indicates the number of times transition $\pmb { T _ { i } }$ <sup>fi</sup>res.

The reachability analysis of Petri-Net is conducted according to the matrix algebra and can be calculated by using the following steps:

## 4. Mechanism development

This research constructs a Business Process Gap Detecting Mechanism (BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism) system to implement the business process gap detection. The BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system is developed based on the environment of Microsoft .NET and the Microsoft SQL Server. This study separates functions of the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system into six fundamental libraries such as abstraction, encapsulation, polymorphism, and inheritance of the object-oriented model to take advantage of the inherent strengths. Therefore, the eEPC can be replaced by other dynamic modeling tools, such as UML (Uni<sup>fi</sup>ed Modeling Language), without rewriting the algorithm of the gap detecting mechanism.

In addition to the six libraries of the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system, this study employed the third party software Lumigent Log Explorer [32] to provide the assistance in extracting the database log. This study requires the use of Lumigent Log Explorer because Microsoft SQL Server does not provide any open protocol to access its database log. The log explorer software can be used to read and export the database log. Protection of the database log is needed to avoid human modi<sup>fi</sup>cation of the database's tracking system. ## 5. Mechanism evaluation

In order to verify the proposed system based on the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism, this study employs a real case to illustrate how the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism works. The case company examined is an ISO (International Organization for Standardization) 9001 quality assured industrial company: K Company. K Company is a trading company and specializes in exporting hand tools, air tools and automotive specialty tools. K Company has 55812 types of items with an operational amount of approximately two million US dollars a year. There are four departments in K Company: sales, stock management, delivery, and <sup>fi</sup>nance. The company has about two thousands customers in China, Hong Kong, Australia and across Europe. It has a good reputation among its customers and has processed many successful orders of Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) from customers. The company uses a good deal of IS and therefore is applicable for our study's purposes.

## 5.1. Case study methodology

The business process gap detecting mechanism in our research is a new <sup>fi</sup>eld within the CAATTs. There is little related literature on this subject and therefore the mechanism lacks signi<sup>fi</sup>cant quantitative indexes with which to compare it. Case study methodology can assist us in solving this problem.

Case study methodology is a common qualitative method used in IS evaluations [4,22]. It is an empirical inquiry that investigates a contemporary phenomenon within its real-life context, especially when the boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clearly evident [55]. The business process gap detecting mechanism tests the control of the IS. The result of it (i.e. business process gap) needs to be explained in terms of “why” and “how” that result was attained. Case study methodology matches this need as it can assist the researcher in analyzing the case and proposing the reasons (why) and the processes (how) of the phenomenon.

This study uses a single-case study type to evaluate the business process gap detecting mechanism, which includes more depth than a multiple-case study [55]. The control failures of the IS are not always accessible. However, once the phenomenon exists in the single case, it can determine the correctness of the business process gap detecting mechanism. It should be noted that every IS has the opportunity to experience control failure. This case can be representative of other companies' IS situations.

## 5.2. Data quality problems of the case company

In order to improve their competitiveness, K Company decided to replace their ERP system. Although the new ERP system was implemented mid-year, K Company uncovered a data quality problem. In fact, some users responded that in the ORD (Order) module, sales orders still contained incomplete data items after these orders were transferred into purchase orders. In this scenario, the user passes the sales order: AST-040601 at Step1. After the sales order converts to a purchase order, the item: KC-A13370 is automatically deleted in the sales order: AST-040601.

K Company does not know why this error occurs, as some sales orders are correct while others are incorrect with no identi<sup>fi</sup>able pattern to explain the phenomenon. The company urgently needs to improve the data quality of their ERP system, and a computer audit project is subsequently announced to examine their IS to determine the reason for the data quality problem. The authors joined the computer audit project as the IT auditor. The project is based on two testing phases:

## 5.3. The substantive test by using the Generalized Audit Software (GAS)

First, this paper extracts the data of the ORD module between 2006/ 01–2006/02 for analysis. The Generalized Audit Software (GAS) – ACL is used to <sup>fi</sup>nd the items lost within sales orders when purchase orders were transferred from sales orders. This paper found that there were 132 lost items distributed on 54 sales orders in this month. The total duration of the test was about one hour.

This study group proposed the substantive result to K Company and met to communicate the result. Four sales department users who were involved in the ORD module attended the meeting, which lasted approximately one hour. This study summarizes some crucial points from the dialogue between IT auditors and users in the following:

1. Every item in the purchase orders should have a mapping item in the sales orders.

2. Account control of the ERP system is satisfactory, as other departments lack permission to modify the sales order. Therefore, the data quality problem should be traced to the sales department.

3. The ERP system does not show any error message about the data quality problem.

4. There is no pattern of the data quality problem.

5. Users did not delete any item of the sales orders during our sample date.

## 5.4. The control test by using the BPGAP-detecting mechanism system

It is easy to <sup>fi</sup>nd data quality problems via the substantive test of Generalized Audit Software (GAS). However, for deeper analysis to know “how” the data quality problem happened, this study utilized the control test of the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system.

First, the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system is used to depict the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow of the ORD module. The ERP vendor documents the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow of the ORD module. The ERP vendor then uses the eEPC diagram to depict their systems, from which the eEPC diagram of the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow can be easily attained. Because the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system also contains the notations of the eEPC, it is easy for us to depict the eEPC diagram in the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system. Second, the work<sup>fl</sup>ow and data<sup>fl</sup>ow models can be mapped to the mathematical model Petri-Net. Third, the Lumigent Log Explorer software is used to assist us in extracting the database log of the ORD module. Since the total log of the ORD module is very large, this paper only extracts INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE commands between 2006/01/01 \~ 2006/01/31 of the log. After exporting the log to the database, the stored procedure of the Resource and Value Dependence Method was performed. The <sup>fi</sup>nal step is to conduct a business process gap analysis. The reachability analysis of Petri-Net will calculate all possible paths according to the database log and test the correctness of the paths. After performing the business process gap analysis, statistical results can be depicted with a pie chart. It is intriguing that 98 (9.4%) IS process <sup>fl</sup>ows are erroneous out of 1048 (100%) total IS process <sup>fl</sup>ows. Four IS process <sup>fl</sup>ows violate the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ows and they are:

1. Pass sales to PO (Purchase Order)→modify sales order. (51)

2. Modify sales order→new stock quantity. (44)

After discussion, this study <sup>fi</sup>nds that the data quality problem is caused by nonstandard actions taken by the end users. 1. The data quality problem is caused by the erroneous information process <sup>fl</sup>ow: pass sales to PO (Purchase Order)→modify sales order. 3. The users and the programmer considered the computer audit project to be extremely useful for the IS, since the control test of the IS can provide them with important information.

## 6. Conclusion

## 6.1. Research contribution

Computer auditing has become increasingly important with the popularization of IS. There are various computer-assisted audit technologies and tools (CAATTs) that can assist the auditor in acquiring knowledge of IS. However, most of them can only perform substantive tests. To construct a general system to perform the entire control test for IS is rather dif<sup>fi</sup>cult since various IS have different data structures and IS process <sup>fl</sup>ows.

The control test of the IS is essential for ensuring that business process gaps do not exist between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. In this research, a business process gap detecting mechanism is provided to detect the business process gap between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The business process gap detecting mechanism transforms the work<sup>fl</sup>ow and data<sup>fl</sup>ow models (eEPC) of internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow into data for the Petri-Net. The automatic mechanism then uses the reachability analysis of the Petri-Net to detect the business process gap. This research constructs the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system in order to implement the business process gap detecting mechanism and evaluate the feasibility of our BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system through a real case. The result indicates the control test of the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism system can uncover the reason for the data quality problem of the case company and provide helpful information to them.

Although the embedded audit module (EAM), one kind of CAATT, had been proposed to monitor business process controls, there are some drawbacks from using this tool [20]. The most signi<sup>fi</sup>cant reason is that EAM must be tightly coupled with enterprise systems [5], and it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to construct a general EAM that can be combined with diverse enterprise systems. Compared with EAM, the proposed mechanism, BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism, is more <sup>fl</sup>exible and usable because it is separate from enterprise systems. The BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism relies on read-only access and the extraction of controls data from enterprise systems. Therefore, the controls data extracted by BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism cannot be manipulated by the <sup>fi</sup>rm personnel before or after extraction. In addition, the code and environment of BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism can be also wellprotected.

This research focused on the capabilities of CAATTs and classi<sup>fi</sup>es them into substantive tests and control tests. This study insists that the substantive test and the control test should complement each other. The substantive test can detect errors of the data. However, to know what causes the errors, this study has shown the need to perform a control test. The automatic mechanism for the control test in this research improves both audit quality and software quality. The authors hope this research can guide a new avenue for academic and practical research about CAATTs.

## 6.2. Managerial significance

The assurance of internal controls and improved business performance demanded by users today are hampered by a contemporary business environment which involves an increasingly copious volume of data and complex business activities. To overcome these problems and achieve their business objectives, more and more <sup>fi</sup>rms have begun to take advantage of technology for conducting internal auditing. The analysis of the business transactions which underlie complex business processes is the key for successful internal auditing. The proposed mechanism, BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism, can analyze business transactions by detecting business process gaps between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. The BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism has a direct managerial implication for <sup>fi</sup>rms and auditors, as <sup>fi</sup>rms can be aided in supporting regulatory compliance and detecting the error of business processes and IS processes, while audit departments also improve audit productivity.

Today more than ever, <sup>fi</sup>rms are being required to comply with more regulations, such as the Sarbanes–Oxley (SOX) Act, Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), and Basel II regulations. The cost and complexity of regulations compliance for <sup>fi</sup>rms have continuously grown. Therefore, <sup>fi</sup>rms are increasingly looking to technology for solutions to effectively transform these burdens of compliance into competitive advantages. The eighth component of the enterprise risk management (ERM) model of the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations (COSO) is monitoring business performance. One of key methods of monitoring performance is “Monitor System Activities”. The BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism can detect business process gaps between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. Consequently, <sup>fi</sup>rms can be assisted in complying with regulations such as SOX, which emphasize the responsibility of management to require organizations to follow their business activities carefully.

Erroneous business processes and IS processes can cause <sup>fi</sup>rms to lose revenue, violate regulations, and damage business operations. These illegal actions violate the internal control before the material misstatements are generated [40], causing a gap between problem incidence and problem recognition. For this reason, business and IS processes should be proactively monitored to uncover any error or abuse. However, the effective check of business processes and IS processes is not easy to perform given increasingly voluminous amounts of data, the complexity and diversity of information systems, and ongoing changes in business processes. System auditing is still inadequate for exposing gaps between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. With the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism, <sup>fi</sup>rms have an effective solution for gap detection and can thereby uncover errors within their business IS processes.

The pressure on audit departments to assist <sup>fi</sup>rms in compliance is increasing because of increased audit responsibilities and a general lack of <sup>fi</sup>rm auditing ability. This is exacerbated by the heavy volume of business data that should be tested and ongoing changed business transactions that should be governed by different regulations in today's business environment. Consequently, audit departments are looking to technology to optimize audit productivity and ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency. The BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism can directly and rapidly uncover the reason for IS problems by detecting gaps between the IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow and the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow. Therefore, the audit departments can audit more productively and ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently by taking advantage of the BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism.

## 6.3. Research limitations and future researches

## 6.3.1. Future research

Since the development of BPGAP-Detecting Mechanism is still in testing, the algorithm of Petri Net may be improvable. Different dynamic models may be found to depict the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow and to create the transformation rules without utilizing the Petri-Net.

## 6.3.2. Research limitations

Due to the dif<sup>fi</sup>culty of extracting the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow, this study only uses the physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow of the database log and extends it to the logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow. However, the extension may have a semantic con<sup>fl</sup>ict problem. For instance, if two functions perform the same actions on the same tables in a database, it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to distinguish which function performs which command. Tracing the altered columns can reduce this con<sup>fl</sup>ict problem. However, the best solution for the problem is to provide a common structure like Business Process Execution Language for Web Services [6] for the work<sup>fl</sup>ow log of IS. With both logical work<sup>fl</sup>ow and physical data<sup>fl</sup>ow capabilities, the mechanism can detect the gap between the internal control <sup>fl</sup>ow and IS process <sup>fl</sup>ow more precisely.

In addition, this study only creates the place notation in Petri-Net of the operations which will affect the data (i.e., INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE). Many DBMS cannot log the SELECT command because one SELECT command may involve many tables. However, the SELECT command is important for the access control issue. DBMS should ideally be able to record the SELECT command. This complete entire database log can improve the quality of the business process gap detecting mechanism.

This study illustrates not only the importance of meta-data such as transaction log for auditing, but also that the some of the DBMS logs are insuf<sup>fi</sup>cient as audit evidence. Thus, we are suggesting that alterative theories of IS and auditing may be needed to address important internal control and audit issues.
