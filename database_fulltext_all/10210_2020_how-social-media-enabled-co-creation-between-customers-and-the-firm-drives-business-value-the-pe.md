# How social-media-enabled co-creation between customers and the firm drives business value? The perspective of organizational learning and social Capital

Hong Zhang<sup>a</sup>, Sumeet Gupta<sup>b</sup>, Wei Sun<sup>a</sup>, Yi Zou<sup>c,⁎</sup>

<sup>a</sup> School of Management, Wuhan University of Science and Technology, Wuhan, 430081, PR China

<sup>b</sup> Indian Institute of Management, Raipur, 493661, India

<sup>c</sup> School of Management and E-Business, Zhejiang Gongshang University, Hangzhou, 310018, PR China

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Co-creation Social media Organizational learning Social capital Business value Organizational performance

## A B S T R A C T

Contemporary business organizations are increasingly turning their attention to value co-creation using social media between individual customers and business organizations in the process of new product development (NPD). However. little is known about the mechanisms underlving social-media-based customer-firm co-creation and their implications for business value in NPD. To address this knowledge gap, this study develops a model from the perspective of organizational learning and social capital to examine how the social-media-based customer-firm co-creation mechanism conceptualized as the structural, cognitive, and relational dimension of social capital influences the first-order knowledge outcome (knowledge transfer efectiveness) and second-order dynamic capability outcome (absorptive capacity), and how these co-creation outcomes ultimately influence organizational performance. The model is tested using survey data from 149 Chinese mobile application developers. The results indicate that social-media-based structural, cognitive, and relational linkage, in particular the structural linkage, is an important co-creation mechanism to improve organizational performance. Knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity have significant mediating efects in this co-creation mechanismoutcomes-performance framework. Further, the moderating efects of social media use level on the relationships between co-creation mechanism and outcomes are largely supported. The study contributes to theory and practice by shedding light on the social-media-based customer-firm co-creation in NPD at a process level.

## 1. Introduction

The process of new product development (NPD) in an enterprise has undergone radical shifts during the past decades [1]. From being confined within an organization, it has now evolved to the alignment between the enterprise and its customers. which is termed as customerfirm co-creation under the rubric of open innovation [2]. Driven by the ubiquitous penetration of social media in customer-enterprise and customer-customer interactions, NPD has gained traction for three main reasons. First, social media support real time, media-rich, and personalized interactions between a company and its customers [3], thus allowing for open innovation, whereby the firm can obtain external knowledge from outside its organization [4]. Using social media, a large number of highly distributed and heterogeneous individuals contribute diverse and commercially attractive ideas for new products. As these ideas are generated by customers themselves, they represent “wisdom of crowds” and are more likely to mirror customer needs, thereby increasing the likelihood of acceptance of new products [5,6]. Second, social media platforms provide design and evaluation toolkits to involve and empower customers during all stages (ideation, product development, commercialization, and postlaunch) of NPD. This facilitates firms to link customer resources and capabilities to improve new product creativity, reduce development costs, and facilitate continuous innovation [7]. Third, social media also incorporate tools that increase virality, whereby customers can eficiently share new product information within their online social networks, and thus facilitate the commercialization of the new product [3].

As social media act as an enabling technology for co-creation [8], a number of high-tech firms have leveraged its potential for involving their customers in the NPD process. For instance, the light manufacturer “Osram” launched an idea competition in the Second Life – a virtual world – and invited users to contribute creative ideas. Similarly, the Chinese mobile phone manufacturer “Xiaomi” encouraged its customers to actively engage in the creation of new products in its brand community by iteratively evaluating and improving the latest virtual prototypes, interactively discussing optimal solution details, testing and experiencing the new product features, and disseminating product marketing information.

An underlying assumption of such strategies is that customer-firm co-creation specific to NPD in social media can produce positive per formance impacts. Although the practices suggest the need for understanding the role of customer co-creation in social media, there have been few studies in this area. The review of prior research on the efects of co-creation (see Table A1 in Appendix A) reveals important insights. First, previous research points to the benefits and risks of customer co creation and reports mixed results. On the one hand, customer cocreation improves business value, such as service innovation performance [9], new product market success [10], market growth [11], and customer satisfaction [12]. On the other hand, customer co-creation leads to higher customer defection [13], reduced producer benefits [14], and poorer brand evaluations [15]. Such results suggest that the overall efect of customer co-creation on the performance of NPD may well be contingent on other mediating or moderating factors. Second, there are only limited empirical studies on social-media-based cus tomer-firm co-creation in NPD. Extant research focuses mainly on al liance between upstream and downstream firms in NPD and customercustomer co-creation practices in social media. Little is known about the implications of customer-firm co-creation in social media for NPD. As social media provide companies with opportunities to harness customers’ innovative potential, which influences innovation performance, it is important to examine how customer-firm co-creation driven by social media influences business value in NPD.

The present study attempts to unravel this specific mechanism, that is, how social-media-based customer co-creation influences the business value (measured in terms of organizational performance) in NPD? Specifically, two important research questions are examined in this study. First, what is the mechanism behind the influence of customer-firm co-creation in social media on a firm’s business value given the contradictory findings from previous studies? Research (e.g., [16,17,2]) reveals that organizations collaborate with their customers to exchange knowledge and construct dynamic capabilities under the premise of gaining strategic competitive advantage and improving organizational perfor mance. Although its importance is acknowledged in research and management practice, a holistic and systematic understanding of the role of customer co-creation in NPD remains elusive. The present study extends Iyengar et al.’s [18] model by embedding social-media-based social linkage as a co-creation mechanism, the first-order knowledge outcome, the second-order dynamic capability outcome, and the business value in a nomological framework from the perspective of organizational learning and social capital and uncovers the process of social media-enabled value co-creation in NPD.

Second, how does social media use moderate the influence of co-creation mechanism on its first-order and second-order outcomes? The literature on value co-creation and information systems (IS) suggests that information technology (IT) is an important contingency factor that influences the value co-creation process [19]. Prior studies (e.g. [7,11,17],) on ITenabled value co-creation report that IT-enabled platforms facilitate firms in executing NPD with customers. Further, the IS studies (e.g. [20,21],) uncover a positive IT-based co-creation cycle, with greater IT embeddedness spawning co-created value. which. in turn. forms the basis for further value creation. While the literature highlights the potential of IT as the contingency factor determining the impacts of value co-creation, few empirical studies link IT to the customer-focused value co-creation process. This study is motivated by calls to understand how IT facilitates customer-firm interactions to create business value in NPD. Regarding the most popular IT- social media, there are no studies that specifically and empirically examine the role of social media use in facilitating customer value co-creation for desired performance goals in NPD. Our study addresses this gap by examining how social media use complements customer-firm social linkage to influence the first- and second-order outcomes of value co-creation in NPD.

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. The theoretical framework

Organizational learning is the core assumption underlying value cocreation between the customers and the firm in NPD in the context of social media. Prior co-creation literature illustrates the learning-based recursive nature of co-creation [4,6,22,23]. Payne et al. [24] proposed a tripartite framework for value co-creation consisting of the customer value-creation processes, the supplier value-creation processes, and the encounter processes. From the perspective of the supplier value-creation processes, value co-creation in NPD is a process of organizational learning through which the organization acquires valuable customer knowledge and translates it into new oferings. Thus, as the organization learns more about its customers, more opportunities become available for it to satisfy customer needs, which thereby leads to better outcomes of co-creation [6]. Prior studies (e.g. [21,20,11,23]) also reveal that IT-enabled co-creation in NPD drives organizational learning. From a broader perspective, IT-enabled value co-creation is an interactive process that takes place in an emergent and dynamic network of multiple sets of entities with diferent resources connected by tech nologies, shared institutional norms, and logics [1]. Thus, IT-enabled value co-creation involves an interactive and cooperative arrangement or configuration of various resources, which enables the acquisition, integration, and dissemination of customer knowledge in NPD. Therefore, we conceptualize social-media-based customer-firm co-creation in NPD as an organizational learning process.

Drawing upon the research on organizational learning, Iyengar et al. [18] developed a model to link organizational learning mechanism to learning outcomes and organizational performance. This learning mechanism-outcomes-performance model (see Fig. 1) serves as the basic framework for our study. The model distinguishes between the outcomes from organizational learning at two levels, namely, the firstorder knowledge exchange outcome and the second-order dynamic capability construction outcome. The first-order outcome – knowledge transfer efectiveness – is concerned with functional competencies and refers to the extent to which a firm acquires useful knowledge for im proving its operational activities [25]. The second-order outcome – absorptive capacity – pertains to dynamic capabilities that ofer strategic insights about how to adapt to changing markets [26]. We conceptualize social-media-based co-creation in NPD as the organizational learning process according to which the social-media-based co-creation mechanism influences the first- and second-order outcomes of cocreation in NPD (knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive ca pacity) and ultimately influences business value.

Based on Iyengar et al. [18], we define knowledge transfer efectiveness in this study as the degree to which a firm acquires useful knowledge from interactions with its customers, and absorptive capacity as a firm’s capability to identify and assimilate valuable knowledge received from its customers and apply it for commercial ends. We focus on the customer perspective and innovation perspective of the organizational performance in NPD, as these two are important elements of customer-firm co-creation. Customer perspective is a key success factor in the competitive scenario and refers to customers’ perception of the firm comprising product leadership, customer satisfaction, and firm image [27]. Innovation perspective reflects the organizational assessment of innovation performance in the NPD process [28]. These two perspectives together are good indicators of long-term profitability and business success.

## 2.2. Social-media-based co-creation mechanism

According to the resource-based view and the relationship view, a firm’s resources that generate competitive advantage through value creation may span firm boundaries and lie in a social network of customer-firm and customer-customer relationships [19,20]. This view is consistent with the value co-creation logic between the firm and its customers. A firm’s social network represents a potential reservoir for the deployment of digitized social capital, which acts as the lubricant to reduce social and economic frictions in customer-firm co-creation [29]. Thus, we contend that social capital built upon interactions between the firm and its customers in social networks, in turn, provides the relationship infrastructure for customer-firm co-creation using social media. Social capital between the customers and the firm in social media forms the skeleton for combing or exchanging knowledge and creating inimitable capabilities to create new sources of business value [19]. Therefore, we focus on the social linkage between a company and its customers for providing a social-capital-based perspective on the co creation mechanism in social media.

As social capital is described or measured by structural, cognitive, and relational dimension, this study conceptualizes the customer-firm co-creation mechanism in social media as the linkages between a firm and its customers along these three dimensions. Given that customerfirm co-creation in this context manifests real-time and media-rich in teractions between the company and its customers in NPD, we focus on the reachability and form, shared understanding/meaning, and collaborative relationships of social-media-based customer-firm interactions for NPD. Structural linkage refers to the pattern of interactions between the customers and the firm in social media; cognitive linkage refers to the extent to which the customers and the firm understand each other’s interpretations of reality through shared codes and language in social media; and relational linkage represents the degree to which the customers and the firm trust each other, respect each other, and work together in social media [30].

## 2.3. Social-media-based co-creation in NPD

From an integrative perspective, IT-enabled value co-creation involves five components that determine business value: knowledge sharing routines, complementary resources, complementary capabilities, efective governance, and new information technologies [19]. The co-creation elements mapped by our study and the representative empirical studies at organizational/interorganizational level are illustrated in Table 1. Co-creation in the context of our study is thus defined as a collaborative NPD process in social media in which a firm and its customers use social media to integrate social resources, exchange knowledge, and build capabilities to achieve improved business value from customer perspective and innovation perspective.

## 3.1. Social-media-based co-creation mechanism and outcomes

Prior co-creation research (e.g., [19,1]) supports the notion that value co-creation enables the transfer of knowledge and development of absorptive capacity. For example, Johannessen and Olsen [35] argued that customer-firm co-creation enables the exchange and integration of knowledge between these two groups, thus enhancing a firm’s dynamic capability. Lusch and Nambisan [1] also asserted that value co-creation supports knowledge sharing, conversion, and application. The social capital literature [36,37] also supports the notion that social capital enables knowledge transfer and is the source of organizational dynamic capabilities. For example, Inkpen and Tsang [38] linked social capital dimensions of a network to the conditions that promote the transfer of knowledge among network members. Tiwana [39] argued that strong ties between a firm and its customers facilitate knowledge integration (aka realized absorptive capacity). The social capital theory suggests that social capital facilitates knowledge exchange between a firm and its customers by providing interaction channels, enhances customerfirm collaboration through a shared vision and strong ties between these two groups, and leads to a fusion of customer knowledge and product markets [40].

Based on the social capital theory, we argue that social capital embedded in the social-media-based interactions has an important role in facilitating knowledge transfer and in improving an organization’s absorptive capacity for various reasons. First, social-media-based structural linkage between a firm and its customers drives the capture, transfer, assimilation, and application of customer knowledge [30]. The rationale behind this is that social-media-based structural linkage facilitates highly interactive customer-firm conversations and enables eficient sharing of diferent ideas, experiences, and insights, thereby leading to improved capacity to leverage this explicit and tacit knowledge to meet customer needs in a better way [37]. Prior literature (e.g. [37,41],) suggests that efective social interactions serve as a mechanism for knowledge transfer as well as an important source of absorptive capacity. Second, social-media-based cognitive linkage in fluences knowledge sharing by enhancing people’s ability to access information about each other [30]. Moreover, cognitive linkage increases the mutual understanding and alignment between the firm and its customers, which are critical for an enterprise to capitalize on its customer resources and construct dynamic capabilities [1l. For ex ample. in the case of “Xiaomi" development community, cognitive linkage is the shared language and codes, which may include how to interpret knowledge about the technologies and oferings. The shared language and codes make it more likely that “Xiaomi” will correctly interpret and apply the knowledge embedded in a customer-firm interaction. Thus, the cognitive linkage may impact each of the three abilities that make up a firm’s absorptive capacity: the ability to identify the potential new product ideas, assimilate new insights, and apply new improvements to the existing offerings [18]. Third, relational linkage ir this study focuses on assets $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ trust and respect) rooted in the customer-firm relationship, which are also critical for knowledge exchange and combination [37]. The trust and respect aspects of relational linkage provide a context conducive for knowledge exchange, interpretation, and integration, thus enhancing the customer-firm coordination capability [41]. Coordination with customers results in organizational learning experiences that enhance “the capability to recognize the value of new knowledge, assimilate it and apply it for commercial purposes” [42]. ## 3.2. Co-creation outcomes and business value

Researchers (e.g., [43,26]) assert that knowledge transfer efec tiveness is an important driver for the development of absorptive capacity. Roberts et al. [26] reviewed the construct of absorptive capacity and noted that a company’s absorptive capacity depends on the transfer of knowledge across the organizational boundaries. In the case of mobile application (app) development, the efective transfer of customer knowledge from users to the developer increases the knowledge overlap between them and thus enhances the developer’s ability to generate associations between new knowledge and its existing knowledge base. These associations assist the developer in absorbing new knowledge and thus increase its ability to identify the potential of new knowledge and apply it [43]. Therefore, we argue that knowledge transfer efectiveness increases absorptive capacity.

H3. Knowledge transfer efectiveness positively influences absorptive capacity.

A number of studies support the positive association between absorptive capacity and organizational performance. For example, Roberts et al. [26] proposed that a firm’s performance is a direct consequence of its absorptive capacity. Iyengar et al. [18] showed that a firm’s absorptive capacity significantly influences its financial growth. Within the NPD context, firms with greater levels of absorptive capacity are able to continually integrate new knowledge with their existing knowledge stocks, replenish them, and leverage them to respond to potential customer needs [18]. Therefore, firms with high absorptive capacity are more eficient in linking the new knowledge to their own product markets and thus enhance the market value and the innovation performance in their NPD process. In other words, firms with greater absorptive capacity can excellently exploit the potential of the new knowledge. For an industry with intense competition, high innovation demand, and urgent time-to-market pressures, absorptive capacity is essential for success [26]. Hence, we hypothesize

## 3.3. Co-creation outcomes as mediators

## 3.3.1. Knowledge transfer efectiveness as mediator

The dynamic nature of absorptive capacity means that it depends not only on prior related knowledge but also more on the external knowledge [17]. This supports the theoretically sound assertion that the efective transfer of customer knowledge across organizational boundaries determines a firm’s absorptive capacity in a customer-fo cused market [18]. Without the efective sharing of customer knowledge, a firm endowed with social linkage will not be able to strengthen, complement, or refocus its knowledge base to exploit NPD opportunities. In addition, the knowledge-based view also highlights the role of new knowledge in creating a firm’s absorptive capacity [17]. Hence, we hypothesize

H5. Knowledge transfer efectiveness mediates the relationships between social capital and absorptive capacity.

H5a. Knowledge transfer efectiveness mediates the relationship between structural linkage and absorptive capacity.

H5b. : Knowledge transfer efectiveness mediates the relationship between cognitive linkage and absorptive capacity.

H5c. Knowledge transfer efectiveness mediates the relationship between relational linkage and absorptive capacity.

## 3.3.2. Absorptive capacity as mediator

Based on the literature on absorptive capacity (e.g., [18,26]) and value co-creation (e.g. [19,17],), we stipulate that absorptive capacity is a key mediating factor between social-media-based social linkage and organizational performance in the new product co-development. First, because of its dominant power as a key organizational dynamic cap ability. absorptive capacity is considered to be more imperative to a firm’s long-term survival and business success as compared to the static resources [26]. Although social linkage serves as a mechanism for gathering information from customers for the NPD, how well this information is leveraged depends on a firm’s absorptive capacity to imbibe new insights and use them into its product development [2]. In other words, it is the absorptive capacity that increases a firm’s ability to take advantage of emerging market and innovation opportunities and subsequently create new business value. Second, social-media-based linkage creates fertile ground for the development of absorptive capacity, and thus increases business value [4]. In the NPD process, a firm’s ability to efectively use social media to support social linkage between a firm and its customers facilitates high-quality information sharing and enables the firm to process information obtained from its customers, thus resulting in improved customer-enabled market knowledge creation [26]. This enhances a firm’s absorptive capacity and ultimately impacts its competitive advantage in NPD [25]. As hypothesized in the previous sections, co-creation mechanism, namely, structural, cognitive, and relational linkage between the firm and its customers will positively influence absorptive capacity of the firm and further impact its performance. Hence, we hypothesize

H6. Absorptive capacity mediates the relationships between social capital and organizational performance.

H6a. Absorptive capacity mediates the relationship between structural linkage and organizational performance.

H6b. Absorptive capacity mediates the relationship between cognitive linkage and organizational performance.

H6c. Absorptive capacity mediates the relationship between relational linkage and organizational performance.

Prior literature consistently indicates that the impact of efective knowledge transfer on a firm’s performance depends on the firm’s absorptive capacity, namely, its capability to transform and apply this knowledge to its own environment [18]. Because of the heterogeneity in organizational environments, the mere transfer of valuable knowledge may not have a direct influence on an organization’s performance. A firm requires a high level of absorptive capacity to bridge the gap between useful knowledge and its commercial exploitation relevant to its own strategic context. Hence, we hypothesize

H7. Absorptive capacity mediates the relationship between knowledge transfer efectiveness and organizational performance.

## 3.4. Social media use level as moderator

In our study, social media use level refers to a firm’ employment of emergent social media applications (e.g., Facebook, WeChat, Twitter, etc.) to engage in a collaborative NPD with its customers [44]. According to the theory of complementarities, the role of value co-creation in NPD can be complemented by IT [19]. Drawing on this theory, we argue that social media use in conjunction with social linkage will forge organizational coordination capabilities and socialization cap abilities, which create the necessary condition for knowledge exchange and integration [26].

We stipulate that the role of social capital in knowledge transfe efectiveness can be complemented by high social media use. First, social media technologies uphold the active customer-firm interactions which create a flux of knowledge and serve as the most important source of market knowledge [3]. In addition, the firm should also put the social linkage mechanism in place to facilitate the flow of valuable knowledge into the firm in NPD [26]. Thus, high social media use combined with social capital magnifies opportunities for knowledge availability, sharing, assimilation, and creation in NPD. Second, a firm with high social media use will develop a close relationship type of exchange with its customers. This will facilitate efective knowledge exchange between the firm and its customers for NPD [11]. Third, social media use can improve the accuracy of members’ meta-knowledge (knowledge of “who knows what” and “who knows whom”) in NPD [45]. Use of social media technologies for supporting customer-firm interactions makes communications (i.e., message content and network connections) among a specific discussion group visible across the cooperative networks. This social-media-enabled ambient awareness allows the rapid and accurate identification of experts who possess knowledge related to NPD [17]. Hence, we hypothesize

H8. Social media use level positively moderates the relationships between social capital and knowledge transfer efectiveness.

H8a. Social media use level positively moderates the relationship between structural linkage and knowledge transfer efectiveness.

H8b. Social media use level positively moderates the relationship between cognitive linkage and knowledge transfer efectiveness.

H8c. Social media use level positively moderates the relationship between relational linkage and knowledge transfer efectiveness.

We also posit that the level of social media use positively moderates the relationship between social capital and absorptive capacity for two reasons. First, social media use can yield vast amounts of customer input. A combination of social media use and social capital helps a firm understand and analyze this customer-sourced information and thus derives novel and actionable insights from large volumes of data [2].

In summary, we argue that the complementarity of social capital and social media use enhances a firm’s ability to discover and imbibe new knowledge, which, in turn, improves a firm’s absorptive capacity. ## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Contextual setting

The mobile application industry was selected as the setting for our study for three reasons. First, the market is highly knowledge-intensive, competitive, and customer-oriented. The majority of mobile apps fail or are not as successful as expected, and only those app developers who meet customer requirements sustain the test of time [7]. Given that the developers need to learn to update their products in response to changing customer requirements, customer-firm co-creation, which enables organizational learning, is vital for their success. Second, the development process of mobile apps is characterized by high reliance on customer knowledge and high levels of interactions with customers in social media. Customers (app users) and firms (app developers) frequently interact with each other in social media, and many developers incorporate customers’ opinions or suggestions into their new products [16]. Third, the developers in mobile app markets have limited channels for selling their products and the terms of access to marketplace are uniformly determined for all developers. Thus, competing developers can be compared easily with each other [46]. Given the popularity and openness of Android open source platform, where large numbers of heterogeneous developers contribute to rich and diverse apps through the use of the system, we selected it as the context for this study.

## 4.2. Instrument

Semi-structured interviews were conducted with firm managers to determine the operational definitions of constructs. We operationalized the independent and mediating variables as reflective constructs. Organizational performance was modeled as a formative construct with two formative dimensions (customer perspective and innovation perspective). In conformation with previous research [44,17], the moderator – social media use level – was measured using single item (“Please indicate the extent to which the firm utilizes social media to interact with its customers in the mobile app development activities”). Firms responded on a 1–7 scale (1 = few usage; 7 = high usage). As our primary focus is on the impact of customer-firm co-creation on organizational performance, we included several control variables in this research model to rule out alternative explanations of organizational performance. Given the salience of firm size and revenues in determining organizational performance [27], we modeled them as control variables that directly afect organizational performance. An organization’s performance in mobile app development depends on its software development background and strategic orientation of mobile app business; thus, we specified firm type as the control variable for ruling out rival explanations in our study. In addition, as more experienced and powerful companies may be better at developing innovative products/services and meeting customer needs, we controlled for mobile app development spending and mobile app development experience. ## 4.3. Data collection

The study emploved a third-party market research firm to collect data between October 2017 and December 2017 from the mobile application industry. According to our specifications, the research firm used a purposive random sampling method to identify subjects for the survey (i.e., a random sample of subjects are chosen from a larger group following a particular criterion) and selected 500 companies (mobile app developers that have used social media to interact with their customers in mobile app development) as a sample from its company base. As senior managers are believed to have rich information and reasonable perspectives about organizational operations and performance, this study targeted product development managers. The market research firm contacted the product development managers by e-mai with a survey hyperlink requesting their participation in our study and followed them up through telephone calls, if required. A total of 166 responses were received (a response rate of 33.2%) over a three-month period. Next, the data were scrutinized to rule out the possible response biases, i.e., social desirability, leniency, and acquiescence biases. Of 166 responses, 2 respondents did not complete the survey, another 12 respondents did not pass the “cognitive dissonance” check, and there were 3 responses where almost all questions were uniformly answered. After removing these 17 incomplete/invalid responses, 149 responses were used to validate the proposed research model. The procedural approaches to control for the response biases and the characteristics of the final sample are summarized in Tables B2 and B3 in Appendix B.

Nonresponse bias was evaluated by comparing the early (those who replied during the first month) and late respondents (those who replied during the last month). T-tests performed on the two samples indicated that the early and late responses did not difer significantly.

## 5. Data analyses and results

Following the two-step approach [48], we first estimated the measurement model to validate the instrument. Then we assessed the structural model and tested our research hypotheses. The data were analyzed by partial least squares using PLS-Graph 3.0, a componentbased structural equation modeling (SEM) technique. Two reasons drove this choice: (1) PLS is well suited to handle formative constructs [49] and (2) our sample was relatively small, and therefore, the PLS technique, which is less restrictive on the sample size and sample data distribution, was a better choice than covariance-based SEM techniques (i.e., AMOS and LISREL).

We did not find support for the positive relationships between cognitive linkage, relational linkage, and absorptive capacity. A possible reason may be that both cognitive linkage and relational linkage between a firm and its custo mers are not suficient to ensure the construction of a firm’s dynamic absorptive capability. An organization’s “absorptive capacity depends on prior related knowledge and the absorptive capacities of its individual members” [26]. Increased inter- and intra-organizational knowledge transfer in a specific area enhances an organization’s market knowledge base in that area, which further increases its absorptive capacity and, in turn, expedites more knowledge transfer in that domain. Moreover, accumulating absorptive capacity in one period will encourage an organization’s more eficient accumulation in the next. This implies that the development of absorptive capacity is firm-specific, domain-specific, and path-dependent [26] and cannot therefore be quickly integrated into a firm. While cognitive and relational linkage enables the associative connections with new market insights and knowledge, the organizational assimilation and application of new knowledge depend more on the transfer of knowledge from customers to the firm [42]. Another plausible explanation is that cognitive linkage and relational linkage do not have any direct influence on absorptive capacity. Interestingly, when knowledge transfer efectiveness was removed from the model, cognitive linkage and relational linkage became positively associated with absorptive capacity $( \beta = 0 . 1 3 9 , \mathrm { ~ p ~ < ~ } 0 . 0 5 ;$ β = 0.102, p < 0.05). The findings suggest that cognitive and relational linkage between a firm and its customers influence a firm’s absorptive capacity by increasing the transfer efectiveness of customer knowledge.

Further the moderating effects of social media use level on the relationships between relational linkage and co-creation outcomes (knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity) were found to be insignificant. One plausible explanation is that social media in this context mainly serve as a conduit for information sharing between the firm and its customers [3] and is therefore unable to complement relational linkage to facilitate efective knowledge transfer and absorption in NPD, which demand technical-market dialogue, close observation, frequent feedback, and experimentation. Our interviews also suggest that social media by themselves may not be enough for supporting these activities and that portfolios of Code Android Group based on the Android platform and social media may be better for complementing customer-firm relational linkage to support the knowledge process.

## 6.2. Theoretical implications

This study offers a few important theoretical contributions. First. in response to the call for studies that provide a holistic and systematic understanding of the role of co-creation [14], the study uses Iyengar et al.’s [18] learning mechanism-outcomes-performance framework to examine the role of social-media-driven customer-firm co-creation in NPD. The findings are in conformation with Iyengar et al.’s [18] framework, whereby the learning mechanism (social-media-based cocreation) has a positive influence on learning outcomes (knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity) and absorptive capacity, in turn, influences a firm’s performance. This study thus sheds some light on the crucial issue of relationship between customer-firm cocreation and the business value in NPD, which is not clear due to mixed results from previous studies [9,10]. In this study, we find that socialmedia-based customer-firm co-creation has an indirect efect on a firm’s performance through its knowledge and dynamic capabilities development. Our adaptation and enrichment of the learning mechanismoutcomes-performance model ofer a fresh vantage point from which to study the value co-creation process dynamics.

Second, this study provides insights into the social-media-based customer-firm co-creation mechanism by conceptualizing it as the three dimensions of social capital, namely, structural linkage, cognitive linkage, and relational linkage. This study reveals that these three dimensions are enablers of knowledge transfer efectiveness, which along with structural linkage influences absorptive capacity, and which eventually influences a firm’s performance. The use of social capital lens to understand the entire nomology from social-media-based social linkage to knowledge process to organizational performance is a novel attempt, although there have been studies (e.g., [27,41,57]) that have examined the interrelationship between value co-creation and social capital. Our study helps understand the social linkages underlying social-media-based customer-firm co-creation and their implications on organizational learning, thus contributing to a richer appreciation of social values of the customer-firm co-creation.

Third, this study adds to IT-enabled value co-creation research. We explicate the moderating role of social media use at a finer granularity by uncovering its interaction efects with specific structural, cognitive, and relational linkage dimensions on key co-creation outcomes (knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity). The addition of moderating role of social media use helps explain the mixed findings of previous studies on the impacts of customer co-creation. We also extend IT-enabled value co-creation research in another way. We frame social-media-based co-creation in NPD as a virtuous combination of five critical co-creation components: new information technology, social resource linkage, knowledge exchange, dynamic capability construction, and business value. This is particularly valuable, as the research in this emerging context is relatively sparse [7]. This study answers calls for a micro-level focus in exploring technological dynamics within the interdependencies among a set of co-creation elements [19] and enhances the theoretical understanding of how IT value emanates in customer co-creation.

Finally, through the inclusion of the first-order and second-order outcomes of organizational learning, this study contributes to our understanding of how dynamic capabilities are nurtured through customer-firm co-creation that serves to promote learning within organizations. The study links social-media-based customer-firm cocreation to knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity and establishes their relationships. The findings indicate that social media-based customer-firm co-creation has a direct and indirect influ ence on a firm’s absorptive capacity. The results support arguments that customer-firm co-creation has an important role in the transfer of knowledge and building of absorptive capacity [35,1], and reiterate the salience of social-media-based customer-firm co-creation in under pinning the creation and renewal of dynamic capabilities [41].

## 6.3. Practical implications

The study findings also reveal a few interesting managerial im plications. A critical result of this study is that the structural, cognitive, and relational linkage between customers and the firm in social media are the enablers of knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity, which eventually create business value. The findings inform practitioners that customer-firm co-creation strategies as the customer based sense-and-respond strategies have the potential to improve organizational business value, especially the customer-oriented perfor mance. Therefore, the ultimate goal of increased business value can be achieved by well-designed implementation of the three types of lin kages. First, it suggests that product managers should set up formal and informal interaction channels in social media to create new social connections and enable intertwining between customers and the firm in diferent ways. For example, Procter & Gamble and IBM often hold face to-face meetings or seminars and invite top customers to share their knowledge and experiences with products and services. Microsoft’s Channel 9 provides an interaction means to permit rich discussions or conversations between customers and employees [1]. Many businesses are now building a community inside social messaging apps such a WhatsApp and WeChat to interact with their customers, where they can share discussions, pictures, and videos on products and services. Second, to facilitate the cognitive linkage between customers and the product development teams, the company should develop a shared language and code, such as a common set of business and cultural as sumptions, vocabulary and descriptions, so as to assist them in learning from each other and improve their shared understanding about the team, products, technology, and requirements [57]. Third, product managers may develop direct co-development processes and integrate these processes into the customer relationship management framework for building a better customer-firm relationship. Furthermore, colla boration mechanisms such as contracts and regulations should be cre ated to reduce the management risks and customer concerns in the codevelopment processes. The company may also designate team members called “buddies” who have strong ties with customers to serve as bridge between customers and the firm to increase customer trust worthiness in the firm.

Another important finding of this study is that the business value of co-creation can be derived from the synergistic efect of an organization’s knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity. Given the growing interest in customer-firm co-creation [58] and dynamic absorptive capacity [26], this is a key contribution of this study to practice. Several studies have emphasized the prominence of service platform in helping an organization liquefy knowledge and enhance knowledge density through eficient and efective knowledge exchange [1]. Given the potential of social media to unleash generativity and open up novel opportunities for knowledge integration, managers may leverage social media to build a co-creation platform to enhance the recursive relationship between knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity. Specifically, enterprises may design interlinked processes and activities to reach a strong customer range and thus enable high-quality information sharing between a diverse set of customers and the NPD team. Based on this platform, they may also deploy infrastructure (e.g., digital technology, resources, decentralized network structure, value propositions, rules/institutional norms, and common codes) to allow the team to process information obtained from its customers and engage in innovation activities. For example, content rating systems may be designed to gauge the novelty and accuracy of knowledge embedded in the customer-firm interactions. Dell developed a platform – Dell IdeaStorm – to allow its customers to submit, discuss, promote, and demote ideas related to Dell’s products/services, where Dell responds by providing feedback, updates, and actions to these ideas [59]. The innovative capability of an organization is a function of the richness or diversity of its knowledge structures, i.e., the breadth of knowledge [42]. Thus, this co-creation platform serves as a venue to facilitate organizational knowledge sharing to build diverse knowledge structures and further increase the organizational ability to acquire, assimilate, transform, and exploit market knowledge through innovation and competitive actions. This further results in a synergistic efect of knowledge transfer efectiveness and absorptive capacity on im proved business value creation.

## 6.4. Limitations and future research

The results of this study should be interpreted in the light of its limitations. First. this study adopts the most commonly used three-di: mensional conceptualization of social capital. that is. structural. relational, and cognitive dimension. Although there are many ways to operationalize these three dimensions, we adapt from Wagner et al. [37] to conceptualize and measure them from the single aspect. For example, structural capital that reflects the overall pattern of interactions may be a function of communication intensity [60], social network ties, network decentralization [36], institutionalized interaction mechanisms [61]. and network stability [38]. However, our study focuses on the interaction mechanisms and defines structural linkage as the mechanisms of interactions between customers and the firm towards the NPD using social media. Such operationalization may limit the richness of the research findings. Future research may use the qualitative method to gain deeper insights into the customer-firm co-creation mechanism and thus identify more comprehensive measures of these three dimensions to portray a complete picture of customer-firm co-creation from the social capital perspective. Furthermore, because our conceptualization of customer-firm co-creation is limited to an organizational learning process, future research can provide novel perspectives on the customer-firm co-creation process. Second, given that we use cross-sectional data to evaluate the research model, it would be useful to conduct longitudinal research to examine customer-firm co-creation from a dynamic perspective [62]. Such research could provide valuable insights into the dynamic co-creation environments and the lagged influence of customer-firm co-creation on its outcomes and organizational performance over time. In addition, for increased rigor, future studies may adopt a design of mixed-methods and multi-source data, such as the independent. dependent. and control variables may be from different sources and the performance measure may be objective [2]. Finally, given that the performance implications of customer-firm cocreation at diferent stages of the NPD process may be diferent [2], future studies may examine the precise role of customer co-creation at every stage or how the timing of customer co-creation in NPD influences its role in organizational performance.
