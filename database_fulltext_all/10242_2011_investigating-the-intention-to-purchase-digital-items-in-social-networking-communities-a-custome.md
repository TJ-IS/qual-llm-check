## A B S T R A C T

Some social networking community service providers have earned revenue by selling digital items to their community members. We examined SNC member decisions to purchase digital items based on customer value theory. Six factors were extracted from three dimensions of customer value: functional, social, and emotional value. Our findings indicated that the effects of value on member purchase intentions were significant in terms of the emotional and social dimensions. Our results should help SNC providers by improving their sales of digital items.

## 1. Introduction

The majority of social networking community (SNC) providers have used advertising as a source of revenue. However a number of researchers and practitioners are trying to find ways to maximize SNC’s revenue generation potential [10]. One alternative source is the sale of digital items to SNC members. These are new media elements used by members for representation, expression, and communication in online environments (e.g., online avatars, avatar accessories, decorative ornaments such as furniture, digital wallpapers, skins, background music, and virtual weapons used in Internet games) [12]. SNC members employ these digital items to decorate their web-pages, and communicate their identities to other members.

The sale of digital items has become increasingly widespread in online SNC, a distinctive form of SNC whose fundamental purpose is to help users connect with their friends, business partners, and other individuals. A representative case of a profitable SNC that sells digital items as its core business is Cyworld (http:// www.cyworld.com/pims/mhsection/mh\_index.asp), whose estimated daily revenue from digital item sales is about US\$0.3 million, and its annual sales are approximately US\$120 million. Following Cyworld, Internet firms such as Habbo (www.habbo.- com). There.com (www.there.com), and Livejournal (www.livejournal.com) have also started to sell digital items to their members.

These SNCs have become increasingly popular and have evolved into massive networked groups with large member bases. Considering the network externalities embedded in the social system, the purchasing powers of these network groups could be substantially augmented. Therefore, a clear motive exists for marketers and practitioners to better understand the digital item purchase and usage behaviors of SNC members, so that the economic potential can be increased.

The literature relevant to purchase behavior reveals a lack of understanding of member motivation to purchase digital items from SNCs. Even though SNCs have clearly provided an innovative and profitable business model centered on the sale of digital items, the reasons that members purchase and use digital items remain unclear.

The principal objective of our study was therefore to examine SNC member digital item purchase behavior from a marketing perspective; we based our approach on customer value theory. The literature identifies customer value as a crucial predictor of consumer purchasing behavior. A number of studies [e.g., 1,21,23,27] have also shown that customer value is an important predictor of customer purchase decisions. Customer value theory is, therefore, a good lens to use in investigating consumer intention to purchase digital items.

## 2. Conceptual background

## 2.1. Digital items

Advertising is a principal source of revenue for a large number of SNCs (e.g., Facebook and MySpace). In search of an alternative revenue source, some SNCs have adopted innovative methods of generating revenue. Some SNCs have started generating revenue by selling digital items to their community members [8]. These ‘‘digital items’’ are new media elements utilized by members for representation, expression, and communication in online environments. There are two types of digital items, graphical digital items and musical digital items (e.g., songs and music). In this study, the focus was placed specifically on graphical digital items and on the consideration of their aesthetic value. A few common examples of graphical digital items include digital avatars, avatar accessories (e.g., clothes, shoes, and hats), decorative items for virtual rooms (e.g., furniture, art paintings, and wallpapers), and background skins. The prices for digital items typically range from a few cents to a few dollars.

Digital items can be used to represent their users. Users typically participate in a virtual world by controlling their avatar, a character that represents them in the virtual context [11]. Avatars are typically two-dimensional animated figures that represent one’s online self [26]. They have long been employed in a variety of fields on the Internet, including games, chat rooms, and live forums for online conversation [4]. Avatar technology has evolved from multiplayer online role-playing games (MMORPGs) and is being gradually integrated into a host of other social interaction platforms. For example, a few years ago, MSN introduced new avatar technology into Microsoft Passport in an effort to monetize its social messaging services by allowing avatars to be downloaded onto mobile handsets [16].

Literature concerning self-identity [27] reveals that an avatar can be viewed as a unique way of representing one’s self-identity and desire for self-disclosure. In text-based communications, users can express their identity using their ID, nickname, and profile. Avatars add a visual dimension in a SNC. With a digital identity, members can control the degree to which they disclose their physical identity to other members. Furthermore, this fascination may encourage SNC members, who are accustomed to free services, to be willing to pay for avatars.

## 2.2. Customer value theory

Marketing and e-commerce literature considers ‘‘customer value’’ to be an important predictor of customer buying behavior (purchase decision) [e.g., 1,6,9,21,27]. Consumer choice is a function of multiple value dimensions that make different contributions in different choice situations. These dimensions are comprehensive, encompass a variety of fields, and form a solid foundation for the extension of existing value constructs. Therefore, they were employed as a basis in other studies [e.g., 18,23].

The value frameworks discussed by Sweeney et al. incorporated three pertinent dimensions of customer consumption value: functional, emotional, and social. In these studies, functional, emotional, and social values were shown to exert significant influence on consumer behavior in their specific contexts. Previous research [e.g., 13] has also incorporated the functional and social aspects of a SNC into our understanding of SNC success (in terms of SNC usage instead of commercial transactions).

Fig. 1 shows our value framework based on work by Sheth et al. Functional value is the perceived utility of a digital item based on the item’s capacity for functional, utilitarian, or physical performance. A product’s functional value may be derived from its characteristics or attributes, including reliability, durability, and price, and is measured through a profile of choice attributes. Traditionally, functional value is presumed to be the principal driver of consumer choice. This assumption is predicated on Stigler’s economic utility theory and is popularly expressed in terms of the rational economic man. It can be measured in either monetary or functional terms. Mathwick et al. [15] explained this via the key roles of service excellence (functional quality) and customer ROI (price utility) in customer decision making. We used two factors: price utility and functional quality.

Fig. 1. Value framework.

Emotional value (hedonic or experiential value) is the perceived utility of a digital item based on the item’s capacity to arouse feelings or affective states. The emotional value of a product is derived from specific feelings that a customer associates with it. Hedonic consumption involves multi-sensory, fantasy, and emotive aspects of an individual’s experience with the products. The emotional value of a digital item depends principally on how it looks and the extent to which SNC members relate to it and employ it in their communication with other SNC members [7]. We identified aesthetics and playfulness as the two factors that defined emotional value.

Social value is the perceived utility of a digital item based on the item’s ability to enhance one’s social well-being. The consumption of a digital item represents a social act in which symbolic meanings, social codes, relationships, consumer identity, and ego may be produced and reproduced. The motives for buying and consuming products depend on the manner in which a customer views him- or herself or wishes to be viewed by others. Apart from their functional utility, products have symbolic or conspicuous consumption values. Digital items can help to enhance one’s self-image among SNC members. The types of digital items one utilizes also help to define the adopter’s social circle. For social value, we therefore identified two factors: self-image expression and relationship support.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

The research model based on the value framework; is shown in Fig. 2. Literature commonly associates quality and price with the functional dimension of customer value. Price utility is an aspect of functional value that contributes positively to total customer value. The price utility of a digital item is derived from the perceived efficient use of money. According to transaction utility theory, the more favorable is the price of a product to customers, the greater will be its perceived price utility. This is because customers perceive the more efficient use of their money as a valuable transaction: the greater the price utility to the customer, the higher the customer’s purchase intention.

H1. Price utility has a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

Digital items, which are principally multimedia elements, can encompass a number of abstract dimensions of quality. However, functional quality (an aspect of functional value) is the perceived overall excellence and expected performance of a digital item, which can take on different qualities across SNCs’; for example, the quality of a musical digital item involves its sound quality. The quality of a graphical digital item may include its features (e.g., motion of digital avatars). This differs from the quality of a tangible product, which is generally measured in terms of its performance. A good quality software product should, of course, be user-friendly, free of bugs, and \ backward- and forward-compatible. Similarly,a digitalitem should be user-friendly, compatible with the environment, and must not crash the system. Therefore, digital items with higher levels of perceived functional quality may result in greater perceived customer value, thus strengthening purchase intention.

H2. Functional quality has a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

Aesthetics involve the perceived visual appeal of a digital item [22]. They should attract the interest of potential buyers. In the offline context, aesthetics are a key component of product design. Many customers consider the visual appeal and aesthetics of a product not only for hedonic products, but also for functional products. A key component of hedonic shopping, the aesthetics of a digital item can therefore affect the intention to purchase a digital item in an SNC. Aesthetics are a key aspect of digital items, because they enhance the visual appeal of an SNC member’s website.

H3. Aesthetics have a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

Playfulness is the perceived intrinsic enjoyment, interest, fun, or curiosity generated from engaging in absorbing interactions with a digital item [17]. Prior research [19] has shown that attitudinal outcomes including positive affect, pleasure, and satisfaction, result from a playful experience. Huang et al. argued that the use of emoticons (symbolic representations of one’s expressions) made communication richer and more enjoyable. The consumption of goods and services is frequently associated with emotional response, contributing to an enhanced emotional value for the consumer. Perceived playful responses of fun and fantasy evoked via the consumption of digital items can be regarded as a kind of emotional value for the consumer. Therefore, intrinsic value brought about by a perceived playfulness may trigger SNC members’ purchase intentions.

H4. Playfulness has a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

deValck et al. [5] argued that customers use SNCs as social and information networks that affects their decision-making. Social self-image expression is an aspect of social value and is the perceived capability of a digital item to enhance one’s image in the eyes of others. People consume products to enhance their social image because the purchase, display, and use of goods can communicate one’s symbolic meaning to others. Therefore, products are consumed for their symbolic social meanings. Digital items can help to enhance the representation and articulation of SNC members’ online self-images, making it likely that SNC members will purchase and use digital items as symbolic goods to express and enhance their image.

H5. Social self-image expression has a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

Social relationship support is another aspect of social value that refers to a digital item’s perceived capability to help form, maintain, and enhance interpersonal relationships. SNCs are places in which individuals can find and provide emotional support, companionship, and encouragement. Walker [25] showed that SNC members create home pages in order to maintain pre-existing relationships. By embellishing them with attractive digital items such as music, SNC members expect to attract more visitors (such as friends) with whom they could potentially establish relationships Therefore, digital items appear to be useful for building and maintaining social relationships and the social value of digital items affects SNC member motivation to purchase digital items.

H6. Social relationship support has a positive effect on the intention to purchase digital items.

An individual presents his or her identity and image to others in order to establish a relationship [3]. People make use of proximal objects (e.g., physical possession) and other effects (e.g., bodily enactments and aural effects) to present their image [20]. The ability of a digital item to enhance one’s self-image in the eyes of others will be affected by the item’s functional quality (sound quality of a musical digital item, functional quality of a graphical digital item, etc.) and emotional aesthetics (possession of pleasing graphics, etc.). Furthermore, people experience elation when they possess unique items or items with high functional quality or aesthetics than others. Similarly, people tend to decorate their houses with antiques to project a desired image in the minds of others. Also, functional quality and aesthetics will affect a digital item’s ability to enhance one’s image.

H7. Functional quality has a positive effect on social self-image expression.

H8. Aesthetics have a positive effect on social self-image expression.

## 4. Research methodology

We adopted an online survey method for data collection.

## 4.1. Research context

Data were collected from Cyworld, which began its business in Korea in the early 2000s and received the Wharton Infosys Business

Table 2

Transformation Award for its society-wide transformation of interpersonal interaction. Although it is similar to social networking sites, Cyworld provides social networking with some additions, making networking interaction more realistic and alluring.

In the Cyworld context, users possess their own virtual assets, which they can customize according to their needs. For instance, users maintain digital avatars that can be supplemented with accessories such as clothes, hats, and shoes. Additionally, they have mini-rooms that can be decorated with digital furniture, art pieces, electronics, wallpaper, and music. Instant messaging is included in the service, allowing users to chat with visitors. One may access Cyworld from a mobile phone. In addition to graphical digital items, Cyworld had sold more than 160 million songs as by September 2006, becoming the second-largest music store in the world after Apple’s iTunes. At one point in 2006, Cyworld generated more video traffic than YouTube. In 2008, it sold digital items worth an average of US\$7 million per month.

To purchase digital items, users must first purchase digital Cyworld currency termed an Acorn (where one acorn costs 10 cents US). Prices for digital items vary from about 1 Acorn for a graphical icon to 15 Acorns for wallpaper.

## 4.2. Instrument development

We adopted existing validated scales wherever possible but adapted the scales to reflect the context of our study. As our study related to both IS and marketing, two IS and one marketing researcher reviewed the survey instrument for validity. The instrument was also reviewed by a focus group of five Cyworld members to detect any ambiguities in wording and format. The questionnaire was originally designed in English and then translated into Korean by a bilingual interpreter. It was then back translated from the Korean into English by another bilingual interpreter. There were few discrepancies, and all were resolved before disseminating the questionnaire. All items were measured on a seven-point Likert scale (from 1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree). The survey instrument is shown in Appendix A, along with the sources for the questions.

## 4.3. Data collection

Five hundred Cyworld members were selected randomly, and a survey invitation message containing the URL of the survey website was placed in their mini-rooms. The owners and visitors of these 500 mini-rooms could read the invitation message and participate in the survey. The survey was conducted over a period of one week, and each respondent received US\$5-worth of digital currency as an incentive to participate in the online survey.

The final sample consisted of 225 completed and usable responses (Table 1). Female respondents made up 60.9% of the sample. The majority of the respondents were young, between 20 and 29 years of age (68.8%), with a mean age of 23.1 years. Approximately 72% of the respondents were students: 85.3% of them had at least seven years of Internet experience and 96.4% had been using Cyworld for at least one year.

## 5.2. Hypotheses testing

We assessed the structural model using LISREL. The following indices and standards were applied to assess a model fit: normed $\chi ^ { 2 }$ less than 3.0, RMSEA less than 0.08, RMR less than 0.05, GFI, CFI, NFI greater than 0.90, and AGFI greater than 0.80.

The structural model had good fit indices, with the exception of GFI, which was 0.84 and thus below the recommended threshold, but AGFI was 0.80, which just satisfied the cut-off value. These results indicated that the structural model adequately fit the data. The standardized path coefficients could therefore be employed for hypothesis testing.

The results (see Fig. 3) showed that aesthetics (H3), playfulness (H4), and social self-image expression (H5) significantly affected the intention to purchase digital items, while explaining 40 percent of the variance. However, we were unable to detect significant effects of price utility (H1), functional quality (H2), and social relationship support (H6) on purchase intentions. Also, functional quality (H7) and aesthetics (H8) exerted significant effect on social self-image expression, explaining 30 percent of its variance.

## 6. Discussion and implications

## 6.1. Discussion of findings

Our major findings were that the emotional and social values of digital items were key dimensions of customer value. First, the two aspects of emotional value, aesthetics and playfulness, significantly influence the intention to purchase digital items. Huang et al. determined that the use of emoticons increase the enjoyment inherent to online communication. Individuals may also experience visual appeal from digital items, thus intending to purchase them.

The social self-image expression of a digital item significantly influences member purchase intentions. From the perspective of symbolic interactionism, individual self-images are based primarily on the appraisals of others. People are concerned about what others think about them. This concern is further magnified in online social networking environments where the expression of self or identity can be readily viewed by others via personal profiles and reputation indicators. As suggested by Vasalou et al. [24], digital items provide visible and audible cues that present one’s image to others, thereby reducing the difficulty others may experience in gauging their personalities. Members attempt to express and enhance their social self-image through digital items, as these items offer an increased level of representation and articulation in online space.

The findings further reveal that the functional quality and aesthetics of a digital item significantly affect one’s social self-image expression. However, we were unable to establish a significant association between functional value (price utility and functional quality) and purchase intention. Digital items are rather hedonic than utilitarian. For example, a piece of furniture in a SNC is a showpiece used to decorate one’s room and is not expected to perform the functions of real piece of furniture. Therefore, functional value does not influence purchase intention. The mean value of price utility (mean = 2.30) shows a low perceived price utility.

Additionally, the relationship between social relationship support and purchase intention was not significant. People tend to consider the projection of self-image as more important than establishing online social relationships when they decide to purchase digital items from the SNC. The results of our post hoc analysis showed a significant effect of social self-image expression on social relationship support (path coefficient = 0.57, t = 0.32, R<sup>2</sup> = 0.32).

However, the study had some limitations. First, it was conducted using survey-based data from a single online community. Secondly, our constructs may not have been sufficiently holistic to explain each dimension of customer value. Finally, Cyworld has attempted to expand its business to other countries. There may eventually be cultural and customization issues.

## 6.2. Implications for research and practice

Previous studies examined the role of emoticons in enjoyment and ultimately in the utility of a website. However, the area of digital item consumption behavior was previously unexplored. We have contributed to the understanding of the dimensions and subtypes of value relevant to SNC member digital item purchase decisions.

We also attempted to conceptualize the value framework in the context of SNCs with perceived customer value split into three dimensions: functional, emotional, and social. Each of these has been discussed in the context of SNCs, providing a structured theoretical framework and new insights to SNC researchers. Traditionally, the literature concerning marketing and e-commerce has tended to focus on functional and emotional values. Our study demonstrated that, in the SNC context, social value performs a crucial role in explaining user behavior.

The application of customer value theory to digital items provided some new insights. Its application is different for digital items than tangible products. We found that functional value, although an important predictor in case of real world purchases, was not an important predictor of purchasing a digital item. Thus, our application of customer value theory provides a better understanding of the process of deciding to purchase a digital item.

Our findings identify playfulness, aesthetics, and social selfimage expression as the three most important factors affecting consumers’ intentions to purchase digital items. SNC providers planning to sell digital items should prioritize their efforts and focus on these three factors to increase digital item sales. In particular, practitioners should pay attention to the playfulness and aesthetics experienced by members during digital item consumption and the digital item’s capability to enhance a member’s social self-image by designing high-quality digital items: avatars can be made to talk and artificial intelligence technology can be employed to allow them to move as though they were alive.

## 7. Conclusions

The sale of digital items is a relatively new source of revenue for SNC providers. Examining the motivation to purchase digital items is crucial for the success of this source of revenue. Despite the importance of identifying motivators, there is a lack of understanding about the manner in which SNC members elect to purchase digital items and the factors on which their decisions are based. We examined the intention to purchase digital items by developing a conceptual framework of customer value and by examining motivators along different dimensions of customer value. Based on the three general dimensions of customer value, we identified the digital item-specific value determinants in digital item sales. Our findings showed that emotional and social values were key dimensions of customer value for purchasing digital items.
