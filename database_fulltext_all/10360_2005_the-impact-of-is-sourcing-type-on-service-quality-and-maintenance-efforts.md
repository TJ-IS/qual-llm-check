# The impact of IS sourcing type on service quality and maintenance efforts

Joo-Yeon Park<sup>\*</sup>, Joon S. Kim

The School of Business, Yonsei University, 134 ShinChon-Dong, SeoDae Moon-Ku, Seoul 120-749, South Korea

Accepted 1 August 2003 Available online 30 October 2004

## Abstract

The purpose of our work was to examine the effectiveness of information system sourcing from the perspective of service quality and maintenance efforts. We empirically found that there were significant differences in service quality, but not in maintenance efforts, between insourced and outsourced systems. We also found that the level of service quality and maintenance efforts across system sourcing types varied as a system grew older. Our results suggested that outsourcing may not always be the correct choice for reducing costs and increasing efficiency over the long term. This work also considered the effect of issues like productivity gains from a reduced need for system maintenance and the cost savings achieved by choosing sourcing options wisely. <sup>#</sup> 2003 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Outsourced system; Insourced system; Service quality; System type; DSS; TPS; Maintenance efforts

## 1. Introduction

In order to meet rapidly changing technology trends with low costs and high quality, companies tend to rely on external vendors and contractors to manage and maintain their information systems (IS). In recent years, due to changes in IS and a concomitant lack of expertise, many organizations have been devoting large portions of their budget to IS outsourcing. According to an IDC survey [16], IS outsourcing has been expanding quickly and by 2005 will grow to a value of US\$ 136 billion. With such increasing emphasis, outsourcing is now often regarded as a solution for all IS-related problems. However, the lack of clear understanding of the effectiveness of IS outsourcing could lead to problems in system management and maintenance. Moreover, outsourcing could have a negative effect on efficiency and performance, and consequently create dissatisfaction with a system and eventually distrust of IS the services [1], even though IS outsourcing is known to provide advantages to some organizations [19]. Moreover, companies often experience a degradation of service quality and increasing hidden costs [6,14].

System maintenance is often viewed as a simple matter of bug repair or system error correction [42]. However, it requires a great deal of effort and a large portion of an organization’s budget over the system lifetime. Furthermore, it is closely associated with overall IS performance, which is related to organizational performance. One recent report suggested that, over the useful life of a software application, maintenance costs total twice as much as development costs [27]; in fact, about 50–80% of IS expenditures in an organization go toward maintenance [29]. Thus, the efficient maintenance of IS is as important as their development.

Regardless of such increasing demands on IS outsourcing and the importance of system maintenance, little research has focused on IS outsourcing from a system maintenance and service quality perspective. The purpose of our work was therefore to examine the effects of IS sourcing type on service quality and maintenance, as well as to look for a pattern of service quality and maintenance efforts that depend on system age. The difference between system types was also investigated, in order to find whether a different pattern occurred across system types. The goal was thus to provide knowledge to IS managers that will allow them to make effective system-sourcing decisions. The following research questions were drawn:

\- Which system sourcing type provides a better level of service quality?

\- Which requires less maintenance?

\- Will the level of service quality and maintenance differ by system type?

\- Will the level of service quality and maintenance of outsourced systems change as they age?

Studying outsourcing maintenance and service quality could potentially yield solutions for reducing IS costs and improving IS effectiveness. In addition, by examining the effectiveness of and differences between IS sourcing types and system types, this research could provide an understanding of service and maintenance issues for system outsourcing. For our field study, a financial industry was chosen.

## 2. Theoretical background

Although companies may have diverse reasons for outsourcing—such as simplifying their management agenda, striving to focus on core business, and meeting the need for flexibility—most of the literature shows that cost reduction is the key impetus [25,36]. According to a report by Senn and Gefen [37], most IS professionals and managers do not believe outsourcing reduces the cost of information technology. But they also found differences in viewpoint between IS professionals and managers on the value of IS spending. While IS professionals think IS outsourcing detracted from the firm’s informationsystem value, many managers believed that outsourcing contributes to the value returned from IS spending. Lacity and Hirschheim [21] found, in her research, that IS costs can successfully be reduced with insourcing rather than outsourcing. With respect to labor issues, employing outsourced workers can also lead to negative effects, due to a high level of individualism and a low level of interaction with insourced workers [39]. In addition, system outsourcing includes many disadvantages for system maintenance and management, including loss of control, failure to develop in-house expertise, and diminished data security [17,30]. IS outsourcing also often leads to problems of service degradation a few years after an initial contract is established, and that in turn increases service costs [12,34].

Since software maintenance is closely associated with its development, maintenance costs can be reduced by well-defined and clearly-documented software architecture [13,9]. Dekleva [11] stated that maintenance time is a predominant component of the maintenance cost structure. His research on IS methodology regarding maintenance showed that modern development methodologies, such as prototyping and computer-aided software engineering (CASE), do not decrease maintenance time but do decrease maintenance time in emergency situations involving error correction. Many maintenance problems are derived from inadequate software development practice and thus reduced maintenance efforts are associated with the use of structured programming and packaged software [8].

System development and maintenance, however, are seen as two discrete parts. System maintenance is a ‘‘service’’ to customers while system development involves the development of ‘‘products.’’ Niessink and Vliet [28] argued that software maintenance has more service-like than development aspects, because it involves activities for the benefit and satisfaction of customers. Pitt et al. [33] also suggested that the IS function includes a significant service component and thus IS service quality should be included in a measure of IS effectiveness.

## 2.1. Research model and hypothesis

Fig. 1 is the research model for this study. The independent variable is the system sourcing type, insourcing or outsourcing, and the dependent variables, possibly affected by the sourcing type, are maintenance efforts and service quality. Since the system sourcing type is not the only factor affecting maintenance efforts and service quality, it is necessary to control factors believed to influence both dependent variables. Following previous research, three variables, system age, size, and type, are known to exert the most influence on maintenance. They are controlled in the model.

The service quality of outsourced systems is one of the major issues in IS outsourcing, along with cost reduction. Many researchers indicate that factors, such as untimely responses to service requests and delayed operating system and software upgrades, lower the service quality of outsourcing systems [5]. Companies often have to cope with the misalignment of IT with organizational goals and the failure of services and management that external vendors have promised to provide [15]. The outsourcing literature shows examples of low service quality, such as poor response time and turnaround time, delayed software updates, etc. [20].

According to Ang and Slaughter’s study, outsourced IS workers display a higher level of individualism and are less trustworthy than insourced IS workers. Outsourced workers also rated lower on performance, due to the fact that outsourced workers did not easily blend with insourced workers and the firms’ environments [2,3]. Service performance decreases and users are dissatisfied with responses to their service requests when system development and maintenance employees are constantly shifting and when the capacity to provide service exceeds the demand. The lack of outsourced IS workers’ trustworthiness and coordination with insourced workers is also a cause of unsatisfactory service. Benko [10] stated that loss of control and difficulties meeting changing business requirements present further outsourcing disadvantages. Such disadvantages can reduce the overall quality of maintenance services and workers’ motivations for maintenance tasks. In addition, the level of service quality can change as a system grows old. An outsourcing organization is often faced with degradation of service quality and misalignment between an IS contract and an organizational goal just a few years after the initial contract. Accordingly, the level of service quality may be different between outsourced and insourced systems; outsourced systems may provide a lower level of service quality than insourced systems over time. This leads to the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1-1. Outsourced systems provide a lower level of service quality than insourced systems.

Hypothesis 1-2. The level of service for outsourced systems will decrease more rapidly than that of insourced systems as the system ages.

Based on their field study of IS outsourcing, Lacity and Hirschheim [22] suggested that outsourcing service costs three times as much as in-house service. Such increasing costs follow directly from system service and management overload compounded by a dearth of maintainers or IS specialists after the system is developed. A system developed by outsourced workers and technology may be especially hard to manage because of the lack of coordination and communication between outsourced and insourced workers. Outsourced workers could not respond to users’ needs in a timely manner and this therefore lower the service quality. Due to such inadequate management, users may not be satisfied with the maintenance service. Their demands, such as requests for software upgrade and system enhancement, may then increase. This poor service quality accordingly could result in inappropriate and inadequate IS outsourcing performance.

Therefore, we expect that an outsourced system that tends to have a degraded service level may require more maintenance efforts than an insourced system. Moreover, maintenance efforts could change as the service level changes. The older the system, the more requests for upgrade and enhancement. Thus, we also expect that the degree of maintenance efforts will show a pattern of change for both insourcing and outsourcing. This leads to the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 2-1. Outsourced systems will require more maintenance efforts than insourced systems.

Hypothesis 2-2. The level of maintenance effort for outsourced systems will increase more than that of insourced systems as the system ages.

## 3. Research methodology

## 3.1. Independent variable

The scope of IS sourcing has been established and classified from various perspectives in transaction cost theory, core competencies, and resource-dependence theory [24]. In our study, since we do not include organizational environments and political issues related to outsourcing, we classified system sourcing into either insourcing or outsourcing. Based on a preinterview with IS managers in the field and the classification used by Lacity et al. [23], an insourcing system is defined as a system that was developed using at least 80% internal workers and technology through the entire system development phase; an outsourcing system, on the other hand, is a system that depends on outside vendors and workers who take up at least 80% of the organization’s IS budget. In many instances, selective outsourcing (in which selected IS functions are provided from external vendors on an as-needed basis) is recommended. However, selective outsourcing was not usually considered an option in financial industries where our data was collected.

## 3.2. Dependent variables

Service quality and maintenance efforts are the two dependent variables in this study. Service quality was measured from a user’s point of view, while maintenance effort was measured from the IS manager’s viewpoint. Accordingly, both service quality and maintenance efforts were analyzed for the sourcing types and normalized with system size in order to avoid bias.

## 3.2.1. Service quality

IS service quality represents the differences between users’ expectation and their perception of service. SERVQUAL, a research tool to measure service quality in marketing constructed by Parasuraman et al. [31], has been widely used to measure IS service quality. Many researchers have suggested that it is an appropriate tool for measuring the service quality for IS functions. Kettinger and Lee [18] developed a newer measurement tool based on the existing SERVQUAL questionnaires, to address IS service quality better. Their 7-point Likert-scale questionnaires consisted of 13 items in each category, expected and perceived IS service quality. Service quality is the gap score in user perception and expectation. Thus, a smaller value of SERVQUAL represents a higher level of service quality. We adapted Kettinger and Lee’s revised SERVQUAL questionnaires to measure service quality by system sourcing type.

## 3.2.2. Maintenance efforts

Maintenance efforts are measured using two variables: the number of user requests for system maintenance and the number of system maintainers logged into maintenance tasks. Many researchers have used maintenance efforts as a means of estimating maintenance cost. They measured the efforts by the total number of person-hours that programmers or maintainers worked in the related system [38], or by the number of staff resources allocated to and expended on a maintenance task [35,40]. Based on previous research and field interviews, maintenance effort here was measured by the total number of maintainers logged into maintenance tasks and the responses to user requests about system maintenance. In this study, however, since the correlation between the number of maintainers and the response to user requests is high, only the number of maintainers is used for statistics analysis. This was divided by system size (which is measured by the number of system users) in order to facilitate relative comparison between insourcing and outsourcing and to gauge the number of maintainers per system user.

IS maintenance can be split into three categories of activity: corrective, adaptive, and perfective. Lientz et al. [26] defined maintenance activities using a classification scheme that has become standard. The categories are:

\- Corrective: Changes made to correct program fail ures, performance failures, and implementation failures, including emergency repairs.

\- Adaptive: Changes made to add or modify functional changes in data requirement or functional processing requirements.

\- Perfective: Changes made to enhance technical performance and improve cost-effectiveness, efficiency, and maintainability. This also includes the changes made by policy and through system change in an organization. Perfective maintenance is known to require more effort and have higher costs than other maintenance types.

In this study, the effectiveness of maintenance efforts for each activity by system sourcing types is examined.

## 3.3. Control variables

System size and age can affect maintenance efforts and service quality. Numerous studies have revealed that system size, age and complexity are strongly associated with system maintenance efforts [41]. The larger the system, the more complex, and therefore the more the maintenance cost [7]. Also, as the system grows older, systems applications or software need upgrades and enhancements to create up-to-date versions. Thus, user maintenance requests for error correction and system enhancement may increase. The system size and age were controlled to examine the pure relationship between system sourcing type and maintenance efforts and service quality. In addition to age and size, system type (transaction processing systems and decision support systems) was used as a control variable.

## 3.4. Research methodology

In order to examine the effect of system sourcing type on service quality and maintenance, a field study using questionnaires was employed. Data were collected mainly from the financial industry, where IS modification and maintenance are considered crucial. As for the questionnaire’s design, two sets were constructed in order to measure maintenance efforts and service quality of a related system. One was distributed to system managers and maintainers to measure maintenance efforts and the other to system users to measure the service quality of the related system. The questionnaire for maintenance efforts consisted of the number of labor-years logged for maintenance tasks [4] and the percentage of maintenance work performed in an IS department. The IS-adapted SERVQ UAL suggested was used for the service quality measurements.

A total of 107 responses<sup>1</sup> were used out of 119 questionnaires distributed to 28 organizations. Since the unit of analysis was a subsystem divided by functions and business tasks, we were able to collect data for multiple subsystems in an organization.

## 4. Analysis of the results

## 4.1. Demographic data

The research data were mainly collected from banks and security companies. Table 1 shows the characteristics of the data. Systems less than 2 years old were more than half of the total and system size ranges were relatively large. Data from 45 transaction processing systems and 62 decision support systems were collected.

## 4.2. Effects of IS sourcing type on service quality (Hypothesis 1)

We used multivariate analysis of covariance (MAN-COVA) to examine the differences in maintenance efforts and service quality across sourcing types. We included the system age and type as covariates and controlled them in order to examine the pure relationship between sourcing type and dependent variables.

Table 2 represents the test results for the significance of each sourcing type for each dependent variable. Sourcing type significantly affects service quality at the 0.1 level; i.e. service quality is influenced directly by sourcing type. Indeed, the effect of sourcing type on service quality shows a different result across system types. For transaction processing systems, as shown in Table 3, outsourced systems seem to provide a higher level of service quality than insourced systems.<sup>2</sup> Conversely, for decision support systems, insourced systems seem to provide better service quality than outsourced ones. Transaction processing systems often require large data transaction over 24 h periods, and immediate repair or maintenance, without any loss of connectivity of the system. Thus, it seems that in the few years after winning a contract, outsourcing vendors put tremendous effort into maintaining transaction processing systems in order to increase customer satisfaction. For decision support systems, insourced workers have a better understanding of organizational policy, decision processes, and users’ requests than do outsourced workers. Thus, they can provide appropriate response and fulfill users’ needs.

In addition, since sourcing type and system age are significantly correlated (correlation ¼ 0:17), at the 0.1 level, we also examined the interaction effects between sourcing type and system age to test the changes of service quality for both insourcing and outsourcing over time. We found that the interaction between system sourcing and system age, as it influences service quality, is significant at the 0.1 level. This means that the influence of IS sourcing on service quality differs according to system age, and in turn that the level of service quality, which is influenced by sourcing type, differs over time. From this result, we presumed that there is a pattern of changes in service quality for both sourcing types depending on system age. We then developed a graphic approach focusing on system age in order to provide a clear explanation for IS sourcing on service quality.

To observe the patterns of service quality over time, we divided system age into four phases, based on the data collected at certain points in time: less than 2 years, 2–3 years, 3–4 years, and greater than 4 years. Even though the data were collected in a cross-sectional period of time and included various systems with different system ages, we could assume and predict the pattern of changes over the long term by examining the mean value of grouped data belonging to each divided year. The graphs display an easy visual comparison of each sourcing type with respect to service quality over the system’s age.

The pattern of service quality by system year for both insourced and outsourced systems is shown in Fig. 2, where the pattern of service quality for TPS and DSS looks quite different. For TPS, the level of service quality for insourced systems was lower at first phase but increased with systems age. Conversely, the level of service quality of outsourced systems was higher until the system was 2–3 years old, but gradually decreases as the systems aged. In other words, outsourcing provided the higher level of service quality in the first 2 or 3 years but over time the service quality deteriorated. For DSS, however, the level of service quality for insourced systems steadily increased, while the level for outsourced systems decreased after 2–3 years. Thus, insourced DSS systems had workers who understood and adapted to change in a firm’s policy and structure had a better understanding of users requests and provided better service. These graphs imply that outsourcing vendors tend to incur high costs and make greater efforts in the early stages, in order to meet customers’ needs and improve the quality of service. In the long run, however, their service quality degraded and the needs were neglected, while insourced systems improved.

## 4.3. Effects of IS sourcing type on maintenance efforts

To examine the effects of IS sourcing type on maintenance efforts, we also used MANCOVA. The result showed that maintenance efforts were not significant at the 0.1 level. That is, the sourcing type did not directly influence the number of maintainers per system user (see Table 2). However, in general, the data showed that a higher number of system maintainers seemed to be required for outsourcing systems than for insourcing systems for both system types (see Table 3).

The interaction effect between sourcing type and system age on maintenance efforts, however, was significant at the 0.1 level. Thus, maintenance efforts were not influenced directly by sourcing type, but rather by the interaction between sourcing type and system age. The degree of maintenance efforts for insourced and outsourced systems changed as the system aged. In order to observe changes in maintenance effort over time, we also divided system age into four phases and examined the mean value of grouped data in each divided year, following the procedure we used for service quality.

The distribution of maintenance efforts for both TPS and DSS is shown in Fig. 3. The mean number of maintainers per system user for both types, reveals a similar pattern that shows outsourcing is greater than insourcing throughout all system ages. The number of maintainers in insourced DSS decreases after 3 years of system age but for TPS the number of maintainers is much greater for outsourcing in the first 2–3 years of system age, while insourcing seem to retain a similar number of maintainers over time. However, for service quality case, outsourcing provided higher level of service quality than insourcing through the first few phases. This graphic representation did not support our expectation that the lower the service quality, the more maintenance efforts were required. The possible explanation for this is that outsourced systems do have many more system errors and failures that require more maintenance activity in the first phase than do insourced systems. But if an organization puts many more maintenance workers and greater effort into outsourced systems in the first few years, the level of service quality of outsourced systems actually rises above the level of service quality of insourced systems, regardless of the number of maintenance errors and requests.

According to interviews with system managers in the field, outsourced system management and service often degrade and do not fulfill customers’ demands a few years after the initial contract is undertaken. This is especially true for TPS that play a crucial role in day-to-day business and in satisfying customers who are more sensitive to system failure and service quality. Therefore, we assumed that for TPS the level of service quality can be higher for outsourced systems than for insourced ones in the first few years, with more maintenance effort.

For investigation of maintenance efforts across the two groups of sourcing types, the standard classification of system maintenance activities was employed. As shown in Table 4, a larger number of system maintainers was required for perfective maintenance for both insourced and outsourced systems among other maintenance activities. This suggests that most maintenance work falls into the category of perfective maintenance. Studies of software maintenance have shown that system enhancement occupies the greatest portion of software maintenance workers time [43]. After perfective maintenance, the number of maintainers for corrective maintenance was shown to be slightly higher than those for adaptive maintenance. Adaptive maintenance does not show much difference across sourcing types. This is understandable, in that functional modifications following changes in the processing or data environment do not require substantial knowledge and maintenance effort.

For insourced systems, perfective and adaptive maintenance efforts steadily increased in the 3–4 years after system implementation, while corrective maintenance increased only in the first phase. All types of maintenance activities were, however, reduced as time elapsed. In contrast, the graphic representation, as illustrated in Fig. 4, showed that all types of maintenance activities for outsourced systems gradually increased as the system aged. Over the long term, outsourcing required much more maintenance effort than insourcing. In many outsourcing cases, vendors had an incomplete understanding of organizational policies and experienced a discontinuity between system development and maintenance, which made it hard to communicate and cooperate with internal users and workers. One study showed that a permanent employee in an organization performed and processed tasks more effectively in a more timely fashion, because he/she perceived a greater obligation and commitment to an organization than did a contract employee [32]. Thus, internal workers better comprehended changes of organizational policy and environment and could respond more effectively to users’ requests. Outsourced system disadvantages might well result in higher costs and more strenuous efforts in later phases of system maintenance and management.

## 5. Conclusion and limitations

The purpose of this research was to examine the effects of IS sourcing type on service quality and maintenance efforts, as well as to discover patterns of service quality and maintenance efforts according to the system’s age and system type. We empirically found that, consistent with Hypothesis 1-1, the level of service quality system users perceived differs between insourced and outsourced systems. Generally, outsourced systems seemed to entail a higher level of service quality than insourced systems. However, outsourced systems provided a higher level of service quality for transaction processing systems, while insourced systems provide better service quality for decision support systems. With respect to Hypothesis 1-2, the older the system, the lower the service quality provided for outsourced systems in both TPS and DSS. Even though outsourced TPS that play a crucial role in satisfying customers and are susceptible to service quality provided better service in the early stages, its service level decreased as the system aged.

Contrary to Hypothesis 2-1, the sourcing type did not influence maintenance efforts. This result indicates that the degree of maintenance effort does not differ between insourced and outsourced systems. However, when we considered maintenance efforts with system age, consistent with Hypothesis 2-2, we found not only that greater maintenance efforts were required for outsourced systems in general, but that these efforts had to be increased with time. For TPS, the degree of maintenance effort for outsourced systems was higher in the first few phases, while for DSS the degree was higher after 3–4 years of system age. This indicated that for TPS, the system bugs and failures that accrue in the outsourced systems required more maintenance in the first few years after system implementation. In contrast, DSS seemed to require more maintenance efforts in the areas of system failure enhancement and policy driven modification 3–4 years after system implementation.

We assume from these results that insourcing can provide constant service in a timely manner using inhouse workers who are responsible and trustworthy as well as familiar with an organizational policy and system. However, outsourcing tends to focus on system management and service only in the few years after the contract is negotiated, and afterwards ignores or overlooks service and management issues. The study also indicated that, from a long-term perspective, system outsourcing could have a negative effect on cost reduction and efficiency; the outsourced system had a required additional cost that involved aligning the outsourcing contract with system maintenance and management.

By comparing outsourced with insourced systems, this empirical study yielded a new way of understanding outsourcing service, management and cost savings from the perspective of service quality and maintenance efforts. It also provided insights into outsourcing problems, such as service degradation. We discovered that outsourcing seems to provide better service with lower costs in the early stages, but that, over the long-term, service quality was degraded and management disregarded.

The empirical findings, however, have the following limitations. First, various sourcing options need to be applied. The investigation of selective sourcing options on various IS functions could bring a different result. Second, samples taken only from the financial industry can limit the generalizability of the results. Finally, a longitudinal design can offer stronger evidence for a relationship between the sourcing type and service quality and maintenance efforts. Thus, longitudinal data gathering and analysis could be applied to examine the patterns and differences of service quality and maintenance efforts over time.
