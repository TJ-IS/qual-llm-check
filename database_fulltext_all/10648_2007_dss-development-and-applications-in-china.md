# DSS development and applications in China

Jun Tian <sup>a,\*</sup>, Yingluo Wang <sup>a</sup>, Huaizu Li <sup>a</sup>, Ling Li <sup>a,b</sup>, Kanliang Wang

<sup>a</sup> School of Management, Xi’an Jiaotong University, Xi’an 710049, China

<sup>b</sup> Department of Information Technology and Decision Sciences, Old Dominion University, Norfolk, VA 23529, USA

Available online 6 January 2005

## Abstract

This paper provides an overview of the application and development of decision support systems in China. The curriculum and research in decision support systems in major Chinese universities is introduced first, then selected recent applications of decision support systems in China is described. Finally, the main research topics and direction on decision support systems in China are discussed.

Keywords: Decision support systems; Emerging economies; DSS application; DSS development

## 1. Introduction

Until the 1960s, the major information systems were transaction processing systems for operation level management. Then the concept of management information systems (MIS) was conceived. MIS focused on providing middle-level management with predefined managerial reports that would give them the managerial support they needed for tactical decision making. By the 1970s, it became apparent that the information provided by MIS was not adequately supporting many of the decision-making needs of top management. As a result, the concept of decision support systems (DSS)

was born. In the 1970s, pioneer scholars in DSS, including Keen, Morton, Sprague, and Whinston, developed the concept as well as the discipline called decision support systems (DSS) [30,34,57]. The birth of model-oriented DSS marks the beginning of information systems specifically for the decision support purposes of top management. Progress has been made in both DSS theory and practice since then. DSS further evolved in the 1980s. The concept of group decision support systems (GDSS) was developed. In the middle of 1980s, through integration with networking technology, artificial intelligence, and enterprise information systems, DSS such as distributed decision support systems (DDSS), intelligent decision support systems (IDSS), and integrated decision support systems (IDSS) appeared. By the 1980s, it was evident that most corporate executives did not directly use the analytical capabilities of DSS so the concept of executive information systems (EIS) was developed. In the 1990s, the concept of on-line analytical processing (OLAP) systems was developed. OLAP can be considered as an extension of DSS. The goal of OLAP is to provide subject-oriented, multidimensional, ad-hoc decision support to top management. In the middle 1990s, web-based DSS also became an active research field.

## 2. DSS education and research in major universities in China

In 1998, the college curriculum approved by the Ministry of Education of China integrated five separate specialties, including information science, informatics, management information system (MIS), economic information management, and forestry information management, into a single specialty called information management and management information systems. The purpose of one of the subdisciplines under the socalled management science and engineering was to educate the workforce in information management to meet the demand of information industry development in China. About 300 colleges and universities in mainland China have already offered the specialty of information management and management information system since then; meanwhile, about 40 public or private colleges and universities in Taiwan Province, Hong Kong, and Macao have offered similar specialties. Data indicate that near a quarter of the colleges and universities that offer the specialty of information management and information system offer DSS courses on either the undergraduate or graduate level. Such schools have professionally trained instructors and researchers in related fields, who are engaged in DSS teaching and research. Some universities have provided outstanding DSS teaching and research, in the following, 10 of them will be introduced.

## 2.1. Dalian University of Technology

Dalian University of Technology is one of the first universities that introduced DSS into China. The Institute of Systems Engineering of Dalian University of Technology established in 1977 has been engaged in research and teaching in the area of computer-assisted management. During the period of 1985 to 1988, the institute developed one of the earliest DSS in China entitled <sup>b</sup>A DSS for the Overall Development of Shanxi Province,<sup>Q</sup> which is a DSS integrated with expert systems. The system is able to incorporate experts experiences with intelligent and interactive characteristics. Shanxi is a province abundant in natural resources, such as coal, aluminum, and iron, so mining has become the main source of income in the province. Shanxi Province faced a set of problems. On one hand, how to make policy for developing resource industry and maintaining sustainable development which is mandatory to meet the resource needs of economic development, and on the other, how to reduce and control environmental pollution caused by mining. The system developed was done on a large scale and provided advanced modeling capability.

Under the supervision of Academician Zhongtuo Wang at Dalian University of Technology, a number of DSS have been developed for a variety of application purposes, including agriculture, economic development, and social planning. All of the DSS that were developed have resulted in strong economic benefits. The application of one of its projects entitled <sup>b</sup>A Decision Support System for Comprehensive Development of National Economy,<sup>Q</sup> started in the 1990s, has been very successful in terms of its far reaching impact.

The main research fields today include the impact of information society on organizational reforms, knowledge management, and e-commerce.

## 2.2. Tsinghua University

Thanks to the advantageous location in Beijing, Tsinghua University has greater opportunities to compete for large-scale domestic research projects, as well as to collaborate scholarly with foreign institutions. This endows Tsinghua University with incomparable advantages over other universities and colleges in many fields, including DSS research and practice.

The Department of Automation at Tsinghua has great strength in DSS research. The Institute of System Engineering, established in 1979 in this department, has one of the first six doctoral programs in systems engineering instituted in China. Several national engineering centers and laboratories, as well as some research centers supported at provincial or ministry levels, are located in this department. They include the National CIMS Engineering Research Center, State

Key Laboratory of Intelligent Technology and Systems (branch laboratory), Key Laboratory of Bioinformatics of Ministry of Education, and Joint Laboratory of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS). These laboratories focus on research fields, including control theory, complex system modeling and dispatching optimization, information processing, pattern recognition, artificial intelligence and robotics, and network information security. There is strong technical support available for DSS modeling, designing, and development. The department has received hundreds of research awards at the national level and has made significant contribution to areas such as intelligent decision theory and application and ITS. Projects such as <sup>b</sup>A Forecasting System Integrated with Adaptive Expert Systems for Si Contents in Hot Metal Tapped from Blast Furnace,<sup>Q b</sup>CIMS Experimental Technology System,<sup>Q b</sup>Techniques of Virtual Product Development and System Integration for Developing New Products,<sup>Q</sup> were separately awarded the second prize of National Advancement of Science and Technology; <sup>b</sup>Quantitative Analysis of Chinese Economic Structure in 2000<sup>Q</sup> was awarded the first prize of National Advancement of Science and Technology.

The School of Economics and Management at Tsinghua University has been engaged in DSS research. Research topics include integrated IDSS based on neural networks and qualitative reasoning, decision analysis methods in complex systems, metasynthesis, theories and methods of managerial decisions, knowledge discovery, and business intelligence. Many systems designed and developed by the school have had important impacts. Such projects include <sup>b</sup>Electricity Planning DSS<sup>Q</sup> and <sup>b</sup>A Forecasting System for National Monthly Industrial Outputs<sup>Q</sup> sponsored by the State Planning Commission.

## 3. DSS development

DSS applications have been successful in China since the early 1980s. DSS development in China has experienced three major periods. They are the first period (1978–1988), the second period (1988–1991), and the third period (1991–present) [26]. During the first period, Chinese researchers and IIASA jointly developed a major DSS for policy and decision making at the provincial level entitled <sup>b</sup>DSS for Overall Development of Shanxi Province.<sup>Q</sup> In the second period, through only 3 years of endeavors, thousands of DSS were developed and implemented. During the third period, greater effort has been put into improving the functions and performance of existing DSS; meanwhile, many new technologies have been integrated into DSS development. Such applications primarily concentrate on four areas: (1) DSS applications in macroeconomy regulation and public policy making; (2) DSS applications in industry and commerce administration; (3) DSS applications in natural resource management, ecosystem, and environmental protection; (4) DSS applications in enterprise operations management.

3.1. DSS for macroeconomy and public policy and decision making

In the early 1980s, the Chinese government poured massive investment into the State Economic Information Systems (SEIS), which consists of more than 100 relatively independent information systems (subsystems) that are distributed throughout provinces, cities, and counties all over China.

At the same time, many local governments at different levels started to develop DSS to support policy and decision making. These efforts not only offered great opportunities for DSS development but also had a significant impact upon the DSS industry. The main applications in macroeconomy are shown in Table 1.

## 4. Selected DSS application

## 4.1. DSS for overall development in Shaanxi Province

SEXES-DSS is a DSS for managing the macroeconomy in Shaanxi Province. It has eight functions for helping making decisions in a macroeconomic development environment [13].

## 4.1.1. Objective and function

The tasks of the system can be classified into the following eight aspects: decision support for the development of science and technology, economy, society, large-scale projects of economic significance, short-term planning (annual), public policy making, important top level decisions, emergency management and decision execution supervision. After decomposing the eight aspects into subobjectives with a multilevel hierarchical system, it resulted in 144 objectives. The functions of SXSES-DSS include query, forecasting, simulation, evaluation, alternative selection, and decision execution supervision (see Fig. 1)

## 4.1.2. Modules

SXSES-DSS includes 24 functions and a relatively independent emergency processing system. The system provided support for decisions related to science and technology, economy, and society. The emergency system supports the emergency handling of natural disasters, accidents in industrial and agricultural sectors, and social emergency issues in the Shaanxi Province. The following is a description of the related functions:

(1) The module on corresponding development in science and technology, economy, and society is used to evaluate the current development status of science and technology, economy, and society as a whole. It provides information regarding the establishment of future objectives for overall development.

(2) The module on science and technology is used to forecast regional science and technology development and to provide support for related decision-making tasks.

(3) The module on transforming technology to productivity is used to handle issues related to transforming technology to productivity.

(4) The module on new science and technology industry development is used to handle issues regarding new science and technology development.

(5) The module on national economy is used to facilitate the handling of issues regarding the forecasting of regional economic development.

(6) The module on natural resources is used to handle issues pertaining to natural resources planning.

(7) The module on regulation at a macrolevel is used to handle issues related to the generation of countermeasures to macroeconomic regulation.

(8) The module on industrial development is used to handle issues related to industrial development.

(9) The module on agricultural development is used to handle issues regarding agricultural development.

(10) The integrated module on population and education is used to handle issues involving population and educational development.

The abovementioned functions can be classified into four layers. Layer 1 includes the module of corresponding development about technology, economy, and society. Layer 2 includes the technology integrated module, the national economy-integrated module, and the social integrated module. Layer 3 includes the modules on transforming technology to productivity, new science and technology, industry development, natural resources, and the national economic regulation at the macrolevel, population, and education. Layer 4 includes the industrial and agriculture modules (see Fig. 2 SXSES-DSS function module hierarchy).

## 4.1.3. General structure

The general structure of SXSES-DSS is shown in Fig. 3. In Fig. 3, IMMS (intelligent man–machine system) is at the top of the structure. IMMS provides an intelligent man–machine interface for users. At level 2, intelligent problem processing system (IPPS) can help users to recognize and define problems and locate appropriate models for problem solving. problems base system (PBS) and problems base management system (PBMS) help save those problems that can be solved and maintain the problems until finishing. intelligent general management system (IGMS) at level 3 is used to obtain resources for problem solving. At level 4, database system (DBS) and database management system (DBMS) primarily store related data and provide database functions; MBS stand for model base system and MBMS for model base management system. ABMS and ABS stand for algorithm base management system and algorithms. Knowledge base system (KBS) and knowledge base management system (KMBS) store the related knowledge for problem solving. Finally, GBS stands for graphs base system and GBMS for graphs base management system, TBS is for texts base system, and TBMS is for texts base management system.

## 4.2. DSS for national agricultural policy making

A DSS for agricultural policy analysis has been developed [32]. The system was developed based on the need for a framework for analyzing policies affecting agricultural production, consumption, and price and trade at the national level. The system mainly consists of a partial equilibrium model, which is composed of 13 submodels for each individual commodity. It is considered to be one of the first and most comprehensive models for examining the effects of policies on China’s food demand, supply, and trade. Most of the elasticities used in the modeling were estimated by using state-of-the-art econometrics with assumptions that estimated parameters consistent with theory. Both demand and supply elasticities change over time as income elasticities depend on the income level, and cross-price elasticities of demand (or supply) depend on food budget shares.

Using the system, researchers have conducted studies on the projection of China’s food economy in the early 21st century and the impacts of agricultural R&D policy on China’s food security. The findings of these studies received two science and technology progressive prizes from the Chinese Ministry of Agriculture. The analysis of China’s WTO accession and its agricultural development based on the system has been drawing attention at home and abroad. The system provides policy recommendations for national policy makers. The system is currently migrating to an improved version entitled <sup>b</sup>A Decision Support System for Sustainable Agricultural Development<sup>Q</sup> to include important parameters, such as a regional level study on water resource and land use, regional food security, regional agricultural sustainable development, the relationship between trade liberalization and regional productive distribution, etc.

## 4.3. Integrated DSS for the economic development of the region of Lanchang River, Yunnan Province

The system was jointly developed by Tsinghua University and the Geography Research Institute of Yunnan Province. This is one of the first DSS for the major rivers in China. The system is equipped with five large-scale databases related to geography, population, ecological environment, agriculture, tourism, transportation, water resources, natural disaster prevention, etc. The system is able to provide decision support for policy making in natural resource management, environmental protection, and economic growth in the region of the Lanchang River. The modeling tools include simulation modeling, what-if analysis, mathematical–physical equations, and multiobjective decision models.

The system was adopted by the provincial government of Yunnan Province in July of 2001. The system is able to provide support for decision or policy making in areas such as tourism, population growth, ecological environment protection, commerce, transportation, and water resource management, as well as providing analytical capability for sustainable economic development to the whole Lanchang River region. The project team has trained more than 500 users just for the Dai Autonomous Prefecture of Xishuangbanna and Simao region in Yunnan Province. The implementation results show that the system has offered major support to the decision-making tasks of local economic development.

## 4.4. DSS for water resource management of Haihe River

Due to the serious water shortage in northwestern China, rationally allocating and utilizing water resources has become an important issue. As a result, utilizing the water resource of Haihe River is considered important for the economic, social, and environmental development of China’s northwestern region.

The DSS for water resource management of Haihe River includes quite a few water resource allocation and utilization models. The system is able to provide decision support for water resource planning, allocation, control, and management. The system has been employed to implement the water resource allocation plan proposed by the central government and is able to control the total fluxes in provinces such as Qinhai, Gansu, and Inner Mongolia; meanwhile, the system can help solve the problem of water resource allocation in the neighboring provinces. Overall, the system has significantly improved the efficiency of water resource utilization in China’s northwestern region.

## 4.5. DSS for overall urban development in Wuhan, Hubei Province

It is known that there exists an economic relationship among the growth pattern of the national economy, urbanization, and distribution of the sizes of cities. Statistical data shows that, for a particular country, there is a positive correlation between the gross national product and the degree of urbanization. Since 1983, the Chinese government has approved 14 medium-to-large-size cities as independent economic entities and granted them partial economic autonomy. Although these cities are still governed by the provinces where they are located, their economic development plans, as well as social development plans, no longer need to be approved by the respective provincial government. These independently planned cities are regional business and financial centers that significantly impact the regional economic growth. It is important to trace the trend of urban development in these cities both quantitatively and qualitatively.

The urban system is one of the most complicated systems. Such a complex system is characterized by complex mechanisms, ill-defined systems boundary and layers, multiple variables, and fuzziness. It is a difficult task to provide a comprehensive evaluation on the overall development of urban systems. A DSS for the comprehensive evaluation of urban development was developed in Wuhan, a city with a population of more than 6 million [19]. The system is characterized by a multilayer evaluation index system and a fuzzy, multicriteria, multilayer evaluation model for evaluating urban development. The use of fuzzy modeling techniques as a method of evaluation for urban development is innovative. The system has been implemented by the government of the city of Wuhan in central China and provides decision support for planning urban development in Wuhan, one of the largest cities in China. The system has a major impact. The areas it affects include the insight and consensus on the strategic choices in urban development, the quality of urban planning, and the quality of urban planning information. Many of the decisions on the urban planning of Wuhan are based on the achievements of the system. The success of the system has encouraged the Chinese government to introduce the system to other urban areas.

## 4.6. Integrated DSS for grasslands ecosystems in Inner Mongolia

Maowusu sandy grasslands are located in the middle and south part of the Ertos plateau, Inner Mongolia. This area has typical continental semiarid climatic conditions. Grasslands generally receive between 25 and 75 cm of precipitation per year. Annual precipitation of the area ranges from 401.6 mm in the southeast to 162.4 mm in the northwest, with more than 60% of precipitation concentrated in 2 summer months. It is not difficult to postulate a severe hydrological limitation for vegetation development and to realize the importance of water resources for local agricultural management. The problem of an insufficient water supply is further complicated by sandy soil. In the last few decades, inappropriate agricultural management has caused much degradation of the sandy grassland. In recent years, the implication of ecological systems to sustainable agricultural development has begun to be recognized.

A knowledge-based DSS for the hilly, sandy grassland ecosystems was developed for Maowusu, Inner Mongolia [86]. The system is a component of the Inner Mongolia Grassland Ecosystem Research Information System (IMGERIS). IMGERIS is an important subsystem of the Chinese Ecological Research Network (CERN). The system was designed to help decision-making tasks for managing land use, determining planting species/variety, and selecting optimal coverage of plants. The model system embedded in the system includes advanced theoretical models of soil water dynamics. The system is able to provide policy makers, agricultural consultants, and agricultural enterprise management in Erdos area with decision support in land use, plant species/varieties selection, and the optimal coverage of plants.

## 4.7. DSS for R&D project evaluation and management

Selecting research and development (R&D) projects is a complicated and knowledge intensive decision-making task in which decision models and knowledge rules play an important role. A hybrid knowledge and model system for R&D project selection was developed that integrates mathematical models with knowledge rules for R&D project selection [62]. The system is designed to support the entire decision process of R&D project selection and has been used in R&D project selection process by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC). The system supports the R&D project selection process at the organizational level and provides useful information for decision-making tasks in the R&D project selection process.

A DSS for project evaluation was developed to manage investments in the various parts of the State Economic Information System of China (SEIS) [93]. The core of the system consists of mathematical models and algorithms. Data envelopment analysis (DEA) is used to evaluate the relative efficiencies of the subsystems of the SEIS and the analytic hierarchy process methodology was adopted to form the decision process. To allocate further investment to the subsystems, an investment prediction model for nonprofit organizations was formulated which is in the format of a parametric linear programming model. This new mathematical model can be applied to many decision problems in economics and finance. Project evaluation is a new discipline, which until now has been concentrated on profit making organizations. It is a challenge to construct an evaluation model for the SEIS which is a non-profit-making organization.

## 4.8. DSS for automotive product planning and competitive market analysis

A DSS for automotive product planning and competitive market analysis was introduced by Guo et al. [27]. The system was designed for automotive product marketing, design, and manufacturing in China as a tool to support product planning, competitive market analysis, supply chain analysis, and subsequent manufacturing systems planning and deployment. The system consists of a number of automotive related databases which provide information about manufacturers’ performance in each market segment, as well as production information of all existing market players in the Chinese auto industry.

Under the proposed decision framework, a detailed functional analysis was conducted to provide overall decision-making support for starting automotive manufacturing operation in China. Five modules make up the primary functional components of the prototype DSS. These modules are case base, decision scenarios, knowledge base, data base, and model base. The module of decision scenarios is the core module of the DSS. Key issues of starting a manufacturing operation in China are identified. Both qualitative and quantitative analysis can be provided by this module for overall decision-making support.

Product planning, one of the key modules of the DSS, is highlighted here with a case study. It supports decision makers in formulating suitable strategies for initial market entry by analyzing existing competitors’ status, growth estimation of each market segment, and competitive advantage analysis for new products. The enabling techniques, such as the parametric cost model, the market performance index matrix, and the competitive pattern identification mechanism, allow the decision-making process to be conducted in a systematic and objective manner. Output of the product planning, covering the expected market price, product model complexity, variants, possible market volumes, etc., has a strong impact on downstream decision making, such as manufacturing system deployment and manufacturing cost planning. The DSS prototype and its implementation in the case study demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

## 5.2. Metasynthetic systems

A recent study has systematically identified DSS reference disciplines and traced how concepts and findings by systems researchers have been picked up by DSS researchers to be applied, extended, and refined in developing DSS research. According to the study, in DSS development, it was systems science that provided an essential concept for defining the concept of decision support systems and justifying the need for such systems in organizations [1,73,17,84]. In DSS research in China, more literature has emerged which directs us in developing a complete theory on DSS within the framework of systems sciences. Metasynthetic engineering (MSE) has emerged as a methodology for dealing with open complex giant systems (OCGSs). In OCGS, there are a variety of subsystems with complex structures and interrelations. The goal of MSE is to integrate a large amount of dispersed knowledge from different disciplines in a whole structure [50,51]. As indicated by Gu et al. [26], Qian’s MSE theory has greatly impacted DSS development and practice in China.

To practice well-known Chinese rocket scientist Qian’s theory, a major DSS research project entitled <sup>b</sup>Man–computer Cooperative Meta-synthetic System Support for Macro-economy Decision-making<sup>Q</sup> sponsored by the National Natural Science Foundation of

China (NSFC) was initiated in 1999. After 4 years of research, significant achievements have been made in which Tian has studied the problem of integrating different expert opinions and consensus reaching in the framework of MSE [63,64].

In the complex systems research area, other major topics include systems engineering methods integrated with decision making, utility theory and its applications in decision making, behavioral theory in group decision support systems, Wuli–Shili–Renli systems, decision making under uncertainty, restraint constellation circling theory in decision making, reconstructability analysis, etc. [68,40,25,80,93,67]. Shu [56] applied reconstructability analysis to the design of metasynthesis system to integrate data, information, model, and expert knowledge. He successfully predicted China’s GDP growth rate with a higher precision than any other predictions. Cheng and Zhang and Cheng et al. [7,8,] studied the pattern of information organization to support group decision making and proposed the concept of <sup>b</sup>public electric brain<sup>Q</sup> (see Fig. 4).

## 6. Conclusion

DSS development started in China in the early 1980s. Since then, DSS activity has been steadily growing. In the mid-1980s, China launched the wellknown <sup>d</sup>863 Advanced Technology Project<sup>T</sup> under the late leader Deng Xiaoping. This national research project initiated an era of unprecedented development of DSS technology. Since the launching of the <sup>d</sup>863 Project,<sup>T</sup> China has been host to the emergence of advanced DSS technology. Chinese researchers have developed thousands of DSS that have been successfully applied to national economic policy making, public policy making, industry development, enterprise operations management, transportation, manufacturing, and education.

DSS technology is well established in China. In China, both public and private sectors are gaining tremendous saving and benefits by using DSS technology. Many DSS that are currently in operation are making sizeable impacts in policy making and decision making.
