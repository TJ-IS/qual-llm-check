# The impact of partnership attributes on EDI implementation success

Sangjae Lee<sup>\*</sup>, Gyoo Gun Lim<sup>1</sup>

Department of Business Administration, Sejong University, 98 Kunja-dong, Kwangjin-gu, Seoul, 143-747, South Korea

Received 2 October 2002; received in revised form 2 December 2002; accepted 3 March 2003

## Abstract

The growing interest in the use of electronic data interchange (EDI) to revolutionize the way in which business conducts trading activities leads to research questions concerning the characteristics of electronic partnerships that promote implementation success. The research model in this study shows that partnership attributes affect EDI implementation, which has three dimensions: integration, utilization, and diversity, and performance. Partnership attributes encompass partner trust, interdependence, and commitment. Results show that partner trust, interdependence, and commitment affect the extent to which companies undertake EDI integration and increase the percentage of EDI exchange and performance. This suggests that companies contemplating EDI should set up compatible business processes and a high-quality partner relationship for implementation and performance.

## 1. Introduction

Electronic data interchange (EDI) is a form of interorganizational electronic commerce where one trading partner (a buyer or seller) establishes individual links with one or more trading partners through a computer-to-computer electronic communication method. ‘‘Developing and managing EDI’’ has become a key management issue among IS executives [9]. Innovations on the Internet are creating new EDI communication capability. The Internet, with few geographical constraints and large-scale connectivity, appears to be the most feasible platform for EDI [39]. Internet-based technologies, such as Web browser software and standard TCP/IP communication protocols, stimulated growth of the intranet as a communication platform for standardized electronic information between businesses. This indicates the need to understand the factors that enable successful implementation of EDI within organizations.

Organizational factors that affect EDI implementation include organizational factors and learning [23,32]. Since a comprehensive model including a number of organizational factors would be difficult to test, this study focuses on the role of partnership attributes that lead to implementation and high EDI performance, as these organizational factors have not been previously investigated. They are also important because EDI is different from other types of technological innovations in that it cannot be implemented unilaterally. Firms are encouraged to implement EDI by trading partners that persuade or coerce them into adopting the technology and implementing additional transaction sets to gain coordination benefits.

The research model was empirically tested with data collected from Korean companies that have adopted EDI. The results of this study may help predict the state of EDI implementation in certain organizational contexts and explain its motives. If the conditions of EDI adopters are not appropriate for EDI implementation, they may consider changes toward positive management strategies in these environments in order to tap the benefits of the technology. Organizations that concentrate on utilizing EDI under appropriate interorganizational environments would be more likely to implement EDI successfully into their organization and with their trading partners.

## 2. Theoretical framework

The major areas of research that provide the necessary theoretical foundations for this study are EDI implementation, interorganizational relations and IS implementation research. This study focuses on partnership attributes as antecedents for promoting EDI implementation. These factors are closely related to the notion of uncertainty about the partnership. This uncertainty is dominant in market-like and hierarchical transactions, but it should be examined separately in hybrid arrangements of market and hierarchy [4]. The growing use of IT applications is perceived to be less risky if information processing needs to reduce task uncertainty, such as analyzability, variety, and interdependence, are matched with appropriate information processing capabilities [5]. Furthermore, if partnership uncertainty due to uncertain intentions and opportunistic behavior by the other party decreases, more investment in IS applications crossing organizational boundaries can be implemented.

## 2.1. EDI Implementation Research

Implementation of an innovation normally follow various stages of IS implementation as proposed by stage models [10,19,27]. Most organizational researchers accept the fact that adoption and infusion represent the various stages of innovation [34].

Infusion—‘‘implementation’’ is the term used here—needs to be considered, because there are only a relatively small number of studies dealing with it compared to those for EDI adoption. The study of the infusion of EDI is more meaningful due to the significant strategic benefits that may be derived only after the system is implemented in a comprehensive manner [25].

Researchers have studied interorganizational systems (IOS) and EDI using the diffusion of innovation theoretical frameworks or organizational transformation perspectives [41]. Ramamurthy and Premkumar investigated innovation characteristics, such as compatibility, complexity, and relative advantage; organizational factors, such as top management support, task scope, IS sophistication, and championing; and organization learning factors that are related to internal and external diffusion of EDI. Raymond and Bergeron [33] showed that factors such as organizational support (e.g., training, structure), implementation processes (e.g., planning, testing), and control procedures for data integrity and security affect EDI implementation.

Organizational innovation by EDI is subject to many factors in different categories, including characteristics of industry, organization, task, and partner relations. The development and standardization of EDI messages demand support from industry, associations, and government. Industry-specific conditions, such as external pressure and existence of standards, have been reported to affect the implementation of EDI. Organizational factors, such as size and managerial attitude, are important for EDI implementation, because they affect the willingness of management to incur the high expenses of initial investment and structural changes in business practices. IS should be sophisticated and sufficiently computerized to allow for integrated EDI; this helps to increase the impact of the technology. Organization readiness is concerned with the level of IT usage and structure of business processes [18]. Table 1 summarizes select studies on EDI implementation.

This paper considers three aspects of EDI implementation: (1) integration; (2) utilization; and (3) diversity. Integration is the extent to which data could be directly entered into internal applications without additional preprocessing. The concept is similar to internal diffusion and EDI depth, suggested by Premkumar and Ramamurthy [29] and Massetti and Zmud [22]. This indicates the extent to which a firm’s business is intertwined with those of its trading partners. Effectiveness and efficiency of operations are improved when information collected through EDI is integrated with internal IS applications. Higher-level system integration improves customer service and improves inter-firm relationships. EDI must be integrated with internal applications, such as payment systems, payable/receivable systems, and production planning systems, for documents to be automatically sent, received, and processed by applications.

Utilization represents the extent to which the organization processes its business transactions electronically. Extensive utilization of EDI indicates widespread transfer of the technology to other application systems in the organization and the comprehensive support of higher-level aspects of organizational work. High transaction volume is necessary to reduce administrative costs, so that the high set-up cost is justified. Furthermore, unless EDI volume reaches a sufficient level, the change in an organization’s business process does not provide significant benefits [40].

EDI diversity refers to the number of different types of standardized business documents that are handled through EDI. This is based on the facet diversity of Massetti and Zmud. EDI diversity indicates the spread of the use of EDI in an organization, since each transaction set demands a separate system component. Communication of business documents from diverse functions of trading partners can be supported by using distinct EDI document types. Furthermore, a given document is accepted for diverse application functions by different external partners when it has diverse formats.

Other variables such as external connectivity, breadth, and externalities [44] can be used to assess the extent of expansion to trading partners. It is difficult to measure the proportion of total customers linked by EDI. Respondents to the questionnaire had problems in understanding ‘‘total trading partners that could be electronically linked via EDI’’ (as found when analyzing the results of the preliminary questionnaire). Furthermore, the extent of utilization is considered to be proportional to external connectivity and breadth. As new connections with different trading partners are developed, companies increasingly replace interorganizational commerce, such as fax, post-mail, phone, and e-mail, with EDI. Hence, the study requested material on utilization instead of these variables.

## 3. Research hypotheses

A research model was developed, as shown in Fig. 1, based on a review of the literature on organizational innovation-implementation and EDI adoption. When partnership attributes support the use of EDI in organizations, implementation success is more likely.

Partnership is the strategic relationship between independent firms that strives for mutual benefits and yet acknowledges a high level of mutual interdependence [24]. It can allow a firm access to new technologies, markets, and complementary skills. Economy of scale is possible from an appropriate partnership in joint research and development [28].

Research on strategic alliances has addressed the factors that are associated with partnership success (e.g., Mohr and Spekman [24]) or interorganizational cooperation that leverages IS. Explaining the relationship between organizations from a purely economic point of view is quite limited, because interorganizational relationships evolve through social learning experiences as partners interact. Power-political theory or social-exchange theory can be applied to explain the strategic partner relationship in such fields as IS outsourcing [21] and the use of IOS [14,15]. Successful partnerships tend to show some behavioral characteristics (e.g., commitment and trust) that help guide the flow and the complex interchange of information between partners. The existence of these attributes indicates a willingness to work for the continuation of the relationship.

Selection of partners with such attributes is especially important in IOS, as their implementation demands technological and administrative adaptations and social-learning. In order for IOS to increase the profitability of a company, it must be widely adopted by the trading partners of the company that created it. A high level of customer involvement may be possible and this is one of the key enabling factors in exploiting strategic advantages [36]. The adoption of customeroriented strategic systems is affected by customer awareness of need and support. Adjustments in organizational practices and procedures are possible only when trading partners support appropriate resources, because of the interorganizational nature of the system. Companies commonly demand ‘‘confidence’’ in their partner’s performance.

Partnership attributes such as power, interdependence, commitment and trust among trading partners, value added networks (VAN) are important for EDI implementation. Power is affected by partnership interdependence. Some studies have shown that customers have utilized their power to encourage suppliers to use EDI (e.g., [37]). Hart and Saunders found that power was negatively related to the volume of EDI transactions.

## 3.1. Partner trust

The literature on trust supports the contribution of trust to the interorganizational relation (IOR). The belief that a party will perform its obligation in the relation enables successful management of stress and dispute. The trust is highly associated with the desires of firms to collaborate [31] and the trust lowers stress and improves the adaptability [45]. Also, information exchange is facilitated and the effectiveness of joint problem solving is improved by trust [47]. Once trust is established, the joint efforts result in better outcomes than that resulting from the efforts of one firm alone [1].

High trust ensures that the trading partners do not violate the agreement and reduces the vulnerability of the system as the number of partners increases. This is especially important for EFT systems. The sender of transactions has to rely on the recipient to have sufficient integrity and security controls in place to guarantee that transmission is not altered once received. So it is hypothesized that high trust is associated with high EDI performance through its effect on EDI implementation.

Hypotheses 1-1. Partner trust is positively related to EDI integration.

Hypotheses 1-2. Partner trust is positively related to EDI utilization.

Hypotheses 1-3. Partner trust is positively related to EDI diversity.

Hypotheses 1-4. Partner trust increases EDI performance through its effect on the implementation of EDI.

## 3.2. Partner interdependence

When entering into a partnership, all partners acknowledge a high level of mutual interdependence. This allows a firm to access new technologies, markets and skills. The formation of interorganizational partnerships is motivated to achieve goals that each firm, acting alone, could not attain easily.

Successful partnership is possible only when there are mutual benefits from the relationships. The system design decisions faced by one company affect the implementation cost of its trading partners. Unless two companies feel that their relations are so interdependent that operational savings and benefits can be possible from the IOS, they are unlikely to bear the cost in implementing EDI.

Hypotheses 2-1. Partner interdependence is positively related to EDI integration.

Hypotheses 2-2. Partner interdependence is positively related to EDI utilization.

Hypotheses 2-3. Partner interdependence is positively related to EDI diversity.

Hypotheses 2-4. Partner interdependence increases EDI performance through its effect on the implemen tation of EDI.

## 3.3. Partner commitment

Other important antecedents for promoting EDI implementation are partner commitment to the trading relationship. Commitment represents the willingness of trading partners to make an effort towards the relationship. EDI requires a richer, more cooperative relationship [13]. EDI adopters working in concert with their trading partners can provide better service to customers (or suppliers), thereby increasing their market share.

Maintaining a cooperative partnership requires a strong commitment. Coordinated actions based on partner commitment should be exerted to attain mutual objectives in successful working relationships [1]. The confidence that the affiliation will endure is central to committed relationships [8]. Partner commitment is necessary in making user departments become familiar with the technology and changing interorganizational work processes. Companies may effectively utilize an innovation after learning and adapting to the new technology gradually through communication channels, joint-problem solving, training, team-work, and value analysis across organizational boundaries. The modification of current IS applications to interface with the EDI technology and to establish procedures and adjustments in interorganizational process details with trading partners significantly requires committed partner efforts [30]. The implementation of EDI demands integration of incompatible IS infrastructures and business processes and companies need to help trading partners gain appropriate expertise by providing some training and education. Training for the new technology and rules and procedures are needed while expanding the number of trading partners and the transaction sets. Hence, partner commitment, i.e. the willingness of trading partners to exert the effort necessary to enhance relations, is positively related to EDI implementation.

Hypotheses 3-1. Partner commitment is positively related to EDI integration.

Hypotheses 3-2. Partner commitment is positively related to EDI utilization.

Hypotheses 3-3. Partner commitment is positively related to EDI diversity.

Hypotheses 3-4. Partner commitment increases EDI performance through its effect on the implementation of EDI.

## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Research design

A field survey was used to investigate the research questions and to test the hypotheses. The survey instruments were pre-tested through direct interviews with EDI practitioners in 10 companies and with four IS professors. Some items were modified, and others were added to these, scales based on the pilot test responses.

A structured interview was used as the main data collection method. Participation was first solicited from the contact person over the phone. The data used in validating the research model was gathered as part of a larger investigation concerning EDI controls [20]. Respondent organizations were sampled from industries that have implemented EDI comprehensively and from publicly available company databases (through the Chollian Network by DACOM). 110 companies were selected among these diverse groups. All of the firms agreed to participate in the survey and were included in the final sample.

The respondents were members of EDI staffs or managers who had been contacted over the phone. The respondents occupied the roles of EDI management and this made them knowledgeable about the pertinent issues. They took the questions to their colleagues in user or IS departments if they lacked sufficient knowledge and were willing to communicate with the researchers. As most of the interviewees were at the EDI manager level and were involved in EDI implementation and management, their views were believed to be representative. The unit of analysis was the individual organization that has adopted EDI.

Eight companies refused to participate. The response rate was therefore 92%. This was high because participation was solicited through a direct call to EDI managers and the objectives were explained. A comparative analysis of industry membership and revenues was conducted in order to see if responding firms have significantly different characteristics from non-respondents. No significant differences were found, supporting the concept that response bias is not a concern in this study.

The measures for implementation were adapted from EDI adoption and implementation studies. The items of implementation include integration, utilization, and diversity. The integration and utilization for each of the five application systems was first assessed and the average value was used. The level of integration was obtained using a seven point Likerttype scale that asks the extent to which EDI data can be directly processed within applications without human intervention. The measure of utilization represents the proportion of processing through EDI in the five applications. For instance, if a company uses fax or e-mail 30% of the time in sending invoices to their customers, and utilizes EDI the remaining 70% of time, the utilization is 70% in the task of sending invoices. Diversity is measured using a single item and the number of different types of ED documents.

The measure for performance is based on various EDI survey results [2,3,16] and EDI literature [17,38]. The performance items were measured using a seven point Likert-type scale. Respondents answered the extent to which they agree or disagree with each statement. The objectives of EDI usage that have been considered by EDI adopters were used to compose the measures of perceived EDI performance. Improved customer service, cost reduction and reliability of information, and the reinforcement of ties with a business partner are the most critical organizational impacts of EDI implementation.

## 5. Results

## 5.1. Validity and reliability of measures

Content validity was established through the adoption of constructs that have been used in former studies and through a pilot test with EDI practitioners and IS researchers. Convergent validity tests were performed to see if all the items measuring a construct clustered together and formed a single construct. Discriminant validity concerns the degree to which a concept differs from others, this is indicated by a low correlation with measures from which they should conceptually and theoretically differ. The convergent and discriminant validity testing can be done using principal component analysis. Analysis was performed on the items that measured the partnership attributes, and on the items for EDI implementation and performance. Table 8 shows the results of factor analysis. Items with factor loading values lower than 0.5 were abandoned from further analysis. One item for the partnership trust was dropped as it had factor loadings lower than 0.5. All the factor loadings of the final items were larger than 0.5, which indicates the unidimensionality of the items. The items converged on appropriate variables, as originally envisaged as a result of exploratory factor analysis on the items. The results generally show that each item loaded higher on its associated construct than any other variable; this represents convergent and discriminant validity.

## 5.2. Test of hypotheses

Prior to testing the research model, the possible presence of multicollinearity was tested. The variance inflation factors (VIF) were examined for each independent variable. VIF is defined as the reciprocal of the tolerance where $R _ { i } { } ^ { 2 }$ is the multiple correlation coefficient when the ith independent variable is predicted from the other independent variables

$$
\mathrm{VIF} _ {i} = \frac {1}{1 - R _ {i} ^ {2}}
$$

The VIFs for the independent variables range from 1.37 to 2.29 and are well below the acceptable value of 10 suggested by Netter et al. [26]. This suggests that multicollinearity is not a concern.

While regression analysis can be used for testing the research hypotheses, a better test of direct and indirect causal effect can be conducted through path analysis, which validates the research model by estimating the magnitude of the linkage between variables, it can examine a causal link rather than showing just an empirical association among variables to characterize real-world processes. Information about the underlying causal processes can be provided by examining the direct and indirect effects of the variables in the model. This helps the development of theory [7]. The causal path is represented in Fig. 2. The observed variables are enclosed in square parentheses. A oneway path between variables indicates a hypothesized direct effect of one variable upon another.

The path analysis was performed using LISREL. The variables were not measured in a temporal sequence.

In Table 9, the results of path analysis are summarized. The path coefficients from partner trust, interdependence, and commitment to EDI integration and utilization are significant. The indirect effects of partner trust, interdependence, and commitment on performance through their effects on implementation are also significant. These results generally show that partnership attributes are important determinants of EDI implementation, especially integration and utilization, and EDI performance.

Interorganizational factors, such as dependence, power, and transaction climate within electronic dyad are important for the implementation of EDI [29,14,15]; customer commitment and trust in a customer-supplier relationship affects volume of transactions and diversity of EDI transaction sets. Commitment of the trading partners to interorganizational relations is possible from the relative advantage perceived from the technology. Since they feel that the system is beneficial and compatible with their organization, they participate in system development and faithfully devote their time and resources to the system. Furthermore, the maintenance of relations will be mutually beneficial only when each of the parties exerts a high degree of interdependence and commitment. These EDI adopters will voluntarily initiate the implementation and show a more proactive attitude and develop integrated projects with internal applications [6].

Interorganizational interdependence enhances the need to further EDI systems; companies fail to perceive the benefits that the technology should offer to their business operations when their business is not dependent on trading partners. Companies that have partner commitment can receive technical subsidies, such as training and on-site assistance during the development and maintenance of the systems. This will induce the participation of trading partners in the development process and make the system more acceptable to everyone as well as increasing the perceived relative advantage.

Diversity of EDI implementation is not significantly affected by partner attributes. This is partially explained by the notions of Massetti and Zmud that increasing diversity is compounded by other social and technological issues. In order to increase diversity, companies convert transaction processes with diverse trading partners that have proprietary document standards. Companies striving to expand the range of their EDI transaction sets should consider internal document conversion issues in transaction processing applications to generate, transmit, receive, and process EDI transactions. Thus, the short span between initial EDI adoption and data collection of the study partially explains the low number of transaction sets and the relatively weak effect of partner attributes on diversity.

The insignificant effect of partnership attributes on diversity of EDI may be due to the critical role of VAN in implementing EDI documents. EDI message originators transmit data to the VAN in format, protocol, and communications speed that are convenient. As the VAN supports the conversion of data, firms do not have to be encumbered by the format or requirements for the specific processing environment of trading partners. The heavy reliance on a VAN or influential trading partners may lead to the reactive attitude of EDI adopters. VAN service may also help EDI adopters overcome incompatibility problems of technological platforms or message standards and make them concentrate on their own business practices when making new electronic document types. The VAN can help companies come to an agreement on such fundamental issues as message standards, communication protocols, and operation procedures to implement the technology. Hence, diversity of EDI may be less subject to partner relationships, as organizations simply rely on the service provided by VAN to develop additional document type.

The significant coefficients of the paths from integration and utilization to performance indicate that integration and utilization are important determinants of EDI performance. These significant relationships between implementation and performance lead to indirect effects of partnership attributes on performance. EDI should be integrated and utilized in a comprehensive manner to facilitate the widespread transfer of the technology to other departments in the organization.

The path from diversity to performance is not significant. EDI performance depends on the extent of integration and utilization of the system rather than the diversity, i.e. the extent to which different types of a firm’s business documents are handled through EDI. This indicates that integration and utilization appear more beneficial than developing different document types to expand system usage. EDI adopters have to establish proprietary standards and expand their connections with trading partners in order to increase diversity. This demands substantial support from trading partners. It is easier and more cost-effective for companies to expand integration and utilization than diversity through support from internal departments.

## 6. Conclusions and implications

This study is based on innovation-implementation, and technology innovation theories applied to EDI. The main purpose was to study how partnership attributes affect EDI implementation and performance. The results of the testing hypotheses are given in Table 10.

The results of this study have significant implications for EDI practitioners. Companies that plan to adopt EDI can decide whether their partner relationship is appropriate for EDI implementation. For instance, if these internal work processes are nonroutine, they may need efforts to restructure the process to make it amenable to standardized rules and operations, and consequently more routine and repetitive. If the trading partners do not have commitment in their relations, they may provide various promotion strategies or educational services in order to increase the perceived relative advantage of the system and encourage their participation in the implementation.

It is much easier for companies to expand links with trading partners and increase the extent of integration and utilization when they have a good partner relationship. Integration of EDI information in internal applications may create major changes in the job descriptions and result in encountering resistance in the diffusion of EDI.

Respondents answer the extent to which they agree or disagree with each statement about variables. The seven-point Likert type scales are used except items for utilization and diversity.

## A.1. Partnership attributes

(1) Partner trust

(1) Our company highly trusts the representative trading partner while processing EDI tasks.

(2) Our company expects a fair deal from the representative trading partners’.

(3) Our company has been doing business with trading partners without conflict and dispute.

(4) Our company and trading partners attain organizational objectives well from the interorganizational business.

(2) Partner interdependence

(1) The possibility of changing the representative trading partner with others by our company is high.

(2) The possibility of changing our company with others by the representative trading partner is high.

(3) Partner commitment

(1) Our company strongly wants to continue the relationship with the representative trading partner.

(2) The representative trading partner strongly wants to continue the relationship with our company.

## A.2. EDI implementation

## (1) Integration

The extent to which EDI data can be directly processed within five applications (selected by respondents) without human intervention.

(2) Utilization

The proportion (%) that a company uses EDI processing. These can be processed using other complementary means. It is measured as the proportion that a firm’s information exchange and processing are handled through EDI in five applications (selected by respondents).

(3) Diversity

The number of distinct EDI document types.

## A.3. Performance

(1) Relations with the representative trading partner are greatly improved through reduced response time after adopting EDI.

(2) Our company maintains improved relations with the representative trading partner by reducing delay from errors.

(3) Our company improved trust in relations with the representative trading partner by enhancing confidentiality of documents.

(4) Relations with the representative trading partner are greatly improved by reducing omission and inaccurate transmission.

(5) Our company maintains high trust with the representative trading partner by protecting messages from disclosure to unauthorized third party.

(6) The efficiency of interdepartmental transaction processing is greatly increased.

(7) Accuracy is greatly improved by reduced paperwork.

(8) Transaction processing costs are greatly reduce after adopting EDI.
