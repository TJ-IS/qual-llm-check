## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Media coverage Consumer engagement Movie sales Panel vector auto-regression

## A B S T R A C T

The advent of new media, such as Google, Twitter, and Facebook, has ofered the movie industry new opportunities to market movies and increase sales. In this study, we investigate the relationship between online media activities and movie sales. In particular, this paper examines how media coverage (online news and social media posts) and consumer engagement (information search and reading) afect the sales of original movies and sequels in the opening week. By applying the panel vector auto-regression (PVAR) method, we found that online news, social media posts, and information reading were positively related to future sales of originals and sequels, while information search was not a significant predictor of movie sales for originals. Media coverage had a stronger and more durable marketing efect on the sales of originals, and consumer engagement had a stronger efect on the sales of sequels. Compared with social media posts, online news had a higher promoting efect on consumer engagement for sequels. Further, online media coverage and consumer engagement also had mutual efects on each other. This study suggests that managers of movie studios and theaters should apply diferent channel strategies and allocate diferent resources for originals and sequels.

## 1. Introduction

The film industry makes a major contribution to the global economy and culture [1,2]. According to comScore.com, the worldwide box office grossed more than \$40 billion in 2018 for the first time and has been growing since then, even though the uncertainty faced by movie studios in producing films is high. It is dificult to capture consumer attention given a movie's short window so that movie makers may not be able to collect revenues in a short period of time [1–4].

Movie studios have to make careful decisions about what movies to invest in. Sequel movies, which are made based on existing movies, novels, or TV shows (originals), are believed to reduce the risk of movie production and increase movie sales because sequels may have a good existing consumer base that is familiar with the originals [5]. For ex ample, nine of the top 10 global-grossing movies in 2017 were sequel or remakes [6].

In addition, after movies are produced, movie studios and theaters have to effectively utilize various marketing channels (both traditional media, such as TV and newspaper, and new media, such as internet and social media) to run week-long or month-long campaigns before a movie's public premiere. The global spending on advertisement of movies was about \$2.8 billion in 2018 [2], which was about 7% of the total movie sales in the same year (\$40 billion). Movie studios have to make efective decisions on how to allocate advertising expenditure among diferent media channels [1–3].

The global success of the movie industry also suggests that consumers seem to have unprecedented confidence in shopping for movies even though a move is considered an experience product with uncertainties and risks ([7,8]; Elberse and Eliashberg 2003). The literature in computer science, information systems, and marketing suggests that the advent of new media, such as internet and social media including Google, Twitter, and Facebook [4,7,9,10], has provided universal access for movie studios, theaters, and consumers to actively engage in online media, such as generating, collecting, and sharing information and opinions about movies before consumers decide to go to theaters. These new media channels are found to be efective for movie studios and theaters to increase movie sales [4,7,9–12].

Online media coverage has become an influential and/or reputable source for movie studios and theaters to advertise movies and difuse information and opinions about movies (such as word-of-mouth (WOM)) [4,7,8,13]. An examination of how diferent types of online media coverage (such as internet search engine and social media) im pact movie sales is necessary and timely. In addition, when consumers seek movie information through online media, they demonstrate diferent participation behaviors. Consumer engagement that includes a consumer's diferent interactions (such as searching, reading, reviewing, and sharing) has important implications for movie studios and theaters to increase movie sales from better understanding of consumer engagement behaviors [14], which may represent more prevalent online consumer activities than writing online reviews or blogs. Actual consumer engagement behaviors may be more likely to reflect consumers' interests and preference for movies.

To help movie studios and theaters make efective decisions in allocating resources among diferent online media and understand how consumer engagement afects movie sales, this paper aims to investigate the impacts of online media and consumer engagement on the sales of original movies and sequels in the opening week. It is important to focus on the opening week because a movie could not collect enough revenue if it is not successful in the opening week, as prior research suggested that about 41% of the revenue of a movie was generated in the opening week [4].

Our research questions are

What are the relative impacts of online media coverage and con sumer engagement on sales between original movies and sequel movies?

How do these efects vary over time in the opening week?

• How do online media coverage and consumer engagement react to one another?

To address these questions, we collected movie data from China. Specifically, movie information was collected from Maoyan.com, which provides basic movie information and collects day-to-day box ofice data. Data about media coverage and consumer engagement were collected from Baidu.com and Sougou.com, both of which are Chinese search engines that provide summaries of user search queries and other online consumer activities, similar to what Google Trends provides.

Our empirical analysis is a dynamic system model, i.e., the panel vector autoregressive (PVAR) method. PVAR treats all key variables as jointly endogenous and examines unobserved individual heterogeneity. This is especially important to address the concern about the bidirec tional causality between movie sales and other variables [7,12]. In addition, the model allows for lagged efects so that we can examine the dynamic interactions between all key variables. A high-frequency daily panel data collected from the open week of the movie is able to reveal immediate and dynamic responses of movie sales to other variables. These features enable us to reveal findings that have not been revealed in prior studies which are based on low-frequency data, such as the opening-day or opening-week gross movie sales [3,4,15].

The main research findings are as follows. First, we found that online media coverage had a higher positive efect on the sales of originals, while the sales of sequels was more influenced by consumer engagement. Regarding the two types of media coverage, online news had a stronger impact on originals while social media had a stronger efect on sequels. The efects of the two types of consumer engagement (information search and information reading) were also diferent. Second, movie sales (both originals and sequels) reacted diferently to online media coverage and consumer engagement as a function of time. That is, online media coverage and consumer engagement had diferent time-varying efects on the sales of both originals and sequels. Third, online media coverage and consumer engagement responded difer ently to one another as a function of time.

The contributions of this study are two-fold. Conceptually, we in tegrate online media coverage and consumer engagement into one research model to compare the relative strengths of their impacts on movie sales, as well as the interactions among these variables. We also reveal how these efects are diferentiated between originals and sequels. Methodologically, this is one of the first studies to apply the

## 2. Literature review

Prior research finds that new media platforms have significant im pacts on movie sales [4,7,9–12]. There are generally two streams of studies in the prior literature. The first stream is focused on online media coverage, which examines how movie sales is influenced by online media, e.g., online news and reports published by mass media agencies, as well as expert and third-party reviews published on social media. The second stream examines consumer engagement as a mediating factor between media coverage and movie sales [7,8]. This literature focuses on how movie consumers participate in online news platforms and social media and the subsequent impact of consumer participation on movie sales [7,8,16].

In this section, we review the relevant literature to develop a research framework as shown in Fig. 1. We summarize the relevant literature in Table 1 and highlight how our study complements prior studies.

## 2.1. Media coverage

Media plays an important role in difusing information to consumers in the internet era. Information typically originates from media outlets, experts, and key opinion leaders in the publications of online news, critical reviews, and social media posts. In this study, we categorize online media coverage into online news (the number of online news posts about a movie retrieved by a search engine) and social media posts (the number of posts of media outlets on a social media platform). Online news is generated by oficial media outlets and institutional websites that follow a top-down approach, similar to web 1.0 websites [21]. The information available on online news websites is mediated and influenced by conventional media outlets, journalists, and editors and then reedited and rereleased by other competing websites shortly after appearing on the first website.

Conversely, rising social media such as Twitter and Facebook promotes the diffusion of information from both official and unofficial resources, which can break the leading role of the traditional media in creating and distributing news. Social media provides an information distribution platform for individual- and expert-generated contents, which follows a bottom-up style of communication and promotes peerto-peer conversations and sharing. Not just simply quoting from elite media sources, social media provides the access to the news and information generated by individuals or news agencies. This type of information may not be subject to journalistic norms but provides the content that is similar to online news. Therefore, social media coverage is more readable and easier to disseminate than online news.

Online news influences the sale of movies. Zhang and Skiena [22] used online news data generated by a high-performance text processing system to predict movie grosses. They found that news frequency and sentiment were highly correlated with movies' total grosses. In addition, online reviews also have a significant influence on movie sales. Kim et al. [19] found that the valence of expert reviews had a significant impact on movie sales in the U.S. domestic market, while the frequency of expert reviews had no significant efect. Chen et al. [11] observed that third-party movie reviews had a dynamic efect on the value of the firm that owned movie studios but they did not diferentiate online news and expert reviews.

Accordingly, we expect that media coverage, i.e., online news in mass media and posts in social media, impacts movie sales. In addition, the two-step theory suggests that information first flows from conventional mass media agencies to key opinions leaders and then to imitators [23]. Therefore, we also expect an interaction between online news and social media posts, which has not been revealed in the prior literature.

## 2.2. Consumer engagement

Consumer engagement describes a consumer's exposure to, involvement in, and interactions with products or services [24]. There are generally three levels of engagement: consumption, contribution, and creation [25]. Several studies have explored the influence of consumer engagement on movie revenue [7,8,16]. Duan et al. [7] found a sig nificant impact of consumers' online posts on movie revenue. Oh et al. [16] found that participating activities of Facebook and YouTube consumers, not those of Twitter users, had significant efect on box ofice revenue in the opening week. Ding et al. [2] revealed the sig nificant impact of clicking the ‘like’ button on social media on box ofice sales.

Diferent from previous studies of the efect of word-of-mouth on movies, which is a type of creation, we focus on the efect of con sumption. Consumption is the most frequently used measure of consumer engagement, such as a consumer's participation in online communities or social networks (searching, reading, watching, and following products or services) [26]. Similar to Ding et al. [2], we categorize consumer engagement as social metric and non-social metric. Information search is a non-social metric, which means the number of searches of a movie through internet search engines. Information reading is a social metric, referring to the number of readings of social media posts about a movie.

Scholars suggest consumer search as a predictor of movie sales, and movie studios can use consumer search to monitor consumer interest [9]. In this study, we consider information reading as a new social metric to evaluate movie performance because reading, rather than searching, movie information better indicates consumer interests. In formation reading involves clicking and sharing information and is a stronger indicator than information search to inflect consumers' decisions to go watch movies. However, this factor has not received suficient attention in previous studies.

We posit consumers' perceived risk may be the reason that drives the number of searches for information [27]. Consumers will read, explore, and examine the information received from their searches in order to reduce their perceived risk about the quality of a movie. For example, consumers develop an understanding of products' quality from reading reviews written by peer users and experts [28]. We posit that information reading of online news and social media posts can reduce the consumption risk and lead to movie sales. Overall, we expect that consumer engagement, i.e., information search and information reading, afects movie sales.

## 2.3. Originals and sequels

Sequels are movies that are produced from novels, comics, previous movies, or television programs [19]. Movie studios choose to make sequels that may have a good existing audience base who might have watched the predecessors of the sequels so that continuous and subsequent revenues can be generated [5]. Previous researchers have investigated how sequels interacted with traditional marketing activities such as advertising. Basuroy et al. [1] revealed that advertising had a positive efect on the opening-week gross box ofice of sequels. Kim and Bruce [6] argued that there existed diferences between sequels and originals in terms of the efectiveness of advertising. Compared with originals, sequels required higher financial investments because of more expensive budgets [1].

In the current study, we posit that media coverage may exhibit diferent impacts on originals and sequels because movie producers may allocate diferent budgets among diferent media and platforms. In addition, the uncertainty of the quality of a movie and the degree of movie information exposed on diferent media may impact consumer engagement. Prior studies found that originals and sequels received diferent ratings [17], and that WOM had a higher efect on non-sequels than on sequels [4]. Thus, we expect that media coverage and consumer engagement have diferent impacts between originals and sequels.

## 2.4. Panel vector auto-regression method

We employ the PVAR approach to examine the relationships among media coverage, consumer engagement, movie sales, and the two types of movies. Widely applied in the economic literature [29], PVAR is a dynamic system model that examines the efects of time-varying interactions among variables. In the information systems literature, Dewan and Ramaprasad [12] conducted one of the first studies adopting PVAR to examine the relationships among new media, traditional media, and music sales. Diferent from the traditional regression approach, PVAR has the following advantages. First, PVAR considers all variables as endogenous and is suitable for situations where the relationships between variables are uncertain because of limited theoretical guidance to support the relationships [12]. This may be especially beneficial to empirical studies of movie sales because it is dificult to tackle the complex relationships among antecedent factors and movie sales. The causal relationships could be in both directions where the antecedent factors are also influenced by prior movie sales $[ 7 , 8 ]$ . PVAR allows us to examine all variables in a holistic manner to see the joint efect of both media coverage and consumer engagement on movie sales, similar to the exploratory study of Dewan and Ramaprasad [12]. Second, the method is not only able to capture the lagged efects of online media coverage and consumer engagement on movie sales but also reveals the trend in the variation of how one variable reacts to the shocks incurred by other variables. Therefore, we are able to reveal the time-varying efects of media coverage and consumer engagement on movie sales. Third, using the PVAR approach, we can understand the dynamic relationships between all the related variables and identify how these factors interact to one another.

## 3. Research method

We tested the research framework in the context of the Chinese movie market. First, movies are popular in China. The supply of screens in China keeps increasing, and China became the country with the most screens in the world in 2016. The gross box ofice in China was over RMB 55.9 billion in 2017. Second, the number of Chinese Internet users reached 772 million, and the number of search engine users was 640 million in $2 0 1 7 . ^ { 1 }$ Chinese consumers are able to engage with movie information on multiple channels such as search engines and social media. Baidu is the biggest search engine in China with over 148 mil lion users and holds a 76.05% market share in China's search engine market (Baidu Inc. 2018). Baidu is also a provider of online news feeds and connects with > 500 authoritative news agencies and websites. In addition, WeChat is the biggest social media platform in China with over 1.09 billon active users on a daily basis (Tencent Inc. 2019). A user can register a regular account to start a personal social space and reg ister a WeChat oficial account (WOA) to post and push feeds or posts. Opinion leaders and oficial media outlets also use WeChat to deliver opinions and information to their subscribers who can read and share the contents to their friends. Baidu and WeChat are the main platforms for media outlets and consumers to distribute and consume information. Therefore, both platforms can represent all media platforms in China.

## 3.1. Data collection

Data were collected from three sources from April 2017 to December 2017. Titles, features, release time, and sales of movies released into the Chinese market were collected from piaofang.maoyan. com, a public platform of movie data. A total of 170 movies were first collected from the site.

Consumer online activities about these movies were collected from Baidu Index (index.baidu.com) and Sougou Index (zhishu.sogou.com). Baidu Index provides data about users' search behavior and online news in Baidu. Similar to Google Trends, Baidu Index provides summaries about the popularity of keywords used by consumers based on the raw data of search behaviors of consumers. Similarly, Sougou Index provides WOA data including users pushing and reading data on WeChat.

Before collecting data from Baidu Index and Sougou Index, we defined a list of keywords that might be used by the media and consumers as the title of a movie when the movie was covered by the media or searched and read by the users. For example, for a movie with a long title such as Pirates of the Caribbean 5: Death without Proof, users might have used abbreviations such as Pirates of the Caribbean 5 or Death without Proof. After developing our comprehensive list of keywords, we collected data related to media coverage and consumer engagement on Baidu Index and Sougou Index during the opening week of a movie. Movies without online data in these two indices were excluded from the final sample. In addition, we excluded movies with ambiguous names. For example, the movie Grab the Red was ambiguous with the activity Grab a Red Envelope.

Finally, we obtained a panel dataset of 133 movies on a daily basis during the opening week of these movies. The sample size was similar to those reported in previous studies $^ { [ 6 , 1 6 , 2 0 ] }$ . Table 2 shows the summary of these movies, including 71 originals and 62 sequels.

## 3.2. Measures

Movie sales was retrieved from piaofang.maoyan.com. We used the box ofice revenue of movie i on day t as the indicator of the sales of the movie $( B O _ { i t } )$ . Regarding online media coverage, we used the data provided by Baidu Index and measured online news (News ) as the total number of online news about movie i on day t. Online posts on social media (Wechat ) were reported by Sogou Index and measured as the total number of arti cles published by WOA for movie i on day t. For consumer engagement, we measured information search (Search ) provided by Baidu Index of movie i on day t as the volume of daily searches. We also used the WeChat measure as proxy of consumer engagement, i.e., we measured information reading (Read ) as the total number of reads of all WOA articles for movie i on day t reported by Sougou Index.

We included several control variables, including the number of days by day t after movie i was released $( A g e _ { i t } ) ,$ , the number of movie i goers in prescreen on day $: ( P e o p l e _ { i t } ) _ { : }$ , and whether day t was Friday, Saturday, or Sunday $( W e e k e n d _ { i t } )$ when movie i was shown. Prior studies considered several other exogenous variables such as studio, budget, movie genres, director and actor power [4,15] that might afect movie sales. However, because these sectional data do not vary over time, we eliminated these variables. That is, these movie-specific efects were controlled in our model indirectly because of the panel nature of the data [10].

## 4.2. Step 2: Forecast Error Variance Decomposition (FEVD)

FEVD examines the contribution of each variable on the right side of the above equation to the ones on the left side. We examined the contribution of media coverage and consumer engagement to movie sales. FEVD reports statistics that are similar to $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ to demonstrate the relative dynamic power of endogenous variables in explaining movie sales. We applied FEVD to the PVAR model for originals and sequels separately. Based on the FEVD results, we were able to assess how media coverage and consumer engagement contributed to movie sales diferently.

## 4.3. Step 3: Orthogonalized Impulse Response Function (OIRF)

We also examined OIRFs based on the parameters of the PVAR model. IRFs show the reactions of movie sales to an exogenous shock (i.e., a one standard deviation shock) in another variable (media coverage or consumer engagement) in PVAR. OIRFs are based on Cholesky decomposition and request that orthogonalized IRFs be estimated. We obtained standard errors at 5% on each side generated by Monte Carlo simulations with 500 repetitions. Because of a movie's short period to collect revenue, we focused on the dynamic relationship between media coverage, consumer engagement, and movie sales in the opening week. Based on the results of IRFs, we analyzed significant period, wear in, and wear out of each re sponse. Significant period means how the reaction of one variable to a shock in another variable varies over time from significance (insignificance) to insignificance (significance). Wear-in indicates how long it takes to reach the peak of the response trend. Wear-out means the response trend becomes weaker after reaching the peak.

## 5. Results

First, we applied the Levin-Lin-Chu (LLC) test to conduct the unit root test on the panel data [32]. All the results were significant $( p ~ < ~ 0 . 0 1 )$ , indicating that the panel data did not contain unit roots and that all the variables were stationary. These results supported us to analyze a full dynamic system through PVAR. Next, we examined the dynamic relationships between media coverage, consumer engagement, and movie sales for originals and sequels respectively through FEVD and OIRF.

## 5.1. FEVD result

We first analyzed all the movies without splitting them into originals and sequels. The results showed that there was no response of movie sales to either media coverage or consumer engagement respectively. Therefore, we spilt the sample into the two groups.

As shown in Table 3, for original movies, both online media coverage and consumer engagement had significant explanatory powers on movie sales in the opening week. Online media coverage (Total: > 39%) had a stronger explanatory power on the variance of movie sales than did consumer engagement (Total: about 8%), suggesting that online media coverage played the dominant role. Considering the two types of media coverage, social media posts (WeChat: > 35%) had a stronger explanatory power than did online news (News: < 8%). Specifically, WeChat accounted for 43.25% of the variance of movie sales in day 4, exceeding movie sales' own explanatory power (41.55%). On the consumer engagement side, information reading (Read: about 7%) accounted for more variance than did information search (Search: < 1%). Among all four variables, social media posts (WeChat: > 35%) had the highest explanatory power, followed by information reading (Read: about 7%), and online news (News: < 7.2%). Information search barely explained any variance.

For sequel movies, both media coverage (Total: < 23%) and con sumer engagement (Total: > 41%) explained the variance of movie sales in the opening week, while consumer engagement played the dominant role. Online news (> 14%) had a larger efect on movie sales than did social media posts (WeChat: < 8%) except for day 2. Regarding the two types of consumer engagement, information reading (Read: > 32%) had a stronger explanatory power than did information search (Search: about 9%). Overall, all four variables had explanatory power on movie sales, in the order of information reading, online news, information search, and social media posts.

Comparing originals with sequels, a few interesting findings emerged. While the variance explained for originals was dominated by media coverage (Total: about 40%–50%), consumer engagement (Total: about 41%–43%) played the dominant role for sequels. Looking at the four variables, social media posts had a stronger impact on originals (WeChat: about 35%–44%) than on sequels (WeChat: about 6%–8%), while all the other three variables had higher power for sequels than for originals.

## 6. Discussion

With the goal of investigating the dynamic relationships between online media coverage, consumer engagement, and movie sales regarding the movie industry, we have found several interesting difer ences between original movies and sequels.

First, online media coverage and consumer engagement explained a substantial portion of the variance of movie sales. Specifically, online media coverage had a stronger explanatory power on originals, while consumer engagement had a stronger efect on sequels. In addition, information reading had a significant power on movie sales (both originals and sequels), stronger than did information search. These findings are reasonable and can be explained from the perspective of consumer learning. Because originals are new to the market, they usually have a smaller potential audience base than sequels.

In terms of sequels. consumers might have learned about these sequels from past experience with and prior knowledge of the novels and originals from which the sequels were made. Therefore, consumers are more knowledgeable before making decisions on whether going to watch sequels. In addition, regarding the efect of information reading, we believe that it is the consumer's comprehension and evaluation of the persuasiveness of online movie reviews that drives their consumption of the movie, rather than the mere efect of authority reviews as an external information source. This perspective explains why consumer engagement had a more positive impact on the sales of sequels than did media coverage and why consumer engagement played a dominant role. These findings suggest that managers need to pay more attention to consumer engagement (especially social metric) when advertising sequels.

Second, several findings have emerged in the response of movie sales to the four metric variables. Social media posts seemed to have advantages over online news in influencing originals' sales, as well as a quicker efect on the sales of sequels than did online news. However, online news had a longer efect on the sales of sequels than did social media. Because consumers usually face higher uncertainty and perceived risk for experiential products, the source and quality of the information about the product are important in order to persuade consumers to try out the product [34]. Social media posts are more accurate to push, abundant to produce, attractive to read, and better in quality and interactions than online news available via search engines. Although online news may have a better coverage, social media posts are more difused through an acquaintance network and social niche communication, which is more efective in driving consumption. These findings suggest that movie studio and theater managers should allocate diferent marketing budgets and resources across diferent online media platforms. When facing constraints in resource allocation to advertise originals, these managers should allocate more resources to the generation of social media posts. Moreover, media coverage had a lower efect and quicker wear-out on sequels than on originals, suggesting that the efectiveness of media coverage declined and changed faster for sequels, similar to the dynamic ad efectiveness on movie sales [6].

Third, diferent from information reading, information search had no significant efect on originals, and the significant efect was lower on sequels. Information search may be likely influenced by the uncertainty of the quality of the movie so that consumers want to reduce consumption risk through searching for more information. However, this risk perception may be alleviated by a wider media coverage and a deeper level of information reading. In addition, after reading the articles of WOA, consumers may share the information on social platforms or with their friends. On the other hand, simple search behavior on search engines does not possess such social attributes that may lead to social dissemination of movie information.

Fourth, there were interactions between the four metric variables. On one hand, there was a positive interaction between online media coverage and consumer engagement. Media coverage improved information search, and the impact was faster for sequels than for originals. In addition, media coverage had no significant impact on reading. However, online news had a significant efect on information reading of sequels, which indicated that online news could improve the sales of sequels through improving consumer readings. In addition, online news had higher efects on information search and information reading than social media posts did for sequels. Therefore, managers need to pay attention to the efect of media coverage on consumer engagement and adjust their allocation strategy. Our results showed that the volume of social media posts did not improve information reading so that managers should encourage media outlets, movie critics, and social media authors to produce higher quality and more attractive content. Similar to Subramani and Rajagopalan [35], we suggest that managers should collaborate with influential bloggers and critics to fill the knowledge gap of potential consumers when advertising original movies.

Finally, there was also an interaction between the two types of online media coverage. Social media posts had an immediate positive response to online news, while the opposite efect was negative. This indicated that even with diferent features and sources, online news provided the most current information to generate social media posts but not vice versa. Therefore, managers need to pay attention to the impact of online news because online news is a basis for building social media posts. Actually, with transformation and development of tradi tional media, news agencies have started to build WOAs to difuse movie information.

## 7. Conclusions, limitations and future research

Our research findings may have the following implications for research. First, we have compared the relative impacts of online media coverage and consumer engagement on movie sales and found diferent efects between originals and sequels. Future studies can continue com paring the efects of these two factors. Second, we have further examined the relative marketing eficiency of online news and social media. Their efects are also diferent between originals and sequels. We call for future studies to examine the relative efects of these diferent online media. Third, as one of the first studies to examine the efect of information reading on movie sales, we find that the efect is more important than that of information search. Additional research is necessary to investigate the efects of diferent consumer engagement behaviors.
