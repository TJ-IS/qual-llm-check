# Does IT outsourcing deliver economic value to firms?

## a b s t r a c t

The question of whether or not IT outsourcing initiatives deliver economic value to firms is an important yet under examined one. This study extends extant outsourcing literature by evaluating how firm value is created through IT outsourcing. Using Porter’s Value Chain Model as the theoretical framework, the study systematically traces and measures the value added through IT outsourcing for firms in the manufacturing and retail industries. The results indicate that the effect of IT outsourcing is better detected at the intermediate process level rather than at the firm-level. Firms are able to realize economic benefits of cost savings, but less so in efficiency. Improved cost is observed in inbound logistics and supporting activities. With the exception of inbound logistics, no efficiency improvements are observed in operating activities and outbound logistics, suggesting that the positive impact of IT outsourcing is limited.

## 1. Introduction

Instead of being tightly controlled in-house, information technology (IT), the lifeblood of contemporary business organizations is being outsourced at an unprecedented speed and scale. The Gartner Group found that the total contract value of publicly announced IT outsourcing (ITO) deals exceeded \$150 billion from 2003 through 2006. In 2008 alone the top 20 worldwide ITO contracts were worth nearly US \$20 billion (Gartner, 2008). Although the extensive investment in ITO signals that firms of all sizes view this practice as a viable value-adding strategy, few studies have rigorously evaluated the economic return of ITO and systematic evidence regarding its tangible business value remains limited (Wang et al., 2008). With IT permeating firms’ entire value chain and enabling, if not driving, the most recent transformations (Gunasekaran et al., 2008; Porter and Millar, 1985), it is imperative that firms base their sizable ITO investments on evidence rather than on assumed benefits or unwarranted beliefs. Research that appropriately assesses the business value of ITO becomes even more important when industry reports have suggested that one-fourth to one-half of ITO initiatives fail to deliver their bottom-line promises and the mimicking of peer firms has been widely cited as an important impetus of outsourcing IT (Lacity and Hirschheim, 1994; Venkatraman et al., 1994). ITO represents large and difficult-to-reverse investments and has been associated with various inherent risks and costs (Watjatrakul, 2005). This puts pressure on researchers and professionals to ensure that firms make informed and effective strategic ITO decisions and avoid the pitfalls of commitment to isomorphic behavior

Hence, the objective of this study is to examine the extent to which firm value is created through ITO initiatives. To understand the business value of ITO, managers and researchers must have in place an appropriate framework designed to systematically capture the impacts of each outsourcing endeavor on different business aspects. Consequently, this study draws on Porter’s Value Chain framework and develops a process-oriented conceptual model that (1) considers the possible impacts of different ITO initiatives on the discrete business processes in a firm’s value chain and (2) predicts how such impacts will be reflected in specific process-level accounting performance measures. The adopted process-oriented approach is important because outsourced IT is intended to support activities and processes, and therefore its impact should be assessed at the process level where its direct effects are expected to be manifested.

This research extends the current literature on the returns of ITO in two important ways. First, it provides useful empirical insights on the economic returns of ITO. By using objective, audited, and externally reported accounting performance measures, the findings of this study serve as an important corroborator of prior results that are predominantly descriptive in nature and based on self-reported perceptual measures. Second, prior research has suggested that the business value of ITO is best documented at the process level (Wang et al., 2008). But without an appropriate framework, the selection of appropriate business processes and performance measures for examination can become a hit-or-miss endeavor, leaving less obvious yet critical impacts of ITO unexamined. Porter’s Value Chain framework is well recognized and has been used in the literature to identify important business processes of value creation (Dehning et al., 2007). By applying this framework, this research provides a systematic approach to map the performance consequences of each ITO initiative onto a corresponding value process. This mapping enables us to not only thoroughly examine different aspects of ITO impacts but also identify the appropriate accounting measures that capture such impacts. It also allows for detailed analyses and subsequent monitoring of value creation through ITO and better guides firms’ IT sourcing decisions as a result. While various ITO related issues have been examined by prior studies, including some that have appeared in this journal, such as the determinants of ITo (Watiatrakul. 2005 and the conditions under which ITO can be successful (Cullen et al., 2005), empirical work that focuses on firm performance after an outsourcing initiative is relatively scarce. This study adds to extant streams of research by evaluating and documenting the economic ramifications of ITO.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. First, the work on IT business value and ITO is reviewed. Thereafter, the theoretical framework used to govern the discussion and to identify the measures considered is presented. The research methodology and the results of an empirical analysis are then reported. Finally, the strategic implications for practitioners and the directions for future researcher are outlined, followed by the conclusions and limitations of the study.

## 2. Related work

## 2.1. Business value of IT outsourcing

Given the level of expenditure and the high potential benefits and risks associated with ITO, it is vital that outsourcing decisions be supported by systematic and conclusive empirical evidence regarding their returns. However, to date only a few studies have sought to provide such evidence and it is still currently unclear whether ITO delivers tangible business value for the contract-granting firms (Han and Mithas, 2009; Jiang and Qureshi, 2006). For instance, using expected cost reduction as an indicator of ITO success, Lacity and Willcocks (1998) interviewed 145 US and UK IT managers (61 sourcing decisions) and found that only 56% achieved their cost reduction expectation, 23% did not, and 16% were unable to determine. Using stock market price as a measure for ITO outcome, Hayes et al. (2000) indicated that outsourcing is a value-added business exchange only for smaller and/or service firms. Oh et al. (2006) suggested that investors reacted more favorably to ITO announcements only when they perceived a low transactional risk.

With the limited body of research, several important gaps can be identified. First, most current studies employ an approach which seeks to establish a link between ITO and broad high-level firm performance indicators such as the overall cost objective achieved (Lacity and Willcocks, 1998), stock price changes around ITO announcements (Oh et al., 2006), or satisfaction with the overall economic benefits of ITO (Lee et al., 2004). Conclusions solely based on broad performance indicators can be elusive because these indicators can be influenced by factors other than ITO (Dehning et al., 2007). This approach is conceptually insufficient because researchers have found that the effects of ITO are better detected at the intermediate process level as outsourced information technologies are intended to target specific business processes (Wang et al., 2008). Thus a process-based approach is more appropriate. Second, performance measures adopted in prior studies are predominantly self-reported perceptual metrics such as managers’ self-reported perception of whether the outcome of their IT sourcing decisions met their expectations or objectives and their satisfaction with the achieved strategic, economic, and technological benefits (Jiang and Qureshi, 2006; Lacity and Willcocks, 1998). Recently, there has been a call for using externally reported and more objective accounting metrics in the examination of the effect of ITO on firms’ performance (Wang et al., 2008). A systematic analysis of the effects of IT outsourcing on objective accounting performance measures complements studies using self-reported perceptua measures. thereby serving as an important validation and confirmation on the return of ITO.

However, the choice of appropriate accounting-based performance measures requires extra caution and effort because accounting metrics do not directly capture the effects of IT (Dehning et al., 2009). In the literature, researchers have suggested the use of Porter’s Value Chain framework in the analysis of IT business value and the identification of corresponding accounting measures that reflect such value (Dehning et al., 2009, 2007). The following section briefly reviews the literature of IT payoff using value chain analysis.

## 2.2. Value chain analysis and IT returns

Porter’s Value Chain framework (Porter and Millar, 1985) maps out a set of discrete interrelated primary and support activities through which firms can create value. It suggests that to achieve or sustain competitive advantage it is imperative for a firm to coordinate resources and optimize activities across the value chain. Primary activities are crucial for creating competitive advantage while support activities facilitate primary activities. Primary activities include inbound logistics, operations, and outbound logistics and services while support activities include procurement, infrastructure management, human resource management, and technology development.

The value chain framework has been widely advocated and applied in various forms (e.g. simple vs. full value chain) to evaluate the business value of IT. A prime advantage of this framework is that it allows researchers to map the impacts that various technologies or management practices have on each of the value adding activities of a firm. This in turn permits the identification of appropriate accounting/economic measures that are able to reflect these impacts. For example, Anandarajan and Anandarajan (1998) make use of the value chain framework to explain how extranets can potentially add value to firms. Their study finds significant cost reductions in inbound logistics, outbound logistics, and production. Matolcsy et al. (2005) use the framework to examine whether Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems deliver economic value to firms. Their empirical results indicate that the adoption of ERP systems leads to significant improvements in operational efficiencies and limited improvements in marketing, sales, and distribution. Dehning et al. (2007) use a simplified value chain framework to study the impact of IT-based supply chain management (SCM) systems on firm performance. They use gross margin and raw materials inventory to measure efficiencies in inbound logistics, asset turnover and work-in-process inventory to measure operational efficiencies, relative market share and finished goods inventory to measure efficiencies in outbound logistics, and selling, general, and administrative expenses to measure cost saving in support processes. Overall firm performance is measured by total inventory turnover, return on assets and return on sales Their empirical results indicate that IT-based SCM systems lead to improvement in gross margin, inventory turnover, and relative market share, return on sales and reduce selling, general, and administrative expenses.

## 3. Theoretical framework

This section employs the value chain framework proposed by Porter (Porter and Millar, 1985) as a theoretical backdrop to examine the economic effects of ITO. While widely applied in other settings, to our best knowledge, the value chain framework has not been used in the context of ITO. Following Dehning et al. (2007), this study adopts a simplified version of the value chain model which includes four key components: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, and support activities (see Fig. 1). In general, ITO is expected to positively impact the four value chain activities through integration and technical advantages. The information used in each value chain activity may originate from a variety of entities (e.g. suppliers, distributors, and customers) and information flows across these entities directly impact the efficiency and effectiveness of the value chain activities. Integration of the information systems and business processes of all the entities involved hence becomes critical for the success of a firm (Davenport and Short, 1990; Dehning et al., 2007). ITO can improve many dimensions of systems integration over in-house IT, including delivery speed and quality. By specializing in providing cutting-edge IT and services, ITO vendors enjoy many advantages over in-house IT departments to facilitate the integration and consolidation of processes and systems (McLellan et al., 1995). In some cases, different entities involved in a firm’s value chain may share a common ITO vendor, which could enable a smoother systems integration process and a better integrated system that could not be realized by in-house IT departments (McLellan et al., 1995). Further, IT vendors typically serve a larger client base and are hence exposed to a greater number and variety of IT-related tasks and problems (DiRomauldo and Gurbaxani, 1998; Hayes et al., 2000). Such experience and knowledge and their specialized personnel can all contribute to a more efficient and effective systems integration and deployment process (Bennett and Timbrell, 2000; Smith and Rupp, 2002).

After integration is achieved, ITO vendors can provide higher levels of services for the value chain activities due to the technological advantages that they enjoy over in-house IT departments. In this rapidly changing technological age, the average life cycle of a system is 3–6 years, leading to heavy demands for technology upgrading and employee training (Selig, 1997). When multiple entities are involved, the costs and complexity of technological maintenance and upgrades can strain the technical, financial, and human resources of any in-house IT department, even for the largest firms (Selig, 1997). By contrast, as specialists of IT, vendors can often afford to invest in state-of-the-art technologies and innovative practices at a scale that cannot be realized by many firms’ in-house IT departments (Barthélemy, 2001; DiRomauldo and Gurbaxani, 1998). Some of these technologies and practices could be essential to the success of the value chain activities.

The following sections discuss how the integration and technological advantages of ITO will be manifested in individual value chain activities.

## 3.1. Inbound Logistics

Inbound logistics include activities associated with acquiring and receiving raw materials from suppliers, thus it involves both internal (procurement, scheduling, etc.) and external entities (suppliers).When a firm outsources its inbound logistics IT, it can benefit from the vendor-rendered integration advantages discussed above, and obtain better logistics related services that can improve information flow, information sharing, collaboration, coordination, and control with suppliers. Improved integration, communication, collaboration, and coordination can in turn lead to effective management and reduction of raw materials. From an economic perspective, this efficiency improvement derived from inbound logistics ITO should be reflected in the raw materials turnover ratio (RMTO), calculated as cost of goods sold/raw materials inventory, because for the same level of cost of goods sold, the reduced raw materials inventory level would yield higher RMTO ratios. Since the components of cost of goods sold (COG) include cost of raw materials, transportation cost of raw materials from suppliers, storage cost of raw materials inventory, as well as direct labor and overhead cost, a lower level of raw materials thus reduces cost of goods sold, leading to lower COG (cost of goods sold/sales). Gross profit margin (GPM), calculated as 1- (cost of goods sold/sales), or 1-COG, measures profit made for each dollar of sales. And due to the anticipated impact of outsourcing on COG, GPM is considered here for measuring the profitability improvement of inbound logistics ITO. More effective handling of raw materials derived from inbound logistics ITO should directly decrease COG and improve GPM (Dehning et al., 2007).

With the technological advantages offered by IT vendors, ITO firms are more likely than non outsourcing firms to enjoy the state-of-the-art logistics related technologies such as RFID. They can thus better direct and monitor inventories, which could in turn reduced raw materials inventory level and result in higher RMTO, lower COG, and higher GPM. For these reasons, we expect inbound logistics ITO to have a greater positive impact on firms’ inbound logistics activities than in-house IT and we hypothesize:

$\mathrm { H } _ { 1 } .$ Firms that outsource their logistics-related IT will demonstrate improved performance in inbound processes as reflected in RMTO, COG, and GPM.

## 3.2. Operations

Operations include all the activities involved in transforming raw materials into work-in-process inventory and finally into finished goods (Porter and Millar, 1985). Since the IT involved in operations are multifaceted, outsourcing such technol ogies to specialized vendors has the potential to improve a firm's operations in a variety of ways. First, information flows among members of a firm's value chain have been found to directly impact the firm's operations efficiency and effectiveness Lack of information sharing or delays in information flow can lead to forecasting errors, which in turn could result in poor production scheduling, unscheduled jobs in production lines, and inventory wastage (Heikkilä, 2002). With the vendor-rendered integration advantages discussed earlier. ITo firms are more likely than non outsourcing firms to achieve full integra: tion of operations related IT with other systems such as logistics systems and customer relationship management systems. This can result in better flow, coordination, and control of critical information required for the effective management of work-in-process inventory and better allocation, coordination, and utilization of assets. Effective asset utilization can be captured by the total assets turnover ratio (TATO), calculated as sales/total assets, because given the same level of sales, a lower level of assets through effective utilization vields higher TATo. For the same level of costs of goods sold, reduction in total inventory and work-in-process inventory wastage and obsolescence can be reflected in a higher inventory turnover ratio (IN-VTO, calculated as costs of goods sold/total inventory) and work-in-process inventory turnover ratio (WIPTO) calculated as costs of goods sold/work-in-process inventory)

Enjoying the technological advantages IT vendors offer over in-house IT, ITO firms can also take advantage of the state-ofthe-art operations management systems (OMS). Research has indicated that OMS are essential for the effectiveness and efficiency of manufacturing operations (Bardhan et al., 2007, 2006), allowing the outsourcing firms to allocate and coordinate their resources and manage their work-in-process inventory optimally, leading to higher WIPTO, INVTO, and TATO.

Additionally, as IT specialists and service providers, many IT vendors provide unique industry solutions that are considered the best practice for improving operations for a wide variety of industries (Matolcsy et al., 2005). By delegating their IT to such vendors, outsourcing firms can improve their operations processes as a result of learning best-practices associated with asset utilization and inventory wastage reduction. This could contribute to higher WIPTO, INVTO, and TATO.

Finally, optimal allocation, utilization, and coordination of assets and effective management of inventory in operations process requires not only advanced technologies, but also changes in a business focus, organizational structure, and business processes to truly drive value (Nykamp, 2002). Research has suggested outsourcing provides the catalyst required to counter employee reluctance of massive organizational change and it can unlock the organizational structure that is entrenched firmly in a firm’s culture and bureaucracy (Barthélemy, 2003; McLellan et al., 1995)

Taken together, operation related ITO is expected to enable firms to be better able to utilize their assets, manage their inventory, and reduce work-in-process inventory. These benefits can be detected by examining total inventory turnover (IN-VTO), work-in-process inventory turnover (WIPTO) and total assets turnover (TATO) (Dehning et al., 2007; Matolcsy et al., 2005).

$H _ { 2 } .$ Firms that outsource their operation related IT will demonstrate improved performance in operations processes as reflected in WIPTO, INVTO, and TATO.

## 3.3. Outbound logistics

Outbound logistics include the activities associated with supplying finished goods and services to buyers. The technological advantages of IT vendors should enable outsourcing firms to implement state-of-the-art technologies for automatic order identification and tracking such as RFID or intelligent multi-agent systems. Due to the vendor-rendered integration advantages discussed earlier, firms that outsource their outbound logistics IT are expected to be better able to fully integrate the data from such technologies with those for the enterprise wide applications, and thus they enjoy more current and accurate order and delivery information and better coordination and decision making across the transactional areas of the outbound logistics system (e.g., production, shipping, distribution, warehouse, retailer, and customers). Improved information, coordination, and decision making enable the outsourcing firms to optimally plan their capacity and production and reduce the transit time, thereby reducing unnecessary finished goods on hand. These efficiency improvements derived from outbound logistics ITO will be reflected in the finished goods turnover ratio (FGTO), calculated as cost of goods sold/finished goods inventory.

$H _ { 3 } .$ Firms that outsource their outbound logistics IT will demonstrate improved performance in outbound processes as reflected in FGTO.

## 3.4. Supporting activities

The abovementioned primary activities are facilitated by supporting activities including accounting, order processing, record-keeping, human resources, etc. Given the components of selling, general, and administrative expenses (SG&A), i.e. the sum of all direct and indirect selling expenses and all general and administrative expenses of a company, SG&A is considered here as a cost performance measure for supporting activities. When outsourcing their IT-related supporting activities such as order processing and record-keeping, firms are likely to reduce their selling expenses and administrative overhead (lower SG&A) because (1) they tend to contract these activities to vendors located in countries and regions where high-quality and lower-cost labor pools are readily accessible (Smith et al., 1998) and (2) vendors can negotiate lower prices with hardware and software providers and spread the fixed cost over their clients (Barthélemy, 2001). With the technological advantages offered by IT vendors, outsourcing firms can implement automated systems directed at support tasks, leading to cost saving on support personnel and reduced SG&A. Further, IT vendors often have access to specialists with valuable knowledge and experience about superior administrative processes and management methods (DiRomauldo and Gurbaxani, 1998). Hence, by delegating IT-related supporting activities to a high-quality vendor, outsourcing firms may be able to transfer such knowledge and perform supporting activities more efficiently and cost-effectively than if IT is kept in-house, leading to reduced administrative overhead and ultimately lower SG&A. Thus, we hypothesize:

$H _ { 4 } .$ Firms that outsource their IT will demonstrate improved performance in supporting processes as reflected in their SG&A.

## 3.5. Overall performance

This research advocates a process-oriented approach because outsourced IT is intended to target specific business processes and thus its impact should be better detected at the intermediate process level. Firm-level measures such as return on asset (ROA) and return on sales (ROS) aggregate the net effects of sales and operating costs, therefore, the effects of ITO may not be evident at this level. To validate this assertion, a firm-level analysis on the effects of ITO on the overall performance of the firm is included. Following prior studies (Bharadwaj, 2000; Dehning et al., 2007; Hitt and Brynjolfsson, 1996), ROA (calculated as net income/total assets) and ROS (calculated as net income/sales) are used as measures for firm-level performance.

## 4. Research design

## 4.1. Sample selection

We identified companies with ITO initiatives by performing keyword searches of the Lexis/Nexis Academic Database. We also searched prior literature for lists of ITO announcements. This collective effort yielded 4678 ITO announcements during the period January 1, 1988–December 31, 2005<sup>1</sup>. After eliminating unrelated announcements (e.g., product announcements from ITO vendors, repeated announcements regarding the same ITO event, etc.), 602 announcements remained in the sample. Among the 602 announcements, 254 had enough details for the classification of outsourcing type (e.g., which value chain activity is the outsourcing targeting). Merging our sample with Compustat Database further reduced the sample down to 146 observations. Finally, to appropriately test the theoretical framework proposed above, we included firms only in the manufacturing and wholesale/retail industries because the value chain model used in our framework is more suitable for the analysis of firms in these two sectors. It would be senseless to ask what is received, produced, and shipped for a service industry sector such as insurance (Stabell and Fjeldstad, 1998). On the other hand, logistics and operations are a vital part of the daily operations for manufacturing and wholesale/retail firms. After eliminating companies that did not belong to either manufacturing or whole sale/retail industries, we had 100 observations left in the sample

When ITO initiatives are undertaken they are typically geared towards impacting one or more processes of the value chain. In order to take this into account in our analysis, we had two raters independently read through the ITO announcements and classify each ITO initiative based on which process(es) of the value chain the initiative was geared towards. A Cohen’s Kappa statistical test was performed (Cohen, 1960) to assess inter-rater reliability. A Kappa (j) statistic of zero indicates slight agreement between the raters while a value of greater than 0.8 indicates outstanding agreement. (Landis and Koch, 1977).The results revealed outstanding levels of inter-rater reliability (inbound logistics j = 0.942; outbound logistics $\kappa = 0 . 9 2 6 ;$ ; operations $\kappa = 0 . 9 5 4 ;$ support services j = 0.938). Finally, following Gerstner and Day (1997) total agreement was realized via discussion by the raters.

## 4.2. Methodology

To investigate whether ITO helps companies improve cost and efficiency in the value chain activities, we conduct three types of tests: (1) pre-post-event single differencing t-tests where we examine the pre-post performance difference within each ITO firm; (2) pre-post-event double differencing t-tests where we examine the pre-post performance difference between the ITO firms and matched control firms; and (3) a multiple regression method to examine whether the pre-post performance difference of the ITO firms is different from that of the control firms while controlling for firm size, book-to-market ratio, and pre-event performance. Together, these tests should provide a rigorous examination of the payoff of the ITO initiatives. The test procedures are described below.

## 4.2.1. Matched control firms

According to Barber and Lyon (1996), the best matching procedure should match the sample and the control firms by industry, firm size and pre-event performance rather than by industry alone, or by industry and size. Following this guideline, we first identify firms with the same four-digit SIC code for each ITO firm. Then, in that industry sector, we choose the firm with the closest total assets (in year t  1) to that of the ITO firm. Next, we perform a series of matched pair pre-event ttests to check whether the ITO firms and the control firms are matched on pre-event performance (e.g., t-test for $\overline { { { \mathrm { R M T O } _ { \mathrm { I T O } , t - 1 } } - { \mathrm { R M T O } _ { c o n t r o l , t - 1 } } } } = 0 ,$ , where ITO denotes an IT outsourcing firm, control denotes the matched control firm, and $t - 1$ is 1 year before the ITO announcement. Insignificant difference would suggest that the ITO firms and the control firms are matched on pre-event performance). Untabulated results of the pre-event t-tests suggest that there are no significant differences in all performance measures between the ITO firms and the control firms in year t  1 except for total assets turnover $( \mathrm { T A T O } ) , ^ { 2 }$ indicating that the ITO firms and the control firms are well matched on pre-event performance. We also conduct a search in Lexis–Nexis Academic Database to check whether the matched control firms had ITO announcements in years t  1 to $t + 3 .$ The search confirmed that none of the matched control firms had ITO announcements in the specified periods. In summary, the above procedures and tests provide adequate assurance that the ITO firms and the control firms are matched on size, industry (4-digit SIC code), and pre-event performance.

## 4.2.2. Pre-post-event single differencing t-tests: IT outsourcing firms

In this test, we conduct a series of pre-post-event single differencing t-tests to compare ITO firms’ performance in year t  1 with their performance in years $t , t + 1 , t + 2$ , and t + 3 respectively, where year t is the year in which an ITO initiative is announced. This method has been frequently used in prior literature to investigate whether a strategic initiative is effective in improving firm performance in the periods subsequent to the initiative (Dehning et al., 2007). For this study, the t-test is performed by outsourcing type for each performance measure specified in the hypotheses. For example, to examine whether there is improvement in raw materials inventory turnover (RMTO) in years t to t + 3, we perform the following paired t-tests for firms that outsourced their inbound logistics IT: $\mathrm { R M T O _ { 1 : I 0 , t } } - \mathrm { R M T O _ { 1 : I - 1 } } , \mathrm { R M T O _ { 1 : I 0 , t + 1 } } - \mathrm { R M T O _ { 1 : I 0 , t - 1 } } .$ $\mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t + 2 } - \mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t - 1 }$ , and $\mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t + 3 } - \mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t - 1 } ,$ , where ITO denotes an IT outsourcing firm, and t is the year in which the ITO firm announces its outsourcing initiative. If $\mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t + 1 } - \mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t - 1 }$ is significantly greater than zero, the result would indicate that on average ITO geared towards inbound logistics are able to increase outsourcers’ raw materials inventory turnover rate.

## 4.2.3. Pre-post-event double differencing t-tests: IT outsourcing firms vs. matched control firms

In this test, we perform a series of double differencing t-tests where we compare the pre-post performance difference of the ITO firms with that of the matched control firms. These tests are more rigorous than the single differencing t-tests because the pre-post performance difference of the matched control firms here serves as a control in investigating whether the improvement made by the ITO firms is beyond the contemporaneous improvement made by the contro firms. For instance, to examine whether inbound logistics ITO initiatives improve raw materials turnover (RMTO) in year $t + 1$ , we conduct the following matched-pair t-test: $( \mathsf { R M T O } _ { \Pi \mathsf { T } \mathsf { T } 0 , t + 1 } - \mathsf { R M T O } _ { \Pi \mathsf { T } 0 , t - 1 } ) - ( \mathsf { R M T O } _ { c o n t r o l , t + 1 } - \mathsf { R M T O } _ { c o n t r o l , t - 1 } ) = \mathsf { 0 } .$ . If $\mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t + 1 } - \mathrm { R M T O } _ { \Pi 0 , t - 1 }$ is positive from the single differencing test and the above t-test is also positive and significant, the result would indicate that the ITO firms are able to increase raw materials turnover in year $t + 1$ , and the improvement is better than that of the industry peers during the same period. We conduct the double differencing t-tests between the ITO firms and the control firms by outsourcing type in each time period from year t to year t + 3.

## 4.2.4. Multiple regression

Other factors may affect future firm performance besides ITO initiatives. It is necessary to develop a multiple regression to control the effects of potential confounding factors. Both Brown and Perry (1994) and Barber and Lyon (1996) suggest that pre-event performance may have a ‘‘halo” effect on post-event performance. Hall and Liedtka (2005) also find that poor performers outsource IT. To control for the halo effect of pre-event performance, we include $\mathrm { P E R P } _ { t - 1 }$ , firm performance 1 year before the ITO announcements, in a multiple regression where we regress pre-post performance difference on ITO (by type) and other control variables. Growth can affect a company’s future performance by increasing investments that may affect operating efficiency. To control for the effects of growth, we use book-to-market value of equity ratio (i.e. BM, the reciprocal of market-to-book value of equity $\Gamma \mathsf { a t i o } ^ { 3 } )$ as a proxy for growth in the multiple regression. In addition, outsourcing IT may have a different impact on large verses small companies. Accordingly, we used total assets (TA) to control for the size effect in our regression model. In summary, our multiple regression is specified as follows:

$$
\operatorname{PERFDIF} _ {i, t + j} = \alpha_ {i, t + j} + \beta_ {1} \text { Dummy } _ {i t} + \beta_ {2} \text { PERF } _ {i, t - 1} + \beta_ {3} \text { BM } _ {i, t + j} + \beta_ {4} \text { TA } _ {i, t + j} + e _ {i, t + j}\tag{1}
$$

where $\mathrm { P E R F D I F } _ { i , t + j }$ is the performance in year $t + j$ minus performance in year t  1 for firm i, where j = 0, 1, 2 or 3. The performance measures are as defined in Table 1, $\mathrm { P E R F } _ { i , t - 1 }$ the pre-event performance in year t  1 for firm i. The performance measures are defined in Table 1, Dummy the 1 if firm i announces IT outsourcing initiatives (by outsourcing type) in year t, else $0 , \mathsf { B M } _ { i , t + j }$ the book-to-market value of equity ratio for firm i in year t + j, where $j = 0 , 1 , 2 \mathrm { o r } 3 ,$ , and $\mathrm { T A } _ { i , t + j }$ is the total assets for firm i at the end of year t + j, where $j = 0 , 1 , 2 \mathrm { o r } 3 .$

## 6. Discussion and conclusion

While the general contention is that ITO can deliver value for firms, empirical evidence has largely been limited to anecdotal affirmations. With the sizeable investment in ITO on one hand and the lingering questions regarding whether firms have actually realized the assumed benefits of this strategy on the other, it is imperative that IS researchers and managers alike develop a fuller understanding of the economic payoff of ITO based on theory-based frameworks and rigorous empirical testing. To this end, we drew on Porter’s Value Chain framework to construct an ITO model that encompasses a relevant set of performance assessments of the economic impact of ITO on firms’ value chain activities. The rationale for the choice of Porter’s Value Chain framework as the theoretical backdrop was derived from: (a) prior use of this theoretical perspective to test the payoff of information systems and practices including ERP, company extranet, and SCM; and (b) the compelling articulation of the potential benefits of ITO on firms’ value chain. With regard to performance, we used objective accounting data to mitigate the disadvantage of perceptual evaluation that dominates the current ITO literature.

Overall our results regarding the performance impact of ITO on firm’s value chain activities are mixed. The results of the three sets of tests consistently indicate that ITO initiatives help firms improve their performance on inbound logistics (RMI-TO, COG, GPM) and supporting activities (SG&A). Nevertheless, no improvements were observed for efficiency of outbound logistics (FGTO) and operations (INVTO, WIPTO, and TATO). In addition, the results provide no evidence that ITO firms were able to improve overall firm performance (ROA and ROS).

Unlike the majority of the studies which employ broad high-level firm performance indicators (e.g., Lacity and Hirschheim, 1994; Lacity and Willcocks, 1998; Hayes et al., 2000; Oh et al., 2006; Grover and Cheon, 1996; Lee and Kim, 1999), this study examines the impact of ITO on both the process and firm-level performance measures. An important finding is that the effects of ITO are best documented at the process level and less so at the firm-level. This is an key finding because it validates assertions made by Wang et al. (2008) that the impact of ITO should be properly modeled through intermediate (i.e., process) level contributions. Unlike the study of Wang et al. (2008), which aggregated all types of ITO, we categorize each ITO initiative based on which value chain process it targets and then match it with appropriate corresponding accounting performance measures through value chain analysis. The logic here is that if an outsourcing initiative is targeting logistics, it should have greater impact on logistics related performance measures, and a limited impact on other process-level outcome variables. By taking ITO types into consideration and utilizing the value chain framework, we are able to more precisely capture the performance impact of ITO using more refined accounting measures. Wang et al. (2008) used sales per employee as a general measure of efficiency for all types of ITO and established that ITO is positively associated with effi ciency improvement. Nevertheless, the currently study indicates that only ITO firms geared towards inbound logistics are able to achieve efficiency improvements. This finding highlights the importance of considering types of ITO and selecting appropriate and refined performance metrics for evaluation

## 6.1. Implications for research

Interestingly, we observe consistently positive results regarding the cost measures (COG, GPM, and SG&A) but consistently negative results for the efficiency measures (with the exception of RMTO) in our sample. We can only conjecture on the negative impacts of ITO on efficiency. It could be that most ITO decisions are predominantly motivated by cost saving rather than strategic orientation. Another potential explanation stems from the notion of IT complementarity. Isolated ITO investment may not generate benefits that are sufficient to bring efficiency improvements. Unless efforts are undertaken simultaneously with an ITO initiative to re-design processes, firms may not be able to effectively exploit the full potential of ITO to enhance their efficiency. Lending support to this coniecture, findings in the general IT business value literature have suggested that IT investment must be accompanied with complementary initiatives such as process re-designing, process outsourcing, IT capability building, in order to create value (Bardhan et al., 2007; Bharadwaj, 2000; Dehning et al., 2007). Another explanation lies in the differences between ITO and other types of IT investments. Unlike other types of IT invest ments where value is added through successful implementation, use, and management of technology, ITO creates value through effective sourcing strategies (e.g., in-house IT vs. outsourcing, total outsourcing vs. selective outsourcing, solevendor outsourcing vs. multiple vendor outsourcing, etc.). Given this, it has been unclear whether the findings from the general literature on the business value of IT investment would automatically transfer to the ITO domain. For instance, in the general IT business value literature, IT has recurrently been found to improve efficiency (capacity utilization and inventory turnover) in the context of EDI. E-commerce strategy, general IT spending. SCM. and enterprise-wide IS (Barua et al., 1995; Mukhopadhyay et al., 1995; Rabinovich et al., 2003; Zhu, 2004). Yet the current study has not been able to establish a clear and consistent link between ITO and efficiency improvement, This difference in results highlights the importance of further conceptual and empirical work that assesses the economic payoff of ITO.

The limited positive impact ITO also provides motivations for future research to obtain a finer-grained picture of the possible reasons, including the ones we contemplated above, behind the lack of a straightforward link between ITO and improvements across multiple performance areas (both cost and efficiency) and value chain activities. Researchers are encouraged to trace the mediating mechanisms and contingency conditions that are necessary for the maximization of ITO returns. Such academic inquiry would provide firms with valuable knowledge on how to best outsource IT in order to create greater competitive advantage.

## 6.2. Strategic implications for practice

The current study offers several strategic implications for practice. First, our results suggest that when justifying the business value of ITO managers should evaluate its impact at the intermediate processes level where its direct effect is expected to be manifested. Second, despite the lingering doubt voiced in anecdotal and case studies on the cost benefit of ITO, using audited financial statements, our results suggest that cost reduction through ITO is highly likely. Third, the results imply the importance of measuring the impact of ITO along multiple dimensions. Although a focus on cost reduction may indicate that ITO is a value-adding strategy, our findings also suggest a consistently negative impact of ITO on efficiency. Hence, managers must balance cost-oriented goals and strategic-oriented goals when making ITO decisions. They may also consider making complementary investments such as investments in process re-design, vendor governance capability building to fully realize the potential of ITO. Finally, collectively, we conclude that the value chain model, albeit within a two-industry setting, received adequate empirical support indicating that it could be used as a useful approach to systematically evaluate the economic payoff of ITO. Thus, an important contribution of this paper lies in the development of a model of ITO payoff based on a simplified value chain. This model maps the return of ITO initiatives onto firms’ value-adding processes, thereby allowing the firms to more precisely pinpoint and monitor the processes where the potential benefits of each ITO initiative have or have not been achieved. Information systems scholars and managers alike benefit from this research, as the results consistently point towards a limited positive impact of ITO. This is an important finding as it warns against any over optimism when considering ITO as a strategy.

## 6.3. Limitations

As with all studies, this research has limitations which future work may wish to address. First, while the approach adopted to collect data was rigorous, the resulting sample which was subsequently used in this study is small. This small sample size limits the generalizability of our findings. As data becomes available, future studies may include a longer time frame and use both survey and news announcement data to increase the sample size and avoid sample bias. Second, we only used accounting performance metrics. Future studies may wish to combine fine-grained perceptual measures and account ing measures. Given that perceptual and accounting performance measures tend to mitigate each other’s disadvantages, ta ken together they may be able to provide a richer picture on the economic value of ITO. Finally, future research may wish to consider the impact of various contingency factors. While this study found that ITO positively impacts cost, we also detect a consistently negative impact of ITO on efficiency. Since data availability constrained our sample size, many contingent factors could not be included in the model. As more data becomes available, future research may be able to uncover the impact of various contingency factors on the relationship between ITO and the value chain.
