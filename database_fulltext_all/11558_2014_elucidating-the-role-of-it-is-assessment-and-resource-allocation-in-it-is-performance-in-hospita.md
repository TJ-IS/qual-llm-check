# Elucidating the role of IT/IS assessment and resource allocation in IT/IS performance in hospitals

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: IT/IS assessment IT maturity IT/IS resource allocation IT/IS performances Organizational characteristics Hospitals

## A B S T R A C T

Although similar to firms in other industries that strive to increase their operational efficiency by implementing IT/IS, hospitals face difficulties in obtaining the corresponding benefits of investing in IT/ IS. This study investigates the relations among IT/IS performance, IT maturity, IT/IS assessment, IT/IS resource allocation, and user satisfaction in hospitals. IT/IS performance in hospitals is evaluated using a designed questionnaire, along with a case study undertaken to discuss IT/IS assessment and the effect of IT/IS on hospital procedures. The analysis results indicate that hospitals have a higher IT maturity, stronger intention to implement IT/IS assessment, and better IT/IS resource allocation capabilities than firms in other industries. Moreover, both IT/IS assessment and user satisfaction affect IT/IS performance in hospitals.

## 1. Introduction

Despite the extensive use of information technology and information systems (IT/IS) in industry, most enterprises are concerned with technical issues as well as the continuous development and acquisition of innovative and efficient devices and thus often neglect the potential benefits of effective IT/IS assessment methods [33]. As a result of the accelerated development of IT/IS in recent years, enterprises now face greater more challenges than those a decade ago [10]. Unlike industrial machinery, IT/IS affect many aspects within enterprises, including work processes, management strategies and planning, culture, and structural reorganization. Even with the numerous studies on IT/IS investment appraisals [2,10,27], many enterprises are still concerned with IT/IS investment. Implementing IT/IS assessment can enhance performance, highlighting the importance of using a comprehensive IT/IS assessment model [23]. Many hospitals have thus implemented IT/IS into their daily operations. However, implementing IT/IS in the medical sector is expensive, and each IT/ IS investment may generate risk [18]. Medical institutions at all levels must integrate a hospital information system (HIS) into their daily operations [13]. Given the rapid growth of Internet usage and personal mobile devices, medical institutions that implement IT/IS have becomes one of the fastest growing industries in Asia Pacific, necessitating an assessment of IT/IS performance in the medical industry. Before implementing IT/IS, hospitals must use a comprehensive assessment method to examine the effectiveness of implementing IT/IS in order to ensure that they make the most efficient investment with their limited resources [20]. Therefore, this study develops a predictive model for implementing IT/IS in the medical industry in order to determine the factors affecting IT/ IS performance. A comprehensive verification model is also designed. Importantly, the proposed model can identify mistakes in implementing IT/IS in the medical industry and then correct and improve upon them as well as optimize IT/IS performance.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. IT/IS assessment

Carr [3] asserted that IT/IS resembles basic and necessary daily operations and cannot generate a competitive advantage for organizations because the popularity of implementing IT/IS might cause management crises due to over investment [2,26,38,43]. IT/ IS investment is unlike investment in common machinery, as IT/IS investment costs may not initially be expensive. However, expenses associated with IT/IS maintenance and upgrades might be prohibitive during the later stages of implementation, and changes in organizational process may be unpredictable. Therefore, organizations must assess the effectiveness of IT/IS to prevent investment losses. IT/IS assessment focuses on assigning values to the software and hardware in organizations. The aim of implementing IT/IS in organizations is to enhance performance and to develop a measurement procedure [19]. As IT/IS assessment is a complex task [14], the benefits of implementing IT/IS are usually unexpected.

IT/IS assessment initially involves assisting organizations in making decisions related to implementing IT/IS. During the early stages, such an assessment entails calculating investments in assets using accounting methods. During the later stages, such an assessment focuses on strategic decision making [24]. MIS staff must be responsible for providing quality decision support and precise information during decision making. However, IT departments have generally been considered the least staffed and equipped departments in hospitals for quite some time [29]. Therefore, implementing an objective and scientific IT/IS assessment method can provide comprehensive results to convince executives to support IT/IS investment in the decision-making process before IT/IS implementation in hospitals [15].

Incorporating IT/IS assessment into management practices facilitates organizations’ ability to gather critical information from diverse sources [24]. According to previous studies, performing IT/ IS assessment can improve organizational performance and create additional benefits [27]. IT/IS assessment is a strategic method for senior managers and executives to further accumulate information in order to generate various organizational benefits [24].

## 2.2. IT/IS resource allocation

Organizational competitiveness derives primarily from an organization’s resources, including its equipment, staff skills, capital and finances, whereas organizational productivity depends on its organizational resources. Moreover, organizational competency derives from three resources: human resources, entity capital, and organizational capital.

Most studies indicate that the internal resources of organizations significantly affect IT/IS implementation [5]. IT/IS resource allocation refers to herein to the resources (e.g., human resources and various supporting resources) required for implementing IT/IS and optimizing organizational performance.

## 2.3. IT maturity

As a result of rapid advances in information technology, IT maturity has received increasing attention among organizations, and thus, accurate assessment of IT maturity is important. Preceding the development of many IT maturity models [1,22], in his pioneering research on IT maturity assessment, Richard Nolan proposed a model with four stages [11,30] and later expanded it to six stages [31]. Nolan’s model has been constantly referenced, expanded, modified, and formed into new models [7,35]. Of those models, the six-stage model of Galliers and Sutherland is widely used [9]. As a detailed, concrete, and easily understood model, Galliers and Sutherland’s model includes seven factors that influence IT maturity, and thus, the model is especially appropriate for evaluating various stages of maturity in modern organizations. Rocha [34] recently applied Galliers and Sutherland’s [9] IT maturity model in the healthcare sector, which is the field examined in this study. However, many proposed IT maturity models have not enough time to validate, making it still impossible to verify their effectiveness. Thus, based on the IT maturity model of Galliers and Sutherland [9], this study assesses the IT maturity of hospitals.

## 2.4. User satisfaction

IT/IS implementation fails in organizations not because of the system itself but because of individuals’ use of the system [25]. According to Kappelman and McLean [17], most scholars assess IT/ IS based on user satisfaction and system utilization. Although user attitudes usually determine the success of IT/IS implementation [4], user attitudes and reactions are not easily determined; thus, user satisfaction is used to measure the success of IT/IS implementation.

DeLone and McLean [6] adopted user satisfaction as a major factor affecting IT/IS performance. Chircu and Kauffman [5] indicated that humans represent a unique resource in implementing IT/IS. Notably, user satisfaction is a valuable measure in evaluating IT/IS performance. Thus, users who are more satisfied with IT/IS performance are more likely to accept organizationalsponsored training sessions and become a valuable human resource.

## 2.5. IT/IS performance

Despite their importance, desired target benefits pre-investment of the IT/IS assessment and reviews how to implement’ IT/IS cannot ensure that organizations achieve the desired benefits of IT IS implementation. While acquiring benefits from IT/IS requires a considerable amount of knowledge, some studies have noted the complexity of effectively and reliably assessing IT/IS performance in organizations [33].

Seddon [36] explained the effects of IT/IS by describing their consequences and net benefits. DeLone and McLean [6] also indicated that net benefits profoundly affect the success of an information system model. Factors that affect IT/IS performance in organizations are the target achievement, productivity, economic benefits, innovation capabilities, and quality control. Therefore, this study investigates the role of IT/IS assessment, organizational characteristics, and user satisfaction in optimizing IT/IS performance in the medical industry.

## 2.6. Organizational characteristics

Many researchers contend that organizational characteristics profoundly affect the success of IT/IS implementation [41] and thus need to be considered in determining how to implement IT/IS in the medical industry. IT maturity and IT/IS resource allocation are among the organizational characteristics considered in this study. Standing and Lin [37] reported that organizations must fully utilize the existing IT infrastructure to overcome the complexity of IT/IS assessment. Thus, organizations with higher IT maturity have a higher probability of undertaking complex assessments [9,42]. Lin et al. [22] asserted that IT/IS assessment plays a critical role in the assessment of IT/IS performance. Eilat et al. [8] and Ooi and Soh [32] also suggested that organizations ensure that IT/IS resources are evenly allocated during IT/IS assessment.

According to the limits-to-value model of Chircu and Kauffman [5], organizations in certain industries should assess the benefits of IT/IS implementation. The results of such an assessment results indicate potential benefits of IT/IS implementation, which are the maximum benefits that organizations can obtain from successfully implementing IT/IS. However, organizations may encounter barriers to IT/IS implementation if they inappropriately distribute resources during the implementation process

## 3. Research method

This study evaluated IT/IS performance in hospitals using a survey questionnaire as the data collection method to investigate the relations among IT/IS performance, IT maturity, IT/IS assessment, IT/IS resource allocation, and user satisfaction. A case study was also used to more closely examine IT/IS assessment and the effect of IT/IS on hospital procedures. Combining the two methods allowed us to increase the effectiveness and reliability of our research [28]. Specifically, the quantitative data that were analyzed from the questionnaire compensated for the limitations of the qualitative data, and the depth of the qualitative data in the case study compensated for the limitations of the quantitative data.

## 3.1. Research model

The model of IT/IS assessment and organizational characteristics (IT maturity and IT/IS resource allocation) proposed by Lin et al. [22] served as the theoretical framework of this study to investigate factors affecting IT/IS performance. Moreover, the effect of user satisfaction on IT/IS optimal performance is another important factor in a successful IS model [6]. Therefore, this study investigated the relations among IT/IS assessment, organizational characteristics, and user satisfaction and their effects on IT/IS performance. Fig. 1 illustrates the proposed model of this study.

## 3.2. Research hypothesis

This study investigates the relations among IT/IS assessment, IT maturity, IT/IS resource allocation, user satisfaction, and IT/IS performance in hospitals. The proposed hypotheses, definitions, and details of each factor are described in this subsection.

## H1. IT/IS assessment positively affects IT/IS performance.

Being vital for economic production, an IT/IS assessment method can ensure that organizational benefits are realized from IT/IS investment. Additionally, an IT/IS assessment method is a strategic instrument for senior managers and executives to gather information to obtain organizational benefits [24]. Previous studies have indicated that adopting IT/IS assessment can facilitate organizations’ ability to improve their performance and effectiveness [27,40]. When adopting IT/IS assessment to assess investment projects, hospitals should examine the comprehensiveness and rigor of the assessment method [16,22,44].

H2. IT maturity in hospitals positively affects the implementation of IT/IS assessment.

IT/IS implementation is highly associated with hospital effectiveness; thus, an assessment of IT/IS performance cannot rely solely on an assessment method. A multidimensional assessment is important to address the complexity of finance-related issues, organizational processes, and technology. However, a complex IT/IS assessment may result in failure. Standing and Lin [37] noted that organizations must use the existing IT infrastructure to overcome the complexity and difficulty of IT/IS assessment. Thus, organizations with higher IT maturity are more likely to carrying out a complex assessment [9,42]. Hospitals focus on the development of IT strategies, organizational structures of IT departments, levels of information systems, types of IT, and capabilities of applying IT [9].

H3. IT maturity in hospitals positively affects IT/IS resource allocation.

Properly allocating unique resources for implementing IT/IS can optimize the IT performance of hospitals [5,22]. With fewer potential benefits of IT/IS, the benefits of IT/IS implementation diminish. Organizations should thus eliminate or reduce barriers to IT/IS implementation by increasing their IT maturity.

## H4. IT/IS assessment positively affects IT/IS resource allocation

Lin et al. [22] indicated that an IT/IS assessment method is crucial for realizing the benefits of IT/IS. According to previous studies, adopting IT/IS assessment ensures effective IT/IS resource allocation [8,32].

## H5. IT/IS resource allocation positively affects IT/IS performance.

While expecting to optimize their IT/IS performance, organiza tions may encounter barriers when transferring resources. Organizations can eliminate transformation barriers and increase the benefits of IT/IS by allocating IT/IS resources appropriately [5].

## H6. User satisfaction positively affects IT/IS performance.

According to DeLone and McLean [6], user satisfaction profoundly affects the net benefits of IT/IS. Chircu and Kauffman [5] asserted that human resources are important resources for IT/IS implementation. User satisfaction is a practical indicator of the effectiveness of IT/IS. When they are satisfied with the benefits of IT/IS, users may be willing to accept organization-sponsored training sessions and become a supporting resource.

## 3.3. Questionnaire design

Based on three main research variables and six main hypothesized paths, this study investigates the relations between IT/IS assessment and the organizational characteristics proposed by Lin et al. [22]. Additionally, ‘‘user satisfaction’’ was also incorporated as the fifth variable in the sixth main hypothesized path to investigate the effects of user satisfaction on IT/IS performance. Based on studies by Lin et al. [22] and Hung [16], this study adopted a survey questionnaire to investigate IT/IS performance in the Taiwanese medical industry, focusing on the relations among IT/IS assessment, IT maturity, IT/IS resource allocation, user satisfaction, and IT/IS performance. As for the questionnaire contents, IT/IS experts and scholars were invited to examine the adequacy of the content, the language use in each questionnaire item, and the validity of the questionnaire. A pilot study was conducted as well. For the pilot study, 48 Taiwan hospitals were randomly selected, and 144 questionnaires were distributed to their IT departments. A total of 39 questionnaires were returned, yielding a response rate of 27.1%. Additionally, the questionnaire was modified, and the internal consistency was tested; with respect to the questionnaire, Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient was 0.911 for IT/IS assessment and 0.947 for IT/IS resource allocation. In all, 38 questions were included in the questionnaire to measure the studied variables and were assessed using a 7-point Likert scale, with ‘‘7’’ denoting ‘‘fully agree’’ and ‘‘1’’ denoting ‘‘fully disagree’’.

The trial questionnaire was modified based on statistical analyses such as the criterion of internal consistency, correlation analysis and homogeneity analysis. Through a series of questionnaire construction processes, the questionnaire was finalized and narrowed down to 37 items. After the questionnaire was completed, 483 questionnaires were distributed to 161 hospitals.

## 3.4. Case study

A case study was performed to examine IT/IS assessment and effect of IT/IS on hospital procedures. A case study investigates a phenomenon within its real-life context and attempts to understand its uniqueness and complexity. Therefore, the case study was performed after analyzing the questionnaires to further verify the questionnaire results, clarify misunderstandings of the questionnaire items, and repeat questions to ensure that the interviewees comprehended the questions fully [20,21]. Because of the use of semi-structured interviews, the respondents were able to discuss and raise issues covered in the questionnaire. The results of the quantitative cross-verification supported the practical methods and procedures in evaluating IT/IS performance in hospitals.

## 3.5. Data analysis and tools

The questionnaire was analyzed using statistical software SPSS 18.0. Structural equation model analysis was then performed using LISREL8.7. Lastly, coding and analysis of the case study were assessed using ATLAS.ti.

## 4. Data analysis and results

The reliability of the questionnaire was determined using Cronbach’s Alpha. The questionnaire items were validated using confirmatory factor analysis, and the goodness of fit was assessed by ‘‘x<sup>2</sup>/df,’’ ‘‘CFI,’’ ‘‘NNFI,’’ ‘‘NFI,’’ and ‘‘RFI’’. The convergent validity was determined using the t-value, composite reliability (CR), and average variance extracted (AVE). Lastly, the relations among variables were examined using structural equation modeling (SEM).

## 4.1. Questionnaires analysis

A questionnaire survey served as the data collection method in this study. The participants were IT department personnel from 161 Taiwanese hospitals. Four hundred and eighty-three questionnaires were distributed by mail in January 2011. Two hundred and thirteen questionnaires were returned from 83 hospitals. Among them, 61 hospitals returned all three questionnaires; eight hospitals returned two questionnaires; and 14 hospitals returned one. Two hundred and eight valid questionnaires were returned, yielding a response rate of 43.06% (Table 1).

## 4.2. Sample structure under analysis

Based on the participants’ data (e.g., gender and age) via use of the nominal scale, sample structural analysis was performed to understand the sample distribution and characteristics (Table 2). Table 2 reveals that more there are male personnel in the hospital IT departments than female personnel. IT personnel range in age from 30 to 49 years old. As for their educational level, most personnel have bachelor’s or master’s degrees. Notably, the supervisors compose 30% of the total sample. Although the distribution by seniority is rather even, relatively fewer personnel have more than 20 years of work experience. Moreover, distribution by hospital type is even. The hospitals are primarily private and foundation hospitals.

The results of the survey questionnaire and case study are as follows:

C1-IT/IS demand: Hospitals implement IT/IS owing to external factors; a few hospitals implement IT/IS to increase their competitiveness. Thus, hospitals should thoroughly consider the potential benefits of IT/IS. Meanwhile, information provided by the interviewees with respect to ‘‘C1-IT/IS demand’’ provides informa tion that the survey questionnaire did not contain.

C2-IT/IS resource allocation: The questionnaire results indicate that IT/IS resource allocation does not significantly affect IT/IS performance. However, according to the interviewees, IT/IS resource allocation affects IT/IS performance. Additionally, an uneven distribution of IT/IS resource results in insignificant H5, as mentioned earlier in the questionnaire results, thus warranting a further study of their relations. Another finding is that IT/IS resource allocation and IT/IS assessment methods and procedures complement each other.

C3-IT/IS Assessment: The interviewees in the case study all agreed that an adequate IT/IS assessment can improve IT/IS performance and increase IT/IS resource allocation, which supports H1 and H4 in the questionnaire results.

## 5. Conclusions

## 5.1. Conclusions of questionnaire results

The study investigated the relations among IT/IS assessment, IT maturity, IT/IS resource allocation, user satisfaction, and IT/IS performance in hospitals. Six hypothesized paths were proposed. Recommendations are made based on the results of the questionnaire and case study for the medical industry.

The results of the structural equation model (SEM) analysis support five of the six hypothesized paths. The hypothesized path in H5 cannot adequately explain why IT/IS resource allocation improves IT/IS performance. Therefore, the researchers further analyzed this relation in the case study and found that IT/IS resource allocation depends on the scale of the hospital. Most interviewees also mentioned that fewer IT/IS resources might adversely affect IT/IS performance in hospitals.

## 5.2. Conclusions of the case study

The case study demonstrated that adequate and official IT/IS assessment methods and procedures and IT/IS resource allocation can improve IT/IS performance. IT/IS assessment methods and IT/IS resource allocation are positively related. The above findings also revealthathospitalsimplementIT/ISowingtopressurefromhospital accreditation organizations and other environmental factors. The scale of IT/IS projects affects IT/IS resource allocation in hospitals.

## 5.3. Research contributions and recommendations

Based on the results of this study, we propose the following recommendations for hospitals regarding IT/IS implementation:

1. Implementing IT/IS assessment in hospitals improves IT/IS performance (i.e., if a hospital intends to increase its IT/IS performance, an official IT/IS assessment method must be implemented and adequate IT/IS assessment procedures must be developed as well). However, the results indicate that most hospital do not adopt an official IT/IS assessment method. Instead, they only conduct a preliminary investigation before implementing IT/IS and do not perform follow-up assessments.

2. This study investigated different factors related to IT/IS performance and developed a model based on an analysis of questionnaire data. This model can serve as a reference model for other hospitals during the IT/IS implementation process. The quantitative analysis results indicate that IT/IS assessment and IT/IS resource allocation are positively related. Moreover, the results of the case study analysis indicate that IT/IS resource allocation and IT/IS assessment are positively related.

3. The case study further demonstrated that the hospitals focus on the short-term benefits of IT/IS. However, further assessment of IT/IS is necessary to generate long-term benefits. The results of the qualitative and quantitative cross-verification indicate that IT/IS assessment, user satisfaction, and IT/IS resource allocation affect IT/IS performance in hospitals. The hospitals evaluated in this study have high IT maturity, have a high probability of implementing IT/IS assessment and perform better in terms of IT/IS resource allocation. However, IT/IS resource allocation is performed less in the IT departments of the hospitals in this study.

## 5.4. Research limitations

Despite its contributions, this study has certain limitations. This study focused mainly on evaluating IT/IS performance in hospitals. However, due to limitation related to human resources, time, finances, experience, and other factors, further research is warranted in the following areas:

1. This study only collected data at a single time point. Thus, without a time constraint, long-term data collection for an entire fiscal year could be used in a comparative analysis.

2. Most Taiwanese enterprises are unfamiliar with IT/IS assessment methods and IT/IS resource allocation, which may have hindered data collection.

3. Because of the time constraints and limited resources, no statistical analysis was conducted to determine whether respondents within the same hospital held different opinions regarding IT/IS.

4. Given the difficulty of interviewing the senior supervisors of the hospitals in the case study, only a few interviews were conducted. We recommend that future research include more interviews to ensure the validity of the results.

## 5.5. Implications

Given the relatively few hospitals in the case study, future research should include more interviews as part of a case study to more thoroughly understand the actual circumstances in the medical industry. Nevertheless, this study identifies factors that affect IT/IS performance. Further research is required to more thoroughly investigate IT/IS assessment methods and develop more reliable assessment methods. For such research, multiple responses from the same hospital may be useful for examining different perspective through the survey questions.
