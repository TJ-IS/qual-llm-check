# Extending the TAM for a t-commerce

Jieun Yu<sup>\*</sup>, Imsook Ha, Munkee Choi, Jaejeung Rho

School of Business, Information and Communication University, 119 Munjiro,Yuseong-gu, Daejeon 305-714, Republic of Korea

Accepted 10 November 2004

Available online 7 January 2005

## Abstract

Broadband communication at home allows television to become an e-commerce medium that merges video, voice, and transactional data. t-commerce is electronically mediated commerce using interactive television. Theoretical and empirical analysis was undertaken and reported here to explain the factors that influence potential users’ adoption of t-commerce by extending the technology acceptance model. The study also compared the factors influencing t-commerce adoption between experienced and inexperienced users. Seven factors were identified: perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, perceived enjoyment, trust, attitude, normative belief of family and friends, and subjective norm. Although the factors were not the same between the two groups, the differences were not large. Perceived enjoyment is the most important factor affecting attitude and behavioral intention toward t-commerce. From a theoretical point of view, the study extended the TAM for t-commerce adoption by potential users in the home environment. Moreover, this study also provided practical implications for t-commerce providers. <sup>#</sup> 2004 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Technology acceptance model (TAM); Perceived enjoyment; Trust; Potential users; Home environment; t-commerce

## 1. Introduction

Convergence of voice and data, fixed and mobile services, and communication and broadcasting is an important recent phenomenon in IT. This occurred because of the increasing importance of personalized services and the need for ubiquitous networks. Digital broadcasting provides high quality video and audio services, multiple channels, and high performance interactive services. Hence, it will cause a large ripple effect in business, the information appliance industry, and the creation of new services in communication and broadcasting.

Development of interactive services on television will allow it to become an e-commerce medium merging video, voice, and transactional data in television commerce (t-commerce), and combine Internet interactivity with high quality television video [30]. t-commerce is electronically mediated commerce using interactive television (iTV). Its purpose is to facilitate the purchase of goods and services in the home using a remote control, or wireless keyboard and mouse, instead of a telephone,

PC, or PDA. It is regarded as a potential killerapplication and is an example of the convergence of communication and broadcasting.

Predictions of the performance of t-commerce are optimistic. Forrester Research, predicted that t-commerce will generate more than US\$ 23 billion in sales by 2005, up from US\$ 130 million in 2000. Digital television connections will grow from 62 million in 2001 to 350 million in 2006, according to Ovum, which also predicted that t-commerce revenues will be worth US\$ 45 billion by 2005. A study by TechTrends Inc. revealed that 46% of consumers are interested in t-commerce services.

t-commerce is expected to change an individual’s purchasing style and to create a new business opportunity for providers. Therefore, research on consumers’ adoption of t-commerce is meaningful in providing business models and consumer analysis.

The technology acceptance model (TAM), proposed by Davis in 1986, has been a widely cited model for predicting and explaining user behavior and IT usage. One objective was to predict user behavior before obtaining experience of the system. Generally, empirical tests have focused on technology acceptance in business environments and systems that are already in use. However, potential users are different from real users; they show different determinants for acceptance, and inexperienced users show different determinants of intention and usage to those of experienced users. Potential users of t-commerce will have different determinants because it is in the home environment. Our research therefore provides a theoretical and empirical analysis to explain factors influencing a potential user’s adoption of t-commerce. Our work had three objectives, to:

(1) propose and verify a TAM specific to t-commerce,

(2) identify the factors that determine consumers’ adoption of it, and

(3) compare the difference in factors influencing its adoption by experienced and inexperienced users.

## 2. Research mode

## 2.1. Theoretical background

The theory of reasoned action (TRA) [23] posited that human behavioral intention is affected by attitude and subjective norms. However, it had a weakness in using abstract concepts such as ‘belief’ and ‘evaluation’ as factors influencing attitude. Davis [7] suggested TAM, now a powerful, robust, and commonly employed model for predicting and explaining user behavior and IT usage [8,15,22, 25,32]. However, it did not include social factors that play an important role in an individual’s attitude. Recently, researchers have proposed modified models that overcome this defect. For instance, Venkatesh and Davis [34] proposed TAM2, which included subjective norm, voluntariness, and image, and cognitive instrumental processes (job relevance, output quality, result demonstrability, and perceived ease of use [PEOU]). They found that social influence and cognitive instrumental processes significantly influenced user acceptance.

Taylor and Todd [31] studied the role of prior experience. They compared the determinants of IT usage for experienced and inexperienced users. Inexperienced users placed a different emphasis on the determinants of intention and usage than experienced users. There were important differences between the groups with regard to the relative influence of perceived usefulness (PU), which was the strongest predictor of intention for the inexperienced group, while PEOU was a more important predictor of attitude. In contrast, experienced users placed less weight on PU. There was a stronger relationship between behavioral intent (BI) and behavior for experienced users because they employed the knowledge gained from their prior experiences.

Research predicting online shopping users’ behavior has also used TAM. Vijayasarathy [21] augmented it to predict consumer intentions to use online shopping. He included compatibility, privacy, security, normative beliefs, and self-efficacy. Compatibility, usefulness, ease of use, and security were found to be significant predictors of attitude. Thus, attitude, normative beliefs, and self-efficacy strongly affected intention to use online shopping. Shih [14] also applied TAM to predict user acceptance of e-shopping on the Web, showing that attitudes toward e-shopping were strongly and positively correlated with user acceptance, and that PEOU and PU significantly determined individual attitudes toward e-shopping.

## 2.2. Hypotheses

Factors influencing consumers’ adoption of technology can vary, depending on the technology, target consumers, and context. The factors of PU and PEOU are insufficient to explain consumers’ adoption of tcommerce because of its characteristic—electronically mediated commerce using television. Taking into account an entertainment characteristic, an intrinsic attribute of television, and trust, an important factor of commerce, we therefore added perceived enjoyment and trust as expected factors if interest. Thus, in the context of t-commerce, we proposed an extended TAM having eight constructs. Since t-commerce is a future service, we omitted the intention–behavior relationship. The model is depicted in Fig. 1.

Because TAM was used as a baseline model, we verified the following five TAM hypothesized relationships in the context of t-commerce.

Hypothesis 1. A consumer’s PEOU of t-commerce affects his/her PU of t-commerce.

Hypothesis 2. A consumer’s PEOU of t-commerce affects his/her attitude toward using t-commerce.

Hypothesis 3. A consumer’s PU of t-commerce affects his/her attitude toward using t-commerce.

Hypothesis 4. A consumer’s PU of t-commerce affects his/her BI to use t-commerce.

Hypothesis 5. A consumer’s attitude toward using t-commerce affects his/her BI to use t-commerce.

Davis et al. [10] introduced the concept of perceived enjoyment to model the role of intrinsic motivation. They reported that it and PU had a significant effect on intention to use a word processing program. Perceived enjoyment was defined as ‘‘the extent to which the activity of using the technology is perceived to be enjoyable in its own right, apart from any performance consequences that may be anticipated’’. Heijden [12] added perceived enjoyment and verified that it positively affected an adopter’s attitude and BI towards personal website adoption. Moon and Kim’s study [18] showed that intrinsic motivation was positively related to attitude towards using the Web. Teo et al. [33] found that perceived enjoyment had an influence on using the Internet with PU. Igbaria et al. [24] found that PU and perceived enjoyment are major reasons for using computer technology. Since a characteristic of television is enjoyment, including interest and pleasure, perceived enjoyment was expected to influence attitude and BI. Hypotheses 6 and 7 were therefore added to verify the effect of perceived enjoyment.

Hypothesis 6. A consumer’s perceived enjoyment of t-commerce affects his/her attitude toward using it.

Hypothesis 7. A consumer’s perceived enjoyment of t-commerce affects his/her BI to use it.

Many different definitions of trust have been proposed [5,19,29], and various studies have found that it is strongly associated with attitude towards products and services and towards purchasing behaviors. We defined trust as party trust and contro trust, based on the study of Tan and Theon [36]. According to their study, transaction trust consisted of trust in the other party and trust in the contro mechanisms. Party trust was subjective trust in a transaction with another party and had both an action and information perspective. We limited party trust to a belief in a t-commerce provider’s after-services, such as cancellation, exchange, and refund arrangements, or to good reputation. Control trust was then trust in control mechanisms that ensured the successful performance of the transaction. We limited this to stable payment, system security, and personal infor mation protection. Since interactive information exchange takes place in the use of t-commerce, perceived variables about system security or personal information protection seriously affect adoption [4]. Even though consumers could purchase goods in a high quality video and real-time environment on iTV, they could not directly examine goods or services. Hence, trust in the product provider or the medium was very important. Gefen [3] suggested that trust in an e-commerce vendor increased a user’s intention to use the vendor’s web site and was the most efficient factor for reducing uncertainty. Grazioli and Jarvenpaa [28] found that customers’ attitude was determined by trust in the context of an Internet shopping mall. Doney and Cannon [26] showed that customer trust was related to intention to use the vendor in the future. Therefore, trust would be an important factor in influencing consumers’ attitudes and BI toward the adoption of t-commerce. Hypotheses 8 and 9 were established to verify the effect of trust.

Hypothesis 8. A consumer’s trust in t-commerce affects his/her attitude toward using it.

Hypothesis 9. A consumer’s trust in t-commerce affects his/her BI to use it.

Many studies have verified that subjective norm has a direct influence on BI. Subjective norm was defined as a ‘‘person’s perception that most people who are important to him think he should or should not perform the behavior in question’’ [16]. Karahanna et al. [6] found that users and potential adopters of IT differed in their determinants of BI, attitude, and subjective norm. Whereas the normative component was a dominant factor of BI, the attitudinal component predominated. Choi et al. [11] proposed TAM for iTV users. PU, opportunity for trial usage, and result demonstrability were factors in users having a positive attitude toward adopting iTV. The subjective norm had the greatest impact on BI. As t-commerce is used in the home environment, users strongly consider the opinions of the family. Potential users regard a significant person’s attitude as one of the decision criteria. Thus, future adopters can be affected by positive word-of-mouth from family, friends, and other adopters [35]. Since potential users have never experienced t-commerce, social influence will greatly affect BI. Hypotheses 10 and 11 were therefore established to verify the effect of normative belief.

Hypothesis 10. Normative belief from family and friends will affect subjective norm of adoption for tcommerce.

Hypothesis 11. Subjective norm will affect BI to use t-commerce.

## 3. Testing and results

## 3.1. Data collection

We conducted an online survey to verify our research model. The respondents consisted of 947 experienced users and 115 inexperienced users of the technology. Experienced users were defined as people who had used other commercial services such as online shopping or home shopping services on television. Inexperienced users were those who have never used such services. After data-filtering to eliminate invalid responses, we had responses from 886 experienced users and 115 inexperienced users. Most questions in our questionnaire were taken from prior studies that had proved their validity and reliability. Each item of the questionnaire was assessed using a 7-point Likert scale with end points of ‘strongly disagree’ and ‘strongly agree’ (Table 1). All items are shown in Appendix A.

## 3.2. Reliability and validity

Our data analysis was conducted using SPSS 10.0 and AMOS 4.0. Internal consistency reliability reflects the stability of individual measurement items across replications from the same source of information; it was assessed by computing Cronbach’s alpha whose coefficients for the eight constructs were above 0.6, indicating a reasonable level of internal consistency among the items making it up [17].

We conducted a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to test convergent validity of each construct. A single factor model was specified for each of the constructs. The measurement model included 36 items describing eight latent constructs: PU, PEOU, perceived enjoyment, trust, normative belief from family and friends, attitude, subjective norm, and BI. Convergent validity of the constructs was demonstrated because all factor loadings exceeded 0.4 on their own constructs (Table 2).

## 3.3. Model fitness

We conducted structural equation modeling using AMOS 4.0 to test the causal model [27]. Multiple fit indices were determined (Appendix B). The traditional chi-square $( \chi ^ { 2 } )$ test results are reported in Table 3. Because the $\chi ^ { 2 } .$ -test is inappropriate for large sample sizes, five other indices were included: GFI, AGFI, RMSR, NFI, and CFI. The test demonstrated a reasonable fit between the data and the proposed structural model for experienced users. Most values were within the accepted thresholds for CFA. However, RMSR is a little above the recommended value. A less reasonable fit was obtained for inexperienced users, although CFI showed a relatively better fit than the other ones.

## 3.4. Hypothesis testing

Structural equation modeling results for experienced users and inexperienced users are presented in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively, and the hypotheses tests are summarized in Table 4.

## 3.4.1. Experienced users

The data fully supports the model and all hypotheses are supported. There are four findings worthy of mention.

Firstly, as many researchers have verified, PEOU has a significant effect on PU. Thus potential users who have experienced similar services may think that PEOU will positively affect PU. Therefore, Hypothesis 1 was not rejected.

Secondly, perceived enjoyment $( \beta = 0 . 5 5 7 , ~ t =$ 15.94), PU $( \beta = 0 . 3 6 5 , \ t = 9 . 6 7 )$ , PEOU $( \beta = 0 . 0 9 4 .$ $t = 2 . 3 6 )$ , and trust $( \beta = 0 . 1 3 6 , ~ t = 4 . 4 1 )$ positively affect attitude. Therefore, Hypotheses 2, 3, 6, and 8 were supported. The attitude of users was largely explained by perceived enjoyment, implying that perceived enjoyment was the most important reason for users having a positive attitude. Enjoyment can be a competitive advantage over other services, such as online shopping and home shopping. As previously suggested, the positive influence of PU and PEOU on attitude was confirmed. Even though trust influences attitude, its affects were less than other factors. Because people think television was considered more trustworthy than other media, such as the Internet, radio, and newspapers [13], and because users could confirm the product indirectly through the high quality video of television, trust in a product or for a seller was not as crucial as in e-commerce.

Thirdly, normative belief from family and friends (b = 0.951, t = 37.90) strongly affected the subjective norm. Therefore, Hypothesis 10 was accepted.

Perceived enjoyment, as a factor that affected BI, was verified in prior studies. Although Karahanna et al. found that subjective norm was a dominant factor of BI to adopt by potential adopters, its relative importance in determining BI was less than that of attitude.

Finally, trust positively affected BI. This result corresponded to prior research.

## 3.4.2. Inexperienced users

The data supported the model and 6 of the 11 hypotheses. Fig. 3 illustrated the estimated coefficients and their significance in the structural model for inexperienced users. Their PEOU (b = 0.828, t = 7.87) affected PU, and normative belief from family and friends (b = 0.972, t = 16.44) highly affected subjective norm in the same manner as for experienced users. Perceived enjoyment (b = 0.709, t = 7.38) and PU (b = 0.439, t = 3.61) positively affected attitude. Perceived enjoyment had more effect than PU. Attitude (b = 0.66, t = 4.33) and subjective norm (b = 0.42, t = 3.93) had a positive influence on the BI. As for experienced users, attitude was a dominant factor influencing BI. The finding of Karahanna et al. that subjective norm is a dominant factor of BI was also confirmed.

## 3.4.3. Comparisons of experienced and inexperienced users

PEOU was an important factor for both experienced and inexperienced users because it positively affected PU, and PU affected attitude. While PU,

PEOU, perceived enjoyment, and trust affected experienced users’ attitude, only PU and perceived enjoyment affected inexperienced users’ attitude. Since the estimated coefficient of the effect of perceived enjoyment on attitude was larger than that of PU in both cases, we could conclude that perceived enjoyment was the most important factor for potential users. While PU, perceived enjoyment, trust, subjective norm, and attitude affected an experienced user’s BI, only subjective norm and attitude influenced an inexperienced user’s BI. Therefore, we found that subjective norm and attitude had a significant impact on potential user’s BI.

Comparison of factors influencing attitude and BI

## 4. Discussion

Our study extended the TAM, by considering the characteristics of t-commerce, and showed that it can be adapted for potential users in home circumstances. A survey was conducted to examine whether experienced and inexperienced users held the same determinants of attitude and BI. PEOU was found to have a direct influence on PU, and it affected a potential user’s attitude toward t-commerce. Therefore, it is important for providers to help users believe that t-commerce is easy to use.

One important finding from this study concerned the determinants of attitude and BI. For experienced users, perceived enjoyment, PU, PEOU, and trust had a positive influence on attitude, and perceived enjoyment had the greatest impact. Perceived enjoyment, PU, trust, subjective norm, and attitude positively affected BI, but attitude has the greatest influence. For inexperienced users, perceived enjoyment and PU positively affected attitude, and attitude and subjective norm had an impact on BI. Therefore, perceived enjoyment and PU were common factors affecting potential users’ attitude towards t-commerce, and perceived enjoyment had a greater influence than PU. Moreover, attitude and subjective norm were common determinants of BI. Since t-commerce is used in the home environment, users will strongly consider the opinions of family, friends, and others.

We conclude that perceived enjoyment is the most important factor affecting attitude and BI toward t-commerce. Perceived enjoyment may be important because television normally provides enjoyment and ease of use, inducing interest and pleasure. As people have already experienced it as a medium of enjoyment, they may think enjoyment is an important factor in t-commerce.

We have successfully applied the extended version of TAM in an IT context (t-commerce) that had not previously been examined. Although our findings provide meaningful implications, our work has limitations. Firstly, we only considered party trust and control trust. Secondly, longitudinal investigation of the factors that determine consumers’ adoption of t-commerce in the home environment would be useful after the introduction of t-commerce: we were only able to study potential users’ adoption.
