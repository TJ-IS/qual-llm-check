# Innovation research in information systems: A commentary on contemporary trends and issues

Indian Institute of Management Calcutta, Kolkata, India

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Article history: Received 4 October 2015 Accepted 29 October 2015 Available online xxx

Keywords: Meta-analysis Review Stages of innovation Technological innovation Theoretical framework

## A B S T R A C T

There is a need to unify the different strands of innovation research in information systems (IS) literature. We analyze 113 articles published over the past 15 years in top 10 IS journals. We classify the literature by (a) using the overall process spectrum of conceptualization from its innovation to its diffusion and (b) using the various theories of innovation referred to and validated in the articles. We identify that innovation diffusion theory is the most popular theory used by researchers. We conclude that future research must focus on the conceptualization and the generation phase of innovation through exploratory or empirical studies.

## 1. Introduction

Innovation has been an active area of research in almost all fields of scholarly research for many decades now [15]. Innovation has been linked to higher productivity, growth, and development of not only firms but also states and nations [42,70]. With that kind of impact, innovation has always been one of the most intriguing research topics for most management academicians as well as practitioners, and the field of information systems (IS) is no exception to this. With the information and communication technologies (ICT) becoming ubiquitous over the past few decades, the impact of ICT on all kinds of innovation is increasing [9]. In this context, innovation as a research context is of a very high value to IS researchers [138]. In this paper, we focus on the innovation research from IS researchers’ point of view.

Our primary aim in this study is to understand and synthesize the developments in the field of innovation research in IS over the past decade and a half. Innovation research in IS has validated and led to further development of multiple theories over time including but not limited to innovation diffusion theory [108], social cognitive theory [14], technology acceptance model [32,126], unified theory of acceptance and use of technology [127], etc. These studies have used both qualitative and quantitative methods for exploratory as well as confirmatory research. The vast amount of literature and theoretical lenses necessitate the need to reflect back on the work produced so as to find the gaps in research. Along with synthesizing the available extant literature, we develop a framework inspired by the product life cycle model to depict the complete innovation cycle from conceptualization to creation of impact. We portray the vast array of literature on innovation based on this framework and identify the gaps where substantial contribution is still needed to better understand the cycle of innovation. Drawing on the innovation literature published in the past 15 years (starting from the year 2000) in the top 10 widely accepted IS journals, we first define the extent of IS research in innovation and propose a framework for analysis. Subsequently, we explain the process of conducting this literature review. We then go on to analyze the literature based on the framework and report the gaps before concluding with directions for future research in the final section.

This study contributes to the available scholarly literature on innovation in two ways. First, it synthesizes the contribution of research published in top IS journals over the past 15 years to the field of technological innovation in IS. Using an easily understandable framework, the organization of extant literature brings together many seemingly unconnected strands of innovation research in IS and helps provide comprehensive information about the research area. Second, the article, through its critical analysis of the literature, brings forth the unexplored territories of innovation research for IS researchers. It offers a plausible research agenda for both academicians and practitioners to further explore the field. We find in our critical analysis of the literature that although the area of innovation has received considerable attention on several aspects including adoption, assimilation, and diffusion of innovation (DoI) through various theoretical lenses, some practical and theoretical questions about the conceptualization and generation of innovative products and processes remain less explored and offer exciting opportunities for future researchers.

## 2. Innovation ecosystem and innovation cycle

Innovation is such a widely studied topic that there exist various acceptable definitions of the term ‘‘innovation’’ [15]. These multiple definitions draw from various theories and ideas. Baregheh et al. [15] identify innovation as a part of organizational change and state that innovation is tightly coupled with change. Dosi [34] defines innovation as new ideas that enable change of production. Rogers [109] draws from the Schumpeterian view and defines innovation as ‘‘introduction of a new product or a ‘qualitative change’ in a product, a process that is new to an industry, the opening up of new markets, or the development of new sources of supplies, or some other significant changes in industrial organizations’’ [108], p. 6. Fagerberg [42] defines innovation as a first attempt to carry out in practice a new idea for a product or a new process. We use the definition of Dosi [35] and define innovation as ‘‘a process of putting to use new ideas to enable change of processes of organization that constitutes of three overlapping stages, i.e., invention, implementation and diffusion.’

To understand the complete context of innovation research, it is necessary to explore the way the innovation ecosystem is organized and understood. In their authoritative literature review of ICT innovations for emerging economies, Xiao et al. present a simplified framework for the innovation ecosystem. This framework can help us visualize the various constituent processes and modules of innovation. They categorize innovation as a threephase cycle of designing, diffusion, and impact [138], as shown in Fig. 1.

Although the above framework offers a very good starting point for understanding the different aspects of innovation, the threephase archetype represents a simplistic understanding of the innovation ecosystem. To understand the finer details of the innovation ecosystem, we expand the ecosystem into its finer constituents. Klepper’s (1996) work on product life cycle links the innovation to product life cycle equating and finding the impact of product and process innovation at different stages of the product life cycle. This conceptualization indicates that the innovation life cycle has stages analogous to the product life cycle beginning at the conceptualization and ending at impact where it again reinforces the eventual conceptualization of the next phase of innovation. The net-enabled business innovation cycle (NEBIC) indicates that emerging IT can affect the business innovation cycle at its various stages. Thus, it can have a profound impact on one of the multiple stages of research including conceptualization, adoption, or assimilation [141]. Fig. 2 characterizes the flow of product and process innovation at various stages of the product development cycle, in turn acting as a very good initiation point for depicting the innovation life cycle [124]. The process of innovation cycle can, therefore, be visualized as a multistage process [73]. The processes can range from the conceptualization of innovation, its generation, and adoption by few experienced users to its potential diffusion within the organization and finally the analysis of its impact and the resultant changes in the firm.

## 3. Method of review

That the field of IS needs more frequent and comprehensive review of the extant literature is a widely accepted fact. However, such a review has to be based on sound scientific principles so as to help advance knowledge in the most scientific manner [134]. To ensure that we produced a ‘‘cohesive framework and not a review comprising of a huge number of citations’’ [146], we have followed the principles of inductive categorization outlined by [36]. These steps can be summarized as follows: (a) selection of relevant scholarly output medium, (b) identification of relevant research papers, (c) categorization and analysis of articles based on their contribution, (d) analysis of previous literature to identify the gaps, and (e) development and reporting of a model to synthesize the findings.

To analyze the literature on innovation over the past 15 years, we have focused on journals that are most widely credited with advanced research in the field of IS. We have relied on journal articles over books or conference articles owing to the widely accepted belief that journals are the most reliable and preferred source of theory development and advancement [96,33]. Journals that are used to identify the relevant articles belong to the Association for Information Systems (AIS) senior scholars’ basket of eight journals [7]. In addition to these eight journals, we have added two other internationally acclaimed journals of great repute: Management Science (MS), which is a premier outlet of research in management sciences, and Information and Management (I&M), one of the oldest and most authoritative outlets of research in IS. Our final list of 10 journals includes (a) European Journal of Information Systems (EJIS), (b) I&M, (c) Information Systems Journal (ISJ), (d) Information Systems Research (ISR), (e) Journal of Association of Information Systems (JAIS), (f) Journal of Information Technology (JIT), (g) Journal of Management Information Systems (JMIS), (h) Journal of Strategic Information Systems (JSIS), (i) MS, and (j) MIS Quarterly (MISQ).

The initial list of articles is generated using a keyword search on the EBSCOHOST search tool. The keywords include ‘‘Technology Innovation,’’ ‘‘Technological Innovation,’’ ‘‘IT Innovation,’’ and ‘‘Information Technology Innovation.’’ The final list of articles is independently reviewed by both authors, and a search of these keywords is also performed on other major databases and search tools including ScienceDirect and Google Scholar<sup>1</sup> to ensure that no relevant article is left out. Innovation in itself is a commonly used term in a variety of research articles. Even with the utmost careful selection of relevant keywords and imposition of strict boundaries, we end up with a total of 872 results for the keywords in the specified time period. Next, we filter the articles by reading the abstracts of the articles closely. We remove all articles that fall under any of the following three categories: (a) duplicates of existing articles in the list, (b) literature review articles, and (c) articles that describe certain aspects of a technological innovation and do not focus on the theory of innovation. Certain articles that we filter out at this stage describe the implications of some special types of innovation. This includes articles that focus on dynamic capabilities of firms and their impact on firm performance. The concept of innovation in many of these papers is found to be incidental and not the central theme. A third round of elimination of some of the articles is performed after reading the complete papers. Using the methods mentioned previously, we finalize a list of 113 papers directly dealing with the core concept of innovation using a variety of theoretical lenses. ## 4. Theoretical and methodological issues in innovation research

IS research on innovation, just like most other research topics in the field, is not limited by methodological or theoretical discourses, and this is visible in the review as well. A vast array of theoretical lenses has been used by researchers in their attempt to understand and analyze the questions related to IS innovation. These theoretical lenses, in addition to the core IS concepts of diffusion of innovation (DoI) and technology acceptance Model (TAM), have been borrowed from areas as diverse as economics (applied economic theory) to strategy (absorptive capacity). The variety of theoretical perspectives has enabled the researchers to look at issues related to innovation from multiple perspectives.

However, some of the above theories have been used at a much higher rate compared with others in innovation-related research. The DoI theory has been used by researchers over the past one and a half decades to fine-tune the concept of innovation diffusion. Adoption and diffusion has been studied in a variety of contexts over the years using both DoI in its simplest form [26,22,92,44,58] to its applied derivatives and other theories such as heterogeneous diffusion model [113] and task technology fit [72]. The theory of DoI has been supplemented in several papers over the years with the addition of three models of DoI [16], a resource-based view of the firm [121], the dynamic capability theory [106], and absorptive capacity [111].

Other aspects of innovation such as conceptualization and generation of innovation and analysis of impact have also been studied, although to a lesser extent. Multiple theories have been used to understand other aspects of innovation. A lack of widely accepted theoretical lenses to analyze these aspects has prompted researchers to propose multiple conceptual and mathematical models for conceptualization of innovation. These include theoretical models for managing new product development [25] and for studying the impact of demand heterogeneity on new innovation [1]. Kietzmann [71] used activity theory in his action research to understand all aspects of innovation from conceptualization to generation of its impact in a firm. Even with the wide array of theories and concepts proposed by researchers, the lack of strong theoretical development (other than DoI) in this research stream is apparent.

## 5. Research on different stages of the innovation cycle

In this section, we explore how the IS research on innovation stacks up against the innovation cycle framework proposed in the earlier section. This is a classification method for organizing the extant literature in this field according to its constituting subtopics [134].

## 5.1. Conceptualization of innovation

Conceptualization of innovation is the first step in the innovation life cycle [107]. The cyclic nature of innovation ensures that any conceptualization effort itself is influenced by previous innovation [56]. This stage is very crucial as it determines the direction that the innovation effort at any organization will take. At this stage, various ideas are typically discussed and evaluated within a firm and, based on the firm’s vision and capabilities, a specific direction is chosen by the leadership [107,5].

In IS-related innovation efforts, conceptualization is a very critical stage. Due to rapid technological advancement, numerous innovative routes are possible for the development of any new offering [27]. A proper evaluation of the strategy and innovation footprint is essential for the success of the firm [71,56,103]. In their research on process innovation, Tarafdar and Gordon [121] analyzed the factors that influence the conception to the deployment of innovation. Most researchers report that the conception of innovation plays a very critical role in the final success of the innovation effort of firms. However, as shown in Table 2, the number of scholarly articles targeting this segment of the innovation life cycle is very small. Relatively less theoretical development is seen in this segment. Owing to the lack of strong theoretical development in the field, we find few examples of empirical research but a large number of conceptual models to explain the conceptualization of innovation.

## 5.2. Generation of innovation

Generation of innovation is the second distinct stage of the innovation cycle as outlined in our framework. At the conceptualization stage, multiple ideas are evaluated and explored and only a few are chosen for implementation. During this stage, systems and processes are put in place to allow a concept to be successfully implemented [95]. The distinctness of the innovation creation and deployment stage demands special institutional systems to be put in place for achieving success [49]. This phase with both its successes as well as failures improves the institutional knowledge and advances its capability to innovate further [95,100]. Using the theory of technological evolution, Chen and Turut [27] have found that the success of previous innovation effort and its impact have a significant bearing on the capability to conceptualize and generate innovation for the firm in future. The processes for generation of innovation have an important impact not only on the future innovation conceptualization efforts but also on the adoption and diffusion of the generated products and processes [110]. The amount of inclusiveness and cohesion that the implementation team imbibes during this phase is found to have a critical impact on the success of the innovation diffusion effort [110].

The IS research literature on innovation creation and generation faces multiple issues related to its lack of theoretical development and empirical analysis. Kietzmann [71], in the research on understanding the innovation process of a firm using activity theory, offers some guidance about the typical processes required for successful creation of innovation within a firm. Drawing from Engestrom’s (1987) model, the paper proposes that innovation as an outcome is dependent on appropriate tools, appropriate rules, community support, required subjects, required objectives, and division of labor. This view suggests that creation of innovation is not a static or a linear process but a complex multidimensional process where the six elements described previously continually interact with each other. In a more recent study, Busquets [18] analyzed the process of innovation creation from its ideation stage using exploratory case studies. This research offers primary evidence of the dynamics of business networks that lead to innovation in firms. Busquets [18] suggested that orchestrating firms use the power of their central position to exert influence over the network and hence can innovate more. This view points to the complex dynamics of multiple factors and adds one important variable to Kietzmann’s research, that is, the firm’s position in the network as an important determinant of its capability for generation of innovation. Qu et al. [101] adopted a business process perspective to analyze two major innovation processes (information technology (IT) insourcing and outsourcing) to establish the association of the business processes to innovation outcomes in firms. IS researchers have also drawn from the social network theory to understand how the social network has provided a mediating effect on the research and development (R&D) effort [135].

Similar to the stage of conceptualization of innovation, the stage of generation of innovation also suffers from a paucity of empirical research validating its constructs as well as firmly grounded theories that extend the understanding of this stage. ## 5.3. Adoption of innovation

Adoption and DoI are often clubbed together and treated as a single stage in many articles. However, there exists a small but important difference between the two stages that mandates a separate analysis of these two stages [52]. The difference is that in the adoption stage, the innovative product or process is adopted by few champions of innovation. These may be the original thought leaders of innovation. The eventual DoI depends considerably on the adopters and the adoption mechanism and the related incentive structure that they follow [52,65].

Although separable for the purpose of deeper understanding, the processes of adoption and DoI are so closely interlinked that most scholarly studies in this area focus on both stages together. Adoption and assimilation of innovation along with DoI are some of the most widely studied areas in the domain of information. The technology acceptance model (TAM), first proposed by Davis [32] and subsequently enhanced and modified [126,127], has offered the foundational principle of innovation adoption by bringing in concepts of perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness. Even after multiple modifications and critiques, these still form the foundation for research related to adoption of technological innovation.

A large number of research articles have analyzed various theoretical underpinnings of the innovation adoption conundrum from multiple perspectives. Undoubtedly, this is one of the betterresearched areas of IS innovation [66]. Goode and Gregor [53] use cluster analysis to form clusters of major factors responsible for driving intentions for adopting a particular innovation. A small selection of three articles shows the depth of methods and theories used in this stage of the innovation cycle. Table 2 provides the required complete representation.

## 5.4. Diffusion of innovation

The DoI theory [108] has been one of the most used theoretical bases in IS research. Multiple variations of the DoI theory have been proposed and empirically tested in the past decade and a half in extant IS literature. Other major theories and concepts employed by researchers for this stage of innovation research include absorptive capacity, network effect [59], resource-based view [143], and institutional theory [60], among others.

A number of methodological streams have been used for researching this stage of innovation [136]. It can be observed from Table 2 that the research articles focusing on DoI and related concepts use established theories from multiple streams and use survey-based methods and statistical analysis to test, validate, and extend the findings. These methods have been deployed in articles based on organizational theories like knowledge-based view of the firm [101], resource-based view of the firm [143], organizational climate theory [133], and transaction cost theory [122]. In order to find unique solutions to some issues of innovation diffusion, some novel theories such as the dialectical analysis [29] and the abduction methodology [18] have been used as well.

## 6. Research gaps and future research areas

The contemporary research on various issues related to innovation, as discussed previously, have been built on the rich theoretical and methodological strength of IS as a discipline. However, there are many areas of research within the area of

innovation that need a much more specialized and in-depth approach, explained as follows:

## 6.1. Initial stages of innovation

The depth offered by the strong research tradition in the area of diffusion and adoption is evident from the large number of articles that focus on these stages of the innovation cycle. However, there is a lack of research on the initial stages of innovation that needs to be dealt with. There is a need to strengthen the theoretical and mathematical models (like [1,99]) proposed by researchers working in this field by use of empirical analysis and firm-level data. The innovation research in IS does not answer questions related to the different antecedents that are essential for innovation generation for products/services and processes. Treating product and process view of innovation separately may enable contemporary research to be more relevant for industry practitioners. At the same time, this approach may lead to uncovering of different drivers for these types of innovation.

## 6.2. Multistage research

As mentioned previously, extant IS research focuses more on the later stages of innovation cycle than on the initial stages. However, one additional gap in the extant IS literature is the lack of multistage research. Articles tend to focus on one or two of the innovation stages and then dive deep into the theoretical causes related to that stage. The impact of research covering multiple stages of innovation, which is currently missing, can be instrumental in understanding how the overall ecosystem in organizations affects the spread of innovation. Research papers such as those by Cho and Mathiassen [28] and Kietzmann [71] are among the very few that take a longitudinal view of innovation. In these papers, multiple stages of innovation have been analyzed by use of exploratory methods such as interpretive case studies and linked to different theories including cultural-history theory and agency theory. However, more rigorous work aimed at understanding the impact of the complete innovation ecosystem using diverse research methods and theoretical lenses is urgently needed.

## 6.3. Individual level versus organizational leve

IS studies related to conceptualization and generation of innovation focus more on the organizational level view. Research in this domain has used theories of organizational behavior and network effect of organizations. However, individual determinants are also quite crucial in determining the success of initial stages of innovation that may include factors such as top management support and perceived usefulness in future. The analysis from individual perspectives on creation of innovation in firms can complement the extant research at the organizational level to develop the understanding further and enable practitioners to make the best use of proposed theories. Similarly, for adoption and diffusion research, the tilt toward determinants at the individual level is highly visible. The same conclusion was drawn in a previous literature review by Jeyaraj et al. [66], and no dramatic change in the focus of the research was visible in the 8 additional years that were covered by our article. A comprehensive balance in the treatment of issues being researched from both individual and organizational perspectives is required for the development of holistic theories in this field.

## 6.4. Geography specific research

Most of the papers covered by this literature review draw their conclusions from samples collected from either European or

American firms or individuals. The strong prevalence of research environments in these geographies can be one of the factors responsible for this prevalence. However, multiple studies mentioned in this paper have found that there is a strong impact of contextual and behavioral issues at all stages of the innovation cycle. Thus, more diverse geographical studies are needed for stronger validation of the theoretical postulates. With so much action seen in the field of IT and related innovation efforts in emerging economies such as China and India, among others, much more geographically diverse research samples will be of greater significance. Another benefit of analyzing technological innovation from different geographies, including emerging economies, is the possibility of analyzing the impact of reverse innovation, which involves adaptation of technological innovation done in emerging economies to satisfy the needs of developed economies. A sound theoretical perspective to understand the underlying determinants and implications of such adaptations will be highly beneficial for both practitioners and academicians.

## 6.5. Societal innovations and innovations at the bottom of the pyramid

Although a substantial segment of IS research, particularly egovernance-related research, focuses on adoption of innovation by government agencies and citizens in general, a sound theoretical understanding of IT innovations directed toward societal systems is presently lacking. Analysis of antecedents and consequences of IT innovations for the bottom-of-the-pyramid segment of the society will greatly extend the reach of research done in the area of technological innovation. Such research will enable the generalizations of theories and practices from the corporate world to diverse communities across the globe.

## 6.6. Diversity of research methods

The research methods used in IS research on innovation-related topics appear to be more or less positivistic in their philosophies. Empirical research methods, as have been reported in Table 1, have a very strong prevalence in IT innovation research. More exploratory research using diverse techniques, such as action research, multi-case studies, and participatory action research, is required to explore the theoretical issues at various stages of innovation. The strong IS tradition of empirical investigation can then be used to further test and validate the propositions as in most scientific traditions. In the initial innovation stages, the impact of upcoming research will be maximized by use of empirical investigation methods to supplement the theoretical exploratory research that is already present.

## 7. Conclusion

Innovation has been an intriguing topic for academicians as well as practitioners for a long time. IS researchers equipped with the solid theoretical foundations in diffusion theory and technology acceptance models have analyzed and made significant contributions to the understanding of innovation adoption and diffusion. From the analysis of the literature published in the top IS journals over the past 15 years, we have found that both organizational and individual determinants of innovation have been researched in the IS literature. However, the extant research appears to be more tilted toward the final stages of the innovation cycle, rather than the initial stages. Innovation cycle is a complex multistage process, and an all-round analysis of all constituent stages of the innovation process is necessary for the advancement of knowledge in this field. More interdisciplinary studies are required to build on the theories of related fields of research such as organizational behavior and strategic management. Such studies can facilitate the development of a sound theoretical basis for future inquiry and enrich the field further.
