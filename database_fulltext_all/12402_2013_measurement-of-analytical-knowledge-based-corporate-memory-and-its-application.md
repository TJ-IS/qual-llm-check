# Measurement of analytical knowledge-based corporate memory and its application

## a b s t r a c t

In the current knowledge-driven economy, businesses are increasingly required to function as knowledge-based organizations. In these organizations, knowledge usually serves as the means for attainment of competitive advantage. It is clear that organizational knowledge has to be carefully managed, and knowledge management measurement is important to businesses. In this paper, corporate memory (CM) is viewed as an organization memory for managing knowledge. Generically and concretely, CM is constructed using analytical knowledge (AK), which is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as the knowledge formatted with 5W1H (who, when, where, what, why, and how). AK is extracted from data storage systems and domain experts by aggregating information, where data analysts, knowledge workers, and knowledge users are involved in a knowledge discovery process. The research objective of this study is to propose a measurement approach, which provides a generic and applicable methodology for measuring the performance and quality of CM. To represent the uncertainty and fuzzy terms in the evaluation environments, and to explicate the invisible impact induced by information technology (IT), the fuzzy set theory is applied. An effective procedure is also proposed to apply the measurement approach in practice.

## 1. Introduction

In current organizations, knowledge usually serves as the means for attainment of competitive advantage. Knowledge resources in an organization are often complex and multifaceted, ranging from tacit components to knowledge that is explicitly represented [21], and including descriptive knowledge, procedural knowledge, and reasoning knowledge [31]. Knowledge quality is important since high-quality knowledge yields high value and returns for the business. It is clear that organizational knowledge has to be carefully managed rather than being left to serendipity [12].

In order to support organizational learning and manage organizational knowledge, CM is viewed as an organization memory for knowledge management. The construction of CM affects effectiveness and ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency of KM directly. However, various de<sup>fi</sup>nitions of CM from various perspectives; for example, organizational behavior [29], management science [3], and organizational learning [13,27] result in constructing CM not in general, but partial manner. To present a precise and concrete de<sup>fi</sup>nition of CM, this paper focuses on analytical knowledge (AK)-based CM [12,13], where AK denotes knowledge extracted from data storage systems and domain experts by aggregating information, where data analysts, knowledge workers (knowledge analysts, knowledge editors, knowledge managers), and knowledge users are involved in a knowledge discovery process. AK is also structured by 5W1H (who, when where, what, why, and how) and is the integration of general information, technical information, captured information, re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge, and feedback knowledge (called <sup>fi</sup>ve basic elements). The AK-based CM provides a fundamental and standard procedure to develop CM.

The quality of organizational knowledge and information determines the value of organizational knowledge assets [6]. The evaluation of knowledge quality has become an increasingly important issue in measurement researches [1,26]. Measuring the knowledge, e.g., AK, in CM is crucial to CM measurement. In addition, it is obvious that the quality of CM affects the core competence of a business [25], and enhances a business's competitiveness in the marketplace [16].

To date, studies on measurement of knowledge quality have been scarce, and there is not much literature on measurement of CM. The research objective in this paper is to propose a measurement approach to the performance and quality of CM. We expect that our research results can contribute to several aspects. First, this research <sup>fi</sup>ts to the nature of the AK generation processes, which provide a fundamental and standard procedure to develop CM. Second, this research integrates the evaluation and assessment mechanisms based on a solid and complete foundation. Third, this research is able to represent the uncertainty and fuzzy terms in the evaluation environment, and to explicate the invisible impact induced by information technology (IT). Fourth, this research provides an effective procedure to support the measurement approach in practice. The value of the proposed measurement approach in managing information/knowledge and developing strategies in businesses is shown using illustrated examples. With the strategies, business is expected to enhance competitiveness.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. AK-based CM is introduced and measurement approaches are surveyed in Section 2. The foundation of CM measurement including a measurement framework and related measurement concepts is presented in Section 3. The methodology that includes the evaluation and assessment methods is proposed in Section 4. Examples of the measurement approach applied in practice are illustrated <sup>fi</sup>nally in Section 5.

## 2. Literature review — CM studies

In this section, the AK-based CM is introduced <sup>fi</sup>rst. Measurement approaches are surveyed and their weaknesses are summarized next.

## 2.1. AK-based CM

The de<sup>fi</sup>nitions of CM in previous literature are developed from various perspectives and implemented in different applications. A precise and concrete description about the intrinsic properties [7] of CM, for example, descriptions of inputs, outputs, controls, and mechanisms, is required for CM measurement. It is useful to construct a measurement framework so that the scope of measurement could be clari<sup>fi</sup>ed, the complexity of the measurement could be reduced, and the measure target could be focused, which results in precision of the measurement [7]. In this paper, from the decision-making perspective [24], the relativity of decision/activity perspective [25], and the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts to the organization [27], CM is viewed as “the contents of organizational memory” that the various types of knowledge in the repository are accumulated over time. CM is also viewed as “the impact induced by organizational memory” that implies by which knowledge from the past is brought into to bear on present activities or decisions and leading in higher levels of organizational effectiveness to increase competitiveness of a business.

Huang et al. [13] and Huang and Kuo [12] present an explicit structure of CM named “AK-based CM” which provides a generic way to construct CM for businesses. Since AK can <sup>fl</sup>exibly externalize most knowledge and is helpful for decision support and problem-solving to improve businesses' competitiveness [12], a CM is constructed using AK in this paper.

AK in the CM is generated at different stages, with each stage involving contemporary technology and management tools. The process of generating AK with the child activities is introduced in Fig. 1 [12,13], where IDEF3 (DEFinition for Process Description Capture Method) is used. In IDEF3 modeling, each activity includes an input, output, controls, and mechanisms [14]. Events and data (inputs) are transformed into AK (outputs). Necessary controls (top arrows) and mechanisms (bottom arrows) support AK generation. A four-activity process of generating AK can construct CM step by step. Each activity has speci<sup>fi</sup>c controls and mechanisms to support operations of the activity and produce outputs as the inputs of the next activity. In essence, AK is the integration of <sup>fi</sup>ve basic elements: general information, technical information, captured information, re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge, and feedback knowledge and serves as a capstone in CM.

## 2.1.1. Activity 1: event classification and event subject analysis

Activity 1 includes two procedures: Event classi<sup>fi</sup>cation and Event subject analysis. First, event classi<sup>fi</sup>cation procedure is responsible for determining the relationship between an event occurring during business operations and a business organization. In this procedure, events will occur during business operations produce new data, the event transferring function (f ) will transfer the input data of business organization (o) and business process (p) to the analytical event (E). At the same time, the data storage system transfer function $\left( f _ { D } \right)$ will transfer the input data of business organization (o) and business process (p) to data storage system (D) as well. Second, event subject analysis procedure aims at determining the relationship between a subject and <sup>fi</sup>ve event characteristics (5W), including $w _ { 1 }$ (What): analytical objects of an event, $w _ { 2 } \colon$ places of an event (Where), $w _ { 3 }$ (Who): people of an event, w (When): timing of an event, and w (Why): motivations of an event. In this procedure, the subject transferring function $( f _ { S } )$ will transfer the aforementioned <sup>fi</sup>ve parameters (from w<sub>1</sub> to w<sub>5</sub>) to analytical subject (S).

In activity 1, a database management system (DBMS) will support all operations of databases and ensures that databases can work seamlessly to meet the needs of all users. In addition, the data warehouses and data marts will obey the controls in extracting the data of databases. Output of activity 1 is the general information, which is described as fundamental in the process of AK generation. General information is not the most important information, but it is indispensable.

## 2.1.2. Activity 2: information technology (IT) analysis and application activity

Activity 2 includes two procedures: IT analysis and Information technical application. First, information technology analysis is accountable to decide the relationship between analytical technology and analytical subjects. In this procedure, technology transferring function $( f _ { T } )$ will transfer the input data of analytical subject (S) to the $j ^ { t h }$ analytical technology of the $i ^ { t h }$ analytical <sup>fi</sup>eld $( T _ { j } ^ { i } )$ . At the same time, data storage system transferring function $\left( f _ { D } \right)$ will transfer the input data of analytical subject (S) to the $i ^ { t h }$ data storage system (D ). Second, information technical application is mainly used to capture information that depends on analytical subjects, analytical technology, data storage systems, and technical experts' knowledge. Also, it models two types of expertise provided by technical experts, an analytical subject-orientated and an analytical technology-orientated expertise. In this procedure, captured information transferring function $\left( f _ { C } \right)$ will transfer the input data of analytical subject (S), the analytical technology of the analytical <sup>fi</sup>eld $( T _ { j } ^ { i } )$ , the $\overline { { i ^ { t h } } }$ data storage system (D ), and professional technique $\left( P _ { t } \right)$ to captured information (I ). Also, two different types of expertise, professional subject $\left( P _ { 1 } \right)$ and professional technology (P ) are generated by the professional function (P). The professional function (P) will transfer the input data of analytical subject (S) to professional subject $\left( P _ { 1 } \right)$ and transfer the input data of the analytical technology of the analytical <sup>fi</sup>eld $( T _ { j } ^ { i } )$ and the data storage system (D ) to professional technology (P ).

The mission of activity 2 is continuing learning and searching useful information from general information. The data analytical techniques that are employed by this activity come from many different <sup>fi</sup>elds, for example, statistics, arti<sup>fi</sup>cial intelligence, and database <sup>fi</sup>elds. Each <sup>fi</sup>eld is not mutually exclusive but related to each other. Therefore, this activity can eliminate unrelated information and identify the relationship between different types of information, as well as forecast trends. Output of activity 2 is the technical information and the captured information.

## 2.1.3. Activity 3: refinement activity

Activity 3 is responsible for re<sup>fi</sup>ning knowledge captured by subject-oriented experts' information. The re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge combines captured and technical information and relative <sup>fi</sup>eld experts' intelligence. In this activity, re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge transferring function $\left( f _ { R } \right)$ will transfer the input data of captured information (I ), subject professionalism (P ), and the $i ^ { t h }$ <sup>fi</sup>eld content category of subject-related issues $( C ^ { S } { _ { i } } )$ to re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge $\left( K _ { R } \right)$ . A communication platform provides an interface for subject-oriented experts to exchange opinions, construct subject-oriented expertise, and generate re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge. Knowledge workers (knowledge analysts, knowledge editors, and knowledge managers) enable re<sup>fi</sup>nement activity to convert, exteriorize, store, and manage re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge. Knowledge analysts are in charge of knowledge conversion, while knowledge editors are in charge of knowledge externalization. Knowledge managers are responsible for knowledge storage and management. Output of activity 3 is the re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge.

## 2.1.4. Activity 4: feedback activity

Activity 4 includes two procedures: User feedback and Analytical knowledge. First, user feedback procedure aims at gaining the feedback knowledge, the best supporting knowledge except re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge, and friendly towards knowledge users, which is obtained from re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge innovated by users including experts and non-experts in a speci<sup>fi</sup>c <sup>fi</sup>eld. In this procedure, feedback knowledge transferring function $\left( f _ { F } \right)$ will transfer the input data of re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge $\left( K _ { R } \right)$ , people who are organization-related and users of re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge $( \overline { { P } } )$ , and the $i ^ { t h }$ <sup>fi</sup>eld content category of user-related issues $( C _ { i } ^ { P } )$ to feedback knowledge $( K _ { F } )$ . Second, analytical knowledge procedure aims at integrating general information $( I _ { G } ) ,$ , technical information $\left( I _ { T } \right)$ , captured information $\left( I _ { C } \right)$ , re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge $\left( K _ { R } \right)$ , and feedback knowledge (K<sub>F</sub>) into analytical knowledge (AK).

In activity 4, a communication platform provides an interface for knowledge users to present their opinions, ideas, feelings, etc. on re-<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge and then integrate re<sup>fi</sup>ned knowledge with feedback to feedback knowledge. Knowledge workers (knowledge analysts, knowledge editors, and knowledge managers) enable feedback activity to convert, exteriorize, store, and manage feedback knowledge and generate AK. Output of this activity is AK, the <sup>fi</sup>nal result of AK generation, which can be expanded through repeats of AK generation.

Additional details of AK-based CM can be found in Huang et al. [13] and Huang and Kuo [12]. Based on the studies mentioned, the measurement approach of AK-based CM is proposed in this paper.

## 2.2. Measurement studie

The measurement of both knowledge quality and KM has become increasingly important since it promotes strategic organizational learning [20,26] and generates the capabilities required to meet customer expectations [17]. Two facets are often referred in measurement research: evaluation and assessment. The measurement of intrinsic properties of a system is called evaluation [7]. Evaluation is directly concerned with evaluating the system itself, and is suitable for studying the intrinsic properties of systems. It focuses on the measurement of the intrinsic properties of CM in the evaluation way, including its quality, e.g., consistency, conciseness, validity, completeness, and incrementalism. Evaluation involves two concepts: veri<sup>fi</sup>cation and validation. The measurement of the system, actual usage and utility within a given organization is called assessment [7] such as cost effectiveness, bene<sup>fi</sup>t and utility analysis, user acceptance, and IT's impact on an organization. Assessment is not directly concerned with measuring the system itself, but rather with the appreciation and measurement of the modi<sup>fi</sup>cations induced by the usage of the system in organizations where the system is applied. Measuring CM in the assessment way focuses on the performance of CM and its impact on individuals, work teams, and the organization as a whole in every critical aspect.

A number of quality measurement approaches, techniques and tools have been developed, such as veri<sup>fi</sup>cation and validation, static analysis techniques, testing methods, and reliability assessment approaches to knowledge-based systems (KBS's) [4,7–9,22], knowledge management systems (KMS's) [28], and management of knowledge (KM) [17]. However, the actual usage and utility of the system within a given organization are not considered in most studies, making the measurement outcome incomplete and yielding an unsatisfactory result for the measuring system. To date, few efforts have been devoted to the measurement of CM and a new measurement approach is required because of inconsistence and variation in measurement between the aforementioned approaches and the AK generation processes. A solid and complete foundation (e.g., a measurement framework to integrate the evaluation and assessment mechanisms) and a general and applicable methodology for the measurement approach are needed [7] so is an effective procedure for performing the measurement approach in real cases [7]. The fuzzy set theory is required to represent the uncertainty and fuzzy terms in the evaluation environments, and application of the S-shaped logistic model is required to explicate the invisible impact induced by information technology (IT) [23]. Furthermore, previous measurement approaches of knowledge quality lack robustness and therefore, are not useful for measuring AK.

In this study, a novel approach is proposed for measuring AK-based CM. The proposed measurement approach in this study promises fourfold: (i) a precise and concrete de<sup>fi</sup>nition of AK-based CM, CM measurement, and related concepts and terminology, (ii) a way to determine what characteristics of AK-based CM are to be measured and what casual relations are to be considered in measuring them, (iii) a general and applicable methodology for measuring the intrinsic properties of CM (evaluation approach) and actual usage and utility of the CM within a given organization (assessment mechanism), where, what and how to measure are speci<sup>fi</sup>ed, and (iv) a de<sup>fi</sup>nite and effective procedure for effective application of the measurement approach to real cases.

## 3. Foundation for measuring AK-based CM

The two facets, evaluation and assessment, are both primarily important in practice, but they are quite different in principle [7]. Both of them have different goals, deal with different issues, and involve different methods. From the perspective of measurement in quality and performance, the different approaches may produce different bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and outcomes. However, from the perspective of systematicness, it never considers one facet of measurement only, but both evaluation and assessment. In this study, these two facets of measurement are not separated but merged. An integrated framework combining evaluation and assessment (i.e., behavior and base measures) is proposed. The proposed framework measures the AK-based CM from the systemthinking viewpoint [30], and reduces con<sup>fl</sup>icts of goals, issues, and methods between the evaluation and assessment processes. The behavior measure of AK employs the assessment way to assess the impact of CM on the organization from the viewpoint of an organization's performance and competitiveness. The base measure of AK utilizes the evaluation way to evaluate the intrinsic properties of CM systematically from the viewpoint of an organization's intellectual capital. Note that in this study, ‘measurement’ denotes either evaluation way or assessment way for later description of the proposed measurement approach.

The framework considers the impact induced by CM (assessment) and the intrinsic properties of CM (evaluation) (Fig. 1).

According to the descriptions of the expected CM impact in users' mind, a set of design objectives is developed to satisfy the users' requirements. According to the objectives of AK-based CM, generation activities are produced to apply expertise, users' feedback, and design rules to this platform, and a mechanism is developed to support the operations of generating activities. Through the series of generating procedures, AK-based CM is constructed and comprises behavior and bases. Users conduct behavior in their operations, e.g., making decisions, research and development (R&D), and learning. Behaviors are affected by bases, which denote the factors for assessing the impact on the organization induced by CM from the viewpoint of an organization's performance and competitiveness.

There are two paths for implementing CM measurement in Fig. 2, including: (i) the direct path (path a), called performance path (assessment way) which assesses AK-based CM behavior versus the expected impact of CM, and (ii) the indirect path (paths b1 and b2) called quality path (evaluation way), where (b1) evaluates the generation activities versus design objectives and (b2) evaluates actual CM bases versus generation activities. In addition, path (a) assesses the performance of CM in a given organization; while paths (b1) and (b2) evaluate the intrinsic properties of CM.

To clarify P&Q measurement, the following four requirements in developing the methodology must be satis<sup>fi</sup>ed.

(1) Property 1: A de<sup>fi</sup>nition of P&Q of AK-based CM, e.g., using the taxonomy for P&Q is required to be in detail through decomposition of the factors.

(2) Property 2: Measuring P&Q of AK-based CM is achieved by measuring independently its factors to obtain the measures, which take weight into account.

(3) Property 3: The indirect path (quality path) is required to support the measurement since behavior is not easy to assess along with the direct path (performance path).

(4) Property 4: Measuring P&Q of CM should be considered as the many-to-many causal relations between behavior components (BCs) and base components (BaseCs) and should be included in the assessment of CM behavior since BaseCs have a signi<sup>fi</sup>- cant impact on the assessment of BCs.

Properties 3 and 4 have a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant impact on the measurement of behavior components (BCs) and base components (BaseCs), which are not considered in previous literature. For example, property 3 requires two different measurement methods to measure BCs and BaseCs, respectively. Since evaluation and assessment are quite different from their measurement concepts, it is inappropriate to measure BCs and BaseCs in the same way. Property 4 shows that the measurement structure for assessing BCs is much more complex than that for assessing BaseCs; and the causal relations between BCs and BaseCs should be considered

To apply the measurement framework (Fig. 2), the performance and quality (P&Q) taxonomy is partitioned into behavior and bases, and the causal relations between behavioral and basic components are explicitly identi<sup>fi</sup>ed. To present the taxonomy in detail, P&Q of CM is de<sup>fi</sup>ned through decomposition of the factors (Fig. 3). From the hierarchical structure (e.g., top to down) in Fig. 3, P&Q of AK-based CM is decomposed to level by level and represented by different <sup>fi</sup>elds of AK. Each AK <sup>fi</sup>eld has the <sup>fi</sup>ve basic elements. Each element of AK is divided to behavior and bases at the <sup>fi</sup>nal stage of decomposition process. Advantage of the proposed taxonomy allows focusing on one speci<sup>fi</sup>c aspect of P&Q at an instance, thus reducing the complexity of the measurement task and improving the precision of the measurement.

The base of CM in<sup>fl</sup>uences the ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency of the behavior in CM, and is required to be designed in such a way to meet the requirements of the organization/users. The components of P&Q are partitioned into two different classes, namely BCs and BaseCs, denoted in the leaves of the P&Q taxonomy descending from behavior class and base class, respectively. They play critical and basic roles in the measurement of AK-based CM.

$$
\begin{array}{l} \text {Measurement of P\&Q of AK elements} \\ = \text {Assessment of BCs + Evaluation of BaseCs} \end{array}\tag{11}
$$

Eq. (11) assumes that components in the same class are independent of each other and BaseCs are causal dependence BCs. Each BaseC may affect one or more BCs. Each BC may be affected by one or more BaseCs. Therefore, the causal relations between BaseCs and BCs are many-to-many relations in Fig. 4.

## 4. Methodology for measuring AK-based CM

The measurement environment comprises three different parts, the methodology, the measurement team, and users (Fig. 5). The Performance&Quality factor (PQF) [7] is constructed to support the measurement of AK-based CM <sup>fi</sup>rst. Secondly, performance of CM is assessed by users with the behavior performance index (BPI) method, which assesses the performance of actual usage and utility of CM in an organization. Thirdly, quality of CM is evaluated by the measurement team applying the criterion-referenced evaluation method, which evaluates the quality of intrinsic properties of CM. Based on the previous results, the aggregated measurement of AK-based CM is generated at <sup>fi</sup>nal.

## 4.2. Behavior assessment of AK elements

In this section, the method for assessing the impact of CM on the organization/users and the performance of CM is presented.

## 4.2.1. Assessment of BCs

An approximation of the measure $m _ { j } ( X )$ of $B C _ { j }$ is obtained using the direct path. $m _ { j } ( X )$ is obtained by assessing users' feeling and computed as the distance between the behavior components and the expected impact of CM in users' mind (Fig. 6). While the smaller the distance, the better is the performance of BCs.

According to the de<sup>fi</sup>nition of CM, measurement of CM is divided into two parts. The <sup>fi</sup>rst part, “the contents of CM”, indicates the quality evaluation of the bases of each AK element (to be discussed later). The second part, “the impact induced by CM”, is divided into two sub-parts. One is the correlation of BC with business activities (CBA) while the other is the impact of BC on promoting effectiveness of business operations (EBO). To assess BCs, the two sub-parts are taken to de<sup>fi</sup>ne the CM's impact on the organization/users and the BPI method which is applied.

## 4.2.2. BPI method for assessing m (X)

Peffers and Dos Santos [23] proposed a method using the S-shaped logistic model to measure IT's impact. The model can be applied to

CM since the knowledge assets are limited resources like IT-based systems [17]. That is, the increase rate would naturally saturate in the decline stage or when competitors have superior CM in response to the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts obtained by innovators.

According to Peffers and Dos Santos [23], the behavior performance index (BPI) method is proposed for assessing the performance of m (X). The BPI, de<sup>fi</sup>ned as a logistic function, is involved in two parts for determining the CM's impact (CMI) on the organization/users: the correlation of BC with business activities and the impact of BC on promoting effectiveness of business operations. When the CMI ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency increases, the BPI also increases. Higher BPI represents better performance of BC with users more satis<sup>fi</sup>ed.

The CMI at time t is proportional to the BPI gained at time t−1 $( \mathrm { i } . \mathsf { e } . , B P I _ { t - 1 } )$ relative to the maximum possible BPI gains from the CMI (i.e., 1) and the remaining BPI yet to be gained $( \mathrm { i } . \mathsf { e } . , 1 - B P I _ { t - 1 } )$ This description of BPI over time t is expressed as [17]:

$$
\frac {d B P I}{d t} = - \mathrm{CMI} (1 - B P I _ {t - 1})\tag{17}
$$

where the CMI is a term denoting ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency of BC's impact on the organization/users. Solving Eq. (17) for BPI yields:

$$
B P I _ {t} = \frac {1}{1 + e ^ {a + - C M I _ {t}}}.\tag{18}
$$

Eq. (18) is the S-shaped logistic model, where ‘1’ is the upper bound of the BPI from the CM. It assumes that constant ‘a’ is zero because each business often initiates a very small BPI. The <sup>fi</sup>nal expression for BPI is in Eq. (19).

$$
B P I _ {t} \frac {1}{1 + e ^ {- C M I _ {t}}}\tag{19}
$$

The CMI in Eq. (20) is a summary of the relative weight of the factor (RWF) of each sub-impact of BC multiplied by the average factor value (AFV) of the corresponding sub-impact of BC. That is,

$$
\mathrm{CMI} = \mathrm{RWF} _ {\mathrm{CBA}} \mathrm{AFV} _ {\mathrm{CBA}} + \mathrm{RWF} _ {\mathrm{EBO}} \mathrm{AFV} _ {\mathrm{EBO}}\tag{20}
$$

where CBA is the correlation of BC with business activities, and EBO is the impact of BC on promoting effectiveness of business operations.

CMI has impact on the ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency/effectiveness of users' activities and organizational operations. Whenever the value of CMI increases, the index BPI also increases, resulting in turning general businesses into knowledge-intensive businesses. The BPI assesses the behavior performance of AK-based CM, and it is related directly/indirectly with businesses' ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency and effectiveness. Note that the BPI method postulates that businesses with good CM also have high BPI, and those with larger BPI lead to higher ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency and effectiveness and vice versa.

To assess the CBA in CMI, the questionnaire is designed by answering the following three questions. (1) Does the current corporate memory have high correlation with current activities and operations? (2) Does the use of corporate memory enable users to work ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently? (3) Does the introduction of corporate memory enable the group users to operate ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently? To assess the EBO, the questionnaire is designed by answering the following two questions. (1) Can the use of corporate memory help users improve the quality of their tasks? (2) Can the support of corporate memory improve the important performance indexes that the business concerns, for example, stock price, R&D expenditure, price earnings ratio (PER), and return on investment (ROI)? The questionnaire uses a seven-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) through 4 (neutral) to 7 (strongly agree), for assessing BPI. Advantages of the BPI method for the assessment of BCs are as follows.

• It is a generic method for every business to understand IT's impact on the organization/users.

• Application of the S-shaped logistic model explicates the invisible IT's impact, for example impact on organizational effectiveness, ef-<sup>fi</sup>ciency and competitiveness.

• The BPI for each BC enables managers to identify the BC's S-shaped curve and understand the current status of each BC.

• According to the S-shaped logistic model, the forecast of BCs' development is possible, which also leads to effective allocation of knowledge assets.

## 4.3. Base evaluation of AK elements

BaseCs are evaluated by the measurement team which comprises technology-oriented experts, subject-oriented experts, and knowledge users. The evaluation criteria are determined by the proposed method, called the criterion-referenced evaluation method. The criterionreferenced evaluation method contains two parts: the fuzzy voting method and the fuzzy grading method.

## 4.3.1. Evaluation of BaseCs and criteria for evaluating BaseCs

In Fig. 7, according to Wang [28], the desired criteria aid the measurement team to evaluate BaseCs. The measurement task is straightforward if there are reference criteria (standards) against which system outputs can be compared [7]. In this study, two sets of validation criteria are proposed. One is derived from the content of knowledge/ information and the other is according to connection of knowledge/ information. The validation criteria of “knowledge/information” content include correctness, consistency, presentation, deepness, broadness, novelty, immediateness, importance, meaningfulness, classi<sup>fi</sup>cation/ indexes, relativity, exactness, instructions, and capacity, referred to the K/I set. Criteria “consistency" is divided into criteria of content consistency, format consistency, and terminology consistency. Criteria “relativity” is included in the behavior assessment in this study and is not considered in the base evaluation. Validation criteria of “knowledge/ information” connection include reference sources, correct experts' catalogs, obtainable experts' catalogs, and novel experts' catalogs. The experts' catalog denotes that users can contact experts when they want according to the catalog. The 17 knowledge/information validation criteria are included in the reference evaluation criteria for base evaluation, with some of them selected as consensus criteria.

To reduce the number of evaluation criteria, a method for representing the imprecise concepts for subjective judgment and to select their preferred evaluation criteria (from the 17 reference evaluation criteria), called the unanimously selected criteria, is required. Furthermore, to avoid bias induced by the dominated control of a few members in evaluation, and to encourage members to participate in the whole evaluation process, an all-member-free-involvement method for appraising the unanimously selected criteria is required. To consider equal contribution without interruption during the decision-making process and acceptable team size, the fuzzy set-based method developed by the reference evaluation criteria is applied in this study for members to vote and to appraise the consensus. Consequently, the evaluation of BaseCs is achieved.

Critical properties are developed to construct the PQF. The BPI method assesses the performance of BCs. It contains the questionnaire to obtain CMI, and the S-shaped logistic model to transform CMI into BPI. Criterion-referenced evaluation method evaluates the quality of BaseCs with a measurement team. It contains fuzzy voting method to obtain the consensus criteria from the reference evaluation criteria, and fuzzy grading method to evaluate the quality of BaseCs by the unanimously selected criteria. Through the PQF, the <sup>fi</sup>nal measurement results of AK-based CM are generated by combining the measures of BCs and BaseCs.

During the years, the Vita KM system gradually lost its performing ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency due to the fast growing rate of data volume in ABC's KMS. ## 6. Conclusion

CM with high P&Q can lead a business to higher levels of organizational ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency/effectiveness and to increase competitive advantage in the marketplace. AK provides a precise and concrete de<sup>fi</sup>nition for CM, and is useful to construct a measurement framework. In this study, the measurement approach including a generic and applicable methodology has been proposed. Performance of CM has been assessed by the BPI method, and quality of CM has been evaluated by the criterion-referenced evaluation method. The de<sup>fi</sup>nite and effective procedure has been applied to the measurement approach in practice. The measurement approach shows a great promise that a business can manage CM and develop strategies for an organization's intellectual capital ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently and effectively.

The following issues require further studies. First, subjective weights have been used frequently by members of the measurement team, users, criteria, and measures. It is necessary to apply an objective method for supporting the determination of these weights. Second, a systematic mechanism is needed to analyze and study the evolution of AK-based CM over time. For example, mining operations on the different types of AK evolve and which could be executed after a particular event, e.g., Event 911 in order to discover more potential knowledge. Lastly, a group decision support system (GDSS) can be utilized to support the measurement team in evaluating BaseCs more ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently and effectively. Further development of GDSS is useful and necessary in the future.
