# Exploring the Mechanisms of Live Streaming Technology and Impulse Buying Tendencies: Do Zero Moments of Truth Value, Trust, and Emotional Engagement Matter?

Xin-Jean Lim,<sup>1</sup> Jun-Hwa Cheah,<sup>2</sup> Nisreen Ameen<sup>3</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Sun Yat-Sen University, China / Universiti Putra Malaysia, Malaysia, lim.xinjean@yahoo.com <sup>2</sup>University of East Anglia, UK / Sohar University, Oman, j.cheah@uea.ac.uk, jackycheahjh@gmail.com <sup>3</sup>Royal Holloway, University of London, UK / The University of Jordan, Jordan, nisreen.ameen@rhul.ac.u

## Abstract

Live streaming e-commerce (LSE) platforms are information systems that blend real-time entertainment with instant purchasing to offer novel experiences to users. Drawing on cognitive appraisal theory, this study explains how LSE experiences stimulate users’ emotions and impulse buying behavior through a sequential process consisting of primary appraisal, secondary appraisal, and behavioral outcomes. Using a sample drawn from millennial users (18-35 years old), we conducted two studies. In the initial pilot investigation (n = 425), we examined users’ perceptions and behaviors when using LSE platforms; in the main study (n = 200), we explored the potential mediating and moderating factors affecting impulse buying tendencies. Overall, the findings show that LSE affordances predict the zero moment of truth (ZMOT) value on LSE platforms. The results also indicate that both trust in the streamer and emotional engagement play a mediating role in the relationship between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies. Finally, we found that trust in the LSE platform plays an important moderating role in strengthening the prediction of impulse buying tendencies. This study concludes with recommendations for future LSE research and practice.

Keywords: Cognitive Appraisal Theory, Live Streaming Platforms, Impulse Buying Tendencies, Zero Moment of Truth, LSE Affordances

## 1 Introduction

Live streaming e-commerce (LSE) platforms have transformed online retail by incorporating sophisticated audiovisual techniques, such as multiple camera angles, professional lighting, and high-fidelity sound into buying experiences. These platforms feature rich interactive elements that provide users with an immersive and realistic experience, simulating physical presence in a virtual shopping environment (Gu et al., 2024). Streamers, acting as virtual sellers, use LSE to reach wider audiences and facilitate engaging interactions with users to sell goods (Luo et al., 2024). By leveraging the immediacy and interactivity of live video, streamers can showcase product and service features while promptly responding to user comments and inquiries during streaming sessions. This real-time engagement allows streamers to build relationships with their audience, creating a unique opportunity to go beyond conventional online shopping and e-commerce experiences.

Building upon these immersive features, the real-time and interactive capabilities of LSE platforms not only foster relationship building but also provide streamers with opportunities to create a sense of urgency and excitement among users, which can lead to impulse decision-making. Across various fields of study, impulse buying is recognized as a powerful strategy for businesses. Streamers frequently leverage this phenomenon by employing promotions and limitedtime offers, which are among the most effective promotional strategies to induce impulse buying (Han, 2023). In fact, by creating a sense of urgency, streamers can effectively leverage the LSE platform to expedite inventory levels for product availability and sales.

To date, numerous studies have examined ways to stimulate impulse buying in various digital commerce contexts (i.e., electronic, social, and mobile commerce) (Lim et al., 2020, 2021; Lo et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2024). However, no consensus exists on the psychological processes triggering this behavior on interactive platforms such as LSE. The features of LSE change the shopping experience in novel ways. For example, while traditional platforms only allow customers to browse static product pages, LSE platforms offer real-time, two-way communication between streamers and users (Wymer et al., 2021), which creates a fluid and unpredictable shopping environment where purchase decisions can be immediately influenced. LSE platforms also integrate multiple modalities—live video, synchronous chat, demonstrations, and social interactions—into one cohesive experience (Yang et al., 2023a). This multimodal approach can trigger different psychological processes compared to traditional browsing. Understanding this underlying process is crucial for streamers aiming to convert “viewers” into “buyers” and sustain the LSE business model (Luo et al., 2024; Miranda et al., 2024). Such insights can also help streamers differentiate themselves in a competitive market.

To explore this phenomenon, this study draws on cognitive appraisal theory to elucidate the underlying mechanisms through which streamers can amplify users’ impulse buying tendencies. Cognitive appraisal theory posits that individuals’ emotional responses and subsequent behaviors are determined by their evaluation of events in relation to their goals and well-being (Lazarus, 1991; Roseman & Smith, 2001). In the context of LSE, this theoretical lens enables the examination of how platform-specific stimuli can trigger cognitive appraisals that may potentially lead to impulsive purchasing decisions.

Unlike interactions in traditional e-commerce, where users rely on peer reviews or recommendations to assess quality, LSE selling primarily depends on platform affordances that capture users’ attention by enhancing the zero moment of truth (ZMOT) value. As conceptualized in traditional ecommerce, ZMOT represents a critical moment when individuals form initial impressions toward an offering based on search information (Chu & Kim, 2011; Lecinski, 2011). In digital commerce environments, this concept has evolved from a pre-purchase information-gathering phase to a more dynamic construct (Moran et al., 2014; Wolny & Charoensuksai, 2014).

In the LSE environment, ZMOT is embedded within realtime interactions between streamers and users. Unlike traditional e-commerce, where ZMOT occurs as a sequential process (Lecinski, 2011; Löfgren, 2005), LSE simultaneously integrates product features, user reviews, live feedback, and demonstrations. This transforms ZMOT into a dynamic and interactive experience. Building on this, Das et al. (2022) defined ZMOT as an indicator of how users evaluate the information search process in LSE. In this context, ZMOT value reflects a streamer’s ability to capture and maintain user attention using LSE platform features.

The importance of ZMOT in LSE is amplified by increasing market competition. With the number of streamers growing rapidly, capturing user attention has become more challenging. Streamers often have only a few seconds to engage viewers before they leave a live session (Chen et al., 2024; Hua et al., 2024). The rise of content creators across platforms has further intensified competition, making effective ZMOT strategies crucial. Successfully engaging users during ZMOT extends the reach of live broadcast and reduces dropout rates, particularly during the exploration stage, when uncertainty is high, such as with unfamiliar streamers or infrequently purchased items. Research shows that first impressions formed during these moments strongly influence purchasing decisions and platform engagement (Zhang & Zhang, 2024; Liu et al., 2023). Despite its significance, little research has examined how LSE affordances influence ZMOT value. Specifically, the factors that trigger this effect in dynamic selling environments remain unclear. To address this gap, the first part of this study explores the following research question:

## RQ1: Do LSE affordances have a positive impact on the ZMOT value within an LSE session?

To provide deeper insights, this study examines the mediating effects of trust and emotional engagement with streamers. Trust is key to alleviating perceived risk and fostering confidence in users’ decision-making (Lacity et al., 2024). On LSE platforms, trust in streamers plays a crucial role in bridging the gap between consumer intent and impulse purchases, especially when users engage with unfamiliar streamers. When viewers perceive a streamer as credible, this trust serves as a psychological bridge that transforms initial interest into spontaneous purchasing actions (Zhang et al., 2023). Specifically, a streamer’s perceived expertise, authenticity, and reliability directly enhance users’ confidence in product recommendations, effectively bypassing the traditional deliberation process (Chen et al., 2024). Consequently, when users perceive a streamer to be trustworthy, they rely on this trust rather than conducting extensive research (i.e., product information gathering, price comparisons across platforms, etc.), resulting in spontaneous purchasing decisions that transform initial ZMOT considerations into impulse buying behaviors during live shopping events.

Beyond trust in the streamer, emotional engagement represents another key factor shaping impulse buying behavior on LSE platforms. Unlike traditional ecommerce, LSE fosters real-time interactions and social presence that establish a higher degree of emotional response. This emotional intensity, though underexplored in the extant literature, appears to bypass rational decision-making and facilitate impulsive purchases (Ahn & Kwon, 2022). When users develop deeper emotional connections with streamers, they experience a sense of intimacy and social bonding, increasing receptivity to product presentations and time-sensitive offers (Luo et al., 2024a). These affective states, whether driven by excitement or social connection, override deliberative thinking, accelerating the transition from product discovery to spontaneous purchase. Based on these considerations, the second part of this study addresses the following research question:

## RQ2: How does trust in the streamer and emotional engagement affect the connection between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies on LSE platforms?

Concurrently, trust in the LSE platform constitutes a multidimensional construct that exerts significant influence throughout users’ decision-making continuum. (Schuetz et al., 2024). Within virtual selling ecosystems predicated on monetary transactions, platforms must establish robust mechanisms for personal and financial data protection, ensure transaction transparency, and facilitate effective dispute resolution processes. Recent empirical evidence from Lankton et al. (2015) demonstrates that platform trust fundamentally shapes users’ cognitive and behavioral responses from initial product discovery to purchase completion. During the preliminary phase of live-session evaluation, platform trust functions as a critical boundary condition in the consumer decision-making process. Platforms that are perceived to be unreliable may substantially inhibit users’ capacity to derive ZMOT value from LSE affordances, whereas those deemed trustworthy can enhance information credibility and consumer confidence. In subsequent decision stages, users’ propensity toward impulse purchasing remains contingent upon their trust in the platform’s security infrastructure, order fulfilment reliability, and transactional integrity— even in instances where streamer trust and emotional engagement are well-established. These considerations suggest that trust in the platform functions as both an enabler and constraint within the LSE purchasing process, influencing how affective and cognitive responses translate into behavioral outcomes. This conceptualization informs our final research inquiry:

RQ3: How does trust in the LSE platform moderate users’ experience during both the initial stage of interest (LSE affordances and ZMOT value) and the subsequent process of impulse buying (effect of trust in the streamer and emotional engagement on impulse buying tendencies)?

To address our research questions, we conducted two studies to examine how impulse buying behavior develops among younger users on LSE platforms. Using a survey method with 425 users aged 18-35 who had experience with LSE platforms, Study 1 found that LSE affordances serve as a primary appraisal in influencing ZMOT value. At the secondary stage, ZMOT value acts as a key antecedent, positively shaping trust and engagement relationships with streamers and then leading to impulse buying decisions. Using 200 participants from the same demographics, Study 2 further confirmed that trust in the streamer and emotional engagement serve as critical mechanisms that strengthen users’ motivation to make impulse purchases. Additionally, the findings highlight that trust in the LSE platform plays a persuasive role in shaping users’ initial interest and the subsequent process of impulse buying during the decision-making process.

This study offers several contributions to both theory and practice. By applying cognitive appraisal theory, we provide empirical evidence that users’ impulse buying behavior is formed through a sequential process that is influenced by trust in the streamer and emotional engagement. For the LSE literature, our work emphasizes that platform affordances should be optimized to capture users’ attention at the exploration stage, before they join a specific live-selling session (i.e., ZMOT stage). We also identify the critical contingent factor of platform trustworthiness in digital commerce. For practicing managers, this study has concrete implications. It highlights the need for streamers to develop effective strategies that maintain trustworthy and engaging relationships with users while fully utilizing LSE affordances to drive impulse buying. Additionally, our findings on platform trust provide managers with specific recommendations for determining when it is more (or less) beneficial to use a trustworthy virtual selling platform.

## 2 Literature Review

## 2.1 Live Streaming E-Commerce (LSE) Platforms

LSE platforms exploit live streaming to offer “over-thetop” buying experiences to users by delivering live streams directly to viewers over the internet. These platforms leverage their unique affordances to create engaging e-commerce experiences that connect businesses with their target audience, showcase products, host events, and foster relationships between sellers and buyers (Zhang et al., 2023). Specifically, the affordances of LSE platforms make it easier for streamers to exhibit real-time interaction with users through several features like live demonstrations, interactive comments, and personalized recommendations—all of which enhance users’ attention and encourage continued participation in sessions.

In the context of LSE, ZMOT represents individuals’ initial impressions of a live streaming session (Lecinski, 2011). LSE platforms afford opportunities to select among many streaming sessions. Hence, it is typical for users to move on to another session when a streamer or product fails to capture their interest during ZMOT. To retain users effectively, streamers must offer unique and interactive experiences. Optimizing ZMOT requires leveraging platform affordances to engage users’ attention from the first moments on (Han & Kim, 2020). Successfully capturing users’ attention during ZMOT increases retention rates and potentially stimulates impulse purchases. In this case, it is reasonable to assume that ZMOT acts as a cognitive filter that fosters trust, emotional engagement, and ultimately impulse purchase tendencies in LSE environments.

Impulsive buying tendencies refer to a distinct classification of individual decision behavior that is unplanned, triggered by exposure to a stimulus, and happens “on the spot” (Rook & Fisher, 1995). On LSE platforms, impulse buying is mainly triggered by the platform’s capabilities. First, the real-time nature of an LSE platform creates a sense of immediacy and urgency. Streamers employ persuasive tactics to build excitement and encourage impulse buying among users (Vazquez et al., 2020). Second, many streamers utilize various promotion selling techniques on LSE platforms, such as offering limited-time promotions, flash sales, and exclusive deals (Chou, 2019), to create urgency that compels users to make immediate purchases. Third, social validation plays a significant role on LSE platforms (Zhou et al., 2019), with users often engaging in live discussions and comments during broadcasts. Observing others making purchases and expressing approval can lead to a sense of social validation regarding impulse purchases. Finally, the convenience and ease of making purchases on LSE platforms are key to increasing tendencies toward impulse buying (Xie et al., 2022).

## 2.2 Cognitive Appraisal Theory

Lazarus (1991, p. 3) defines appraisal as a “cognitive process that explains how an individual evaluates the environment.” This concept forms the core idea of cognitive appraisal theory, which posits that appraisals of stimuli determine why people manifest different emotions and behaviors in response to their environment (Arnold, 1960). This theory provides a robust foundation to explain how and why individuals evaluate and respond to stimuli through a logical sequence of information processing.

The existing literature on cognitive appraisal theory has evolved around two schools of thought. Some scholars argue that emotions are the dominant factor affecting consumption-related outcomes such as satisfaction and loyalty (Zheng, 2020). Others point out that reactions are not automatic responses but rather the result of subconscious mental assessments that correspond to individuals’ characteristics (needs, wants, goals, and past knowledge) (Cai et al., 2018). Consequently, the evaluation process can be classified into two phases. It begins with a primary appraisal in which the individual evaluates each stimulus based on its congruence and relevance to their goals. If the stimulus is considered congruent with personal objectives, a secondary appraisal is initiated (Ma et al., 2013). During the secondary appraisal stage, individuals form various emotional states through psychological processes. When individuals evoke a positive inner reaction, it leads to a favorable response.

In terms of application, cognitive appraisal theory is widely used to justify how users appraise the affordances of a contemporary information systems (IS) tool or make decisions to continue or discontinue its use. For example, this theory offers insights into the opportunities, threats, and salient emotions associated with metaverse usage and user interactions within this platform (Suh, 2024). Researchers have also utilized this theory to explore the factors embraced by users cognitively and emotionally in the acceptance of generative AI tools (Downen et al., 2024). Given the maturity of this theory, this study posits that further exploration is needed to detect specific cognitive and emotional appraisal dimensions that are relatively salient when exploring users’ impulse buying behavior on LSE platforms.

In accordance with cognitive appraisal theory, this study positions the LSE platform’s affordances as the primary appraisal, which is utilized to assess the value of joining a particular live streaming session. Users are exposed to multiple simultaneous stimuli, including peer interactions and streamers’ persuasive strategies (i.e., visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping). Subsequently, these dynamic environments have the potential to influence users to make impulsive purchases, particularly when their affective states are amplified through interactions with streamers. These include ZMOT value, trust in the streamer, and emotional engagement, which represent significant emotional reactions at the secondary stage. The proposed idea is consistent with the adaptive cognitive processing argument proposed by Yu et al. (2023). It posits that appraisals can evolve dynamically based on contextual factors, thereby aligning with the fluid nature of decision-making in LSE. For instance, research has demonstrated that environmental cues in LSE, such as social interactions from LSE affordances, significantly influence consumers’ emotions and cognitive responses, thereby affecting their impulse buying behaviors. Therefore, we frame these ideas in our research model (depicted in Figure 1) and formulate seven hypotheses, as explained in the following section.

## 3 Hypotheses Development

## 3.1 Primary Appraisal: LSE Affordances

Technology affordances are defined as “the possibilities for goal-oriented actions afforded to specified user groups by the technical functionalities of technical objects” (Markus & Silver, 2008, p. 622). Within the LSE environment, affordances are realized through shoppingoriented actions facilitated by the platform’s embedded technical features (Hua et al., 2024). This study postulates that visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping (Sun et al., 2019; Dong & Wang, 2018) are key affordances that work synergistically to shape user experiences and influence the formation of ZMOT value on LSE platforms.

First, visibility features enhance LSE affordances by enabling real-time product demonstrations, providing users with an immersive and interactive product evaluation experience (Ki et al., 2024). Unlike traditional e-commerce, which relies on pre-recorded videos or static images, high-visibility affordances allow streamers to showcase product attributes, benefits, and unique selling points dynamically, strengthening user trust and value perception (Gu et al., 2023).

Second, metavoicing fosters real-time and transparent interactions between streamers and users, reinforcing social presence and engagement (Jia et al., 2024). Metavoicing allows users to engage in live discussions, enhancing the authenticity and credibility of product information (Hua et al., 2024). This synchronous exchange enables community-driven decision-making, where users are influenced by the collective opinions of both the streamer and other viewers.

Third, guidance shopping enhances LSE affordances by offering personalized shopping experiences in a coviewing environment. As noted by Yan et al. (2023), guidance shopping affordances allow streamers to offer live recommendations, mimicking in-store shopping assistance and interactions. This interactive guidance enables users to ask questions, seek tailored advice, and make well-informed decisions.

The integration of these three affordances creates a comprehensive platform that addresses users’ cognitive needs during the critical ZMOT phase, specifically enabling them to have a more realistic experience in a real-time, interactive e-commerce environment (Hua et al., 2024). Empirical evidence suggests that these affordances significantly influence how users perceive and evaluate information during their initial encounter with products in digital environments (Hua et al., 2024; Liu, 2023). At this crucial juncture, when users are forming first impressions, LSE affordances provide multisensory cues that traditional e-commerce platforms cannot replicate. The real-time nature of these affordances reduces information asymmetry between sellers and buyers, thereby enhancing the perceived value of the information search process, a core component of ZMOT value (Das et al., 2022).

Moreover, recent studies have demonstrated that LSE affordances directly address the limitations of conventional online shopping environments by providing dynamic information presentation, social validation mechanisms, and personalized guidance (Jia et al., 2024; Yim et al., 2017). When streamers effectively leverage these affordances, they create a more engaging and trustworthy information environment that positively shapes users’ early evaluations. Extending this perspective through the lens of cognitive appraisal theory (Lazarus, 1991), this study proposes that LSE affordances serve as primary cues that users cognitively appraise when searching for live streaming sessions that best fit their shopping goals and information needs. Thus, we propose:

H1: LSE affordances positively affect ZMOT value.

## 3.2 Secondary Appraisal: ZMOT Value

Unlike the traditional funnel model, where consumers systematically narrow their choices, modern users often expand their options based on new information acquired during the pre-purchase stage (Yen & Chiang, 2021). This shift highlights the critical role of ZMOT value on LSE platforms, where streamers must immediately capture user interest to convert them into actual buyers. The value users derive from ZMOT plays a pivotal role in shaping perceptions of the streamer and the product. When streamers provide real-time product information, answer questions, and demonstrate product use, they enhance ZMOT value, making the shopping experience more informative and engaging. Simultaneously, when users perceive streamers as approachable and knowledgeable, they are more likely to trust their opinions and recommendations, reinforcing their confidence in the purchasing process.

Prior research supports this connection. Kim and Lennon (2013) found that users actively engage during the ZMOT stage to seek information about a streamer’s reputation and reliability, which serves as a foundation for trust formation. When users encounter credible signals, such as expertise, transparency, and authentic engagement, during ZMOT interactions, their trust naturally increases. This aligns with Petty and Cacioppo’s (1986) postulation, which suggests that users engaging in systematic information processing are more likely to form trust-based beliefs. In the LSE context, trust formation at ZMOT is driven by users’ evaluation of streamer credibility, interactive responsiveness, and real-time demonstrations. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis:

H2a: ZMOT value has a positive impact on trust in the streamer.

Cognitive responses often serve as the basis for affective responses (Zhang et al., 2024), with information from LSE platforms needing to be deemed valuable before emotional engagement can be formed. It can be inferred that valuable experience at the ZMOT stage creates a desire for users to foster increased emotional involvement and investment towards the streamer on the LSE platform. This assumption is attributed to the fact that positive and fulfilling experiences garnered during this stage evoke positive emotions (Muzellec & O’Raghallaigh, 2018). Similarly, the usefulness of information must be cognitively assessed before affective behaviors, such as emotional engagement, can be developed, ultimately influencing behavioral outcomes in the context of LSE (Hsu, 2023; Lo et al., 2022). As a result, when users derive high value from the ZMOT stage, this positive experience makes them perceive that the streamer truly understands their unique needs and desires. Therefore, we propose:

H2b: ZMOT value has a positive impact on emotional engagement.

## 3.3 Secondary Appraisal: Trust in the Streamer

Trust in the streamer enhances users’ confidence in their professionalism while reducing suspicion of opportunistic behavior (Zhang et al., 2023). As opinion leaders in live-selling rooms, streamers who are perceived as experts or trusted for their personal taste become important triggers for impulse buying, as decisions require less deliberation when such trust exists (Chen et al., 2024). This aligns with cognitive dissonance theory, which suggests that individuals seek information from trusted sources to justify their actions. Users often seek reassurance from trusted streamers about their purchases, and once strong trust is established, they become more susceptible to impulsive buying decisions because they perceive the streamer’s information to be reliable. Therefore, we propose:

H3: Trust in the streamer has a positive impact on impulse buying tendencies.

## 3.4 Secondary Appraisal: Emotional Engagement

Emotional engagement serves as a heuristic signal that reduces cognitive effort in purchase behavior (Lim et al., 2020). The essence of emotional engagement is the art of capturing individuals’ attention in two forms, i.e., emotional connection and emotional expression, which motivates them to naturally engage in virtual interactions with the streamer. Evidence suggests that when information is shared by a preferred streamer, an individual is more likely to make a purchasing decision with that streamer (Ahn & Kwon, 2022). Several studies also indicate that emotional engagement can engender a sense of excitement and connection, fostering a positive relationship wherein users feel personally connected to the streamer (Luo et al., 2025). These evoked positive emotions may lead users to perceive the streamer as their close friend and their offerings as more appealing and valuable, in turn increasing the likelihood of an impulsive purchase. Accordingly, we propose:

H4: Emotional engagement has a positive impact on impulse buying tendencies.

## 3.5 Mediating Effects: Trust in the Streamer and Emotional Engagement

Cognitive appraisal theory defines emotions as processes rather than states, suggesting that cognitive evaluations of situations interact to elicit specific emotional responses (Lazarus, 1991). Extending the postulations in the LSE context, this study examines how trust in the streamer and emotional engagement mediate the relationship between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies on LSE platforms.

At the primary stage, streamers strategically leverage LSE platform affordances to capture users’ attention during the ZMOT stage. During this exploration phase, users actively seek value-added live streaming sessions that enhance their product knowledge through real-time demonstrations, interactions, and streamer-led discussions. Informative and interesting sessions are more likely to maintain users’ attention and foster deeper interest. The secondary stage focuses on how users form perceptions of streamers after attending a live streaming session, where streamers work to build relational bonds and persuade users to commit both time and financial resources to purchase decisions.

In the relationship-building process, trust in the streamer emerges as a critical psychological mechanism that shapes users’ perceptions of products and purchase decisions (Gvili & Levy, 2023). When users develop confidence in a streamer’s recommendations and product endorsements, this trust increases the likelihood of impulse buying. Trust in the streamer closely relates to perceptions of information credibility (Chaker et al., 2024), which is crucial on LSE platforms where users cannot physically examine products as they would in traditional stores. As such, we posit that trust serves as a mediating mechanism, explaining how ZMOT value translates into impulsive buying behavior through effective relationship building. When users perceive ZMOT information as persuasive and valuable, it creates psychological assurance through trust in the streamer, ultimately enhancing impulsive buying tendencies. Therefore, we propose:

## H5a: Trust in the streamer mediates the path between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies.

The ZMOT stage is followed by another critical moment of exciting users to increase repeat viewership. Emotional engagement, encompassing feelings of excitement, joy, and connectedness, represents the fundamental emotional response linked to consumption experiences (Ahn & Kwon, 2022). On LSE platforms, this engagement is shaped by dynamic relationships between streamers and users, representing the emotional investment allocated toward a particular streamer. When users experience heightened emotional engagement from ZMOT value, it serves as a crucial psychological driver, translating initial cognitive appraisal into impulsive purchase behavior (Khoi & Le, 2023). This engagement cultivates profound connections with streamers during the secondary stage of viewing and selling, fostering authenticity and trust. Users who experience a “mental sensation of engagement” (Shin, 2019) that intensifies emotional connections with content and social interactions are more likely to exhibit purchasing immediacy. This all supports emotional engagement as a mediating mechanism explaining how ZMOT value influences impulse buying tendencies by transforming cognitive evaluations into affect-driven consumption responses. Therefore, we propose:

H5b: Emotional engagement mediates the path between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies.

## 3.6 Moderating Effect: Trust in the LSE Platform

Trust in the LSE platform is increasingly challenged by concerns about data privacy, payment security, and technical reliability (Zhang et al., 2022). Users’ trust can be undermined by technical issues such as video stuttering, data overload, or sudden audio-visual disruptions (Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020). Given these concerns, this study positions trust in the LSE platform by encompassing three key dimensions (McKnight et al., 2011): reliability (belief in consistent proper operation), helpfulness (belief in adequate and responsive assistance), and functionality (belief in the platform’s capability to meet user needs).

According to cognitive appraisal theory, the primary appraisal stage involves an individual’s initial assessment of whether a situation is relevant and beneficial or detrimental to their goals (Lazarus, 1991). In the LSE context, users engage platform affordances, including visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping features, when entering a live streaming session. However, these LSE affordances can only generate a meaningful ZMOT value when users trust the platform as a reliable and helpful environment for product information acquisition. For instance, even if a platform offers various LSE affordances, users who distrust the platform’s credibility or fear deceptive content may not perceive these features as beneficial for ZMOT value. Similarly, suspicions about content manipulation or misleading endorsements can diminish the effectiveness of LSE affordances in fostering ZMOT value. This aligns with the notion that trust-related appraisals shape how individuals process and respond to external stimuli. Hence, trust in the LSE platform serves as a crucial boundary condition in the primary appraisal stage, determining how effectively users can utilize LSE affordances to form ZMOT value. Therefore, we propose:

H6a: The positive impact of LSE affordances on ZMOT value is stronger when trust in the LSE platform is high.

In the secondary appraisal stage, individuals assess their perceived control over the situation and their ability to act upon their initial evaluations (Lazarus, 1991; Moors et al., 2013). During this stage, trust in the LSE platform extends beyond information evaluation to influence users’ impulse purchase tendencies. Users’ trust expectations significantly influence their subsequent usage and outcomes. They are more likely to engage in transactions when they believe the platform processes them efficiently and reliably (Chen et al., 2024). The impact of streamer trust on impulse buying increases when users perceive that the LSE platform provides clear, accurate information about pricing, sales terms, and order tracking (Zhang et al., 2023). However, when platform trust is compromised, particularly in terms of security measures against data breaches and fraud, users may perceive higher transaction risks and uncertainty, potentially switching to alternative platforms, especially for unfamiliar and high-involvement purchases (Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020). Therefore, we propose:

H6b: The positive impact of trust in the streamer on impulse buying tendencies is stronger when trust in the LSE platform is high.

Trust in the LSE platform acts as a psychological enabler that encourages users to establish interpersonal relationships through interactions. When users perceive the platform as trustworthy, particularly in terms of secure data handling and responsible use, they interact more confidently with streamers. This heightened platform trust amplifies the effect of emotional engagement on impulsive purchases by reducing perceived risks and increasing transactional security (Lankton et al., 2015; Lu & Yi, 2023). In contrast, when trust in the LSE platform is low, users may show reluctance even when emotionally engaged with streamers. Their concerns about transaction security, data integrity, or platform reliability can weaken this relationship and cause hesitation in making impulse purchases. Hence, strong trust in the LSE platform serves as a crucial element in strengthening the impact of emotional engagement on impulse buying during the decision-making stage. Therefore, we propose:

H6c: The positive impact of emotional engagement on impulse buying tendencies is stronger when trust in the LSE platform is high.

## 3.7 Moderated Mediation

IS studies have consistently demonstrated that elevated levels of trust not only serve as a catalyst for social interactions, facilitating seamless transactions and exchanges amid social uncertainties (Liu, 2023), but they also foster collaborative transactions and diminish risks inherent in interpersonal engagements (Tsai & Hung, 2019). Despite these significant findings, we lack insights into the specific stage at which trust begins to play an influential role. Based on related findings stating that the level of trust perceived among users is expected to foster a sense of safety and security throughout the process of utilizing a platform (Lankton et al., 2015), this study argues that the establishment of trust in an LSE platform synergistically amplifies the mediating paths connecting ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies.

This argument is triggered by the fact that, at the ZMOT, users are in the decision-making phase and are evaluating whether to engage with the platform. Trust at this stage can significantly influence their willingness to explore and try out the platform. If users trust the platform from the outset, they are more likely to take the first step and join a live streaming session. Consistently, studies have found that trust is the most powerful tool in helping to reduce the perceived risks associated with using a new platform (Choung et al., 2023). Concerns about privacy, security, and the credibility of content can deter users from joining a live stream. By establishing trust early, the platform can alleviate these concerns, making users feel more secure and comfortable. Therefore, we propose:

H7a: The mediating effect of trust in the streamer on the relationship between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies is strengthened when trust in the LSE platform is high.

H7b. The mediating effect of emotional engagement on the relationship between ZMOT value and impulse buying tendencies is strengthened when trust in the LSE platform is high.

## 4 Overview of the Study

This study specifically examines the millennial cohort to provide a comprehensive understanding of impulse buying behavior in LSE. Millennials, born between 1981 and 1996, comprise a significant portion of LSE users and demonstrate high engagement with digital platforms, particularly in social commerce (Smith, 2021). This demographic is not only tech savvy but also exhibits distinct consumption patterns that prioritize convenience, social interaction, and personalized experiences—key elements that LSE platforms are designed to facilitate (Choi & Lee, 2020). Their high participation in platforms that integrate entertainment and shopping, such as live streaming commerce, makes them ideal subjects for understanding impulse buying dynamics (Wang et al., 2023).

This research comprises two complementary studies. Study 1 serves as an initial pilot investigation. It examined the sequential process from primary appraisals (LSE affordances) to secondary appraisals (ZMOT value, trust in the streamer, emotional engagement), leading to impulse buying tendencies (H1-H4), including mediating effects (H5a and H5b). Study 2 served as the main study; it revalidated the findings of Study 1 using a different sample of millennial participants and explored the moderating (H6a to H6c) and moderated-mediation paths (H7a to H7b). This two-study approach strengthened our findings’ robustness while highlighting the incremental process of how live streaming interactivity influences users’ buying behaviors.

## 5 Study 1: Initial Pilot Investigation

## 5.1 Design and Procedures

Study 1 used data collected from millennial participants (i.e., between the ages of 18 and 35) in Malaysia who had an active subscription to at least one LSE platform<sup>1</sup> and had not purchased limited edition products or retrieved discount codes to make purchases from an LSE. <sup>2</sup> Malaysia provided a useful context for our study because approximately 20 million residents there use Facebook or Instagram for LSE shopping (Statista, 2021). There are also four million users of TikTok, one of the most popular LSE platforms in Malaysia, with a notable concentration of users under the age of 30 (Hassan, 2020).

To conduct the data collection, we designed a written descriptive scenario (vignette) to gauge participants’ perceptions and decisions towards the LSE session (see Appendix A). The use of hypothetical scenarios provides a good approximation of the user’s decision-making process and allows for better control and measurement than real-world behavior, hence mitigating external factors that might influence participants’ responses.

Before collecting our data, we tested the validity of the instruments through a pretest (n = 10) and a pilot test (n = 50) with a panel of experts consisting of IS professors and representatives of the target group. These tests helped to determine the relevance of the questionnaire and whether the instructions and statements were appropriate and understood. Based on the pretest results, we modified some of the measurement items to correct grammatical errors, misspellings, and sentence structure. The pilot test results showed that all the variables met the composite reliability requirement with values above 0.7.

We collected the data using an online survey between March 1 and May 31, 2022. Of the 450 responses, we excluded 25 with suspicious response patterns (straight lines or inconsistent responses), leaving 425 responses for analysis (see Table 1). The demographic profile of respondents in Study 1 indicates that the majority identified as female (56.5%) and were single (62.4%). The majority of participants were under the age of 25 (38.8%), held a bachelor’s degree (38.6%), and reported a monthly income ranging from RM2,001 to RM4,000 (approximately 480-960 USD) (48.2%). In terms of purchasing behavior on LSE platforms, the most frequently purchased product categories were fashion and apparel (35.5%), cosmetics (27.8%), food (24.7%), and daily necessities (12.0%), a pattern that is consistent with the statistics reported by Ma (2024). Additionally, 60.0% of the participants indicated a preference for watching LSE sessions hosted by female streamers.

## 5.2 Measures

LSE affordances, encompassing three dimensions— visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping—were adopted from Dong and Wang (2018).<sup>3</sup> The scale for trust in the streamer was derived from Kim and Park (2013)<sup>4</sup> and Gefen et al. (2003). The scale for emotional engagement was adapted from Lim et al. (2015), while the scale for impulse buying tendency was drawn from Rook and Fisher (1995) and Weun et al. (1998). Additionally, the scale for ZMOT value was adapted from Das et al. (2022) and Han and Kim (2020). All items were rated on a sevenpoint Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 7 = strongly agree) (see Appendix B).

## 5.3 Control Variables

The proposed model includes four control variables: marital status, age, income level, and preferred streamer type. Empirical findings suggest that users under the age of 25 demonstrate higher familiarity with online selling technologies and greater impulse buying tendencies due to relatively lower self-control (Badgaiyan & Verma,

2014). Moreover, an extensive literature review highlights a clear preference among users for purchasing from female streamers, attributed to their perceived trustworthiness and meticulousness in product recommendations (Yang et al., 2023b). Additionally, it has been suggested that single individuals with higher income levels demonstrate a greater propensity for impulse buying behavior (Chen et al., 2019).

Similarly, at the secondary appraisal stage, the results show that the relationship between trust in the streamer and impulse buying tendency was moderated by trust in the LSE platform $( \beta = 0 . 1 8 , p =$ 0.00). Trust in the LSE platform also moderated the relationship between emotional engagement and impulse buying tendency (β = 0.21, p = 0.00). Importantly, the inclusion of this moderating effect raised the $R ^ { 2 }$ value from 39% to 53% for ZMOT value as well as 41% to 51% for impulse buying tendency. Meanwhile, the goodness-of-fit statistics indicate that the model had an acceptable fit (<sup>2</sup>/df = 4.87; SRMR = 0.098; RMSEA = 0.050, SRMR = 0.079, AGFI: 0.01, GFI = 0.91, TLI = 0.093, and CFI = 0.092).

Thus, H6a, H6b, and H6c are supported.

## 7 Discussion and Implications

## 7.1 General Discussion

Drawing on cognitive appraisal theory, our research examines the underlying appraisals behind impulse buying tendencies on LSE platforms. In the primary appraisal stage, this research highlights that LSE affordances critically shape users’ perceptions of ZMOT value. Building upon the significant findings of H1, we confirm that individuals’ perceptions of technology’s affordances influence a spectrum of intended and unintended behaviors (Das et al., 2022; Hua et al., 2024; Treem & Leonardi, 2013).

In the secondary appraisal stage, our study elucidates the pivotal role of ZMOT value in fostering user trust and cultivating emotional connections with streamers within the LSE ecosystem (as corroborated by H2a and H2b). Our findings show that users who perceive great value from the streamer at the ZMOT stage exhibit a higher level of trust in the streamer and engage with them emotionally. These findings echo earlier works suggesting that when users perceive a live-selling session as valuable for their decision-making, they tend to develop positive affective responses, such as admiration, trust, or affinity towards the streamer (Luo et al., 2024).

This study further confirms the causal and indirect effects of trust in streamers and emotional engagement as significant secondary appraisals influencing impulse buying through the ZMOT value (supported by H3-H5). These results align with previous research showing that trust in e-commerce settings increases the likelihood of engaging in both planned and unplanned purchases (Cao & Galinsky, 2020). LSE users with higher levels of trust and emotional engagement with streamers demonstrate greater impulse buying tendencies. This finding parallels existing literature suggesting that trust in streamers creates psychological closeness and interpersonal intimacy, enhancing impulse buying behavior (Liu et al., 2023). Additionally, emotional engagement strengthens this effect, particularly when LSE sessions create interactive experiences that evoke positive emotional responses, leading to an increased sense of urgency to purchase showcased products (Gvili & Levy, 2023).

Last and most importantly, our extension study revealed that the previously discussed direct and indirect effects are contingent upon users’ trust in the LSE platform (supported by H6a-c and H7a-b). In the LSE context, trust in the platform serves as a fundamental factor that facilitates relationship-building and transaction behavior by reducing perceived uncertainty and risk. While existing studies show inconsistent conclusions about trust antecedents and their impact on user intentions (e.g., Gu et al., 2024; Lankton et al., 2015), our research validates platform trust as a boundary condition from a broader perspective. The findings reveal that when users perceive the LSE platform as reliable, functional, and helpful, their overall usage experience improves significantly. This enhanced experience strengthens the user’s belief in the LSE session’s ability to enhance ZMOT value during the exploration stage and increases their likelihood of making impulse purchases, particularly when interacting with trusted and engaging streamers.

## 7.2 Theoretical Implications

The theoretical implications of this study are threefold.

First, this research challenges the dominant paradigm that primarily focuses on technology affordances’ postadoption effects. Our investigation reveals that LSE affordances serve as critical primary appraisal mechanisms through which users evaluate the engagement value of each live session. Within dynamic selling platforms like LSE, affordances cannot be delivered through isolated features. Our study emphasizes that the three core features—visibility, metavoicing, and guidance shopping—must be integrated concurrently to capture users’ attention and interest at the ZMOT stage. This insight reveals a critical temporal aspect of user engagement: ineffective integration of affordances during initial interaction can lead to user migration, reducing transaction opportunities. Based on these significant findings, our study provides a more nuanced understanding of technology affordances beyond postadoption to the early-stage evaluative stage.

Second, this study advances cognitive appraisal theory by revealing how trust in the streamer and emotional engagement function as secondary appraisal mechanisms transforming initial evaluations into impulse buying behaviors. Our empirical evidence addresses calls for understanding how affective states accelerate evaluation processes in real-time digital shopping environments (Feng et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). Trust creates cognitive shortcuts that reduce perceived risk, while emotional engagement generates arousal states that compress decision timeframes. These mechanisms work synergistically in unfamiliar virtual environments to lower resistance to impulse buying. This application of cognitive appraisal theory demonstrates how sequential appraisal processes operate in high-velocity digital environments where deliberative evaluation cycles are condensed into near-instantaneous reactions, establishing a foundation for future research at the intersection of LSE affordances, user appraisals, and purchasing behaviors.

Lastly, our study stands out by examining the conditional effects of trust in technology in heightening LSE usage. Despite previous studies acknowledging the imperative role of trust in the IS platform in affecting usage experience (Khoi & Le, 2023; Miranda et al., 2024), more evidence is needed to gauge its influential role in different stages of IS interaction. Our findings imply that trust in the LSE platform plays two significant conditioning roles, in both users’ exploration (i.e., technology affordances— ZMOT value) and interaction stages (moderatedmediation paths). These proven findings draw attention to the need for future IS studies to develop a broader understanding of how trust functions in technological contexts and how it adds value for streamers using the platform to influence desirable responses from users, making the IS platform more sustainable.

## 7.3 Managerial Implications

This study has implications for several key practitioners of the LSE platform, including streamers and platform management teams.

Streamers should strategically leverage LSE platform affordances to enhance ZMOT value through multiple complementary features. Visibility affordances create transparent product presentations that address users’ initial information needs, reducing uncertainty during the critical ZMOT stage when potential customers are still forming impressions. Metavoicing affordances facilitate real-time interaction that validates product claims through community feedback, strengthening perceived credibility during users’ preliminary evaluation and creating a social proof that influences decision-making. While guidance shopping affordances provide personalized recommendations that help users navigate product options efficiently, streamlining their decision-making process by highlighting relevant products based on expressed preferences or browsing history. By integrating these three affordances, streamers create a comprehensive pre-purchase environment that satisfies users’ informational and social validation needs. In fact, effective implementation of these affordances also helps streamers differentiate their offerings in a crowded marketplace while simultaneously addressing the cognitive uncertainty that often prevents users from committing to particular live shopping experiences. This integration is especially important during the exploration stage, when users may have uncertain notions regarding which live streaming sessions to participate in and which will optimize their value-seeking intentions, given their limited knowledge.

Beyond optimizing platform affordances, our findings emphasize that streamers should cultivate positive affective states to transform initial interest into impulse purchases. Trust and emotional engagement serve as critical psychological bridges between ZMOT value and purchase behaviors. To build trust, streamers should project professionalism through appropriate attire and well-designed backdrops during live streaming sessions. This professional presentation significantly enhances viewers’ first impressions and perceived credibility. Additionally, streamers must demonstrate comprehensive product knowledge, providing detailed specifications, usage guidelines, and comparative advantages to help users fully understand product benefits. This expertisedriven approach establishes the streamer as a credible information source, fostering trust that reduces perceived risk in the virtual shopping environment.

To strengthen emotional engagement, streamers should implement concrete strategies that create authentic connections with viewers. Rather than exclusively focusing on product promotion, streamers should integrate personal elements by sharing glimpses of their daily lives, behind-the-scenes content, and authentic reactions to products. This personalization transforms the commercial interaction into a relational experience. Additionally, incorporating emotional storytelling that frames products within relatable narratives—such as how a skincare product solved the streamer’s specific skin issues or how a kitchen gadget transformed their cooking routine—creates memorable emotional anchors. These engagement techniques collectively generate parasocial bonds that bypass rational decision-making processes, making viewers more receptive to impulse purchases driven by emotional connection rather than purely utilitarian evaluation.

To enhance users’ confidence in LSE platforms, system designers and engineers should implement strategic trustbuilding measures across the platform architecture. First, they should establish transparent policies regarding terms of service, privacy, and community guidelines with easily accessible information about data handling practices. Implementing robust security measures—including strong encryption protocols, two-factor authentication, and secure payment processing—protects users’ financial and personal information. Second, platform teams should deploy effective content moderation through a combination of AI-powered filtering and human oversight, ensuring that live streams maintain community standards while preserving authentic interaction. The platform should feature intuitive navigation and search functionality to help users quickly discover relevant content based on their preferences and browsing history. Additionally, implementing real-time technical performance monitoring could prevent disruptions that might undermine user trust, ensuring consistent streaming quality regardless of traffic volume. These comprehensive approaches can help to create a robust trust infrastructure that encourages confident platform engagement, ultimately fostering a vibrant and sustainable LSE ecosystem.

## 7.4 Limitations and Future Research

This study is subject to several limitations. First, the data was collected in Malaysia, a developing country, which may limit the generalizability to regions with different cultural practices. Future research should conduct comparative studies between developed and developing countries, as cultural differences significantly impact technology usage behavior.

Second, despite conducting two complementary studies, both used cross-sectional designs, limiting causal inference and the ability to capture evolving user behaviors and attitudes. Future research should employ real-time data collection methods to assess ZMOT value perceptions. Time-lagged studies comparing pre-live streaming (when users anticipate events and engage with promotional content) and post-live streaming (when users interact directly with streamers and other viewers) phases would provide valuable insights. Additionally, scholars should develop reliable scales to measure both the first and second moments of truth within ZMOT value and examine their impacts on LSE usage behavior.

Third, future research should conduct experimental studies examining streamer characteristics. Characteristics such as authenticity, charisma, and trustworthiness are crucial for attracting and retaining viewers during live sessions (Zhao et al., 2023). When these characteristics are aligned with user preferences, they directly influence ZMOT value, affective responses, and behavioral outcomes. The incorporation of advanced experimental methods, such as eye-tracking and facial expression analysis, would also contribute to a more profound comprehension of streamer characteristics during live streamed interactions (Hong et al., 2021; Mullins et al., 2022; Pfeiffer et al., 2020). These approaches would provide deeper insights into the immediacy and automaticity of evaluations and impulse buying decisions, complementing self-reported data and advancing our understanding of users’ cognitive, affective, and conative responses on LSE platforms.

Fourth, future studies should explore additional LSE features to develop a more comprehensive understanding of LSE affordances. Leonardi (2011) suggests that affordances can manifest through different features depending on technology usage and context, influencing user behavior in various ways. As LSE evolves as a form of social commerce, it not only facilitates product information dissemination but also enables socially interactive and entertaining shopping experiences (Wang et al., 2023). Future research should investigate other potential LSE affordances, including entertainment affordances and social connection affordances (Hua et al., 2024), to better understand the multifaceted nature of user engagement with these platforms.

Finally, future research should incorporate more dynamic theoretical perspectives by integrating cognitive appraisal with dual-process theories of decision-making (Bala & Venkatesh, 2016; Zhang & Ruan, 2024). These frameworks account for both automatic impulse-driven stimuli and reflective deliberate appraisals, along with real-time engagement models that consider feedback loops between users, streamers, and peer interactions (Novak & Hoffman, 2009). For example, initial purchase hesitation in LSE environments may be transformed into impulse buying through positive viewer comments and limited-time offers. Researching this phenomenon would benefit from real-time behavioral tracking methods such as gaze patterns and clickstream data (Pfeiffer et al., 2020), as well as sentiment analysis of live comments (Storey & Park, 2022). These methodological advances are crucial for capturing LSE’s dynamic decisionmaking processes, revealing how impulse buying stems from both immediate heuristic judgments and deliberate systematic evaluations.

## 8 Conclusion

This study illuminates the drivers of impulse buying tendencies on LSE platforms. Leveraging cognitive appraisal theory, we identify users’ perceptions of interactive affordances as pivotal primary appraisals shaping ZMOT value and underscore the significance of affective factors—particularly trust in the streamer and emotional engagement—as secondary appraisals essential to driving impulsive buying tendencies. By uncovering the contingency role of trust for LSE platforms, our findings enrich this burgeoning field, catalyzing a deeper understanding of user experience and behavior formation in these dynamic shopping environments.

## About the Authors

Xin-Jean Lim is affiliated with the School of Business, Sun Yat-Sen University (China) and the School of Business and Economics, Universiti Putra Malaysia (Malaysia). Her primary research interests include consumer behavior, social media marketing, online marketing, customer relationship management and technology marketing. Her research papers have been published in reputable business, tourism, and information system journals, including Technological Forecasting and Social Change, Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, Industrial Management and Data Systems, International Journal of Hospitality and Management, Internet Research, Journal of Strategy Marketing, Asia Pacific Journal of Marketing and Logistics, among others. Jun-Hwa (Jacky) Cheah is an associate professor at Norwich Business School, University of East Anglia and an adjunct associate professor in the Faculty of Business at Sohar University. His areas of interest include digital marketing, consumer behaviour, quantitative research, and methodological issues. Nisreen Ameen is an associate professor in digital marketing at Royal Holloway, University of London, and the director of the Digital Organisation and Society Research Centre. Nisreen has also served as a guest editor for special issues in various top-ranked journals such as Information Systems Frontiers, Computers in Human Behavior, Psychology and Marketing, Industrial Marketing Management, Journal of Business Research, The Service Industries Journal and International Journal of Consumer Studies. Her research interests include digital marketing and entrepreneurship, human-computer interaction consumer behaviour, and organisational use of consumer data. Her research has been published in high-ranking journals, including Journal of Travel Research, British Journal of Management, International Journal of Entrepreneurial Behavior and Research, Psychology and Marketing, Information Technology and People, Information Systems Frontiers, Internet Research, Computers in Human Behavior, Information Systems Management, Information Technology for Development, the British Journal of Educational Technology, and International Journal of Gender and Entrepreneurship.
