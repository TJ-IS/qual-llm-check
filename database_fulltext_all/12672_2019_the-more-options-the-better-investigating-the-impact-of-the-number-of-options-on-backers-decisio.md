## Abstract:

The number of options in reward-based crowdfunding projects plays an important role in influencing backers’ decisions. However, prior studies provided two-pronged outcomes for the impact on the backers’ decision of supporting a project. They also neglected the impact on the backers’ decision on which option to select. Anchored on the theories of assortment decisions, we found that the number of options has an inverted U-shaped relationship with the backers decision on supporting a crowdfunding project. It also has a significant impact on the backers decision on selecting an option. Three studies were conducted, and they collectively provided solid evidence to our findings.

Keywords: Reward-Based Crowdfunding, The Number of Options, Reward Scheme, The Success of Crowdfunding Projects, Backers’ Backing Centroid, Multimethod Design

## 1. Introduction

The impact of an increased number of options on the backers’ decision of supporting a reward-based project has become the most prevalent topic of online crowdfunding research [10, 29, 53, 62, 88]. In a conventional reward-based project, fundraisers usually provide several options with various funding prices and yields to backers. Backers then decide to choose a preferred option if they want to support the project [54]. In this regard, the investigation of this research topic should focus not only on how the number of options influences the backers’ decision on whether to support a reward-based project but also on its impact on the backers decision on which option to choose.

There are two salient research gaps in extant studies of the impact of the number of options. The first gap pertains to the dichotomized outcomes on examining this research issue. Some researchers found that a large number of options are beneficial for a crowdfunding project. This is because there is a high likelihood that a large number of options can satisfy the diverse interests and motivations of backers [53, 69]. However, the findings of other researchers were opposite [12, 91]. They argued that choice overload causes a negative impact [16, 78].

Second, there is a dearth of studies that examined the impact of the number of options on backers’ decisions on selecting an option. In this paper, we use the backers’ backing centroid to depict the backers’ decision on selecting an option. The backers’ backing centroid refers to a pseudo point whose coordinates are the averages of the corresponding coordinates of a given set of backers’ actual selection of the options. In a conventional reward-based crowdfunding project, backers are free to select any option. Crowdfunding project fundraisers are also free to

## 2. Literature Review

Previous research suggests that the backers’ decision could be decomposed into two parts: (1) whether to support a crowdfunding project by choosing an option and (2) which option of decision could be invoked without a predefined order in the backers’ decision-making [33]. In some cases, backers may first consider whether to support a crowdfunding project and then make the decision of choosing a preferred option. In other cases, backers can first screen preferred options in a crowdfunding project and then decide whether to support the project. Thus, the investigations into these two parts are equally important for understanding the impact of the number of options on the backers’ decision-making.

For reward-based projects, the number of options is the most considered feature that determines the backers’ decision in supporting a crowdfunding project [11, 47, 58, 66]. Backers are the decisive stakeholders for fundraising success. From the backers’ perspective, the number of options represents the likelihood of selecting the preferred reward option, which subsequently influences their motivations to support a crowdfunding project [60]. Compared with backers’ other motivations of supporting a crowdfunding project such as gaining recognition, maintaining relationship, and supporting causes by interest or fun, being rewarded is the most recognized one [34, 76, 88]. For example, Ryu and Kim developed a crowdfunding sponsor typology based on sponsors’ motivations for participating in crowdfunding projects [76]. They found that hunting for reward is the highest motivation for participating in a reward-based project. Other researchers also found that the desire to collect a reward is a significant factor in predicting the backers’ decision to pledge in a reward-based project [17, 34]. In this regard, the number of options in the reward-based crowdfunding project would act as a key determinant of crowdfunding project performance.

Each reward-based project is unique in terms of its novelty, originality, and design [8]. If backers want to support a specific project, they need to at least go through the options provided in the project and seek the preferred option [17]. In this regard, the number of options determines the likelihood of making a support decision [16, 24, 73]. At the same time, it also influences the backers’ decision-making in foraging the preferred options [73].

With regard to the impact of the number of options on whether to support a reward-based well-established research areas such as assortment decision [70] and users’ cognitive processing of information [81]. Prior studies indicated that the large number of options in an assortment set could have a two-pronged impact on choice [16]. For the positive impact, researchers have demonstrated that the greater the number of options, the higher is the likelihood for consumers to find a close match to their purchase goals [3]. This stream of literature anchored on the economics view to understand the options in assortment as the resource for the choice. The underlying mechanism of them rests on the positive relationship between resources and the efficiency of time and effort in identifying the preferred alternatives [55]. For the negative impact, the literature on users’ cognitive processing of information provided substantial explanations to understand the impact of the number of options in assortment set. For example, prior studies found that a large number of options could lead to an increase in cognitive costs, thus resulting in a low performance on decision-making [15].

Similarly, the extant studies on reward-based crowdfunding suggested that the number of options might not have a one-sided impact.

By adopting the theoretical foundations from assortment research and users’ cognitive processing of information research, we found that the findings on the impact of the number of of options on the fundraising success of reward-based projects [27, 53]. They argued that the positive impact originates from the concept of versioning and price discrimination [53, 79]. For example, Du and her colleagues found that many potential backers failed to become the actual backers of a crowdfunding project [27]. It is because these potential backers cannot find a proper option that caters their requirements. Thus, according to the concept of versioning, potential backers lack motivations to support a crowdfunding project because of the limited number of versions of options provided [79]. In this regard, an increased number of options could increase the likelihood of finding an appropriate option, thus causing a positive impact on the construct backers’ motivations to support a crowdfunding project.

Other studies, however, reported a negative impact of the number of options [12, 91]. They explained the negative impact by using information overload theory [91] and choice overload effect [16]. The central thesis of their arguments is that the increased number of options could make the backers’ choice decision more complex. In this regard, the task complexity would result in choice overload and impair backers’ actual funding behavior [45]. As a result, a crowdfunding project with a large set of options may not be able to get enough funds during the funding period.

There is a dearth of investigations on the impact of the number of options on foraging the preferred option in a reward-based project. The most related studies originate from the research of assortment size and its impact on the consumers’ purchase decision [24, 33, 65]. Thus, we develop a concept of backers’ backing centroid to investigate the backers’ decision on choosing an option.

The backers’ backing centroid has an important theoretical value and a pragmatic value. This concept could represent the collective funding behavior of backers. In this regard, the backers’ backing centroid is a useful indicator that helps researchers to identify the critical portion of the options that attracts the most of the contributions from the backers. Researchers may use this concept to frame the backers’ backing performance [72], as well as to track/monitor the fundraising project from a dynamic perspective [66]. Second, the backers’ backing centroid could serve as a critical indicator to assess the design of a reward scheme. It can be applied to test whether the design is in line with the phenomenon of extremeness aversion in consumer choice [84], as well as the compromise effect [42, 83]. Its implications are significant for positioning, product assortments, branding, and competitive strategies [50].

With regard to pragmatic value, the backers’ backing centroid could provide helpful suggestions to practitioners on designing and deploying a reward-based crowdfunding project. By analyzing the backers’ backing centroid, fundraisers could place the option with a high commercial value close to it. In this regard, they may have a high likelihood to yield successful crowdfunding project. Besides, fundraisers may monitor the backing centroid and update the reward-based project accordingly with the aim of optimizing the fundraising performance.

In summary, it is very important to investigate the impact of the number of options on the backers’ decision-making. Specifically, we found a noteworthy research gap in understanding the impact in prior literature. To fill this gap, we investigate the impact of the number of options on both the backers’ decision on whether to support the crowdfunding project and which option to choose.

## 3. Hypothesis Development

## 3.1 Dependent Variable Development

In this paper, we investigate the impact of the number of options on the backers’ decision-making in reward-based crowdfunding. Specifically, we examine backers’ decisions on whether to support a reward-based project and which option to choose. Considering the nature of a reward-based project, we use the success of a reward-based project to manifest the backers’ decision-making on whether to support a project. It is because the funding outcome of a crowdfunding project is a collection of the majority of the backers’ decisions on supporting a project [92]. For backers’ decision on which option to choose, we use the backers’ backing centroid to represent the focal area in which they put the majority of their investment to a crowdfunding project. The selection of the dependent variables is in accordance with most of crowdfunding studies [31]. At the same time, the investigation on these two variables could reward-based crowdfunding project.

We measure the success of a reward-based project by using the actual fundraising results from an online crowdfunding platform [67]. This measure is widely accepted by both researchers and practitioners. We also measure the backers’ backing centroid in the reward-based project to indicate their decisions on selecting an option.

## 3.2 Impact on the Success of Crowdfunding Projects

Anchoring on the theoretical foundations from assortment research, we postulate that the number of options has an inverted U-shaped impact on the success of reward-based crowdfunding projects. We elaborate the hypothesis development with three conditions of the number of options.

Condition 1: Under a small number of options. The limited number of options would detriment the success of reward-based projects. According to theoretical underpinnings of assortment research, backers have sufficient cognitive resources to process the content of options. In this regard, backers are able to make an intensive evaluation [77]. However, because of the small number of options in reward-based projects, backers have a low likelihood to identify a preferred option for supporting a crowdfunding project. This is because the diversified needs/preferences of potential backers in that with a small number of options, some optional backers may be lost [38]. Thus, the small number of options would lead to an unfavorable decision on whether to support a reward-based project [82]. As a result, we posit that the small number of options leads to a low level of the success of reward-based projects.

Condition 2: Under medium number of options. Anchoring on the theoretical cognitive information processing resource [28]. Backers still preserve a sufficient cognitive information processing resource when they have a medium number of options in a crowdfunding project [56]. In this regard, they are able to conduct a deliberate assessment on the reward options [89]. A medium number of reward options pertains a high possibility of having the preferred reward options that cater the potential backers’ needs/preferences. In addition, the uniqueness of a crowdfunding project signifies the importance of having an increased number of options. Different from the conventional product that can be easily substituted by its competitors, a product/service in a crowdfunding project usually has its would like to support the project, they have to identify the suitable funding option and choose it within the crowdfunding project. Otherwise, they will lose the opportunity to support the unique project if they do not make any choice. In this regard, the number of options in the crowdfunding project acts as the resource for supporting backers’ funding behavior by providing the opportunity of choosing an option that closely matches to their requirements and the flexibility of choice [4]. Thus, a medium number of options will lead to an improved motivation of backers to support the project, thus leading to a successful crowdfunding project.

Condition 3: Under a large number of options. A large number of options will deplete the backers’ bounded cognitive processing resource for assessing the decision on whether to support the crowdfunding project in a short term [28]. According to the theoretical foundation of assortment research, if the cognitive processing resource is insufficient, the backers cognitive processing for decision-making would be impaired [5, 14, 44]. A large number of consumption. This will lead to backers’ negative affect toward the decision-making task on whether to support the crowdfunding project [1]. For example, Pan and other colleagues found that users usually feel “unsatisfied,” “frustrated,” and/or “agitated” with the demanding task that requires a significant amount of cognitive processing resource [71]. Consequently, users become unwilling to read the content or abandon the task right away [61]. Thus, we conceive that a large number of options lead to a low success rate of the crowdfunding project.

Considering the varying impacts of the number of options on the success of reward-based projects, we posit the following hypothesis:

H1: The number of options will have an inverted U-shaped relationship with the success of a reward-based crowdfunding project.

## 3.3 Impact on the Backers’ Backing Centroid

As explained earlier, backers suffer from the limitation of their cognitive capacity when comparing and selecting a preferred option in reward-based projects [21]. According to the research of human decision-making, there are two types of cognitive processing activities during the selection process of an option in a crowdfunding project [15]. The first one is to search for alternative options that match with the articulated preferences of users. When users have a clear preference about the option, they may apply this cognitive processing approach to choose an option [14]. The second one is to search for alternative options that dominate the other available options. This cognitive processing approach is often applied when users do not have a clear preference [14]. The performance of these cognitive processing activities would cannibalize with each other with the consideration of users’ bounded cognitive capacity [14].

In a conventional reward-based project, an individual backer has the freedom to select any option that satisfies his/her requirements of funding the project. An intuitive estimation of the selection of option would be evenly distributed. However, this estimation is built upon the assumption of the perfect cognitive capacity of backers [30], as well as the possession of a clear preference before choosing the preferred option [78]. Considering the uniqueness of each crowdfunding project, the product/service in each project is different from others, so as the options [95]. In this regard, it is unlikely for potential backers to frame a clear preference for selecting the preferred option before they actually evaluate the project. Therefore, backers would adopt the searching for the dominant option among the other options. In addition, the the backers’ cognitive processing capacity can support both the evaluation and comparison activities of all options. In this regard, backers could make a thorough search and evaluation of all options in a crowdfunding project. According to the Poisson distribution, the backers’ backing centroid would be close to the central point of the reward scheme [18].

On the other hand, when there are a large number of options, backers do not have a sufficient cognitive processing capacity to evaluate and compare every option. As a result, they would constrain their searching scope and comparison range to the easily accessible options [22]. In a conventional reward-based project, the provision of options is ordered by default [67].

Thus, the easily accessible options are usually placed at the beginning. In this regard, there is a high likelihood that the backers’ backing centroid would move away from the central point of the reward scheme to the lower order position of the reward scheme. Thus, we posit the following hypothesis:

H2: The number of options will have a negative impact on the backers’ backing centroid of a reward-based project.

## 4. Research Method

We employ a mixed-method research design to validate our hypotheses. As Mingers suggested, mixed-method research could get richer and more reliable results [64]. Particularly, the endogenous methodological limitation of any single research method may prevent us from identifying the eloquent supports for the following issues:

(1) Can strong support be obtained to validate our hypotheses under the online reward-based crowdfunding field data context by considering different settings of the contingent factors such as product type [12] and the endogenous characteristics between succeeded and failed reward-based projects [41]?

(2) Does the number of options have a causal relationship with the success of a reward-based project, as well as the backers’ backing centroid of a reward-based project? Prior studies argued that the disposition of a crowdfunding project, such as the number of images or the number of options, is the manifestation of fundraisers’ characteristics (e.g., thoroughness/informedness/engagement) [37]. If this is the case, the theoretical explanation of the impact of the number of options should be attributed to fundraisers thoroughness/informedness/engagement. Thus, it is very important to examine the causal impact of the number of options.

(3) Do backers cognitively regulate their search scope? In H2, we posited that the backers bounded cognitive capacity determines their focus toward the options and the search scope. Thus, we need to collect direct evidence to support our theoretical reasoning. N cognition-level evidence, such as backers’ eye tracking data, could be captured to interpret the backers’ cognitive behavior on searching the options in a reward-based project.

To address these concerns, we conducted three consecutive studies. Table 2 summarizes the interrelationship between the research concerns and the corresponding solutions delivered in this paper.

Our dataset contains 10,621 crowdfunding projects that were launched from April 2013 to October 2015. To exclude the influence of crowdfunding projects in which the number of options is extremely large, we dropped 63 crowdfunding projects in which the number of options is larger than 12, that is, the first integer larger than the mean plus three standard deviation of the number of options [75]. Thus, 10,558 crowdfunding projects remained in ou dataset. The definition and operationalization of the variables are described in Table 3.

For each crowdfunding project, we collected a set of objective attributes including the fundraising status after completion, number of options, fundraising goal, duration, presence of a descriptive video on the profile page, number of descriptive images on the profile page, category, start date of fundraising, and province where a crowdfunding project is launched (see Table 3).

In addition to the objective data that were directly collected from the online crowdfunding platform, we developed the operationalization of two dependent variables by the following methods. The status of a reward-based project after completion was coded “1” if the project was successful and “0” otherwise. The backers’ backing centroid was calculated according to its definition (see equation (1)).

Moreover, we developed operationalization of five control variables by the following methods. From the start date of fundraising, we calculated three control variables including the year, month, and day of week when a crowdfunding project starts to raise funds. For the past experience and past expertise of fundraisers, we calculated a control variable that describes the past experience as the number of completed projects that the fundraiser of the project has launched before initiating the project and another control variable that describes the past expertise as the rate of success for the completed projects that the fundraiser of the project has launched before initiating the project [95].

## 4.2 Study 2: Laboratory Experimental Study

The issue of identifying a causal relationship is addressed by conducting a controlled laboratory experiment. This research method has its advantage on ruling out confounding factors and identifying the causal relationship [20]. Specifically, we examine (1) whether the number of options has a causal impact on the participants’ willingness to invest and (2) whether participants’ perceived fundraiser’s thoroughness/informedness/engagement varies significantly across different number of options.

## 4.2.1 Experimental Design

##

We developed a simulated online crowdfunding system for conducting the laboratory experiment. Considering an approach similar to that proposed by Kumar and Benbasat [52], we studied the conventional reward-based crowdfunding platforms and extract the options of these platforms to manipulate the number of options. Specifically, there are three modules in our investor-profiling module to them. Participants are required to enter their demographic information and background experience of crowdfunding/investment in this module. After completing this module, the system navigates the participants to the crowdfunding project module. This module mimics a real crowdfunding project, and it is equipped with a behavior tracking program and a manipulation function of the number of options. By using this module, we are able to set different number of options for each participant. Moreover, the participants online behavior, such as selecting a specific option, can be recorded in the database for analysis. The last module in our experimental system is a post-survey module. It presents a list of questions to the participants after making a decision on backing the crowdfunding project. We used this module to collect participants’ perception on willingness to invest and fundraiser’s dispositions such as thoroughness.

## 4.2.2 Independent Variable Manipulation

We adopted a within-subject design in the experiment. The independent variable in this experiment is the number of options. People are heterogeneous on processing the number of options [90]. Thus, it is inappropriate to set up a between-subject experiment.

We designed three treatment crowdfunding projects for manipulating the number of options. In each crowdfunding project, we placed different sizes of reward scheme. A small-sized reward scheme contains two options, a medium-sized reward scheme has four options, and a large-sized reward scheme has eight options. The setup of the number of options was determined by the actual disposition of the reward schemes from the 10,558 real crowdfunding projects we collected. Furthermore, we incorporated the assortment design 35].

Among these treatment projects, the only difference is the number of options. In this regard, we are able to screen out the impact of confounding factors such as the number of images, quality of images, quality of the project description, fundraisers’ disposition, and other factors that influence the performance of the crowdfunding project and the backers’ backing behavior [92].

## 4.2.3 Dependent Variable Measures

We measured the participants’ willingness to invest as a pertinent manifestation of the success of reward-based projects in a laboratory experiment. The underlying logic is as follows. First, prior studies have demonstrated that the willingness to invest is the primary construct for assessing the success of a crowdfunding project [51]. If the backers’ willingness to invest is high, then there is a high likelihood that the crowdfunding project will succeed. Second, the willingness to invest is a viable measurement for estimating the success of crowdfunding projects in a controlled laboratory experiment. Considering the endogenous limitation of laboratory experimental methodology, the actual backing performance of the crowd of backers cannot be replicated in a laboratory experiment in real time. Because of the sample size constraint, even if all the participants in the experiment provide their maximum support to a crowdfunding project, it does not necessarily indicate a sufficient condition for the success of the crowdfunding project [92]. Thus, the willingness to invest plays a significant role in identifying the likelihood of having a successful crowdfunding project in a controlled laboratory experiment.

In addition to measuring the two key dependent variables, we also measured the perceived fundraisers’ thoroughness/informedness/engagement. ## 4.2.4 Pretest

A pretest was conducted to assess whether the experimental design is appropriate. First, we invited three experienced IS researchers to assess the design of the experimental system and the setup of the treatment crowdfunding projects. All three researchers provided very helpful suggestions on improving the quality of the experimental design. We followed their comments and fine-tuned the experimental design. These researchers conducted another assessment individually, and all their results agreed with the design.

#

Next, we recruited five IS postgraduate students who are fluent in both Chinese and English to translate the questionnaire from English to Chinese. Backward translation was then conducted, and two senior IS researchers assessed the quality of questionnaire translation. We then asked another five postgraduate students to conduct a trial round of using the experimental system and checked the operational robustness of our system. The results indicated that our system is appropriate for the main experiment.

Before launching the main experiment, we randomly recruited 30 participants to run a pilot study. After that, we conducted a short interview for each of them and asked their experience and opinion of using the experimental system and a specific product that would be suitable as a crowdfunding target product in the project. All the participants indicated that the experimental system was well designed, and the questionnaire in the system was easy to understand. They also ranked their level of product knowledge and their willingness to invest over 10 product categories (e.g., headphone). After consulting the top popular products in the 10,558 crowdfunding projects in our database, we selected the bone conduction headphone on which participants indicated the greatest willingness to invest and those for which they had low prior product knowledge for our experiment. The participants were asked to rate their level of product knowledge on a 7-point Likert scale. The results confirmed that the participants had a low level of prior product knowledge about the selected product (Mean=2.29, Std.=1.13). This setup was consistent with most experimental studies on decision-making behavior [40]. The choice of the product is also aligned with the research context of crowdfunding project [94].

## 4.2.5 Participants

We randomly recruited another 90 participants from a public university in China. As a within-subject design, 270 observation data (90 participants × 3 treatment crowdfunding projects) were collected. The number of participants was determined to yield an effect size f of 0.25 and a power of 0.95 for two measurements. Among the participants, 71 (78.8%) were females and 19 (21.2%) were males. All the participants, with ages averaging between 17 and 28 years, were recruited online. To ensure experimental realism, the participants were given certain monetary incentive that equals to their 1-hour income after tax deductio n.

## 4.2.6 Procedure

The experiment was conducted as follows. When participants arrived at the laboratory, they were assigned to a terminal randomly and logged in using a designated account. Subsequently, the participants watched a prerecorded instruction video and viewed the introduction of the experiment’s system. This video, while presenting the features of the experimental system to participants, also demonstrated the background information about crowdfunding.

Participants were given the scenario that they were to make backing decisions for supporting a crowdfunding project of the bone conduction headphone development. As mentioned earlier, this product was chosen not only because participants had low level of prior product knowledge about it but also because it was suitable to the experiment scenario. This setting was consistent with most studies on crowdfunding and decision-making behavior [66], and it was necessary to induce mundane realism. The instructions of the experiment guided the participants to make decisions on supporting crowdfunding projects with various sizes of reward scheme. The sequence of presenting these three treatment crowdfunding projects was randomly assigned. When the participants had decided to support the projects, they were asked to complete a questionnaire. There was no time constraint for the experiment.

## 4.3 Study 3: Eye tracking Experimental Study

We collected and analyzed biometric data from the participants to address the third issue. An eye tracking experiment was performed. Prior studies demonstrated that eye movement data are reliable indicators of users’ attention, awareness, search behavior, and preference [26]. Thus, the analysis of backers’ eye movement data could provide solid evidence on examining whether the number of options has a negative impact on the backers’ backing centroid.

## 4.3.1 Experimental Design

In this experiment, we focused on investigating the participants’ fixation density at the reward scheme area in a crowdfunding project webpage. Fixation density can be calculated as the total number of gaze points divided by the total minimal area to encapsulate all the gaze points [80]. In conventional eye tracking studies, participants’ fixation forms a fundamental unit of analysis in the examination of eye movement data [63]. Particularly, fixation refers to relatively stationary gazes during which the participants take a “foveal snapshot” of interesting stimuli [80]. This foveal snapshot, together with the visual signal received by participants’ eyes, is sent to brain for cognitive processing. In this regard, a high level of fixation density could represent more intense cognitive processing.

## 4.3.2 Participants

Forty participants were recruited from a public university in China. Among them, 30 were female and 10 were male. Their age ranged from 18 to 29 years, with an average age of 22.29 years. According to most prior related papers, a sample size of 40 participants is sufficient for an eye tracking study [2]. To keep the experimental realism, the participants were given a certain monetary incentive that equals to their 1-hour income after tax deduction.

## 4.3.3 Procedure

We used Tobii Pro X2-60 with a sampling rate of 60 Hz to collect participants’ eye movement data. The experimental procedure was similar to the procedure of our laboratory experiment mentioned in Study 2. The only difference was that we embedded some device calibration tasks between the treatment crowdfunding projects. In other words, when the participants completed a treatment crowdfunding project, they were required to complete a brief eye calibration task that took less than 10 seconds. There was no time constraint for the experiment. Figure 4 illustrates the experimental process.

## 5. General Discussion

## 5.1 Discussion of Findings

On the basis of the analysis results from the three consecutive studies, this paper provides solid evidence to support the inverted U-shaped impact of the number of options on the success of reward-based projects in crowdfunding, as well as a negative impact on the backers’ backing centroid. During the data analysis process, we adopted most of the contingent factors in prior crowdfunding studies as the control variables in our model, and the results of the analysis were significant [92]. Thus, our findings provide a strong support to the theoretical explanation. They are also in line with the findings of product assortment research [86]. In addition, we found that the nonlinear impact of the number of options on the success of reward-based projects can be applied to several product categories such as publishing, entertainment, and art. In this regard, the findings of our paper can provide a robust explanation to the impact of the number of options in reward-based crowdfunding.

We also consider the possible detrimental impact of the number of options from the choice

#

overload perspective. Choice overload refers to “a scenario in which the complexity of the decision problem faced by an individual exceeds the individual’s cognitive resources” [16]. With an increased number of options, backers may suffer from choice overload. However, we believe that this effect is not a critical concern in our research setting for three reasons. First, bounded cognitive processing capacity according to the choice overload literature [16, 78]. For example, the number of options of nearly 97.5% crowdfunding projects is less than 9 in our dataset. It is unlikely that the number of options within this range will cause choice overload [16]. Second, prior research found that option ordering could attenuate the difficulty of choosing an item by decreasing search costs [85]. In the three studies of this paper, options are placed by option price in an ascending order. Thus, the ordered options of crowdfunding projects further reduce the likelihood of incurring choice overload [14]. Third, the attributes of options in the reward scheme are alignable in our studies. It will significantly reduce the number of options will not cause choice overload in this paper.

The exclusion of choice overload effect does not mean that we neglected the impact of the number of options on the backers’ cognitive capacity for processing the funding activity. Instead, we examined the backers’ backing centroid and demonstrated that the increase in the number of options in a crowdfunding project can have a significant negative impact on the backers’ backing centroid. This indicates that backers will choose options that are listed at the lower order portion of the reward scheme when a crowdfunding project has a large number of options. This finding complies with the research findings from the human’s cognitive

#

processing discipline, in which researchers adopted the central capacity theory to explain the interplay between the human’s limited cognitive processing capability and the level of complexity of tasks [89]. When the number of options is large, backers, who have bounded cognitive capacity to seek and compare the options, would narrow down the scope of searching of the potential options in the reward scheme. At the same time, they would allocate more cognitive capacities to analyze/compare these potential options to find the most suitable one. Collectively, the two main findings of our paper provide a viable explanation for the underlying mechanism for the impact of the number of options on reward-based projects.

## 5.2 Theoretical Contributions

This paper contributes to the crowdfunding literature by adding solid evidence to the salient impact of the number of options in reward-based projects. Particularly, this paper provides threefold theoretical contribution crowdfunding.

First, this paper demonstrates an inverted U-shaped causal relationship between the number of options and the backers’ decision on supporting a reward-based project. This finding has never been identified in prior literature. Thus, our paper contributes to the existing knowledge base by providing an alternative explanation to the impact of the number of options. The extant studies on this research topic held two-pronged results. Some studies demonstrated that there is a positive relationship between the number of options and the success of crowdfunding projects [53]. Other studies, however, advocated a negative impact. By investigating the details of these studies, we observed that the number of options in the studies with negative impact outcomes is larger than the one in the studies with positive impact

#

outcomes. For example, Chen found that the number of options (reward tier in Chen’s paper)

has a negative impact on the success of crowdfunding projects [12]. The mean value of the

number of options is 11.04. Oppositely, the mean value of the number of options is around 5 in

some papers that held a positive view in explaining such impact [27]. Compared with extant

studies that had a mixed finding on the impact of the number of options, our paper shows that

the number of options could positively influence the success of reward-based projects, but only

to a point. Thus, our paper could provide a pertinent theoretical implication on identifying a

refined relationship between the number of options and the success of reward-based projects.

Second, our paper contributes to the existing knowledge by demonstrating a negative

impact of the number of options on the backers’ backing centroid. This research finding is

seldom found in the extant literature. This fills the research gap on investigating the impact of

the number of options on both the backers’ decision of supporting a crowdfunding project and

the decision of selecting the preferred option [32]. According to the literature of human

decision-making, the backers’ decision can be decomposed into (1) choice-based decision

(whether to support the project) and (2) selection-based decision (which option to select). The

success of a reward-based project is a manifestation of a collective decision on backers

choice-based decisions. Most of the extant literature examined the direct impact of the

crowdfunding projects’ disposition (e.g., the number of comments and the number of images)

on the success of crowdfunding projects. For example, Xiao investigated a list of the

crowdfunding projects’ dispositions on the total amount pledged, which is an indicator of the

success of crowdfunding projects [91]. Kunz examined how crowdfunding projects’ webpage

design, such as the display of preparation time, and the number of options influences the

#

success of crowdfunding projects [53]. There is no study that examines the impact of the number of options on backers’ selection-based decision. Our paper complements this empirical vacuum by the meticulous investigation of backers’ backing centroid. The results from the econometric study and the eye tracking experiment corroborate with each other and centroid. This research finding could provide insights that lead to a full range of examination on the number of options and the backers’ decision.

Third, this paper contributes to the utilization of a mixed-method research design (rather than a single-method research design) to investigate the research questions thoroughly. In this paper, we employed an econometric analysis study, a laboratory experimental study, and an eye tracking experimental study to investigate our research questions. As argued by Johnson and his colleagues [46], the mixed-method research design could develop the research findings as comprehensively and completely as possible, compared with the single-method research design. As suggested by Greene [36], this kind of mixed-method research design could use the results from one method to help inform the other method and gain more insights. In this regard, the mixed-method research design best suits to our research purpose, which Cook termed it as “critical multiplism” [19].

## 5.3 Practical Implications

Our paper also has important implications for practice, especially for fundraisers, to design reward-based projects to enhance the likelihood of succeeding. Several actionable guidelines can be generated with the basis of the findings of this paper. It is important while difficult for fundraisers to well understand that how and why backers will perceive the reward scheme that they provide in their crowdfunding projects to achieve fundraising success. Our findings of an inverted U-shaped impact the number of options on crowdfunding success can be served as a basic guideline for fundraisers when designing the reward scheme of their crowdfunding lower order portion of the reward scheme. The more the options, the lower the order placement should be set for the option with a high business value.

## 5.4 Limitations and Future Works

Although our study has highlighted several notable findings, there are some limitations in this paper. First, the dataset used in our study is from a focal reward-based crowdfunding platform. Thus, some endogenous specifications, such as culture, and the funding mechanism (e.g., keep-it-all or all-or-nothing) may influence the success rate of a crowdfunding project [93]. Thus, the findings of this paper may suffer the limitation on generalizability. To address this concern, future work may use datasets collected from different crowdfunding platforms. Second, our dataset consists only of cross-sectional data. The results will be more convincing if the study is conducted with a dataset of panel data. Finally, our work unveils only the influence of the number of options, which is a fundamental feature of the reward scheme of a crowdfunding project. Future studies are required for examining the joint impacts of the number of options with other important specifications, such as the quality of options.

## 6. Conclusions

The current research discusses how the number of options in reward-based crowdfunding projects can be utilized to enhance the success of the projects. Essentially, we find that the number of options could have an inverted U-shaped impact on the likelihood of having a successful crowdfunding project. At the same time, it has a negative impact on the backers backing centroid. Hence, this study is a modest step toward the development of a theoretically online reward-based crowdfunding context.
