## Abstract

Applying main path analysis, this paper investigates the IT outsourcing (ITO) field by identifying a set of papers that have played a central role in the development of the field and the major research themes emerging from the citation patterns. We selected the top 120,000 main paths from 6.45 million main paths among 798 ITO papers, resulting in a data set of 280 papers which represent the most important nodes supporting ITO knowledge flow. Based on our analysis of the multiple main paths, twelve major research themes emerged: ITO motivations, ITO decisions, ITO risks, debate around transaction cost theory, client-vendor relationship, the vendor’s perspective, psychological and formal contracts, ASP, BPO, opensourcing and crowdsourcing, offshore outsourcing, and multisourcing. Finally, we discuss future directions of ITO research in light of our findings. This is among the few studies that have used bibliometric analysis method to analyze and visualize the citation network of the rich body of ITO literature. The main path analysis precisely identified and pictured the major knowledge flow in the evolution of ITO research and major ITO research streams, thus provides an in-depth understanding of the ITO research in the last 20 years.

Keywords: IT Outsourcing, Global main path, Multiple Main Paths

## 1. Introduction

With more than 20 years’ development, information technology outsourcing (ITO) has experienced tremendous growth and aroused great enthusiasm among academic researchers (Lacity et al., 2009). A large body of diverse theoretical and empirical work in the ITO field demands researchers to continuously review research activities to date so that an in-depth understanding of this growing field can be achieved.

The ITO field has become increasingly complex due to several reasons. First, a variety of theoretical

#

bases have been applied in ITO research. In initial stage of ITO, researchers explained the ITO phenomenon from a neoclassical economic perspective (Loh and Venkatraman, 1992a; Hätönen and Eriksson, 2009). Later many other theories from reference discipline were used to study ITO issues. Lacity et al. (2010) reported that researchers had studied ITO from over 20 theoretical perspectives. Second, findings from ITO publications are often inconsistent and even contradictory (Lacity et al., 2009; Lacity et al., 2010; Fink, 2010). For example, Lacity et al. (2010) found a large number of contradictory conclusions in 741 relationships between 36 dependent variables and 138 independent variables extracted from 164 empirical ITO articles. Third, there are many sourcing varietals such as offshoring, business process outsourcing (BPO), application service providers (ASP), multisourcing, backsourcing, and recent crowdsourcing. New sourcing varietals kept emerging with the advancement of IT and dynamic changes of the business landscape. For example, the economic and market globalization is the antecedent of emergence of offshoring (Gonzalez et al., 2005), and the economic downturn in recent years changed the traditional way of offshoring significantly (Lacity et al., 2009). The prevalence of the Internet makes ASP popular. With the recent development of Web 2.0, cloud computing and Internet of Things, more novel issues related to ITO will appear. In the context of new sourcing varietals, past topics such as ITO decision and client-vendor relationship could have new meanings due to new characteristics of the sourcing environment and novel issues that are specific to the new varietals and have not emerged in previous varietals also stimulated research.

A comprehensive review that precisely analyzes the complex ITO literature would be imperative to gain a good understanding of the constantly updating ITO field. Such a review will help researchers understand the developing path of the field so that they can judge what was important, what is important, and what will be important. This will facilitate the healthy growth of the ITO field by guiding researchers to focus on meaningful research questions. In addition, the sheer scale of the ITO literature makes it difficult to conduct a comprehensive review without quantitative methodologies. Most of the existing ITO reviews are descriptive and based on researchers’ manual processing. These reviews could be constrained by the researchers who had limited time and energy and could only review papers within the limit of their cognitive capacity (Raghuram et al., 2010). It is necessary to confirm the findings of these reviews with more objective methodologies. There is also a real possibility that some important ITO articles are neglected. For instance, the review by Gonzalez et al. (2006a) was based on 131 papers and the review by Lacity et al. (2010) included 164 papers. While the authors of these reviews might have strived to select the most representative papers, the size of ITO literature is much larger than what have been reviewed. A global review that embraces more objectivity and incorporates as many articles as possible is desirable.

In this article, we conduct a bibliometric study to map the field of ITO research. This approach allows us to identify the knowledge structure of ITO research and to uncover underlying knowledge diffusion patterns from different angles. The citation relationships between publications shape a network that reveals structural and knowledge flow, and provide an opportunity to evaluate the importance of specific papers quantitatively. Specifically, we perform a main path analysis in the ITO field to identify major research themes emerged in the history of ITO research and describe how these themes evolved over time, and to compare the findings of the current review with existing reviews. Our analytic approach and findings are of value to ITO researchers. The findings can help to understand the genesis and current state of the field, and hopefully meaningful and fruitful avenues for future research can be found based on this paper. In addition, to the best of our knowledge, this is among the first to demonstrate how the main path analysis method can be employed to examine important IS areas. It can make a methodological contribution to IS research by providing a novel approach to reviewing existing literature.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. It first provides a description of background in section 2. Then it explains the analysis methodology in section 3. Data collection is discussed in section 4, followed by detailed analysis results in section 5. We predict future directions of ITO research in light of our findings in section 6. Section 7 concludes the paper by summarizing limitations and contributions.

## 2. Background

## 2.1 Previous Reviews of ITO Research

Scholars have surveyed the ITO literature and reviewed ITO development in different time periods from various angles. These reviews take three different approaches – qualitative, quantitative, and hybrid. The quantitative approach to reviewing ITO research is rather straightforward. For example, Alsudairi and Dwivedi (2010) surveyed the ITO field quantitatively by employing a meta-analysis approach to profile theoretical and methodological underpinnings of ITO related articles. Statistical analyses were performed to analyze attributes of the papers such as subject category, journal, publication year, author, author’s institution, country, citation frequency, keywords, and abstract. Such reviews focus on characteristics of the publications rather than their content.

Most ITO review articles employed a qualitative/descriptive approach. Among these reviews, Dibbern et al.’s (2004) global assessment of 15-year ITO research is the most noteworthy because of its depth and breadth (Lacity et al., 2010). Some papers examined the ITO literature from a historical perspective. For example, Gottschalk and Solli-Sæther (2006) identified three stages for ITO relationship evolution: cost stage, resource stage and partnership stage. Hätönen and Eriksson (2009) recognized three phases of the ITO development history from both practical and theoretical perspectives. Furthermore, some reviews focused on specific ITO issues. King and Torkzadeh (2008) described the state and issues of offshoring. Chadee and Raman’s (2009) review is focused on empirical studies on international outsourcing of IT services and identified four main research areas during the 1992-2007 period. Lacity et al. (2009) extracted practical insights on six topics relevant to ITO practitioners by reviewing 191 ITO articles.

A few recent reviews employed a hybrid approach by applying both qualitative and quantitative methods. Gonzalez et al. (2006a) indentified major topics and the most often applied methodologies in ITO research in addition to quantitative analysis of paper attributes. Lacity et al. (2010) analyzed 164 empirical ITO articles published from 1992 to the first quarter of 2010, and developed an ITO decisions model and

ITO outcomes model based on empirical evidence of significant relationships between predictors and dependent variables. Using the same method, Lacity et al. (2011b) reviewed the entire body of BPO research to develop BPO decisions and BPO outcomes models.

The existing reviews provide many insights into a variety of ITO issues. These reviews offer rich discussion on specific topics with great depth and have deepened our understanding of the ITO literature. However, given the vast body of ITO literature, there is still much to learn. First, the previous descriptive reviews could be constrained by the knowledge structure of their authors (Raghuram et al., 2010) and a less subjective approach may offer an alternative approach to interpreting ITO research. Second, although some reviews adopted a quantitative approach, the statistical analyses are relatively simple and have not analyzed each paper’s importance in the citation network of ITO research. Third, the reviews taking a hybrid approach often focus on empirical studies which represent a small number of ITO papers. For example, the review by Gonzalez et al. (2006a) was based on 131 papers published between 1992 and 2005. Our search indicates that 230 ITO papers were published during this period. A holistic review of the ITO literature taking more publications into consideration could provide a more complete understanding of the ITO research.

In light of the limitations of previous reviews, we survey the existing ITO literature in a comprehensive manner by conducting a main path analysis. Specifically, we will create a citation network of the ITO field that takes every ITO article’s influence into account and use bibliometric analysis techniques to analyze the evolution and structure of the ITO field.

## 2.2 Bibliometric Analysis

The citation network among ITO papers creates a map of knowledge dissemination over time. It also provides a quantitative and objective basis for describing the development trajectory of the field. This citation network can be analyzed by using bibliometric analysis, a quantitative approach to analyzing and visualizing emerging trends, intellectual structures, and citation patterns in scientific literature (Chen, 2006).

#

There are several ways to conduct bibliometric analysis and the particular method used in this study is main path analysis. Different from the traditional bibliometric methods such as bibliographic coupling and co-citation analysis, the main path analysis focuses on the links between the cited and citing papers instead of the citation counts of a paper. The citation relationship between two articles indicates how knowledge flows, and a large number of these citation links form a network that reveals how knowledge disseminates (Liu et al., 2013). The goal of main path analysis is to trace the main knowledge flow in this citation network. The notion of main path analysis was first proposed by Hummon and Doreian in 1989. Later, Liu et al. (2013) described this method as “local main path” and proposed several variants of the original main path analysis, including “global main path” and “multiple main paths”. The validity of main path analysis methods have been proven in previous studies (Liu et al., 2013; Liu and Lu, 2012; Lu et al., 2012).

Main path analysis can offer several advantages. It makes it possible to reveal the structural backbone of the development of the ITO research. Both direct and indirect influences of a paper are taken into consideration during main path analysis, thus providing a more accurate estimation of the paper than the conventional ‘citation count’ method that reckons only direct influences (Liu et al., 2013). Moreover, the detailed information provided in multiple main paths helps to indentify the important ITO themes that emerged in different times and how these themes evolved over time. These research themes result from objective citation relationships among articles, which can more precisely reflect the actual state of the field and avoid researchers’ personal interpretations (Raghuram et al., 2010).

## 3. Methodologies

Our review method produces two main paths. The global main path shows the development trajectory of ITO research from a satellite view, and the multiple main paths extend development trajectories at a more detailed level by adding more global main paths with smaller weights. As Liu and Lu (2012) described, an integrated approach should be taken to simultaneously generate and analyze these complementary main paths to uncover the evolution of ITO research. In this section we will briefly describe this method.

#

## 3.1 Concepts of Main Path Analysis

In the course of a scientific field’s development, new articles draw information from previous articles and add new ideas. The citation relationship between the citing and cited articles indicates the dyadic knowledge flow. The citation network thus is a directional network which denotes the scientific knowledge flows. The longer has the field developed, the larger and denser the network becomes.

A citation network can be seen as a collection of vertices and edges. A vertex represents an article, and an edge denotes the citation relationship between two articles. There are four types of vertices: sources, sinks, intermediate points and isolate (Verspagen, 2007). Sources make no citations but are cited; they are origins of knowledge. Sinks cite other vertices but are not cited; they are the end points of knowledge dissemination. Intermediate points both cite and are cited. An “isolate” neither makes citations nor is cited by other articles, which is relatively rare. Many paths start from sources, traverse through intermediate points, and end in the sinks, thus forming a citation network.

## 3.2 Global Main Path

The global main path refers to the path that has the greatest weight in a citation network, representing the backbone of knowledge dissemination in the field (Liu and Lu, 2012). In a global main path, the weight of each link reflects the importance of the citation relationship. A citation needed in paths between many articles is assumed to be more crucial than a citation rarely needed for linking articles (Wouter et al., 2005). The weight of each link is indicated by traversal count which measures the frequency a citation link has been traversed if one exhausts all paths from all sources to all end sinks (Liu and Lu, 2012). Following Batagelj (2003)’s recommendation, we apply the search path count (SPC) to calculate traversal count in this paper.

We use a simple citation network shown in Figure 1 to demonstrate how SPC works. There are two sources and two sinks in Figure 1. We can get 6 paths from all sources to all sinks. SPC for each link is defined as the total number of times the link is traversed. For example, link V1-V4 has a SPC value of 3, because it is shared by 3 paths: V1-V4-V5, V1-V4-V7-V8-V9, and V1-V4-V8-V9. The larger the SPC value, the more important the link’s role in transmitting knowledge (Liu et al., 2013).

Figure 1. A simple citation network with SPC values shown

The global main path is the path with the largest overall SPC value, which suggests the overall importance in knowledge flow of the field (Liu and Lu, 2012). In Figure 1, the path V1-V4-V7-V8-V9 is the global main path because its SPC value is 7, larger than any other path’s SPC. We developed a C language program to search for the global main path.

## 3.3 Multiple Main Paths

The global main path only shows the backbone of a citation network, which is insufficient to provide a reasonably complete understanding of a prolific scientific filed with many subfields and research streams. In this study, we found more than six million paths from sources to sinks in the ITO citation network. A single main path would be too simple to represent such a complicated network. To address this issue, multiple main paths can be created to reveal details about the ITO research field. This was done by selecting more paths whose SPC values are high but smaller than the global main path (Liu et al., 2013). The more paths included, the more precisely the citation network can be described. To maintain a balance between precision and conciseness, we selected the top 2% main paths based on SPC values for this review. We also developed a C language program to get the multiple main paths.

#

The multiple main paths form a simplified knowledge transferring network which includes the most representative knowledge transferring routes. Therefore, each of the papers in the multiple main paths plays a critical role in disseminating knowledge among the ITO research community and a collection of these papers provide us a knowledge base for reviewing past ITO research. We can further classify these papers in terms of their topics and the citation relationships between them to identify major research streams. However, the multiple main paths are still a fairly complex citation network and do not show clear research themes. A citation link on the main paths could have different natures. A citing paper is substantially connected to the cited when the citation is based on the semantic content of the cited paper. In contrast, a citing paper is peripherally connected to the cited when the cited paper is used to support a secondary concept, a special terminology, a statistical method, or a logical argument in the citing paper. Two loosely connected papers could have completely different research topics. Thus, we believe a peripheral connection cannot indicate thematic commonality between the two papers. In order to uncover research themes, we need to prune the multiple main paths by dropping peripheral citation links while keeping substantial citation links. Specifically, we carefully reviewed each pair of linked papers and evaluated whether the citation relationship reflected similarity in their research themes. If the two papers shared no clear common topics, the link was deleted (see Appendix A for an example of pruning). After pruning, only papers with similar topics stay connected and a set of such connected papers would define a research theme. Whereas the global main path shows the major artery of knowledge flow, multiple main paths reveal important ancillary developments branched off this main artery. To get a comprehensive understanding of the field of ITO research, both views are necessary (Liu et al., 2013). Therefore, we analyze both the global main path and multiple main paths.

## 4. Data Collection

To get a representative data set of ITO publications, we adopted ISI Web of Science (WOS) as the data source for this study. Specifically, we selected Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-Expanded) and

Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI). The first ITO publication was in 1992; we thus collected ITO papers published from 1992 to 09/01/2013, a 22-year window.

![](/api/attachments/AH5KFQ9F/fulltext/images/9e09bbcad34253eac230618d245877180aed9ad3885c34e151e98ff9fae5a307.jpg)  
Figure 2. Data collection process

Figure 2 illustrates how we collected ITO articles for this study. To get an exhaustive list of ITO papers from WOS, a variety of combination of keywords were used. After several tests, we finalized the search conditions as “((IT OR IS) AND (outsourc\* or offshor\*))” and “((information technology) or (information system\*))” in topic. The wildcard notation asterisks dictate the search engine to take “outsource” and “outsourcing”, “offshore” and “offshoring”, “system” and “systems” into consideration. The searches resulted in 1353 papers. Because many articles in this dataset are not related to ITO, we performed data cleansing. First, we refined the dataset based on the WOS discipline categories and journals successively. A total of 13 obviously irrelevant disciplines (e.g., Zoology) and 136 papers in these disciplines were excluded. After

#

the disciplines were refined, we further excluded 12 journals (e.g., Marine Chemistry) that are unlikely to publish ITO research and 69 papers on these journals. This process reduced the dataset to 1148 papers. Second, we read the abstracts (if necessary, the text) of all of the papers and manually removed 496 papers that contain the word “outsourcing” in the text, but do not study ITO related topics. For example, a paper on supply chain sustainability mentions outsourcing but its research question has nothing to do with ITO. Another example is that for a paper on IT security management, its topic is partially related to ITO and some arguments rely on ITO, but its main research question is not related to ITO at all. This further reduced the dataset to 652 papers.

In addition, we identified some highly cited articles not indexed in the WOS database. Specifically, we used all of the articles cited by the 652 papers to create a citation network and selected the articles with 5 or more citations in this network into our research dataset. This resulted in 57 more papers. We also added ITO papers cited in any of the nine ITO reviews (Dibbern et al., 2004; Gonzalez et al., 2006a; Gottschalk and Solli-Sæther, 2006; King and Torkzadeh, 2008; Chadee and Raman, 2009; Hätönen and Eriksson, 2009; Lacity et al., 2009; Lacity et al., 2010; Alsudairi and Dwivedi, 2010) but not in our dataset. A total of 98 additional papers were added based on these reviews. Thus, 155 papers outside WOS were manually added. Furthermore, we excluded nine review papers from the dataset because these papers address a wide range of ITO topics and cannot be classified into a specific research stream. The final dataset included 798 papers.1 Although this dataset did not cover every ITO paper ever published, our rigorous data selection process help to ensure that it can reasonably represent the ITO field.

We configured all of the 798 papers into the WOS citation format, and fed the data into Histcite (Garfield, 2009), a bibliometric analysis software, to derive a .NET file that contains the citation relationships among the papers. Then we used a self-developed C program to carry out main path analysis based on the .NET file and used Pajek (Batagelj and Mrvar, 1998) to visualize the output.

## 5.1 Global Main Path

The global main path (Figure 3) shows the most important knowledge flow of the ITO citation network (Liu et al., 2013). Each node in the path denotes an article (see Appendix B for details). An arrow between two nodes indicates the direction of knowledge flow. The line thickness is determined by the SPC value.

The global main path suggests how knowledge evolves over time in ITO research. It basically is the

#

citation path that has been gone through most frequently by ITO research. Therefore, it suggests a general overview of significant ITO knowledge flow. A visual inspection of Figure 3 reveals two major themes based on the thickness of the sequence of adjacent links. The first theme, starting from Loh and Venkatraman (1992a) to Arnett and Jones (1994), is focused on motivation, adoption, and decision issues and we label it as “ITO decisions.” The second theme, starting from McFarlan and Nolan (1995) to Nieto and Rodriguez (2011), examines a variety of issues regarding how to manage ITO relationships and activities to achieve ITO success and we label it as “ITO management.” The last two papers on the global main path (Luo et al., 2012; Luo et al., 2013) do not show a clear theme content-wise.2 Given that both papers address the business process offshoring context, they could be interpreted as belonging to the booming research on offshoring. The evolution of themes suggested by the global main path seems to be consistent with how Lacity et al. (2010) categorize ITO research. They proposed that all ITO research basically addresses two important problems: ITO decisions and ITO outcomes. ITO management is closely related to ITO outcomes given that the purpose of ITO management is to achieve various ITO outcomes.

It is noticeable that a few scholars heavily influenced the ITO knowledge diffusion over time whose work has appeared repeatedly on the global main path. In the early 1990s, Loh and Venkatraman (1992a; 1992b) played an important role in studying motivations and determinants of ITO decisions. Later, Lee figured prominently in studying ITO relationship and ITO success by having two papers on the path (Lee and Kim, 1999; Lee, 2001). Levina also heavily influenced ITO research by contributing two articles to the global main path (Levina and Ross, 2003; Levina and Vaast, 2008). It should be noted that some highly cited papers might not appear on the global main path. But it does not undermine the value of the global main path (Liu and Lu, 2012), and it does not devalue the highly cited papers, either. The goal of the global main path is to provide an alternative angle to overview the ITO research field. It is not meant to be complete. There are undoubtedly many important articles missing from this path and this limitation can be overcome by constructing multiple main paths.

## 5.2 Multiple Main Paths

Based on the global main path, we developed multiple main paths to depict the ITO research field with more detailed information. We successively selected main paths with smaller overall SPC values. How many paths to select is an arbitrary decision and depends on how many details one is interested (Liu et al., 2013). Since there are no formal rules regarding the number of paths to be selected, we estimated the number based on previous studies on main path analysis. Liu et al. (2012) suggested selecting paths with SPC values within 20% of the largest SPC value, or the top one-fifth of the citation network. Based on this rule, they selected only 12 out of 421 articles (2.8%). We worried that too few articles would not reasonably represent the entire citation network. Therefore, we increased the range to 33%, or the paths with top one-third of the SPC values. This was exactly 120,000 main paths (or 2% of the 6.45 million main paths), consisting of 280 papers out of 798 (35%).

These 280 papers and their citation relationships form a shrunk network of ITO research, which includes the most representative knowledge transferring routines. Although reducing 6.45 million paths to 120,000 paths is a significant downsizing, the network is still too complicated to show separable research themes (see Figure 4). As explained earlier, we pruned this network to delete peripheral citation links. Two authors independently examined the title, keywords, abstract, and main text of the two papers of each citation link and determined the citation link’s nature. The link was deleted if it was marked as peripheral by both authors. If the two authors disagreed on a specific citation link, a third author’s opinion was obtained to resolve the disagreement. After pruning, the citation network revealed a tree pattern with several recognizable braches (see Figure 5). Each branch represents a research stream of ITO. As Figure 5 shows, along the global main path, we identified 12 research streams. Seven are related to specific issues in ITO, including ITO motivations, ITO decisions, ITO risks, debate around transaction cost theory, client-vendor relationship, the vendor’s perspective, and psychological and formal contracts; and five are concerned with specific ITO types, including ASP, BPO, opensourcing and crowdsourcing, offshore outsourcing, and multisourcing. Because ITO success is a broad topic in ITO and most papers are related to ITO success to some extent, we examine the 12 streams and discuss how ITO success research evolved over time. Table 2 shows the number of papers, start year, and a brief summary of the content of each stream. The 12 citation sub-networks of the research streams which show each paper’s first author name and publication year can be found in Appendix C. Next we will discuss each research stream in detail.

## 5.2.1 ITO Motivations

The works of Loh and Venkatraman (1992a and 1992b) stimulated many studies on the determinants of ITO. Scholars studied this research question from economic, technological, strategic, organizational and political perspectives, and found a plethora of complex, interrelated motives drove firms to adopt ITO (Grover and Teng, 1993; Baldwin et al., 2001). In the early 1990s, companies jumped into ITO bandwagon with little mindful thinking because they believed they could achieve cost reduction and access to high quality IT services and skills via ITO (Loh and Venkatraman, 1992a; McLellan et al., 1995; Ang and Straub, 1998). Some companies outsourced their IT functions for strategic reasons even if their internal IS department could provide similar results as outsourcing vendors (Grover et al., 1994). ITO may also take place in firms which considered IT as core competencies (Saunders et al., 1997; McLellan et al., 1995).

Many motivating factors for ITO have been identified from the organizational level, such as mimetic influences among organizations (Ang and Cummings, 1997; Loh and Venkatraman, 1992b; Hu et al., 1997). Organizations can eliminate the burden of internal IT function and accelerate reengineering through ITO. In addition, some companies outsource for political reasons, such as to get around the political barrier that prevented the IS department from initiating cost savings (Lacity and Hirschheim, 1993), or to solve internal conflicts (Baldwin et al, 2001). Over time, the business environment has changed, and some motivating factors lost relevance gradually. For example, cost saving is not the primary reason to outsource for some companies any more (Gonzalez et al., 2010a; Gonzalez et al., 2010b). Clients are looking for more added-value and higher quality services from ITO vendors (Gonzalez et al., 2010a).

## 5.2.2 ITO Decisions

Articles in this stream are focused on issues surrounding the decision making process of ITO. Three subtopics emerged under this branch: to outsource or not, how to outsource, and re-outsourcing decisions.

(1) To outsource or not. Studies on this topic attempted to explain whether a firm should adopt ITO from several perspectives such as the evaluation practices approach comparing vendor bids and in-house costs (Willcocks et al., 1995b; Willcocks et al., 1996), the strategic perspective focusing on how to use the IT market to leverage business advantages (Willcocks et al., 1995a), and the economic perspective intending to find a balance between benefits and risks (Jurison, 1995). Once companies are determined to get involved in ITO, the follow-up question is “how to outsource?”

(2) How to outsource? Three prominent concepts emerged from our analysis regarding how to outsource – flexible outsourcing, selective outsourcing and how to select a suitable vendor. First, as an

#

important approach to outsourcing, flexible sourcing is found to be strategically imperative in the dynamic business environment (Tan and Sia, 2006) and can contribute to outsourcing success (Sia et al., 2008). Second, another important outsourcing approach, selective sourcing has been applied by firms to decide which parts of IT function should be outsourced and which should be kept in-house (Lacity et al., 1996; Lacity and Willcocks, 1998). Different quantitative methods were employed to develop decision models for selective sourcing such as the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) method (Yang and Huang, 2000; Wang and Yang, 2007) and the group decision making approach (Nazari-Shirkouhi et al., 2011). Third, researchers developed methods to help firms select appropriate vendors by evaluating vendor characteristics (Han and Lee, 2012) and capabilities (Lewis, 1999; Feeny et al., 2005). One popular method is the two-stage contract approach which uses a pilot project to test the vendor capabilities before signing the formal contract. Several quantitative models were employed to examine the selection decision under this two-stage framework, such as game theory model (Snir and Hitt, 2004), combinatorial optimization model (Cao and Wang, 2007), and fuzzy VIKOR model (Chen and Wang, 2009). The AHP method was also employed to assist small and medium sized enterprises to select vendors (Chang et al., 2012).

(3) Re-outsourcing decisions. Many companies face re-outsourcing decisions when their current outsourcing contracts had to be renegotiated or terminated due to dissatisfactory outcomes or changed organizational and business environments. Research related to this topic has focused on understanding why firms continue with the current vendor or switch to a new vendor. It has been found that the re-outsourcing decision is affected by various factors such as the strategic impact of outsourced activities and vendor substitutability (Nam et al., 1996), switching costs (Whitten and Wakefield, 2006), prior relationships with vendors (Chou et al., 2006) negative previous ITO outcomes, loss of control and the degradation of IS services (Gorla and Lau, 2010). If clients decide to discontinue the current ITO relationship, they may resort to backsourcing. Veltri et al. (2008) found a set of internal and external factors such as organizational change and industry change that contribute to the decision to backsource. Whitten

#

and Leidner (2006) found that product quality, service quality, relationship quality, and switching costs are related to the decision of backsourcing. Recently, Chu et al. (2012) based on threat theory to develop some guidelines for clients about vendor transition.

## 5.2.3 ITO Risks

The articles in this branch mainly address four types of ITO risks: domestic ITO risks, offshore risks, BPO risks, and ASP risks. For domestic ITO risks, some conceptual papers identified lists of risks, or developed risks models, and proposed measures to mitigate ITO risks (Earl, 1996; Barthelemy, 2001). Empirical works confirmed the existence of ITO risks and evaluated risk management strategies via case studies (Willcocks and Lacity, 1999; Willcocks et al., 1999). For example, long term large scale total ITO is a high-risk strategy because it can lead to a series of risks such as power asymmetry developed in favor of the vendor (Gonzalez et al., 2005). Willcocks et al. (1999) suggested that this type of ITO could be successful if a series of measures are taken in time, such as careful delineation of outsourcing type and scope, and strict vendor selection criteria and processes. Risks in offshoring are more complicated. Nakatsu and Iacovou (2009) conducted a Delphi study to explore risk factors in both domestic and offshore outsourced software projects, and found that the offshore context not only was more vulnerable to traditional project management risks than the domestic context, but also suffered from unique risks not found in the domestic context. High risks in offshore outsourcing may be associated with the difficulty to maintain control over long distances and different cultures, norms, laws, and languages. Since most BPO projects are non-core activities (Shi, 2007), they were thought to have fewer risks. However, there are still some risks that are unique in BPO. For example, Shi (2007) identified different types of risks in BPO and the findings suggested that clients may face risks in areas of customer services, operations costs, information security, business continuity, and short-term market performance. Researchers also found risks in ASP. Kern et al. (2002) developed a risk-assessment and risk-mitigation framework about ASP and found that many risks in ASP are same as in ITO but emerged with different patterns.

#

Many ITO risks related to vendors. In fact, Gonzalez et al. (2010a) found that in large Spanish firms, ITO risks mainly arise from their providers. Bahli and Rivard (2005) also found that the transaction, client and supplier are three major sources of risk factors in ITO. Lacity et al. (2009) identified 34 risks in ITO projects and a number of risks on the list are related to vendors’ power, trust, capability, and experience. Vendors are not only a major source of ITO risks (Gonzalez et al., 2010b; Bahli and Rivard, 2005), but also face risks themselves. Only a few studies have studied ITO risks from the vendor’s perspective (Taylor, 2007; Liu et al., 2011). For example, Taylor (2007) found that Hong Kong vendors are not only faced with typical risks that threaten project outcome success, but also faced with location-specific risks. Liu et al. (2011) explored the moderating effects of project uncertainty on the relationship between risk management and IS development project performance from the vendor’s perspective.

## 5.2.4 Applicatiblity of Transaction Cost Theory

Transaction Cost Theory (TCT) is the dominant theoretical framework for the study of ITO (Lacity et al., 2011a) and has been employed to explain many ITO practices (Ngwenyama and Bryson, 1999; Wang, 2002). However, as the ITO practices kept developing and became more complicated, concerns over the applicability of TCT were raised. For example, Lacity and Willcocks (1995) found that language ambiguities and the unit of analysis are two major obstacles to the applicability of TCT in ITO decision research. To extend its applicability, TCT has been integrated with other theories to explain ITO decisions, including comparative economic theories and incomplete contracts theories (Ang and Straub, 1998; Bahli and Rivard, 2003; Aubert et al., 2004), resource based theory and resource dependency theory (Schwarz et al., 2009; Watjatrakul, 2005), social exchange theory (Whitten and Wakefield, 2006). Researchers have also investigated ITO phenomena from both economic and knowledge based perspective (Shi et al., 2005; Tiwana and Bush, 2007; Schwarz et al., 2009).

Recent years have witnessed a heated debate on the applicability of TCT in ITO research. Previous studies based on TCT have generated mixed or contradictory findings, and ITO scholars derived drastically

#

different opinions from this observation. Alaghehband et al. (2011) argued that the mixed results were caused by misapplication of TCT and contended that TCT should be applied in the ITO context more faithfully. However, Lacity et al. (2011a) insisted that the ITO phenomenon is too complex to be interpreted by TCT and “ITO research has matured to the point that we could and should be building on ITO-specific research findings and developing what we have defined as indigenous ITO theory rather than continuing to rely over-heavily on reference discipline theories” (p. 139). Lacity and her coauthors also urged researchers to develop endogenous ITO theory in two review papers (Lacity et al., 2010; Lacity et al., 2011b). However, it takes time for new theories to develop and before that TCT is still being applied as the theoretical foundation for studying ITO decisions (Aubert et al., 2012).

## 5.2.5 ASP

Application service provision (ASP) is a sourcing model that promises to deliver best-of-breed, scalable and flexible business applications to customer desktops. Studies on ASP were concentrated on the traditional themes such as determinants of ASP (Schwarz et al., 2009), risks in ASP (Kern et al., 2002), ASP success (Kern et al., 2002; Susarla et al., 2003; Lee et al., 2007), and the client-vendor relationship in ASP (Swinarski et al., 2008). Recently, netsourcing, an innovative form of traditional ASP, has attracted much attention. Netsourcing refers to a stack of various ASP service types such as storage service providers, managed service providers, full service providers, business service providers, and so forth (Kern et al., 2002). Different from ASP’s one-to-many business model, netsourcing incorporates a one-to-one customization business model, in which customers can receive customized services (Kern et al., 2002). Research on netsourcing is limited, addressing problems such as determinants of netsourcing (Loebbecke and Huyskens, 2006) and netsourcing decisions (Loebbecke and Huyskens, 2009).

## 5.2.6 Client-Vendor Relationship

As Figure 5 shows, the client-vendor relationship research stream started with McFarlan and Nolan (1995) who suggested that the fast-changing IT environment makes it desirable for clients and their

#

suppliers to form strategic alliances. Thereafter a large number of ITO papers focusing on inter-organizational relationships emerged. These papers mostly examine client-vendor relationship characteristics and the impact of client-vendor relationship on ITO success.

Research in this stream collectively shows that the client-vendor relationship is a social exchange relationship characterized by the tension between trust and control (Lee, 2001; Mao et al., 2008). The ITO clients and vendors are both contractually and socially related to each other, and therefore their relationship has economic, contractual, and social characteristics (Kern, 1997; Kern and Willcocks, 2000; Kern and Willcocks, 2002). Stakeholder management and norm building are important to successful client-vendor relationship (Kern and Blois, 2002; Gottschalk and Solli-Sæther 2005, 2006). Sabherwal (1999) identified four types of trust in client-supplier relationships. Later research building on Sabherwal’s work examined determinants of trust, how to build the trust mechanism and maintaining trust (Babar et al., 2007; Lander et al., 2004; Mao et al., 2008). Both initial trust and ongoing trust are found to be important (Lee et al., 2008; Lee and Choi, 2011). Information sharing, communication quality, and inter-firm adaptation are found to significantly affect trust building between clients and vendors (Mao et al., 2008).

Clients and vendors not only need to trust each other to achieve efficient cooperation, but also need control mechanisms to monitor ITO performance. Various controls that clients use to regulate ITO outcomes have been investigated. Choudhury and Sabherwal (2003) found that outcome based control is dominant in ITO contracts, especially in the early stage of the projects, and behavior control and clan control are often added when the project encounter problems. Rustagi et al. (2008) suggest that when both parties have strong trust, clients use formal control mechanisms to a lesser extent and when task uncertainty is high clients are likely to use formal controls. Mao et al. (2008) found both trust and contro can affect ITO success significantly. Gefen et al. (2008) demonstrated that business familiarity can imply trust and thus reduce adverse selection and moral hazard risks between clients and vendors.

There is general consensus among ITO researchers that the client-vendor relationship plays an

#

important role in determining ITO success (Lee et al., 2003; Lacity et al., 2010). Research on the impact of ITO relationship on ITO success takes two approaches. One approach attempts to identify ITO relationship characteristics that predict ITO success, and the other is focused on the complementary nature of relational governance and contractual governance. First, regarding predictors of ITO success, Grover et al. (1996) found a strong association between partnership dimensions such as trust, communication, and cooperation and ITO success. Subsequent research shows that partnership quality is a key predictor of outsourcing success (Lee and Kim, 1997, 1999) and can mediate the impact of knowledge sharing on ITO success (Lee, 2001). Factors that contribute to high quality partnership such as mutual benefits and commitment have also been found to better predict ITO success than need-based factors (Lee and Kim, 2005). Researchers also explored the impact of trust on ITO success from an evolution process perspective (e.g. Lee et al., 2008; Lee and Choi, 2011). Qi and Chau (2013) found that interpersonal trust has a stronger effect on ITO success than interorganizational trust and its effect is mediated by knowledge sharing.

In the second approach, the client-vendor relationship has been investigated under a different label – relational governance, and it is often studied vis-à-vis contractual governance. Most studies in this area are built on Sabherwal (1999) which finds that the balance between trust (relational governance) and structure control (contractual governance) is likely to lead to better ITO performance. Recent studies confirmed that formal contracts and relational governance function as complements rather than substitutes (Goo et al., 2009; Qi and Chau, 2012). In addition, Goo et al. (2008) found that commitment can moderate the impact of service-level agreements, a form of contractual governance, on outsourcing success. Goo and Huang (2008) studied the dynamics of interactions between formal control and relational governance and found that certain contractual elements could enhance trust and relational commitment between outsourcing partners. A recent study revealed that the interacting effect of formal contracts and relational governance may not provide symmetric benefits to all parties, but rather benefit the partner who faces greater risk in the ITO exchange (Gopal and Koka, 2012).

#

Our main path analysis shows that client-vendor relationship remained a popular topic in recent ITO research. Some works were conducted in emerging economies (e.g. Ren et al., 2011; Li et al., 2011) and culture-specific factors such as guanxi and mianzi have been considered (Du et al., 2011). Researchers start to emphasize both the clients and vendors’ perspective simultaneously when studying their relationships (Lee et al., 2008; Haried and Ramamurthy, 2009; Lee and Choi, 2011). Some studies have been conducted to examine ITO relationship quality in the context of public sectors which has largely been ignored in the past (Moon et al., 2010; Swar et al., 2012).

## 5.2.7 The vendor’s perspective

To achieve ITO success, both parties involved need to be able to reap benefits from the arrangements. Most researchers solely focused on the interest of clients before Levina and Ross (2003), and the suppliers’ concerns have rarely been explored. Levina and Ross (2003) first proposed ITO value proposition from the vendor's perspective, drawing much attention from ITO scholars. The vendor’s perspective has been taken to investigate a number of ITO problems such as project management methodology (e.g. Mojsilovic et al., 2007), ITO risks (e.g. Taylor, 2007; Liu et al., 2011), BPO (e.g. Lahiri and Kedia, 2009; Lahiri et al., 2012), offshoring (e.g. Jarvenpaa and Mao, 2008; Mao et al., 2008), ITO relationship (e.g. Lahiri and Kedia, 2009; Deng et al., 2013), and contractual governance (e.g. Qi and Chau,2012).

Research from the vendor’s perspective will not end as long as vendors are treated as an important part of the ITO phenomenon (Lacity et al., 2008). As more ITO vendors in emerging economies enter the outsourcing competition, they need to gain management insights from ITO research to guide their strategies. Researchers have done much research on offshoring from Western clients to Indian vendors (e.g., Lahiri and Kedia, 2009; Gopal et al., 2011;Lahiri et al., 2012) and paid relatively less attention to outsourcing vendors in other countries.

## 5.2.8 BPO

This research stream is concerned with various issues of business process outsourcing (BPO). The

#

advanced information technology and telecommunication skills make it possible to outsource standard processes. Economic and strategic benefits such as cost advantages, focusing on core competencies, access to specialized resources and quality improvements spurred many organizations to outsource some business processes to service providers (Gewald and Dibbern, 2009). However, facing risks such as low service performance, unexpected payment, potential lose of critical resources and capabilities, and managers’ job security concerns (Gewald and Dibbern, 2009), companies are cautious to adopt BPO. A multiple-perspective approach integrating the transaction, the organization, and its context is useful for firms to decide whether BPO is appropriate (Borman, 2006). Tanriverdi et al. (2007) found that low modular business processes detachable from their supporting IT infrastructure tend to be offshored while high modular processes tightly coupled with IT infrastructure tend to be outsourced domestically

Partnership quality is consistently found to be a significant predictor of BPO success (Ee et al., 2013; Lahiri and Kedia, 2009). Bharadwaj et al. (2010) found that BPO clients pay more attention on how to build successful relationship with the vendor, rather than the vendor's competencies. Business understanding, commitment, communication and top management support positively influence partnership quality (Ee et al., 2013). Besides partnership quality, the balance between relational and contractual governance can also have significant effect on BPO success. For example, Rai et al. (2012) found that a hybrid relational-contractual governance mechanism has a positive correlation with BPO satisfaction. Wullenweber et al. (2008) showed that process standardization has direct impact on BPO success due to production cost economies, and it also has an indirect impact on BPO success via relational and contractual governance. Narayanan et al. (2011) discovered that the antecedents of process integration such as task complexity, task security, end customer orientation of the client, and IT capability of the BPO can influence BPO success. In addition, some studies suggest that vendors’ resources and capabilities jointly influence BPO outcomes (Lahiri and Kedia, 2009; Lahiri et al., 2012).

## 5.2.9 Psychological and Formal Contracts

Contract is the formal mechanism that legally connects clients and vendors in the ITO relationship. ITO scholars not only studied issues related to the formal contract, but also found that psychological contract is another effective way of engaging vendors. Papers in this research stream address both psychological and formal contracts.

When examining the formal ITO contract, economics theories are mostly applied, including incomplete contract theory (Gefen et al., 2008; Susarla et al., 2010; Susarla, 2012), transaction cost economics, and agency theory (Chen and Bharadwaj, 2009). Social theories such as relational exchange theory are sometimes employed (Chen and Bharadwaj, 2009). Some papers studied antecedents of clients’ contract choice and found that business familiarity, prior experience, and process interdependence influence how the client select formal contract (Chen and Bharadwaj, 2009; Gefen et al., 2008). Efforts have also been made to understand contract extension and termination. Susarla et al. (2010) found that non price contractual provisions, contract duration, and extendibility terms give firms an option to extend the contract and thus to limit the likelihood of holdup. The deficiency of the long-term contracts in ITO such as lack of sufficient flexibility and completeness may lead to several contract cancellations midway and various ways of contract improvement by renegotiation ex post are explored (Susarla, 2012). In addition, ITO performance measure is examined in the context of formal contract. Fitoussi and Gurbaxani (2012) found that it is challenging for firms to achieve multiple objectives through formal contractual governance because the objectives are often interrelated and the increased number of performance metrics specified in the contract often lead to unsatisfactory outcomes. It is interesting to note that many papers in ITO contract governance focus on offshoring software development project (OSDP). This may be because OSDP is a leading business sector in the global IT marketplace (Mathrani et al., 2012) and OSDP often brings about more risks than simple IT service outsourcing (Verner and Abdullah, 2012).

ITO partnerships involve both formal contracts and informal mechanisms such as psychological contract to manage relationships. The psychological contract perspective has been applied to examine various IT sourcing varietals, providing an important theoretical lens for ITO researchers (Agerfalk and Fitzgerald, 2008). Koh et al. (2004) showed that psychological contract can enhance perceived mutual obligations of ITO clients and suppliers and better predict ITO success than formal contract characteristics. Miranda and Kavan (2005) explained that ITO has two governance options or moments, the formal contract governance and the post-contractual psychological contract, and choices of formal contract and psychological contract can interact with each other to influence ITO outcomes. This is consistent with what Srivastava and Teo (2012) found that formal and informal controls function as both substitutes and complements in affecting ITO success.

## 5.2.10 Opensourcing

Opensourcing is a way in which commercial companies collaborate with open source communities to develop products of commercial interest to the company, providing ample opportunities for companies to recruit excellent developers world-wide (Agerfalk and Fitzgerald, 2008). Research has investigated motivations, benefits, and challenges of opensourcing (Agerfalk and Fitzgerald, 2008; Liu et al., 2010). Motivations of opensourcing include cost saving, reduced software development cycle, modularized and distributed development work, access to advanced technology, innovation, and so forth (Agerfalk and Fitzgerald, 2008). At the same time, opensourcing faces many challenges especially in the areas of in-house staffing and project sustainability (Liu et al., 2010). Researchers also examined outcomes of opensourcing. Agerfalk and Fitzgerald (2008) suggested that companies and open-source communities need to build an overall opensourcing ecosystem based on a trusted partnership of shared responsibility to achieve success. Daniel et al. (2013) examined the role of diversity in open source project success and found that three types of diversity - separation, variety, and disparity have significant influence on the projects’ community engagement and market success.

Crowdsourcing is a new way of outsourcing that is similar to opensourcing. Only one paper on crowdsourcing was found in our multiple main paths (Poetz and Schreier, 2012). This paper compared the ideas that generated by a firm's professionals with ideas that proposed by potential users of a firm’s new product design and found that user ideas scored higher in novelty and customer benefit, and lower in feasibility, suggesting that ideas generated via crowdsourcing should be complemented with professional opinions in new product design.

## 5.2.11 Offshore outsourcing

The common theme of this stream is offshore outsourcing. The papers address a variety of issues surrounding offshore outsourcing in light of specific cultures, norms, laws, and languages involved in the offshore context (Nakatsu and Iacovou, 2009). Specifically, we identified five perspectives from which offshoring were studied: costs, cultural distance, innovation, knowledge transfer and boundary spanning.

(1) Cost. Different types of costs are associated with offshore outsourcing projects, such as general transaction costs, extra costs and hidden costs (Sayeed, 2008; Dibbern et al., 2008; Larsen et al., 2013). Transactions costs include pre-contractual costs and contract-based costs. Company size and the maturity level of the firm's offshore sourcing activities can affect the firms’ transaction costs mitigation tactics (Sayeed, 2008). Extra costs include all costs spent by the client beyond contract-based costs which often offset the cost advantages of offshoring such as low labor costs of the vendor. The extra costs emanated from requirements specification and design, knowledge transfer, scale of services, control, and coordination can be reduced by the extent of client-specific knowledge, absorptive capacity of the vendor and increased by the employee turnover rate and geographic and cultural distance between the client and the vendor (Dibbern et al., 2008; Handley and Benton, 2013). Hidden costs refer to the unanticipated costs of implementation that arise in strategic decision-making processes (Handley and Benton, 2013). Clients often fail to accurately estimate the costs in offshoring projects because they omit the existence of hidden costs. Larsen et al. (2013) attributed this error to increased configuration and task complexity in offshoring.

(2) Cultural distance. Compared with domestic outsourcing, cultural distance (or cultural difference) is

#

a more salient issue in offshoring. Decreased cultural distance has been found to provide many benefits to firms in offshoring (Hahn and Bunyaratavej, 2010). Existing studies have attempted to clarify what factors have effects on cultural distance (Winkler et al., 2008; Ang and Inkpen, 2008; Hahn and Bunyaratavej, 2010), how they impact offshoring success (Winkler et al., 2008; Gregory et al., 2009; Hahn and Bunyaratavej, 2010; Suri and Abbott, 2013; Du et al., 2011) and how to manage cultural differences to gain offshoring success (Winkler et al., 2008). Winkler et al. (2008) found that differences in power distance, IS designer values and communication styles can affect relationship quality and offshoring success and these cultural distances can be reduced by a clear definition of roles and mechanisms, strong leadership and culture management. Hahn and Bunyaratavej (2010) showed that host countries with low uncertainty avoidance, high individualism, and high power distance are the ideal location for service offshoring. Mao et al. (2008) found that cultural blending can increase clients’ control over vendors. Beck and Schott (2012) found that the interaction between formal and informal control mechanisms and interorganizational learning help to mitigate cultural differences in offshoring. In addition, Gregory et al. (2009) found that cultural intelligence can improve trust-based interpersonal relationships, shared understanding, and the effective resolution of conflicts in offshoring projects.

(3) Innovation. Offshoring can potentially improve a company’s innovativeness by altering its knowledge base. It is suggested that offshoring can enhance firms’ innovation ability in two aspects: to minimize the risk associated with innovation and to increase the probability accessing new and diverse ideas from different markets and cultural perspectives (Musteen and Ahsan, 2013). However, the relationship between offshoring and innovativeness is not simplistically linear. Mihalache et al. (2012) found that the relationship between the extent of offshoring primary functions and innovativeness is an inverted U-shaped curve: when firms offshore from low to intermediate levels, they can make use of the location-specific advantages and competencies of foreign countries to gain innovativeness, and when the extent of offshoring goes beyond a threshold, the benefits will diminish and even play a negative role on

#

innovativeness. Furthermore, researchers studied moderating factors that can influence the firms innovation ability in offshoring. Nieto and Rodriguez (2011) indicated that R&D offshoring has different impacts on innovation performance depending on the innovation types (product or process innovations) and governance models (captive offshoring or offshore outsourcing) considered. Mihalache et al. (2012) found informational diversity and shared vision of top management teams moderate the relationship between offshoring and innovativeness.

(4) Knowledge transfer. Knowledge transfer is an important antecedent of offshoring success (Williams, 2011; Deng and Mao, 2012). In offshoring, the knowledge transferred from clients to vendors involves both codified declarative knowledge and complex tacit knowledge (Williams, 2011). Given communication barriers, cultural and institutional distances, lack of equivalence in individual competence, and lack of common rules, effective knowledge transfer is difficult to achieve (Huong et al., 2011). Research shows that procedural coordination strategies (Mirani, 2007), formal training and embedment within the client (Williams, 2011) and bridge system engineers (Huong et al., 2011) help to improve mutual understanding between clients and vendors. The knowledge flowing between the client and vendor is a bidirectional process. Cha et al. (2008) argued that long-term projects may disrupt the knowledge supply chain of client, and the client needs to exploit learning-by-doing production knowledge from the vendor's large, scale-driven repository. Westner and Strahringer (2010) found that knowledge transfer, along with project suitability and liaison quality, is a significant determinant of offshoring success.

(5) Boundary spanning. Boundary spanning is an important issue in ITO, especially in the offshoring context because diverse organizational and national contexts often disturb the collaboration between onshore and offshore participants (Abbott et al., 2013; Levina and Vaast, 2008; Du and Pan, 2013). Levina and Vaast (2008) studied how country and organizational factors impact boundaries and associated status differences and found that based on their position and resources, some middle managers were able to alleviate status differences and help others renegotiate boundaries. Du and Pan (2013) tried to understand

#

how vendors span boundaries with clients rather than how clients span boundaries with vendors by examining two key boundary-spanning elements: boundary spanners and boundary-spanning strategies, and found that vendor boundary spanning happens by design, with rational deliberation and planning, rather than by emergence. For example, the Bridge System Engineer can use their IT background and communication skills to promote effective communication and therefore to facilitate knowledge transfer and improve the business relationship between clients and vendors (Huong et al., 2011). In addition, Luo et al. (2012) showed that multinational firms’ projects to integrate internally distributed processes should be aligned with task traits and task interdependence. Luo et al. (2013) also found that business process offshoring is determined by both task traits and the needed process integration when firms try to expand boundaries of their processes internationally.

## 5.2.12 Multisourcing

Multisourcing is a strategy firms take to expand their supply network with more than one service provider around the world. The similar strategy has been extensively studied in manufacturing field, but rarely in the IT offshoring field. Integrating related research from manufacturing into IT service outsourcing, Su and Levina (2011) found that breadth and depth of a firm’s multisourcing have different impact on outsourcing outcomes such as cost, risk, flexibility, innovation, service quality, and delivery speed. Researchers paid close attention to multisourcing decision problems, especially how many suppliers should be selected. Although prior ITO and supply chain management research strongly advised focusing on a small number of strategic partners, Levina and Vaast (2008) argued that this strategy may lead to fewer opportunities to discover new suppliers or supply regions and suggested that the client needs to engage in a more intensive multisourcing in services. In a case study, Su and Levina (2011) found that global financial services firms adopted a multisourcing pendulum strategy by experimenting with different configurations to explore the optimal supply base for a given function. When internal or external business environments changed, the firm swings between single sourcing and extreme multisourcing strategies. Researchers also

#

applied quantitative methods such as the NP-hard multi-objective model and bi-objective 0-1 linear programming model to solve supplier selection problem in multisourcing (Feng et al., 2011; Feng, 2012).

## 5.2.13 ITO success

Almost every paper in ITO aims to answer questions or solve problems with the ultimate goal of achieving success in ITO. Thus ITO success is the most important dependent variable in ITO research. We examine papers that directly address ITO success and summarize the conceptualization of ITO success from the papers that has explicitly defined ITO success. As expected, the measurement of ITO success evolves with the development of the ITO. At the beginning stage of ITO, companies mainly look for economic and technological benefits from ITO, so ITO success is measured by expected cost savings (Lacity and Willcocks, 1998) or economic and technological terms (Lacity et al., 1996). Later, when companies realized that they can achieve strategic advantages from ITO, strategic items were incorporated into ITO success measurements (DiRomualdo and Gurbaxani, 1998; Grover et al., 1996; Lee, 2001). After ITO relationship was recognized a key dimension of ITO success, it was also incorporated to measure ITO success (Saunders et al., 1997; Koh et al., 2004). For example, Saunders et al. (1997) measured ITO success from the contract, IS role and ITO relationship perspective with four dimensions: economic, technological, strategic and overall satisfaction with contract.

As ITO practices became further mature and more sophisticated, more measurements were added into ITO success to address broader strategic concerns (Goo et al., 2008). For example, ITO success can be conceptualized as having four dimensions: economic, technological, strategic and the service quality (Lee and Kim, 1999; Lee and Kim, 2005). When studying the interaction between service-level agreements and commitment, ITO success can be measured as having functional, strategic, and technological dimensions (Goo et al., 2008). To date, most ITO researchers agree that the ITO success construct should include strategic, economic and technological dimensions (Lee et al., 2008; Goo et al., 2008; Lee, 2011). Research in offshore IS project continued the traditional ITO success dimensions, however, added benefits from project level. For example, Westner and Strahringer (2010) interpreted ITO success as perceived satisfaction with the total outcome of offshore IS project, including schedule, budget, functionality, and quality dimensions. Offshore IS project success in Rai et al. (2009) incorporated two indicators: project cost overruns and client satisfaction with the final product. Given that Lacity et al. (2009) has reviewed ITO outcomes in detail, we will not spend much space on this topic. The point we intend to make is that ITO success is a construct whose definition and operationalization evolves with time (Schwarz, 2014)

## 5.2.14 Comparison with Other Reviews

In this section, we compare our findings with prior ITO literature reviews to highlight our contributions. We examined nine previous ITO literature reviews: Dibbern et al. (2004), Gonzalez et al. (2006a), Gonzalez et al. (2006b), Lacity et al. (2009), Chadee and Raman (2009), Lacity et al., (2010), Lacity et al. (2011b), Gantman (2011), and Gonzalez et al. (2013). Some of these reviews have great differences in research focus with the current review. For example, Chadee and Raman (2009) focus on empirical studies on international outsourcing of IT Services (ITS) and Lacity et al. (2011b) review studies in the BPO field. Since the purpose of this paper is to map the field of ITO research, especially to identify main research streams in ITO, we select four reviews that have comparable components: Dibbern et al. (2004), Gonzalez et al. (2006a), Lacity et al. (2009) and Lacity et al. (2010). Table 3 summarizes these four reviews.

The comparative analysis shows that our review has both findings consistent with the previous reviews and findings that are unique. First of all, our review shares many similar findings with the previous reviews. As Table 4 shows, ITO motivations, ITO decisions, and client-vendor relationships have been discussed in detail by all of the four reviews. ITO risks have been recognized as a topic important for ITO practice by Lacity et al. (2009) and an influencing factor for ITO decisions (Lacity et al., 2010). The vendor’s perspective has been noticed by Gonzalez et al. (2006a) and its importance is also reflected in Lacity et al.’s (2009, 2010) discussion about how vendor capabilities affect ITO outcomes. ASP, BPO, and offshoring have been identified as important sourcing varietals by Lacity et al. (2009) that will attract more future research. Finally, the psychological and formal contracts stream partially overlaps with Lacity et al. (2010) who mentioned that contractual governance has strong influence on ITO outcomes. These commonalities suggest that the main path analysis is able to generate findings consistent with the previous reviews, thus confirming the validity of our review.

Second, the main path analysis we employed provides an objective basis to review a large amount of ITO papers more comprehensively. We not only identified research streams consistent with previous reviews, but also found research streams overlooked previously and streams that are just emerging. For example, the debate around transaction cost theory has not been noticed by previous reviews, although the applicability of TCT has been questioned and the development of new theories indigenous to ITO research has been called for (Lacity et al., 2010; Lacity et al., 2011b). In the stream of psychological and formal contracts, we found that the psychological contract perspective played an important role in understanding ITO relationships. However, previous reviews only emphasized contractual governance and did not give enough credits to this perspective. In addition to ASP, BPO, and offshoring, our review discovered that studies started to cumulate around other sourcing varietals including opensourcing and multisourcing. Due to their novelty and small number of papers, these topics were not identified by previous reviews.

Third, even in our findings that are consistent with previous reviews, we are able to complement previous reviews by providing additional insights. For example, from Figure 5 we can see that ITO decision problem used to be a very hot topic. Research on ITO decisions has matured after 20 years’ evolution. Our analysis shows that two decision issues are still worth noting. The first is re-outsourcing decisions. A survey found that among 160 ITO projects, only 70 projects continued and 90 projects terminated the current contracts either by switching vendors or turning to backsourcing (Whitten and Leidner, 2006). Therefore, re-outsourcing decisions problem have great practical and academic significance. Future research can also study the re-outsourcing success problem, which is how companies draw lessons from the failure experience and achieve success in the new ITO contracts. Also, the contract renegotiation design problem in this process is another research direction. The second issue is how to make decision in new sourcing varietals, such as mlutisourcing, opensourcing or crowdsourcing, which are fairly new to both practitioners and researchers. For example, the modularity of business processes in BPO decisions (Tanriverdi et al., 2007), site selection in offshoring decisions (Hahn and Bunyaratavej, 2010), and selection and combination of various service vendors in multisourcing (Levina and Vaast, 2008; Feng, 2012) are emerging research questions for ITO researchers. These two issues have not received much attention from previous reviews.

## 6 Trends and Future Directions of ITO Research

Baskerville and Myers (2009) find that IS research and practice is characterized by fashions. Our study suggests that fashion waves also emerge and dissipate in the ITO field. Each of the major research themes we identified can be seen as a fashion – it emerges at a specific point in time and will fade away as knowledge in an area becomes relatively complete. For example, the traditional ITO motivation problem is seemingly fading away because researchers’ focus was switched into post-decision phases. Although the motivation problem will be revisited when new sourcing varietals emerge, the amount of such research has significantly decreased as the stream is saturated with consensus. In contrast, research interest on offshoring is booming in recent years. To illustrate the evolution of research themes over time, we analyzed the number of articles published in each stream over time. While not many articles are focused on these new sourcing types, the fact that they appear on multiple main paths suggests that they have caught ITO researchers’ attention.

Based on the main path analysis results, our reading of the articles in the identified major themes, and our understanding of the ITO research field, we predict that the following six areas will likely attract more attention from ITO researchers.

(1) New forms of ITO decisions. Although research on ITO decisions has matured after 20 years’ evolution, our analysis shows its resurgence. Future research is likely to focus on two new forms of ITO decision issues. The first is re-outsourcing decisions. A survey found that among 160 ITO projects, only 70 projects continued and 90 projects terminated the current contracts either by switching vendors or turning to backsourcing (Whitten and Leidner, 2006). Therefore, re-outsourcing decisions problem have great practical and academic significance. Future research can also study the re-outsourcing success problem

which is how companies draw lessons from the failure experience and achieve success in the new ITO contracts. Also, the contract renegotiation design problem in this process is another research direction. The second issue is how to make decision in new sourcing varietals, such as multisourcing, opensourcing or crowdsourcing, which are very new to both practitioners and researchers. For example, the modularity of business processes in BPO decisions (Tanriverdi et al., 2007), site selection in offshoring decisions (Hahn and Bunyaratavej, 2010), and selection and combination of various service vendors in multisourcing (Levina and Vaast, 2008; Feng, 2012) are emerging research questions for ITO researchers. These two issues have not received enough attention from previous reviews.

(2) ITO relationships from new angles. Client-vendor relationships have been extensively studied by ITO researchers and substantial knowledge has been accumulated in this area (Lacity et al. 2010). Based on our review, we predict that relational governance will continue to attract academic attention, particularly for outsourcing contracts involving vendors from emerging economies and thus cultural clashes. As ITO relationships become increasingly complex, it is difficult for formal contracts alone to achieve ITO success (Goo et al. 2009). The psychological contract perspective will provide a useful angle to examine how to effectively manage complex client-vendor relationships. While most prior research addresses the relationship between a single client and a single vendor, with multi-sourcing a client firm could be dealing with several vendors. This one-to-many relationship poses new challenges to both the client and vendors and present interesting problems that deserve research. For instance, how do a firm’s existing vendors influence its selection of other vendors? How should a firm manage multiple vendors so that they can efficiently cooperate with each other? From the vendors’ perspective, a vendor not only collaborates with, but also competes against other vendors when they work with the same client. It is important to find a balance between collaboration and competition to achieve success for the overall outsourcing arrangement. These relationships, particularly the vendor-vendor relationship, have rarely been studied before and represent a fruitful path for future ITO research.

#

(3) ITO risks. A number of risks and risk-mitigating strategies for general and specific type of outsourcing have been studied in the past (Lacity et al. 2009). As new sourcing varietals emerge and new practices in arranging sourcing activities diffuse, firms will face unprecedented risks that have to be addressed. For example, as more firms adopt offshoring, the risks originated from differences in cultural, regulatory, institutional environments will become particularly prominent. As firms work with cloud computing service providers, data security, privacy, and interoperability issues will surface. These risks arising from ITO practices will serve as a strong impetus for academic researchers to understand how the risks affect ITO outcomes and seek effective measures to mitigate the risks. Therefore, although much attention has already been paid to ITO risks (e.g., Gonzalez et al. 2010a; Hahn and Bunyaratavej 2010; Larsen et al. 2013), we predict that ITO risks will remain a popular topic in the future.

(4) Development of ITO theories. Our review shows that there has always been a debate regarding whether and how appropriate TCT is for explaining ITO phenomena. This debate is more of an academic matter and has less practical relevance. We agree with Lacity et al. (2010, 2011b) that indigenous ITO theories are necessary to generate in-depth understanding of ITO issues and advance the ITO field toward maturation. However, we are conservative on the argument that ITO issues are too complicated to be sufficiently explained by TCT and other theories. As Alaghehband et al. (2011) contend, there is no indisputable evidence to show that the mixed findings from ITO studies based on TCT are due to the innate weakness of TCT; rather, they are most likely caused by misuse of TCT. We predict that the debate over TCT and the quest to develop indigenous ITO theories will continue. Attempts to develop ITO theories will be made; however, the process will be slow because theory development takes time and only a small number of scholars will be devoted to this challenging task.

(5) Offshoring. As Figure 7 shows, offshoring is clearly the most popular ITO research stream. Given its short history, many research questions related to offshoring remain open and warrant continuous investigations. Based on our review, we argue that the most unique characteristic of offshoring is the

#

distance between the client environment and the vendor environment including cultures, norms, regulations, and standards. These distances dynamically interact with firms’ actions to influence offshoring decisions and outcomes and more research is needed to unravel the complex underlying mechanisms. Other researchers also called for more research in applying theory-driven approaches to understanding offshoring contexts and pay particular attention to the pre-implementation stage of offshoring (Wiener et al. 2010). In addition, beyond its traditional objective on cost saving, offshoring has been treated as a strategic choice to enhance innovation by leveraging vendors’ capabilities (Mihalache et al. 2012; Musteen and Ahsan 2013; Nieto and Rodriguez 2011). However, limited efforts have been spent on examining how to effectively achieve innovation through offshoring. Research in this specific area will make a significant contribution to the ITO literature.

(6) Emerging ITO varietals. Our review has identified a limited number of publications on new ITO varietals such as multisourcing, crowdsourcing, and opensourcing. Each new ITO varietal will present unique benefits and risks, and motivate researchers to question whether the knowledge drawn from previous ITO varietals can be readily applied to explain issues related to the new varietal. Studies will be conducted by taking the unique characteristics of the new sourcing varietal into account to answer the fundamental questions such as why does a firm adopt this varietal and what factors influence or impede the success of this varietal. According to Baskerville and Myers’ (2009) observation, a new popular ITO practice will lead to a fashion wave in ITO research. For example, the recent development of cloud computing is likely to trigger a wave of studies on issues related to outsourcing to cloud providers.

## 7 Discussion and Conclusion

The ITO industry has evolved rapidly since the Eastman Kodak’s outsourcing practice. A rich and diverse body of ITO research has accumulated during the past 22 years. However, the continuing expansion of the ITO field makes it enormously difficult for researchers to complete a global assessment of the existing literature. In this paper, we trace the ITO literature development process with the assistance of

#

main path analysis. The method is quantitative and citation based. It is used to find a series of important articles that map the main knowledge flows and subareas of ITO studies. The global main path is the most important knowledge diffusion route over time and the multiple main paths are a collection of main paths with smaller overall SPC values. The global main path and multiple main paths provide complementary perspectives to depict the ITO research field.

This paper makes several contributions to the ITO literature. First, it is one of the first studies that rely on citation analysis techniques to objectively map the field of ITO research. Compared with traditional reviews completely based on researchers’ subjective interpretations, our review integrates an objective methodology and the findings can reflect the actual state of the ITO field by precisely assessing the citation relationships among articles. This type of review can reduce potential biases due to human subjectivity and provide a useful complementary perspective to our knowledge extracted from previous reviews. Second, the global main path concisely describes the major knowledge flow in the evolution of ITO research, and the multiple main paths identify twelve major ITO research streams. These results help researchers gain a clear and quick understanding of the rich body of ITO research. Third, our research can be used to predict trends of ITO research in the near future. Although prior studies have recommended a number of potential topics for future research, based on our findings we suggest six future ITO research directions: new forms of ITO decisions, ITO relationships from new angles, ITO risks, development of ITO theories, offshoring, and emerging ITO varietals. Finally, the main path analysis method used in our study can be an instrumental tool to assist IS researchers in general. Besides the ITO field, it can be used to conduct comprehensive review of other important fields to identify major streams within the fields. In addition, based on multiple main paths, articles belonging to a specific stream can be easily identified. Such categorization is based on citation patterns which are expected to produce more accurate results than keyword search and be more efficient than reading the articles.

This study has a few limitations. First, two factors could influence the accuracy of our main path

#

analysis. One is the citation motivation of researchers. Some researchers may cite papers remotely related to their work simply to increase the size of citations or make their research appear grounded in prior seminal works (Liu et al., 2013). This kind of citation motivation makes the main path analysis vulnerable because this method assumes that the citation relationship between two papers indicates the knowledge flow from a cited work to the citing document (Liu and Lu, 2012). The direct consequence of this problem is the difficulty to explain the juncture of two articles that have low degree relevancy in main path. The application of the SPC value which indicates the importance of each citation relationship can avoid this situation to a certain extent. In addition, we conducted pruning by manually removing the link between two unrelated papers after comparing their content. Another factor is the reference missing between two articles that have high degree relevancy in research themes or methods. This may be due to the limitation of researchers’ knowledge stock when they were carrying out their research. Thus the main paths lacked the continuity and coherence of research themes or methods. However, the tactics of considering the multiple main paths which take more useful main paths and more influential works of ITO into consideration enable us to classify these works into different subareas according to their research themes. Therefore, some papers can still belong to the same subarea despite no direct citation relationship. Second, we assume that the ITO articles we retrieved from WOS can reasonably represent the ITO field. However, the retrieval conditions and data sources cannot guarantee the coverage of all possible ITO publications. We made great efforts to find ITO papers not indexed in the WOS database and integrated them into our dataset. As a result, the missing papers are likely to account for only a small percentage of the ITO literature. Third, when analyzing the 280 representative papers, we focused on their citation links, but did not look into specific theories applied in those studies and their detailed findings. A future study can be conducted to summarize the theories and findings to examine whether new patterns can emerge beyond what we have found in this review. Finally, we focused on journal publications and did not include the major IS conferences such as International Conference on Information Systems, American Conference on

Information Systems, and European Conference on Information Systems (except Hawaii International Conference on Information Systems, which is included in the WOS database). This is because when we collected data from WOS, these conferences were not indexed by WOS. We do not expect this omission to have a significant impact on the main path analysis because most high quality conference papers have been eventually published on journals.

In future research, the main path analysis could be improved. The current methodology treats all citations as equal but ignores the relevancy of articles. One ways to improve this method is to integrate text-mining techniques with main path analysis that was proposed by Liu and Lu (2012). Another method is the integration of document co-citation analysis (DCA) and main path analysis. The DCA can scale the relevancies between any citing-cited pair of documents (Ramos<sub>‐</sub>Rodríguez and Ruíz<sub>‐</sub>Navarro, 2004).

Developing algorithms to integrate DCA and main path analysis would be a significant improvement to the methodology.

In summary, this paper employs the main path analysis method to review the field of ITO research. The global main path based on 798 papers and 6.45 million citation paths reveals two major themes in ITO research – ITO decisions and ITO management. Further multiple main paths analysis based on 280 papers 120,000 citation paths shows that 12 ITO research streams can be identified – ITO motivations, ITO decisions, ITO risks, debate around transaction cost theory, client-vendor relationship, the vendor’s perspective, psychological and formal contracts, ASP, BPO, opensourcing and crowdsourcing, offshore outsourcing, and multisourcing. These findings not only confirm previous reviews, but also provide new information that helps to better understand the current state and future trends of ITO research.

Appendix A. Example of pruning multiple main paths

We use an example to illustrate how to prune multiple main paths. In the citation network, an article is typically cited by a number of other articles. For example, Lee et al. (2008) is cited by eight articles, as shown in Figure A1. The eight citation paths could be either substantial or peripheral, and the purpose of pruning is to remove peripheral paths so that the connected articles share semantic similarities. We examined how Lee et al. (2008) was cited by each of the either articles by locating the specific sections where Lee et al. (2008) was cited and reading the full texts to understand the rationale behind the citation. If Lee et al. (2008) was cited to support the research question, theoretical foundation, or hypothesis development, the path was labeled as substantial. In contrast, if Lee et al. (2008) was cited to provide background information or support a specific logical argument or because its scale for a construct or its data analysis method was adopted, we labeled the path as peripheral. As Table A1 shows, after careful examination, we found four citation paths to be peripheral and the other four to be substantial. Specifically, Westner and Strahringer (2010), Moon et al. (2010), Lee and Choi (2011), and Qi and Chau (2013) draw heavily from the substantial knowledge of Lee et al. (2008) to develop their own studies, while the other four papers cited Lee et al. (2008) only for peripheral purposes such as providing background information (Gonzalez et al. 2010a; Han and Lee 2012)), supporting an argument (Jain et al. 2011), and justifying the appropriateness of a theory (Qi and Chau 2012). After removing the four peripheral paths, the citation network surrounding Lee et al. (2008) was greatly simplified (see Figure A2).
