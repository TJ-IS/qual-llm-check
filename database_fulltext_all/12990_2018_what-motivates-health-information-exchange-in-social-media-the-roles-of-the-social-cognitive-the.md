## Abstract

Social media has become an attractive platform for users to exchange health information with others. However, little research has been done to identify the determinants of health information exchange. By integrating aspects of the social cognitive theory and perceived interactivity, this study proposes a research model to investigate the antecedents of health information exchange in social media. Data collected from Facebook users with health information exchange experience were used to examine the proposed model. The results demonstrate that human-to-human interaction, human-to-information interaction, outcome expectation of health self-management competence, and outcome expectation of social relationships have a significant impact on health information exchange behavior. The results also reveal that human-to-human interaction exerts a significant influence on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and the outcome expectation of social relationships, while human-to-information interaction has a positive effect on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence. The implications for the theory and practice and future research directions are discussed.

Keywords: Health information exchange; Perceived interactivity; Social cognitive theory;

Outcome expectations; Social media

## 1. Introduction

People require information about illnesses and associated treatments to help them cope with illness [10] and make well-informed decisions [10,64]. From a healthcare education perspective, health information such as personal health awareness, treatment options, and personal success experiences are necessary for successful healthcare management [23,37]. In the past, people tended to turn to friends and family for information and advice on how to cope with illness [37]. However, the growing popularity of social media, the blogs, social networking sites, and other media for social interactions supported by Web 2.0 technologies [4] has changed the way people manage their healthcare and chronic conditions [37]. To date, people can search for health information and discuss their experience about medical treatments through social media [4,72]. People can also build sophisticated virtual communities using social media to exchange health information [4].

Although social media makes it possible to exchange personal health information with others, questions still remain about what factors can facilitate users of social media to exchange private health information with others [72]. Strictly speaking, online users have serious concerns about how their personal health information is used [7]. This indicates that health information privacy concern and personal sense of information sensitivity are the key factors preventing online users from engaging in health information exchange [7]. In addition, from a socio-economic perspective, the rationale action for users of social media is to access information regardless of its provision [10,66]. As such, it is important to investigate what may motivate users to exchange personal health information with others in social media.

Since previous studies, researchers have begun to identify the motivations for sharing health information in social media [70,72]. While health information sharing is an important activity in social media, seeking and use of health information could be another important activity in social media [31]. Prior literature argues that the use of an information system (e.g., social media) requires that users are willing to exchange their resources with others (e.g., sharing information and seeking for information) [61]. The social exchange theory viewpoint also asserts that people sharing their information may perceive it to be fair when others do the same thing, and such fairness perception will lead to greater social interactions (e.g., information exchange) in the future [76]. Hence, examining the factors affecting health information exchange could advance our understanding on how to sustain information sharing and information-seeking behaviors in social media and thereby maintain the success of social media.

In addition, most prior studies on health information sharing mainly examine the effect of perceived benefits on personal behavior from various perspectives [70, 72]. Social media, as a social computing tool, provide an attractive platform for people to seek and share information through the Internet without face-to-face interaction [71]. The environment for interactions in social media could be a critical factor that may have an impact on information exchange behavior. Yet relatively little attention has been paid to investigate the relationships between the social media environment, perceived benefits, and an individual’s behavior simultaneously.

Motivated by the aforementioned knowledge gaps, this study attempts to investigate the antecedent factors of health information exchange by integrating the perspectives of social cognitive theory (SCT) and perceived interactivity. SCT is a well-established theory that has been used in information systems studies [12,46,47,76]. SCT asserts that one’s behavior is shaped and controlled by personal cognition and environmental elements [5,14]. Following the literature of SCT [12], the outcome expectations, the judgment of the likely consequence of performing the task [6], are considered as an individual’s personal cognition to investigate the effect of perceived benefits on information exchange behavior. In particular, this study divides outcome expectations into two different constructs—the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and the outcome expectation of social relationships.

In addition, given that social media is a social computing technology [74], the interactivity features of social media may determine the extent in which a user can control a medium in terms of modifying its form and content in real time [59,73] which will have an impact on the communication quality [48]. Researchers also agree that perceived interactivity could be considered as users’ perception of the social media environment [73]. Perceived interactivity is therefore considered as the environmental factor that may have an impact on outcome expectations and health information exchange. In the study, perceived interactivity is classified into two different types: human-to-human and human-to-information interactions [32].

In summary, the purpose of this study is to identify the antecedents of health information exchange by integrating the perspectives of SCT and perceived interactivity. Accordingly, this study aims to address the following three questions:

(1) Do the interactive features of social media affect outcome expectations?

(2) Do the outcome expectations affect health information exchange?

(3) Do the interactive features of social media impact health information exchange?

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1 Related literature

The health information exchange in this study refers to the information about illness and associated treatments that are sent/received to/from others through social media [21]. Obviously, the exchange of health information usually includes two different actions, namely, information sharing and information seeking. Many researchers agree that perceived benefits are the factors that motivate users to participate in social media to seek and share health information. For example, Johnston et al. [37] consider that information utility and social support are the two major benefits derived from online health community participation. From

#

the standpoint of the social exchange theory, Yan et al. [72] report that perceived self-worth, face concern, reputation, and social support are the benefits that may enable users to share health information in an online health community. Wicks et al. [70], on the other hand, find that perceived benefits such as gaining a better understanding of their seizures, learning more about a symptom or treatment, and feeling greater control over their condition will motivate users to participate in an online health community. Moreover, researchers also postulate that demographic characteristics (e.g., gender, age, income, and education) have a strong association with the use of social media for sharing health information [15, 62].

While prior literature has substantially enhanced our understanding of how intrinsic and extrinsic factors have an impact on user behavior and the attitudes associated with the use of social media for health information sharing and seeking, little investigation has been done to identify the antecedent factors affecting an individual’s health information exchange behavior. From the viewpoint of the social exchange theory [8], a reciprocal relationship is a vital factor facilitating online interactions [74]. In general, perceived balance between information sharing and acquisition will motivate users to engage in social interactions in social media [74]. Many researchers agree that social interactions in social media will enhance information exchange among users [61]. Thus, understanding the predictors of health information exchange may provide some valuable insights about how to facilitate user participation in social media and information exchange.

#

Furthermore, the aforementioned studies have provided empirical evidence to support the influence of perceived benefits on health information sharing [72]. However, environmental factors such as the interactive features of social media may be critical factors that determine users’ intentions to use social media to exchange health information with others. Little attention has been devoted to integrating the interactive features of social media and personal benefits to examine the antecedents of health information exchange. To fill this knowledge gap, we use social cognitive theory (SCT) [6] as our theoretical base to develop our research model.

## 2.3 Social cognitive theory and health information exchange

Developed by Bandura [6], SCT emphasizes that human behavior is shaped and controlled by personal cognition in a social environment. Bandura [6] further proposes two types of expectation beliefs such as outcome expectations and self-efficacy and suggests that they are the two major cognitive forces that will guide an individual’s behavior. Bandura [6] also considers that an individual will not perform a specific behavior if he/she has no confidence in his/her ability to perform such behavior, especially in the context where an individual’s behavior is voluntary. Therefore, following Chiu et al. [12], self-efficacy is not considered in this study.

Outcome expectations refer to the expected consequence of one’s own behavior [6]. In SCT, the outcome expectations generally consist of three major forms such as physical effects

#

(e.g., pleasure and discomfort), social effects (e.g., social recognition and applause), and self-evaluation effects (e.g., self-satisfaction) [6]. By extending the aspect of outcome expectations, prior literature argues that outcome expectations could be divided into various types according to the characteristics of different settings. For example, in the study of outcome expectations from the viewpoint of self-interest: performance-related outcome expectations (e.g., the improvements in job performance) and personal outcome expectations (e.g., the change in image/status and rewards). In the setting of virtual communities, Chiu et al. [12] and Hsu et al. [33] suggest that an individual’s expected outcomes through knowledge sharing can be divided into two dimensions: personal expectations (e.g., gaining more respect and making more friends) and community-related expectations (the impact of knowledge sharing on communities).

Health information exchange is different from other information exchange behavior in social media because it is closely related to the physical and mental health of members [72]. As a general rule, the major motivation for people to participate in health communities is to enhance their knowledge and social value [72]. Such a viewpoint is in line with the assertion of Wasko and Faraj [67] that members of online communities usually expect to obtain two main outcomes: enhancing self-capability and increasing social acceptance. Therefore, in this study, we recognize that the benefits people expect to receive by using social media could be divided into two different types, including health self-management competence enhancement and social relationship enhancement. Therefore, we suggest that the outcome expectations in this study could be divided into two types, including the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and the outcome expectation of a social relationship.

Health self-management competence refers to one’s confidence about his or her competence to manage personal health [30]. According to previous literature [37, 53], using social media is a useful way for people to increase their health self-management competence. Prior studies argue that the exchange of health information allows individuals to leverage the collective wisdom of others and thus help them to obtain first-hand insight into treatment methods [37, 63]. With enhanced health self-management competence, people can conduct a variety of different health behaviors (e.g., using medication, controlling diet, and managing stress) [44,55] to create a healthy lifestyle [36]. Therefore, in this study, the outcome expectation of self-management competence is defined as an individual’s anticipation that participating in an information exchange in social media may enhance his or her competence for managing personal health.

Conversely, from the viewpoint of users and the gratification theory, social media may enable people to build their personal network and thus satisfy the emotional need for social relationships [4,56]. Kujath [41] observes that social media users usually spend more time searching for people they already know offline and adding those people to their “friends list”

#

compared to time spent seeking people who have similar interests or hobbies but who are unfamiliar. Dietz-Uhler and Bishop-Clark [20] find that users who effectively manage their online relationships generally have good communication and enjoyment in their real-world relationships. Thus, social media may play an important role in strengthening the social friends [2]. In this study, the outcome expectation of social relationships is defined as the extent of an individual’s judgment about the consequence that participating in an information exchange in social media may satisfy their needs for social integration and facilitate their social relationships with others.

Outcome expectations have been considered as the vital predictors of knowledge sharing in social media [12,33]. Therefore, we believe that the expectation of self-management competence and the outcome expectation of social relationships will have an impact on the health information exchange as well. In addition, social media is the artifact with unique technical features that could support users to interact with others [73]. Prior literature has demonstrated the importance of interactive features in social media use. For instance, Wei et al. [68] find that perceived interactivity has a significant impact on attitude, which in turn affects a user’s intention to remain in social media. Zhao and Lu [74] find that perceived interactivity has a positive relation to satisfaction, which in turn influences a continuance intention toward social media. However, prior studies drawing upon SCT have overlooked the role of the interactive features of social media. Given that social media focuses on the participation and interactions of users using information technologies, interactive functions will create a mediated environment that enable users to provide immediate feedback based on the input of others [39,60] Therefore, the aspect of perceived interactivity could be introduced and behavior.

## 2.2 Definition and dimensions of perceived interactivity

Perceived interactivity is recognized as an essential factor in the information exchange process, whether the exchange occurs online or face-to-face [69]. Three perspectives of perceived interactivity have emerged in the literature: perceived interactivity as a characteristic of technology, as a process of information exchange, and as a user perception [32,74]. In this study, the user perception perspective was applied and we define perceived interactivity as the extent to which users perceive their experiences as a simulation of interpersonal interaction and sense that they are in the presence of another user in a social setting [42,74].

Perceived interactivity is a construct with multiple dimensions. Generally, perceived interactivity could be divided into two different dimensions in social media: human-to-human interaction and human-to-information interaction [40,68,74]. Human-to-human interaction refers to the use of social media for sending and receiving information and the reciprocal responsiveness of other users [32]. Human-to-human interaction could reflect the feelings or affective response of an individual when engaging in a social interaction (i.e., connecting with others) as well as the perception of how fast and how frequently members of a social media network post messages with feedback content [74]. In this study, human-to-human connect with others [49].

In contrast, human-to-information interaction refers to the acquisition and sharing of information through social media [24]. According to Marchionini [51] and Zhao and Lu [74], the essence of human-to-information interaction includes the process through which humans interact with information. Human–information interaction may enable users to select, search, and edit the information in social media [68]. High human-to-information interaction also increases effectiveness in searching for objects in information [32]. In this study, human-to-information interaction is defined as the extent to which users believe that they can use social media to interact with content [68].

As mentioned earlier, past literature agrees that human-to-human interaction and human-to-information interaction are the predictors of behavior [32]. Researchers also assert that users of social media will use interactive functions provided by social media to fulfil their needs [32,68], implying that perceived interactivity may have an impact on the expected outcomes derived from social media use. Therefore, this study argues that human-to-human interaction and human-to-information interaction could be the factors affecting outcome expectations and health information exchange in social media.

## 3.1 Human-to-human interaction and outcome expectations

Human-to-human interaction refers to two-way and reciprocal communication between social media users [40]. That is, it is the interpersonal interaction in which users can communicate with others through social media in discussions, message feedback, and dialogue [68]. In general, human-to-human interaction allows an individual to deliver health information to others, while other users can provide the individual with feedback. According to Bolle et al. [10], social media users prefer to discuss their health issues and problems with health education professionals or with specific individuals. Through interpersonal interactions, an individual usually expects that he or she will be able to provide the related expertise and advice that can improve their knowledge and literacy in health self-management [58]. Thus, we may propose that human-to-human interaction could facilitate users’ outcome expectations about their self-management of competence enhancement.

H1a: Human-to-human interaction has a positive association with the outcome expectations of self-management competence.

The development of a social relationship depends on the quality of interpersonal interactions, including their frequency and duration [57]. Generally, social media is a useful mechanism for developing social relationships because it enables users to interact with peers in an online environment. Researchers have found that users in social media will interact with others with high-level subjective knowledge [22,71] to build an online personal network of friends [4]. Studies also provide empirical evidence to show that social media could satisfy the needs of socialization by enabling users to connect with and communicate with peers [49]. Thus, in this study, we may expect that human-to-human interactions will increase one’s expectation that their social relationships among users can be strengthened and maintained. Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H1b: Human-to-human interaction has a positive association with the outcome expectation of social relationships.

## 3.2 Human-to-information interaction and outcome expectations

Conversely, human-to-information interaction refers to interactions between users and information [68]. In other words, users can select, search, edit, and modify the information using social media [40]. Wei et al. [68] postulate that users of social media tend to interact with information to obtain considerable information and knowledge. Ko et al. [40] also argue that interactive features such as human-to-information interactions provide users with better opportunities to process information to meet their intrinsic needs. Similarly, the media synchronicity theory [19] asserts that information processing capabilities of social media, enhance communication performance. Past literature also suggests that using a knowledge management system to access knowledge will increase one’s capability in an organization [1, 31]. By synthesizing the above arguments, we may expect that human-to-information interaction may increase users’ outcome expectation of health self-management competence. Therefore,

H2a: Human-to-information interaction has a positive association with the outcome expectation of health self-management competence.

In addition, human-to-information interaction is expected to increase one’s expectations as to social relationship enhancement. As noted by researchers, human-to-information interaction could enhance information search and immediate feedback in social media [49]. Researchers find that providing feedback to the information shared by others may strengthen social relationships among users [32,75]. Similarly, researchers also argue that sharing information may obtain the recognition of others and responding to other users’ shared information may also reduce one’s feelings of isolation and helps build friendship among social media users [72]. Accordingly, we may propose that interactions with information will increase the density of social relationships among users in social media. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H2b: Human-to-information interaction has a positive association with the outcome expectation of social relationships.

From the viewpoint of the social exchange theory, the principle of an individual’s behavior is that one tends to conduct a specific behavior to maximize benefits and minimize

In the literature of knowledge sharing, knowledge growth has been considered as a kind of positive benefit that will exert a significant influence on knowledge sharing behavior [9]. Studies also show that users who perceive that social media provides useful content tend to engage in reasonably propose that the outcome expectation of health self-management will have a impact on health information exchange.

H3: Outcome expectation of health self-management competence has a positive association with health information exchange.

As noted above, the exchange of information with others is a major motivation for people to use social media [32]. McNeill and colleagues [52] suggest that social relationships affect user perceptions of social norms that guide positive health behavior such as health information exchange activity in virtual communities. Based on the viewpoint of the social capital theory and the social network theory, many studies also agree that social relationships are the important factors that can facilitate information exchange among users [12,61]. He et al. [29] also find that social relationships play an important role in information exchange. Thus,

H4: Outcome expectation of social relationships has a positive association with health

information exchange.

## 3.4 Perceived interactivity and health information exchange

Researchers argue that two-way communication is an essential component of human-to-human interaction [48]. Generally, users will engage in reciprocal communications when a communication technology provides effective two-way communication [48]. Previous literature also argues that users tend to use social media frequently if social media enables people to communicate with others by providing the ability to reply to messages and post or respond to opinions [68]. According to the above arguments, we may recognize that users are more likely to exchange health information with others when they perceive that social media can create effective and efficient discussions for them. As such, we may reasonably propose that human-to-human interaction will have an impact on users’ health information exchange.

H5: Human-to-human interaction has a positive association with health information exchange.

Human-to-information interaction refers to the extent to which one believes that he or she can effectively find or browse using the function of social media [49]. Lu et al.’s study further reveals that users feel satisfied with social media and tend to use social media to exchange information with others when they believe that social media can provide some useful interactive functions for them [49]. Wei et al. [68] also find that human-to-information interaction will affect users’ attitude, which in turn affect their intention to use social media. Therefore, we may recognize that human-to-information interaction may have an impact on the exchange of health information.

H6: Human-to-information interaction has a positive association with health information exchange.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1 Development of measurement instrument

To ensure the validity of the scale, measurement items were adapted from the previous literature wherever possible. Two experts in information management in Taiwan were invited to review the measurement items before the survey began. The experts in the IS field were invited to assess its logical consistencies, ease of understanding, sequence of items, and contextual relevance. Based on their comments, some minor modifications of the questionnaire were conducted. Furthermore, an online pilot test was conducted involving 33 Facebook users who had used Facebook to seek and share health information. Based on their comments and suggestions, the measurement items were modified slightly. All survey items were measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) (see Appendix).

#

Human-to-human interaction was measured using the items adapted from Hsu et al. [32]. The items measured five essential aspects of human-to-human interaction in social media, namely communicating with others, exchanging and sharing opinions, connecting with others, developing interpersonal relationships, and connecting with friends. Human-to-information These items measured four characteristics of human-to-information interaction: searching for information efficiently, filtering information, speedily accessing information, and obtaining meaningful information through social media. The outcome expectation of health self-management competence was measured using items adapted from Oh et al. [55]. The measurement focused on personal confidence in health self-management competence enhancement due to social media usage. The outcome expectation of social relationships was assessed using four items adapted from Hsu et al. [32] and Chang and Chuang [11] to reflect the expected social benefits of using social media, i.e., maintaining social relationships, interacting with friends, using useful communication channels, and improving the quality of social relationship. For the exchange of health information, four items adapted from Oh et al. [55] were used to measure an individual’s health information sharing and seeking.

## 4.2 Samples and data collection

A web-based survey was performed to collect empirical data for users who had used Facebook, a well-known social media platform in Taiwan, for health information seeking and sharing. Google Docs was used to construct an online questionnaire. All participants were adults on the “friends” lists’ in the Facebook account used by the authors. Those who received the survey message were also asked to send it to others on their “friends” lists.’

Of the 212 questionnaires retrieved in this web-based survey, 207 questionnaires were characteristics of the participants were consistent with the results of a survey by the Pew Research Centre, which found that Facebook is used by 77% of adult female social media users but by only 66% of adult male social media users [17]. Thus, the sample was consistent with the overall population of Facebook users in Taiwan. The largest age group was 40–49 years (81, 39.1%) followed by an age group of more than 50 years (63, 30.4%). In terms of the education level, 58.0% (120) of the participants had a bachelor’s degree, and 37.7% (78) of the participants had a graduate degree. Regarding the hours of Facebook use per week, 70.5% (146) of the participants used Facebook for 1–5 hours per week, and 49.7% (133) of the participants had 2–3 years of experience using Facebook. ## 4.3 Data analysis and results

The Analysis of Moment Structures (AMOS) 5.0 and the statistical package for Social Sciences 18.0 were used for the statistical analyses. AMOS was used because it supported covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM) techniques and the objectives of this study (i.e., testing a theory and comparing/contrasting alternative theories) [27]. The two-step approach suggested by Anderson and Gerbing [3] was used for the data analysis: estimating a measurement model and then examining the structural relationships among latent constructs. The main purpose of the two-step approach was to assess the reliability and validity of the measures before applying them in the full model.

## 4.4 Measurement model assessment

All constructs had Cronbach α values exceeding 0.7, which is considered the minimum value for establishing reliability in the social sciences [54]. As can be seen in Table 2, the values of Cronbach α were greater than 0.763, indicating the acceptable reliability of constructs. In addition, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was applied to assess the construct validity of the constructs with AMOS 5.0. Convergent validity was verified by applying the three criteria recommended by Fornell and Larcker [25]: all indicator loadings should be significant and should exceed 0.7, composite reliability (CR) should exceed 0.7, and the average variance extracted (AVE) for each construct should exceed 0.5. For the current CFA model, the results showed that factor loadings for one item of health self-management competence 1 (OHSC1) (0.640) and one item of health information exchange (HIE3) (0.668) were lower than the standardized threshold and were deleted from the analysis. The CR of the constructs ranged from 0.788 to 0.877. The AVE ranged from 0.554 to 0.605 (Table 2). All three conditions for convergent validity were met.

Discriminant validity was assessed using the criteria suggested by Fornell and Larcker [25]. The square root of the AVE also exceeded all other cross-correlations shared between the latent construct and the other latent constructs in the model. Table 3 shows that the correlation coefficients between constructs and all of the square roots of AVE exceeded the inter-construct correlations coefficient, which indicated an acceptable discriminant validity. ## 5. Discussion

## 5.1 Major Findings

This study helps to understand the process in which interactive features and outcome expectations influence health information exchange in social media. The results demonstrate that the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and the outcome expectation of social relationships both have a significant influence on health information exchange. These findings are in line with the SCT literature, which states that outcome expectations are strong predictors of an

#

individual’s behavior [12,33]. Our results also reveal that human-to-human interaction has positive effects on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and the outcome expectation of social relationships. These findings are similar to the SCT assertion that environmental factors will have an impact on personal cognition [6]. The results also provide empirical evidence to support the fact that two-way interpersonal communications through social media will increase users expectations about the enhancement of health self-management competence and online social networks.

Our results also demonstrate that human-to-information interaction exerts a significant effect on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence. This finding is consistent with Wei et al. [68], providing additional empirical evidence that users expect that they can use information shared in social media to find the information they need to increase their knowledge and competence. Contrary to our expectations, human-to-information interaction does not have a significant effect on the outcome expectations of social relationships. This finding is in contrast to the prior studies Hsu et al. [32] and Zhao et al. [75] that more human-to-information interactions will enhance interpersonal relationships established online. One possible explanation may be that users require social cues to determine whether the user they are interacting with is predictable and trustworthy in an online environment, based on a relationship-building perspective [33]. However, the health information users contributed may not provide sufficient social cues in the interpersonal interaction situation for the user to predict the reactions of other users. Hence, social interactions may not be facilitated and thus shared understanding and social interactions also cannot be developed.

Our results also demonstrate that human-to-human interaction and human-to-information interaction are significant predictors of health information exchange. The results are similar to the findings of previous literature [68,74], which demonstrate that the interactive features of social media will have an impact on the use of social media. Finally, our results reveal that outcome expectations exert stronger effects on health information exchange than perceived interactivity. This finding is consistent with the findings of previous studies of SCT [33], providing additional evidence to support the theory that personal benefits are the most vital antecedent factors driving an individual’s behavior.

## 5.2 Theoretical implications

The results of this study provide some implications for theory. First, although past studies have revealed the antecedent factors affecting health information sharing in social media [72], social media users tend to seek fair balance between what they share and what they receive from others [74]. Therefore, understanding the predictors of health information exchange is important because it will enhance our knowledge about how to facilitate social media users in their exchange of information as well as how to receive information from others.

In addition, although past literature has extended the aspect of outcome expectations to knowledge sharing in the online context [12,33]. Little attention has been paid to examine the association between outcome expectations and health information exchange in social media. This study extends the concept of outcome expectations to include both health self-management competence and social relationship outcome expectation. The results of this study contribute to the current SCT studies by demonstrating that these two types of outcome expectation are strong predictors of health information exchange.

Moreover, although prior studies have used SCT to explain knowledge sharing [12,33], few have considered how various interactive characteristics of social media affect personal cognition and behavior in social media. By using the human-to-information interactivity and human-to-human interactivity as environmental factors, this study contributes to the current SCT literature by revealing the association between the interactive features of social media, outcome expectations, and personal behavior.

## 5.3 Practical implications

From a practical perspective, this study also provides several implications for the management of social media. First, the results show that human-to-human interaction is an important factor that may increase users’ outcome expectations and information exchange in social media. Thus, to enhance the perceptions of human-to-human interaction, the managers of social media should provide some strategies to strengthen personal identity. For example, management should encourage users to present their personal profile such as photos, background and experience or to use avatars to present their personal personality and attributes to increase interpersonal interactions [50]. In addition, managers could enhance the compatibility of current popular health services and products through various complementary services to increase human-to-human interactions as well [74].

In addition, human-to-information interaction is a predictor of outcome expectations of health self-management competence and health information exchange. Huang et al. [35] find that emoticons may enhance the exchange of social information by providing additional social cues beyond the textual electronic information. Thus, management of social media could improve the richness of emoticons to strengthen visual cues and enhance user perceptions of virtual presence when browsing health information in social media. Additionally, to enhance social interactions in social media, managers of social media may also deploy some synchronous communication tools embedded in information shared by users that could allow users to conduct two-way conversations with an information contributor immediately [19].

## 5.4 Limitations

Although this study has several interesting findings, certain limitations are noted. First, the findings might have been influenced by selection bias because the participants were limited to active Facebook users who had experience in exchanging health information with their social media peers. Users who have already ceased using Facebook might reveal different perspectives. Further studies are required to examine whether the results can be generalized to nonparticipants and disaffected participants as well. Second, although our study suggests that perceived interactivity is a vital antecedent factor of outcome expectations, there may be other important factors. Future studies should include some factors such as absorptive capability and source credibility into the model.

Third, social media sites could be classified into several different types based on the applications and technologies such as social networking sites (e.g., Facebook, MySpace and Weibo), micro blogs (e.g., Twitter), and content communities (e.g., YouTube) [16]. Given that the data of this study were collected from users of Facebook, further research is necessary to test the generalizability of the results of this study to other social media types. Furthermore, individual behavioral patterns of information technology usage are influenced by national cultural characteristics [38]. Owing to the fact that this study collected data from social media users in Taiwan, the findings might not be applicable in other countries/regions. Further studies are required to investigate our model in different cultural contexts. Finally, the main purpose of this study is to identify the predictors of health information exchange. Yet healthcare activities have become the important issues in many countries. Thus, understanding the factors affecting an individual’s health self-management competence is important. Further studies, for example, could verify whether the perceived interactivity factors are the antecedents of social support and information exchange, which in turn lead to improved health self-management behaviors, based on the findings of this study.

## 5.5 Conclusion

Owing to the rapid growth of Web 2.0 technology, social media has become an attractive platform for people to acquire timely and personalized health information [4]. Thus, understanding the predictors of health information exchange may provide several insights for practitioners and researchers about how to facilitate health information sharing and use. Drawing on the perspectives of SCT and perceived interactivity, this study investigates the effects of perceived interactivity and outcome expectations on health information exchange. The results show that perceived interactivity (human-to-human interaction, human-to-information interaction) and outcome expectations (outcome expectation of health self-management competence and outcome expectation of social relationships) exert positive influences on health information exchange. The results also reveal that human-to-human interactions have a significant influence on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence and outcome expectation of social relationships, while human-to-information interaction has a positive effect on the outcome expectation of health self-management competence.
