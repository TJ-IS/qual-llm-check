# Assessing the product review helpfulness: Afective-Cognitive evaluation and the moderating efect of feedback mechanism

Keywords: Product review helpfulness Product review length Product review author's response Inverted U-shape relationship Afect-oriented processing Cognition-oriented processing

## A B S T R A C T

Product review length has been demonstrated as one of the key factors that influence the product review helpfulness. However, we have little knowledge of a deeper understanding on how consumers process the product review length to assess product review helpfulness. Anchoring on the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making and the prominent coping approaches, this research revisits this key issue from consumers afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing perspective. Our findings show that (1) consumers apply both the afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing to assess the helpfulness of product review, (2) a significant inverted U-shape relationship between the review length and the review helpfulness, and interestingly, (3) such a relationship is further moderated by whether the product review author has provided a response to consumers’ comments. These findings not only provide further robust evidence to the consideration of both product review length and feedback, but also suggest a refinement of the underlying mechanism on how con sumers process product review length to assess product review helpfulness.

## 1. Introduction

Product review helpfulness, which is represented by the votes from consumers who assess whether a particular product review is helpful for facilitating consumers’ online shopping decision, has been demonstrated as one of the key research topics in the digital commerce context [1]. Online product review is an indispensible information source that may provide useful information to consumers to process [2]. Consumers could retreive useful information from either the review content or the communicative interaction between product review author and other consumers in the form of posting comments attached to the focal product review. On the basis of processing of information retrieved from product review, consumers then evaluate whether the product review is helpful or not. In this regard, consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness is a decision-making behavior with the processing of information from product review. Both researchers and practitioners alike have strong interests to understand what factor influences consumers assessment of product review helpfulness and how such an impact happens?

The extant literature of online product review helpfulness has made significant advancement in understanding what factors influence consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness from the product review’s disposition perspective. For example, researchers have found the review valence, review quality, and review length have salient influence on the product review helpfulness [3]. Among them, researchers have reached a consensus that the product review length is the primitive information retrieved from product review when consumers are processing the product review [4]. Moreover, the product review length has a significant impact on consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness [2,5]. However, from the information processing perspective, we have little knowledge of a deeper understanding on the underlying mechanism of how consumers process the product review length so as to evaluate the product review helpfulness. As a result, the extant literature yielded mixed findings [2,6]. For example, Baek and her colleagues found the product review length has a monotonous positive relationship with product review helpfulness [7]. The other researchers, however, observed an inverted U-shape relationship between them [8]. Thus, to reconcile the inconsistent findings in prior literature and dig deeper to understand the underlying mechanism of how consumers process the product review length to assess the online product review helpfulness, we intend to examine two important research questions: (1) What is the impact of the product review length on product review helpfulness? (2) How consumers process the product review length to evaluate the product review helpfulness?

To this end, one possible solution is to find a pertinent theoretical explanation that harmonizes the mixed research findings. This idea is driven by Ullmann’s argument on the theoretical development for resolving the inconsistent findings [9]. As Ullmann argued, a lack (of inclusiveness) in theory is one of the main reasons for the inconsistent findings. Elaborately, prior researchers mainly anchored on either the cognitive decision-making theories or mental load theories to investigate the utilities of product review length for interpreting the linear relationship (or nonlinear relationship) between itself and the product review helpfulness [2,6]. Although these findings collectively imply that consumers do not exclusively conduct rational information processing when processing product review length to assess the product review helpfulness [10], we still have little knowledge on whether consumers apply both the rational and irrational information processing of product review length concurrently. Moreover, if they $\mathbf { d o } ,$ how would such a process influence the consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness? To be more specific, research from decision science discipline suggests that individuals’ decision-making activity usually consists of both the cognitive and afective processes [11]. Thus, it is promising to investigate how consumers process product review length to evaluate product review helpfulness from both the cognitive and afective perspectives at the same time. However, there is no prior study that investigated this issue, thus leaving substantial room for improvement.

The lack of inclusiveness in theory is also echoed by the dearth of studies on investigating the possible moderating efect that alters the relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness. As a direct complementary information source, the communicative comments from both the product review author and other consumers provide strong support to cope with the deficiency of information processing resource [12]. Particularly, the product review author’s response to other consumers’ comments determines the quality of communication [13]. Thus, it may provide a salient moderating effect to influence the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness. In this regard, we intend to examine the third research question: (3) What is the impact of product review author’s response on the consumers’ processing of product review length to evaluate the product re view helpfulness?

To address these research questions, we anchor on human’s afec tive-cognitive model of decision-making and the prominent coping approaches to investigate the underlying mechanism of how consumers process product review length to evaluate the product review help fulness. Moreover, we examine the moderating efect of product review author’s response on such impact. Elaborately, the human’s afectivecognitive model of decision-making is an inclusive theoretical framework that takes a human’s afective and cognitive processing into consideration for interpreting a human’s decision-making behavior. It states that a human’s decision-making is influenced by both the afect oriented and cognition-oriented processing [11]. Anchoring on this theoretical framework, we postulate that a diferent length of product review would invoke various status of consumers’ afective and cognitive information processing, thus leading to diferent impact on the product review helpfulness. This theoretical reasoning is then represented by an inverted U-shape relationship between product review length and the product review helpfulness. In addition, we postulate that this nonlinear relationship can be moderated by product review author’s response with the consideration of the problem-centered and/ or emotion-centered coping [14]. The product review author’s response would neutralize the negative impact on the review helpfulness and make the relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness to be monotonously linear.

By conducting a controlled laboratory experiment (Study 1) and the analysis of a field dataset (Study 2), we demonstrate that our hypotheses are supported. In this regard, this paper intends to provide several contributions to researchers and practitioners. First, this paper provides a theoretically sound explanation to understand how consumers process product review length to evaluate the product review helpfulness from the afect-oriented processing and cognition-oriented processing perspective. Second, this study demonstrates the moderating efect of the product review author’s response. We also provide a theoretical explanation on how this add-on IT artifact (e.g., commenting function in the product review system) shifts consumers evaluation performance for assessing the product review helpfulness. Third, this paper provides a salient contribution to consolidate the dichotomized research findings on the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness in prior studies. Fourth, this paper provides actionable suggestions to practitioners with a low-cost and easy-to-deploy plan for maintaining the product review helpfulness.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making

Consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness is a decision making activity [15]. It is because consumers who read the product review need to process the information in the product review and judge whether the review content can facilitate consumers to make an online shopping decision [3]. According to the literature on decision sciences, when conducting a decision-making activity, both of the consumers’ afect and cognition would influence the final decision and outcome [16]. In this regard, the review of the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making would provide a concrete theoretical basis for understanding how consumers evaluate product review helpfulness.

The human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making is developed by Shiv and Fedorikhin in their seminal paper on examining the conflict between heart and mind during the consumers’ decisionmaking process [11]. This model is built upon Berkowitz’s theory of how afect and cognition interact to influence a human’s behavior [17]. In particular, the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making states that a human’s decision-making is constituted with two types of mental methods of processing. The first method of processing is called afect-oriented processing, which is likely to occur in a relatively automatic manner [18]. The afect-oriented processing will lead consumers to have an afective reaction (e.g., sad and happy) to the decision-making activity. Then, the formulated afective reaction will have an influence on the decision’s outcome [11]. The second method of processing is termed as the cognition-oriented processing. It means consumers would deliberatively use the cognitive resource to process incoming information and make a decision [10].

According to the human’s afective-cognitive model of decisionmaking, the afect-oriented processing and cognition-oriented processing are exclusive with each other during the decision-making process [11]. Being exposed to a decision-making task, users will execute these types of processing methods selectively with the consideration of the availability of information processing resource. If the information processing resource is suficient, both processing methods will be executed to make a decision. In particular, afect-oriented processing lead to a rapid assessment of the afective significance of the decisionmaking task [19]. Then, the cognition-oriented processing is activated for generating more solid supporting reasons to make a decision. Conversely, if the information processing resource is not suficient, the cognition-based processing will be restrained and the afective reaction from the afect-oriented processing will dominate the impact on decision outcomes [11].

The human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making can be seamlessly adopted from the decision science discipline to examine the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness with the consideration of consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing in a holistic manner. The consideration of a dual schematic mental processing of this model is aligned with other seminal dual process theories of human’s information processing in psychology and social psychology discipline [20]. For example, the heuristic-systematic model proposed a dual-process model of information processing in the human’s decision-making activity [21]. Another important dual-process model, elaboration likelihood model, describes how users rely on dif ferent routes (central route or/and peripheral route) in their mind to process informational stimuli for decision-making [22].

At the same time, the human’s afective-cognitive model of decisionmaking difers from these widely used theories by shedding light on the interaction between afect and cognition during the decision-making process [23]. In particular, this model adopts the human’s available information processing resource as a regulator to govern the interplay between afect-oriented processing and cognition-oriented processing. In this regard, the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision making can provide an inclusive theoretical foundation to investigate how the product review helpfulness is being evaluated by consumers from both afective and cognitive perspectives.

## 2.2. The product review length and product review helpfulness

Drawn upon the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision making, we could explicate how consumers apply either afect-oriented processing or cognition-oriented processing to evaluate the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness.

## 2.2.1. Afect-oriented processing

As illustrated in the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision making, afect-oriented processing occurs automatically in regard to the exposure of decision tasks [16]. It results in preattentive action tendencies on a rapid assessment of the afective significance of the decision tasks [18]. It is to be noted that this kind of afect is directly in duced by the product review itself, not the other kinds of afect that is carried over from other sources such as the emotional terms in the product review content [24]. In this regard, the afect-oriented processing is centered on examining the afect that is being induced by the exposure of the primitive information of product review, such as the length of a product review.

There is no prior study that considered the impact of afect-oriented processing when examining the product review helpfulness. Instead, most of the extant literature examined the sentiment-induced emotion and its impact on consumers’ perception of product review helpfulness [25]. The sentiment-induced emotion is mainly originated from two sources: the extremity of review rating as well as the sentimental terms embedded in the product review content [26,27]. The extremity of review rating in a product review represents the attitude extremity of prior consumers who bought/used the related product [28]. For instance, a very low rating (one star) means the extremely negative attitude toward the product; a moderate rating (three stars) shows the middle-ground attitude [29]. Considering the nature of review rating extremity, prior scholars found that it is an indicator of the perceived quality of the related product, even when the underlying product quality is controlled [30]. Thus, the extremity of review rating can invoke the sentiment-induced emotion of consumers when reading the product review. Sentimental terms refer to the particular word(s) that represent the emotion of the consumer who wrote the product review [31], such as “pleased” and “concerned.” Yin and his colleagues examined the impact of sentimental terms on product review helpfulness evaluation [25]. They demonstrated that the sentimental terms could result in spillover efects on consumers’ judgment and behavior. Consequently, consumers, who are influenced by the sentimental terms in a product review, intend to make a biased evaluation of product review helpfulness.

Although many studies have been conducted, prior studies neglected the impact of afect that associates with the evaluation activity of product review helpfulness [10]. A predominant yet fully disclosed assumption in prior studies is that consumers will undertake a cognitive method to process the sentiment-induced emotion in the product review, such as the processing of review rating extremity and the processing of sentimental terms [25]. However, as stated in the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making, online consumers will automatically generate an afect toward the evaluation activity of product review helpfulness [11]. This preattentive afect is formulated by the stimuli of the low-order primitive information of a product review, such as the product review length. As Ullman discussed, the “crude and primitive” afect could result in afective reactions and ac tion tendencies (approaching or avoiding) toward the decision task [32]. Applying the afect-oriented processing in the research context of product review helpfulness, we would expect that the short product review will invoke a favorable afect toward the evaluation task [33]. Conversely, with the growth of product review length, we conceive that consumers will gradually generate an unfavorable afect toward the evaluation of product review helpfulness because consumers usually feel unhappy and/or distracted when given a lengthy product review [34].

## 2.2.2. Cognition-oriented processing

The cognition-oriented processing refers to the consumers’ assessment of product review helpfulness based on the rational interpretation of product review’s disposition [8]. When conducting the cognitionoriented processing to evaluate the product review helpfulness, consumers examine whether the information provided in the product review can facilitate them to make a reasonable purchase decision.

In contrast to the small number of extant studies on afect-oriented processing, there are several studies in the extant literature that focused on consumers’ cognitive evaluation of product review length and the subsequent judgment on its helpfulness [3]. The central thesis of these studies states that consumers use systematic information processing to examine product review content. In this regard, consumers would treat the product review length as a proxy of product review content.

Many researchers concurred that the product review length has a significant impact on the product review helpfulness. However, different researchers hold varying opinions about how the product review length influences the product review helpfulness. In general, there are two streams of research studies in the extant literature. The first stream of studies holds the belief that product review length has a monotonous relationship with the product review helpfulness. The underlying logic of these studies is that a higher level of product review length indicates the product review has more content. In addition, more content usually enhances the likelihood of collecting helpful information from the product review. Subsequently, the product review helpfulness can be secured.

Building upon the underlying logic, prior researchers in this stream of research took a step forward to study the latent construct that represents the review content dispositions, such as review content objectivity [35] and review content diagnosticity [36]. Among them, review content diagnosticity plays a major role in shaping consumers’ perception toward the review helpfulness [2]. The review content diagnosticity means the extent to which review content discriminates between alternative interpretations of a problem and possible solutions to it. According to this definition, the underlying rationale of the relationship between review content diagnosticity and review helpfulness lies in how the product review content empowers consumers with the discrimination ability for choosing a product out of many alternatives. The discrimination ability is a high-order cognitive construct for interpreting review helpfulness. This high-order construct can be exemplified by the low-order product review dispositions, such as the length of a product review [37]. According to a seminal study con ducted by Mudambi and Schuf, product review length can significantly enhance the content diagnosticity of a particular review, thus leading to a high level of product review helpfulness [2]. Other studies had also made a similar finding on the positive relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness [7,38]. From the product review readers’ perspective, a long product review means a high possibility of acquiring useful information from reading the review for making an informed decision. In the meantime, a long product review usually contains more statements of the evaluation of the related product. In this regard, long product reviews would reduce consumers’ uncertainty of decision-making, bring more evidence for discriminating a product from other alternatives, and increase consumers’ confidence in making a shopping decision. Consequently, a large value of product review length will lead to an improved evaluation outcome of product review helpfulness.

The second stream of studies, however, believe that consumers cognition-oriented processing on product review helpfulness may not always be positive with the increase of product review length [39]. This conception is originated from the research on human’s bounded in formation processing capacity [6]. The gist of these studies states that the increase of product review content could bring favorable support to the review helpfulness evaluation, but only up to a point. For example, Schindler and Bickart [8] examined the relationship between the number of statements in product review content and review helpfulness. They found that the maximum of quantity rule in conversation research sheds light on the negative influence of the excessive length of a product review. In other words, providing more information in a product review may lead to consumers’ confusion when interpreting the review content [40]. As a result, the product review helpfulness will be impaired. The nonpositive, nonlinear relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness is also demonstrated by the findings from behavioral science studies, in which the researchers found that the limited working memory of consumers would result in the inferior impact on review helpfulness perception [41].

In summary, we reviewed the prior literature of product review length and product review helpfulness from the theoretical lens of a human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making [11]. We found that there are several significant theoretical gaps in the extant literature. First, prior research findings are not consistent, which signals a substantial room for improvement in terms of theoretical development [42]. Second, prior studies ignored the consumers’ afect on the evaluation task of product review helpfulness [43]. Third, prior studies do not consider the joint impact of consumers’ afective and cognitive evaluation [44]. All these gaps urge us to make an evaluation of the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness from the perspective of consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing.

## 2.3. Product review author’s response as a coping method

Based on the literature review of prior studies on product review helpfulness, under some conditions, either the consumers’ afect-oriented processing or cognition-oriented processing may have unfavor able outcomes. To attenuate the impairment on the review helpfulness evaluation, the online shopping platforms and the product review au thors strive to develop more opportunities to make efective communication with the consumers to improve their learning and evaluation with the focal product review. Among many attempts, the review commenting function plays a pivotal role in the conventional online product review system.

The product review commenting function is an important IT artifact that enables a sustainable conversation between consumers and the product review author. In the conventional product review system, consumers would make comments on a product review by using the review commenting function. The product review author can also use the same function to reply to consumers’ comments. The conversation between consumers and product review author is logged and it is dis played along with the product review. Consumers can easily access the review commenting function and find the comments made by other consumers and the responses made by the product review author when reading the product review. In this scenario, the product review au thor’s response would represent as an approach for alleviating negative impacts toward the product review helpfulness evaluation from both the problem-centered and emotion-centered coping perspectives [45].

The problem-centered coping approach refers to the approaches that aim in changing the person-environment realities behind negative outputs. The product review author’s response could bring in additional information to mitigate consumers’ uncertainty toward the claims and/ or statements in the product review [46]. The product review author’s response usually aims at addressing the specific questions/concerns raised by other product review readers. In this regard, the existence of product review author’s response could guide the consumers’ attention to the most critical/popular evaluation criteria for the purchase decision, thus leading to the efective utilization of the product review [47].

The emotion-centered coping approach means the approaches of changing consumers’ emotional state. The product review author’s response is a positive signal of maintaining interaction between the communicative parties. Prior researchers found that the repeated failure to respond typically leads to a bad impression of the communication recipients, which eventually results in the termination of communication [13]. In this regard, the product review author’s response is a vital signal of the intention of maintaining communication [48].

In addition, the product review author’s response represents the willingness of having proactive communication, particularly for the communication initiator. Although the content of a response is not predictable, prior scholars observed that the response typically shows the communicative parties will respond to others, and they will address the content of preceding messages [49]. This reason is particularly viable in the online environment, in which no one is obligated to reply to others’ comments/questions. If the communicative parties respond to others, then the response could provide a strong justification for their willingness to communicate.

Moreover, product review author’s response could indicate the in terpersonal afect to the other communicative parties. For example, researchers found the communicative parties’ response shows the communicative parties’ interest between them [50]. Moreover, the response represents respectful communication. People will perceive courtesy and respect of the others when the response is present [51]. Consequently, they will generate positive afect toward the communicative parties. As a result, the negative feeling will be compensated by having the response. In a similar vein, consumers’ negative feeling would be compensated emotionally by having the response of product review author in the product review helpfulness research context.

Research on the impact of product review author’s response in the review commenting function has been neglected in the extant literature. The existing studies mainly focus on the features of product review (e.g., review depth, review readability, and review age) [25,42], review rating (e.g., negativity bias and positivity bias) [38], and product review author’s informational cues (e.g., reviewer expertise and reviewer social network) [52,53]. However, the review commenting function is an indispensable component in the conventional online review system, and it is often displayed next to the review content. Thus, its impact cannot be easily ignored. Our study fills the research gap by investigating the moderating role of the product review author’s response in the review commenting function. It will highlight how the add-on IT artifact shifts consumers’ behavior when interacting with the online review system in the digital shopping platform.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

Based on a human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making, we propose the following overarching thesis of this paper. Fig. 1 presents the research framework of this paper.

The product review length will have an inverted U-shape relationship with the product review helpfulness. The inverted U-shape relationship will become a positive monotonous relationship if the product review author provides a response to consumers’ comments. These phenomena can be explained by the joint consideration of consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing as well as the problem-centered and emotional centered coping approach of the product review author’s response.

## 3.1. Main efect

Applying the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making to the product review helpfulness research context, we posit that consumers would adaptively use the afect-oriented processing and/or the cognition-oriented processing to evaluate the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness. In addition, the application of these two types of mental processing would not lead to a monotonous relationship between the product review length and product review helpfulness. Our statement is counterintuitive and diferent from the findings of some prior studies. To elaborate our conception, we anchor on three typical types of product review length that have been studied in the extant literature, namely short product review, moderately long product review, excessively long product review, and develop our hy potheses from the afective-cognitive processing perspective.

## 3.1.1. In the short product review condition

According to the literature on a human’s mental processing and decision-making [54], short product review does not consume a significantly large volume of a human’s information processing resource [55]. In this regard, based on the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making, online consumers will execute both afect-oriented processing and cognition-oriented processing for assessing the helpfulness of product review when they have enough information processing resource. Meanwhile, the cognition-oriented processing will play a more important role in determining the overall evaluation outcome [11].

Based on the theoretical foundation, we would expect that the online consumers’ afect-oriented processing occurs automatically once the evaluation task is initiated [11]. After that, online consumers’ affect-oriented processing will lead to a favorable afective assessment toward the product review helpfulness evaluation. It is because the length of short product review usually does not lead to a cost of a significant amount of information processing resource. Moreover, online consumers usually tend to use their information processing resource in an economical way. They favor the task that requires minimal information processing resource [56]. As a result, short product review often invokes a favorable afective inclination toward the evaluation of product review helpfulness [11].

In the short product review condition, online consumers have en ough information processing resource so that they have high possibilities to make intensive cognition-oriented processing of the product review content and generate an informed evaluation of the product review helpfulness [57]. However, we argue for a counterintuitive statement in which online consumers cannot develop a favorable evaluation of product review helpfulness, even though they have enough information processing resource in the short product review condition. The underlying rationale lies in the limited content provided by the short product review. Although having enough information processing resource, online consumers are unable to generate many meaningful statements from the interpretation of the review content to support the evaluation of product review helpfulness [38], thus leading to unfavorable cognition-oriented processing performance on the product review helpfulness evaluation. ceteris paribus, short product review cannot carry more messages than other longer product reviews for evaluating the product review helpfulness. As a result, we posit that the short product review leads to low level of consumers’ evaluation on product review helpfulness.

## 3.1.2. In the moderately long product review condition

In this condition, the volume of product review content, which i represented by the product review length, will not deplete online consumers’ information processing resource [55]. According to the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making, both the afect oriented and cognition-oriented processing will be utilized for evalu ating the product review helpfulness. In addition, the cognition-oriented processing will play a dominant role in determining the overall evaluation outcome [11].

From the afect-oriented processing perspective, the moderately long product review does not generate positive afective inclination toward the assessment task of the product review helpfulness, and it does not result in negative afective inclination [58]. The length of moderately long product review represents a signal that indicates on line consumers need to use a considerable amount of information processing resource to process the product review content for evaluating the product review helpfulness. As Fiske argued, conventional consumers are the “cognitive miser” [59]. If an evaluation task requires more information processing resource, then consumers will not have a positive afective inclination toward the task. However, not having a positive afective inclination does not mean online consumers hold a negative afective inclination toward the product review helpfulness evaluation [58]. In the condition of processing a moderately long product review, the expected cost of information processing resource does not exceed the maximum limit of the consumers’ information processing resource. In this regard, consumers will not generate negative afect on the evaluation task [33]. According to the human’s affective-cognitive model of decision-making, the non-positive/nonnegative outcome of afect-oriented processing will not bring any positive nor negative afective influence on the cognition-oriented pro cessing of the product review content for evaluating its helpfulness. As a result, the overall evaluation of the product review helpfulness rests on the performance of online consumers’ cognition-oriented processing [11].

From the cognition-oriented processing perspective, we argue that online consumers have suficient information processing resource when facing a moderately long product review [60]. According to the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making, online consumers can conduct a deliberative assessment on the product review content as well as the product review helpfulness. Compared with the short product review, the moderately long product review pertains to a high possibility of having useful statements and meaningful contents in a product review [8]. In this regard, the more words provided in a product review (which means the length of product review is longer), the higher possibilities that online consumers would identify useful statements from the product reviews for facilitating their online shopping. As a result, ceteris paribus, online consumers will make a favorable evaluation outcome on the product review helpfulness. Thus, we posit that the moderately long product review would have a positive impact on consumers’ evaluation of the product review helpfulness.

## 3.1.3. In the excessively long product review condition

The excessively long product review will drain of online consumers’ bounded information processing resource for interpreting the product review content in the short term [55]. Based on the human’s afectivecognitive model of decision-making, if the online consumers’ information processing resource is not suficient, the cognition-oriented pro cessing will be restrained and its performance will be limited [11]. At the same time, the afective reaction from online consumers’ afectoriented processing will dominate the evaluation of the impact of the length of product review on product review helpfulness.

From the afect-oriented processing perspective, the length of excessively long product review provides an instant signal that indicates online consumers need to spend extensive information processing resource to process the product review content. It will lead online con sumers to generate a negative afect on the task [61]. For example, Pan and other colleagues found that users usually feel “unsatisfied,” “frustrated,” and/or “agitated” with the demanding task that requires a significant amount of information processing resource [62]. Consequently, users become unwilling to read the content or tend to abandon the task right away [63]. In a similar vein, the length of an excessively long product review will invoke online consumers’ negative afective inclination toward the interpretation of the product review content. It subsequently inhibits the possibility of performing a comprehensive evaluation of the product review content, which ultimately brings a negative impact on the product review helpfulness evaluation.

According to the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision making, if the consumers’ information processing resource is not suf ficient, the cognition-based processing will be restrained and the affective reaction from afect-oriented processing will dominate the impact on decision outcomes [11]. In the excessively long product review condition, considering the negative afect generated toward the demanding job on interpreting the excessively long product review as well as the restrained performance of cognition-oriented processing of the product review content in a holistic manner, online consumers will have an unfavorable assessment outcome toward the product review helpfulness. ceteris paribus, we posit the excessively long product review leads to a low level of consumers’ evaluation on product review helpfulness.

After consolidating our theoretical reasoning in the short/moderately long/excessively long product review conditions, we posit that consumers will apply both the afect-oriented processing and cognition oriented processing to assess the product review helpfulness. In addi tion, we would develop an overarching hypothesis that posits a nonlinear relationship between the length of product review and the product review helpfulness.

H1a: The consumers would leverage afect-oriented processing to process the impact of product review length on the product review helpfulness.

H1b: The consumers would leverage cognition-oriented processing to process the impact of product review length on the product review help fulness.

H1c: The length of a product review has an inverted U-shape relationship with the product review helpfulness.

## 3.2. Moderating efect

The provision of product review author’s response to consumers’ comments on the product review would serve as an important moderator for influencing the relationship between the length of product review and product review helpfulness. The product review author’s response could provide a moderating efect for two reasons: First, the product review author’s response could act as a problem-centered approach to mitigate online consumers’ concerns on the uncertainty of interpreting the content of product review, thus bringing a favorable evaluation on the product review helpfulness (from the cognition-oriented processing perspective). Second, the product review author’s response would provide an emotion-centered approach to rectify the negative afects that emerged when online consumers process the pro duct review and evaluate the product review helpfulness (from the af fect-oriented processing perspective) [14]. In the following subsection, we elaborate our theoretical reasoning of the moderating efect in aligning with the hypothesis development fashion of the main efect.

## 3.2.1. In the short product review and moderately long product review conditions

In these two conditions, the length of the product review does not deplete the consumers’ bounded information processing resource [11]. In this regard, the moderating efect of product review author’s response may not be salient because there is no significant hindrance to cope with on the relationship between the product review length and product review helpfulness. From the afect-oriented processing perspective, the provision of product review author’s response could bring favorable afect. Online consumers would perceive the product review author is benevolent and is willing to communicate and share his/her option in a readily comprehended form [13]. In this regard, consumers will generate a favorable afect toward the evaluation of product review helpfulness, thus leading to a positive inclination to conduct the cognition-oriented processing [11]. From the cognition-oriented processing perspective, the product review author’s response will bring additional knowledge that is highly related to the product review and consumers comments. It will strengthen the consumers’ capacity to have a better understanding of the product review content. As a result, it will im prove the performance of product review helpfulness evaluation. In this regard, the length of a product review is positively associated with the product review helpfulness.

## 3.2.2. In the excessively long product review condition

The provision of product review author’s response, however, will make a substantial change to the relationship between the length of product review and product review helpfulness in the condition of having an excessively long product review. Based on the hypothesis development in the main efect subsection, online consumers would experience negative afect when processing the excessively long product review as well as the deficiency of information processing resource. To cope with these negative issues, the provision of product review author’s response could benefit the impact of the length of product review on product review helpfulness from both the emotioncentered coping and problem-centered coping aspects [14]. From the emotion-centered coping perspective, the product review author’s response could strengthen a favorable afect on the product review. As a result, consumers will have a positive inclination to the evaluation of product review helpfulness.

From the problem-centered coping perspective, the provision of product review author’s response could enhance the consumers’ ability to digest the product review content within the bounded information processing resource for two reasons: First, having the product review author’s response enhances the possibility of highlighting the key claims and/or statements made by the product review author [64]. Second, the product review author’s response could address the specific questions/concerns raised by product review readers, thus leading to an efective interpretation of the product review. In this regard, the provision of product review author’s response creates a “shortcut” for understanding the product review content [65]. Even though the length of a product review is excessively long, consumers are guided to learn from the product review efectively with the provision of product review author’s response. As a result, online consumers’ information processing resource is conserved and they can process additional information in the product review content. ceteris paribus, considering both the emotion-centered and problem-centered benefits provided by the product review author’s response, the length of the product review will not have a significant negative impact on the product review helpfulness.

In summary, the provision of product review author’s response could significantly improve the impact of the product review length on the product review helpfulness in the excessively long product review condition. Considering the theoretical reasoning of the moderating ef fect in short/moderately long/excessively long product review condi tions collectively, we posit:

H2a: The product review author’s response has a moderating efect on the afect-oriented processing of product review length

H2b: The product review author’s response has a moderating efect on the cognition-oriented processing of product review length

H2c: The provision of product review author’s response will moderate the relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness. In particular: the relationship will shift from an inverted U-shape relationship to a monotonous positive relationship.

## 4. Research methodology

We conducted two consecutive studies to test our hypotheses. Specifically, we designed a controlled laboratory experiment (Study 1) to investigate the relationship between the length of product review and product review helpfulness from the consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing perspective. We also examined the moderating role of product review author’s response in the laboratory experiment. Next, we extended our findings from the laboratory experiment to a generalized online environment in which we adopted the review-level data from a real online shopping website to delineate the relationship between the length of product review and product review helpfulness as well as the moderating role of product review author’s response (Study 2). The consolidation of the research findings from these two studies would provide theoretically solid and empirically appropriate implications to the existing scholarly work and conven tional practice alike.

## 4.1. Study 1

## 4.1.1. Experiment design

A 2 × 3 factorial laboratory experiment was designed (see Table 1), in which the 2 represents the status of providing product review au thor’s response in the review commenting function (with or without), and the 3 represents three diferent lengths of a product review (short, moderately long, and excessively long). In total, there were six treatment groups in Study 1. In each of the treatment groups, we also provided two scenarios with the consideration of capturing the efect of product type.

We did not control the consumers’ afect-oriented processing status and cognition-oriented processing status because they were the latent variables, which cannot be directly controlled with given manipulation [66]. However, we measured the consumers’ afect-oriented processing, cognition-oriented processing, and other subjective measures by adopting questionnaires from prior literature (see Appendix A for details). These measurement items were embedded in a simulated online shopping website. In this website, we provided two modules to participants. The first module is an online product presentation module, which presented descriptive product information to participants. The product information in this module was first downloaded from a real online shopping website. We then used a fictional brand and fictional price to rule out the efect of diference on brand and price [67]. The purpose of having such manipulation is to attenuate the contingent efect of the price [68] and brand [69]. In this experiment, we used both the search products and experience products as the target product in the stimulated shopping website. The search products were an LED flat TV, a professional GPS navigator, and a professional drilling machine. The experience products were a professional headset, a professional fogless mirror set, and a sports music player [2]. A pretest was conducted with eleven volunteers for classifying these products. The Fleiss Kappa value of the volunteers’ rating agreement (Kappa = 0.65) indicates a good classification of the search product and experience product in our experiment [70].

The second module is a product review presentation module, which provided a product review and its related consumers’ comments to the participants. The length of the product review was controlled with three categories: short, moderately long, and excessively long. We first sur veyed over 10,000 product reviews from online shopping websites about the review length and then took the first quantile, second quantile, and third quantile as the reference points for the three types of product review in our experiment. Next, we downloaded the product reviews from a real online shopping website with the consideration of these reference points. After that, we cleaned the identity information and other unrelated information in the content of product reviews. In general, the short product review has around 140 words. The moderately long product review has around 1200 words and the excessively long product review has around 4000 words.

We provided only the product review and related consumers’ comments in the “without product review author’s response” treatment groups. In addition to this, we provided a product review author’s response in the “with product review author’s response” treatment groups. All the product reviews, consumers’ comments, and product review author’s responses were downloaded from a real online shopping website and then tailored to fit the research settings. For example, we kept the length of consumers’ comments to be the same across different treatment groups. We controlled the valence of product review by using the same star rating across diferent treatment groups [71]. We also controlled the spillover efect of the sentimental terms/star rating embedded in the product review content on the consumers’ afect toward the evaluation of the product review helpfulness by screening the unified and neutralized emotional terms and the star rating across treatment groups [25]. In addition, we provided the same length of product review author’s response across diferent product reviews in the condition of “With product review author’s response.” In this regard, the contingent efects of product review valence and the length of comments and feedback can be controlled. Other than these manipulations, the setting of the simulated website was set such that it was like a conventional online shopping website.

In the experiment, a prologue was displayed to the participant upon logging in the mockup website. After reading the prologue, par ticipants had an opportunity to try out the navigation of the simulated shopping website and a practical task that mimics the main experiment task. Then, participants proceeded to the main experiment tasks, in which the two above-mentioned modules presented the descriptive product information and product review, respectively. Once they finished an experiment task, participants were then asked to answer the follow-up questionnaires. After that participants had the opportunity to continue the next experiment task with a randomly selected product or quit the experiment. The participants were not given a time limit during the experiment with the consideration of the diferences in the learning speeds among the participants.

## 4.1.2. Participants

A total of 174 participants from a public university in the Asia Pacific region were recruited and they were randomly assigned to the treatment groups, each having 29 participants (see Table 2). After the experiment, each participant was given a monetary incentive that was equivalent to USD 10.

## 4.1.3. Procedure

The experiment was conducted as follows. When participants arrived at the lab room, they were randomly assigned to one desktop terminal and logged in the system with the use of a designated account (which diferentiated the treatment groups). Subsequently, the parti cipants began the experiment with the sequence described in the ex periment design section. Participants were given the experiment pro logue that they were to make purchase decisions for themselves. Each of them was asked to complete two shopping tasks: one is for a search product and the other one is for an experience product. After completing the experiment, the participants were given the monetary in centive and dismissed.

## 4.1.4. Data analysis

4.1.4.1. Manipulation check. We conducted the manipulation check to examine whether participants perceived the control over the length of the product review correctly. During the experiment, participants were asked to use a horizontal scrolling bar question to indicate their perceived length of content after they read the product review. The horizontal scrolling bar question has two labels “short product review” and “excessively long product review” at two ends. Participants could use the mouse to move the scrolling bar to a place inbetween the two ends of this question where they perceived to be appropriate for reflecting the product review length. At the same time, the experimental system translated participants’ answer to a numerical value, ranging from 0 to 1, in the backend database. This numerical value was not presented to participants during the experiment. It was for manipulation check purpose only. For example, if the scrolling bar is close to “short product review” end, it means participants perceive the product review to be short and the numerical value could be 0.3 in the backend database.

We then examined if participants’ answers of this horizontal scrolling bar question are aligned with the length of product review provided. As expected, the data analysis on the numerical value showed that the manipulation of product review length was successful. Participants perceived the short product review to be short (Mean = 0.24 and SD = 0.09); the moderately long product review to be in the middle range (Mean = 0.45 and SD = 0.08); and the excessively long product review to be long (Mean = 0.81 and SD = 0.50). There is a significant diference between the three treatment conditions (p < 0.001). The manipulation check on the provision of product review author’s response was also successful.

## 4.1.5. Discussion

The findings of Study 1 provide solid support to our theoretical explanation of the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness. This study demonstrates that consumers do utilize both the afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing for the evaluation of product review helpfulness. This finding complements most of the prior studies on product review helpfulness, which presumed that the con sumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness is solely based on their rational thinking [25]. In this regard, Study 1 opens a new avenue toward a comprehensive investigation of consumers’ online behavior from both the afect- and cognition-processing perspectives. This study also indicates that product review author’s response can act as a viable coping method to attenuate the negative afective impact on the product review helpfulness evaluation.

However, because of the limitation of the laboratory experiment method, the categorical setting of product review length cannot provide a refined quantitative validation of the remaining proposed hypotheses (H1c, H2c). In addition, the laboratory experiment setting is not as same as the real online shopping environment. Thus, the general izability of the research findings in Study 1 may not be strong. To address these concerns, we conducted a second study to investigate the impact of product review length and the moderating efect of product review author’s response by analyzing product review data collected from a real-world online shopping website.

We developed a web crawling program to collect a product review from a leading online shopping platform. Specifically, for each of product reviews, we collected the data of review dispositions (e.g., review title, review author name, review star rating, review content, consumers’ comment, product review author’s response, etc.) and the data of the related product (e.g., product name). ## 4.2.3. Hypothesis testing

The hypothesis testing is conducted by applying the Tobit regression to our dataset. The product review helpfulness voting has two salient features: First, the source of helpfulness voting is bounded with two extreme options (Yes or No). Second, the existing helpfulness voting is censored [2]. That means the website does not force every consumer who viewed the review to vote for its helpfulness. In this regard, there is a possible selection bias in the sample set. Considering these two features in mind, we followed the suggestion from prior literature and used Tobit regression in this study [75].

The first step is to build a regression model that consists of dependent variables, independent variables, and control variables. The model should represent the potential relationship developed in the hypotheses. In this regard, new variables will be created for presenting these proposed relationships. H1c posits that the product review length will have an inverted U-shape relationship with the product review helpfulness. Thus, we provide both a linear term (Review length) and a quadratic term (Review length<sup>2</sup>) in the testing model. If the linear term is positive and the quadratic is negative at the same time, then it indicates the existence of an inverted U-shape relationship. H2c posits that the product review author’s response will moderate the relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness. In this regard, we include interaction terms in the testing model (e.g., Review author’s response × Review length). The control variables, such as product type, have been added to the model. ## 4.2.4. Robustness test

The robustness test is performed to validate the inverted U-shape relationship and the moderating efect that has been supported by the data analysis. The reason for conducting robustness test originates from the debates of the legitimacy of the method used for testing the inverted U-shape relationship by adding a non-linear term (usually quadratic) in a regression model. If the quadratic term is significant and the estimated extremum point is within the data range, then researchers usually conclude that there is a U (or invert U) shaped relationship. However, Lind and Mehlum argued that the criteria are too weak [76]. There is a high possibility that the true relationship is convex but monotonous [7]. As a result, the significant quadratic term may not always lead to the conclusion of the existence of a (inverted) U-shaped relationship.

## 5. General discussion

This paper is the first to adopt the human’s afective-cognitive model of decision-making to investigate the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness as well as the moderating efect of the provision of product review author’s response. By conducting a controlled laboratory experiment and an analytical study on the real product review data, we present the theoretically rigorous and empirically appropriate evidence to support the claim of an inverted Ushaped relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness. Importantly, this paper develops a pertinent theoretical explanation of the inverted U-shape relationship with the consideration of consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing. In this regard, we are able to address the first two research questions of this paper. Specifically, based on our findings, when the product review length is short, the low level of product review helpfulness is mainly determined with the poor performance of consumers’ cognition-oriented processing of the product review. When the product review length is excessively long, however, the low level of product review helpfulness is largely determined with the low status of consumers afect-oriented processing outcomes. This finding is novel and has not been researched in the extant literature.

We further demonstrated that the inverted U-shaped relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness can be moderated with the provision of product review author’s response to the consumers’ comments in the review commenting IT artifact. By considering the theoretical explanation of problem-centered coping and emotional-centered coping, we found that the provision of product review author’s response to the consumers’ comments would provide adaptive moderating efect with the consideration of the deficiencies in consumers’ afect-oriented processing/cognition-oriented processing under diferent product review length contexts. In the short product review condition, our findings demonstrate that the product review author’s response compensates the poor performance of consumers cognition-oriented processing of the product review. In the excessively long product review condition, however, the product review author’s response mainly compensates the negative afect generated during the processing of excessive information in a product review. It subsequently attenuates the negative impact on the product review helpfulness. Thus, we successfully address the third research question. This finding is unique. It corroborates our theoretical reasoning on dual processing (afective-cognitive) of the product review length and its impact on product review helpfulness. More importantly, this finding presents the dynamics of the moderating efect across diferent status of a continuum. In this regard, it provides meaningful implications to future studies about how to excavate the full potential of the moderating effect.

## 5.1. Theoretical implications

This paper intends to provide three key theoretical contributions to the existing knowledge on the product review helpfulness. First, this paper digs deeper to understand the underlying mechanism of how consumers leverage both the afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing to evaluate the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness. We found that consumers would use both the afectoriented and cognition-oriented processing to assess the product review helpfulness. In addition, the utilization of these two types of processing is subject to the availability of consumers’ information processing resource, which is significantly influenced by the primitive information of the external stimuli such as the product review length. Our paper enhances the inclusiveness of theoretical perspective that builds the linkage between the information being processed and the consumers’ internal processing comprehensively. In this regard, our paper complements the extant literature that holds the unitarian view of consumers’ internal processing for assessing the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness [2]. Although, the theoretical foundation of the extant studies provides a good basis for understanding consumers’ behavior in interpreting the product review content in general. However, it cannot ofer pertinent explanations to the conditions that consumers’ afect-oriented and cognition-oriented processing takes place when evaluating the product review helpfulness [6], which is more relevant to the conventional individual decision-making process from the perspective of decision science and psychology disciplines that found the afect is also a critical factor for influencing decision outcome [16]. For example, Lerner and Keltner argued that a human’s afect (emotion) carries information that will be used as an input for decisionmaking [77]. It indicates that the consideration of the influence of affect should be assimilated into the theoretical understanding of decision-making behavior. In this regard, our paper sheds light on a novel theoretical framework for understanding consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness from both the afect-oriented and cognitionoriented processing perspectives.

Second, this study demonstrates the moderating efect of product review author’s response in the review commenting function on the relationship between product review length and product review helpfulness. In this regard, we provide a salient theoretical explanation on how the add-on IT artifact shifts consumers’ evaluation performance for assessing the product review helpfulness. Most of the prior literature focused on the influence of some product review features. Their arguments are mainly characterized within the boundary of endogenous features of a product review, such as product review length [39], content readability [37], and content valence [25]. However, with the development of business logic of providing product review in the online shopping website, there are many websites that provide interactive addon functionalities in the product review system, such as the review commenting function. In this regard, it is imperative to investigate the efect of the add-on IT artifact on the consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness. Our study contributes to the existing knowledge by investigating the salient moderating efect of product review author’s response. The research findings shed light on a more comprehensive examination of the related factors that influence the product review helpfulness in the online environment.

Third, this paper provides a salient contribution on consolidating the mixed research findings of the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness in most of the prior studies. As discussed, prior studies yielded inconsistent findings on the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness [7,8]. In our study, the theoretical explanation of the moderating efect corroborates with the underpinning of the main efect of product review length on product review helpfulness. As a result, our paper provides a possible explanation for the mixed findings in prior studies. We argue that most of the mixed findings were due to the neglect of the influence of the communicative interaction between the product review author and other consumers. In this regard, this paper complements prior studies by addressing the dichotomized findings of the impact of product review length on product review helpfulness.

## 5.2. Practical implications

Besides the theoretical contributions, this study could help practitioners identify the appropriate methods for delivering product review information to consumers with the aim of enhancing consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness. First, we demonstrated an in verted U-shaped relationship between the length of product review and product review helpfulness. It indicates that both insuficient and excessive information in product review has detrimental impacts on consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness. For the practical implementation of this finding, an adaptive content length indicator could be designed and implemented in the product review system. When composing a product review, the adaptive content length in dicator would provide instant feedback to review authors with suggestions of either increasing the product review content or reducing the information provided in the product review. Consequently, the product review could receive a good evaluation of its helpfulness from other consumers, compared with a short product review or extremely long product review.

Second, the finding of the moderating role of product review author’s response in the review commenting function could provide an actionable suggestion to the practitioners for the low-cost and easy-todeploy maintenance method of product review helpfulness, particularly for the short- and excessively long product review. To address the concern of information overload of the excessively long product review, prior studies often suggested that practitioners use text mining technique or structured composition technique to highlight the key opinions in a product review [78]. These techniques are very useful for enhan cing the product review helpfulness. However, practitioners often face significant challenges when adopting these techniques on websites. For example, practitioners need to develop (or purchase) the text mining packages and the related hardware to process the large volume of product reviews generated every minute. Or they need to recruit additional employees to monitor the quality of product review. Compared with these techniques, our suggestion to practitioners does not require a significant amount of investment to the existing product review system. The only thing they need to do is to encourage the product review authors to respond to the consumers’ comments in time. This method can be easily deployed by adding a notification message in the product review composing interface window or using the existing email system to send the courtesy email for notifying product review authors about the benefits of responding to the consumers’ comments.

## 5.3. Limitation and future research

Despite the contributions to researchers and practitioners, this study sufers limitations that serve as a suggestion for future research. First, we did not obtain the objective measures of consumers’ afect and their available cognitive information processing resource when reading the product review. Objective data of consumers’ afect and the available cognitive information processing resource can be measured by the neurophysiological method $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . g . , } }$ , EEG and fMRI) [79]. However, applying the techniques would bring significant challenges to violating the legitimacy and relevancy of the research method. Most of the widely adopted neurophysiological methods are obtrusive [80]. In this regard, participants may not behave like a normal online shopping with the devices on (or around) their head. Nevertheless, we do acknowledge the lack of examining the objective data of consumers’ afect and cognitive information processing resource is a limitation of this study. Future scholars could use the unobtrusive method to collect data.

Second, we did not examine the impact of the presentation format of product reviews in this paper, such as video-based and photo-based reviews. Prior scholars found that diferent presentation formats of product review content could result in varying impacts on evaluation toward a product review [81]. It is reasonable to infer that the product review presentation format has an impact on consumers’ afect and cognitive processing style [82]. In this study, we controlled the presentation format of a product review in both Studies 1 and 2. Future studies could extend the investigation scope by adopting another type of product review presentation format, such as video-based or photobased product reviews, with the established voting outcomes of the product review helpfulness and the commenting records from consumers. It will be very promising, if such a kind of study can be built upon the current paper. The potential outcomes will be fruitful for contributing our knowledge base of product review and consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness in the e-commerce environ ment.

Third, we did not have a similar number of female participants and male participants in Study 1. Even though the gender ratio has no significant diference across the treatment groups in the experiment, the male-skewed sample set may restrict the generalizability of the research findings to the female cohorts. In future studies, we will endeavor to have balanced female and male participants in the experiment.

Fourth, we did not examine other important product review’s dispositions, such as review quality, and their impact on the consumers’ evaluation of product review helpfulness. In this paper, we dig deeper to understand the underlying mechanism of how consumers process product review length to assess the product review helpfulness. Thus, the investigation of other product reviews’ disposition is out of the scope of this paper. Nevertheless, we do acknowledge it as a limitation of this paper and we will plan to investigate it in future research.

## 6. Conclusion

Electronic commerce has grown and continues to grow exponentially. Following the progression of global economic integration, electronic commerce has become a new economic growth point for many countries. Hence, it is essential to gain a better understanding of how online product reviews are best utilized during the decision making process. The helpfulness of product review, as one of the most important indicators of online commercial website’s business performance, reflects the extent to which consumers perceive the review in facilitating judgment or purchase decisions. A helpful product review significantly influences consumer purchase decisions. The present study is a modest step toward developing a theoretically sound interpretation for product review helpfulness. The implications will be beneficial to both scholars and practitioners.
