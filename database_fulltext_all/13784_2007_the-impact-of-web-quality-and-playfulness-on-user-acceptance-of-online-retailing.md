# The impact of Web quality and playfulness on user acceptance of online retailing

Tony Ahn <sup>a,\*</sup>, Seewon Ryu <sup>b</sup>, Ingoo Han

<sup>a</sup> Graduate School of Management, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, 207-43 Cheongryangri-Dong, Dongdaemun-Gu, Seoul 130-012, Republic of Korea

<sup>b</sup> Department of Healthcare Management, Inje Institute of Advanced Studies, Republic of Korea

Received 11 August 2005; received in revised form 15 November 2006; accepted 26 December 2006 Available online 3 March 2007

## Abstract

We investigated the effect of playfulness on user acceptance of online retailing and tested the relationship between Web quality factors and user acceptance behavior. A survey of 942 users of Web-based online retailing was conducted to test our model.

The results showed that playfulness plays an important role in enhancing user attitude and behavioral intention to use a site. We also found that Web quality, categorized into system, information, and service quality, had a significant impact on the perceived ease of use, playfulness, and usefulness, and consequently, that it encouraged website use in the context of online retailing

Our study thus provided a balanced and integrative framework for determining Web quality. It enhanced our knowledge of the effect of playfulness, which should help Web practitioners and researchers better understand user behavior in Web-based online retailing.

Keywords: Playfulness; Web quality; Service quality; Technology acceptance model; Online retailing

## 1. Introduction

Online retailing, or e-tailing, is an Internet-based business which delivers products and services over the Web: a storefront in cyberspace, where customers can shop from their home and merchants can offer merchandise and services for less overhead than a physical storefront. The Internet is a communication channel that is not bounded by time or geography. Online retailing supports a variety of alternative approaches to retailing that extend beyond store- or catalog-based models of selling. Online retailing is not only an IS but also a full representation of a store to the customer.

To the online retailing where customer retention is a key success factors, it is necessary to develop effective Web presence and back-office operations. An organization with a poor Website or ineffective services may project a poor image and weaken the organization’s position. It is, thus, critical to determine the factors that enhance user’s attitude and their Website use. Individuals would visit a site if it provided high quality service at low price with fast delivery of goods and/or enhanced their job performance. A satisfied customer results when a purchase seems as economical but there is also satisfaction when the experience was pleasurable. Studies have shown that the likelihood of a repeat visit to a Website is enhanced when the visitor has found the visit enjoyable [16]. In the computer-mediated environment, distinction between work and play are becoming less clear and computer-fostered playfulness can enhance job productivity. Accordingly, we examined the playfulness and its quality antecedents as the intrinsic factors of user acceptance of the technology in online retailing.

The TAM model postulated that perceived ease of use and usefulness results in increased system use; but although it has been successfully used in studying Internet related technologies, these two factors have not been found to be enough to explain Web users’ motives. Among the additions proposed, playfulness has been regarded as a factor that can increase user acceptance [15,17]. Researchers have studied playfulness as either an individual trait or a state of mind [29]. A trait tends to be stable but slowly changes over time while a state of mind represents a short-lived cognitive experience felt by an individual. Individuals who ranked high in playfulness seem to seek flow experience and have positive attitude towards a computer-mediated web environment. Playfulness may be more important because customers obtain pleasure in using the system as well as in purchasing the needed product.

Web practitioners are also concerned with Web quality factors that appear to have an impact on user acceptance. That is because Web quality factors are under the control of the company and are ways of influencing user beliefs and behavioral intention. Some researchers have discussed peripheral aspects of Web quality in a descriptive manner without rigorously delineating their core dimensions or providing tested scales (e.g., Ref. [10]) while others focused on evaluation or success factors of Websites (e.g., Refs. [1,5]). The technology-focused approach sees a Website as an information system and focuses on system and information quality, while a service-focused approach sees a Website as a service provider and includes service quality. But though Web quality measures may change according to research objectives or scope, the main category is in a balanced stream of system, information, and service quality. System quality is an engineeringoriented performance characteristics (rating interface design, functionality, response time, etc.) Information quality has both engineering and operational characteristics (rating data format, completeness, and timeliness, etc). Service quality refers to availability of communication mechanisms for accepting consumer complaints and timely resolution of them with responsiveness, assurance, and follow up services (i.e., how well a delivered service level matches customer expectations).

Many studies have investigated the relationship between Web quality and user acceptance. However, most studies have given little or no attention to service quality factors. We therefore decided to explore measures of Web quality from both a technologyand service-focused viewpoint. We first tested the validity of playfulness as an intrinsic belief of user acceptance and intention to use a site. Then we explored Web quality measures and tested the relationship between these measures and user beliefs. Online retailing, such as retailers of Internet shopping malls were chosen as the domain. The subjects were the users of online retailing systems who bought products through them.

## 2. Theoretical model and hypotheses

The model of our study was based on the original TAM [6] as extended by Moon and Kim [17]. It included three Web quality constructs for online retailing (system, information, and service quality) as antecedents of user beliefs. Fig. 1 shows our research model which thus extended TAM by including playfulness and Web quality.

## 2.1. Playfulness as a factor in user acceptance

TAM is a well-accepted intention model for predicting and explaining IT usage. However, it has been criticized for ignoring social influence and individual characteristics. Venkatesh and Davis [28] extended it to include variables relevant to social influence and the cognitive instrumental process. Others suggested the considerations of social cultures [26], the nature and specific influences of technology and usagecontext [7]. Intermediate variables have also been added to TAM, including confirmation, satisfaction, loyalty incentives [3], perceived control, concentration, cognitive absorption and fashion involvement [23], enjoyment, and playfulness.

Webster and Martocchio defined Micro-computer playfulness (MCP) as a situation-specific individual characteristic that represented intellectual or cognitive playfulness: an individual’s tendency to interact spontaneously, inventively, and imaginatively with computers. Later studies demonstrated that it was a function of both individual traits and psychological states [30]. Playfulness in recent studies has usually implied a belief that interacting would result in enjoyment or cognitive absorption (including concentration, and curiosity). Teo et al. [27] employed a Web-based survey and showed that respondents used the Internet because of high level of perceived usefulness and enjoyment. Heijden [9] also demonstrated that perceived enjoyment positively influenced the attitude and intention to use a portal site. Lin et al. showed in their extended ECT model that playfulness and satisfaction contributed significantly to the users’ intent to reuse Websites. Shang et al. demonstrated that fashion and a cognitive absorption experiences on the Web were more important than their extrinsic factors in explaining online behavior. Moon and Kim defined three dimensions of perceived playfulness: concentration (the extent to which a user perceived that his or her attention was focused), curiosity (the extent to which the user was inquisitive about the interaction), and enjoyment (the extent to which the user found the interaction fun or interesting). They showed that individuals who had a more positive belief of playfulness in Websites viewed its interactions more positively than those who did not.

A satisfied customer not only comes from an extrinsic reward of purchasing products or services but also from personal and emotional reward from purchasing-derived pleasure. Liu and Arnett showed that playfulness was directly related to Website success of electronic commerce. Jarvenpaa and Todd [11] posited that a satisfied customer comes from both the extrinsic reward of purchasing products or services but also from the personal and emotional reward of purchasing-derived pleasure. These results suggest that shopping produces both hedonic and utilitarian outcomes. We therefore proposed the following hypotheses.

Hypothesis 1. There is a positive relationship between playfulness and intention to use online retailing.

Hypothesis 2. There is a positive relationship between playfulness and attitude toward use of online retailing.

## 2.2. Web quality and user acceptance of online retailing

Although studies have suggested the importance of the antecedents to user beliefs, there is no clear pattern of researchers’ choice of external variables in the context of online retailing. Some of the variables considered have been search mechanism, security, network speed, access cost while others have proposed user’s demographics or psychological factors such as positive challenge, Web skill, experience and education, confirmation, cognitive absorption and fashion involvement. The quality of the Website affects the user view of the site because it is the portal through which the transactions are conducted.

Many studies have explored the relationship between Web quality and user acceptance in the Web context. Most of them showed that Web quality had a positive impact on user beliefs of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Although they showed that some quality factors had a positive effect on site acceptance, few took service quality into consideration. Shih [24] demonstrated that service quality significantly determined consumer willingness to use online retailing. Chung and Tan [4] suggested that cognitive aspects, Website characteristics, and motivation for searching were the antecedents of perceived playfulness and that they had a dominant role in influencing a user’s feeling of playfulness.

## 2.2.1. System quality

System quality depends on the users’ needs, as defined during the system’s analysis and development. It is an important factor in user satisfaction with a web purchase: appearance, technical adequacy, delay, navigation, security, and privacy are important factors. As Web technologies have improved, more sophisticated and user-familiar solutions have been added, including audio-visual support, user customization, and virtual reality.

The system-oriented view suggests that the impediments to consumer acceptance are technology-based. High level of system quality may provide users with more convenience, privacy, and faster responses. Lederer et al. [13] and Liao and Cheung [14] showed that such system capabilities had a positive impact on perceived ease of use and usefulness of Website. Koufaris [12] showed that the use of value-added search mechanisms could make the shopping experience more fulfilling and enjoyable. Thus, we hypothesized the following.

Hypothesis 3. System quality has a positive effect on user beliefs in acceptance of online retailing.

By breaking user belief into playfulness, perceived ease of use, and usefulness, this hypothesis can be restated as follows.

Hypothesis 3a. System quality has a positive effect on the user’s perception of playfulness in online retailing.

Hypothesis 3b. System quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived ease of use of online retailing.

Hypothesis 3c. System quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived usefulness of online retailing.

## 2.2.2. Information quality

The type, level of detail, and variety of information are usually determined at the system design and development phase while the timeliness, accuracy, and reliability result from the system operations. Srinivasan [25] chose ‘‘report content and form’’ as a measurement of user perceived effectiveness of the system, content included accuracy, relevance, adequacy, and understandability of report contents, while form included quality of format, timeliness of reports, mode of presentation, and sequencing of information. In the Web environment, the information is related not only to the report but also to user interpretation of it. The most frequently used measures are content and content quality [22]. Web technologies today provide usercustomized or multi-media contents which are interactive and easy to understand.

Studies have shown that information quality has a positive impact on perceived ease of use and usefulness of a Website. Lederer et al. found that information quality had a significant relationship with perceived usefulness. Jarvenpaa and Todd showed that positive comments from customers were related to the clarity and visual appeal of information on a product, service, or shop. Koufaris suggested considering two types of information: non-value-added and value-added. He showed that more enriched and satisfying shopping information could be interesting and helpful. High levels of information quality (various, complete, detailed, accurate, timely, relevant, and reliable) were also likely to provide users with convenience, enhanced enjoyment, and better purchase decisions. Thus, we proposed the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 4. Information quality has a positive effect on user beliefs in acceptance of online retailing.

Again, this can be broken into following three hypotheses.

Hypothesis 4a. Information quality has a positive effect on the user’s perception of playfulness in online retailing.

Hypothesis 4b. Information quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived ease of use of online retailing.

Hypothesis 4c. Information quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived usefulness of online retailing.

## 2.2.3. Service quality

Service quality for a Web-based enterprise depends on its provision of multiple communication mechanisms for accepting user complaints and their timely resolution; it also involves assisting users in being effective, suggesting complementary products or service, and jointly solving problems. The measures of service quality are tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy [18,20,21]. Pitt et al. analyzed IS service quality using SERVQUAL of Parasuraman et al. Myers et al. discussed the importance of service quality in end-user computing and a decentralized environment. They argued that there is a danger that IS researchers will incorrectly measure IS effectiveness if they do not include a measure of IS service quality in their package.

In the Web environment, the service concept should be extended from IS support for internal users to company support for external users. Barnes and Vidgen [2] applied SERVQUAL and IS SERVQUAL of Pitt et al. and built WEBQUAL, a measure composed of 10 dimensions: aesthetics, navigation, reliability, competence, responsiveness, access, credibility, security, communication, and understanding the individual. It seems to provide an integrative concept and viable measures of service qualities for e-commerce site.

However, there is still a need to consider other values of service from the consumer’s viewpoint. Service quality is likely to be more important for online retailing, because it should provide all the services in the purchasing process (finding, ordering, and delivering the products). A few studies have explored service quality as success factors of Websites, but there are fewer that have tested the relationship between service quality and technology acceptance behaviors. Thus, we posited the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 5. Service quality has a positive effect on user beliefs in technology acceptance of online retailing.

Once more, this may be broken into the following.

Hypothesis 5a. Service quality has a positive effect on the user’s perception of playfulness in online retailing.

Hypothesis 5b. Service quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived ease of use of online retailing.

Hypothesis 5c. Service quality has a positive effect on the user’s perceived usefulness of online retailing.

## 3. Research methodology

An online Web-based survey was the predominant measuring device used in our study. The unit of analysis was an individual user of an online retailing system, and the population consisted of individuals who use it for purchasing items. For all variables in our study we used multiple item (7-point, Likert-type) scales. A questionnaire was developed to measure the relevant constructs.

## 3.1. Measurement development

Measure items for the research constructs were derived from prior studies, a number of which had been developed and validated for measuring Web quality, etc. We selected the most commonly used items for perceived ease of use, usefulness, attitude toward use, and behavioral intention to use. These constructs demonstrated substantial reliability and internal consistency. Although numerous studies have suggested the constructs of playfulness in computer interactions, they have different measurement techniques and there has been no attempt at unification. A review of the existing flow studies in computer-mediated environments also showed ambiguities in the conceptualization of playfulness measures.

We defined playfulness as a short-term systemspecific trait or state, because an individual can feel more or less playful at various times during his or her visit to an online retailing system. To be consistent with a prior study, we adopted Moon and Kim’s nine items for the playfulness construct (categorized into curiosity, enjoyment, and concentration). The variables were adopted following studies of Chung and Tan and Lin et al.

The process we used to define the Web quality variables was:

1. We selected items from Aladwani and Palvia, Barnes and Vidgen, Jarvenpaa and Todd, and Palmer [19] removing overlapping and redundant ones—this resulted in 54 items.

2. Four IS experts were asked to evaluate these items and make changes to eliminate any repetitive or technical/non-user oriented items, and sub-attributes of higher attributes. After three evaluation rounds, 21 Web quality attributes remained.

3. The experts deleted items, such as preparation to answer, timely feedback, personal interest, politeness, business knowledge, user customization, club and community, search functionality, and encryption. After this, the ideas from most of the key items could be integrated into one of the system, information, or service constructs.

4. Prior to administering the survey, a pilot test was conducted among 65 graduate students and MIS professors to pre-test for content and readability.

The results of this test indicated that some changes to descriptions were needed. The authors therefore reworked the descriptions to clarify the meaning of each item. The resulting questionnaire then consisted of 54 items measuring the eight latent variables. The constructs and their measures are presented in Table 1, and all items of the questionnaire are shown in Appendix A. Self-reported measures rather than direct observations were used to determine the value of each construct, because they are more convenient to obtain and free of specific targets, contexts, or time frames.

## 3.2. The Web-based survey

In order to target online users, a Web-based survey was employed. Online surveys have several advantages over traditional paper-based mail-in-surveys: (1) the sample is not restricted to a single or local geographical location, (2) lower costs accrue, and (3) faster responses are likely. We posted the banner for our survey at various portal sites, Internet shopping malls, and various online retailing systems. We also placed an advertisement which offered a prize to make the try to improve the number of high-quality responses.

The questionnaire consisted of an instruction page that opened a separate Web browser window containing the items to be assessed. Each respondent was asked to provide the name of an online retailing system that he or she used recently for retailing that was the representative site he or she selected for answering the questionnaire. The respondents were then asked to answer all questions based on their experience when using that particular system. Respondents were asked answers each of the questions using a 1–7 Likert-type scale with an anchor of 1 for ‘‘strongly disagree’’ to 7 for ‘‘strongly agree.’’

In total, 942 responses were gathered for the first 2 weeks of June 2005. Twenty-six winners were selected in our draw, they were given gift certificates. There was no missing data in the sample because participants could not submit their response unless it was complete. The responses were placed into an MS Access<sup>TM</sup> database, filtered to check for duplicates, and converted into a form usable in LISREL. Fifty-nine percent of the respondents were female, and most respondents were in their 20s or 30s and had more than 2 years of experience with the Web. More than seventy percent used the online retailing system at home although their jobs were ranged from students to clerks, specialists, technicians, and housewives. More than 80% of the participants had previously made a purchase through online retailing; this suggested that the information we collected represented the views of ‘‘buyers’’ rather than ‘‘browsers.’’ Detailed descriptive demographics of the respondents are shown in Table 2.

## 4. Results

The research model was analyzed using structural equation modeling (SEM), supported by LISREL 8.12 software. SEM can estimate a series of separate but interdependent multiple regression equations simultaneously. It consists of a number of statistical techniques that can be used in various theoretical models. Model estimation was performed using the maximum likelihood approach. Data analysis proceeded in two stages: the measurement model was first examined for validating and refining the research instrument, after which an analysis of SEM was made for finding the associations of our model.

## 4.2. Structural model

## 4.2.1. Model fitness

After assessing the reliability and validity, we tested the overall fit of our path model, which evaluates the correspondence of the actual or observed input matrix with that predicted from our proposed model. The summary of the overall fit indices of our research model is shown in Table 6. The GFI surpassed the recommended value of 0.80 and other fit indices indicated a reasonable fit of mode and data.

We compared the hypothesized model with that of the original TAM. The proposed model excelled on $\chi ^ { 2 } /$ d.f., NNFI, PNFI, and PGFI, while the original TAM was better on GFI, AGFI, and RMSR. The fit indices showed reasonable fit values with no substantive differences. In our research, the path coefficients and $R ^ { 2 }$ values indicated a very good fit for all constructs. Therefore, we were assured that the path diagram for our research model was an adequate representation of the entire set of causal relationships.

## 4.3. Discussion

Our study sought to achieve two objectives; to investigate the presence of playfulness as one of the intrinsic user beliefs of technology acceptance and to examine the effects of Web quality measures, with a focus on service quality, on a user’s intention to utilize Internet-based retailing.

Our results showed that user playfulness had a significant positive effect on individual attitude and behavioral intention to use online retailing. It implied that users were more likely to continue to use an online retailer when they felt that it was playful and that they would move their loyalty to more enriching and enjoyable Websites. Improvement in customer relations could be achieved by:

\- incorporating a value-added search mechanism, dynamic navigation, and user-customized Web page;

\- providing price comparison, user-customized information, and multi-media contents;

\- adding question and answering facilities using a bulletin board system, customized navigation to purchasing, and e-mailing at each purchasing stage.

Our findings supported the viewpoint that an online retailing is not merely an IS, but is also a service provider that maintains full functionality of the customer purchasing process. They can enhance user service through e-Mail or bulletin board during customers’ electronic purchasing activities where orders and charges are placed electronically. They can apply an incentive program to enhance user intention to revisit their online retailing. Service may be most important during the after-sales phase where timely delivery, follow up service, and problem resolution are required.

Our results have implications to managers about investing time and resources when designing and operating Websites. If customers find an online retailing to be too difficult to navigate and use in making transactions, they will move to competitor sites.

Practitioners should also be aware of the offline features of online retailing. The users of online retailing consider the Website not merely as an information system but also as a virtual store that provides full service will be more likely to retain customer loyalty.

## 5. Conclusion

We examined playfulness as an aspect of a consumer’s reason to return to an online retailing website, and found how user beliefs were related to the Web quality features. The results showed that customers’ intention to visit an online retailing site depended on its playfulness as well as two beliefs: perceived ease of use and usefulness. Our results corroborated the findings of previous work on the need for playfulness in both Internet systems and the marketing arena. Online customers are not purely utilitarian, valuing only efficiency. They also enjoy shopping online enough to make them return. We also found integrative constructs as antecedents to user satisfaction: service, system, and information quality. These constructs help individuals accomplish their tasks. Organizations can address these three through improved Website design, Web content management, and process integration. Thus, successful online retailing must provide a high level of system, information, and service quality, and consequently, entices customers to revisit their Website.

Our study introduced a balanced approach to online retailing both as an end-user IS and a service provider. It also demonstrated that the online customer was regarded as both a computer user and an online shopper. Accordingly, we have provided an integrative approach to categorizing and evaluating Web qualities from both technology and service point of views. In terms of content, the items are relatively balanced and integration of items involving Website system, information, and service quality. It also reconfirms playfulness as one of the salient beliefs that have a positive effect on user attitude and intention to use.

Practitioners can use these results as guidelines in Website development, daily operations, and customer support processes in online retailing. The measures of Web quality constructs can be a useful diagnostic tool for assessing the perceived quality of an organization’s e-commerce capability. It may be particularly powerful where used to provide a benchmark against competitors’ Websites and can also be applied longitudinally to evaluate the impact of development activities on online retailing.

There are, of course, limitations in our study. Although we tested playfulness and Web quality as important constructs for user acceptance of online retailing, other factors may play an important role in explaining user acceptance behavior, such as loyalty incentives, perceived control, technology experience and trust. Also, in our study all external variables were based on online quality measures. As online retailing is a full representation of a store that supports the all stages of the consumer buying process (information finding, ordering, delivery, and post-purchase service), the effect of the quality of back-office operations may also become a major factor. Finally, the generalization of the results is limited by the context of online retailing in Korea: all of the observations were from Korea and online retailing in other countries may not resemble those in Korea.
