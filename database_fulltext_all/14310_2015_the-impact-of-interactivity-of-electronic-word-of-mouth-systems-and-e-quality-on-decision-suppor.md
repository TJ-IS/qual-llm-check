## Abstract

This study aims to investigate the impact of the interactivity of electronic word-of-mouth (EWOM) systems and E-Quality on decision support satisfaction. In order to develop hypotheses, we adopted three theoretical lenses: the cognition-to-action loyalty framework, the interactivity theory, and the E-Quality model. The model and measurements were based on literature. A survey was conducted to validate the research model. The findings illustrate that positively influence the website quality. E-Quality was found to have a significant impact on decision support satisfaction. Several theoretical contributions and managerial implications are discussed.

Keywords: Electronic word-of-mouth, e-loyalty, e-quality, customer decision support

## INTRODUCTION

The advent of the Internet has revolutionized word of mouth activity [1]. Consumers now use blogs, search engines, internet communities, social media, consumer review systems and online instant messenger services to gather and disseminate product information. These tools are the basis for a new form of word of mouth, known as electronic word of mouth or EWOM. These tools are used for information searching, decision-making support, and are critical tools for the formation of brand images [2, 3].

One of the best examples of EWOM systems, and indeed a prototypical EWOM system, is the customer review system. Customers can post text-based comments, insert video reviews, and even respond to other customers‟ opinions on the product or service in question through EWOM systems. In this brave new world of faceless ecommerce, today‟s online shopper can complement and complain any time of the day [4]. The emergence of these EWOM channels has not only changed the way customers interact with other customers; it has also changed the way that businesses engage the customers as well as other businesses [5].

Hennig-Thurau et al. demonstrated that the information provided by online forums is very influential [6]. This is substantiated by market research. For example, eMarketer found that 61% of consumers check online forums, consumer blogs and other sources for online customer reviews before they purchase products [7]. INFOGROUP found that 80% of potential online

consumers who plan to purchase a product turn to online consumer reviews before they make a purchase decision [8]. These studies and others coupled with anecdotal insight are the reason that businesses turn to EWOM. The simple reason is that customers trust consumer-produced information [9].

Researchers have recognized the growing importance of EWOM in e-business. For example, there have been several studies on EWOM participation involving customer motives (e.g., [5, 10]). Similarly, there have been studies conducted on the relationship between trust and EWOM (e.g., [11, 12]) as well as how EWOM information influences consumers (e.g., [9, 13-16]).

Most of these studies deal with the influence of EWOM as an information source for customers, or focus on how to motivate customers to participate in the process. However, little is known about the effect of the interactivity of EWOM systems and quality of commercial websites. This is particularly true of the way that the EWOM facilitates the decision-making process in terms of decision support. In essence, EWOM systems facilitate the decision-making process and positively impact customer satisfaction.

This issue is closely related to our research questions: (1) what attributes of the EWOM system influence customer decision support satisfaction in an online purchase? And, (2) how does the interactivity of EWOM systems influence overall e-business quality?

To answer these questions, we adopted three theoretical lenses: the cognition-to-action loyalty framework, the interactivity theory, and the e-business quality (E-Quality) model. The cognition-to-action loyalty framework is used to investigate how the EWOM system in conjunction with the E-Quality of e-marketplace site leads to customers‟ decision support satisfaction, which in turn leads to E-Loyalty.

This study is expected to contribute to the theoretical and applied literature in two ways. Firstly, this study attempts to identify the attributes of EWOM systems that influence customer decision support satisfaction. Secondly, we want to determine the role of EWOM systems in overall ebusiness quality. In our review of the literature we could not find any evidence on the role that EWOM systems play in delivering overall e-business quality.

The rest of this article is structured as follows: We first introduce the previous studies on EWOM. In the next section, we present the theoretical development process and hypotheses based on the interactivity theory and the E-Quality model literature. Subsequently, methodology and analysis process is presented. Lastly, we conclude with the implications of our findings.

## LITERATURE REVIEW ON ELECTRONIC WORD OF MOUTH

EWOM (Electronic Word of Mouth) is defined as "any positive or negative statement made by potential, actual, or former customers about a product or company, which is made available to a multitude of people and institutions via the Internet" [10]. There are two important research streams in the EWOM literature. A majority of EWOM studies focus on the consequences of EWOM, such as revenue growth, customer retention, and promotion of customer‟s purchase intention [2, 9, 11, 14-18]. The other studies investigate the motivation and process concerning how EWOM is created and used by customers [5, 6, 10, 19, 20].

According to previous studies, EWOM positively influences business performance and customer purchase behavior [11, 15]. Chevalier and Mayzlin [13] illustrate that Amazon had far more positive EWOM in both quantity and quality than Barnes and Noble did, and the more positive EWOM is, the greater the book sales are. Pavlou and Dimoka [17] analyzed textoriented EWOM and found that EWOM in the form of text, as opposed to simple customer ratings, constructs more trust and creates price premiums on the products. Bickart and Shindler [21] found that customers paid more attention to the information provided by other customers than that of salespersons or marketers. That is, the information other customers provide is more appealing and meaningful to potential customers than that provided by a marketer.

Dellarocas [1] stressed the importance of EWOM interactions. He found that trust problems are typically caused by a lack of interaction in e-commerce transactions, as well as a lack of pathways that allow customers the opportunity to investigate the product being offered. Gruen et al. [22] viewed EWOM as a kind of know-how exchange among customers where development depends on opportunity, motivation, and the ability of customers.

Previous studies also address the motivation of customers who participate in EWOM activities. Hennig-Thurau and Walsh [10] investigated customer motives for reading online word of mouth and the resulting effects. They found that the primary motivations for reading reviews were: diminution of risk, decreasing search time, reduction of purchase regret, the discovery of new products, and increasing social status among others.

Hennig-Thurau et al. [6] also examined the motivation to post EWOM on the internet and found that consumers‟ desire for social interaction, economic incentives, and increase in social status were the major factors. Cheung and Lee [5] emphasized intrinsic motivations. They found that the important motivators for participating in EWOM activities were egoism, collectivism, altruism, principlism, and efficacy.

There is another research stream that investigates the effect of positive or negative EWOM on customer behavior. Chatterjee [2] illustrates that negative consumer review has a critical influence on consumer trust and purchase intention at the internet shopping mall. Moreover, in the case of consumers who prefer to purchase at a lower cost, the negative word-of-mouth impact is even greater.

To summarize, most studies on EWOM show how EWOM influences customer behavior and how it benefits online shopping malls. However, the importance of the EWOM system itself is overlooked. For example, text based EWOM systems and video embedded EWOM systems could have different impacts on customer purchase decision and business performance. The objective of this study is to fill this gap.

## HYPOTHESES DEVELOPMENT

In order to investigate the impact of the interactivity of EWOM systems and E-Quality of a website on decision support satisfaction (see Figure 1), Oliver‟s cognition-to-action loyalty framework is adopted as an overarching theory [23, 24]. Oliver [23, 24] argues that consumers build loyalty toward a brand cognitively first, then affectively, next conatively, and finally behaviorally. Quality of website and its attached services (e.g., EWOM systems) are perceived and evaluated by customers [25]. This process involves how customers think about the website and its attached services [26]. So, adopting interactivity theory and E-Quality is appropriate to represent the cognitive aspect of loyalty phases.

The cognitive phase is critically important in forming emotion toward the website that provides products and services [26]. An online shopping website triggers purchasing behavior by providing relevant stimulations. Ultimately, these stimulations are related to decision support. When decision support reflects customers‟ needs and preferences, customers feel satisfaction with this support [27]. Hence, adopting the construct, decision support satisfaction is useful in describing the emotional phase of the loyalty framework.

And lastly, E-Loyalty is employed to illustrate the conative phase of loyalty. Based on this theoretical framework we explore the relationships of interactivity of EWOM systems, E-Quality, decision support satisfaction, and E-Loyalty by proposing four research hypotheses.

## Interactivity

In this study we define interactivity as the extent to which interaction between one customer and another is facilitated by EWOM systems. Interaction is regarded as one of the most important advantages of the Internet [28, 29]. EWOM systems provide a channel through which customers experience a high level of interactivity on online shopping websites.

In this study we investigate the interactivity of EWOM systems through the lens of the general conceptualization of interactivity, since EWOM systems are technological media through which EWOM information is delivered and places where interpersonal services are provided by sellers or site managers. For instance, technological factors, such as user interface and response speed of EWOM systems, influence a customer‟s online experience. In addition, a seller‟s or site manager‟s active responses to a customer‟s request in EWOM systems are also an important factor in providing customers with successful shopping experiences.

From the general perspective, interactivity is formed with four facets: reciprocity, responsiveness, nonverbal information, and speed of response [30]. Reciprocity refers to consumers‟ opportunity to participate jointly in conversing with firms as opposed to hearing a monologue from them [30]. The more reciprocal the communication between a visitor and website owner is, the more the site can respond to the particular needs of the visitor [31]. A fundamental premise of EWOM systems is the allowance of customers‟ participation. However, some online shopping sites delete negative customer reviews since they are afraid of their negative impact on sales. Thus, reciprocity is used as a concept for EWOM systems that keep EWOM information untouched and presented as it is posted.

Responsiveness is referred to as the extent to which a customer can obtain the information relevant to her query [32]. For example, if EWOM information in websites lacks relevance and is insincere, simple, and repetitive, EWOM responsiveness is low. Low responsiveness mitigates the interactivity of a website and leads to low use satisfaction [30]. For that reason, online shopping sites strive to present better EWOM information to customers by using filtering or recommendation mechanisms.

Nonverbal information refers to the use of multiple channels for communicating information [30]. An example is to use images, video clips, or sound to deliver information, rather than text alone. Images or video clips may add richness to the established text, resulting in greater information. This is especially true for products of e-commerce sites which are heterogeneous or require direct experience. Ramirez and Burgoon [33] demonstrate that increases in media richness result in reduced uncertainty, which in turn increases customer satisfaction. Recently, more websites allow customer ratings to extend beyond text to include video clips filmed by customers or uploaded photographs.

Speed of response refers to how fast the response is. Interactivity is achieved when users are provided with immediate feedback and perceive that a mediated environment is modified based on their input [34]. Speed of response has been considered by many researchers to be an aspect of interactivity [35, 36]. Speed of response is high when replies from other customers or on-line retailers are added quickly after a customer posts a compliment or statement of dissatisfaction with a product or service on EWOM systems.

## Interactivity of EWOM Systems and Decision Support Satisfaction

Decision support satisfaction is defined in terms of the capability of an information system to assist in decision-making and better performance of the user‟s jobs [37]. Interactivity of websites is an important antecedent to customer satisfaction with web-based consumer decision support systems that promote customers‟ system use [27]. According to previous studies that investigate the role of interactivity of websites, a high level of interactivity of a website results in customer enjoyment and increases intention to purchase [38].

An EWOM system is an important channel through which customers communicate with other customers and the seller. Interactive EWOM systems make information more relevant and credible by increasing the participation of previous customers. Besides, customers are likely to be satisfied with interactive online shopping sites since they can experience flow status cognitively and affectively [39, 40]. Hence, it can be assumed that interactivity of website positively influences customers‟ overall satisfaction through customer involvement and it also helps customer decision making by providing information through quality communications. Thus, we posit the following hypothesis:

H1: Interactivity of EWOM systems has a positive influence on customer decision support satisfaction.

## E-Quality

Previous studies dealing with the quality of e-business websites have mostly focused on the quality of information provided to users, and the way information is both delivered and interacted with by users [41-46]. These studies provide prominent insights into the construction of a successful e-business website. However, they do not encompass all of the characteristics appropriate to current e-business environment.

The current e-business environment consisting of the Internet, web, and hypermedia technologies is highly dynamic and interactive [47]. Such characteristics imply a different set of success factors for new e-businesses and include improved information accuracy, information relevance, and information completeness, along with a user-friendly interface, the overall design of the web, and history maintenance [48, 49].

E-Quality addresses the web usability problem which could not be solved using the previous information quality model, including irrelevant information, disorientation, and cognitive overhead [47]. Kim et al. [47] developed a comprehensive E-Quality model that included information content, information form, and information time as its structure to address the usability problem.

Content dimension deals with the intrinsic information content issues that are geared toward providing users with accurate, relevant, and complete information. Form dimension manages problems related to information presentation that the website provides. And lastly, time dimension deals with the relationship between time and information provision; this helps users make sense of their activities when surfing from website to website. ## Interactivity of EWOM systems and E-Quality

When EWOM systems are interactive, EWOM information could be more relevant to customer needs and more accurate. Reciprocal communications and the seller‟s online presence influence website quality, especially when they are active and strong in the interactive EWOM systems [50, 51]. Besides, according to previous studies, the information provided by EWOM systems is perceived credible and influential by customers [21]. Interactivity of EWOM systems ensures that relevant and undistorted customer reviews and opinions are on the website. Hence, the customers‟ perception of overall quality toward the website can be increased when EWOM systems are reciprocal and responsive.

When the e-marketplace includes nonverbal content, such as review videos, customers can better enjoy visiting the website [52]. In other words, EWOM systems could be regarded as a good source of website playfulness. Increasing the playful factor in the website is positively associated with website quality [53]. Thus, it is assumed that EWOM system interactivity has a positive relationship with E-Quality, which suggests that the general quality of a website, because of its higher EWOM system interactivity, positively influences customers‟ attitude towards using the website.

H2: Interactivity of EWOM systems positively influences the E-Quality of a website.

## E-Quality and Decision Support Satisfaction

According to previous studies, individuals endeavor to lessen the amount of cognitive effort associated with decision-making [54]. The assertion that people are normally willing to adapt to imperfect accuracy in their decisions, in return for a reduction in effort, is well studied [55, 56] and is consistent with the concept of bounded rationality and satisficing [57]. Due to this tradeoff between effort and accuracy, decision makers often satisfice when alternatives are numerous and/or difficult to compare, or when the complexity of the decision environment is high [58]. In the context of web-based consumer purchasing decision systems, it is critical to include an assessment of the degree to which the system supports or reduces the decision making efforts of users [27]. In this sense, it can be assumed that a high level of E-Quality supports customers in the decision-making process.

In addition, previous literature on website quality presents a positive relationship between website quality and user satisfaction [45, 46, 59]. For instance, Zviran et al. [60] demonstrate that a higher degree of website quality and usability is associated with a higher degree of perceived user satisfaction. Since a high level of website quality indicates quality provision and presentation of information, when a customer feels high E-Quality from a website, she is likely to be satisfied with diverse purchase decision supports on the website.

H3: E-Quality positively influences customer decision support satisfaction during shopping on a website.

## Decision Support Satisfaction and E-Loyalty

E-Loyalty refers to customers‟ favorable attitude towards an e-business website, which leads to repetitive visits and repurchase behavior, including both attitudinal and behavioral aspects [61]. The concept of E-Loyalty extends the idea of offline brand loyalty to online consumer behavior [62]. In the e-marketplace, decision support is critical because a customer cannot judge the quality of a product directly. Therefore, the customers‟ perception that they are taken care of by the seller (i.e., the website) in terms of purchase decision is critical in developing revisit intention.

The literature on E-Loyalty indicates a positive association between satisfaction and E-Loyalty [61, 63]. Garrity et al. [27] demonstrate that decision support satisfaction plays an important role in web-based information system success. We extend this idea by asserting that customers high decision support satisfaction with a website leads to high loyalty to the website.

H4: Customer decision support satisfaction is positively associated with their E-Loyalty.

## Measurement Development

Johnson et al. [30] argued that the concept of interactivity is a formative construct constituted by its facets. That is, indicators are defining characteristics of the construct, not manifestations of the construct [64]. Using reflective measurements is a common approach in empirical studies where a latent variable explains the variation in its indicators [65]. On the other hand, under a formative approach, indicators are assumed to affect a latent variable [66]. In this sense, the four first-order factors cause the variation in the second-order construct of interactivity, as shown in Figure 1.

To measure the various constructs, items validated by other studies were adapted. E-Quality as a second-order factor is explained in terms of content, form and time in this study [67]. Decision support satisfaction induces questions as to whether or not EWOM system interactivity and E-Quality are of great value. As for E-Loyalty, we utilized the concept drawn from Anderson & Srinivasan [61, 68] and Zeithaml et al. [61, 68]. Specific details of the questionnaire are provided in Table 2.

A survey questionnaire was developed in English and careful attention to measurement issues was given in order to ensure psychometric validity. ## Data Collection

Data was collected using a paper-based survey in a consumer exhibition in Seoul, Korea. We requested that one consumer organization distribute our paper-based questionnaires during the middle of a lecture, in one section of the convention. The participants were told that the focus of the study was to understand the individual‟s thought process and actual behavior on online shopping sites. Each informant was asked to respond to the questions based on actual experiences with one favorite online shopping site she frequently visited. A 1–5 Likert scale with an anchor of 1 for „„strongly disagree‟‟ to 5 for „„strongly agree‟‟ was used. After finishing the survey, each respondent was offered a gift certificate with a \$5 dollar value.

A total of 327 responses were collected; 313 responses were used for analysis due to the missing value issue. All participants were residents of Seoul, South Korea. Table 3 shows the profile of respondents. Half of the respondents (52%) were customers of three leading e-commerce companies in Korea: gmarket.co.kr, auction.co.kr, and interpark.com.

We collected data in South Korea because the e-commerce environment of South Korea is very well developed, and customer involvement in EWOM systems is prevalent. The Korean ecommerce environment is considered to represent general customer behavior online. Plus, online marketplaces in Korea provide well-built EWOM systems (e.g., video review). This environment was considered to be an appropriate environment for this study and an adequate representation of online markets in other countries.

## DISCUSSION

The main goal of this study is to investigate antecedent factors influencing customer decision support satisfaction with the EWOM systems and its impact on E-loyalty. To achieve this purpose, we adopted four variables from the interactivity theory and three variables from the E-Quality model. This study then empirically examined interactivity of EWOM systems and its impact on E-Quality and decision support satisfaction. Our findings indicate that customers perceptions regarding interactivity of EWOM systems is very influential on their evaluation of an entire website and their level of satisfaction with decision making support.

It is a very different approach from previous studies where EWOM systems were described as a black box. In those studies the outcomes of EWOM, such as the amount of EWOM information and its impact on revenue, received the attention. The characteristic of EWOM information and the systems that support the delivery of information as a communication channel were neglected. This study addresses this important issue from the lens of interactivity. Consequently, the importance of interactivity of EWOM systems is demonstrated as critical antecedents of E-Quality and decision support satisfaction.

The analysis of sub-dimensions of interactivity also gives us important insight. In the hierarchical model of interactivity of EWOM systems, responsiveness shows the highest path coefficient. Because every variable of interactivity of EWOM systems is a formative construct, our results indicate that responsiveness influences interactivity of EWOM systems the most. This finding is consistent with Rafaeli‟s [75] research, which shows responsiveness is the most important element in interactivity. Interestingly, nonverbal information, one of the attributes of interactivity, shows significant relationship with interactivity. This implies that building EWOM systems with visual and auditory factors is very important to customer decision support satisfaction, even when the communication characteristics of EWOM systems have not been focused.

## Theoretical Implications

This study has three major implications for future research. First, the cognition-to-action loyalty

#

framework [24] was extended to the online context in this study, although it was built to explain the formation process of offline brand loyalty. The extension has been done in three ways. First, in the cognitive phase, unlike the offline brand loyalty formation where customers depend on indirect knowledge or on recent experience-based information, customers could experience the website in person. The use of EWOM systems in websites facilitates belief construction.

Secondly, instead of satisfaction and attitude being developed toward a brand on the basis of satisfaction with the product [23], satisfaction with appropriate decision support of EWOM systems and websites is investigated. This study illustrates that EWOM systems and websites with E-Quality help customers enhance their decision making process. Brand loyalty was extended to include E-Loyalty as revisiting behavior.

The second implication is the empirical verification of the impact of E-Quality upon decision support satisfaction in e-commerce. The E-Quality model proposes the role of e-business quality for success in the Internet and hypermedia environment. Especially the factors related to time mentioned in most previous studies [44-46, 60]. In this study, the E-Quality model is shown to explain the impact of e-business quality on the success of a website in the recent Internet environment.

Finally, we have set interactivity of EWOM systems as a new antecedent of the E-Quality model. In the previous e-business literature, EWOM systems have been regarded as one of the functions that websites provide. However, EWOM systems are a unique channel that could enhance interactivity of an entire website. EWOM systems are distinguished from other functions of a website since customers get and share more credible information. As other studies point out, EWOM systems could be considered as a critical success factor in e-business [10]. EWOM systems, therefore, deserve to receive researchers‟ attention as a main construct in ebusiness quality and success.

## Practical Implication

Our findings lend support for the effectiveness of EWOM for better marketing on e-commerce sites. When the four aspects of EWOM systems interactivity (reciprocity, responsiveness, nonverbal information and speed of response) are well managed, users are likely to experience decision support satisfaction with the e-commerce site. This result indicates that e-commerce sites should be encouraged to provide a better EWOM environment for reciprocity and advanced EWOM systems functionality, which enables multiple channel communications as well as quick and proper responses to customer requests. As Rietjens [76] demonstrated, elaborate and sophisticated EWOM systems are necessary in order to retain customers confidence in the customer-to-customer e-business.

The result of this research study also explains why e-commerce companies are eager to develop effective EWOM systems as a reference and knowledge sharing system among customers. Recently, many e-commerce companies are advancing their own EWOM systems and encouraging their customers to post customer reviews with detailed contents. Some companies even give bonus points for doing so. This study suggests a direction to move for the improvement of EWOM systems that allow more objective evaluations of websites and products sold.

Finally, e-commerce sites need to organize EWOM systems with non-arbitrary management and easy access to customers. They need to devise systems or methods through which they can encourage customers to produce relevant EWOM, respond immediately to negative EWOM, and reply properly to after-use comments. In terms of nonverbal information, which may result in group behaviors among customers as the objective reference points and knowledge, ecommerce companies may have to develop new EWOM systems supporting image and video clip registration. This may enable customers to participate in the value creation process and experience more decision support satisfaction, and thus E-Loyalty.

## Limitations and Future Study

This study admittedly has some limitations. First, we did not classify the types of product that respondents mainly purchase online, such as experience goods or search goods. Interactivity could be more influential in the case of purchasing experience goods since the consumer needs more information.

Secondly, we could not precisely evaluate the nonverbal information side of EWOM system interactivity because it is not well-known and not widely-used in e-commerce. Therefore, if experiments are performed after a customer utilizes the nonverbal information, we could see changes in the results. Finally, the Cronbach‟s Alphas of reciprocity and content were 0.698 and 0.639 respectively, which is modestly lower than the suggested value of 0.70.

#

Further research is desired to extend this study to provide a much richer description of the interactivity of EWOM systems, decision support satisfaction and E-Loyalty relationship for different types of goods, such as experience goods and search goods. Further development of appropriate E-Loyalty measurements and precise analysis of the effects of E-Quality will also help clarify the role of interactivity of EWOM systems in e-commerce transactions. Considering a variety of individual characteristic, purchasing motive, and transaction experience in terms of EWOM system use is also needed.

## CONCLUSION

We believe that EWOM has become an important part of the online shopping experience. Understanding the phenomena is essential for the success of electronic commerce systems. This study has helped bring additional insight to the relationship between interactivity of EWOM systems and consumer E-Loyalty. The empirical findings suggest that interactivity of EWOM systems positively affects decision support satisfaction, which is in line with prior EWOM research. At the same time, the results of this study also reveal that interactivity of EWOM systems has a positive association with overall website quality.

These insights not only help researchers better understand that EWOM systems have become an important part of web based information systems, but also provide guidelines for online retailers to better adjust their management strategies for EWOM systems in order to improve customer retention. This study paves the road for further studies. It is hoped that future research further investigates the attributes of EWOM systems.
