# Can digital consumption boost physical consumption? The efect of online music streaming on record sales

College of Business, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), South Korea

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Access-based consumption Ownership-based consumption Music streaming services Physical album sales Digital downloads

## A B S T R A C T

Despite a consensus that sales of physical albums have declined with the increasing prevalence of digital music, this seemingly unequivocal observation may not be true. Physical formats still occupy a fraction of the market, with a noticeable increase in the sales of physical albums in a few countries where digital media is predominant. This study empirically examines the impact of two online music channels (streaming and downloading) on physical album sales in the South Korean music market. By utilizing a unique dataset representing nationwide music sales, our empirical findings suggest that the growth in digital music services can be positively associated with an increase in physical album sales. In particular, this finding is more salient for male, solo, and lesserknown artistes. This empirical evidence implies that burgeoning music streaming services (access-based digital consumption) may lead to another ownership-based physical consumption. Our findings provide useful man agerial implications for practitioners in countries where streaming services are growing.

## 1. Introduction

Technological innovation has transformed the music industry. Physical album sales have been declining consistently over the past two decades, especially after the widespread acceptance of digital music. In other words, music consumption preference has changed from purchasing physical bundles of songs to downloading digitally released single songs [30]. However, from an economic perspective, down loading unbundled digital tracks is not as profitable for the producers and sellers as the bundled album sales. Previous studies showed that widespread digital music file sharing is associated with a decrease in physical album sales [48,54].<sup>1</sup>

More recently, online streaming services such as Spotify have been rapidly replacing legitimate digital downloading since their introduction in the early 2000s. Most music streaming services are based on a monthly unlimited subscription model that enables listeners to access music online but does not grant them permanent ownership [29].<sup>2</sup> In other words, music streaming service users engage in access-based consumption rather than ownership-based consumption [61]. In particular, with a rapidly increasing penetration of smartphones, online music streaming services are gaining popularity [46]. As described in Fig. 1, music streaming services have become one of the largest components of the music industry, accounting for 43% of digital music sales in the US in 2015, while download revenues decreased by 10.5% [4]. Similarly, Apple unveiled a paid streaming service (Apple Music) in 2015, which showed significant growth, as described in Fig. 1.

Before the widespread acceptance of online music streaming platforms, related studies generally echoed that growth in digital music consumption by downloading is negatively associated with physical album sales, mainly due to illegal sharing [48,54]. Digital music downloads are replacing physical album purchases as both belong to the same consumption format—ownership-based [22,40,50]. However, online music streaming services were introduced as a substitute for downloading services with the prevalence of stable and afordable network connections [7]. This shift has brought about a mix of chal lenges and opportunities for practitioners, altering the relationship between digital and physical music consumption. For example, Fig. 2 shows the trend of sales' revenues of long plays and extended plays in the U.S., which has markedly increased since 2008. In 2016, the sales of vinyl records increased by 53% compared to 2015, being the highest in the U.K. since 1991 [32,56]. Sweden—one of the world's most saturated music streaming markets—saw a 6.2% growth in the recorded music market in 2016 as sales of vinyl records increased by 38.7% [68]. These commonly occurring phenomena indicate that, in the era of digital music, an increasing number of consumers may still want to accept physical music formats, thus verifying the possibility of burgeoning online streaming services (i.e., access-based consumption) providing consumers with a higher incentive to seek a particular type of physical consumption.

Drawing on these interesting observations, this study formally investigates the relationship between digital and physical music consumption by diferentiating digital music streaming (access-based consumption) from downloads (ownership-based consumption) according to whether consumers are allowed to own the goods permanently. In fact, the access-based consumption has also become widespread in other types of media such as movies and books; therefore, this study can be considered a pioneering work that presents the paradigm shift from the prior cannibalizing relationship between digital and physical goods to a potentially complementary one. In this research stream, most previous studies investigated the impact of digital goods downloads on physical goods sales [36,48,49,64], but they did not consider the tangibility and ownership of digital goods. Our study elaborates on this research stream, and this is one of the first that empirically diferentiates between consumption channels by the tangibility and ownership of goods using matched data of physical albums and digital consumption acquired from real-world sales.

Our study addresses the following two research questions: i) how do diferent digital music services (streaming versus downloading) impact sales in an ownership-based physical channel? ii) how does the impact vary across artiste types? We evaluate the efect of digital streaming using a unique panel dataset of nationwide sales in South Korea of songs listed in the top 400 in music charts during 2011–2012. This was a transition period from downloading to streaming has actively occurred, and total digital music sales have become close to physical re cord sales in the country. Also, the South Korean music market provides a suitable setting to examine our research questions because digital music sales have risen at the fastest rate in the 2000s, and the declining trend of physical album sales has flattened in the early 2010s. Similar transitions are recently happening in other countries and other fields. Therefore, the empirical evidence provides useful implications for practitioners, where streaming services gained popularity later on.

Our empirical findings are summarized as follows. First, as long as the songs are listed on the top 400, the sales of physical albums are positively associated with both online music streaming counts and the number of digital downloads. Notably, a one-percent-point increase in streaming (or downloading) increased physical CD album sales by about 0.447 (0.029) percent points. Second, appearance on the top 10 in digital charts leads to a significant increase in the sales of physical CD albums. Third, the impact varies across artiste types, as both digital streaming and downloading sales have a positive impact on CD sales for male, solo, or lesser-known artistes, but only streaming exerts a positive impact on CD sales for female, group, or popular artistes. Our finding provide important managerial implications.

## 2. Related literature

## 2.1. Digital channel vs. physical channel

With the rapid growth in digital content, scholars have been increasingly paying attention to the relationship between digital (online) and physical (ofline) channels. Deleersnyder et al. [24] studied how online channels afect traditional print media and found a cannibalization efect. Brynjolfsson et al. [16] explored how online and brick-andmortar retailers compete with each other and found that online channels heavily influence ofline channels for popular products, but less so for niche products. For the music market, a large body of the literature has investigated how the introduction of digital music channels influences physical album sales, with most studies confirming a negative efect (see Danaher et al. [22] for a comprehensive review). Listeners hesitate to purchase physical albums when digital alternatives are available; thus, sales of CDs and DVDs have shrunk substantially [12,34,36,48,49]. From Napster to YouTube, both illegal and legal digital music channels have afected the decline in physical CD album sales [12,40,44,50].

Some scholars have also provided evidence that digital channel growth is not the only factor behind the consistent decline in physical channels' sales. Danaher et al. [21] demonstrated the impact of legitimate digital distribution channels on physical consumption and online piracy in the context of the DVD market and found that the contemporary reduction in legitimate digital distribution channels significantly increases online piracy, but does not afect sales of physical channels. Chen et al. [17] studied how delaying releases for e-books afect sales of print books and found that it only influences e-book sales, not print book sales. Hashim et al. [37] explored the efects of digital channels on physical channels in the movie DVD market and found that, overall, digital channels do not decrease physical channels' sales, except digital rental channels. When it comes to the music industry, Bhattacharjee et al. [11] investigated how online music sharing influences album sales charts and found evidence of ‘pre-purchase sampling, which means that online sharing activity increases before the album sales increase. Kretschmer and Peukert [45] demonstrated that blocking online music videos does not influence physical album sales; however, it afects digital music downloads, since music enthusiasts consume music videos as sample products for digital music.

## 2.2. Access-based channel vs. ownership-based channel

Consumption channels can be divided into ownership-based and access-based according to whether consumers can own the goods. As digital access-based platforms (e.g., Spotify and Netflix) becomes prevalent, it would change what (and how) is produced and consumed. Scholars have examined how access-based channels influence ownership-based channels in physical space. The access-based was earlier witnessed in the public sector—e.g., sharing books at public libraries [53] or providing access to drawings at museums [18], which resulted in the replacement for ownership-based channels. In the private sector, however, only a few studies have examined this issue explicitly. Purohit and Staelin [57] investigated the impact of access-based on ownership based channels in the automobile market and found no significant relationship between them. However, other scholars have suggested that people would purchase more after experiencing access-based consumption platforms. Bardhi and Eckhardt [10] showed that using carsharing services can lead to a higher desire to own cars, which can be evidence that the access-based channel may have a positive influence on the traditional purchase channel. Regarding experience goods, Knox and Eliashberg [43] showed that, in the movie industry, customers having a propensity to pay for rentals at a lower price have a greater tendency to buy rather than rent. Similarly, Hashim et al. [37] also demonstrated that rental channels significantly increase DVD sales.

With the growth in online services, scholars began investigating the relationship between access-based and ownership-based channels in digital platforms. Datta et al. [23] studied the relationship between digital music streaming and downloading and found that both channels cannibalize each other. Aguiar and Waldfogel [7] studied the impact of subscription-based (access-based) digital music on the download sales for the top 50 songs on Spotify and found that streaming replaces to downloads. Gong et al. [35] explored the relationship between digital rental and digital purchase channels in the DVD market and found that price promotion in the purchase channel does not afect sales in rental channels. Aguiar and Martens [6] showed that digital music streaming can increase download sales.

Despite such studies, there is still uncertainty about whether consumption through access-based channels is indeed replacing consumption through ownership-based channels. Moreover, studies have mainly focused on cross-channel relationships, and only a few have compared the relationships regarding both tangibility (digital vs. physical) and ownership (access vs. ownership) [66]. Thus, there is a need to comprehensively consider all possible channels from the aspects of both access-based vs. ownership-based and digital vs. physical consumption.

This study investigates the relationships among all three available channels (access-based digital, ownership-based digital, and ownershipbased physical channels) in the music market. Also, our study accounts for the efects of the digital music platforms' charts as previous studies have examined the impact of popularity on experience goods such as books and music [19,20,25–27]. Although some studies measure popularity based on reviews or postings on social media, ranking on digital music charts is considered a measure of popularity in this study. We conjecture that sales of physical albums may be influenced by vis ibility and sales in digital streaming platforms. ## 3. Research context and data

## 3.1. Research context—the music industry in South Korea

The South Korean music market provides a suitable setting for examining our research questions for several reasons. As shown in Fig. 3, the predominant share of sales in the music market quickly shifted from physical to digital in the early 2000s, with monthly paid subscriptionbased online streaming services emerging as a major method of music consumption in the late 2000s. This transition is attributable to the enactment of strict anti-piracy laws and favorable conditions following technological advancements in telecommunications infrastructure [46]. Furthermore, it appears that the notable shift from digital downloading to streaming occurred much earlier in South Korea than any other country.

Digital music charts are popular and widely available in the country since most online music streaming platforms provide their own charts based on how frequently each song has been streamed and downloaded by day, week, month, or even in real-time. Specifically, for the songs in a high position of the chart, the impact is massive, because most music streaming platforms in Korea have a feature called “Listening to songs on the Top 10 chart” (see Fig. 4) which helps listeners to be more exposed to popular songs. Note that in the U.S., popular artistes like Taylor Swift, Maroon 5, and Bruno Mars have kept releasing their albums as physical records as CD and vinyl formats in the early 2010s, and many people were still purchasing physical records to fulfill their desire to own and collect physical albums [32,56]. However, a vinyl format was not used in South Korea, and CD is the only format for physical albums. In this respect, examining the South Korean music market with online streaming/downloading and CD sales data is appropriate.

## 3.2. Data

To address our research questions, we use rich and novel weekly nationwide music sales data obtained from the industry association in

Korea between March 2011 and December 2012. This was a period when the transitions from physical to digital and from downloading to streaming were actively taking place in the country in a sizable manner: the digital share slightly exceeds 50% of the domestic music market, and streaming share reaches 40% of the digital music market in the country as of 2011 [1]. After our study period, the streaming share has kept increasing and reached 90% in 2014 [3]. The 90% share maintained in 2018 [5], which implies that it is dificult to observe the efect of the transition from downloading to streaming after 2014. Also, the South Korean music market is suficiently sizable by showing that the number of streaming users with paid-subscription plans was much greater than those in any other country at that time. For example, in 2011, the number of paid-for streaming subscribers in South Korea was over 3 million, while it was 1.8 million in the U.S. [2].<sup>3</sup> More importantly, the decline in CD sales in South Korea was stabilized from the early 2010s, as the digital music market matured and became saturated with the rapid penetration of streaming services in the 2000s. In summary, the snapshot of the Korean music market in 2011–2012 provides an adequate setting for understanding the transitions from physical to digital in the music industry and from download to streaming in the digital segment simultaneously, which was not observed in other countries in other periods. This is suitable to examine phenomena in the point of access-based and ownership-based consumptions.

Another reason is that Korean pop music (K-pop) has begun globalized since late 2012, and the growing popularity of K-pop worldwide also afected the Korean domestic music market in terms of CD and digital music sales.<sup>4</sup> Thus, the shifting dynamics of the domestic music market before 2013 was not influenced by external factors but represented by natural evolution. ## 4.1. Model

Our empirical analysis is based on three-stage least squares (3SLS) estimation. Three music sales outcome variables (CDSales, Streaming, and Download) influence each other directly or indirectly. Accordingly, to avoid the possible reverse causality, we employ 3SLS estimation. The 3SLS procedure is used to derive the parameters of the full system since endogenous variables in some equations of the model are used as explanatory variables in other equations. Further, there is a possibility of a correlation among error terms across the regression equations due to each case being based on data from the same respondent. 3SLS combines two-stage least squares (2SLS) and seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) methods to account for both dependent regressors and cross-equation correlation of errors, as it is the recommended approach for triangular structural systems [47], as in this study. 4.2. Identification strategy for the efect of digital music on physical album sales

One can argue that streams (downloads) may be correlated with the error term of our equations for physical album sales, which may lead to possible reverse causality and biased estimations. To address this po tential endogeneity bias, we introduce instrument variables (IVs). This instrument should be correlated with the endogenous regressor (ln (Streaming ) and ln(Download )), but orthogonal to any other omitted characteristics. In other words, the IVs are associated with total streams (downloads) of songs, but not incorporated directly into our physical album sales equations. Thus, we employ the logarithm terms of AroundRankStrm $\imath g _ { i j t }$ and AroundRankDow $\iota _ { i j t }$ as IVs for Eqs. [2] and [3], respectively. AroundRankStrmg (AroundRankDown ) denotes the sum of streams (downloads) of five songs ahead of a focal song's rank and five songs behind the focal song's rank. For example, assuming that the focal song of the physical album is ranked 10th on the streaming chart, AroundRankStrmg is the sum of sales of the songs ranked between 5th and 9th (i.e., five songs ahead of the 10th) and 11th to 15th (i.e., five songs behind the 10th).

The suggested IVs provide an exogenous source of variation to identify the efect of streaming (downloading) sales on physical album sales. Moreover, the sales of songs near the given rank of the focal song may be highly correlated with the sales of the focal song, since the ranking is inherently determined by sales in the given week. While the inclusion of five songs before and after a certain rank can provide suficient variations across the ranking, it is related to the fact that consumers are likely to listen to the songs consecutively because they are displayed on the chart. However, streams (downloads) of adjacent songs near the rank of the focal song may be exogenous with respect to physical album sales, including a focal song in a given week, since it is unlikely that listeners of adjacent songs consider buying a physical album containing the focal song. Our IV approach is similar to the techniques used in Forman et al. [33]. Furthermore, to check the validity of the suggested IVs, we test the model with alternative numbers of adjacent songs and achieve consistent findings. We also perform Stock and Yogo's [62] test for weak instruments and report the outcomes in the next section.

## 6. Discussion and conclusions

## 6.1. Summary of findings and discussion

According to Rifkin [59]. consumers want to “access" rather than “own” products in the digital era, and this notable shifts are observed in many fields, such as books, newspapers, and movies. In this regard, the transition from analog to digital has changed music consumption behaviors. Similarly, in digital music market, streaming and downloading services can be regarded as access-based and ownership-based consumption, respectively. In this sense, this study examines the relationships among music streaming, downloading, and physical album sales when the digital music has suficiently emerged. Our unique nationwide dataset allows us to distinguishes the number of digital streams from the downloads and to match the digital music sales with physical album sales for each song. Our empirical findings consistently suggest that considering the efects of the two diferent digital formats (i.e., streaming and downloading services) is useful to measure the impact on physical goods consumption. Unlike conventional beliefs, artistes can increase their incomes by selling more physical albums as a complement to the predominant digital formats when the digital con sumption turns to be access-based. In particular, as streaming services become more widespread, digital music consumption can afect physical album sales more positively. To address the underlying mechanism of this result, we additionally interviewed 100 panels randomly selected. Nearly 75% of the respondents have subscribed to one of the paid digital streaming services, and 30% of the respondents said they had purchased physical albums in the past five years because they want to tangibly possess favorite artistes' music. Many respondents also answered that they listen to digital music before purchasing the CD al bums. 70% of the respondents said that subscribing to streaming services afects their buying decision of CD albums in a positive direction.

Our results also clarify how online charts influence the relationship between digital content and physical goods. In the digital market, the role of visible charts seems important, since they reduce the search cost for newly-releasing digital content. Our results show that being on the top 10 list in digital music platforms is important for CD album sales. We also show the diferential efects of streaming and downloading across artiste types. While streaming sales present a larger impact on CD sales, both streaming and downloading significantly influence physical album sales for male or solo artistes. For female or band artistes, only streaming afects physical album sales significantly. Perhaps, fans of male or solo artistes are considerably diferent from fans of female or bands, and they may have a higher willingness-to-pay for CD albums. In other words, fans of male or solo artistes may have a stronger and closer relationship and attachment than fans of female or bands [42], which leads to the fact that fans of male or solo artistes want to buy and possess more products [41]. For example, female fans of male artistes with high loyalty may listen to the more songs by streaming and downloading, and at the same time, they are more likely to buy CD albums. However, male fans of female artistes may only listen to the music by the digital format, not physical records. Similarly, the ripple power of solo artistes can be greater than that of band artistes, and the loyalty of fans of solo artistes may be higher than that of band artistes, which can result in higher sales in all formats. This result can be helpful for setting the new release plan such as the amount of physical albums to produce according to artist characteristics.

## 6.2. Contributions and implications

This study has important academic and managerial implications. First, the size of the recorded music market is showing a tendency to increase in some developed countries such as Sweden, the U.K., and the U.S., after a long downtrend since the 2000s as described earlier by Plunkett [32,56,68]. In fact, these observations were induced by the combined growth in digital streaming and physical album sales. Several studies have addressed the impact of streaming services on the digital music market, but did not relate the issue to the physical recording market [6,39,61]. Prior studies that have attempted to investigate this issue have mostly used survey data, which include information on consumers' intention and attitude rather than their actual consumptions [52,61]. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the first studies observing the phenomena using combining sales datasets of all available formats to consumers in the music market. Out findings show that, in a fully-served digital music market where a decline in the physical music market has been stabilized, the physical album sales could potentially rise to some extent.

Second, we introduce a new framework to divide the consumption types of the digital content based on product tangibility (or intangibility) and ownership (or access). Some studies addressed the tangibility issue of digital and physical products [8,55,60,65], and others focused on the access/ownership issue [10,18]. Our study takes into account both the tangibility and ownership aspects. The suggested framework in this study can be applied to other types of content such as videos and books. Assuming that digital transformation in the music industry preceded that for other types of content, practitioners may need to focus on the noticeable increase in the consumption of tangible physical goods as digital streaming services become prevalent.

Last, we address the relationship between digital and physical consumptions, where access-based digital consumption is available. Digital content mostly served as a substitute for physical content at first, so they were considered to be cannibalizing each other [36,48–50]. However, it is necessary to further account for the consumption type (access or ownership), because this transition has provoke a new relationship between the goods in the digital and physical channels. Also, we find that the efects of streaming or downloading are diferent according to the artiste type. Content providers should be aware of this potential heterogeneity when they consider whether and how many physical goods are produced. Our findings can be useful when firms build business strategies and allocate resources regarding physical and digital goods.

## 6.3. Limitations and future research

Our study ofers several avenues for future research. First, we ob serve only published physical albums; some artistes produce only digital tracks when recognizing that producing physical albums is not a profitable strategy. Second, we cannot precisely see that consumers listening to a particular song digitally are those who who purchase a CD album containing that song. Third, the dataset used in this study covers physical albums for the top 400 songs on the chart. While top-tier sales cover most of market sales, the availability of more detailed datasets can complement the current study.

In this regard, further investigations can be conducted as future research. A possible direction is to examine the impact of physical al bums' characteristics on sales. It can be observed physical albums are released in various formats with supplementary materials such as exclusive photo cards, posters, photo books, or lottery tickets to signing events or concerts, which can ofer extra value to consumers who consider buying a CD album. Another possible direction is to examine the impact of consumers' loyalty to artistes more rigorously. Some consumers may feel a sense of loyalty to maintain their collections or support specific artistes or genres; thus, they invest in collecting physical albums and products of their favorite artistes. This study considers popularity using digital charts, so measuring fandom in a diferent way will extend this research. Also, some emerging digital channels, such as e-books, digital magazines, music videos, and online video clips are used for the future studies.
