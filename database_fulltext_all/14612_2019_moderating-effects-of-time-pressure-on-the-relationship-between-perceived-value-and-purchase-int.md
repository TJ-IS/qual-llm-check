# Moderating e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on the relationship between perceived value and purchase intention in social E-commerce sales promotion: Considering the impact of product involvement

Lifang Peng<sup>⁎</sup>, Weiguo Zhang, Xiaorong Wang, Shuyi Liang

School of Management, Xiamen University, Xiamen, China

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales Perceived value Time pressure Product involvement Purchase intention

## A B S T R A C T

How does customer perceived value in<sup>fl</sup>uence purchase intention in online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales on social e-commerce platforms? This study investigates the role of time pressure and product involvement in the relationship between perceived value and purchase intention. Drawing on survey data from wjx.com in China, we found that perceived value is positively related to purchase intention, whereas time pressure negatively moderates the e<sup>f</sup>ect of emotional/social value on purchase intention. The three-way interaction among time pressure, perceived functional/emotional/social value, and product involvement was also statistically signi<sup>fi</sup>cant. We discuss the theoretical and practical implications of these results.

## 1. Introduction

To boost sales and pro<sup>fi</sup>ts, most merchants on online business platforms (e.g., Rue La La, Gilt, Amazon, Tmall, JD) employ <sup>fl</sup>ash sales with limited purchase time. Using this emerging promotion strategy, retailers o<sup>f</sup>er deep discounts for a limited time [82]. However, experience shows that time limits are a double-edged sword that can si multaneously promote and compromise sales. On the one hand, time limits may create an “opportunity cost perception” among consumers [48]—in other words, consumers who fail to purchase items within the designated time period will “regret refusing to buy” [81]. The time limit creates a sense of urgency [75], thus encouraging consumers to pur chase [34,58]. On the other hand, by requiring consumers to make a purchase decision within a short time, online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales can induce time pressure [17], as the decision maker is likely to become anxious and under pressure because the available time is less than the time normally required to make such a decision [73]. This perceived time pressure has a negative impact on purchase decision-making, thus compromising the sales promotion [60].

With the development of social e-commerce, information dissemination continues to accelerate as customers increasingly share shopping experiences with others and are in<sup>fl</sup>uenced by online com ments from friends and onlookers. In this situation, customers must process more information, and the emotional experience of purchase decision-making is more complex, and the consumer’s perception of time pressure is more intense. For that reason, understanding the role of time pressure and preventing the failure of social e-commerce online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales are of great signi<sup>fi</sup>cance for merchants and scholars.

Academics have conducted in-depth research on the correlation between time pressure and purchase decision-making [5,10,20,49,60,61]. For example, Payne et al. [61] showed that consumers process information selectively, and Petty et al. [63] reported that time pressure interferes with the individual’s optimal strategy. In contrast, other researchers have suggested that consumers exhibit better decision-making skills when placed under time pressure [49]. Despite extensive previous research, most of these studies were con-<sup>fi</sup>ned to analysis of the immediate relationship between time pressure and customer behavior. Product category, another integral component of sales promotion [72,79,85], is also of relevance in this context, as it determines the level of product involvement [25,28,32], which in<sup>fl</sup>uences the consumer’s cognitive and behavioral responses to marketing stimulation [32,42,59]. Again, past work has neglected the possible e<sup>f</sup>ect of any interaction between time pressure and product involvement on the relationship between customer perceived value and purchase intention.

To address the limitations of previous research, this study tests the assumption of a positive relationship between customer perceived value and purchase intention based on the “value perception-purchase intention” framework. Second, the present study analyzes the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between each dimension of customer perceived value and purchase intention in the context of online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales. Finally, to situate the research closer to the reality of business, the paper examines changes in the e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure with high and low product involvement. To test the study hypotheses, we designed an online experimental scenario and collected data from wjx.com, a professional data services company in China.

This study has both theoretical and practical implications. First, it enhances existing understanding of the e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on decision-making and broadens the scope of research on online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales in social e-commerce contexts. Additionally, it provides guidance for companies seeking to implement e<sup>f</sup>ective online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales promotion strategies for speci<sup>fi</sup>c products with varying degrees of involvement in social e-commerce while avoiding any waste of marketing resources.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Customer perceived value

Customer perceived value is known to be the most critical determinant of consumers’ decision to purchase [6,8,21,26,51], where perceived value refers to “the consumer’s overall assessment of the utility of a product or service, determined by a consumer’s perception of what is received and given [91]. If the perceived bene<sup>fi</sup>t to the cus tomer outweighs the perceived cost, the customer will perceive the item as having a positive value [52]. According to the value-intention framework, if an item has a higher perceived value, the customer will exhibit a stronger purchase intention [11,51]. Perceived value is closely related to customer demand; in particular, scholars have de<sup>fi</sup>ned per ceived value in terms of di<sup>f</sup>erent dimensions of customer demand, such as product function, economic interest, emotion, and social environ ment. For example, Young et al. [88] divided perceived value into two dimensions (hedonic and utilitarian), whereas Sweeney and Soutar di vided perceived value into three dimensions (functional, emotional, and social) [76]. Li et al. discussed perceived value in terms of social/ emotional, utilitarian, and economic value [44], and Chi and Kildu<sup>f</sup> referred to price value, social value, emotional value, and quality value [9]. Chen and Tsai divided perceived value into six dimensions: emo tional, social, quality, health, epistemic, and educational [7,8].

The present study categorizes the dimensions of customer perceived value on the basis of customer requirements in social e-commerce situations. First, based on rational shopping demands (regardless of the type of shopping channel or platform), most customers’ <sup>fi</sup>rst concerns are price and product function. If the price of a new product is low, customers will be more willing to try that product, thus making price a purchase-decision factor [54]. The functional value of a product or service is the primary motive for purchase, thus referring to a customer’s sense of its quality, design, durability, appearance, and safety [67]. The present study considers the dimensions of price value and functional value.

In addition, customers are paying increasing attention to the online shopping experience, including such factors as ease, convenience, fun, communication, and self-satisfaction. As the experience of buying goods on an online shopping platform creates emotional value for customers [37], this study also examines emotional value as an important dimension of customer perceived value.

In social e-commerce contexts, customers often have social contact with other people, and their comments on a product, as well as word-ofmouth and dissemination of information, signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly a<sup>f</sup>ects customers’ shopping decisions based on conformable psychology [47]. Alternatively, as customers may strengthen their social identity and social self-consciousness through the perceived social value of their purchase [7,8], this paper also considers social value as a dimension of customer perceived value. To summarize, price value, functional value, emotional value, and social value are considered to be dimensions of customer perceived value in social e-commerce environments. ## 2.2. Online flash sales and time pressure

Given the prevalence of online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales on e-commerce and social e-commerce platforms, it is surprising that only a few researchers have addressed the topic [27,82]. A <sup>fl</sup>ash sale involves a speci<sup>fi</sup>c limit, such as a quantitative or time limit [12], and promotional mechanisms vary for di<sup>f</sup>erent types of limit. While a quantitative limit commonly encourages consumers to perceive a product as extremely rare, stimulating their competitive purchase mentality [1], a time limit is mainly intended to increase consumers’ willingness to buy [75]. There is some evidence in support of these assumptions; for example, in a series of experiments, Inman et al. concluded that imposing a time limit sends a signal to consumers and increases the probability that they will select the product [35]. However, other studies have shown that, when purchasing time is limited, consumers form a negative view of the purchasing process [71]. Many scholars believe this is because a time limit results in “time pressure perception” among consumers. Time limit and time pressure are distinct concepts; while the former is an objective phenomenon, the latter is a subjective feeling [45]. If a time limit does not make a customer feel urgent and anxious, there is no time pressure as far as that individual customer is concerned [74].

While scholars’ views vary in relation to the e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure, its characteristics can be said to include time limits, attitude toward time limits, and emotional experience. For example, Svenson and Edland [73] stressed that time pressure can cause anxiety if the decision maker feels that the deadline is approaching for task completion. Payne et al. [60] characterized time pressure as an individual’s subjective response and emotional reaction to a time limit. Svenson and Maule [74] concluded that time pressure is the result of limited time for decision-making.

Studies of consumer behavior focus increasingly on the impact of time pressure on customers’ decision-making, and there is extensive research on this relationship. For instance, Spears [72] investigated the impact of time pressure on purchase decision-making by examining the relationship between time pressure and information processing, and Kocher and Sutter [39] studied that impact by analyzing at the relationship between time pressure, material stimulation, and decisionquality. While there is some evidence [55] that time pressure accelerates customer decision-making and may even improve its quality, many studies have shown that customers tend to make worse decisions under time pressure, thus impacting negatively on the quality of decision-making [60,61].

In general, potential purchasers who are under time pressure have insu<sup>fi</sup>cient time to <sup>fi</sup>nd out enough about a product’s attributes to make a comprehensive evaluation [36]. These customers tend to apply some form of heuristic rule [19,40,74], thus focusing, for example, on key product information or others’ opinions or consulting their acquaintances. When having to make judgments or decisions under time pressure, customers experience psychological stress [57] and develop negative emotions or become risk-averse [56,70,91]. In response to a high level of perceived risk, people tend to adopt a strategy that minimizes uncertainty, usually with the least e<sup>f</sup>ort or complexity [46].

## 2.3. Product involvement

Product involvement is the relationship between a consumer’s perception of the product and the consumer’s internal demands, value, and interest [89,90], thus indicating the consumer s focus on the product or its signi<sup>fi</sup>cance for that individual. Product involvement di<sup>f</sup>ers from one consumer to another depending on personal factors and perceptions of product signi<sup>fi</sup>cance and risk. In general, products with a high price and a high level of misleading risk (such as real estate) entail high involvement [41], which strengthens customers’ information seeking and processing and engagement in complicated decision behaviors [22].

## 3. Research model and hypothesis development

## 3.1. Research model

To explore the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relation ship between customer perceived value and purchase intention in on line <sup>fl</sup>ash sales, the present study de<sup>fi</sup>nes customer perceived value in terms of price value, functional value, emotional value, and social value. According to the value-intention framework, perceived value has a positive impact on purchase intention [51,52], and previous studies have examined the positive relationship between these values [8,11,26,44]. On that basis, we predict that customer perceived value will impact positively on purchase intention in online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales. We also examine the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between customer perceived value and purchase intention. To approximate real business practices, we further assume that product involvement will have an impact on this moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect. Fig. 1 describes our research model.

## 3.2. Hypothesis development

## 3.2.1. Consumer perceived value and purchase intention

Customer perceived value serves as a bridge between customer value and purchase behavior, thereby linking the consumer’s attitudes to purchasing and psychological behavior during decision-making. Customer perceived value is closely related to customer needs and involves di<sup>f</sup>erent dimensions [87]. Based on the observed behavior of online customers, customer perceived value is considered here in terms of four dimensions: price value, functional value, sentimental value, and social value. Extensive research has shown that perceived value has a positive impact on consumer purchase intention [16,51,52]; high perceived value is positively correlated with purchase intention and increases a customer’s willingness to make repeated purchases [16]. While this positive relationship is well established, we have included some related hypotheses in this model for the sake of completeness

## 3.2.2. The role of time pressure

Time pressure is a subjective feeling of urgency and anxiety as perceived by the customer. Time limits are one assumed cause of time pressure, but in the context of customer purchase decisions, a time limit does not necessarily produce time pressure. Rather, a time limit will cause time pressure only when the customer experiences a desire to purchase because he or she has realized the value of a commodity and must make a purchasing decision within a speci<sup>fi</sup>ed timeframe [55]. This means that perceived value is another potential cause of time pressure [48]. Additionally, as customers may have insu<sup>fi</sup>cient time to collect information for product evaluation under time pressure [36], this will hinder information processing, which underpins the formation of customer perceived value [30]. In this way, consumer expectation and consumer perception of value may deviate [39,74,89], and we consider that the interaction between time pressure and perceived value may impact consumer purchase intention. In the next section, we analyze how time pressure interacts with each dimension of perceived value to a<sup>f</sup>ect purchase intention.

3.2.2.1. The role of time pressure in price value-purchase intention. Merchants’ use of price discounting to promote sales con<sup>fi</sup>rms that price value is an important dimension of customer perceived value. Price discount promotions involving a limited purchase time are considered useful because “customers will feel regret if they do not buy within the limited purchase time” [81]. Because time limits prompt consumers to create an “opportunity cost perception” [48], they increase the probability that a purchase will be made [35]. Based on the success of limited-time price discount promotions, researchers have suggested that time limits strengthen the positive relationship between price value and purchase intention. In practice, however, such promotions often fail, and many scholars have argued that time limits weaken the positive relationship between price value and purchase intention.

Here, we argue that time limit is not identical to time pressure; if customers make decisions within a modest time limit that does not cause them to experience time pressure, purchase intention may be enhanced as a result of “opportunity cost perception” [35,48,81]. However, if (as a great deal of the evidence suggests) customers must make decisions within a time limit that triggers a sense of time pressure, anxiety, and worry, decision-making quality will be negatively a<sup>f</sup>ected [60,61]. Where the unpleasant experience of time pressure exceeds the pleasant experience of “gaining” derived from price discount, the importance and meaning of that discount is degraded [14,15,77,83]. Additionally, time pressure may impair the customer’s ability to make an objective and accurate judgment [3,36,46], thereby causing them to underestimate the price value of the commodity [39,74,88]. From thi grounded perspective, we hypothesize that time pressure may weaken the positive relationship between price value and purchase intention.

H5a. Time pressure moderates the relationship between price value and purchase intention; when time pressure is high, the e<sup>f</sup>ect of price value on purchase intention is weakened.

3.2.2.2. The role of time pressure in functional value- purchase intention. In general, customers judge a commodity’s functional value in terms of technical parameters or by comparing its performance with available alternatives. As most consumers have limited knowledge of a product’s performance, they need time to collect the relevant information or to consult others. If there is insu<sup>fi</sup>cient time to evaluate a product’s attributes in this way, potential purchasers are likely to apply some heuristic rule [19,40,74] using an information-<sup>fi</sup>ltering strategy for decision-making [3]—for example, focusing on key product information. Additionally, when customers judge a commodity’s functional value under time pressure, their experience of psychological stress is likely to induce risk aversion [56,57,70,92]. Once they perceive risk, customers tend to look for ways of reducing uncertainty to minimize that risk with the least e<sup>f</sup>ort, thus adopting a simple approach [46]. This may lead them to delay decision-making or abandon purchasing to avoid loss [51,52]. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H5b. Time pressure moderates the relationship between functional value and purchase intention; when time pressure is high, the e<sup>f</sup>ect of functional value on purchase intention is weakened.

3.2.2.3. The role of time pressure in emotional value-purchase intention. Emotional value is the utility derived from feelings or a<sup>f</sup>ective states generated by the process of buying a product [7,8]. This process includes the purchase decision-making stage. According to previous research, when customers make shopping decisions under time pressure, they generate negative emotions such as tension, anxiety, and worry [57], thus indicating that time pressure immediately degrades perceived emotional value. This negative emotional experience reduces both perceived emotional value and other dimensions of perceived value [60,61,70,91], thereby lowering the level of overall perceived value and undermining the customer’s purchase intention. On that basis, we argue that, in limited-time promotions, time pressure will interact with emotional value to weaken purchase intention. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H5c. Time pressure moderates the relationship between emotional value and purchase intention; when time pressure is high, the e<sup>f</sup>ect of emotional value on purchase intention is weakened.

3.2.2.4. The role of time pressure in social value- purchase intention. Social value is de<sup>fi</sup>ned as the utility derived from a product’s ability to enhance social self-concept [7–9]. By obtaining the perceived social value of their purchase, the customer strengthens their social identity and social self-consciousness [7,8]. Social value can be seen to relate to subjective factors such as interest, preference, emotion, culture, belief, and personal value criteria. These factors are more irrational than rational [68], and as a result, the customer’s judgment of social value becomes complicated. For example, social value is often judged on the basis of other people’s comments or the opinions of friends. However, individual preferences or value criteria do not accord exactly with those of others, as the customer needs to balance these elements. The need to judge a product’s social value and make a purchase decision in a limited time is therefore likely to create a strong sense of time pressure and anxiety.

In general, the individual customer may realize that it is risky to make a purchase decision based on social value in a limited time because this includes many factors that are subjective and less rational. As this risk awareness can also degrade perceived social value, anxiety and risk awareness may combine to diminish purchase intention when time pressure interacts with social value in limited-time promotions. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H5d. Time pressure moderates the relationship between social value and purchase intention; when time pressure is high, the e<sup>f</sup>ect of social value on purchase intention is weakened.

## 3.2.3. The role of product involvement

The literature review above characterizes product involvement in terms of perceived product relevance to the individual consumer, based on their inherent needs, values, and interests [90,91]. As this means that product involvement is closely related to customer perceived value, it seems likely that the two variables may interact. For example, a highprice commodity with a high level of misleading risk (such as real estate) will usually be seen to entail high product involvement [43]. If in turn a customer sees something as a high-involvement product, this creates a higher expectation of value—for instance, in terms of product attributes or brand loyalty [41,90].

Product involvement relates to the attention that customers pay to a product over a period of time [84]. In the decision-making process, the customer’s cognitive mechanism changes with the level of product involvement [81]. For low-involvement products, customers tend to adopt a noncompensatory incomplete processing strategy based on attributes, investing less cognitive e<sup>f</sup>ort in decision-making [60]. In the case of high-involvement products, however, customers are likely to collect more information and undertake a more complex decisionmaking process, thus processing information carefully (i.e., within a systematic thinking model) to avoid risk [22]. Because the customer’s attitude and the mechanism and psychological features of decisionmaking vary with level of involvement, their perceptions of time pressure in online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales will also di<sup>f</sup>er, which means that product involvement may interact with time pressure in purchase decision making. We analyzed the interaction of product involvement, time pressure, and each dimension of perceived value in terms of their e<sup>f</sup>ect on purchase intention.

3.2.3.1. The role of product involvement in time pressure-price valuepurchase intention. In the case of high-involvement products, the customer is likely to be more sensitive to price value—that is, to the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of price discounts [63]. At the same time, as high involvement creates a strong sense of the need to avoid risk and loss, more time is needed to judge overall value and perceived time pressure increases. Because the customer is strongly attracted by the price discount while their perception of time limitation is heightened, they are likely to experience greater anxiety and worry when purchasing a highinvolvement product in a limited-time promotion. Common sense suggests that unpleasant feelings and negative emotions under more serious time pressure may exceed the pleasant experience of “gaining” derived from a price discount, thus impairing their ability to judge goods objectively and accurately. This means that the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the positive relationship between perceived price value and purchase intention will be stronger in the case of a highinvolvement product. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H6a. The negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between price value and purchase intention is stronger when product involvement is higher.

3.2.3.2. The role of product involvement in time pressure-functional valuepurchase intention. The customer’s basic motivation for purchasing a product is to use that product—that is, the product must provide use value—which explains why customers usually devote considerable attention to a product’s functional value. For high-involvement products, that attention is correspondingly more intense. Customers also evaluate such products mainly on cognitive criteria while lowinvolvement products are evaluated mainly on a<sup>f</sup>ective criteria [45,57]. For high-involvement products, customers regard careful thinking as an important factor in product selection and they need to spend more time collecting product information [18]. With high product involvement, perceived time pressure and risk awareness become stronger [49]. On the basis of this analysis, we posited that the interaction of high involvement, time pressure, and functional value impacts purchase intention, and we formulated the following hypothesis.

H6b. The negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between functional value and purchase intention is stronger when product involvement is higher.

3.2.3.3. The role of product involvement in time pressure-emotional valuepurchase intention. As discussed earlier, product involvement relates to the signi<sup>fi</sup>cance of the commodity for the individual consumer; the higher the level of product involvement, the stronger is the customer's attention. For higher involvement products, the customer’s decisionmaking mechanism becomes more complicated, and decision-making time becomes longer. This in turn means that perceived time pressure intensi<sup>fi</sup>es, as do anxiety, negative emotions, and perceived risk. For that reason, perceived emotional value will be seriously weakened when purchasing high-involvement products in limited-time promotions, and negative feelings increase signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly. This strongly unpleasant purchase experience will seriously damage customer perceived value and purchase intention. In short, high product involvement will strengthen the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the positive relationship between emotional value and purchasing intention. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H6c. The negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between emotional value and purchase intention is stronger when product involvement is higher.

3.2.3.4. The role of product involvement in time pressure-social value purchase intention. Literature con<sup>fi</sup>rms that high product involvement heightens relevance and intensi<sup>fi</sup>es attention [63]. When purchasing high-involvement products, customers adopt a cautious attitude, thus strengthening information collection and engaging in a complex decision-making process as well as processing information carefully to avoid risk [22]. This means that customers depend on more rational judgments when purchasing high-involvement products in limited-time promotions. However, the mental process of judging social value by referring to other people’s value criteria depends on a<sup>f</sup>ective or irrational factors [7–9,68]. For that reason, in judging the social value of a high-involvement product, the struggle between rational and irrational factors will cause customers to feel con<sup>fl</sup>icted. If a customer must decide whether or not to purchase a high-involvement product with a sense of urgency and limited time, perceived time pressure and negative emotions will be experienced more strongly. In these circumstances, customers may delay making a purchase decision or abandon their purchase intention. On that basis, we formulated the following hypothesis.

H6d. The negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between social value and purchase intention is stronger when product involvement is higher.

## 3.3. Control variable

In addition to the underlying constructs described above, we also postulated that purchase intention may rely on such variables as gender, age, education, online shopping experience, consumption on social e-commerce platforms patronized in the last year, and experience of participating in sales promotions. To better understand the e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on the relationship between perceived value and purchase intention in the proposed research model, we included these variables as control variables.

## 4. Research method

## 4.1. Scenario design and procedure

An online scenario was developed to empirically examine and va lidate the proposed research model. The experiment employed a $2 \times 2$ (time pressure level: high vs. low x product involvement level: high vs. low) between-subjects factorial design. The use of 1 h/1 day before the end of the promotion as two types of time pressure re<sup>fl</sup>ects current practice on large social e-commerce sites and pilot work in China. Product categories included low-involvement and high-involvement products; on the basis of previous research, we selected a laptop as the high-involvement product and a cup as the low-involvement product [29,59,90]. In addition, the selected discount promotion features (“Save 50%”) re<sup>fl</sup>ected the sales promotion strategy during the Double Eleven Day holiday promotion (similar to Black Friday in the U.S.) on most e-commerce and social e-commerce websites.

All participants were members of wjx.com, a professional survey website in China, and they were randomly assigned to one of four scenario groups. Respondents were <sup>fi</sup>rst asked about their level of involvement with the laptop or the cup. Our scenarios then displayed the promotional information for each product, including end of promotion period, product price, and product features (see Appendix A). Subjects were then asked to simulate online shopping and were subsequently asked to <sup>fi</sup>ll out a questionnaire. In total, 654 responses were collected during the study period (November 1–18, 2017), and 570 valid samples were included in the subsequent analysis. Table 2 summarizes participants’ demographic data. Most respondents were aged 25–34 years, and most had 5–7 years of online shopping experience. This pro<sup>fi</sup>le (relatively young and quite experienced) matched the general characteristics of online customers.

## 4.2. Measures

All constructs and measures were adapted from previously validated studies (see Table 3). Purchase intention was measured using four items adapted from Dodds et al. [16] and Kim et al. [38], who investigated purchase intention in an online context. Price value, functional value, emotional value, and social value were assessed by items adapted from Sweeney and Soutar [76] and from Rintamäki et al. [80]. A three-item scale developed by Suri and Monroe [65] was used to measure time pressure. A seven-item scale adapted by Hong [32] from Zaichkowsky [89] was used to determine the level of product involvement. Responses were captured on Likert-type scales ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) for each statement of perceived value and purchase intention. Responses related to time pressure and product involvement were captured on a scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). In addition, in relation to promotion time limit, 1 day and 1 h were coded as 0 and 1, respectively, thus representing the low and high time pressure groups; for the product category, we substituted cup and laptop for 0 and 1 to represent low involvement and high involvement, respectively. Finally, we employed a commonly accepted double translation procedure to ensure the Chinese equivalence of instruments and measures originally written in English because the survey was conducted in China [4].

## 5. Data analysis and results

## 5.1. Manipulation check

For the purposes of this study, we manipulated the time limit and product category. By using a repeated-measures independent t-test to understand manipulation e<sup>f</sup>ectiveness, the results (Table 4) indicate that respondents in the 1-hour condition reported higher time pressure than those in the 1-day condition (time pressure: mean low = 3.703; mean high = 5.029; t(568) = 12.159; p < .001). At the same time, subjects in the laptop condition reported higher involvement than those in the cup condition (product involvement: mean low = 4.970; mean high = 5.934; t(568) = 12.186; p < .001). As the results con<sup>fi</sup>rmed that the manipulations of limited time and product category were successful, samples of the four conditions were used for the purpose of data analysis.

## 5.2. Measurement model

To verify the measurement model, we <sup>fi</sup>rst scrutinized the collected data by conducting a PCA with varimax rotation, using SPSS 23.0. We analyzed seven factors with eigenvalues of more than 1.0 from all items, which accounted for 75.204% of the total variance. Using AMOS 23.0, we then conducted a con<sup>fi</sup>rmatory factor analysis. The <sup>fi</sup>t indexes for the measurement model were CMIN/DF = 2.010, RMSEA = 0.042, NFI = 0.941, IFI = 0.969, TLI = 0.965, and $\operatorname { C F I } = 0 . 9 6 9$ . On the basis of the guidelines for determining model <sup>fi</sup>t [33], the measurement model shows good <sup>fi</sup>t for this study.

We also tested the convergent validity of the measurement model using three indices [23,24]. The criteria for good convergent validity are as follows: factor loading > 0.7; combination reliability (CR), Cronbach’s alpha > 0.7; and average variance extracted $\begin{array} { r } { \left( \mathsf { A V E } \right) > 0 . 5 . } \end{array}$ Table 5 shows the <sup>fi</sup>nal convergent validity results. As the value of factor loadings, AVEs, and CRs for all constructs met the recommended standards, our instrument was deemed to exhibit acceptable convergent validity.

We tested discriminant validity against the criterion that the potential variable correlation coe<sup>fi</sup>cient should be less than the square root of its AVE value. In Table 6, the square root of the AVE value is on the diagonal; the other data are the correlation coe<sup>fi</sup>cients. Col lectively, across the criteria examined, measurement of all constructs in our research model exhibits high reliability and good validity.

## 5.3. Common method variance

As the present study used a self-report approach, the potential problem for common method bias needed to be addressed. We performed two statistical analyses to assess the severity of common method bias, the <sup>fi</sup>rst of which was Harman’s one-factor method with exploratory factor analysis [31,64]. All items related to the seven con structs in our model were subjected to principal component factor analysis, and the explained variance of the <sup>fi</sup>rst principal component was 18.738%. We then conducted CFA with a common method factor [64] and found that the one-factor model exhibited considerably worse <sup>fi</sup>t than the measurement model. On that basis, we concluded that common method bias was not a serious problem here.

In the third step, we added the two-way interaction terms between time pressure and perceived value, and results can be seen in model 3 of Table 7. ## 6. Discussion and implications

This study attempts to understand how time pressure and product involvement a<sup>f</sup>ect customers’ decision-making in the context of social e-commerce <sup>fl</sup>ash sales. To achieve this objective, we <sup>fi</sup>rst tested the positive e<sup>f</sup>ects of price value, functional value, emotional value, and social value on purchase intention. Considering the temporary nature of sales promotions, we further proposed time pressure to be a moderating variable in the model. We also tested the impact of di<sup>f</sup>erent product categories on consumers’ online shopping behaviors. Our results show <sup>fi</sup>rst that each perceived value had a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant positive in<sup>fl</sup>uence on purchase intention. Second, time pressure had a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant negative regulating e<sup>f</sup>ect on the relationship between perceived emotional/so cial value and purchase intention. Third, time pressure had strikingly di<sup>f</sup>erent regulating e<sup>f</sup>ects on di<sup>f</sup>erent product categories. For highinvolvement products, the negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between perceived functional/emotional/social value and purchase intention was stronger in that this positive relationship is weakest when time pressure is high. However, for lowinvolvement products, there was a smaller di<sup>f</sup>erence in the negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ect of time pressure on the relationship between per ceived value and purchase intention. On the basis of these intriguing <sup>fi</sup>ndings, we discuss the theoretical and practical implications of thi study.

## 6.1. Research implications

This research produced some important theoretical insights. First, while past research on sales promotion has focused mainly on traditional shopping environments, the present study extends the applica tion of the perceived value-purchase intention framework to customer purchase behavior in social e-commerce environments. The study also assigned perceived value to four dimensions: price value, functional value, emotional value, and social value. The results indicate that emotional value and social value are very important for online sales promotions, deepening existing knowledge about customer purchase decision-making in the context of social e-commerce.

Second, much of the earlier research investigating the relationship between time pressure and customer decision-making was relatively narrow in scope. By examining the impact of time pressure as a moderating variable in the relationship between customer perceived value and purchase intention, the present study deepens and expands our understanding of time pressure. While past research con<sup>fi</sup>rmed the signi<sup>fi</sup>cant impact of time pressure on customer purchase decisionmaking, it did so mainly from psychological and behavioral perspectives. By demonstrating the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on the relationship between customer perceived value and purchase intention, the present research illuminates the mechanism and process of time pressure in customer purchase decision-making. This also provided a more nuanced understanding of how customers formulate an intention to purchase under the time pressure of sales promotions.

Third, this study augments the existing literature by investigating the boundary conditions for time pressure as an in<sup>fl</sup>uence on sale promotion. Because customer behavior in real business contexts is the result of multiple factors that include product involvement and time pressure, it is important to take account of these for a more nuanced analysis. The present <sup>fi</sup>ndings enable us to more accurately predict the result of customers’ decision-making and bring us closer to the reality of contemporary business.

## 6.2. Practical implications

This research contributes important new insights concerning limited-time promotions in social e-commerce. The most obvious of these is the positive correlation between functional value and purchase intention; our <sup>fi</sup>ndings show that function value remains the most important criterion for customers purchase decisions on online business platforms. Regardless of the rapid development of e-commerce or social e-commerce, it is clear that merchants should continue to highlight the functional value of their products as the basis for successful sales promotions.

Second, our <sup>fi</sup>ndings show that time pressure and the positive correlations between emotional value, social value, and purchase intention play an obviously negative role in social e-commerce environments, where the impact of emotional and social factors is stronger than in physical stores. For this reason, companies implementing <sup>fl</sup>ash sales on social e-commerce platforms need to take e<sup>f</sup>ective measures to prevent (or at least reduce) the negative impact of time pressure on customers—for example, by setting a suitable time limit, thus providing customers with product information for reference, increasing interactivity in customer consulting and creating an atmosphere that delivers positive emotional and social experiences.

Finally, our <sup>fi</sup>ndings reveal that high product involvement does not reinforce the moderating e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on the positive correlation between price value and purchase intention. However, high product involvement strengthens the negative moderating e<sup>f</sup>ects of time pressure on the positive correlation between functional value, emotional value, social value, and purchase intention. This means that, in online <sup>fl</sup>ash sales, the price value of high-involvement products still attracts customers. However, if other aspects of the product are overlooked, product involvement may interact with time pressure to further impact the positive correlation between functional/emotional/social value and purchase intention. As a result, customers may choose not to buy, hence compromising the <sup>fl</sup>ash sale. For high-involvement products, merchants on social e-commerce platforms should pay particular attention to two issues. (1) Publicity and guidance should focus on customer perceptions of product functionality, brand, cultural signi<sup>fi</sup>cance, positive and social commentary to reduce the e<sup>f</sup>ect on purchase intention of the interaction among product involvement and time pressure and functional/emotional/social value. (2) Merchants should also publish data for customer reference, such as core product information, functional parameters, and comparative advantages. This will help to reduce the time that customers need to collect information and their perception of time pressure, hence reducing the negative effect of high time pressure and product involvement on the positive relationship between perceived value and purchase intention

## 6.3. Limitations and future research

The use of a scenario experimental method enabled us to simplify complicated problems, identify key issues, and clarify development rules. However, this approach excludes other in<sup>fl</sup>uencing factors. First, because the time stress experienced by customers during online pur chasing is less than that when shopping in a physical store, future research should explore the boundary conditions of time stress in online settings by setting the time limited promotion to 5–10 min rather than the 1 h/24 h settings used here. A second limitation is that the study settings for high and low product involvement may be very simplistic. In subsequent research, it would be useful to classify product involvement in more detail, based on more representative corresponding products. Finally, the research model fails to consider the e<sup>f</sup>ects of inter vening variables such as personality, social dissemination, risk perception, or brand e<sup>f</sup>ects, and these might usefully be incorporated in future research.
