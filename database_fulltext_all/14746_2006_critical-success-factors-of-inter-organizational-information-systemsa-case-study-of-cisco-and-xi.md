# Critical success factors of inter-organizational information systems—A case study of Cisco and Xiao Tong in China

Xiang-Hua Lu <sup>a,\*</sup>, Li-Hua Huang <sup>a</sup>, Michael S.H. Heng

<sup>a</sup> School of Management, Fudan University, Shanghai 200433, PR China

<sup>b</sup> School of Computer and Information Science, University of South Australia, Adelaide, SA, Australia

Accepted 1 June 2005 Available online 6 October 2005

## Abstract

This paper reports a case study of an inter-organizational information system (IOS) of Cisco and Xiao Tong in China. We interviewed their senior managers, heads of departments and employees who have been directly affected in their work. Other sources of information are company documents and publicly available background information. The study examines the benefits of the IOS for both corporations. The research also reveals seven critical success factors for the IOS, namely intensive stimulation, shared vision, cross-organizational implementation team, high integration with internal information systems, inter-organizational business process re-engineering, advanced legacy information system and infrastructure and shared industry standard. <sup>#</sup> 2005 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved

Keywords: Inter-organizational information systems; Critical success factors; China

## 1. Introduction

As corporations rely more on collaboration with partners to enhance their position in the business world, they transcend their traditional information system boundaries. They use IT-based inter-organizational information systems as a powerful strategic tool to link with their partners in their supply chain. China, as a huge manufacturing base and a huge market, has been a very important part of international supply chain. Interorganizational information system (IOS) is increasingly being used by Chinese business firms to enhance their cooperation with transnational corporations. The case of Cisco and Xiao Tong in this study has been reported to be the first successful IOS that strictly conforms to XML protocol and e-Business RosettaNet standard in China [7]. This paper reports the findings of our research, which used a case study to find out critical success factors (CSFs) of this IOS project. It is hoped that the findings provide some insights for other corporations that want to implement IOS in China.

CSF occupies a prominent place in IS research field, a fact easily revealed by thumbing through well known IS journals. Rockart [39] defines CSFs as those few key areas of activity in which favorable results are absolutely necessary for a particular company to reach its goals. CSFs are the key areas where ‘‘things must go right’’. As Thierauf [45] states, if the results in these areas are not adequate, the organization’s efforts for the period will be less than desired. For obvious reasons, the critical success factors are areas of activity that must receive due attention from management. CSFs have been one of the earliest and most actively researched topics in the IS field. As a result of such research efforts, we know a number of factors, which influence an information system’s successful implementation, e.g. commitment of top management and adequate funding. These factors are applicable to IOS, since it is also a sort of IS. However, IOSs are more complex and multifaceted than traditional information systems in terms of their technological and management issues.

Johnston et al. [20] define an IOS as an automated information system shared by two or more companies, to facilitate the creation, storage, transformation and transmission of information. An IOS differs from an internal distributed information system by allowing information to be sent across organizational boundaries through communication technology. The strategic value of IOS has been well recognized for its realtime interaction, higher transaction accuracy, more efficient and quicker payments [16], rapid response, reduced search costs, reduction in inventory and tighter links to customers [20]. These benefits enable all parties to have high operational efficiency and capability [3], and more and more corporations tend to adopt IOS in order to gain competitive advantages. It comes as no surprise that IOS is playing an increasing role in international trade and global supply chain. The above definition of IOS encompasses many systems such as extranets, EDI, Internet EDI, B2B e-commerce and e-SCM. What they differ are just the technologies used, scope of application, stakeholders and so on [12].

The IOS system of Cisco and Xiao Tong is a webbased B2B application using RosettaNet standard, and is a part of the e-SCM one enterprise project of Cisco. Compared with traditional EDI or VAN-based extranets, web-based applications provide corporations an opportunity to develop new communication standards that can be used within an entire industry and even across industries. And RosettaNet is pioneering the development of such standards.

RosettaNet is a worldwide consortium of more than 400 of the world’s leading electronic components, IT, semiconductor manufacturing and solution provider companies. They work together to create, implement and promote this open e-business process standards. RosettaNet is breaking language barriers in e-business and is opening the lines of communication so that companies can realize the full potential of new business opportunities. The mission of RosettaNet is to drive collaborative development and rapid deployment of Internet-based business standards, creating a common language and open e-business processes that provide measurable benefits and are vital to the evolution of the global, high-tech trading network (www.RosettaNet. org). Companies such as Intel, IBM, Hewlett-Packard, Cisco, 3Com, Dell, Nokia and many of their trading partners have also announced initiatives to adopt RosettaNet’s Web-based open industry standards for their e-commerce processes.

We look for the CSFs through a case study research method for three reasons. First, CSFs have been heavily used by IS research, but mainly in intra-organizational information systems [33,13,44]. Second, though much is known about the CSFs of information systems in general, there are some CSFs specific to a given technology in a given organization environment. Moreover, there are still knowledge gaps regarding those factors in the development, implementation and operation of IOSs, e.g. EDI system’s successful implementation [3,12,26]. Third, though there are many successful cases RosettaNet-based IOS (e.g. [29]), only one paper (namely Eric, 2002) focuses on CFSs.

Based on an in-depth case study of the IOS project of Cisco and Xiao Tong, this paper groups the CSFs uncovered in our research into three clusters, namely the cluster of decision motivation, the cluster of implementation process and the cluster of infrastructure conditions.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 presents a brief review of research works in the IOS, drawing on the findings of the body of knowledge already gained in EDI research, which provides a conceptual framework for us to formulate the questions, which we asked our research subjects. Section 3 describes the research method. This is followed by a description of the background of the IOS in Section 4. Section 5 reports the findings of the interviews and questionnaire. Section 6 concludes the paper with a discussion.

## 2. Literature review

When learning from the works of others for this research, we include both web-based and non-webbased IOSs research findings are discussed. Although web-based IOSs have some differences with non-webbased IOSs, the research findings of the latter are still valuable and helpful in our investigation of CSF of webbased IOSs.

As information systems, inter-organizational systems share common features of other information systems, including common critical success factors. For example, Raymond and Bergeron [36], in their investigation of EDI success, uncover factors such as organizational support (e.g. training, structure), implementation processes (e.g. planning, testing) and control procedures for data integrity and security affect EDI implementation. According to Emmelhainz [9], the most significant factors that contribute to successful EDI implementation are: a talented and competent champion, top management support, early user involvement, industry efforts and management information system capability and support. In another study, the importance of EDI standards, managing change in the organization and work design was identified [4].

Perhaps a good starting point to study in what ways IOSs may have CSFs that are specific to themselves is to look at the special characteristics of IOSs. One study identifies the following six characteristics of interorganization systems compared with gegernal IS [41]: (1) they require partners which are willing, able and ready to cooperate, (2) a key role for standards (e.g. data communications protocols, company policies), (3) third parties are often involved, (4) the work must be synchronized among partners, (5) technical aspects are less important than the new electronic relationships involved and (6) efforts often cannot be secretive, i.e. IOS require more openness especially when industry standards are adopted.

By virtue of these special characteristics, IOS may have its own CSFs besides those of general information systems. The most obvious and distinctive of IOS is that it involves two or more parties being electronically linked up for the purpose of conducting their business activities. So, issues related to working partnership are likely to feature prominently. Indeed, McKinney and Gerloff [27] assert that trust is the most critical factor for sustained business relationships and that it needs to be nurtured between partners for successful IOS implementation. This point has broad conceptual underpinning drawn from organizational literature [15] and has enjoyed empirical confirmation in Hart and Saunders [15] and Ibbott and O’Keefe [18]. There are additional considerations like risks [40], control [11], scope, switching costs, confidentiality, security, consensus and understanding and different levels of interest between partners. Unless two companies feel that their relations are so interdependent that there are operational savings and benefits from the IOS, they are unlikely to bear the cost in implementing IOS [37].

In terms of resources and strategic intent, partners are very often not similar. One dissimilarity is power, which is the ability of one firm to influence the action of another firm [5]. Firms frequently exercise their power over their suppliers and customers so as to further their own interests. Given that the success of electronic cooperation depends on the actual use of IOS by their partners, the more dominant company tends to exercise its power to influence its IOS-related preferences on their trading partners [17,23,42,43]. Thus, a firm’s commitment to IOS depends on the firm’s strategic position in the channel as well as its strategic goals. The importance of a firm’s strategic goals leads to the decisions to commit resources for payback [3]. Past research on EDI systems indicates that the critical factors for their adoption include a proactive technological orientation and an internal strategic push, advantages of the new system and competitive pressures as well as internal needs [19,28]. The shared vision of IOS between both sides is a vital element for IOS success [33,10,44].

The picture becomes more complex when the IOS is an international one, which has to consider the cultural differences in thinking and operational methods. The differences in opinions, management styles and processes, if not well handled, have the potential to destroy collaboration in e-business [24].

A rather comprehensive set of critical success factors of IOS has been offered by Allen et al. [1]: (1) effective communications, (2) trust, (3) operational uncertainty, (4) different objectives, (5) changes in business processes, (6) information, data standards and protocol, (7) power relations and politics, (8) cross-cultural issues, (9) disparate expectation levels and (10) relationship management.

In another study, Angeles et al. [3] gives additional CSFs in EDI implementation, namely commitment and ownership of a cross-functional EDI team and business process re-engineering (BPR). The goal of refining and re-engineering business processes has been a consistent theme in businesses over the past few decades. Successful IOS implementation requires re-engineering business processes by partner organizations to achieve seamless integration of key processes [25]. At the minimum, this requires the partners to establish smooth exchange of data and information between themselves. To that end, it may be necessary to modify the current IS applications to interface with the IOS and to establish procedures and adjustments in inter-organizational process details with trading partners [14]. While IOS provides the possibility of exchanging data, the objective of doing so is to better utilize these data. So, the potential value of IOS is realized only when it is fully integrated with the other internal information systems [35]. The capability of the legacy systems and IT infrastructure affects to a significant extent the difficulty of implementing IOS and reaping the benefits of using the system. Firms that possess the necessary legacy IS capability experience less risk and derive more benefits from IOS [34]. Dailey [6] asserts that trading partners must organize cross-functional IOS teams as they move towards deeper and more meaningful business relationships. Moreover, both parties need to make a commitment to change or streamline business processes to achieve optimal benefits and even strategic advantage in the marketplace. The interorganizational business process re-engineering is an indispensable part of IOS, which asks for openness and standardization of internal business process in order to connect two corporations’ business process. Though this is an easy statement to make, corporations find it extremely excruciating in real life to have to divulge information, particularly if the operations involve proprietary materials [32].

Among all published research papers about CSF, only one paper focuses on the CFS of RosettaNet standard-based IOS. Olson [30] concludes four CSFs from the RosettaNet implementation case of Avnet: (1) a clearly articulated business objective, (2) a dedicated cross-functional team with the appropriate skills and executive support, (3) adequate attention to technology integration, systems testing and performance measurement and (4) a detailed project plan and effective communication. These factors may be grouped broadly under the following categories:

(1) psychological perception by organizational players—trust, motivation and consensus;

(2) organizational preparation and response—crossorganizational team, preparation and coordination, project management and maintenance and BPR;

(3) IS/IT infrastructure;

(4) industry standard.

## 3. Research method

The first two authors were invited by RosettaNet organization in China to visit Xiao Tong in Beijing at the end of 2002 to study the adoption of RosettaNet standard in China. The IOS of Cisco and Xiao Tong was an attractive research subject for reasons we mentioned in Section 1. After obtaining the agreement from Xiao Tong, we began our case study to find its CSFs. To learn more about the story and analyze the benefits of the IOS to Cisco and Xiao Tong, we used the following research methods: interview with core personnel in each corporation, satisfaction evaluation survey to find out the differences before and after the IOS implementation and data from company documents and publicly available news and reports.

We interviewed the following persons during our 5- day field trip:

\- Five senior managers: the CEO and the CIO of Xiao Tong and the IT Director from the IT vendor; two senior managers of Cisco, who supervised the IOS project with Xiao Tong from both business and IT aspects. (The interviews with Cisco managers were conducted via the telephone.)

\- Five heads of departments that were affected by the IOS, such as inventory and logistics in Xiao Tong.

\- Six employees who went through the implementation of the IOS project and experienced the change associated with it. They were the key staff in the purchase order, inventory and logistics departments in Xiao Tong.

The interviews were conducted based on the following procedure. Using the name list provided by our host, we contacted the five senior managers of the two firms to obtain their permission for interview, their help in identifying other persons to interview and in providing us with company documents related to the IOS project. We were given the names and contact details of the five heads of department and six other employees whom we subsequently contacted. We used the simple framework developed in Section 2 as a basis for our structured interview, inviting them to comment on our findings based on literature research. (The interview protocol can be found at Appendix A at the end of this paper.) Every formal interview, either faceto-face or through telephone, lasted for 1.5–2 h. We were aware that respondents to telephone interviews were more likely to discuss issues perceived as legitimate than to share more sensitive issues related to politics and culture [38]. However, telephone interviews may reduce interviewer bias because there is no face-to-face contact between interviewer and respondent [8].

All interviews were audio recorded and transcribed by our research assistants. The transcripts were checked by the researchers and verified with the interviewees; minor clarifications were made and corrections incorporated. We then wrote the case using all the background materials and findings of the interviews. These were presented to all the interviewees to seek their final approval before presenting them to the public.

Besides the interview, we also designed a structured satisfaction evaluation questionnaire to record the difference before and after the IOS implementation. The aim was to obtain some quantitative data as well as more information on corporation background and the degree of implementation success. The questionnaire adopted five grades, from 1 to 5, representing very unsatisfied, slightly unsatisfied, average, slightly satisfied and very satisfied, respectively. The questionnaire is in Appendix A. One IT staff of Xiao Tong helped us to distribute these questionnaires to all the interview participants and the staff members of the business departments who used the IOS. Because of his help, all the 40 questionnaires sent out were returned.

Most of the background information of this case was from minutes of meetings and discussion memo, project plans, project management documents and progress reviews. We also consulted publicly available materials inside and outside the Xiao Tong, including those on organizational structures and responsibilities, external news and annual reports of Xiao Tong.

## 3.1. How to evaluate success

Although most of stakeholders of this project share the view that the IOS has been a success, we need to employ a set of criteria to reach this conclusion. The business value evaluation of IOS is neither simple nor straightforward. There is no general agreement on an absolute indicator for successful IOS implementation and operation. Many criteria on effectiveness, such as the gains in operational efficiency from various retail distribution processes, the reduction of operational personnel and/or their time and product sales increasing due to more timely and accurate market information, seem useful to compute the cost/benefit of this IOS. But in practice, it is difficult to provide meaningful estimations [28].

Evaluation is never a totally rational judgment, independent of the situation of an organization and of the background and bias of the stakeholders. In essence, evaluation is a subjective thinking process wherein the relevant evaluators make comprehensive considerations based on the understanding of firms, market, technique and other aspects [22]. Satisfaction-based evaluation is one efficient and simple supplementary method that can be used in IOS effectiveness evaluation. The satisfaction evaluation of the stakeholders is as important as other objective methods employed to judge the project’s success or failure. Many famous evaluation theories adopt satisfaction evaluation as the main tool, such as Delone’s IS Success Model in 1992; Oliver’s ABCD Evaluation, Delphi method, etc. In this paper, we demonstrate the success of this case both from the calculated effectiveness and satisfaction evaluation of internal staffs of Xiao Tong.

## 4. The inter-organizational information system of Cisco and Xiao Tong

## 4.1. Case background

With the dot-com implosion in few years ago, and also with the fierce competition in IT market, Cisco Company, one of the biggest network equipment providers, experienced its toughest years ever in 2001 and 2002. To survive under such a tough market, Cisco was embarking on cost-saving, cutting down the manufacturing cost and sales cost. However, these traditional ways had reached the point where there was little left to be gained [12]. Today’s managers are focusing more on the new Internet enabled collaborative strategies to gain competitive advantage through value generation. In early 2001, Cisco began to promote their

B2B plan: integration of its commerce site and customers’ back-end systems through a combination of off-the-shelf software and RosettaNet standards [21].

The B2B plan in China began in May 2001. Cisco chose Xiao Tong, one of its four first-tier distributors in greater China, as the first partner in the IOS project for reasons, which we would explain later. Xiao Tong was founded in 1990, being in charge of the distributing of Cisco’s network production, such as router, switch, to the lower levels’ wholesalers. With the rapid development of China, the revenue of Cisco in China’s network products market was growing rapidly, accounting for almost 6% of the whole global market revenue. The revenue of Xiao Tong was responsible for 11.8% of Cisco’s revenue in China (2001). And for the Xiao Tong, Cisco was its biggest supplier, about 60% of its transaction was related to Cisco’s products. So, Cisco was very significant for Xiao Tong.

## 4.2. The inter-organizational information system of Cisco and Xiao Tong

The IOS project of Cisco and Xiao Tong was reported as the first successful one that strictly conforms to XML protocol and e-Business RosettaNet standard in China. The project began in May 2001 and lasted almost one and a half years. The business processes involved in this case were mainly related to Purchase Order (PO) execution between Cisco and Xiao Tong, including product information update, product configuration, PO submission, PO status update and invoice pre-check-in, respectively. Significant re-engineering occurred in these business processes after the IOS implementation. Fig. 1 shows the cross processes diagram of Cisco and Xiao Tong with the new system support.

The key difference between pre-IOS processes and post-IOS processes was shown in the dashed lines.

When there was no IOS between Cisco and Xiao Tong, all the information was transmitted in batch by hand. But things changed drastically after the IOS implementation when all the information between the two companies could be transferred automatically in real time by means of off-the-shelf IOS software and RosettaNet standard. According to the different focuses, we would like to introduce the process change in five parts from left to right in the Fig. 1.

## 4.2.1. Part I: product information updating

Cisco could deliver part numbers and price information to Xiao Tong’s ERP system through this process directly. This information could be used to verify the data integrity of Xiao Tong’s PO (purchase order)/SO (sales order) entry.

## 4.2.2. Part II: MLC integration

This phase removed the need to do configuration check in Cisco Connect Online (CCO). Any salesperson or other staffs who had the right to configure could just do it in their own ERP system. In the old process, when the products needed to be configured, the salesperson sent the request to two mediating layers before reaching the person responsible for it in Cisco. And the result was given back to the salesperson after the configuration. The time spent on the transfer was much more than the time of configuration.

## 4.2.3. Part III: submitting PO

With this process in place, Xiao Tong could submit PO in its own ERP system instead of on CCO. In the previous process, the staff in Xiao Tong’s product planning department had to visit CCO to do configuration, and then submitted PO on CCO, followed by retyping PO data in their own ERP system. The new process eliminated the procedure of submitting PO on CCO and consequently removed the work of retyping the same set of data in ERP system and CCO, which would reduce typing errors and processing time.

## 4.2.4. Part IV: PO status updating

It provided the possibility to the product planning department and the distribution division to check the status of PO. Instead of checking PO status on CCO or calling Cisco by phone, they could now check their status in Xiao Tong’s own ERP system.

## 4.2.5. Part V: invoice pre-check in

All the information in Cisco’s commercial invoice would be sent to Xiao Tong through this process. After receiving all the information in its ERP system, Xiao Tong could know the product shipping schedule precisely, which makes it feasible for Xiao Tong to do sufficient preparations for the incoming goods. As soon as the products arrived, they could scan in all products according to the serial numbers without error. Thus, Xiao Tong could have the accurate information for every product in its warehouse. With it, they could trace the serial number for sales commission calculation. Besides, they could also make their smart payment schedule according to this information.

The implementation of Cisco and Xiao Tong’s IOS project was a great success. The cycle from SO to PO was around 3 days in the old ‘‘submitting PO’’ process for repeated input in ERP system and CCO, and the new process had been reduced it to 2.5 days. Although only 0.5 days was cut, its related cost saving was the equivalent of US\$ 33,000 annually. In addition, from the qualitative business perspective, there were other improvements in organizational performance. One of intangible benefits was that the managers of Xiao Tong could get data related to its sales activities more quickly, more accurately and more integrally than ever before, and those data supported more scientific decision making. The managers expressed satisfaction with the IOSs support of their ability to improve decision making, as indicated in the pre-IOS figure of 3.00 to post-IOS figure of 4.33 (on a scale of 1.00– 5.00).

The successful implementation of IOS brought Cisco significant advantages as well. The satisfaction concerning strengthening the relationship between Xiao Tong and Cisco was increased from 3.12 to 4.12 (on a scale of 1.00–5.00). The benefits for Cisco through Xiao Tong’s successful implementation of IOS appeared in the form of more accurate information, like inventory, sales quality per product and sales speed. These data helped to manage the distributors, predict market trend and reduce operational cost. One year ago, Xiao Tong could merely provide Cisco with monthly sales report. Monthly sales reports had the problems of long interval between two reports of data and poor accuracy of these data. Besides, some individuals had the chance to tamper data for their own interests during the long data submission process, which reduced the profits of Cisco. After the implementation of IOS, Cisco could get the daily sales report from Xiao Tong. More timely and accurate information helped Cisco to improve the accuracy of forecasting and deploy lean production. It also facilitated the transmitting of various policies to its distributors, which reduced the total cost of operation.

## 5. Critical success factors of the interorganizational information system

The successful implementation of Cisco and Xiao Tong IOS project depended on a set of critical successful factors. To avoid repeating familiar factors that were often cited in traditional information systems like leadership, investment, communication, training [2,31], we just present the most important and distinctive CSFs of IOS on this case. By combining the interview findings and literature review, we list out the CSFs of IOS in Cisco and Xiao Tong in three clusters. They were (1) decision motivation, (2) implementation process and (3) infrastructure conditions.

## 5.1. CSFs which motivated the decision

When corporations were considering IOS, those factors, which motivated them to adopt IOS were extremely significant for their future success. Unlike the case of internal information system, the motivation for IOS was more complex. Therefore, strong motivation became one important factor to influence the success of IOS.

## 5.1.1. CSF1: strong motivation was essential to gain and thereby guaranteed the commitment from partners

The project of this case was initiated by Cisco. The motivation of Cisco was mainly based on long-term strategy. Cisco possessed a very good track record in developing, implementing and operating internal information systems. In order to compete in the global market at the level of supply chain, Cisco needed to expand its information systems with its global agents and partners. Such strategic rationale was the organizational underpinning of its very strong commitment in this project.

The motivation of Xiao Tong was relatively weaker. The pressure from Cisco was the main reason why Xiao Tong agreed to implement IOS. Firstly, as Cisco was the dominant player in this supply chain, Xiao Tong had to go along with the business strategy of its principal supplier Cisco. And secondly, the capital arrangements proposed by Cisco was designed to win the active participation of Xiao Tong in the whole project. The capital arrangement consisted of two parts. The first part was certain sales profit rebate in the first 2 years after implementation of IOS. Cisco promised to return some profits to Xiao Tong to compensate for the costs in IOS implementation. The second part was that Cisco covered all software cost for Xiao Tong, amounting to about US\$ 350,000. It was admitted by the manager of Xiao Tong that if the cost of software were to be paid by his company, this project could have been postponed for financial reason. And another reason of Xiao Tong was from the perspective of competitiveness. Its market share and revenue were lagging behind those of its direct competitors, another three major distributors of Cisco in China. As part of the strategy to narrow the gap with its rivals or even to overtake them, Xiao Tong wanted to cut the logistic cost and reduced the procurement cost through IOS.

Cisco wished to improve the transparency of its distribution channel and acquired as much information of sales and marketing as possible. Xiao Tong hoped to lower cost and to get faster response time so as to enhance its comprehensive competition in the supply chain. Neither of them could realize their business vision without the collaboration of the other. So they became natural allies with compatible business vision. The strong motivation from both sides was the main driver to initiate IOS and then to guarantee the commitment from both sides to the project. This, in turn, affected how the effective the IOS would be used.

## 5.1.2. CSF2: shared motivation and vision

After about 10 years of cooperation, the basic cooperative relationship and the resulting trust between Cisco and Xiao Tong had been established by the time the IOS project was initiated. However, since the operational activities of these two companies would occur through IOS, there was still a need for further communication to reach consensus on the vision. Before the launch of IOS project, the top managers of Xiao Tong and Cisco had a long and thorough discussion about the blueprint and objectives of IOS. The final consensus on process was reached after many negotiations and compromises. The blueprint and objectives of IOS were transmitted rapidly to stakeholders of this project by both sides. The stakeholders were asked for their feedbacks, which turned out to be relatively minor. These were duly incorporated, thereby laying an important foundation stone of the project.

Armed with the clear business vision by the top of both firms, the managerial and technical staffs could embark on the project together. The shared vision of IOS among all stakeholders would reduce the divergences of opinion in the implementation process and in turn lowered the risk and shorten the period of IOS project. It also played an important role to ensure the success of this case.

## 5.2. CSFs in implementation process cluster

This cluster focuses on implementation process of IOS project. The CSFs here will pay more attention to the detailed operational and management aspects in the implementation process.

## 5.2.1. CSF3: cross-organizational implementation team

The implementation team for the IOS in this case consisted of three parties, i.e. Xiao Tong, Cisco and the third-party implementation vendor. Four sub-teams were built, namely the management team, the business team, the technical team and the partner team based on these three parties, as shown in Fig. 2.

The management team was composed of top executives of the two companies, reflecting the strong commitment of their top management to the project. This team functioned as the organizer of the whole project, directly contacting and coordinating the other three teams.

The technical team mainly consisted of the technicians from the third party vendor. It was a professional team with strong technological ability. The business team was made up of people from the business departments related with this IOS project. They were involved early so that they could define optimized processes, provide primary data and implement the new processes later on. The partner team refered to Xiao Tong’s business partner Cisco who played a highly active part in the whole IOS project. During whole implementation process, the four teams from different organizations collaborated closely to ensure the final success.

From the project initiation to the plan deployment and to the implementing process, Xiao Tong never deviated from effective communication and close cooperation with Cisco. On the other hand, Cisco also shared with Xiao Tong its abundant knowledge and experience accumulated from previous IOS implementation with other partners in other countries. Moreover, Cisco proposed the detailed planning schedule to Xiao Tong and timely provided important parameter configurations for the new system. By so doing, it enabled the implementation process to proceed smoothly throughout the project life cycle. And the multi-party implementation team, to some degree, was also helpful to bridge the culture differences between Cisco and Xiao Tong.

The success of this approach illustrated the importance of cross-organizational implementation team in IOS project. In order to reap mutual benefits, each party should be actively engaged in setting up the implementation team and in timely solving the problems as they appeared in the implementation process.

## 5.2.2. CSF4: high integration with internal information systems

Inter-organizational information system between two corporations was in itself not the ultimate goal of any IOS project. After all, the value created by IOS, such as faster response time, higher data integrity, was the direct reflection of the internal operation performance of a firm and not simply the result of IOS. In the case under study here, the project had generated value for both Cisco and Xiao Tong. One reason was that both of them had very good internal information system foundation, which could handle the exchange data efficiently and create added value.

If a corporation had no way to integrate IOS with internal information system and to utilize or handle exchanged information, then the value of IOS would be greatly diminished. It was true for both initiator and follower. The initiator might have stronger interests on this point and will tend to bring about the integration between IOS and internal information systems. The follower may need the initiator to ‘‘pull’’ (offer aids) or ‘‘push’’ (exert pressure) so as to achieve better integration with its internal information systems.

## 5.2.3. CSF5: inter-organizational business process re-engineering

The inter-organizational BPR in this case includes two aspects. The first one was the adjustment of process. Non-value-added parts of business process were expunged after the implementation of IOS. For example, Xiao Tong needs not check the latest status of PO from CCO any more since the PO status of Xiao Tong system was totally synchronous with Cisco’s after IOS. And Xiao Tong could improve post-sales service by entering the serial number of damaged product into its ERP system directly, and need not fax serial number to Cisco first and wait Cisco confirmation then change or repair the product for customers, two parts of this process were omitted after inter-organizational BPR.

The other aspect was mainly about new form of cooperation between two firms. In the old process, only the production assistant of Cisco had the right to configure product for customers. So, it usually needed four persons to deal with product configuration, namely the salesperson of Xiao Tong’s subsidiary ! the PO assistant of Xiao Tong’s subsidiary ! the PO assistant of Xiao Tong’s headquarter ! the PO assistant of Cisco. The time used for configuration was less than 0.33 h, whereas the time consumed by transfer during the process was almost 1.5 h. After implementing IOS, all the authorized sales persons could log on Cisco configuring interface directly from Xiao Tong’s own ERP system to configure products for customers, and customers could get their product configuration result within 20 min. This was one of the examples how Cisco and Xiao Tong redefine their duties and responsibilities to ensure the smooth operation of IOS.

## 5.3. CSFs in infrastructure condition cluster

The CSFs here mainly deal with the technological foundation or platform of IOS. In traditional internal system, technology was less emphasized since it was relatively mature. IOS was more demanding in technological terms, especially in terms of existing information system and technology standard.

## 5.3.1. CSF6: advanced legacy information system and infrastructure

In this case, Cisco has mature infrastructure and adequate capability that could align well with the IOS. The conditions in the partners were likely to vary from very preliminary to moderate mature. To ensure the success of its first IOS in China, Cisco carried out a holistic evaluation of the legacy systems and infrastructure in each of its distributors in China, particularly the ERP system before making final decision. Xiao Tong’s ERP modules had covered almost every aspect of its operations, such as procurement, finance management, order management, logistics and inventory management. Moreover, all the modules were integrated seamlessly. Xiao Tong also had experts of information system implementation. With all these technological features, Xiao Tong was chosen by Cisco to implement its first IOS in China.

## 5.3.2. CSF7: shared industry standard

For companies belonging to the same supply chain, it makes sense for them to select the same standards when they are going to implement IOS. There are already some industry standards that could satisfy the requirement of electronic transaction among cooperative partners, from earlier EDI or present Web Based Inter-exchange (WBI), or the RosettaNet e-Business standard in this case. It was generally the IOS initiator that would propose one kind of widely recognized IOS standard, or continues to use one standard that has been adopted successfully with other partners. The followerpartners will usually accept and follow the standard proposed by initiator.

Compared with other earlier standards, RosettaNet standards are the fruit of rich experience and intimate cooperation of the leading high-tech companies in the world. RosettaNet Partner Interface Processes (PIPs) define business processes between supply chain partners, providing the models and documents for the implementation of standards. Such standards enable companies to establish better business cooperative relationship, reduce cost and improve productivity. Meanwhile, RosettaNet also has good practicability and flexibility. It permits users to self-define the process when there are no existing standards to meet their particular needs.

Thanks to the high quality of RosettaNet standards, the implementation of IOS in Cisco and Xiao Tong was very efficiently carried out and at low cost. In the considered opinion of the participants, it was one CSF of the IOS project.

## 5.4. Discussion of the findings

The findings of our case study suggest that critical success factors for web-based IOS are very similar to EDI-based IOS. Though web technology offers much more flexibility and convenience and it provides a cheaper way to develop and operate IOS, there is much that IT managers of web-based IOS can learn from the experiences of developing, implementing and operating EDI-based IOS. In a way, this is not surprising as these two categories of IOS perform similar tasks and face similar kinds of organizational problems. Both of them need certain operational environment like basic database, internal information systems and data communication network. And they are one kind of complex application that needs experienced project manager and team. Most important of all, the organizations using the IOS must derive benefits from the system, must be able to co-operate with each other and must perceive their business relationship as interdependence. In our case study, they are all reflected in the three clusters as summarized in Table 2.

We did query our interviewees about the likely cultural differences between the two companies and their likely consequences of the project. They explained that Cisco was a multi-national company, and it had Chinese subsidiaries in Beijing and Shanghai. Cisco had been conducting most of its business with Xiao Tong via Cisco China, so there were almost no significant cultural differences between them. In addition, the culture differences, even they did exist, had diminished during their 10 years fruitful cooperation before the start of the IOS project.

## 6. Conclusion

This research uses a case study method to examine the success story of the IOS of Cisco and Xiao Tong in China. It confirms the organizational benefits of IOS as reported in research literature and trade magazines, such as increased co-ordination, shorter sales or production turnabout times, greater monitoring capability and elimination of redundant tasks. Its major contribution lies in identifying seven critical success factors which are grouped into three clusters, and in offering some guidelines for corporations that consider IOS adoption with partners, especially for those which want to cooperate with Chinese business firms.

The research has some limitations that one should bear in mind. Although the authors have gone to great length to probe the most essential as well as general critical success factors for inter-organizational information systems implementation, the seven factors might not be complete since it is a single case based study. It is believed that with more and more IOS cases, the list of factors can be modified. Besides, the survey research method should be used to quantify the weight of these CSFs in the IOS project, and examine the interaction among them. And finally, different countries have different cultures, business practices and business institutions. Whether the CSFs of IOS of companies operating in China can apply to those operating in other countries remains to be tested.
