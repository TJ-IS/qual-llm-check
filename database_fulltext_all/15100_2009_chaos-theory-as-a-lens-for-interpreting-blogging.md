Abstrac t: Blogging is becoming increasingly popular as a global phenomenon. Individual blog traffic and blogosphere structure are of interest to academia and practice. Although it is difficult to get a snapshot of the blogosphere with enough blogs over a long enough period to capture the real situation, chaos theory finds underlying order in this apparent random and complex phenomenon. This study provides an overall view of blogging from micro (individual blog traffic dynamics) and macro (blogosphere structure) levels through a chaos theory lens. Key concepts of chaos theory are used to construct an interpretive framework to illustrate blog system behavior dynamics. Blog systems tend to be nonlinear, dynamic, and deterministic, as well as sensitive to initial conditions. The study also demonstrates the feasibility of applying chaos theory thinking to areas such as knowledge management and the recent global financial crisis. Implications for practice and research opportunities are presented.

Key word s and phrases: blogging, blogosphere, blogosphere structure, blog traffic, chaos theory, global financial crisis, knowledge management.

Continued ac elerated use of computer-mediated communication (CMC) technologies (e.g., Facebook, wiki, and blog) provides new opportunities and challenges. Organizations may consider knowledge management issues in this collective community with huge numbers where individuals do not simply congregate. The interaction between these communities and major sociopolitical events, such as the global financial crisis, warrants attention. At the micro level, highly divergent motivations, demographics, and so forth characterize these CMC users. At the macro level, users as a collective community exhibit large-scale, exponential growth in a complex manner that challenges traditional analysis techniques.

Chaos theory exposes underlying order in this apparent random and complex phenomenon. Chaos theory is “the qualitative study of unstable aperiodic behavior in deterministic non-linear dynamical systems” [22, p. 2] that shows promise in a wide range of domains. Management theorists have used chaos theory concepts to explain the complexity in organizations [33] and complex organizational dynamics (e.g., [14, 23, 27, 33, 39]). More recently, attention has been given to information systems (e.g., [13, 32]). These studies apply chaos theory to complex systems in a variety of fields through which the theory shows its power and attraction.

Blogs (also known as Web logs or weblogs) present an especially attractive target for chaos theory application. Blogs are a form of CMC technology through which people can conveniently publish and establish networks online. Through blogs, people express their personal ideas and opinions. They can also share their experiences of life and work, as well as participate through collaboration and discussion in the form of conversations (interrelated blog posts and comments) [16]. Advanced blogging tools facilitate easy, fast, and inexpensive Web composition. It is as easy as clicking a button to update a blog so that anyone can manage it easily [8].

Blogs are becoming increasingly popular. According to Technorati’s “State of the Blogosphere 2008” report,<sup>1</sup> there have been 133 million blog records indexed by Technorati since 2002, with 900,000 blog posts in 24 hours. Blogs are now a global phenomenon attracting extended attention. Technorati tracked blogs in 81 languages in June 2008. Firms have joined blogging in the form of corporate blogs; for example, there were about 61 Fortune 500 companies including Microsoft, IBM, and Google that had at least one public-facing corporate blog as of October 9, 2008.<sup>2</sup> Corporate blogs emerge with their own specific purposes. For example, Amazon.com uses a public blog to announce product enhancements and provide support. Boeing’s vice president of marketing, Randy Baseler, gives Boeing’s forecast of the airline market and comments in his personal blog to publicize Boeing’s products.<sup>3</sup>

These popular blogs are read by thousands of people, with the implication that blogs have a potential for advertising. Companies pay thousands of dollars to popular blogs for the purpose of advertising. Technorati reports that one in four bloggers use three or more means of advertising.<sup>4</sup> Online advertising is accepted in the model of costper-click, and thus traffic is emphasized. They assume that advertisements thrive on wide readership, and so traffic is important to these bloggers. Technorati’s “State of the Blogosphere 2008” reports that among the 48 percent of bloggers who currently do not have advertising on their site, one in five bloggers feel that they do not have a critical mass of traffic to their blog. With the increased popularity of blogging, the blogosphere is growing rapidly, and blogs have to face readership competition. In the blogging ecosystem, traffic distribution follows a power law distribution [38]. A small set of blogs accounts for a majority of the traffic in the blog world, whereas the others typically suffer from poor readership, possibly used only by friends and family members. For blogs with limited audiences, the readership and communication are also important motivators for bloggers [34]. In short, most bloggers desire traffic.

Blogs are about links [7]. Blog tools with technology features facilitate easy, fast, and inexpensive Web composition with the possibility for blogs to easily link to each other. This mechanism leads to the huge size of the blogosphere defined by Technorati (www.technorati.com) as the ecosystem of interconnected communities of bloggers and readers that takes place at the convergence of journalism and conversation with a complex topology structure. With the convenient technology features of permalinks, comments, trackbacks, and so forth, many blogs update with high frequency and receive feedback rapidly throughout the blogosphere. It is this kind of intense interaction with freedom of choice that is the core of blogosphere linkage structure and blog traffic.

Resultant broad and deep social and collaborative effects are of interest to academia and practice. At the micro level, what are the main characteristics of blog traffic dynamics? What are the factors affecting blog traffic? How do they affect it? What are the characteristics and underlying mechanisms for the topology structure (e.g., power-law distributions [38]) of the blogosphere at the macro level? Traditional Web and Web graph studies make use of a static snapshot, but what happens over time for the blogosphere? This paper explores these questions interpretively through the lens of chaos theory from a systems perspective.

## Literature Review

A blog is d efined as “frequently modified web pages in which dated entries are listed in reverse chronological sequence” [19, p. 1]. As a CMC, most blogs support the following common tools [19]: comment apparatus allowing readers/visitors to express their opinions on the blog entry, trackback capabilities informing bloggers of traffic related to their original entries, archives (storerooms of blog entries) enabling visitors to find historical content, and permalinks allowing blogs to link posts with one another. These blog attributes contribute greatly to the mechanism with which the blogosphere is maintained with topology structure and become a networked mass of linked people and information [8]. It is argued that the connective and contributive attributes of blogging have caused it to spread virally throughout the Web, thus differentiating it from traditional home pages [31]. These tools offer technology features to facilitate interactions between bloggers and visitors, and also enhance conversations among bloggers. For example, through permalinks and comments, both the bloggers and readers can discuss their opinions on specific topics through an iterative cycle of postings and comments.

At the micro level, individual blog traffic emanates from these interactions. Some famous blog services Web sites, such as Technorati (www.technorati.com) and Truthlaidbear (www.truthlaidbear.com), use inbound links—that is, links from other blogs—as the measure to compute popularity ranking, an indirect measurement of blog traffic. Traditional Web traffic statistical services, such as Alexa (www.alexa. com), provide reach and page views traffic rank to measure Web site traffic. These are some ways to approximate the real readership and visit of Web sites.

Highly divergent characteristics of blogs have been studied by researchers and blog motivations have been explored. Among the thousands of blogs created every day by bloggers, many are motivated by different reasons: documenting one’s life, providing commentaries and opinions, expressing deeply felt emotions, articulating ideas through writing, forming and maintaining community forums, and so forth. Blog content is equally diverse, ranging from journals of daily activities to serious commentaries on important issues [35]. In the literature, the distribution of blog types has been summarized according to their primary purpose as personal journals, filters, k(nowledge)-logs (blogs created as environments for knowledge sharing), and mixed-purpose blogs [19]. The studies examine blogs from the viewpoint of motivation, type, and activity pattern.

A few researchers have focused on blog traffic. Du and Wagner [15] examine blog success from the technology perspective. Visitation and interaction patterns between bloggers and visitors, and some specific technological factors, have also been explored [21]. These factors have been studied through traditional linear analysis in a short period of time (e.g., several months or days). However, blog traffic is affected by factors in other dimensions, such as external events (e.g., terrorist acts, political events) [20], contents, blogger’s social relationships, and so forth. These factors do not affect blog traffic in a simple linear relationship and are not easily measured with the traditional models.

At the macro level, earlier studies about topology structure examine World Wide Web (WWW) graph and link structures. The in-degree of the Web graph follows a power-law distribution [5]. Link structure has been applied for community identification [41]. As blogs are more frequently updated and with fast evolving properties, the blog phenomenon has proven to be fertile ground for academic research, such as the occurrence of “bursty” link creation [26]. Kumar et al. [25] conclude that layers in the blogosphere are explained in terms of geographic or demographic correlations, shared locations or shared interests, and bursts of activity. Conversational blogging practices have also been explored [16]. Diversity plus freedom of choice creates inequality, and the greater the diversity, the more extreme the inequality [38].

Unregulated growth leads to a huge and complex web, which then becomes a large directed graph. The enormous size and the continual changing of documents and links in the WWW make it impossible to catalogue all the vertices and edges [2]. In addition, conventional social network analysis methods have developed stochastic static relationship structures but suffer from limitations of understanding online communication with high volatility and temporal elements [40]. The same is true for the blogosphere. Blogs are updated with high frequency, and hence the links among blogs. They also change over time. Properties of traffic distributions and clustering coefficients depend on the time frame. Highly divergent motivations, demographics, and so forth characterize blogs. Large-scale, exponential continuing growth with a highly diverse linkage in the complex manner of the blogosphere gives challenges to traditional linear methodologies. It is not easy to get a snapshot of the blogosphere with enough blogs over a long enough time to capture the real situation. Past research has been restricted to unshared collections of individual research groups where each captures small pieces of the complex blogosphere topology structure [29].

## Chaos Theory

Chaos theory was originally pioneered by Lorenz’s [28] work on the dynamics of turbulent flow in fluids. His interest in chaos came about accidentally through the work on weather prediction in 1961. However, during the past 50 years, interest in chaos theory has also grown among researchers from different fields, such as mathematics, biology, computer science, physics, politics, population dynamics, psychology, material science, organization science, and economics (e.g., [27, 37, 39]). Systems characterized as complex, unpredictable, and irregular (with which traditional models, largely focusing on regularity and stability, are not able to deal) have been studied by chaos theory. Chaos theory or chaotic systems thinking (CST) gives an explanation of nonlinear systems that marks a fundamental change and strikes a better understanding of the balance between order and disorder [6]. In nonlinear systems, small inputs can sometimes lead to large outputs, and small disruptions in the initial conditions sometimes (but not always) can make the system volatile and impose significant variances. Periods may be punctuated by sudden change, apparent patterns of behavior may disappear, and new patterns unexpectedly emerge [32].

## Chaotic Systems Characteristics

Chaotic systems meet the following three criteria [22, 27]:

1. Nonlinear. Relationships between cause and effect are not constant, and systems behavior responds to input nonlinearly and in an unanticipated fashion.

Small inputs can have large effects, and large inputs can result in no significant change [32]. Linear systems can be calculated and predicted by dissecting their components; for instance, function (or operator) f maps an input x(t) to an output f (x(t)). In linear systems, $f ( \alpha x _ { 1 } ( t ) + \beta x _ { 2 } ( t ) ) = \alpha f ( x _ { 1 } ( t ) ) + \beta f ( x _ { 2 } ( t ) )$ holds, where a and b are any scalar values. By contrast, nonlinear systems usually cannot be analyzed by adding their respective components because the subsets of systems interact. $f ( \alpha x _ { _ 1 } ( t ) + \beta x _ { _ 2 } ( t ) ) = \alpha f ( x _ { _ 1 } ( t ) ) + \beta f ( x _ { _ 2 } ( t ) )$ does not hold, because the independent variables $x _ { 1 } ( t )$ and $x _ { 2 } ( t )$ interact. It is possible that the system behavior $f ( \alpha x _ { _ 1 } ( t ) + \beta x _ { _ 2 } ( t ) )$ under factor $\alpha x _ { _ 1 } ( t ) + \beta x _ { _ 2 } ( t )$ is quite different from $\mathsf { o } f ( x _ { 1 } ( t ) ) + \mathsf { \beta } f ( x _ { 2 } ( t ) )$

2. Dynamic. Systems are unstable and shift between semistable states.

3. Deterministic. Chaotic systems are dependent on their initial conditions, and they are seemingly random but actually do not lack order. For every affecting factor $x _ { i }$ , there is a specific $y _ { i } ( t ) = f ( x _ { i } ( t ) )$ , and thus the system behavior $Y ( t ) = f ( X ( t ) ) = f ( x _ { 1 } ( t ) , x _ { 2 } ( t ) , . . . , x _ { N } ( t ) )$ is determined, where N is the number of affecting factors (independent variables).

## Suggestions Given by Chaos Theory

The following qualitative properties are often observed in chaotic systems:

•	 Sensitivity to initial conditions. Small disturbances, through the iterative nonlinear feedback that reinforces the original change, will exponentially accumulate and, as a result, lead to large fluctuations. Thus, chaotic systems are sensitive to initial conditions. In fact, two otherwise identical chaotic systems with slightly different initial conditions will eventually diverge greatly, no matter how small the difference of initial conditions is [22].

•	 Fractal: scale invariance. Systems behavior can be generated through infinitely nested simple elements. Similar patterns are traced by a system, whatever horizon is used to view it. Information between part and macrocosm is isomorphic.

## Definition

This paper examines blogs from a systems perspective. A chaotic system has both an exterior (perceptible surface) and interior (internal essence) [43]. Here, a blog system is defined as a combination of the blog, its environment (social environment and technology background), and the blogger’s behavior (social/off-blog interaction and online/on-blog communication). At the micro level, the system is an individual blog including its environment and blogger’s behavior. At the macro level, the blog system is the blogosphere with the interaction structures in its environment.

## Internal Versus External

Blogs act within both internal and external environments. A blog is a CMC through which people conveniently interact with each other online. At the same time, blogs feature in people’s offline/off-blog life—that is, the real world. There is an intersection of blogging and offline relationships. Blogs enable relationship formation, with some of the new relationships later extending to offline meetings [3]. As Herring [20] points out, the blogosphere has also been fertilized by a series of external events that have inspired blogging activity. These activities are realized through technologysupported functions: permalinks and trackbacks enabling bloggers’ online discussion and conversations as well as comments enabling bloggers’ and readers’ frequent and deep interactions. Thus, blogs act in the real world. The bloggers communicate with their friends and families in the real world as well as with unknown readers on the Internet [34].

## Objective Versus Subjective

Blog systems exhibit both subjective and objective elements. Through technologysupported blogs, people exchange their opinions on social events and technology (objective, outside the blogger’s control). People’s social interactions (subjective, within the blogger’s control), both online and offline, are influenced by (and influence) the social environment and technology background.

Based on the dimensions in Figure 2, forces affecting blog system behavior come from three facets: the internal environment, external environment, and interface (blog and blogger). ## Characteristics of Blog Systems

Involved in the complex interactions among a number of forces, blog system behavior has the following characteristics.

## Nonlinear

At the micro level, the first point is that relationships between forces and blog traffic are nonlinear; for example, the celebrity effect on the lead in the sharp jump of traffic; another example is the famous Chinese actress Xu Jinglei, who began her blog on October 25, 2005. Her first post: “Shall I begin my blog career? Not prepared . . . Wait for me . . . not prepared . . .” received 448 comments and 107,623 readings. Thousands of people read it just because she was popular, which soared her to the top in Technorati’s ranking on May 8, 2006. Yet not all famous people’s blogs enjoy this “celebrity effect.” That is, the factor of “celebrity” sometimes (but not always) has striking effects. While most posts and ideas quickly disappear in the huge information database of the blogosphere, certain ones enjoy massive readerships and linkages. The second point is that interactions exist among factors. An example is “celebrity” and “comments.” Clearly, comments can increase the popularity of the blog and, hence, the celebrity of the blogger. The increasing popularity of the blog, in turn, brings more visits, and hence more comments. The third point is that interaction exists between factors and traffic, as evidenced that blog visits increased with the number of new comments and the increased visits were also coupled with more new comments [21]. It seems that these complex interactions between blog traffic and factors raise challenges to reductionism methods in traditional linear analysis. The nonlinear relationship between individual blog traffic and affecting forces indicates that the linkage structure among blogs is determined in a nonlinear manner at the macro level. An example is the “resonance” phenomenon where a massive response in the community is triggered by a tiny event in the social environment [17]. Blog systems react to social events nonlinearly at the macro level.

## Dynamic

Blog systems affecting factors vary with time. Naturally, blog system behavior $Y ( t ) = \{ Y _ { m i c r o } ( t ) , Y _ { m a c r o } ( t ) \} f ( x ( t ) )$ is expected to be dynamic. At the micro level, blog traffic can change every minute as a result of complex interactions. Figure 3 shows Matt Drudge’s blog (www.drudgereport.com) traffic in terms of reach in a five-year strong period recorded by Alexa (www.alexa.com). At the macro level, the blogosphere is exponential, continuing growing in size and influence with connected blog communities appearing with global events. An example is the 2008 global financial event for which Figure 4 shows the dynamics of blog posts about “Financial & Crisis” during September and October 2008, which is traced by BlogPulse (www.blogpulse.com). Blogosphere posts are only a piece of the blog system behavior, but we can estimate that the sudden huge new posts will change the individual blog traffic and the links among the blogs; hence, the blogosphere topology structure.

## Deterministic

Each factor has a specific effect on blog systems. Given all the forces in a blog system, the system behavior is deterministic. In other words, the future state of the blog system is causally determined by the current and past states. Given blog systems state $Y ( t )$ and all the affecting factors X(t) specified during the time period from t to t + 1, the system behavior $Y ( t + 1 )$ is determined. This shows that there is possibly some order behind the blog systems’ complex and seemingly random appearance.

In conclusion, a blog system is a nonlinear, dynamic, and deterministic system. This leads to

Axiom 1: Blog systems are chaotic both at the micro and macro levels.

## Strange attractors

For blog systems, a strange attractor is a system behavior pattern that is a subset of the whole. the behavior could temporarily waver in this subset. at the micro level, in situations where most factors are static and do not act nonlinearly, traffi c may stay be still or fl uctuate in a narrow range; some factors may also attract a higher degree of attention and the traffi c may jump suddenly. In a short time period, it may still remain very large; however, these temporary patterns are fragile. any “knock” on the internal, external, or surface conditions may lead to the traffi c moving to another pattern or a new strange attractor. at the macro level, the blogosphere usually exhibits “storming”

accompanied with social events. For example, Figure 4 shows the dynamics of the “Financial & Crisis” blog posts during September and October 2008. Noteworthy is the sudden “jump” of “Financial & Crisis” posts around September 17. Through a closer look at the posts traced by BlogPulse, a huge number of posts are talking about the U.S. government seizing control of American International Group Inc. (AIG)— one of the world’s biggest insurers—in an \$85 billion deal that signaled the intensity of concerns that the danger of a collapse would pose to the financial system. The blogosphere sustained very high interest in “Financial & Crisis” (about 1.2 percent of all blog posts) around September 17. However, the temporal high interest began to wane around September 19. The blogosphere’s extremely high interest in “Financial & Crisis” is a strange attractor; the fact that this pattern is fragile is evidenced by interest dismissal in the following days.

## Bifurcation

A bifurcation in a blog system is the sudden appearance of oscillations that shift between two patterns. The bifurcation process is usually caused by nonlinear factors such as external events or blogger characteristics. Familiar examples include a unique report of political events leading to a sudden jump of traffic, or a superstar’s behavior in the real world causing people’s attention to his or her blog. The financial crisis creates a huge number of posts and conversations in the blogosphere at a sudden time. The U.S takeover of AIG in an \$85 billion bailout on September 17 led the bifurcation of blogosphere behavior in terms of post numbers, new famous blogs and linkage structure, and so forth.

## Iteration

Iteration is the process of cycles of interaction between blog system behaviors and various factors. An example is the power law distribution and an inequality suggested by Shirky [38]; that is, those who garnered traffic early in the history of blogging for whatever reason will tend to continue to gain traffic over time in the iteration of “more traffic, much celebrity; much celebrity, more traffic.” Blog systems’ affecting factors vary with time. Blogs usually react to these changes with high-frequency updating (usually one or several times in a day), and blog tools (such as comments, trackbacks, etc.) facilitate intense further interactions in the blogosphere. Thus, the frequency of iteration from $\{ Y _ { t } , X _ { t } \}$ to $\{ Y _ { t + 1 } , X _ { t + 1 } \}$ in the blogosphere can be high, along with these affecting factor variations and blogs updating.

## Scale Invariance

A notion of chaos theory is that the patterns of system behaviors are independent of scale, exhibiting fractal behavior. In other words, similar patterns could be expected in different scales. In Figure 3, similar layers of wrinkly appearance that are visually distinguishable (following studies in the stock market, using mathematical methods, including time series, to compute the “dimension of fractal” are expected for further validation) can be observed both at the five-year and the one-year level. In fact, in accordance with the nature of fractals, if we record traffic every minute, it will be observed that whatever the horizon, similar patterns appear. That is, the traffic evolution over years will reflect monthly and daily evolution. What is more, one of the major achievements of chaos theory is its ability to demonstrate how a simple set of deterministic relationships can produce patterned yet unpredictable outcomes [27]. For our specific case, all the patterns can be generated through combinations, nesting, and iteration of the five fundamental patterns shown in Figure 5. Patterns a and b are mainly the factors when linear relationships are working, patterns d and e are the nonlinear effects working, while pattern c is the result of all factors being generally static or the effect of factors counteracting.

## Axiom 2: Blog traffic exhibits self-similar dynamics.

The blogosphere is huge; thus, we first select one small piece of a blogger’s conversation case to analyze the basic interaction pattern. The conversation is about different attitudes and opinions toward the recent global financial crisis, initiated from a film about the credit system crisis. The case concerns a relatively small-range conversation surrounding the initial post and 15 comments afterward, within two days (October 2–3, 2008).<sup>5</sup>

![](/api/attachments/3UXXZV5U/fulltext/images/8507dc2877b512fcdb07d6cdcf9711425780558cdeb0d49c130c33de7ae2d13a.jpg)

In order to illustrate so-called blogosphere stories [12], we analyzed the relationships among the original posts and all the comments. We are cognizant of the fact that it is difficult to fully capture every detail of the conversation. Figure 6 focuses on two dimensions—“person” and “time.”

Figure 6 illustrates two points: first, regarding the “person” dimension, there are several core (“hub”) people in the conversation structure—that is, participant 1 (Edward) and participant 2 (Brandon). This is evidenced by: (1) they contribute more than their counterparts, which can be represented to an extent by the number of post/ comments uploaded by them (one post and four comments by Edward, five comments among 15 in total by Brandon), and (2) most of the links (i.e., those arrowed) are connecting (either pointing to or pointing from) post/comments by Edward and Brandon with those of others. If we remove all of the post/comments and links related with participants 1 and 2, then there are only three lines left. Second, with respect to the “time” dimension, as we mentioned, the whole conversation is introduced by the external objective events, based on its quickly achieving a summation or conclusion and then “disappearing” in a sense.

In this simple conversation, there is a core, Edward and Brandon. A large-scale study by Broder et al. [9] reveals that the Web graph shows the bow-tie structure, with a giant component, the CORE, which is strongly connected. There is also a CORE in the blogosphere, the “A‑ list” traced by blog services Web sites, such as Technorati’s “top 100” [38]. This shows some similarity structure between the huge blogosphere and basic communities.

In fact, the blogosphere is composed of communities aggregated by blogs with the same topic, demographic, and so forth. Communities have subcommunities with finer inner structure. This self-similar structure is illustrated through the Sierpin;ski triangle in mathematics in Figure 7. In the basic simple communities, interactions are enabled through blog tools such as comments, permalinks and trackback, and so forth. The global financial crisis case shows this interaction. These basic communities evolve with similar patterns and thus compose a huge blogosphere. This leads us to Axiom 3.

Axiom 3: The blogosphere is composed of a self-similar topology structure.

## Prediction in Short Term and Long Term

According to the assumptions in Axiom 2, traffic patterns are generated from the five fundamental components exhibited in Figure 5. Given the condition at time t, computer simulations can specify the blog traffic at time t + 1 under a certain kind of deterministic algorithm (usually a linear relationship is used to approach nonlinear relationships between factors and traffic, which is a common and fundamental method in computational mathematics discipline). Through this step-by-step approach, forecasting is possible for a short period. However, in the long term, given the sensitive nonlinear relationship between traffic and initial conditions, even a small change of the initial condition multiplied over time through iterations will cause large fluctuations. Also, blog traffic evolves in a dynamic fashion over time as a result of complex interactions among factors of interior, surface, and exterior. It was impossible to do a quantitative study with our limited resources and information technology. The same logic applies to the macro level of blog systems. While we could use fundamental patterns and the evolving network model with algorithms to detect community structures (e.g., [18]), it was not possible to take the future social events and their impact into account, thus the long-term prediction was impossible. This leads to the conclusion that for blog systems either at the micro level or macro level, short-term prediction is doable, but long-term prediction is not.

Implications, Limitations, and Directions for Future Research

Chaos theory prov id es a means of und erstand ing blog systems within the complex interactions among factors. Based on our explanation at the micro and macro levels, there are implications for individual bloggers and possible domains for application. At the micro level, there are several suggestions for bloggers:

![](/api/attachments/3UXXZV5U/fulltext/images/662262d415ebf306e2ed37598f551c7fb9256888ab2fa2b7d4844dc48ed3b00a.jpg)  
Figure 7. Blogosphere with Self-Similar Topology Structure

1. U se small changes to increase large effects through the iteration. Blog traffic is sensitive to initial conditions, and a small positive change may lead to unexpected returns. Bloggers are suggested to maintain details that make for sustained positive change. Examples include broadcasting their blogs in many places (e.g., offline communities, blog communities, etc.) and trying to write the content with exact resources in every post.

2. Convert incidents into new opportunities through the effect of nonlinear relationships. Blog traffic sometimes responds abruptly to some factors (patterns d and e of Figure 5), which means opportunities for bifurcation—shifting abruptly to the pattern fluctuates on a much larger value level. It is suggested that bloggers should be prepared and actively make use of these opportunities. For example, political blogs should trace social events timely.

3. Be sure that any apparent current state is treated as temporary. Blog traffic is dynamic and impossible to predict because traffic is sensitive to initial conditions that can change with time. New initial conditions and strange attractors may come out the following day or even in the next minute. Bloggers should be alert and diligently engage traffic competition in the fast-growing blogosphere.

As Agarwal et al. [1] point out, the networks enabled by digital platforms and technologies create substantial value for the larger society in multiple ways. In our case of blogging, at the macro level, understanding of blogging from a chaos theory framework is promising with implications in various domains.

1. Increasing participation in online communities in the blogosphere. Communities exhibit self-similar structure and “A‑ list” blogs that account for most of the overall traffic. For this structure and blog unique characteristics as a

CMC, which mechanisms and policy could increase the online communities participation? Based on specific context (e.g., corporate blogs), implications may arise.

2. Information diffusion and knowledge management. Understanding knowledge management in the topology structure of the communities and blogosphere is important, given the huge quantity of posts and interactions among bloggers and readers. The information and knowledge are diffused through the self-similar topology structure. Ways to detect and manage this information/knowledge diffusion based on this structure can assist managers and marketing people.

3. Organization management. For example, a corporate public-facing blog (which is built to support interaction with customers and publicly represent the company) traffic is partly an indicator that presents company perspectives for public attention and market position. Through chaos theory thinking, managerial implications are suggested: (a) It would help to explore significant issues in the organization through watching bifurcations in the traffic. For example, if bifurcation occurs, what is the reason? What are the possible reasons behind the strange attractor? An examination through the interaction between the posts and comments may give some clues. (b) Through these accumulated experiences, a proactive orientation could be established before or during the coming of forces that cause bifurcation. Of course, this demands (and hence develops) an organization’s ability to rapidly respond to changes in turbulent environments and generate new knowledge.

Beyond blogging lies a rich set of domains that are opportunities in waiting for chaos theory application. Examples of unstable aperiodic behavior in deterministic nonlinear dynamical systems abound in a wide range of business and sociopolitical arenas. Financial crises, pandemic communication, societal upheavals, and election campaigns are but a few examples. Chaos theory invokes a new way of thinking about (and understanding) these potentially volatile phenomena that is beyond the scope of traditional analytical approaches.

This is but a first attempt to study blogging through the lens of chaos theory that originated and was developed in physics and mathematics contexts. Several limitations exist in this study. First, the study applies suggestions from chaos theory to blogs. More mathematical consequences are warranted. In Axiom 2, it is insufficient to only visually represent fractals. A mathematical framework needs to be constructed to analyze the similar patterns of blog traffic in different time scales similar to the study on cotton and stock price curves by Mandelbrot [30]. Second, more examples and cases for different blog types (in terms of content, motivation, etc.) are needed to validate the axioms as a precursor to propositions. Our strategy is to pick small pieces of the blogosphere to capture the fundamental patterns. However, huge scale data support for analysis like the work in Web graph structure (e.g., [9]) will add rigor to the insights.

Despite these limitations, this constructed conceptual platform opens new avenues for future research, especially empirical studies to further provide directions for practice, including:

![](/api/attachments/3UXXZV5U/fulltext/images/693d8a6b7315160ea7b78f382b0c578db43a7ff33365eb794588c5ededae3f16.jpg)  
Figure 8. Blog Traffic Progress Patterns of the Successful Group

1. Categorizing the blogosphere in terms of traffic dynamics progress patterns. As suggested by chaos theory, complexity could come from iteration of a set of simple deterministic patterns. In our study, we identified five fundamental patterns in blog traffic dynamics. We could apply these patterns to identify blog traffic dynamics. For example, we collected 77 blogs from the top 100 on June 7, 2007, from Technorati and analyzed traffic (in terms of rank in the whole Internet) during the period (typically two to five years) that the rank was within the top 100,000. Thirty-two (41.6 percent) of them (including 25 blogs) ranking in the top 50 exhibits the consistency with respect to Figure 8. This is the cluster of successful ones, but what about the others in the blog ecosystem? For different groups, the balance between chaos and order appearing in periods is different.

Determining the role, type, and rank of blogs in the ecosystem may lead to specific implications for strategy formation. A well-known example is Boeing’s vice president of marketing, Randy Baseler, who chose to open up his blog the day before the Airbus A380 was formally introduced as a strong competitor of the Boeing A747. As the vice president of marketing, Baseler saw the role of his blog as publicizing his product. He chose to strategically utilize the event so that attention would be given to Airbus’s major competitor during the introduction of a new important product. Thus, his “celebrity effect” would garner large traffic and achieve his marketing goal.

2. T he effect of the dimensions on the evolution of blog systems and short-term prediction. To understand the nonlinear effect between external factors and blog system behavior, empirical relationships deserve exploration. For example, Krishnamurthy [24] found that the daily number of posts increased, while the number of links per post decreased in the week after the events of September 11, 2001. Through the accumulated understanding of external factors and their effect on blogs (although precise prediction in the long term is still impossible), less uncertainty of blog system prediction could be achieved or at least it would shed light on our understanding of blog system behavior in turbulent environments.

3. Knowledge management in blog communities. Some researchers (e.g., [4, 42]) realize that weblogs can be a potential tool for supporting knowledge management because they are easy to use at a low cost. As explained earlier, blog systems exhibit specific structural characteristics. There is usually a core in blog communities and “A‑ list” in the blogosphere. What is their role in the knowledge creation, sharing, and distribution process? We did a content analysis of all the post/comments in the global financial conversation cases in order to better capture the roles the core Edward and Brandon played in the conversation. Similar with de Moor and Efimova [12], we categorized all the post/comments into opinion, vote, response, summary, and resource, and then analyzed their mutual relationships. Along with the arguments, debates, and knowledge contributions, the core Edward and Brandon played critical roles at stimulating the whole conversation, re-leading following ones, and summarizing previous discussions. The Appendix summarizes the types and relationships based on our content analysis. Additional field studies could be carried out to help understand the roles by the core group and, hence, proactive/ incentive policies to help improve the knowledge management effectiveness and efficiency. Further, some design issues (e.g., community design), based on the core group role, also warrant additional exploration.

## Conclusions

This paper presents c haos theory as a framework for understanding blog system behavior over time as a result of complex interactions among factors. Based on chaos theory thinking, a blog system is defined from two dimensions (internal versus external and subjective versus objective) according to its communication mechanism as a CMC. Affecting factors are categorized. By conceptualizing a blog system as a chaotic system, axioms are drawn. While a blog system exhibits a certain kind of deterministic order that enables short-term prediction, long-term forecasting is impossible, and emergent fluctuations appear unexpectedly due to the system behavior’s sensitivity to initial conditions. Further, chaos theory suggests that at the micro level, blog traffic evokes self-similar dynamics in terms of time scale; at the macro level, the blogosphere is composed of a self-similar topology structure. This signals that blog system behavior seems complex but does not lack order. There are many implications for practice that suggest opportunities for future research across a wide range of domains.
