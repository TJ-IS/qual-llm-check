## Abstract

Mobile data services (MDS) are wireless value-added pay-per-use services that have attracted increased attention in recent years. In this paper, a theoretical framework is proposed to investigate key drivers of user behavior in wireless pay-per-use services based on a value perspective. This study examines the role of three evaluation criteria – utilitarian, hedonic, and social values – in adoption decisions. Potential adopters have no direct experience with MDS; thus, they likely conceive value based primarily on indirect experience with it, such as through communication with peers or advertisements. In this study, the influence of members in the social networks and external sources are regarded as the major sources of information in order to capture the role of these factors on the perceptions of value. Additionally, according to the age and gender of potential adopters, changes in the degree to which the antecedents lead to MDS acceptance are examined. The proposed model is empirically tested using survey data collected from 287 potential adopters. The analysis results show that the proposed model, based on the aforementioned view of value, provides a significant explanation of the variance in the level of adoption intention toward MDS. The results of this study indicate that utilitarian and social values dominate adoption decisions, whereas the impact of hedonic value in MDS acceptance is weaker than other values. Information from relevant others and from mass media play a critical role in forming the perceptions of value obtained from the use of MDS. The results also shed light on the moderating effects of age and gender on MDS acceptance. Theoretical and practical implications of this work are discussed. ## Introduction

M <sup>obile</sup> <sup>data</sup> <sup>services</sup> <sup>(MDS)</sup> <sup>have</sup> <sup>diffused</sup> <sup>all</sup> <sup>over</sup> <sup>the</sup>globe due to the rapid adoption of Internet- globe due to the rapid adoption of Internetaccessible mobile devices. Advances in mobile technology have allowed people unprecedented opportunities to access digitalized content without time and space constraints. MDS is considered as a technological revolution in that it provides users, regardless of their location, with a constant source of information and data while also offers them highly personalized services (Hong et al., 2006). However, even in Asia, which is ahead of North America in terms of the adoption of MDS, its revenue only accounts for between 5% and 20% of the total revenue made by telecommunication service providers in 2005 (Minges, 2005). Moreover, recent service providers are faced with strong price competition in the voice market due to the entrance of new participants as well as the maturity of this market, resulting in a steady decline in the average return per user (Funk, 2007; Market Research, 2007; Research and Markets, 2007). From a service provider perspective, the focus is likely to be on value-added services such as MDS in order to increase revenue (Funk, 2007; Seo et al., 2008). On the research side, initial adoption studies are needed in order to identify user adoption criteria for MDS, and to suggest strategies for the market development of MDS (Lu et al., 2005; Hong and Tam, 2006). As initial adoption is the first step in long-term usage, understanding adoption decision processes would effectively help MDS providers to attract new users.

MDS researchers (Kim et al., 2007; Turel et al., 2007) stress that user acceptance of MDS is not fully explained by the theory of reasoned action (TRA) (Ajzen and Fishbein, 1980), the technology acceptance model (TAM) (Davis, 1989), or the innovation diffusion theory (Rogers, 1995), which are all widely used to explain adoption trends as they pertain to information systems (IS). These perspectives have focused on the employee’s adoption within the work place, where IS is widely conceived as a tool to improve task-related performance $( \mathrm { e . g . , }$ Jeyaraj et al., 2006; Bunker et $a l . ,$ 2008). Most systems in organizational settings are either free for users or charge a one-time fee that grants daily usage $( \mathrm { e . g . , }$ Oh et $a l . ,$ 2003; Choudrie and Dwivedi, 2006; Dwivedi et al., 2007). In contrast, MDS is charged on a pay-per-use basis, so users decide on the usage of a service by comparing the received and given components (Garbacz and Thompson Jr, 2007; Kim et al., 2007; Turel et al., 2007). Several works on the marketing and IS disciplines regard perceived value as a prominent determinant in the formation processes of behavior toward pay-per-use services (Zeithaml, 1988; Venkatesh and Brown, 2001). In the context of MDS, a great deal of empirical research on MDS has recognized the importance of perceived value in MDS acceptance (e.g., Kim et al., 2007; Turel et al., 2007). Seen from this point of view, the ability of MDS providers to offer a high level of value would encourage many customers to consider it as an important means of obtaining different types of content regardless of time or location. In a similar vein, this study attempts to develop a research model in terms of the value perspective.

Zanna and Rempel (1988) suggested that the attitudes and perceptions of individuals form on the basis of three types of information: information concerning past behavior, affective information, and cognitive information. Given that potential adopters have no direct experience with MDS, they form their attitudes and perceptions primarily based indirectly through affective and cognitive information (Karahanna et al., 1999). For potential adopters, uncertainty is likely created in terms of its expected value due to relatively superficial information on MDS. In psychology and consumer behavior studies, it has been demonstrated that individuals are generally hesitant when confronted with uncertainty (Salancik and Pfeffer, 1978; Burkhardt and Brass, 1990); thus, it follows that they would interact with members of social networks or seek information to reduce their uncertainty in their adoption of MDS (e.g., Hoch and Ha, 1986; Dwivedi et al., 2007). Potential adopters obtain their knowledge about MDS from friends, family, and secondary sources and these beliefs are defined as normative beliefs according to Brown and Venkatesh (2005). Thus, interpersonal and external sources’ influences fulfill a critical role in the process of the formation of value regarding MDS. In this regard, the influences of relevant others and external sources on the potential adopters values are examined.

Researchers on IS have been interested in the moderating effects of demographic characteristics on the behavior toward IS $( \mathrm { e . g . , }$ Agarwal and Prasad, 1999; Venkatesh and

Brown, 2001). The unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT) model (Venkatesh et al., 2003) recognized that the age and gender of IS users are the key elements in individual IS acceptance outcomes. Psychological research suggests that men and women are different in terms of information processing (e.g., Eagly and Carli, 1981; Lundeberg et al., 1994). Like gender, age has been theorized to affect individual decision-making processes (Agarwal and Prasad, 1999; Morris and Venkatesh, 2000; Iscan and Naktiyok, 2005). However, relatively little attention has been paid to the moderating role of age and gender in terms of how it affects the adoption of MDS. The current study examines how the age and gender of MDS users influence MDS acceptance.

This study has three objectives. First, it aims to develop a theoretical framework based on the perspective of value. This perspective can offer the better understanding of MDS acceptance by identifying important value dimensions of its adoption. Second, the impacts of interpersonal and external sources’ influences on potential adopters’ perceptions of MDS value are investigated. Such findings can clarify the relative importance of interpersonal and external sources influences on the decision-making process. Finally, this study empirically examines the moderating role of age and gender on the adoption of MDS. From theoretical and practical perspectives, ample information on the determinants of user acceptance regarding MDS is given.

The next section deals with related theoretical backgrounds. The subsequent section introduces the theoretical framework of this study identifying the antecedents, moderating variables and their influences on adoption behavior with regards to MDS. Following this, the research methodology is described. The results of structural equation modeling (SEM) are presented in the further section. In another section, the theoretical and practical implications of this study are discussed. The last section concludes the paper, identifies its limitations, and outlines directions for further research.

## Theoretical development

For the past two decades, research on IS acceptance has been a dominant issue in the literature related to user behavior. A variety of theoretical perspectives have been developed to understand the adoption of IS. Theories related to TRA, TAM, and innovation diffusion are widely and popularly used to analyze the behavior and perceptions of IS users (Ajzen and Fishbein, 1980; Davis, 1989; Rogers, 1995). Since the early 2000s, numerous studies have attempted to find and explore the key factors in MDS behavior in order to understand user’s adoption decisionmaking processes (e.g., Lu et al., 2005; Hong and Tam, 2006; Kim et $a l . ,$ 2007). Many works on MDS have pointed out that the recent acceptance model cannot fully account for user acceptance levels of MDS, as the perception of a usage fee is regarded as an essential barrier of behavioral intention to employ a pay-per-use type of MDS, in contrast to traditional IS acceptance (Hong et al., 2006; Kim et al., 2007; Turel et al., 2007).

Kim et al. (2007) considered MDS users as service consumers because they evaluate MDS value based on its benefits and costs. The value-based theory was developed based on the theory of consumer choice and decision making from a maximization view of monetary value. This line of reasoning identified perceived usefulness and perceived enjoyment as the benefit components of monetary value, and considers perceived fees and technicality as sacrifice components. User acceptance of MDS is largely explained by perceptions of the monetary value of MDS. Hong and Tam (2006) developed a model consisting of five sets of adoption factors: general technology perceptions, technology-specific perceptions, user psychographics, social influence, and demographics. They empirically showed that the antecedents of MDS adoption not only differ from those of traditional IS in the work place, but that they also depend on the nature of MDS and its usage context. In their study, the monetary value of MDS has a strong effect on the adoption of MDS. Hong et al. (2006) and Lee et al. (2007) also found that the perceived value plays a critical role in MDS adoption regardless of different mobile data categories and different cultural profiles.

Both the marketing and the IS disciplines have proved that perceived value is multi-dimensional and can be measured by a variety of instruments (e.g., Sweeney and Soutar, 2001; Turel et al., 2007). In the marketing domain, Sweeney and Soutar (2001) conceptualize perceived value as four distinct value dimensions: quality, emotional, monetary, and social. Quality value is a functional value that captures the utility resulting from instrumental value. Emotional value is the utility derived from the affective feelings of customers that the service generates. Monetary value is a functional dimension connected to the monetary benefits and costs involved in using the service. Social value is the public recognition that would be achieved as a result of using the service. In light of this, Turel et al. (2007) examined user acceptance of short messaging services (SMS). Research in IS has shown that three evaluation criteria are key factors to determinate adoption intentions of IS (Venkatesh and Brown, 2001; Brown and Venkatesh, 2005). First, utilitarian value relates to the effectiveness and efficiency resulting from the use of IS. Its value is highly associated with functional values such as the quality and monetary value considered in marketing disciplines. Second, hedonic value represents the fun or pleasure derived from using the IS. Third, social value is defined as enhancement of the social image of the user through the use of the IS.

## Research model and hypotheses

## Antecedents of adoption intention

In both IS and marketing disciplines, utilitarian, hedonic, and social values cover a broad set of perceived values that individuals regard as being important in pay-per-use services. To capture a holistic view of MDS acceptance, this study attempts to extend the research framework into a broader perspective of value, including not only utilitarian but also hedonic and social value. A broad view of value can provide further knowledge regarding the determinants of MDS acceptance.

## Utilitarian value

Utilitarian value is related to a functional dimension; thus, the adoption of MDS can be understood as a means of accomplishing some task-related goal (Holbrook and Batra, 1987). MDS has several useful services such as traffic information, mobile banking, and location-based information, so it offers users a good opportunity to attain certain goals. Several studies on MDS have confirmed the saliency of utilitarian values in explaining MDS acceptance (e.g., Kim et al., 2007; Turel et al., 2007). Thus, it is expected that utilitarian value fulfills a dominant role in adopting MDS.

H1: Utilitarian value has a positive influence on adoption intention.

## Hedonic value

Hedonic value reflects enjoyment, pleasure, and anxiety related to the use of a product/service (Holbrook and Hirschman, 1982). According to Berlyne (1974), hedonic value occurs when something is pleasurable or rewarding in itself. As MDS technology is used for personal needs more than it is for workplace purposes, the entertainment potential of MDS is expected to have a strong influence on the adoption decision (Ha et al., 2007). Van der Heijden (2004) showed that hedonic value has a stronger impact on adoption intention as it pertains to the hedonic-oriented IS compared to utilitarian value. Similar to video games and PC games, MDS provides both online and casual games as well as entertainment services such as MP3 music and digital comic books. Thus, we hypothesize that potential adopters who perceive MDS as enjoyable and emotionally fulfilling are more likely to adopt MDS.

H2: Hedonic value has a positive influence on adoption intention.

## Social value

Several investigations in the IS domain reported that the desire to gain status is an important determinant prompting adoption intention (Fisher and Price, 1992). Specifically, it has been suggested that the desire for social outcomes is driven by the resultant reference power, which gives the actor performing the behavior power within her or his social group (e.g., Rogers, 1995; Brown and Venkatesh, 2005). Social value is justified by its consistent predictive power of adoption toward innovative IS (Venkatesh et al., 2003; Brown and Venkatesh, 2005). Individuals can enhance their status by adopting MDS, as MDS may be perceived as trendy and innovative (Rice and Katz, 2003). Such enhanced status enables them to serve as role models for those who come later and allows them membership into a particular social class. Thus, it is expected that social value will serve as a critical criterion in the choice of a potential adopter.

H3: Social value has a positive influence on adoption intention.

## Antecedents of perceived value

A basic tenet of marketing and consumer behavior theory holds that when consumers make purchase or adoption decisions, they use different types of information sources to evaluate a product or service, and to make choices (Keaveney and Parthasarathy, 2001). Information sources are characterized in interpersonal and external sources. Prior studies on consumer searching behavior have shown that the types of information sources differently affect customers’ assessments of their choice in terms of service characteristics (e.g., Hoch and Ha, 1986; Dwivedi et al., 2007). Different types of information sources and their effects on perceived value are discussed in this section.

## Interpersonal influences

Interpersonal influences are defined as the extent to which members of a social network influence the behavior of one another (Rice, 1990). The UTAUT model recognizes the prominent role of interpersonal influences in the employee’s adoption processes within the organizational setting. Interpersonal influences in the UTAUT model are interactions and communication with managerial personnel, supervisors, and peer worker in an effort to understand the value of a specific type of IS in the work place. Brown and Venkatesh (2005) showed that relevant others such as friends, family, and other important connections serve as the key predictors of the adoption decision of a home PC. Potential adopters of MDS have little prior experience with MDS and have little confidence in their own ability to judge the service. According to research on consumer information search behavior, potential adopters prefer to ask advice from others to help them judge the level of value (Furse et al., 1984). For this reason, the opinions, decisions, and behaviors of other people can help to form perceptions of the value of MDS by potential adopters. Moreover, using a type of MDS that is widely accepted by group members is a means of enhancing membership through increased interactions within the group; thus, potential adopters can achieve improved status within a social group (Hong and Tam, 2006). For these reasons, it is hypothesized that the value of MDS is influenced by the views of social proximal referents.

H4a: Interpersonal influences have a positive influence on utilitarian value.

H4b: Interpersonal influences have a positive influence on hedonic value.

H4c: Interpersonal influences have a positive influence on social value.

## External sources’ influences

External sources’ influences are the extent to which information from TV, newspapers, the Internet, and other secondary sources influences behavior (Venkatesh and Brown, 2001). In the marketing discipline, it is assumed that secondary sources are fundamental media that can transfer messages and offer information about a target product or service. Prior research in the marketing and IS areas has found that customers engage in external information search when considering whether to adopt a new product or service (Hoch and Ha, 1986). In particular, customers who seek impersonal sources are interested in obtaining objective and factual information about the service attributes. ## Moderating role of age and gender

Demographic characteristics have been regarded as a key element in the innovation diffusion literature (e.g., Agarwal and Prasad, 1999; Venkatesh and Brown, 2001; Choudrie and Dwivedi, 2006). Of the many demographic variables, this research focuses on the moderating role of age and gender on MDS adoption. Research in the MDS domain has identified the importance of the moderating role of age and gender on the decision-making processes of users (e.g., Ha et al., 2007; Seo et al., 2008). Several empirical studies have examined the effect of gender differences on adoption intention (e.g., Venkatesh and Morris, 2000; Venkatesh et al., 2003). The UTAUT model provides evidence that women are normally more anxious than men when considering new IS and that they are much more likely to be sensitive to the opinions of others compared to men. These gender effects are likely driven by the differences in the information processes for women and men that result from socially constructed gender roles (Lubinski et al., 1983). Age-related differences in adoption toward IS have been studied by Brown and Venkatesh (2005) and by Ha et al. (2007). Innovation diffusion theory suggests that young people are likely to adopt new IS products due to their tendency to pursue innovativeness (Rogers, 1995). For this reason, the age and gender of potential adopters likely play a crucial part in adoption intention in an MDS context. The overall research model is presented in Figure 1.

## Research methodology

## Instrument development

From prior studies concerning marketing and IS, constructs that have been used and validated by other researchers are adopted in this study. The question items were reworded to suit the target MDS context. Each question was measured on a seven-point Likert-type scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). Before implementing the survey, two professors of MIS and marketing, six doctoral students of MIS, and three practitioners engaged in the MDS industry were asked to read and refine the questionnaire. Based on their feedback, the modified questionnaire was pilot-tested on 32 university students. All of the questionnaires were shown to have reliability and validity. The survey items are listed in Appendix A.

## Subjects and data collection

The proposed model was empirically tested using a paperbased survey data in South Korea. South Korea is a suitable place for the adoption research of MDS since the penetration of Internet-accessible mobile devices is one of the highest in the world (Minges, 2005). Moreover, it first launched the third generation high-speed network based on the code division multi access (CDMA) 1x EV-DO in January 2002. In the data collection, several teachers, professors, and informants were asked to gather data from the students in their classes or the employees at their companies. Small gifts are given to the respondents. Approximately 1400 questionnaires were distributed in the first 2 weeks of November 2006. In this study, potential users are defined as people who have never used MDS but who may be experienced with SMS. SMS differs from other types of MDS in that users do not need mobile access to the Internet to use it. Respondents with no direct experience with MDS numbered 302, and 15 responses were discarded because they were incomplete. The final sample numbered 287, and Table 1 shows the demographic data of the respondents. The age of the respondents in the final sample ranged from 13 to 57 years; the median of age was 26. This study divides age by the median into younger and older people. According to the 2007 survey on MDS conducted by National Internet Development Agency of Korea (2007), young people are considered to be the biggest MDS adopters. The result of our demographics reveals similar figures.

## Research results

## Measurement mode

A confirmatory factor analysis using partial least squares (PLS) via the PLS Graph Version 3.0 was conducted to test the measurement model. Frequently used in IS research, PLS is particularly useful for this study because it is robust to relatively lean sample sizes (Chin, 1998). First, to check reliability, composite reliability (CR) and the average variance extracted (AVE) values were calculated (Fornell and Larcker, 1981). The reliability was deemed acceptable if the CR value was 0.70 or higher and the AVE value was 0.50 or higher. As shown in Table 3, all factors met both criteria for acceptable reliability. Second, convergent validity can be established if item loadings are 0.60 or higher (Chin et al., 1997). The lowest loading of this study was 0.83, satisfying the convergent validity prerequisites. Third, to examine discriminant validity, the shared variances between factors were compared with the AVE values of individual factors (Chin et al., 1997). The diagonal in Table 4 contains the square root of the AVE values. All AVE values were greater than the off-diagonal elements in the corresponding rows and columns, which confirmed discriminant validity. Lastly, to overcome the concern of common method bias in survey research, Harman’s one-factor test was used to check common method variance (Podsakoff and Organ, 1986). If a single factor emerges from the analysis or when one general factor accounts for the majority of the covariance in the interdependent and dependent variables, the threat of common method bias is high. Factor analysis did not indicate a single factor that explained the large covariance. Thus, common method bias does not account for the results of this study.

## Discussions and implications

## Theoretical implications

In this study, a theoretical model based on value perspective was formulated and empirically tested. The research framework provides an in-depth understanding of the key values influencing MDS acceptance. From a literature review of the marketing and IS domains, this study suggests that the intention to adopt MDS is dominated by the three evaluation values: utilitarian, hedonic, and social values. By demonstrating that these evaluation criteria are the key drivers in the intention to adopt MDS, this research provides preliminary evidence that the value perspective succinctly covers a broad set of factors that individuals regard as being critical in MDS adoption decisions. In particular, utilitarian value is found to be the strongest predictor of behavior toward MDS as IS researchers have posited that the utilitarian feature related to the productivity is the strongest determinant in IS acceptance (Venkatesh and Brown, 2001).

According to motivation theory, the perceptions of utilitarian and social values represent extrinsic motivation while the perceptions of hedonic value belong to intrinsic motivation (Zanna and Rempel, 1988). Prior research in the IS domain suggests that individuals with high perceptions of extrinsic and intrinsic motivation have greater intention to adopt IS $( \mathrm { e . g . , }$ Van der Heijden, 2004; Turel et al., 2007). This study confirms the saliency of extrinsic motivation in determining MDS acceptance. On the other hand, the influence of intrinsic motivation on adoption intention is weaker than the effect of extrinsic motivation. A possible explanation of this finding may be related to the decisionmaking processes of potential adopters. Research concerning consumer behavior has suggested that customers search for arguments and reasons to justify their choices (Dhar and Wertenbroch, 2000). Hedonic consumption delivers benefits primarily based on experiential enjoyment that is obtained from MDS (Millar and Millar, 1996). From this point of view, potential adopters identify their enjoyment based on relatively superficial information from social and secondary sources, implying that it is difficult for them to evaluate hedonic value without any experience with MDS. In addition, according to marketing research, consumers have a guilty conscience about hedonic consumption when deciding whether to purchase new products or use new services (Strahilevitz and Myers, 1998). Thus, for MDS based on usage-based pricing, the use of hedonic services would be associated with a feeling of guilt, implying that perceptions of intrinsic motivation weakly affect adoption intention toward MDS.

The influences of indirect experience serve as key elements in conceiving the value obtained from MDS. As potential adopters do not have broad knowledge of MDS, they are likely to communicate with members of social networks such as friends, family, and important others or obtain their information from messages conveyed through external sources such as advertisements and news. The results of SEM indicate that these influences adequately explain the perceptions of value; they account for 42% of the variance of utilitarian value, 42% of the variance of hedonic value, and 53% of the variance of social value. However, although prior studies (e.g., Venkatesh et al., 2003; Choudrie and Dwivedi, 2006) suggested that individuals are affected by both interpersonal and external sources’ influences, researchers have shed little light on the relative impacts of these influences in the initial adoption decision processes. The results of this study suggest that potential adopters consider information from social interactions as more influential compared to messages from mass media. In line with these findings, psychology and decision-making research has suggested that individuals view knowledge from important others as more informative and trustworthy than knowledge from secondary sources (O’Reilly, 1982). Notably, Burnkrant and Cousineau (1975) showed that individuals tend to comply with group norms toward a conforming perspective. This study helps MDS researchers to understand the relative importance of the sources of information in the formation processes of MDS acceptance.

This study examines the degree to which the antecedents leading to MDS behavior change depending on the age and gender of potential adopters. The analysis results reveal that the utilitarian value of older persons is more strongly affected by interpersonal influences compared to younger people. Concurrent with the present results, psychology studies suggest that older people are more likely to conform to the opinion of others than are young people (Evans et al., 1979). Gender was also found to have a significant effect on MDS acceptance, which is in accord with the results of Seo et al. (2008). Psychology research has shown that males adopt a competitive attitude regarding the evaluations of others while females are more accepting of the opinions of others (Miller, 1976; Eagly and Carli, 1981). The UTAUT model also suggests that females are more sensitive to the opinions of social proximal referents in adopting IS. Consistent with this view, social influences are more salient among women than among men when forming an intention to adopt MDS. In contrast, men put more weight on secondary sources in the overall decision process concerning the adoption of MDS compared to women. In this regard, knowledge gained from external sources represents information that is more critical in the decision-making process of men compared to women.

## Practical implications

This study provides several important implications for MDS practitioners. First, it shows that potential adopters are strongly affected by the utilitarian value as obtained from MDS use. MDS practitioners need to understand that the perception of utilitarian value plays a critical role in accelerating MDS diffusion. MDS practitioners can launch useful and highly personalized services to help potential adopters perform their tasks with little mental effort. For instance, MDS service providers in South Korea have cooperated with several banks in the initiation of mobile banking services that can perform account inquiries and transfers via cellular phones with identification chips. The usefulness and innovation of these types of mobile banking services have encouraged many bank customers to adopt them, thus these banking functions via cellular phones have been appraised as a successful service in South Korea.

Second, MDS acceptance is largely explained by social value, implying that this value is a salient factor in deciding whether to adopt MDS. MDS provides users with highly developed mobile services such as mobile banking service, mobile knowledge search service, and digital mobile broadcasting. The use of these services enhances their social image. Consumer behavior research has suggested that many new users tend to choose novel products or services in order to gain status by differentiating themselves from others. Hong and Tam (2006) also consider services that help users establish their uniqueness as the major source of MDS revenues. The continuous introduction of new services that improves the status of users is likely to be highly associated with MDS success. Thus, MDS practitioners can attempt to leverage this aspect of MDS adoption behavior.

The perceptions of value are found to be more strongly affected by interpersonal influences than by external sources’ influences. Recently, individuals have been able to interact with their relevant others easily through social network services and instant messaging services. In addition, they can search for a variety of information though knowledge search services such as Yahoo Answers and Naver Knowledge In. MDS practitioners can work to enhance the potential adopter perceptions of value through several communication channels and via electronic word of mouth, using these types of social network services as well as online advertisements. Owing to the salient effect of communication with the relevant others, these strategies provide various benefits to MDS providers that can result from positive word-of-mouth communication. Examples are reductions of marketing costs and a growth in per-user revenue. In South Korea, SK Telecom, one of the major MDS providers in the country, offers a service that users can access, the social network service Cyworld through a hand-held mobile device. This service helps users easily communicate with the members of social networks about their shared interests as well as post pictures and text at their phone-based Cyworld homepage without restrictions in time or place. SK Telecom gains several benefits from improving the level of value of MDS, and it has increased the volume of MDS use through these positive network effects (The Korea Times, 2008).

Lastly, MDS practitioners must understand the relative impact of interpersonal and external sources’ influences between men and women. Marketing managers can learn the differences in the key determinants for each group. For men, external sources’ influences are the most dominant determinant forming perceptions of value; for women, perceptions of value are only affected by interpersonal influences. According to gender, MDS marketers transfer messages efficiently through different means. For example, they can launch a virtual community for women to exchange knowledge about their shared interests. By differentiating the communication channels, MDS providers can facilitate more targeted marketing for MDS.

## Conclusions and limitations

This study proposes a theoretical framework based on the three criteria – utilitarian value, hedonic value, and social value – which are all regarded as key drivers in the marketing and IS disciplines. Relevant others and mass media are considered as the major sources of information through which potential adopters can encounter messages or information about MDS. Additionally, this study observes the moderating role of age and gender that factor into adoption decision processes. The proposed model was empirically tested using survey data from 287 potential adopters, and the data were analyzed using PLS. The value perspective contributes to the development of a comprehensive understanding of MDS acceptance. Interpersonal and external sources’ influences are justified by their consistent predictive power in forming perceptions of value that can be obtained from MDS. The findings of this study provide evidence suggesting that adoption intention toward MDS is determined by different relative influences according to age and gender. In particular, the results of SEM reveal that women rely on information from members of social communities whereas men gain information from both relevant others and secondary sources. Understanding the moderating factors that are important in the adoption decision process enables MDS practitioners to employ more targeted investment and marketing techniques for each group.

This study has a number of limitations. First, it involves a type of snapshot research, as this study may not fully capture the dynamics of adoption decision processes regarding MDS as these dynamics change with time. Although the value perspective derived in this paper is considered in the adoption stage, the research framework can be applied at the stages of post-adoption. Further research should examine how the key factors of the value perspective evolve temporally from a longitudinal perspective. Second, this study does not consider different categories of MDS. As Hong et al. (2008) recognized the importance of the contexts of MDS, additional empirical research should be conducted related to different types of MDS to add richness to the understanding of MDS acceptance. Finally, the results of this study should be interpreted carefully, given that the data collection is geographically limited to South Korea. Therefore, further study is necessary to replicate the findings through data from several countries to allow for the generalization of the results.
