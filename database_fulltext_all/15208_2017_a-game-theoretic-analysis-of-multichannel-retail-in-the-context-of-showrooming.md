# A Game Theoretic Analysis of Multichannel Retail in the context of “Showrooming”

Abstract: “Showrooming” as a market phenomenon in multichannel retailing has grown in importance over the last few years. Consumers nowadays use the brick-and-mortar store to research about a product before purchasing it online. This leads to the offline stores being converted into showrooms for the online retailers. Therefore, popular notion suggests that showrooming should benefit the online retailer. In this paper, our objective is to analyze multichannel retailing under showrooming and determine the veracity of the popularly held belief. We develop a series of game theoretic models that involve a traditional retailer and an online retailer under showrooming. We determine optimal pricing strategies for each player and also the sales effort expended by the traditional retailer based on the interplay of “power” dynamics, market potential and the impact of showrooming. Our results indicate that profit for the traditional as well as the online retailer decreases with rising levels of showrooming. Hence, high levels of showrooming are not beneficial from the perspective of the online retailer. Thus, contrary to popular intuition, lessening of showrooming benefits not only the traditional retailer but also the online retailer. Nevertheless, from the consumer’s point of view showrooming is beneficial as it leads to overall reduction in retail prices. We also analyze the viability of a click-and-mortar model as a strategy of the traditional retailer to counter the threat of showrooming.

Keywords: E-commerce, Multichannel retail, Showrooming, Brick-and-mortar, Click-and-mortar, Game theory

## 1. Introduction

Multichannel retail is a process of using multiple channels for selling similar products across different platforms in order to reach customers along their choice of convenience. In today’s world, brick-and-mortar stores and online stores co-exist to form an integral part in the realization of a multichannel retail environment. Multichannel retailing has gathered importance in recent times with consumers seeking the best options provided by the salient features of each selling channel (Rigby, 2011). Products such as electronic items, appliances, apparels and toys have experienced proliferation in sales through multiple channels. Consumers, nowadays, are exceedingly demanding and gather as much information as possible about a product that they would like to purchase by checking it through multiple channels. The online channel provides hard information about the product such as detailed specifications, price comparisons, while the offline or the brick-and-mortar channel<sup>1</sup> provides softer information such as the actual touch and feel of the product. Consumers use this mix of online and offline channels to gather information before purchasing the product.

Additionally, we also explore the viability of a hybrid model, click-and-mortar, that is, the introduction of an online channel by the traditional retailer to counter showrooming. ## 3. Model

We study a dual-channel retail structure consisting of a pure brick-and-mortar retailer and an online retailer who sells the products bought from the same wholesaler to the end consumer. The brick-and-mortar retailer puts effort to showcase the product to the consumer. This effort increases the potential market demand of both the retailers as the consumer, can check the product in the brick-and-mortar store and decide to buy the product, either from the same store or from the online retailer. Thus, the effort expended by the brickand-mortar retailer may increase its own potential market demand and also that of the online retailer.

We analyze two scenarios. Firstly, the traditional retailer sets the prices before the online retailer. Secondly, the online retailer sets the prices before the traditional retailer. In our analysis, the retailer who is a leader has the capability to set prices before the other retailer can set prices. If one of the retailers is already present in the market before the entry of the second retailer, then the first retailer can be assumed to be more powerful than the other. The traditional retailer as well as the online retailer buys the product from the same wholesaler. Both the retailers are strategic firms and procure the product from the wholesaler at the same price w. We assume the wholesaler is non-strategic i.e., the retailers procure the product at market determined wholesale price. Both the retailers subsequently add respective margins to the wholesale price to determine the retail price of the product. The retailers are strategic firms and the final retail price charged by them is not identical. Price $p _ { 1 }$ and $p _ { 2 }$ are charged by the traditional and the online retailer respectively. The effort dispensed by the traditional retailer contributes to the demand creation for the traditional as well as the online retailer. The demand for the traditional retailer is given by:

$$

As we consider all the information to be known to both the parties, at equilibrium both the retailers will best respond to the other retailer’s optimal price. Moreover, this shift is more prominent at higher values of relative shift in market potential for the traditional retailer. At high values of showrooming, click-andmortar becomes the optimal strategy. Additionally, pure brick-and-mortar model remains optimal for the rating the parallel online channel. When we compare optimal strategies under different price set ency, we find that when the traditional retailer is the price setter, it switches from the pure -and-mortar to the click-and-mortar model at lower values of showrooming even when the shift in the relative market potential is comparatively low. The above analysis helps the traditional retailer to derive optimal strategies in order to counter the threat posed by showrooming in the presence of an online competitor.

## 8. Conclusion

With the proliferation of online retail and availability of ecommerce platforms, consumers nowadays can choose between multiple buying options. Recently, showrooming has become an important phenomenon where a consumer uses the traditional brick-and-mortar store to research about a product but then buys the product online. Due to this, traditional stores end up providing product information to the consumer but the product sale does not occur there leading to no revenue earnings for the traditional store despite putting effort to showcase the product. The retailer with its efforts creates demand in the market but the demand is fulfilled by the online channel. Showrooming takes away the consumer demand from the traditional retailer to the online retailer. Our paper is an endeavor to study showrooming in detail and derive critical managerial insights. We develop a series of game theoretic models that analyze various market scenarios characterized by changing market potential and “power” dynamics between the online and the traditional retailers.

Our study leads to important findings under the changing market dynamics driven by showrooming. We determine optimal pricing strategies for the traditional and the online retailer and the sales effort expended by the traditional retailer based on the interplay of price setting power, market potential and the impact of showrooming. Firstly, we analyze the scenario where the traditional retailer operates as a pure brick-and-mortar store. We derive two major conclusions. Firstly, we show that the traditional retailer is adversely affected by showrooming but the magnitude of this impact varies based on its market potential. A traditional retailer who enjoys a favorable market potential is affected relatively less by the phenomenon of showrooming. Similarly, online retailers who have high market potential will be able to extract the benefits of showrooming more than online retailers with low market potential. We also find that comparative price setting power can be used to counter the ill-effects of showrooming.

Secondly, we show that the online retailer is negatively affected by high showrooming. Contrary to popular belief that showrooming only affects the traditional retailer; we deduce that showrooming induces pain to both the online retailer. The online retailer suffers because of the lowering of the market demand.

Based on the above results, we propose a strategy matrix that identifies optimal prices to charge and sales effort to be expended by the traditional retailer under varying conditions of market potential, price setting power and showrooming. We find that the online as well as the offline retail prices decrease with increase of showrooming. This leads us to conclude that from a consumer’s perspective showrooming is beneficial as it leads to overall lower retail prices.

Next, we extend our model and analyze the viability of adding an online channel and operate as a click-and-mortar model to counter the threat posed by showrooming. We derive zones based on the effect of showrooming and the relative shift in market potential for the traditional retailer where either only a pure brick-and-mortar model or a hybrid click-and-mortar model becomes optimal for the traditional retailer. Our analysis highlights the strategic choice before the traditional retailer to put effort to increase its profit in a pure brick-and-mortar store or shift to a click-and-mortar model through which it is able to capture some of the online retailer’s market share.

## 9. Limitations and Future scope of work

# ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

Our objective in this paper was to develop a generalized model that analyzes multichannel retail under the influence of showrooming. One of the limitations of our model is that the customers are non-strategic. The model can be extended where the customers may strategize based on the prices charged by the retailers and the showrooming intensity of the product. Strategic customer behavior will bring in dynamics that will make the demand model more realistic. Another limitation of our modeling framework is that it considers a one-period Stackelberg game between the two retailers with one of the them being a dominant price setter, as opposed to a repeated interaction between the retailers, wherein they could revise their strategies after each interaction. The modeling framework may be extended as a repeated dynamic game between the retailers for determining optimal prices and sales effort in a multi-period setting. In the present set-up, the showrooming intensity is modeled as an exogenous parameter. The traditional retailer may influence the showrooming intensity through initiatives such as loyalty programm s and after-sales service. Analyzing multi-channel retail under these initiatives can be interesting future research endeavors. Further, we have adopted a deterministic demand model for our analysis. The model can be extended to a stochastic demand model to get further insights. Also, we can use Nash bargaining to model the negotiation of the retailers to achieve a mutually agreeable outcome in case of showrooming.
