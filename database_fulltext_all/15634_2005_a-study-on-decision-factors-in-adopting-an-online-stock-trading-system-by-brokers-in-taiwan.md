# A study on decision factors in adopting an online stock trading system by brokers in Taiwan

Shi-Ming Huang<sup>a</sup>, Yu-Chung Hung<sup>b</sup>, David C. Yen<sup>c,</sup>\*

<sup>a</sup>Department of Information Management, National Chung Cheng University, Taiwan

<sup>b</sup> Department of Information Management, Tatung University, Taiwan

<sup>c</sup> Department of Decision Sciences and Management Information Systems, Miami University, Miami, USA

Received 1 January 2003; accepted 1 February 2004 Available online 6 May 2004

## Abstract

With the influence of the Internet booming and global competition expanding, E-Commerce (EC) is receiving considerable attention worldwide. Currently, most people in this new era are thinking about whether or not EC could bring unlimited business opportunities and new competitive advantages. Among all EC applications, Internet-based stock trading is really one of the best ways to understand the attraction and popularity of EC. The purpose of this paper is to figure out the decision factors that affect the brokers in Taiwan in adopting an online stock trading system. This study had scrutinized 16 factors that are related to affect brokers’ decision in adopting an online stock trading system. Further, the results from this research also indicate that five decision factors significantly distinguish the adopters from the non-adopters in terms of the brokers’ decision to adopt a stock trading system. In addition, this study provides some useful suggestions and/or implications for the academician, government, and practitioners in the area of online stock trading.

## 1. Introduction

## 1.1. Research background and motivation

To cope with the need of global trade and an increasingly intensive competition, modern organizations are prompted to adopt various information and/ or communication technologies to facilitate and/or improve their business operations. In fact, innovative applications of these newer technologies are constantly remolding the organizational landscape in terms of reducing costs, lowering cycle time of business processes, enhancing customer service, and improving product quality. One area receiving considerable attention is the field of Electronic Commerce (EC) [15].

Though EC was anticipated to have a highly promising future by a majority of scholars, experts, and market research institutions in the subject area, it seemed to be a quite different story from what was originally anticipated when EC was put into actual practice. A general review of some unsuccessful EC cases summarized the following findings:

 Lack of a full preparation and analysis as well as an understanding of the unique characteristics of the Internet before utilizing EC applications.

 Technical problems of the Internet itself yet to be overcome.

 Unable to adjust and/or resist to the change of the new work style.

It could be clearly observed that the majority of EC pioneers were confronted with difficulties/problems, while only a few could make a profit out of it [10]. Hence, the general doubt was whether the Internet market was a virtual pie or not. Meanwhile, the development of Internet-based stock trading was booming all over the world such as America, Hong Kong, Singapore, Taiwan, and etc. Regardless of the different functional areas or disciplines, most have shared a similarly successful experience; and hence corroborating the justification that Internet-based stock trading is truly one of the best areas to fully understand and implement EC [5].

According to the data collected from Taiwan Stock Exchange Corporation (TSEC), the number of Internet brokerages in Taiwan had increased from an initial 5 in 1997 to 29 in 1998. By the end of 1999, it had rapidly increased to a total of 68, which consequently intensified the competition of the market. The competition reached a climax in the middle of 1999, when every Internet brokerage started to adopt various marketing strategies to entice investors by giving commission discounts, giveaways, awards, and etc. It has obviously become a trend for brokers to enter into the market of Internet-based stock trading.

Under this circumstances, two issues that are worthy of exploration and further investigation arose. For the first issue, what are the factors that would affect brokers to adopt an online stock trading system? To be more specific, what are the dominant decision factors for brokers to ponder while adopting such a system? For the other issue, what are the factors that will have impact to distinguish the adopters vs. nonadopters? In other words, what factors will exert a decisive dominant influence on the behavior in terms of adoption vs. non-adoption? These were the main driving forces that motivated this research.

## 1.2. Impact of technological innovation to the brokerage industry

Challenged by the new era of paradigm shifting into technology advancement and market competition, innovation has emerged as another critical resource, which becomes the key factor to gain competitive advantages among different countries or enterprises [1]. Take U.S. brokerage industry for example. Even Merrill Lynch (the leader of the U.S. brokers) could not help but change the past resistance attitude toward a favorable one implementing online trading under the threat of E-Trade and, additionally, Charles Schwab started the online trading operation on June 1st, 1999.

As for the brokerage industry in Taiwan, with the prevailing majority of Internet users and strong functions that network obsesses, it is natural for the brokers to go along with the trend by adopting an online stock trading system to provide additional Internet-based stock trading services [19]. However, enterprises should naturally take many factors, which will affect the adoption and/or implementation into consideration before adopting an innovative technology. For example, innovation attributes, organization characteristics, and the business environment, etc. are all important factors frequently explored and discussed in technology innovation-related literature. As a result, the purpose of this manuscript is to investigate the decision factors in adopting an online stock trading system.

## 3. Research methodology

## 3.1. Research goals and objectives

From the above discussion, the research goals and objectives of this study includes the following items:

 To find out the decision factors of importance before the adoption of an online stock trading system.

 To find out important decision factors which will exert dominant influence to the behavior in terms of adoption or non-adoption attribute.

## 3.2. Research justification

From the above discussion, the authors have summarized a total of 62 factors, which are related to the adoption of IT discussed in prior researches/ studies and then categorized all of them into 7 dimensions. In order to determine the relevancy of all factors summarized (Table 4) in this study, the Lawshe’s approach [11] is applied to justify the content validity of each factor by a steering committee. This assessment approach of content validity is basically utilized to ensure the representativeness or adequacy of the factors in specific domain [12]. To use this aforementioned approach, a steering committee is formed to serve as the content evaluation panel. This committee comprises eight professionals from both industry and academia. In specific, three committee members are MIS professors who come from different universities, three committee members are CEOs or MIS managers from stock trading companies, and the last two panelists represent the opinions from the business consults in the area of online stock trading. These eight committee members are requested to evaluate each item listed in Table 4 on a three-point scale. Namely, this three-point scale uses ‘‘1’’ as ‘‘not relevant’’, ‘‘2’’ as ‘‘important (but not essential)’’, and ‘‘3’’ as ‘‘essential’’. Based on the response obtained, this study utilizes the following formula to calculate the content validity ratio (CVR) for each item:

$$
\mathrm{CVR} = (n - N / 2) / (N / 2)\tag{1}
$$

where n is the frequency count of the number of committee members rating the item either ‘‘3— essential’’ or ‘‘ 2—important (but not essential)’’, and N is the total number of panelists, namely N = 8 in this study.

The CVR for each item was determined for statistical significance at the 0.05 level with the table published by Lawshe [11]. According to Lawshe, this study eliminates the items which CVR ratios are less than 0.75. Based on the outcomes of this screening procedure, this study ended with a list of selected 22 factors from 6 dimensions for the further empirical research to determine the decision factors of online stock trading systems (see Table 5).

## 3.3. Research framework

From the discussion above, the proposed research framework is composed of the following six dimensions/categories. Namely, these six dimensions are innovation attributes, organizational factors, environmental factors, factors of suppliers, factors of networking technology, and government policy and legal regulation. Consequently, the proposed research framework in this study is shown as Fig. 1.

## 3.4. Research hypothesis

Based on the research framework in Fig. 1, the null hypotheses to be tested are summarized as follows:

H1. Factors of the innovation attribute, organization, environment, suppliers, network technology, and policy as well as legal regulation cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-1. Each factor of the innovation attribute— namely, compatibility, relative advantage, and relative risk—cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-2. Each factor of the organization—namely, organizational scale, centralization, formalization, involvement and support by top management, awareness of adopting benefits by top management, and IT maturity—cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-3. Each factor of the environment—namely, industry competitive intensity, the adoptive pressure from the fellow traders, market size, contacting with more potential customers, and the prevailing of Internet users—cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-4. Each factor of the suppliers—namely, vertical coordination, and marketing supply—cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-5. Each factor of network technology—namely, speed of transmission, accuracy of transmission, security and privacy of network, and the setup, management and cost of maintaining the website— cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H1-6. Each factor of government policy and legal regulation—namely government policy and legal regulation—cannot affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

H2. There are no differences in the way each decision factor is viewed by adopters of an online stock trading system and non-adopters.

## 3.5. Questionnaire design

This study uses a questionnaire as a means of data collection. The questionnaire was designed to include the variables/factors discussed in the previous sections. Further, it basically consists of two parts—‘‘factors that affect brokers to adopt online stock trading system’’ and ‘‘broker’s general characteristics’’. However, with brokers’ general characteristics excluded, all questions about the factors are measured using the Likert’s fivepoint scale, using five ratings—‘‘strongly agree’’, ‘‘agree’’, ‘‘no opinion’’, ’’disagree’’, and ‘‘strongly disagree’’,—to assess the interviewee’s attitude.

## 3.6. Analysis method

The SPSS for windows V10.0 was used to analyze all of the usable questionnaires returned. Five statistical methods, which include reliability analysis, descriptive statistics, chi-square test, t-test, and correlation analysis were utilized in this study.

## 3.6.1. Reliability analysis

Using Cronbach a to examine the reliability for the whole questionnaire and each instrument used in the study.

## 3.6.2. Descriptive statistics

Using the average value and percentage of each question to describe the characteristics of the samples.

## 3.6.3. Chi-square test

Using two types of the chi-square test—testing goodness of fit and independence of test—to examine whether each factor put forward in this study would actually affect brokers to adopt an online stock trading system.

## 3.6.4. T-test

Using the T-test to examine what factors are of significant difference between the adopter and nonadopter groups.

## 3.6.5. Correlation analysis

To be used to examine the relationship between adoption behavior (adoption vs. non-adoption) and factors affecting the broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system.

## 5. Conclusions and suggestions

## 5.1. Conclusions

The factors affecting a broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system are summarized and shown in Table 15.

## 5.1.1. Innovation characteristics

Among the three factors of compatibility, relative advantage, and relative risk, only the compatibility factor can affect the adoption of an online stock trading system. It indicates that brokers are more concerned with the compatibility of an online stock trading system and the existing operating process or organizational structure in contrast to the benefits and risks of Internet-based stock trading.

## 5.1.2. Factors of organization

Except for centralization and formalization, the other four factors—involvement and support by top management, awareness of adopting benefits by top management, organization scale, and IT maturity— can affect the broker’s decision to adopt an online trading system. It shows that top management plays an important role in the process to make an adoption decision. With such factors as involvement, support, and/or awareness of adopting benefits by top management, it would be conducive to the adoption or attempt to set up an online stock trading system.

In addition, the bigger the organization scale or the more maturity to use IT (i.e., having more financial resource, professionals, related technology, and/or experience), the more likely for the brokers to adopt an online stock trading system.

## 5.1.3. Factors of environment

Except for the factor of competitive intensity, the other four factors can all affect a broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system. To begin with, adoptive pressure from fellow traders is not only the factor affecting the adoption of an online stock trading system, but it is also an important factor that forces the non-adopter to adopt the system. Then, since the prevailing Internet users is fashionable, the benefit of being able to contact potential customers and the extended market size will no doubt prompt brokers to adopt an online stock trading system.

## 5.1.4. Factors of suppliers

Of the two factors about suppliers, vertical coordination and marketing supply, the former does not significantly affect the adoption of an online stock trading system but the latter does. It indicates that brokers are more concerned about the marketing activities provided by suppliers (such as the beta sites or preferential price), than the relationship with suppliers.

## 5.1.5. Factors of network technology

The four factors about network technologies are all related to the adoption of an online stock trading system. It indicates that brokers relate a critical significance to the issues associated with network technologies such as speed, accuracy, security, and privacy, and etc. In addition, an interesting point is that non-adopters are more concerned about the cost of the website in setup, management, and maintenance than the adopters group.

## 5.1.6. Government policy and legal regulation

Both government policy and legal regulation are related to the adoption of an online trading system. It shows that the system of network trading still depends on the promotion and guidance of the government. In addition, the related legal regulation must be sound enough to protect the implementation of network trading.

Furthermore, this study uses a T-test to examine whether adopters and non-adopters have different viewpoints about factors that affect adoption. From the survey result, the authors find out that there are five significant factors—organization scale, IT maturity, compatibility, marketing supply, and the costs of the website in setup, management and maintenance. The results of the correlation test also support this aforementioned finding. Therefore, these five factors can be viewed as the basic and primary considerations before brokers adopt an online stock trading system.

## 5.2. Suggestions and recommendations

Based on the discussion presented in Section 4, the following suggestions are recommended for brokers, suppliers, and authority government.

## 5.2.1. Brokers

For brokers not yet adopting an online stock trading system, those affecting factors can be taken into consideration as evaluation criteria for future possible adoption. These following five elements such as organization scale, IT maturity, compatibility, marketing supply, and the costs of website setup/management/maintenance are extremely important to consider an adoption of online stock trading system. Further, these factors are also very helpful to analyze the feasibility of adoption and/or improving the weaknesses for those who have already adopted the systems. It is needless to say that the brokers must pay close attention to the attitude of top management. With the factor of ‘‘involvement and support of top management’’, the adoption of the online stock trading system could certainly be facilitated without a hitch.

## 5.2.2. Suppliers

For suppliers, other then establishing a closer relationship with brokers through seminars, visits, and/or such similar activities, it is more important to provide brokers with marketing supply activities such as providing technical assistance to assist brokers in trial and adoption, and/or offering preferential prices to promote adopter’s desire for a(n) trial and adoption. Furthermore, the brokers that are larger in organizational size, possess more mature IT, and have a favorable top management’s attitude towards adoption of the system should be the areas to which suppliers should pay closest attention to.

## 5.2.3. Government

Government should continuously reinforce the policy to promote and guide EC development such as the distribution of propaganda of the EC concept, the training of professionals, the construction of network infrastructure, and etc. Moreover, the legislation of relevant rules and regulation is also important. Due to the limitation and imperfection of the existing laws, important elements such as electronic signature, certificate authentication, limitations of two-phase consignment, and inability to open new account online would make brokers reluctant to adopt the online trading system and hence, hinder the development of Internet-based stock trading in Taiwan.

## 5.3. Limitations and future research

The main contribution of this study is expected to identify and put forward some factors that affect a broker’s decision to adopt an online stock trading system, making it known as to what are the necessary considerations to brokers when adopting an online stock trading system. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the adoption of online trading systems is indeed growing up in the current stock trading market.

However, this study has quite several weaknesses and, fortunately, these limitations are capable of being improved in the future.

## 5.3.1. Research target

This study only examines the decision factors related to the professional brokers in general, and only fit to explain the professional brokers in Taiwan in particular. Hence, follow-up research studies may be conducted to investigate the domain of the decision factors of the brokers that serve concurrently by a financial institute and then compare their result with the decision factors of professional brokers.

## 5.3.2. The choice of factors

There are 22 factors chosen for in this study. However, these factors may not be the only factors that can affect the adoption of an online stock trading system. Limited by time and financial resource of this study, not all the factors can be brought up for discussion at the same time. Therefore, future research may be developed to explore such factors as the characteristics of decision-makers, the attitude of employees, the attitude of customers, and etc.

## 5.3.3. The classification of adoption behavior

To simplify this study, the adoption behavior was divided mainly into two categories—adopters and non-adopters. However, grouping all non-adopters into a single category may lose some interesting information relevant to the adoption decision. Since the decision process is a continuous one, the decision-maker reaches a decision through different stages, which lead to the final determination to adopt or reject an innovation, or just evaluate whether or not to adopt this innovation. That is, for a fixed time span, an organization could be classified as one of the following three groups—adopters, rejecters, or the undecided [3]. Hence, to understand the brokers considerations more deeply and thoroughly, future research can divide the adoption behavior into the three categories as mentioned above instead of two used in this research.

## 5.3.4. Measurement of variables

Due to a lack of an appropriate approximation instrument, many variables are measured and/or quantified by only a single item in this study. To increase the accuracy of measurement, it is necessary to develop an appropriate instrument in the future.

## 5.3.5. Data collection method

In this study, mailed questionnaires approach was adopted for data collection. However, as the respondents cannot be identified and/or controlled, the weakness such as bias in statistical figures and results may arise. Therefore, case study or person interviews approaches would be a good solution to solve the aforementioned weakness.
