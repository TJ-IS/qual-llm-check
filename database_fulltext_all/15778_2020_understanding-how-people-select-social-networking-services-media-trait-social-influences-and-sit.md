# Understanding How People Select Social Networking Services: Media Trait, Social Influences and Situational Factors

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Social networking service Media repertoire Uses and gratifications Media selection

## A B S T R A C T

Social networking services (SNSs) have become very popular, and many Internet users use two or more SNSs. However, little research has been done to understand how and when users select multiple SNSs in series. This study investigates Internet users’ SNS selection behavior in daily life. We adopt the notion of media repertoire to explore how Internet users select SNSs in combination, and we adopt uses and gratifications theory to identify the values that drive these users to choose specific SNSs. We interviewed 40 Taiwanese people to explore the media traits. situational factors and social influences that affect users' SNS selection behavior. The findings of this study can help SNS companies to improve SNS functions, and help marketers devise marketing strategies for target audiences and find suitable SNS platforms on which to communicate with consumers.

## 1. Introduction

A social networking service (SNS) is a virtual community in which people can construct profiles, share a list of users with whom they are connected, and view and track individuals’ information to establish and maintain relationships via interactions between individuals and groups [3]. Based on their functions, SNSs can be divided into two types: social networks and messenger apps. Recent years have seen a great surge in the number of SNS users [51]. More than 50% of Internet users use two or more SNSs [34]. Marketers need to use multiple SNSs to integrate their marketing message into peoples’ consciousness. A cross-platform social media marketing strategy must be developed based on where target audiences spend time and how best to communicate with that audience [5]. Therefore, understanding how and when people select and use a combination of SNSs is important to marketers.

Many studies have investigated media choice behavior in the or ganizational context [7,13,29,41]. These studies considered this choice from the perspective of diferent types of media, such as electronic media and face-to-face communication. Taking a “media repertoire” perspective is useful when exploring the complexities of selecting a combination of media [50]. A media repertoire is a collection of communication channels and identifiable usage routines for specific communication purposes. This perspective can also help marketers choose the right platforms on which to reach target audiences. Although many SNS platforms exist, people do not generally use all of them. Instead, people cope with the abundance of choices by relying upon relatively small subsets or “repertoires” of their preferred platforms. Our study focuses on general Internet users, rather than on companies, to investigate their SNS repertoire selections as they go through their daily lives.

User behavior on individual SNS platforms is driven by diferent motivations [16]. Uses and gratifications (U&G) theory has been widely adopted to understand the motivations that drive people to use specific media. This theory postulates that people select and use media in order to satisfy specific needs and achieve gratification [23]. It has been commonly used to explore SNS usage motivations [20,31,40,52]. In order to understand how and why people select a specific collection of SNSs, we integrate U&G theory with the concept of media repertoires to identify SNS repertoires and compare the motivations for using specific SNSs within those repertoires.

Our study focuses on users’ selection of a series of SNSs as opposed to a single SNS. We adopt the concept of media repertoire to discover the regular rules or patterns of SNS usage developed by Internet users. With U&G theory as its basis, this study also explores the values people pursue when choosing diferent SNSs, and the key SNS attributes that can lead to those values. We adopt an interview approach to identify SNS media repertoires, and the trait, social and situational factors that drive Internet users to use these repertoires. This paper addresses the following research questions: (1) Why do users choose specific combinations of SNSs? (2) What are the distinct traits of diferent types of SNSs and the values that drive users to use a given SNS? (3) What are the social and situational factors that determine which of the various

SNSs users select?

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. The second section reviews relevant literature. The research methodology and interview protocol are described in the third section. Data analysis results are described and discussed in the fourth section. The paper concludes with the model of SNS selection behavior.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Media selection

Previous studies on media selection focused mainly on the selection of email, teleconferencing, and traditional communication media in the context of the organizational environment. The factors that influence media selection can be divided into three categories: (1) media traits, (2) situational factors, and (3) social influences [4,50]. Media richness [6], media synchronicity [8], and social presence [4] are the media traits that determine the selection between traditional media and computer-mediated media (e.g., face-to-face vs. email). However, since our study focuses on SNSs that have similar media traits (e.g., high synchronicity and social presence capability), such traits might not be suficient to explain users’ SNS selection behaviors. A new set of SNS traits must be identified.

In addition to media traits, there may be certain situational factors that determine the media selection. Media choice is a process of elim ination, limiting media repertoire to fit the particular situation [44]. For SNS users, the message topics they want to talk about, the receivers of the messages, the urgency of the communication, and user characteristics may significantly afect their media choices. According to previous studies on media selection, there are three types of situational factors: individual diferences, facilitating factors, and direct constraints [11]. The variables of individual diferences include cognitive style, computer experience, and the individual’s age [1]. One of the key facilitating factors is medium expertise. This refers to the user’s famil iarity with and willingness to use new communication technologies. In fact, a lack of the skill required to use the medium can decrease the user’s perception of the medium’s richness [43]. Individual usage is enhanced by other facilitating variables such as document protection and pricing policies, and the reliability, flexibility, and compatibility of the technology [11]. Direct constraints on media use include such variables as distance, expediency, time pressure, system access, confidentiality, accountability, social interaction, and so on [32,48].

Social influence theory postulates that an individual’s selection of a media channel is subjectively and socially influenced by the opinions of coworkers [43]. A salient individual’s opinions, information, and behavior are factors that influence the selection behaviors of coworkers of that salient individual. Social influence theory has three dimensions that are diferent from those in theories related to media traits [12]. First, the media traits perspective focuses on the capabilities of the medium, which are objective attributes that can afect media selection. In contrast, the social influence perspective focuses on others’ attitudes, statements, and behaviors, which are subjective factors. Second, the media traits perspective postulates that individuals’ perceptions of media properties and task attributes afect the salience of media properties, while the social influence perspective emphasizes how coworkers' and supervisors' assessments impact the salience of media properties. Third, the media traits perspective suggests that the fit between media traits and task requirements is the key to determining users’ selection behavior, whereas the social influence perspective argues that an individual selects media based on social norms and others past statements and behaviors.

Diferent SNSs have similar media traits, therefore, media trait theories cannot suficiently explain how users choose among SNSs. Situational and social factors can influence media choice behavior, but extant situational and social influence theories cannot explain why SNS users tend to use a combination of SNSs to interact with their friends.

We adopt the concept of media repertoires to investigate the combinations of SNSs that users commonly use in daily life, and to advance our understanding of how and when diferent SNSs are selected. Moreover, we adopt uses and gratifications theory to determine the kinds of gratification that users are pursuing when they choose to use a specific SNS.

## 2.2. Media repertoire

The concept of repertoires proposed by Orlikowski and Yates [30] came from a collection of communication genres. The study originally investigated how members of an organization choose communication genres routinely, and why the selection of these genres changes over time. Media repertoire is defined as the entirety of media that a person regularly uses [17]. Taneja, et al. [47] investigated how users choose diferent media tools (e.g., television, cellphone, website) to form their media repertoires. They found that audience availability and individual demographics can explain user-defined media repertoires. Hasebrink and Domeyer [17] argued that the notion of media repertoires estab lishes a link between two areas of research on media use: audience behavior, and media use as social practice. A repertoire-oriented approach to media usage research adopts a user-centered perspective, stresses the need to consider the entire variety of media regularly used by a person, and presents the interrelations and functions of the components of a combination of media. Hasebrink and Domeyer [17] found that a person’s media repertoire is a meaningful composition of different media contents. The composition is closely related to social contexts, and the individual’s values and ambitions.

Extant studies on media repertoires in everyday life have focused on a variety of media types, including mobile phones, computers, and television. Moreover, they have mainly used content preferences and individual demographics to explain the user’s selection behavior [18,45,47]. In contrast, our study explores users’ regular usage of SNSs that belong to the same type of media, and we explore users’ motives and task purposes to determine how they choose a specific set of SNSs.

## 2.3. Uses and gratifications theory

Uses and gratifications (U&G) theory was proposed by Katz, who transferred the efect of mass media from “What do the media do to people?" to "What do people do with the media?" [23]. This theory emphasizes that people are in the dominant position to choose the media they want. Media usage is a mechanism by which to gratify people’s expectations and needs. In the 1990s, U&G theory was applied to media selection behavior in organizations and proved that social context (e.g., media dependency, facilitation, afiliation, escapism, social learning, and role position) is an important factor that afects media choice behavior [9]. As the Internet emerged, it represented not only a technical change; it also introduced new patterns in communication. Since the Internet can satisfy some of an individual’s communication expectations, the Internet can be viewed as a new research field in which to study the reasons behind the usage of the technology, and to discover the gratifications pursued by the users [40].

Prior studies have shown that U&G theory is suitable for understanding SNS usage behavior [20,31,40,52]. Table 1 summarizes the motivations behind SNS usage according to SNS studies that adopted U &G theory. The SNS usage motivations (i.e., gratifications) can be categorized into six types: socializing, entertainment, information seeking/sharing, afection, connection, and self-status seeking. How ever, these studies all focused on a single, specific SNS, e.g., Facebook. To understand why people need to use a collection of SNSs, our study compares the gratifications of using SNSs in repertoires.

## 3.1. Means-end chain

A means-end chain is a model that explains how consumers select a product or service to facilitate the achievement of desired values. It represents the linkages between attributes (the means), the consequences of those attributes, and the personal values (the ends) the consequences reinforce [38]. When the linkages between attributes and the consequences and values caused by these attributes are identified, such linkages create several hierarchical structures, or ladders, which represent combinations of key perceptual elements that serve as the basis for distinguishing among products or services [15]. This approach allows us to more deeply understand the motivations behind people’s selection behaviors, and to specify the rationale behind the importance of certain attributes

The means-end chain is formed in three steps [38]: (1) eliciting the distinctions of the objects’ attributes from respondents, (2) conducting an in-depth interview (aka “laddering interview”) to explore the personal consequences and values related to the objects’ attributes, and (3) linking the attributes, consequences, and values in order to develop a hierarchical value map (HVM). A matrix is produced in which each row represents a respondent’s ladder (one respondent can have multiple ladders). Next, an implication matrix is built to display the number of times the respondents mentioned the connections between the elements. Then, an HVM is created by connecting the relationships among elements. An HVM is a structure connecting attributes, consequences, and values to represent the important relationships between the ele ments. In order to identify important chains based on the number of linkages between elements shown in the implication matrix, a cutof level can be set to ensure all the relationships in the HVM are useful and informative.

In a laddering interview, the interviewer begins by inducing the respondent to list the attributes that distinguish particular products or services. Second, after eliciting distinctions, the key distinctions for building ladders can be selected by the interviewer judgmentally or determined by relative importance rated by the respondent. Third, for the key distinctions, asking the question: “Why is this attribute important to you?" The interviewer is on the basis of this way to explore the reasons of respondents’ selection and climb the ladder to probe from concrete attributes to abstract values.

## 3.2. Sample

To ensure the sample size was big enough to explain SNS users behaviors properly, we interviewed 40 people, which exceeds the minimum requirement of 20 respondents for laddering interviews [39]. According to Taiwan’s 2016 Internet Usage Report, people between the ages of 20 and 29 are the main users of social media (22%), and people between the ages of 30 and 39 are close behind (20.7%). It reported that 51.3% of social media users in Taiwan are males. Therefore, this study focused on social media users aged between 20 and 39 and the respondents we chose were in line with the demographics of social media users. We invited 20 respondents who were between 20 and 29 years of age (10 males and 10 females, in accordance with the gender proportion noted in the report). The other 20 respondents were 10 males and 10 females, all of whom were between 30 and 39 years of age. All respondents had to meet the following qualifications: (1) at least one year of SNS usage experience, (2) regular usage of three or more SNSs, and (3) regular usage of each SNS for 20 minutes per day, which is in accordance with the average time Taiwanese spend using SNSs per day [21]. We used a snowball sampling technique to gather respondents who met the qualifications and the requirements of age and gender from our surroundings, who introduced their friends, who then also became respondents.

## 3.3. Interview

Each interview lasted about one hour and involved one interviewee, one interviewer, and one note taker. Interviewees used a computer to log into the SNSs they often used, which allowed them to recall real situations in order to answer the questions during the interview. We followed the protocol listed in Appendix A to conduct the interview. At the beginning, the interviewer explained the research purposes and the definition of an SNS. A small talk was had to help the respondent feel relaxed and open-minded, and no pressure was given during the in terview process. Second, the respondent was asked to list the SNSs that he or she used for at least 20 minutes per day (for each SNS). and to provide the communication purpose behind using each SNS. Third, for each SNS used by the respondent, the interviewer used triadic and free sorting techniques to elicit the key distinguishing attributes from the interviewee. Next, the interviewee was asked to rate the importance of each SNSs’ functions and features in comparison to other SNSs, using a 7-point scale in which 1 represented “not at all important,” and 7 represented “extremely important.” If the score was greater than 4, the interviewer asked why these attributes are important to the interviewee (see phase 3 of the protocol). The interviewer kept asking these questions until the interviewee revealed the distinct types of value obtained by using the SNS. The end result of this phase was a number of ladders linking SNS attributes to values. Finally, the interviewee was asked to identify the major communication purposes (usage percentage higher than average) for which the SNSs were used. If two or more SNSs were used for the same purpose, the interviewee was asked to explain when and how the selection was made between these SNSs (see phase 4 of the protocol). To add to the SNS attributes and personal values found in the previous phase, this phase aimed to discover other factors (e.g., social and situational factors) which afect the respondent’s SNS selection. Through these phases, we discovered the media repertoires of SNSs for each purpose. The traits, social, and situational factors identified through these phases increase our understanding of how and when users select among diferent SNSs.

## 3.4. Data analysis

The sample demographics are shown in Appendix A. The interviews were recorded by a note taker and a voice recorder pen, and were then transcribed into text. Two researchers independently inspected the words in the sentences in order to identify attributes, consequences, and values. Once these independent lists were complete, they were compared, and any conflicts were resolved by discussion. After analyzing the first 36 interviews, no new codes can be found. It was evident that our categories of attributes, consequences, and values had reached data saturation. These elements in the ladders were categorized into the main codes and classified into the same types of attributes, consequences, and values to generate the summary coding form. Each code was assigned a unique number, allowing a ladder to be encoded via a series of numbers. An HVM was created for each SNS by connecting the relationships among elements. We set the cutof level in accordance with Reynolds and Gutman [38] and Pieters, et al. [35] to ensure that all the relationships in the HVM were important.

The comparison phase focused on social and situational factors that afect respondents’ SNS usage. The questions “What are the places and situations where you use the listed SNSs?” and “Who are the people and groups with whom you communicate? Do they influence your SNS selection?” were used for eliciting situational factors; the question “Do people who are important to you influence your SNS selection?” was used for eliciting social factors. Two researchers analyzed the responses collected from the interviews and identified social and situational fac tors that affect the selection behavior.

Key themes (e.g., media trait factors, situational factors, social factors) and patterns of SNS usage were identified, based on the coded files. Media traits and social and situational factors were found to influence users’ choice behaviors, depending on the usage purpose. This analysis discovered all the factors that afect how and when Internet users select a repertoire of SNSs for diferent purposes.

## 4. Results and discussion

## 4.1. Media traits

Respondents identified 7 SNSs: Facebook, LINE, Instagram, WeChat, Plurk, Dcard, and LinkedIn. Facebook and LINE were listed by 40 respondents. Instagram was listed by 31 respondents. The other SNSs were listed by 9, 3, 2, and 1 respondents, respectively. We chose three SNSs (i.e., Facebook, Instagram, and LINE) to analyze means-end chains in accordance with the minimum requirement of 20 respondents for the laddering interview method [39].

We identified 14 attributes, 25 consequences, and 10 values for Facebook. The attributes are the distinctive features of Facebook, the consequences are the benefits of the attributes, and the values are user needs that can be met by using Facebook. The definitions of these elements and the HVM are shown in Appendix B. The numbers of linkages between elements are displayed next to the links in the HVM. Facebook has its own distinct attributes: “Search,” “Comment,” “Share,” “Check In,” “A Ton of Users,” and “Instantaneousness.” When Facebook is used with the following chain, “Search—Looking for Someone Who Has not Been Contacted for a Period of Time—Interaction—Warm Relationship With Others,” we see that in order to recover a friendship, Facebook users use the search function to look for someone who has not been contacted for a period of time. The chains “Comment—Interaction—Warm Relationship With Others” and “Comment—Interaction—Self-Respect” show that Facebook users use the comment function not only to enhance their warm relationships with friends, but also to share knowledge with others, which makes the users feel confident about their own ability and personal value. The chain “Share—Let Others Know Information—Self-Fulfillment” shows that users like to forward news or information on their News Feed to help others stay current. The chain “Share—Let Others Know Information—Expressing Emotion—Psychological Stability” shows that some users like to share their opinions on topics such as political issues on their News Feed to express their emotions and reach psychological stability. The chains “Check In—Sharing—Let Others Know Information—Self-Fulfillment” and “Check In—Recording Life—Retrospection” show that the users use the “Check In” function, indicating their current location, in order to share entertainment places with friends. The “Check In” function is also used for recording life, so that past events can be reviewed later. The consequences of the attri bute “A Ton of Users” are “Keep Track of Friends,” “A Variety of Information,” and “Easy to Search for Others,” which lead to the value “Productivity.” This means that using Facebook is an eficient way to contact friends and get information because of the huge user base. “Instantaneousness” is another key attribute of Facebook. Users can update information quickly to get knowledge from Facebook, so that they can feel a sense of enjoyment, self-respect, and security.

As shown in the HVM for LINE, we identified 11 attributes, 12 consequences, and 6 values (see Appendix C). LINE’s unique attributes are: “Upload Photos,” “Albums,” “Free Call,” “Video Call,” “Stickers,” and “A Variety of Stickers.” The chains “Upload Photos—Instantaneousness—Communication—Productivity” and “Upload Photos—Instantaneousness—Communication—Warm Relationship With Others” indicate that users communicate with colleagues by uploading product photos for the sake of task eficiency, and upload photos of daily life to share with friends and enhance warm relationships. LINE “albums” attribute leads to the chains “Albums—Retaining Information—Productivity,” “Albums—Retaining Information—- Retrospection,” and “Albums—Retaining Information—Obtaining Information—Self-Respect.” LINE’s albums can retain images permanently, whereas its “Upload Photos” feature keeps photos for seven days only. Retaining images in perpetuity has three benefits. First, finding earlier pictures is easy, which saves time. Second, the user has the opportunity to recall great memories and how they felt regarding past events. Third, it is useful to ofice workers who search through archived pictures to get inspiration for new brand designs. LINE’s attributes “Free Call” and “Video Call” are similar to telephone attributes, but they are more beneficial to users because LINE’s free calling feature saves money that can be used to enioy life. Video calls have the added advantage of allowing communicators to see each other’s body language, and gives the feeling of being together, which helps maintain a warm relationship. The two chains “Stickers—Expressing Emotion—Warm Relationship With Others” and “A Variety of Stickers—Expressing Emotion—Warm Relationship With Others” emphasize stickers, which LINE users find attractive because they seem to express true emotions more fully.

As the Instagram HVM shows, we identified 8 attributes, 11 consequences, and 7 values (see Appendix D). The unique attributes are: “Stories,” “Filter and Edit,” “Hashtags,” and “Picture Posts.” The chain “Stories—Sharing the Moments Disappearing After 24 Hours—Fun and Enjoyment of Life” shows that the “Stories” feature provides a place where users can post and browse moments that then disappear after 24 hours. Browsing “Stories” can allows users to quickly learn about popular attractions, which can increase the fun and enjoyment of life. The chain “Filter and Edit—Attractive Pictures—Being Well Respected” shows that Instagram users edit their pictures with filters to generate attractive images that can show their personal style and establish their sense of status. The chain “Hashtags—Centralization of Information—Easy to Browse—Productivity” shows that “Hashtags” can classify pictures regarding similar subjects (e.g., restaurants, brands, artwork). Users who want to collect diferent pictures of similar subjects can type keywords, which can reduce the time spent searching on Instagram. The chain “Picture Posts—Enjoying Pictures—Fun and Enjoyment of Life” shows that “Picture Posts” helps users to relate to others’ posts, while also making it fun to see diferent kinds of beautiful pictures (e.g., people, landscapes, food).

Prior studies on media selection paid attention to media richness, media synchronicity, and social presence, and postulated that the fit between those traits and task requirements determines the media selection [8,28,49]. In contrast, the media traits that afect SNS selection include the traits regarding information, instant communication, and recreation. The benefits and values that result from those traits determine SNS selection behavior. The HVMs indicate that each SNS has its own key attributes that lead to diferent consequences and values. Because of its massive user base, Facebook is the main collection place for information. Twitter is another example of an information hub with a large user base. LINE is an instant communication tool used for instant messaging, voice over IP, or video chat. WhatsApp, WeChat, and Skype also belong to this communication type of SNS. Instagram is a collection place for pictures, mostly for recreation. Snapchat and Pinterest also belong to this recreation type of SNS. Diferent types of SNSs provide users with diferent attributes that can lead to the user’s required benefits and values. As a result, people are motived to use diferent SNSs. Thus, we propose the following.

P1: SNS traits (attributes) and the benefits and values that result from these traits influence Internet users’ SNS selection behavior.

## 4.2. SNS repertoires

Socializing refers to interacting with friends on the SNS to maintain relationships. Two repertoires are used for socializing: (1) Facebook and LINE, and (2) Facebook and Instagram. The Facebook and LINE repertoire is most common among users between the ages of 30 and 39. In general, users post videos and pictures to interact with friends on Facebook, but send messages to interact with friends on LINE. On Facebook, users browse friends’ posts and learn about their lives in a passive way, whereas they ask about friends’ status and lives directly and proactively on LINE.

I passively read others’ posts on Facebook because many friends on it are not so familiar, but I proactively ask for my friends’ current status on LINE since friends on LINE are close friends. (B04)

Social activities on Facebook are more exciting, including the News Feed, photos, videos, and other content, LINE can only send messages to achieve the purpose of socializing. (B18)

The repertoire of Facebook and Instagram is commonly used by those who are 20–29 years old. On Instagram, they communicate privately, and only with intimate friends of similar ages. Communication with relatives, colleagues, and new friends is done on Facebook. When users want to share posts, videos, and links, they select Facebook. To share pictures, they choose Instagram.

Facebook has more users and more people with whom I can be in con tact. I can interact with all kinds of people. Instagram is a more private space and a place to socialize with familiar friends. (A05)

Instagram is the place where pictures and photos are shared. Most friends post statuses on Instagram. Facebook is the place where articles and videos are shared. It has more text messages. (A06)

Entertainment is activity designed to allow an escape from pressure and to experience enjoyment. Facebook and Instagram is the main repertoire for entertainment purposes. Users watch long videos (e.g., music videos) and read posts on Facebook, but watch short videos (e.g., a video of a friend’s activity) and view pictures on Instagram. Users get a variety of information from fan pages and groups on Facebook, but get mainly friends’ information (e.g., where they play outside) on Instagram. Facebook provides diferent kinds of information such as business, education, and news. In contrast, the main information Instagram provides is regarding fashion and brands, with more pictures. Facebook users use many functions including videos, votes, and live streaming to escape from the pressures of daily life. In contrast, Instagram users look at friends’ news feeds and stories to have fun.

I like to watch short videos and photos on Instagram. It provides friendly search interface. I obtain a variety of information including jokes from Facebook. (A03)

Instagram is mainly used to check the news feeds and stories of close friends. Facebook provides popular information, has various fan pages and groups, and is more entertaining. I share and watch recommended videos that many people like, and I use more features such as voting and live broadcasting. (A15)

Information seeking refers to how users acquire knowledge and get inspiration for new ideas, and information sharing refers to providing useful information and announcing interesting events to others. Facebook and LINE is the main repertoire used for information seeking and sharing. Users searching for a variety of information use Facebook. When searching for specific information, they use LINE groups. Entertainment information is the most popular type of information users search for on Facebook, and job information is the most popular information type sought in LINE groups. To browse and collect a variety of information, users turn to Facebook. In contrast, they use LINE to ask specific friends for answers and suggestions. Since Facebook users friends lists include less-familiar acquaintances, users usually share general information on Facebook. Other information—particularly private information such as work or personal information—is shared with friends on LINE, because LINE allows users to choose specific recipients.

Using LINE, I seek for work-related information from work groups, ask questions or share personal emotions via one-on-one communications. The dialog history is easy to find. I search for and share entertainment information such as videos about children or dogs on Facebook. (A12)

The information shared on Facebook is one-to-many, and consists mostly of public announcements. LINE messages are one-to-one messages, even oneto-many messages in a group, are more private messages. (B10)

Afection is expressing emotions when asking for support and advice from others. Facebook and LINE is the main repertoire used to garner afection. When users face daily problems (e.g., how to remove grease from T-shirts), they post requests for advice on Facebook. When users face life problems, such as how to choose a job after graduation, they ask for advice by sending person-to-person (one-to-one) messages on LINE. There are two reasons for choosing one-to-one messages on LINE: (1) it is more private than posting on Facebook, and (2) it ensures a response from the receiver. Posting on Facebook does not necessarily guarantee a response.

I talk about my personal afairs and release my emotions with specific friends or groups on LINE. Facebook is more popular and public, so I use it for sharing feelings when I do not need to be comforted such as giving my opinions on current events, purely venting unpleasantness, or simply conveying happy information. It doesn’t matter if anyone responds. (B02)

I talk about personal afairs and life problems via one-to-one chatting on LINE. Facebook can reach many people, so I talk about some little things in my daily life. (B17)

Connection refers to the continuation of ongoing relationships with old friends and the establishment of relationships with new friends via the SNS. Facebook and LINE is the main repertoire used to achieve connection. Since the number of Facebook users is greater than that of LINE users, one can easily make new friends on Facebook. On Facebook, users can add friends via account name, phone number, email, etc. On LINE, however, they are required to add friends via a phone number or LINE ID. Therefore, adding friends is easier on Facebook than on LINE. Thus, users usually communicate with lessfamiliar acquaintances on Facebook, but communicate with close friends on LINE. Users prefer using LINE groups to using the group chat rooms on Facebook Messenger. Users find the LINE group interface easier to use, and they like stickers that can express real emotions, allowing the users to feel more connected with friends. There are two communication task subjects for connection: work and entertainment. If users want to turn over work afairs to colleagues, they select LINE as the communication channel. LINE informs the message sender as to whether or not the receiver has read the message. To share humor with new friends, users communicate via Facebook.

It's more dificult to add friends using LINE as compared to Facebook because you must know their phone numbers or IDs. Thus, friends who are connected on LINE are familiar friends. Facebook has many users. It recommends friends to you, so it is a good place to make new friends or to find missing friends. (A02)

LINE interaction is relatively direct and one can quickly make contact and respond. It is useful for work discussions. Friends on Facebook usually start topics with highly entertaining content, such as funny things or diferent ideas, to attract others’ attention in order to resonate with this idea or topic, leave messages, talk and connect to each other. (B17)

Self-status seeking refers to building up one’s personal identity and gaining respect from others. Facebook and Instagram is the main repertoire used for self-status seeking. Users comment on public afairs or post business issues on Facebook to establish a professional identity. Users share their daily lives (e.g., food, beautiful scenery) on Instagram to establish their identity as someone who has a rich and colorful life. As mentioned above, Facebook is formed around acquaintances, and Instagram is formed around close friends. Therefore, users are accustomed to making positive posts on Facebook and sharing negative emotions on Instagram. Users consider Facebook as a way to create an objective impression for friends because it includes basic information such as one’s birthday and education, and Instagram is better used to establish a subjective impression for friends because picture posts show one’s style.

Instagram posts should be matched with pictures, so before posting I think about which type of articles and pictures can present my image and characteristics. Facebook allows users to maintain their profile, including cover photos, birthdays, birthplaces, education, relationship status, etc. The profile can give others an objective impression of me. (A06)

People use a combination of SNSs for specific task purposes.

Complementarity among SNSs is the reason why people choose specific SNSs [14]. The use of SNS repertoires can be determined by information complementarity, in terms of type (e.g., text, pictures, and video) and variety. SNS repertoire usage can also be based on communication complementarity, in terms of role (e.g., receivers and senders), type (e.g., one-to-one and one-to-many), and task $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . g . , } }$ , work or entertain ment). SNS repertoire usage can also be based on communicator complementarity, i.e., the level of intimacy between communicators, the need for privacy and responsiveness, and the sort of identity and im pression the user wants to create. The complementarity of platform features such as the size of the user base and the ease of emotional expression (e.g., stickers) can also determine SNS repertoire usage. Based on these findings, we propose the following.

P2: People select multiple SNSs for specific purposes because of the complementarity of information, communication, communicators, and platform features.

## 4.3. Situational factors

Six situational factors afect users’ SNSs selection behaviors: (1) message topics, (2) communicator characteristics, (3) the individual’s age and medium expertise, (4) task characteristics, (5) message characteristics, and (6) time pressure.

Message topics: As mentioned above, users prefer to post politics, business knowledge, and technology issues on Facebook, which has more SNS users, and they can share each other’s opinions to expand their view. Users prefer to post food, fashion, and sightseeing pictures on Instagram, which aggregates a multitude of pictures under a single hashtag. Users prefer to announce job afairs on LINE, which includes groups of company members, and a “read” function, which allows everyone to follow the progress and schedule.

Communicator characteristics: Since users’ Facebook friends list may include coworkers and supervisors (e.g., colleagues, bosses, and teachers), users may be reluctant to share their daily lives on Facebook, and are more likely to post only positive events or express only positive emotions on Facebook. To express negative emotions and share sensitive issues, users choose Instagram and LINE, because Instagram is formed around close friends (since there is only one way to add friends), and LINE enables them to control who can receive the message. The roles of Facebook friends (e.g., colleagues, bosses, and teachers) are another reason why users share their daily lives on LINE instead of on Facebook, because such sharing may give the impression that they do not work hard or that they can take on more work.

I use Instagram to express my personal thoughts and feelings. It is a more private space than Facebook. Facebook makes me feel stressed because my parents and boss are on it. (A01)

Individual’s age and medium expertise: Our respondents were 20–39 years old. Based on our interviews, 97.5% of 20–39-year-olds use Facebook to search for various kinds of needed information. Within our sample, 80% of those aged 20–29 reduce their use of Facebook and turn to Instagram because they can easily adapt to new technology and a new SNS interface. Of the respondents between the ages of 30 and 39, 55% commonly used Facebook instead of the telephone to communicate. This is the result of medium expertise: users are familiar with using Facebook to connect with each other despite Facebook’s lack of richness compared to the telephone. Users between the ages of 20 and 39 use LINE, but they use it for diferent purposes. Half (50%) of the interviewees aged between 20 and 29 years ask friends out and chat with friends on LINE. In contrast, 45% of the interviewees between the ages of 30 and 39 commonly used LINE to communicate with colleagues and bosses regarding work issues.

Task characteristics: Diferent task categories afect users’ selection of diferent SNSs. When searching broadly for various kinds of information, users choose Facebook, because of its large user base. To search for specific information (e.g., restaurants, clothes), they choose Instagram for its picture posts. When asking for opinions from relevant groups (e.g., how to educate kids), users choose Facebook groups and LINE groups.

I obtain a variety of information from Facebook, use LINE for work discussions and chatting with friends, and find and enjoy photos on Instagram. (B15)

Message characteristics: To communicate sensitive information (e.g., family problems and unemployment) and negative emotions (e.g., anger and disappointment), users between the ages of 30 and 39 choose LINE, and those between the ages of 20 and 29 choose Instagram, because friends on LINE and Instagram are closer than those on Facebook. To express opinions on a formal issue (e.g., opinions on news) and to share positive emotions (e.g., happiness and enjoyment), 20–39-yearolds choose Facebook because they want to get praise and more feed back from Facebook’s large user base. When sharing the same happy event on Facebook and LINE simultaneously, users make a short Facebook post of the event results, and share the process of such an event via long messages with a specific person on LINE.

I generally share the results of events on Facebook but talk about the detailed processes of the same events with familiar friends by one-to-one chatting on LINE. (A12)

Time pressure: Users between 20 and 29 years old select Facebook to discuss reports. However, if the message is urgent or they need to meet the deadline, users change their SNS choice. They usually have both a Facebook group and a LINE group for the same members in case they need to communicate instantly. For example, to change a plan or fix something on a report, group members use LINE’s “Free Call” function to have an immediate discussion on the issue. After discussing, they update the schedule on Facebook. Users between 30 and 39 years old choose LINE to discuss work issues. When confronting problems, they are used to sending email or calling directly on the phone, rathe than using SNSs.

If something is urgent like suddenly changing the meeting time and place the LINE free call and video call can instantly communicate with group members and ofer telepresence. (A19)

Similar to media selection in the organizational context, time pressure, individual's age and expertise, and task characteristics are important situational factors of SNS selections. More urgent communications should prompt users to select a medium that possesses realtime response capabilities [32,48]. Younger people are less resistant to adopt new technologies [1]. Previous medium experience is a predictor of future media selection behavior [1,43]. Users can improve performance by matching media characteristics to the needs of the information processing tasks [32]. In addition, message topics, characteristics, and receivers are influential in SNS selections for general Internet users. Based on these findings, we propose the following.

P3: Message topics, communicator characteristics, task characteristics, message characteristics, time pressure, and the individual’s age and medium expertise influence users’ selection of SNSs.

## 4.4. Social factors

Three social factors afect users’ SNS selection behaviors: (1) network externalities, (2) subjective norms, and (3) companies (oficial accounts) and celebrities.

Network externalities: This term is defined as the increase in the value and benefits obtained from products or services as a result of the extended number of users [24]. “Large user base” is the main social factor that drives users to choose Facebook. One benefit is that Facebook’s large user base creates more information and more friends, which can be easily searched by users.

I can meet more friends on Facebook because of its large number of users. I find people with the same interests through the groups and fan pages. For example, if I find people who are interested in investing, I can learn from their investment views and logic so I can figure out and modify my investment methods. (A03)

Subjective norms: This term refers to how a person’s behaviors are afected by his or her perception of the opinions and behaviors of important others such as parents, friends, and colleagues [42]. Friends are the key social factor that influences users’ selection of Facebook. People are encouraged by their friends to use Facebook so that they can talk about a common topic and increase the entertainment value. Moreover, friends tell their friends to follow stores on Facebook because they can shop in the same stores and share shipping fees. Colleagues, customers, and friends are social factors that influence users to choose LINE, which is usually used for work communication. Ofice workers discuss issues with colleagues in LINE chat rooms. Albums and notes can be used to record the messages exchanged during the discussion. In addition, customers influence ofice workers to choose LINE as the communication channel. Customers find searching through the message history on LINE to be a convenient way to find information, such as a product’s price during a specific transaction. Users are encouraged by friends to use LINE groups in order to make free group calls and discuss things with each other in real time.

I use Facebook because the majority of my friends use it. My colleagues and relatives commonly use LINE so I use it in order to communicate with them. (A02)

Companies (oficial accounts) and celebrities: Oficial accounts are another social factor that causes users to increase their LINE usage. Oficial accounts may give free stickers as a reward for adding them as LINE friends. Users get not only free stickers but also new information from oficial accounts. Such accounts also have rewards cards that entice consumers to accumulate points for gifts. This encourages customers to continue to visit the physical stores of oficial accounts, increasing customer retention. In addition, oficial accounts make it easy for consumers to ask questions. Oficial accounts, Internet celebrities, and foreign celebrities are social factors that influence users to select Instagram. “Post pictures” is Instagram’s primary characteristic. Brands that offer such things as cosmetics and clothing can take advantage of this Instagram feature to promote their products via their oficial accounts. In addition, oficial accounts allow users to ask questions about the products directly—or even place orders—using Instagram’s message box. This marketing strategy makes it convenient for Instagram users to buy commodities. Some Internet celebrities use Instagram as their only SNS platform because their personal homepages are comprised of hundreds of pictures which show their unique style and build up thei personal image. Users usually collect brand and fashion pictures from these Internet celebrities to imitate their style or stimulate design ideas for work. Furthermore, the accounts of foreign celebrities (e.g., Korean celebrities) are easier to find on Instagram than on Facebook because of Instagram’s English ID. Users who are eager to follow and browse for eign celebrities’ information are increasingly likely to turn to Instagram.

LINE oficial accounts can increase my usage of LINE because they provide me with free stickers and special ofers. (A03)

I use Instagram for entertainment because almost all of the Internet ce lebrities I follow use Instagram and post only on Instagram. (A13)

In the context of the organizational environment, the opinions of coworkers and supervisors have an impact on media selection [43]. In contrast, the number of users, the opinions of friends, colleagues, and customers, as well as brands and celebrities the user follows have an impact on SNS selections. In summary, we propose the following.

P4: Social influence from network externalities (e.g., user base), subjective norms (e.g., friends, colleagues, customers), and companies (oficial accounts) and celebrities determine SNS selection.

## 5. Conclusions

Previous studies on media selection focused mainly on the selection among diferent types of media in the context of the organizational environment for work purposes [7,13,29,41]. The present study focuses on the selection among the same type of media, i.e., social media, in the context of users' daily lives for various purposes. Prior studies on SNS usage did not consider traits that distinguish SNSs, nor did they consider the social and situational impacts [20,31,40,52]. Those studies cannot explain why users choose specific combinations of SNSs. Our study identifies SNS repertoires and reveals the reasons why people need to use a set of SNSs for specific purposes. We combined media selection theories, the media repertoire perspective, and uses and gratifications theory to understand how and when a series of SNSs is selected by users. This study systematically identified the factors that have an impact on Internet users’ SNS selections. The factors are comprised of media traits, and situational and social factors. The con cept of media repertoires advances our understanding of how we select a combination of SNSs for specific purposes. The complementarity among SNSs—in terms of information, communication, communicators, and platform features—determines the use of SNS repertoires. We conducted laddering interviews based on the means-end chain model to discover distinct attributes of diferent SNSs and the benefits and values that result from those attributes. Since the HVMs were developed based on comparisons (i.e., the respondents were asked to compare the SNSs they used), the HVMs can show the distinctions between SNSs. In addition, the comparison phase in the interview elicited the respondents' decision criteria for choosing SNSs, which enabled us to identify the social and situational factors. Fig. 1 summarizes our findings and presents a conceptual model to explain SNS selection behavior. Based on our findings, we developed propositions that can provide direction for future research on SNS selection and usage.

Our study shows that Internet users do use a specific combination of SNSs for specific purposes. To shape their marketing messages for every SNS in the repertoire and increase audience engagement with the brand, marketers should know their audiences’ SNS repertoires as well as the usage motivations and distinct traits of the SNSs. Prior studies have shown that consumer engagement with and loyalty to the brand increase if the brand can satisfy their motivations or needs via SNSs [20,22]. We recommend that marketers use the information type of SNS (e.g., Facebook) to ofer various sorts of information to their audience, and to use the recreation type of SNS (e.g., Instagram) to hold activities and have fun with the audience while using the communication type of SNS (e.g., LINE) to provide customer services. For branding purposes, marketers can also utilize the key attribute of each SNS, such as Face book's check in, LINE's stickers, and Instagram's picture posts.

Social factors (e.g., large user base, important others, and favorable celebrities and companies) can also influence SNS selection. An SNS becomes more valuable as its user base expands. Network externality plays a critical role in Internet users' SNS selection decisions. A large user base is also a quality cue that induces the user perception that the SNS has a relative advantage over its alternatives [27]. SNS providers must expand their network to generate network efects by acquiring new users. SNS providers can provide incentives to encourage current users to invite their friends to join the SNS, and utilize the influence of popular celebrities and brands to attract more users. In addition, a plenitude of complementary services can create indirect network efects and increase the value of the network. For instance, users can log into many online services (e.g., Spotify) using their Facebook account, which expands the network value of Facebook.

Both marketers and SNS providers should consider the impact of situational factors. People use diferent SNSs based on the topics they want to discuss, the communicators with whom they want to talk, the type of tasks they want to perform, the generality or sensitivity of the issues they want to share, and the urgency of the communication. To strategically devise content and create a marketing buzz, marketers should understand which topics people like to discuss and which tasks people usually do on which SNSs [10]. SNS providers should enable users to easily group their friends and specify recipients, so that users can create both public and private spaces for diferent issues. The free phone and video call functions are important for urgent communications. People need both synchronous and asynchronous communications, so they always use both social networks and messenger apps. SNS providers should support both synchronous and asynchronous communications by providing two types of apps or by cooperating with other SNS providers to create an integrated user experience. Since young people can easily adapt to a new SNS, late entrants into the SNS market should target young people and entice them to switch from the incumbent SNS.

The study has some methodological constraints that might be addressed in future research. Given that Facebook, LINE, Instagram were the most popular SNSs in Taiwan, our study analyzed SNS selection behavior based on these three SNSs. Future research may benefit from considering usage and selection behavior in the context of other SNSs. Qualitative interviews were used to reveal insights into how and when people select SNSs, and to build a model for future research. We recommend that future studies examine the relationships between the constructs via quantitative methods in order to understand their statistical significance.
