# The Relative Values of Big Data Analytics Versus Traditional Marketing Analytics to Firm Innovation: An Empirical Study

## ABSTRACT

Although big data analytics (BDA) does enhance innovation performance, it remains unclear whether BDA contributes more or less than traditional marketing analytics (TMA) to firm innovation. This study tests hypotheses based on data collected from multiple sources by using four-wave survey and archival data from 201 firms. The results indicate that BDA has a stronger positive effect on new product development (NPD) performance than TMA, over time. However, TMA, when coupled with exploitative innovation strategy, has a stronger positive effect on NPD performance than if coupled with exploratory innovation strategy. Conversely, BDA, when coupled with exploratory innovation strategy, has a stronger positive effect on NPD performance than if coupled with exploitative innovation strategy.

Keywords: Big data analytics; Traditional marketing analytics; Innovation strategy; Exploration; Exploitation; New product development performance

## 1. Introduction

Big data analytics (BDA) is becoming increasingly important in innovation development, and recent studies have shown examples of the contribution of BDA to innovation or new product development performance (Mikalef et al., 2020; Lehrer et al., 2018; Johnson et al., 2017). Scholars also indicate that a growing number of firms, such as IBM and Xerox, are becoming BDA-dominant because BDA enables them to more effectively create new products and services (Ciampi et al., 2021). However, although the contributions of previous studies on BDA are substantial, two research gaps remain in the extant information systems (IS) literature.

First, in spite of an increasing number of studies investigating the effects of BDA on firm performance or other types of performance, no studies have been conducted comparing BDA and traditional marketing analytics (TMA) and their relative effects on innovation or new product development (NPD) performance (see Table 1). In addition, although BDA has been widely lauded for the immense potential it can bring to firms (Cao et al., 2022; Elia et al., 2022; Dong and Yang, 2020), relatively little attention is given to whether such potential brought by BDA to firms is really greater than what TMA brings. We believe that comparing the effects of BDA versus TMA is worthy of significant research efforts for two obvious reasons.

First, although BDA boasts its size and capability to explore anything hidden in the data (Ciampi et al, 2021), TMA is adept at exploiting data assisted by modeling tools for making marketing decisions (Iacobucci et al. 2019) and can still give insights for firms’ decision making (Cao and Tian, 2020). In other words, both BDA and TMA are expected to bring benefits to the firm via their different capabilities (Sheth 2021; Ransbotham et al., 2017), and we cannot be sure if BDA brings greater benefits than TMA to firms without properly comparing the two. Second, IS researchers show that, in spite of an increasing adoption of BDA among firms (Wang et al., 2021; Teubner and Stockhinger, 2020), TMA is still being widely used by firms across the world, whether they adopt BDA or not. In fact, not all the data of a firm is kept in the same way as big data. According to Hamilton and Sodeman (2020), for those firms that have adopted BDA, most of them still embrace TMA. This means that many firms use BDA and TMA simultaneously. Thus, it is beneficial not only for the IS research community but also for managers to come to grips with the relative effects that BDA and TMA can bring about.

In the IS literature, both BDA and TMA focus on how to generate knowledge from the process of finding relationships among data, leading to more effective performance (Mikalef et al., 2019; Wedel and Kannan, 2016). Specifically, both BDA and TMA can do the same job in identifying relationships. One difference between the two analytics is that BDA finds these relationships without researchers hypothesizing them by using machine learning or other means, whereas TMA needs researchers to specify hypothetical relationships that are then tested (Cao et al., 2022). The IS literature echoes this view that BDA is very good and efficient in identifying relationships between variables, but TMA can do the same, albeit not as well or as efficiently (e.g., Oesterreich et al., 2022; Mikalef et al., 2020; Wedel and Kannan, 2016). In this regard, we draw on gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970), which provides a theoretical understanding that insight learning occurs suddenly, when people discover new relationships within their prior knowledge. As such, gestalt insight learning seems more likely to occur in BDA, but this is not to disassociate the potential for insight learning from TMA. Adopting appropriate statistical techniques under TMA and visual what if scenario analysis empowered by technological advances, TMA can also help discover new, unexpected relationships between variables. Therefore, both BDA and TMA can draw on gestalt insight learning theory in exercising their expected positive effect on NPD performance. We expect BDA’s effect to be greater because of the reasons cited previously, but even if its effect is greater, would this greater effect be statistically significant? This question has not been answered in the current literature. Accordingly, our first research question is: BDA versus TMA which has a stronger effect on NPD performance?

Second, extant studies indicate that either BDA (Mikalef et al., 2021; Gupta et al., 2020) or TMA (Ghasemaghaei et al., 2017; Germann et al., 2013) is necessary, but not sufficient to ensure competitive advantages. IS researchers believe that selected organizational factors could hold the key to the resulting level of effectiveness of BDA/TMA and therefore call for more studies for identifying what organizational factors could mediate or moderate the effectiveness of BDA/TMA (Mikalef et al., 2021a; Wedel and Kannan, 2016). For instance, every business initiates different strategies from time to time, and these strategies will dictate how the business is run (Onufrey and Bergek, 2021). Therefore, strategy is expected to influence the effect of BDA versus TMA on NPD performance. Among all strategies, innovation strategy has been singled out as the one that shares more features with information technology (IT) than any other types of business strategies (Onufrey and Bergek, 2021). This is because both innovation strategy and IT are recognized as resources for firm upgrading (Teubner and Stockhinger, 2020) and are involved in exploration and/or exploitation of knowledge sources (Eseryel, 2014), which eventually can bring innovation benefits to firms. The closeness of innovation strategy to IT is also consistent with the studies of Cui et al. (2022), Gregory et al. (2015), and Cui et al. (2015), who indicate that the use of IT should be coupled with innovation strategy for innovation development purposes. However, so far, the current literature has not investigated innovation strategy as potentially influential in linking BDA or TMA to innovation performance (see Table 1).

In this study, we integrate the strategic information technology alignment (SITA) perspective with the innovation ambidexterity theory to propose the potential moderating effects of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies on the roles of BDA/TMA in influencing NPD performance. In IS research, a key tenet of the SITA perspective is viewed as the fit between IT and business strategy (Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011). According to this perspective, firms will achieve superior performance when key IT resources are aligned with appropriate business strategy (Chau et al., 2020). For example, a review study indicates that the alignment of IT applications and business strategy has created superior business value (Coltman et al., 2015). Other empirical IS studies further confirm this important business truth because they have found that SITA improves business performance (e.g., Sabherwal et al., 2019; Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011; Chan et al., 1997). Although SITA is important for firms seeking enhanced performance through aligning IT and business strategy, so far there is a lack of research examining the alignment of BDA/TMA (as IT resources) and innovation strategy (as one of the business strategies).

Among all innovation strategies, exploratory and exploitative are widely considered as the two central innovation strategies in the IS literature (e.g., Roberts et al., 2021; Syed et al., 2021; Werder and Heckmann, 2019). The innovation ambidexterity theory (Gregory et al., 2015; Benner and Tushman, 2003) further highlights exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies as critical factors for achieving superior performance, in which firms can manage both in a pole position to more effectively implement IT, leading to better firm performance. Exploratory innovation strategy refers to radical departures from existing offerings, creating new markets, and identifying needs for emerging markets, whereas exploitative innovation strategy refers to incremental improvements to offerings that satisfy existing customers in known markets (Hill and Birkinshaw, 2014; He and Wong, 2004). With exploratory innovation strategy, firms engage in discovery, experimentation, and variation to create new products. Conversely, with exploitative innovation strategy, firms focus on refining and extending existing processes and technologies for the development of new products (Müller et al., 2021). In addition, exploratory innovation strategy requires new knowledge or departure from existing knowledge, whereas exploitative innovation strategy is based on existing knowledge and reinforces existing skills, processes, and structures (Enkel et al., 2017). Thus, exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies are two fundamentally different mechanisms related to how firms implement innovation.

In the IS domain, exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies have been applied to various areas, such as IT ambidexterity capability or organizational structural ambidexterity, and have been considered among the most important firm-level factors influencing innovation performance (Syed et al., 2021; Park et al., 2020; Božič and Dimovski, 2019). However, as discussed previously, exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies are fundamentally different in their focus and the knowledge bases they use (Benitez et al., 2018; Gonzalez and de Melo, 2018). As such, we contend the roles of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies in leveraging BDA/TMA are also significantly different from each other. In this regard, because of pursuing different objectives at the firm level, the role of exploratory innovation strategy in leveraging BDA/TMA is expected to be different compared with the role of exploitative innovation strategy. So far, to the best of our knowledge, no research has investigated how each of the two innovation strategies leverages BDA/TMA. This paper aims to fill this void and enhance our knowledge, which is academically and practically important. Additionally, considering knowledge base plays an important role in exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies (Enkel et al., 2017; Zhou and Li, 2012), we therefore incorporate the knowledge-based view (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996) with its concomitant concepts of knowledge breadth and knowledge depth in the development and justification of our conceptual model (Cao et al., 2020; Ko and Liu, 2019). Overall, this study addresses the following second research question: Does the relative effect of BDA versus TMA on NPD performance vary when coupled with exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies?

The proposed model (Figure 1) outlines the hypothetical interrelationships based on these two research questions, as well as where each of the theories exerts its influence in the model. To test the hypotheses, we use multiple sources of respondents, survey data, and archival data from 201 firms. The results support our expectation that the effect of BDA on NPD performance is greater than that of TMA over time, whereas NPD performance driven by BDA and TMA varies depending on whether it is coupled with an appropriate innovation strategy or not. Our theorization and empirical findings make a distinct contribution to the IS literature by being the first to empirically examine (1) the relative effects of BDA versus TMA on NPD performance and (2) the effect of the alignment between BDA/TMA and innovation strategy on NPD performance, thereby responding to the call from IS studies for more empirical investigations into the relative values of BDA and TMA (Cao et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2016), as well as into the relationship between BDA and strategy (Talaoui et al., 2023).

## 2. Theoretical Background

First, we draw on gestalt insight learning theory to explain the relationships among BDA, TMA, and NPD performance. We then apply the SITA perspective and the innovation ambidexterity theory for justifying the choice of strategy, in general, and provide rationales supported by published literature for deciding on the innovation strategy, in particular, as moderator in the above relationships. Finally, we rely on the knowledge-based view (KBV) for hypothesizing which of the two couplings of BDA\*exploratory innovation strategy and TMA\*exploitative innovation strategy would produce the best NPD performance.

## 2.1 Gestalt insight learning theory

Gestalt insight learning theory originates from the field of psychology (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970). Its maxim is that new insights might be triggered by looking for new relationships among a variety of variables. Proponents of this theory see insight as a sudden moment of fantastic ideas as the researcher discovers “how parts of a problem match together to make a gestalt (a coherent whole)” (Ghasemaghaei and Calic, 2019, p. 70). One key premise of gestalt insight learning theory is that all of the problems we face and all of their respective solutions are about discovering previously-unfounded relationships (Ash et al., 2012). As such, insights could be found via what-if analyses of statistical modelling of potential new relationships between factors. Herschel and Jones (2005) concur with this premise, as they posit that insights usually occur suddenly, as the researchers discover new relationships within their current knowledge which, in turn, result from a process of knowledge restructuring.

BDA involves speedy integration of large amounts of data from a variety of sources (Mikalef et al., 2019), whereas TMA involves the collection, analyses, and management of data to create new insights (Wedel and Kannan, 2016). This potentially leads to discovery of insights in the form of new relationships or patterns that can solve the problems at hand (Ash et al., 2012; Herschel and Jones, 2005). Particularly, researchers indicate that BDA possesses a number of Vs, most notably velocity, volume, and variety (Akerkar, 2014). It is the last V (variety) that has been hailed by Ellis (2014) as the catalyst for the potential of BDA to be exploited in such a way that insights in the form of new relationships hidden in the data can be discovered. These insights, in turn, can be used for developing strategies that enhance customer satisfaction and ultimately business performance (Sheth, 2021). In a more recent study, Ghasemaghaei and Calic (2019) further categorize BDA into descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive insights. Descriptive insights are about discovering what has happened in the past, whereas predictive insights are about what will happen in the future, and prescriptive insights are about identifying the optimal course of action to deal with a certain problem or scenario. Because both BDA and TMA are expected to produce insights that can contribute to firm performance, including NPD performance, gestalt insight learning theory is deemed ver appropriate for supporting the underlying theoretical framework of this study. As can be found in Figure 1, this theory is chosen to support the expected relationship between BDA/TMA and NPD performance. However, as shown in Table 1, extant studies have not considered the moderating role of innovation strategy on the relationship between BDA/TMA and NPD performance. Thus, we draw on the SITA perspective to address this critical research gap.

## 2.2 The SITA perspective and innovation strategy

The SITA perspective was introduced by Henderson and Venkatraman (1999), who conceptualized SITA as the fit between business strategy, IT strategy, organizationa infrastructure and processes, and IT infrastructure and processes. In a thorough review of IS literature, the SITA perspective can be viewed as a state of congruence, the degree to which a firm’s IT applications conform to business strategies, which directly affects firm performance (Coltman et al., 2015; Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011). Empirical studies adopt the SITA perspective as a state of congruence by focusing on its effects on the performance of the IT applications and the organization (e.g., Sabherwal et al., 2019; Chan et al. 1997). Luftman (2004) defines a good SITA as the application of the appropriate IT at the right time and place, with the purpose of helping firms to achieve their objectives. Queiroz (2017) further describes a good SITA as one when firms’ activities and the IS that support them remain in harmony. Overall, this study recognizes the SITA perspective as the fit between realized IT applications and business strategy, which is consistent with “how well the content of the realized business strategy matches the content of the realized IT strategy” (Wu et al., 2015, p. 503).

IS research has continued to build on empirical evidence that reveals positive effects of SITA on business performance (e.g., Ilmudeen et al., 2022; Oehlhorn et al., 2020; Sabherwal et al., 2019). Particularly, based on the SITA perspective, a wealth of studies (e.g., Chatterjee et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2015; Coltman et al., 2015) has validated empirically that the use of IT aligned with business strategy has a positive effect on innovation performance. Because this study involves IT-enabled analytics (BDA and TMA) and business strategy for the innovation activities of firms (innovation strategies), the SITA perspective is considered perfect fo theorizing our conceptual model build-up. To the best of our knowledge, no previous SITArelated studies have delved into the effect of the alignment of BDA/TMA and innovation strategy.

Business strategy is a generic strategy term comprising innovation strategy and other tactical-level strategies (Kitsios and Kamariotou, 2019). The rationale of such a perspective is that a firm can use innovation strategy to enhance the effect of IT, thereby resulting in better business performance (Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011). This alignment can then enable more effective resource allocation to innovation (Chan et al., 1997). In this vein, BDA and TMA, as in the internal IT application domain, should not be decided alone but instead should be aligned with innovation strategy to achieve superior performance. In the context of this study, BDA and TMA have strong advantages in predicting customer needs and improving the NPD process through the amount of data obtained from markets. Because of the possible direct relationship between BDA/TMA and NPD performance, this study treats the alignment as a moderation type of fit. The moderation perspective of fit is also validated in IS research. For example, Gao et al. (2019) find that the effect of IT on organizational agility is moderated by the alignment of IT and the business strategy. In an in-depth systematic review of IS literature, Günther et al. (2017) indicate that the alignment of a firm’s business strategy is crucial for BDA to translate its potential into economic value. Thus, the moderation perspective of fit is an appropriate lens to explain the role of BDA/TMA–innovation strategy alignment in the NPD process.

Although the SITA perspective has been used widely to explain business performance variances, the innovation ambidexterity theory places emphasis on the role of innovation strategy in influencing business performance. Thus, we believe that the innovation ambidexterity theory offers a useful theoretical lens to understand how innovation strategy could moderate the effect of BDA/TMA on NPD performance.

According to the innovation ambidexterity theory (Gregory et al., 2015; Benner and Tushman, 2003), exploratory and exploitative are two distinct and contradictory dimensions of innovation strategy and have been used extensively in innovation strategy studies. Originally, March (1991) proposes the concept of ambidexterity, exploration, and exploitation. This is followed by Tushman and O’Reilly (1996), in which exploration is more important than exploitation in developing new products. A recent review of innovation ambidexterity in IS research also shows that researchers classify innovation strategy into exploratory and exploitative innovation, and many researchers conduct extensive studies on their antecedents and consequences (Werder and Heckmann, 2019). Therefore, the innovation strategy literature indicates that exploratory innovation strategy involves the combination of existing and new knowledge and the creation of novel knowledge, whereas exploitative innovation strategy involves refining, deepening, and reconfiguring existing knowledge, and applying it in new ways (Enkel et al., 2017; Benner and Tushman, 2003).

Despite the appeal of striking a balance between innovation strategies, this objective faces a challenge because both exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies are distinct and contradictory (Werder and Heckmann, 2019; March, 1991). This is especially the case for firms that have limited internal resources and poor access to external resources, and these firms are more likely to struggle to find a balance between exploratory and exploitative strategies (Simsek, 2009). This argument is applicable to firms using BDA/TMA because these firms cannot fulfill all of the requirements of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies (Werder and Heckmann, 2019). As such, relying on the SITA perspective (Coltman et al., 2015; Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011; Chan et al., 1997), we disentangle the 2 (data analytics approaches: BDA and TMA) \* 2 (innovation strategies: exploratory and exploitative) matrix puzzle by finding out how to couple one data analytics approach with one innovation strategy to produce the best NPD result. It is worth emphasizing here that innovation ambidexterity theory per se is not used to explain any hypothetical relationships in the model. Instead, the theory is referred to here as coming to grips with the two most common strategies (exploratory and exploitative) in the context of innovation strategy.

Overall, the SITA perspective points to the appropriateness of strategy as a potential moderator in our study. Then, the empirical results of extant studies (e.g., Chatterjee et al. 2020; Cui et al., 2015; Coltman et al., 2015) are used for supporting the choice of innovation strategies rather than any other strategies as the potential moderator. Knowledge from the innovation ambidexterity theory is further solicited for dissecting innovation strategy into two major types (exploratory and exploitative) to be used for our conceptual model building.

The innovation literature has indicated that knowledge base is regarded as an important theoretical foundation of exploratory and exploitative innovation (e.g., Enkel et al., 2017; Zhou and Li, 2012). These arguments are consistent with those in the KBV, which focuses on how firms deal with acquiring new knowledge (exploratory innovation) or trying to take advantage of their existing knowledge base (exploitative innovation). Therefore, we apply the KBV to discuss the roles of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies.

## 2.3 Knowledge-based view

The KBV emphasizes the importance of organizational knowledge for competitiveness and success, which can be achieved via different means including superior innovation performance (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996). Indeed, there have been studies demonstrating the close linkage between knowledge and innovation. For example, Chen et al. (2021) find that coopetition positively and indirectly affects radical innovation via external knowledge integration. Additionally, Zhou and Li (2012) discover that a firm with greater knowledge breadth is more receptive to knowledge sharing for its radical innovation activities, whereas a firm with greater knowledge depth is more responsive to market knowledge acquisition for the same activities. Grillitsch et al. (2019) further show that a firm’s knowledge base can foretell its ability to understand and apply new knowledge for innovation purposes. This new knowledge occurs in different forms, such as new technological knowledge and new market knowledge, both of which are needed for different stages of the innovation process. No wonder Majchrzak et al. (2013) proclaim that the knowledge base possessed by a firm is a unique and significant resource for it to carry out innovation activities successfully.

Meanwhile, the knowledge literature highlights that NPD is a process that involves transforming firms’ embedded knowledge into new products (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Baines and Lawton Smith, 2020; Pitt and MacVaugh, 2008). The innovation literature has also recognized the knowledge base as an important driver of innovation (Wei et al., 2022; Grillitsch et al., 2019; Ko and Liu, 2019). For example, based on the KBV, Wei et al. (2022) investigate how firms’ IT broadens and deepens their knowledge base, which consequently improves digital innovation performance. In addition, according to Ko and Liu (2019), exploratory innovation involves a shift to a different knowledge base with the aim of creating new products, whereas exploitative innovation builds on the firm’s existing knowledge base to improve its existing products. This suggests that the essence of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies is rooted in the concept of the knowledge base. Consistent with IS studies (e.g., Hwang et al., 2019; Majchrzak et al., 2013), we build on the KBV to propose that examining the roles of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies must be considered together with the nature of the knowledge base.

According to the KBV, the knowledge base can be characterized in terms of its breadth and depth (Terjesen and Patel, 2017; Majchrzak et al., 2013; Zhou and Li, 2012). Knowledge breadth refers to “the extent to which the organization’s knowledge repository contains distinct and multiple domains,” whereas knowledge depth refers to “the level of sophistication and complexity of knowledge in key fields” (Zhou and Li, 2012 p. 1091). In the IS literature, high knowledge breadth means that a firm has rich knowledge in diverse domains, which is beneficial for generating novel knowledge. On the other hand, high knowledge depth pertains to the extent of thoroughness of a firm’s knowledge and technical expertise within its specialized fields, which is beneficial for exploiting current knowledge (Tang et al., 2020; Majchrzak et al., 2013). Overall, knowledge breadth captures the structure of firms’ knowledge horizontally and highlights the possession of heterogeneous knowledge content. In contrast, knowledge depth captures the structure of firms’ knowledge vertically and highlights the possession of within-field knowledge content (Wei et al., 2022; Zhou and Li, 2012).

In sum, the success of exploratory innovation strategy depends on firms’ ability to acquire new and diverse knowledge, while the success of exploitative innovation strategy depends on firms’ ability to build on existing knowledge. Knowledge breadth is achieved by seeking new and diverse knowledge, whereas knowledge depth is achieved by seeking to increase firms existing knowledge in specific areas. In this sense, knowledge breadth can be seen as the foundation of exploratory innovation strategy, while knowledge depth is considered as the foundation of exploitative innovation strategy. This perspective has been widely developed by scholars (Jin et al., 2019; Chang et al., 2016). Accordingly, we use knowledge breadth and knowledge depth as the underlying theoretical foundation of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies, respectively, to examine their moderating effects on the relationship between BDA/TMA and NPD performance, as we discuss next.

## 3. Hypotheses Development

## 3.1 BDA and NPD performance

A review of the literature indicates that using BDA enables firms to discover more readily what has happened in the past, to predict what will happen in the future, and to optimize the best courses of action (Mikalef et al., 2018; Müller et al., 2016). In addition, to generate more profound insight, firms can rely on BDA to integrate large amounts of data from different sources for discovering patterns that otherwise are beyond our imagination. Making effective use of these patterns can then help firms counter threats or create opportunities (Wang et al., 2022). This is in line with the gestalt insight learning theory, which states that fostering insight generation can significantly enhance learning through integrating and analyzing data from a wide array of sources and identifying latent relationships in the thick of large amounts of data (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970). Gaining knowledge of these latent relationships can help firms to make the most appropriate decisions at the strategic level, such as the optimal combination of key attributes of a new product to be developed, or at the tactical level, such as the bes marketing tactics to cater to consumers’ preferences (Mariani and Wamba, 2020; Grover et al., 2018).

Several research studies have demonstrated that BDA can offer substantial business value when targeted to specific domains, such as product innovation (Johnson et al., 2017), service innovation (Lehrer et al., 2018), and business model innovation (Ciampi et al., 2021). Particularly, Ransbotham et al. (2017) indicate that firms using BDA are likely to produce better NPD performance compared with those that are not using BDA. For these reasons, we expect that BDA has a positive impact on NPD performance. Thus, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis 1: Big data analytics has a positive relationship to new product development performance over time.

## 3.2 TMA and NPD performance

TMA is a domain of business analytics, and involves the collection, management, and analysis of marketing data to obtain insights for decision making (Wedel and Kannan, 2016). In TMA, researchers use different statistical techniques to find out if relationships between specific variables are significant or not. These relationships can be plain relationships or causal relationships. Essentially, TMA is deployed in marketing planning related to segmentation advertising, marketing mix allocation, and NPD (Sheth et al., 2021). The IS literature has identified that TMA can be used in a number of areas, such as enhancing marketing decisions (Gao et al., 2019), developing new products (Cao et al., 2022), and improving firm performance (Liang et al., 2022). For example, Gao et al. (2019) indicate that firms can enhance their marketing decisions through the insights gained from their TMA. The marketing literature also highlights that the insights derived from firms’ use of TMA are expected to enrich and expand the knowledge base for firms to further enhance their learning and strengthen their new knowledge generation (Xu et al., 2016). This perspective is consistent with the gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970) that insights are generated by discovering new combinations of information, which are created from reasoning processes.

In line with this, firms’ use of TMA can be expected to improve their NPD performance. The literature on business analytics, to which TMA belongs, supports this prediction, suggesting that a firm can use business analytics to improve its ability to innovate (Duan et al., 2020; Sharma et al., 2014), create products and services from analyses of data (Appelbaum et al., 2017), or offer innovative ways to allow it to differentiate its products (Shi et al., 2022). As Sheth (2021) indicates, firms using TMA are better able to sense market opportunities and threats which helps firms evaluate their new product initiatives and make accurate decisions. Hence, the use of TMA enables firms to access and process market information better, which allows firms to stimulate market insights and create new knowledge and, thus, to meet emerging market opportunities for NPD, leading to new product success. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis 2: Traditional marketing analytics has a positive relationship to new product development performance over time.

## 3.3 Relative effects of BDA versus TMA on NPD performance

Earlier in this paper, we posit that gestalt insight learning theory can help explain how BDA and TMA positively improve NPD performance. Between BDA and TMA, we suggest that the improved NPD performance brought by the former is greater than that from the latter because of the greater capability of BDA to provide unexpected insights. These insights are often more accurate, based on a much greater amount of raw data and of a more real-time nature than those provided by TMA. Next is an explanation of these three enablers of greater insights generated by BDA.

First is the accuracy of prediction. Data collected via TMA can be analyzed to serve different purposes, one of which is NPD-related prediction (Hajli et al., 2020). This is primarily because TMA involves the use of different statistical techniques and the results can be interpreted for obtaining new product insights (Wedel and Kannan, 2016). However, compared with BDA, which usually has a much bigger and usually more representative sample, TMA and its results are more susceptible to bias (Xu et al., 2016).

BDA creates a new way to derive better insights from a huge amount of data, which helps firms truly understand what customers really need (Holmlund et al., 2020). This is because a large amount of data is collected for the possibility of analyzing predictions by using machine learning and artificial intelligence of BDA (Tan and Zhan, 2017). Given machine learning and artificial intelligence of BDA, firms can extract real-time information that is applied to the

NPD process to produce better innovative products that meet customers’ needs (Johnson et al., 2017). As such, compared with the use of TMA, firms that use BDA are better able to make accurate predictions by detecting possible relationships among the variables, such as predicting customers’ needs or generating new product insights. This is consistent with gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970), which enables firms to cultivate new insights effectively in the long run, leading to superior NPD performance over time.

Second is the use of data probing. The majority of TMA deals with smaller data sets with limited analytic platforms (Lilien et al., 2013). Firms that use TMA’s data-probing techniques boost firm profit and revenues; in particular, consumer surveys may provide useful new product insights for developing successful new products (Wedel and Kannan, 2016). However, NPD involves complex activities and requires a large amount of data to explore customer demand (Holmlund et al., 2020). In contrast, the use of BDA enables firms to conduct large-scale data probing during the NPD process (Tan and Zhan, 2017). One example is Line, a freeware app for instant communication on electronic devices that uses probing techniques to extract wha users text message and then to quantify users’ attitudes toward new products, enabling Line to turn data into new product insights (Liao and Hsu, 2020). In the long run, this is essentially a data probing of BDA that provides firms with new product insights to develop successful new products.

Third is the ability to generate real-time market information. Although market information can also be drawn from TMA, extracting this type of information has become one of the most important applications of BDA. In fact, compared with TMA, the use of BDA enables firms to gather and track data on customer behaviors more effectively, and to gain greater new product insight (Gao et al., 2019). Specifically, because the collected market information is gathered and tracked in real time, firms that use BDA are able to readjust their NPD strategies more quickly than those of TMA. This is seen with retailers such as Carrefour, which use BDA to identify higher margin customers in real time and, accordingly, develop new products for these customers, resulting in greater NPD performance (Wang and Wang, 2020). In addition, while TMA can produce superior NPD performance by making effective use of high-value market information on a relatively small-scale basis, BDA can create much better NPD performance because market information can be generated on a low-cost, high-volume basis (Tan and Zhan, 2017). For example, social media platforms such as Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram conduct BDA hoping to track the intentions of users so that the right new products can be developed to meet their intentions. In the long run, this difference in the experiment of market information could lead BDA to create better new product insights than TMA does, leading to greater NPD performance.

To sum up, because of three fundamental differences between BDA and TMA: (1) the accuracy of prediction; (2) the method of data probing; and (3) the experiment of market information, BDA is more likely to result in stronger NPD performance than TMA in the long run. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis 3: Compared with traditional marketing analytics, big data analytics has a stronger positive effect on new product development performance over time.

## 3.4 The moderating role of exploratory innovation strategy

Direct-effects arguments for the impacts of BDA and TMA on NPD performance are expected. However, a careful inspection indicates that the arguments assume a role for innovation strategy. As we discussed previously, both the SITA perspective and the innovation ambidexterity theory suggest that the links between BDA/TMA and NPD performance require innovation strategy to shape such links.

NPD success is dependent on the utilization of new knowledge and the business strategy of firms to innovate new products (Marzi et al., 2020). Drawing on the KBV (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996), we argue that firms engaging in exploratory innovation strategy are more likely to involve knowledge breadth. Specifically, firms using exploratory innovation strategy have more opportunities to access valuable external knowledge for innovation activities (Wei et al., 2022; Zhou and Li, 2012). In addition, according to Lavie et al. (2010), firms need to have distinct well-developed routines and resources to execute exploratory innovation, since they help firms manage exploratory innovation strategy more efficiently. As such, firms using exploratory innovation strategy are more likely to have an internal organizational structure that maximizes the benefits of valuable external knowledge. With such valuable external knowledge, firms using BDA and TMA are more likely to facilitate the integration of external knowledge, because technologies of BDA and TMA potentially lower the absorption cost of external knowledge, which is the cost for identification, retrieval, exploration, and exploitation of external information (Trantopoulos et al., 2017). This discussion is also supported by the majority of the IS literature that firms need to continuously explore new knowledge through business analytics to create superior business value (Chatterjee et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2015; Coltman et al., 2015). Hence, exploratory innovation strategy is essential for firms using BDA and TMA to consider in order to achieve NPD success. Therefore, we contend that exploratory innovation strategy has a positively moderating effect on the relationship between BDA/TMA and NPD performance.

For the comparison of BDA and TMA, this study expects that, compared with TMA coupled with exploratory innovation strategy, BDA coupled with exploratory innovation strategy is expected to exert a stronger effect on NPD performance for the following three reasons.

First, exploratory innovation strategy offers firms the breadth of knowledge (Cao et al., 2020), whereas BDA is better able to provide new ways to analyze unstructured data, aiding NPD (Haili et al., 2020). Thus, coupling exploratory innovation strategy with BDA should potentially enhance NPD performance, compared with TMA. Specifically, the concept of knowledge breadth across different domains of specialized knowledge is extremely relevant in the NPD process (Jin et al., 2019). To achieve superior NPD performance, new products are normally built on novel technologies and often involve a wide range of technologies from different areas (Marzi et al., 2020; Tan and Zhan, 2017). Essentially, the breadth of knowledge is a critical element for exploratory innovation strategy and BDA in particular is better as a way of extracting value from these huge volumes of knowledge than TMA. As such, when coupled with exploratory innovation strategy, the use of BDA enables firms to explore new and diverse knowledge more effectively, which boosts new product success rates, leading t better NPD performance than that of TMA.

Second, when coupled with exploratory innovation strategy, BDA can have better knowledge creation than TMA. This is because, first, with support from exploratory innovation strategy, firms using BDA have more opportunities to obtain diverse knowledge that is beneficial to NPD (Cao et al., 2020). Second, the use of BDA implies that firms have richer opportunities for creating new knowledge which is essential for successful NPD (Fuchs et al., 2014). Therefore, compared with TMA, BDA coupled with exploratory innovation strategy enables firms to be better able to create solid new knowledge, leading to more successful NPD performance.

Third, given the differences between BDA and TMA as discussed, firms using TMA will face difficulties in trying to analyze in real time a wide range of knowledge acquired b exploratory innovation strategy (Liang et al., 2022; Lilien et al., 2013). In addition, the use of exploratory innovation strategy could potentially cause the firm using TMA to lose sight of any feasible new product insights. This is because the firm could lack appropriate data probing tools and skills to use the huge amount of information (Sheth, 2021; Wedel and Kannan, 2016), leading to the fact that few of these insights will be given sufficient effort to bring them into the NPD process. Furthermore, because of the limited accuracy of prediction (Iacobucci et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2016), firms using TMA may not have the ability to interpret a large amount of information accumulated by exploratory innovation strategy and to effectively integrate it for NPD purposes. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis 4a: Exploratory innovation strategy moderates the positive relationship between big data analytics and new product development performance.

Hypothesis 4b: Exploratory innovation strategy moderates the positive relationship between traditional marketing analytics and new product development performance.

Hypothesis 4c: When coupled with exploratory innovation strategy, big data analytics has a stronger positive effect on new product development performance than traditional marketing analytics.

## 3.5 The moderating role of exploitative innovation strategy

Drawing on the KBV (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996), firms engaging in exploitative innovation strategy are more likely to involve knowledge depth, as discussed previously. The depth of experience and expertise in specific knowledge domains can help them to develop a deeper, more refined understanding of the existing IT, such as TMA (Ko and Liu, 2019). In addition, firms with strong knowledge depth are better able to recognize the value of existing IT and to integrate it with the current knowledge base to develop a novel knowledge base (Wei et al., 2022). Furthermore, research on business analytics has shown that firms with strong knowledge depth in a particular domain tend to have better understandings of the associated interrelationships that exist between them (Ciampi et al., 2020). For example, TMA increases the variety of qualitative and quantitative information available to firms and enables the firms to generate novel knowledge from existing and exploitable information (Cao and Tian, 2020). In addition, various applications of BDA exemplify how BDA generates novel knowledge from existing information (Rialti et al., 2020). The IS literature also highlights that the innovative ideas derived from exploitative innovation are more likely to support firms’ IT applications, such as BDA and TMA, for achieving innovation success (Božič and Dimovski, 2019). Thus, given the theoretical foundation of knowledge depth, exploitative innovation strategy is more likely to enhance the effects of BDA/TMA on NPD performance.

For the comparison of BDA and TMA, this study expects that TMA coupled with exploitative innovation strategy has a stronger effect on NPD performance than BDA coupled with the same exploitative innovation strategy, for the following three reasons.

First, exploitative innovation strategy mainly uses knowledge depth to create innovation in a low-risk way (Tang et al., 2020; Enkel et al., 2017; Majchrzak et al., 2013). Unlike knowledge breadth focusing on actively exploring knowledge across different domains, knowledge depth focuses on reconfiguring its existing knowledge (Yang et al., 2017). In addition, because of its deeply scrutinized knowledge, a firm focusing on knowledge depth can assimilate new product knowledge more easily (Mei et al., 2021; Chang et al., 2016). Thus, when coupled with exploitative innovation strategy, compared with BDA, the use of TMA enables firms to reconfigure their existing knowledge more effectively and to integrate existing and new knowledge, both of which are essential for creating successful new products.

Second, compared with those adopting BDA with exploitative innovation strategy, firms adopting TMA with exploitative innovation strategy have better unique data sets (e.g., smaller and more local) and stronger innovation activities associated with marketplaces (Sheth, 2021; Wedel and Kannan, 2016). This can establish good relations with target customers and have efficient internal and external information communication with them in new product offerings, ultimately yielding new product success.

Third, although overemphasis on exploitative innovation strategy can hurt firms’ ability to create radical innovation (Enkel et al., 2017), if firms can excel with sufficient knowledge and experience in using TMA, they can reduce the uncertainty of innovation development bette by exploiting existing knowledge (Liang et al., 2022; Li and Huang, 2012). In addition, firms adopting exploitative innovation strategy can enhance their exploitative learning in TMA better to use existing knowledge and technologies for innovation purposes (Auh et al., 2022; Ballot et al., 2015). This allows firms to improve the implementation of NPD more effectively through applying their prior technological knowledge, which will eventually help to achieve better NPD success. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis 5a: Exploitative innovation strategy moderates the positive relationship between big data analytics and new product development performance.

Hypothesis 5b: Exploitative innovation strategy moderates the positive relationship between traditional marketing analytics and new product development performance.

Hypothesis 5c: When coupled with exploitative innovation strategy, traditional marketing analytics has a stronger positive effect on new product development performance than big data analytics.

## 4. Research Method

## 4.1 Sample and data collection

To reach a representative sample of firms using both BDA and TMA in their NPD processes, we used the initial set of the top 1000 firms in terms of total revenue as compiled by the China Credit Information Service (2018), a leading business database in Taiwan. Second, we contacted NPD managers by phone to verify whether their firm had used both BDA and TMA in its NPD process within the past three years (2015, 2016, and 2017). This led to a target population of 238 firms. Our target firms must have used two data analytics (BDA and TMA) and two innovation strategies (exploratory and exploitative) to varying degrees to be able to compare (1) between BDA and TMA, (2) between BDA\*exploratory and TMA\*exploratory, and (3) between BDA\*exploitative and TMA\*exploitative, within the same sample of firms.

To reduce common method bias regarding single sourcing (Podsakoff et al., 2003, p. 898) and based on the literature in the earlier discussions (Syed et al., 2021; Park et al., 2020; Božič and Dimovski, 2019), we collected data from different respondents in each firm and the unit of analysis was at the firm level. Specifically, following previous studies, chief information officers were asked to provide information for BDA (Mikalef et al., 2019), whereas marketing managers provided information for TMA (Wedel and Kannan, 2016). Chief executive officers (CEOs) provided information for exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies, because CEOs are responsible for developing, supporting, and supervising firm capabilities that take place (Mikalef et al., 2021). NPD managers provided information for NPD performance because NPD managers are able to respond best to questions regarding their firms’ NPD outcomes (Schleimer and Faems, 2016).

To ensure survey quality, we trained our interviewers before they conducted on-site interviews. In addition, we collected data longitudinally over four waves, mid-2018, mid-2019, mid-2020, and mid-2021, to allow time for the performance effects of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies and NPD performance to materialize (Lisboa et al., 2011; Rindfleisch et al., 2008). Moreover, to corroborate the reliability of the NPD performance survey measure and make our results more accurate, we followed IS research (Baesens et al., 2016) to collect archival data related to each firm’s NPD performance because the self reporting nature of survey data can be a concern and because adding archival data can help with triangulation that, in turn, can enhance the resulting quality of the data used for this study (Berg, 2004).

In mid-2018, we contacted NPD managers of 238 firms and presented the questionnaires regarding NPD performance. In addition, we collected archival data of NPD performance (year 2017). This led to 236 responses in this round of data collection because archival data of two of the original firms could not be obtained.

In mid-2019, we contacted chief information officers and marketing managers of 236 firms and presented the questionnaires regarding BDA and TMA, respectively, to the respondents on-site. This led to 225 responses in this round of data collection, because 11 of the original firms could not be reached.

In mid-2020, our original plan was to collect data on-site. However, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, questionnaires were sent by email. As a result, we emailed CEOs of the same 225 firms to rate exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies. The survey data of 17 of the original firms could not be located, leading to 208 responses in this round of data collection.

In mid-2021, we contacted NPD managers of 208 firms and presented the questionnaires regarding NPD performance. We also obtained archival data of NPD performance (year 2020) and control variables. After we eliminated firms for which the questionnaires were inappropriate and archival data were incomplete, the final sample was composed of 201 firms with paired questionnaires.

The sector of 201 firms included the following: financial and insurance (21.4%), retail and logistics (20.9%), telecommunications and electronics (19.9%), pharmaceutical and healthcare (18.4%), media and entertainment (17.4%), and others (2%). The mean annual sales revenue was US \$278.9 million. Most of these firms have overseas operations, which demonstrates the cross-national nature of their business. The average number of employees was 1,168. The mean industry experience was 15.6 years, whereas the mean NPD experience was 13.8 years. The mean of applying BDA in the NPD process was 9.6 years, whereas the mean of applying TMA in the NPD process was 12.4 years.

We compared participating and non-participating firms in firm size, firm age, R&D intensity, prior innovation performance, and years of using BDA and TMA. The results show no differences between the participating and non-participating firms in terms of firm size $( X ^ { 2 } { = } 1 . 3 6 , \ p { = } 0 . 5 1 )$ ), firm age $( X ^ { 2 } { = } 1 . 4 9$ $\scriptstyle { p = 0 . 4 3 } )$ ), R&D intensity $( X ^ { 2 } = 1 . 6 5 , ~ p { = } 0 . 3 1 )$ ), prior innovation performance $( X ^ { 2 } = 1 . 2 7 , p { = } 0 . 6 5 )$ , and years of using BDA $( X ^ { 2 } { = } 1 . 3 4$ $\scriptstyle { p = 0 . 5 3 } )$ ) and TMA $( X ^ { 2 } { = } 1 . 3 9$ $\scriptstyle { p = 0 . 4 8 } )$ , indicating that the sample is representative of the population. In addition, all those sampled are large enterprises (from top 1,000) and most of them (95.5%) are multinational enterprises accounting for more than 80 percent of Taiwan’s exports. Further, using ANOVA analysis to determine whether differences exist among the five industries being studied in terms of all the measurement items, the results show no statistically significant difference $( p > 0 . 0 5 )$ from the industry distribution. This finding suggests that our research gains the generalizability of the sampling by carrying the explanatory power of being across different industries, implying the applicability of our results to other industries not included in this study. Overall, the results of this study can be applied to an international context and to various industries, responding to the call for greater generalization in IS research (Tsang and Williams, 2012).

To assess respondents’ knowledge, competence, and familiarity with the research topics, we included three questions at the end of the questionnaire on a seven-point scale (1-very low, 7-very high). We also verified the qualifications of respondents by asking them to indicate their tenures with their firms and in their positions. The average knowledge rating was 6.18 and the average working experience related to BDA and TMA was 10.7 years. These results suggest that the respondents were experienced and knowledgeable about the topics under study, providing confidence in the quality of the data.

## 4.2 Measures

All constructs were measured using multi-items with a seven-point scale. For items adapted from the literature and written in English, we first used a double-translation method (McGorry, 2000) to translate them into Chinese. This process included: (1) initially translating the items into Chinese; (2) two other scholars then translating the Chinese version back into English to ensure the accuracy of translation (Douglas and Craig, 2007); and (3) this translation being checked by a third scholar to ensure conceptual equivalence. Second, to ensure the respondents were able to correctly interpret questions, we presented the items to the other three scholars and 29 senior managers and made a few minor changes in line with their feedback (such as replacing “my business unit” and “we” in the items of the measure “Big data analytics” with “our firm” as well as replacing “the firm as one that” in the items of both measures “Exploratory and Exploitative innovation strategies” with “our firm”). As a result, the questionnaire was prepared with satisfactory content and face validity. The measurement items appear in Table 2.

Big Data Analytics. To measure BDA, we adapted the 12 items from Johnson et al. (2017), which assess how a firm deployed big data analytics in terms of big data volume, big data variety, and big data velocity to create novel insights over the past three years. Specifically, BDA possesses a number of characteristics. The six characteristics noted by Gandomi and Haider (2015) are volume, velocity, variety, veracity, variability, and value. Thereafter, Shafer (2018) added as many as 42 dimensions, all of which show what big data is about. However, not all of these characteristics are of equal importance. Three of them, volume, velocity, and variety (3Vs), have been widely regarded as the most crucial (Ghasemaghaei and Calic, 2019).

Data volume is about amounts of data growing at an exponential rate year after year. Data velocity refers to the speed at which data is generated and analyzed. Data variety is all about variety of data types.

Johnson et al. (2017) have been meticulous in developing items for measuring each of the above 3Vs’ characteristics. The 12 items that they developed not only pass the statistical validity and reliability tests but also pass the face validity test because they were scrutinized and accepted by experts in the IT field to be able to represent the 3Vs, respectively. Key words, such as “large quantities” for volume, “speed” for velocity, and “different sources” for variety, of all these 12 items were drawn from academic papers that discussed element characteristics of the 3Vs.

Since Johnson et al.’s (2017) development and validation of the 12 items for the 3Vs, empirical studies on BDA (e.g., Olabode et al., 2022; De Luca et al., 2021; Ghasemaghaei and Calic, 2019) needed to measure the 3Vs in their study adopt Johnson et al.’s (2017) items. As of the date of writing this paper, we have not found any papers criticizing Johnson et al.’s (2017) work.

Traditional Marketing Analytics. Based on our review of relevant literature, we find a study by Germann et al. (2013), which developed new items to measure TMA. These items are all very sensible and indicate how involved a firm is in deploying TMA. However, TMA deployment is not exactly the same as TMA, with the latter about how well-equipped and well prepared a firm is in its TMA. For a firm to be well-equipped with its TMA so that it is ready to be deployed requires data analytics techniques, tools, and skills, as well as classification/arrangement, updating/deleting, presentation/delivery, and synthesizing/condensing activities (Iacobucci et al., 2019; Avison et al., 2008). As such, because the ideal measure of TMA was not specifically found in the literature, we could not draw on existing measures. We followed IS studies on developing construct procedures to develop new items (MacKenzie et al., 2011; Lewis et al., 2005).

First, under the theoretical lens (Iacobucci et al., 2019; Avison et al., 2008), we designed the TMA measure carefully to capture specific features of marketing analytics, including categorization of research methods, data analytics techniques, tools, and skills, as well as four activities as mentioned previously.

Second, following the framework proposed by MacKenzie et al. (2011) and Churchill (1979), we created an initial pool of items based on a thorough review of the literature (e.g., Cao et al., 2019; Liu and Burns, 2018; Wedel and Kannan, 2016; Xu et al., 2016; Germann et al., 2013), as well as in-depth interviews with a sample of 24 senior marketing managers who had experience in developing new products through TMA.

Third, we continued to the content validation (Haynes et al., 1995). After carefully examining the transcripts we and two scholars manually and electronically (NVivo 10) converted interviewees’ open-ended responses into items, as suggested by Gioia et al. (2013). This resulted in an initial set of 32 items.

Fourth, relying on the measurement development methodology (MacKenzie et al., 2011; Lewis et al., 2005; Churchill, 1979), we performed two tests to ensure the measure was reliable and valid. The first test was a pre-test conducted with another sample of 31 senior marketing managers. This process resulted in significant modification and refinement of some of the items, leading to a reduction from 32 to 19 items.

Finally, the second test was the pilot test that was conducted with another sample of 65 senior marketing managers. Based on confirmation with statistical procedures (EFA, Bearden et al., 2001), five items were deleted, whereas the remaining items were adjusted in terms of wording and formatting. After the measure was purified, we conducted another sample of 117 senior marketing managers to check its psychometric properties (MacKenzie et al., 2011). The results of the psychometric properties of the measure, based on the CFA, show convergent and discriminant validity (Gerbing and Anderson, 1988). As a result, the final measure of TMA contains 20 items that reflect how a firm collects, manages, and analyzes marketing-related data via conventional approaches to create novel insights over the past three years.

It is worth noting that all of these stages involve communicating with or getting responses from senior managers and scholars. To ensure we all were working on this measure development work, we presented to them the meaning of TMA as defined by Xu et al. (2015) and the measure of TMA developed by Germann et al. (2013). The results show that our items can measure TMA more accurately and comprehensively, in which all the items developed, retained, deleted, or revised during the process are based on a proper measurement development methodology (MacKenzie et al., 2011; Lewis et al., 2005; Churchill, 1979).

Exploratory and Exploitative Innovation Strategies. We measured both exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies with 12 items we adapted from Lubatkin et al. (2006). Exploratory innovation strategy reflects a firm’s capability to challenge its existing business practices through discovery, experimentation, and search, whereas exploitative innovation strategy reflects a firm’s capability to improve existing knowledge and skills, as well as extend and enhance its existing business practices. Both measures thus capture CEOs’ thoughts and actions in implementing exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies, respectively, over the past three years.

New Product Development Performance. To avoid common method variance, we measure NPD performance by using two sources of data. First, we measure NPD performance with five items that we adapted from Schleimer and Faems (2016), which measure the characteristics of the developed product itself and the success of the developed product in the market compared with major competitors over the last three years. Second, following Kostopoulos et al. (2011), we measure NPD performance over the past three years using archival data as (annual sales<sub>y2020</sub> – annual sales<sub>y2017</sub>)/annual sales<sub>y2017</sub> × 100. This method to measure NPD performance has been used commonly in NPD studies (e.g., Berchicci, 2013; Laursen and Salter, 2006).

Control Variables. We controlled for firm size, firm age, R&D intensity, and prior innovation performance to reflect the sample firm’s resources. First, firm size was measured on a logarithmic scale using the number of employees, whereas firm age was measured on a logarithmic scale using the number of years the firm had been established. This method is used because both firm size and firm age can influence NPD performance (Petruzzelli et al., 2018). Second, we measured R&D intensity as the ratio of R&D expenditures to sales over the past three years (Marano and Kostova, 2016). R&D intensity is included because R&D is the most important investment in the NPD process (Hottenrott and Lopes‐ Bento, 2016). Third, we measured prior innovation performance as the log of the firm’s total patent output over the past three years (Nambisan, 2013) because a firm with a high level of prior innovation performance is more likely to develop new products. Fourth, because of the pace of technological progress in industries, we controlled environmental uncertainty by measuring instability in market sales based on archival data over the past three years (Sutcliffe, 1994). Finally, we controlled the effects of industries using dummy variables.

## 5.2 Hypotheses testing

To test hypotheses, comparative analysis was conducted by using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and chi-square difference tests, followed by a robustness analysis using moderated multiple regression and Chow tests. Specifically, following Hair et al. (2019), we used PLS-SEM based on 5,000 bootstrap samples. The $\mathbf { R } ^ { 2 }$ values for both models are 0.205 and 0.392 for the survey data of NPD performance, as well as 0.173 and 0.337 for the archival data of NPD performance. ## 6. Discussion

Using the gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970), the SITA perspective (Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011), the innovation ambidexterity theory (Gregory et al., 2015; Benner and Tushman, 2003), and the KBV (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996) as the theories underpinning all the hypothesized relationships noted in Figure 1, this study examined the relative contributions of BDA versus TMA to NPD performance and considered exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies to examine how they differentially affect the NPD performance of BDA and TMA. To triangulate the findings, this study collected both survey and archival data through a four-wave data collection procedure (mid-2018, mid-2019, mid-2020, and mid-2021) from three different respondents (NPD managers, chief information officers, and CEOs) in 201 firms. The empirical analysis reveals that the effect of BDA on NPD performance is significantly greater than that of TMA over time. Importantly, BDA coupled with exploratory innovation strategy would contribute significantly to NPD performance, whereas TMA coupled with exploitative innovation strategy would also produce the same positive effect. The results support our theory that, compared with TMA, BDA contributes better innovation values to firms, whereas such values are dependent on innovation strategy of the firms.

In this study, we focus on the interaction between data analytics strategy and innovation strategy. There are two major types of data analytics strategy, BDA and TMA, whereas there are also two types of innovation strategy, exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies. It is therefore a 2\*2 matrix leading to four possible couplings, BDA and exploratory, BDA and exploitative, TMA and exploratory, and TMA and exploitative, each of which may pose a specific impact on NPD performance. These strategies, such as data analytics strategy and innovation strategy in this study, are often entangled with each other and it is always likely that the different decisions on the different types of strategies made interact with each other, leading to changes in firm performance or a specific type of performance such as NPD performance in this paper. Without proper research efforts to disentangle the interactions occurring between the different types of strategies, firms are left in the dark as to the effect of these interactions which can go either way (i.e., contributing positively or adversely to performance).

All the variables in our conceptual model originate from either the IS or innovation research field. Examining the relative values of BDA versus TMA is rightly rooted in the IS field. On the other hand, innovation strategy and NPD performance are clearly based on the innovation field. Therefore, this study is interdisciplinary but arguably is positioned more heavily toward the IS field than the innovation field because BDA and TMA are the main independent variables, whereas exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies serve only to moderate the effect of these independent variables on NPD performance as dependent variable. In addition to the contributions made to theory and managers illustrated next, this study also contributes to a greater strategic clarity for firms that have both data analytics strategy and innovation strategy in sight as part of their overall corporate strategy, helping them to make better decisions which can improve their performance.

## 6.1 Theoretical implications

This study enhances academic understanding of the relative contributions of BDA versus TMA to NPD performance and thus contributes to both IS and innovation literature in two major ways. First, prior studies suggest that either BDA or TMA can help firms improve their NPD performance (Sun and Liu, 2023; Cao et al., 2022; Hajli et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2016), but an understanding of the relative contributions of BDA versus TMA to NPD performance is absent in the extant literature (see Table 1). We do not know the reason scholars have not compared the two, but we conjecture that BDA has been hyped to the extent that TMA might have been completely overshadowed. Another reason is that so far there has been no validated measurement of TMA that scholars can adopt for conducting BDA versus TMA studies. Drawing on gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970), our findings indicate that, over time, BDA has a greater effect on NPD performance than TMA, addressing an important gap in IS research (Cao et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2016). Although a recent relevant study has highlighted the profound implications of gestalt insight learning theory in the use of big data (Ghasemaghaei and Calic, 2019), it has focused mainly on the effectiveness of big data, paying scant attention to the potential divergence between BDA and TMA. More specifically, grounded in gestalt insight learning theory and organizational learning theory, Ghasemaghaei and Calic (2019) collect data from 280 managers to investigate the impact of each big data characteristic on firm innovation competency, mediated through data-driven insight generation. Although Ghasemaghaei and Calic (2019) use the type of problem-solving processes described by the gestalt insight learning theory, they pay scant attention to the learning that results from these processes. Our result reflects the underlying differences between BDA and TMA by reinforcing gestalt insight learning theory (Ash et al., 2012; Herschel and Jones, 2005) that associative insight learning occurs when firms using BDA and TMA discover new relationships within a huge amount of data and their prior knowledge as a result of reasoning processes. Furthermore, this theoretical implication advances gestalt insight learning theory by theorizing how the problems we face of BDA versus TMA and the solutions to these problems are about a gestalt understanding of the problems’ elements and their relationships (Ash et al., 2012; Herschel and Jones, 2005).

What is more, this finding provides specific insights into the IS literature in three aspects. First, compared with TMA, the use of BDA can predict future events more accurately, such as innovation development decision-making, allowing firms to improve their NPD performance. Second, the use of BDA can facilitate the deployment of machine learning on data probing, spot better novel insights, and thus produce greater NPD performance. Third, the use of BDA enables firms to make much quicker management decisions and to provide better analytical solutions for innovation development by using large amounts of real-time data. These three insights hold specifically true that BDA enhances NPD performance to a greater extent than TMA, which sheds light on current debates regarding the effectiveness of BDA versus TMA (Cao et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2016). Thus, this study is the first to provide empirical evidence to confirm the differential effects of BDA versus TMA on firm innovation, which enables us to uncover novel insights on the emerging role of BDA.

Second, this paper makes an important contribution to the IS literature by illustrating how best to apply the SITA perspective. The SITA perspective emphasizes alignment of IT strategy and business strategy, but business strategy is a very broad concept (Chatterjee et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2015; Coltman et al., 2015). This study finds that if we focus on NPD performance, innovation strategy is likely to be the key business strategy to be manipulated when we apply the SITA perspective. In particular, innovation strategy plays an important role in IS success of organizations (Syed et al., 2021; Park et al., 2020; Božič and Dimovski, 2019). Thus, this study enriches our understanding by showing that certain innovation strategies can leverage BDA and TMA to achieve NPD success. Specifically, previous IS studies focus on the potential benefits of IT applications aligning with various dimensions (such as digital technology, IS strategy, and capabilities) on firm performance outcomes and generate mixed results (e.g., Hussain and Papastathopoulos, 2022; Sabherwal et al., 2019; Devece et al., 2017). Extending prior research, this study adopts that IS literature is related to the innovation ambidexterity theory (Werder and Heckmann, 2019) to explicate the differential effects of both exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies and their corresponding performance implications, which helps explain the complex role of BDA versus TMA during the innovation process (Cao et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2016). Particularly, this study discloses that BDA is better coupled with exploratory innovation strategy than with exploitative innovation strategy for achieving better NPD performance. In addition, TMA is better coupled with exploitative innovation strategy than with exploratory innovation strategy for the same performance effect. These findings confirm that, although firms can invest substantially in BDA, this does not guarantee that they can obtain superior NPD performance if the use of BDA does not consider innovation strategy (Müller et al., 2021). These findings also underline that the effective management of BDA and TMA requires firms to enact certain innovation strategies simultaneously (Cao et al., 2020; Enkel et al., 2017).

What is more, our findings enrich the KBV (Pereira and Bamel, 2021; Grant, 1996) with the concept of knowledge breadth, which serves as the underlying theory for exploratory innovation strategy, whereas the concept of knowledge depth serves as the underlying theory for exploitative innovation strategy. In this way, this study contributes to the line of research by combining two disparate literatures, IS and KBV, to yield additional novel synthesized insights. More precisely, this study offers a novel perspective on the roles of knowledge breadth and knowledge depth to highlight that to realize their full potential of BDA and TMA, the use of advanced IT (BDA) should be properly aligned with the certain external knowledge search strategy (knowledge breadth). On the other hand, the use of traditional IT (TMA) should be properly aligned with the certain internal knowledge reuse strategy (knowledge depth). We believe that our theorizing and findings answer research calls (Božič and Dimovski, 2019) to clarify the roles of exploratory and exploitative innovation strategies in leveraging firms’ ITbased resources (e.g., BDA and TMA) on innovation outcomes. In short, our findings provide fresh insights by going beyond a simplified application of the exploratory/exploitative innovation strategy to the IS context, and instead focus on the salient features of knowledge breadth and knowledge depth and highlight their roles in aligning with BDA/TMA to affect NPD performance.

## 6.2 Managerial implications

This study has critical implications for managers. First, our findings suggest that managers should adopt and use BDA for firm innovation because, when compared with TMA, BDA has a stronger positive effect on NPD performance. Although a significant number of resources need to be put into action, managers can make a plan for BDA adoption. In addition, although it may take a long time or additional financial resources for managers to adopt BDA, once in use, BDA can become a valuable asset and improve new product success. Most important, although the use of TMA can be learned quite easily and quickly, managers must have a clear picture of BDA that can bring a new type of data operation and new experience to firms more quickly and with more variety of data. For example, global firms, such as Airbnb, Apple, Google, and LiveNation, use BDA to stay ahead of competitors. In addition, most major manufacturers, such as Toyota and TSMC (a Taiwanese-based semiconductor firm), have been using BDA to improve existing product quality and customize their new product offerings. Furthermore, start-ups and small- to mid-sized enterprises, such as AiRPX (France-based) and DigitalRoute (Sweden-based), compete with established firms by using accessible data to build their own business intelligence. Overall, managers should embrace BDA as firm competencies that will help firms obtain more useful information to generate new knowledge for fueling product/service innovation.

Second, the positive interaction effects of BDA/TMA with exploratory/exploitative innovation strategy on NPD performance have useful implications for managers. There are four possible interaction effects in this study: (1) BDA with exploratory innovation strategy; (2) BDA with exploitative innovation strategy; (3) TMA with exploitative innovation strategy; and (4) TMA with exploratory innovation strategy. Our results show that the first three interactions have been found to be significant and positive, whereas only the TMA with exploratory innovation strategy is insignificant. These findings have huge impacts for managers, when aligning IT strategy with innovation strategy to improve firm innovation. Specifically, both BDA and TMA possess techniques to explore and exploit data. BDA uses computer-intensive data mining algorithms, whereas TMA uses sophisticated software and techniques, such as SAS, SPSS, and multivariate statistical analysis, to try to make the best use of the data. Through this data-digging practice, it is no wonder that both BDA and TMA, when coupled with exploitative innovation strategy, can produce added positive effects on NPD performance compared to the scenario where neither BDA nor TMA is used. However, when explorator rather than exploitative innovation strategy comes into play, added positive effects on NPD performance can be achieved only when coupled with BDA. This is mainly because the result of this exploitation/data digging/data mining can give insights that help firms explore new markets (market dimension of exploratory innovation strategy). In contrast, TMA will not affect NPD performance if exploratory innovation strategy takes the helm.

Why does this happen? We believe the newly found serendipity theory, referring to “the fundamental yet neglected idea that science, research, and innovation in one area often leads to unexpected advances in other areas” (Mastrogiorgio et al., 2019, p. 13624), is at play here. As predicted by gestalt insight learning theory (Koffka, 2013; Köhler, 1970), BDA is much more able than TMA to identify connections between two seemingly unrelated variables as well as to identify insights that would be very difficult if not impossible to obtain via TMA. Therefore, BDA is more able to produce serendipity than TMA. Exploratory innovation strategy is all about new markets and/or new technologies. It is not about any improvement or extension of the status quo. The ability to identify serendipity in BDA is the main reason it, not TMA, can add to NPD performance in exploratory innovation strategy scenarios. Thus, managers should not only grasp the benefit of innovation ambidexterity, when coupled with IS strategies, but also understand that each dimension of innovation ambidexterity has a differen impact when specifically applied to BDA and TMA. Most important, environment-related conditions (e.g., environmental uncertainty or organizational inertia) as popularly chosen by scholars (e.g., Mikalef et al., 2020; Mikalef et al., 2019) in the current IS literature are something that are not controllable by managers. On the other hand, innovation strategy is something within the control of managers and, therefore, results of this study going for controllable variables instead of uncontrollable variables will be more useful and applicable to managers.

Third, our results show that TMA with exploratory innovation strategy is insignificant to NPD performance. Therefore, when considering employee training for enhancing NPD performance, managers should not have their attention solely on BDA skills training but should also continually develop and update employees’ TMA skills. To further investigate the result of the relative effect of “BDA + TMA + exploratory innovation strategy” versus “BDA + exploratory innovation strategy” on NPD performance, we conducted additional analyses. The results show that TMA combined with BDA produces better NPD performance than using BDA alone in exploratory innovation strategy. This additional finding is important for managers because there are times when managers should deploy exploratory innovation strategy, such as when the technology has been fully used or when the market has been full exploited. If this happens, then TMA will be able to demonstrate its benefits to the firm involved. Further, the implication of this to a firm’s innovation strategy on developing its resources is that, in spite of all the hype of BDA, managers should continue to invest in both human resources and other resources (such as software) for the use of TMA so that TMA can be conducted in the best possible manner when it is called upon. Overall, in spite of the hype of BDA and all the talk of its superiority to TMA, managers should not overlook the value that TMA can bring.

## 6.3 Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations, which provide opportunities for future research. First, even for the variables used in this study, there is scope to treat these variables in a more tailor-made manner. For example, exploratory innovation strategy can be about the technology alone, or the market alone, or both technology and market (Duodu and Rowlinson, 2019). Fundamentally, BDA is about getting as much data from our users/customers, from as many channels as possible, and making as much use as possible of the data we have. It is therefore still data produced by our users/customers and thus it is unlikely that we can use what we learn from BDA to develop new technology for innovation. It is far more likely that BDA, with its wonderful ability to identify relationships that could not be found in the past, can signal to us the new market that we miss or that simply does not exist for now but is ready for us to create. As such, the top limit of what BDA can achieve stops at what the data can provide and the data from BDA are still just data from past and current sales/customers. Therefore, this is a limitation as this study talks about exploratory innovation strategy, in general, when actually what is exploratory in this paper is mostly the market or marketing, and much less the technology. As our additional analyses imply, managers could combine insights from BDA and TMA along with exploratory innovation strategy to build a complete picture of innovation development. Simply put, BDA shows managers the “What,” whereas TMA answers “Why.” Therefore, it seems better for future studies to supplement BDA with TMA information to flesh out the findings and to extend theoretical perspectives.

Second, the focus of this study was on NPD performance. Future studies could analyze firm innovation more thoroughly by further classifying it as radical versus incremental NPD performance if data are available. Third, other big data applications, such as big data management, big data security, and big data capabilities, may also influence NPD performance and additional research could test their effects on NPD success. In addition, BDA and TMA are complex phenomena. Although we examine important enabling factors, other effects may

influence their effectiveness as a path to new product success.
