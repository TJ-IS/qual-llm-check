# DSS Applications Development Research: Leading Institutions and Most Frequent Contributors (1971–April 1988)

Hyun B. EOM

Department of Computer Information Systems, Middle Tennessee State University, Murfreesboro, TN 37132, USA

Sang M. LEE

Management Department, University of Nebraska, Lincoln, NE 68588, USA

This research identifies leading institutions and the most frequent contributors in the field of decision support systems [DSS] development research. Two hundred and three specific DSS were extracted from over 1100 English-language journals published world-wide in the last 18 years (1971–April 1988). From the bibliography, this survey identified 37 leading DSS development publishing institutions from the U.S.A., Canada, and European countries as well as 35 frequent contributors from academic institutions all over the world.

Keywords: DSS development research, Leading research institutions, Frequent contributors.

## 1. Introduction

Since M.S. Scott-Morton [9] first articulated the concept of “decision support systems” [DSS] in the early 1970s, DSS has emerged as one of the most important areas in information systems. Over the last two decades, the number of DSS applications published increased yearly. Our previous survey results indicated that DSS applications development has continuously accelerated since 1971. We expect this trend to continue several more years, perhaps into the 1990s.

This paper presents the survey results on the leading academic and non-academic institutions actively publishing DSS development research and the most frequent contributors in the field. A few years ago, Vogel and Wetherbe [15] published a profile of leading U.S. journals and universities that were publishing management information systems (MIS) research. ## 2. The Data and Methodology

As a first step in identifying leading institutions and frequent contributors, we compiled a comprehensive bibliography of DSS applications. Using that bibliography, we created a second database file containing authors and their affiliations. Approximately three years were devoted to compiling the comprehensive bibliography of 203 specific applications.

To compile the initial list of DSS applications, we searched several DSS textbooks and related reference books [1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,10,11,12,13,14] as well as the major journals that had published a substantial number of DSS applications. We then searched the Business Periodical Index from January 1971 to July 1988. As of July 1988, the index includes approximately 330 English language periodicals. Unfortunately, the Index does not include several MIS journals that publish large numbers of DSS application articles. We also used the Compact Disk - Read Only Memory (CD-ROM) version of ABI/INFORM database by UMI/data courier to search articles from the most recent 5 years (May 1983–April 1988). As of August 1988, ABI/INFORM contains 150-word abstract of articles from nearly 800 business and management journals worldwide. But ABI/INFORM excludes several relatively important journals such as Omega: The International Journal of Management Science, IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, Journal of Management Information Systems, and Communications of the ACM.

Our last step was to manually examine important journals in the initial database compiled by searching major DSS textbooks and related reference books published in the last 18 years. We also searched references used in each article. An essential part of search activity is manually examining the articles retrieved from the commercial databases to determine whether they meet the several criteria for specific DSS.

## Selection Criteria

From various definitions $[1,2,3,8,10]$ , a DSS can be described as a computer-based interactive system that

\* supports decision makers rather than replaces them (or automates decision making);

\* utilizes data and models;

\* solves problems with a varying degree of structure - nonstructured (unstructured or ill-structured) tasks [3], semistructured tasks [2,8], semi-structured and unstructured tasks [10].

\* focuses on effectiveness rather than efficiency in decision processes (facilitating decision processes);

We used the following minimum selection criteria:

1. Description of a (set of) Semistructured or Unstructured Decision;

2. Description of a Human-Computer Interface and the computer-based support for decision makers' intuition and judgement;

3. Description of a language system, a knowledge system, and a problem-processing system [3] or Data-Dialogue-Model systems [10].

By these criteria, we excluded any articles with DSS application-like titles, but with little discussion on Decision, Support, and Systems. Concomitantly, a large number of articles were included in the survey even though the title did not include the term DSS.

## 4. Frequent Contributors

Three hundred ninety-two persons have authored or coauthored 203 DSS applications articles over the past 18 years. Figure 2 shows that 77 percent of the authors (300) are affiliated with institutions in the U.S.; the remaining 23 percent (92) are from 21 other countries. Approximately 60 percent of the authors (235) are affiliated with 113 academic institutions (76 U.S. institutions and 37 non-U.S. institutions) and 40 percent (157) with 98 non-academic institutions (73 U.S. institutions and 25 non-U.S. institutions), primarily business-organizations, as shown in figure 3.

Only 35 authors, 15 percent of 235 authors in academic institutions, appear more than once. We have not attempted to rank either institutions or individual contributors. Instead, table 2 categorizes all authors into three groups: Group I (4 appearances–4 authors); Group II (3 appearances–10 authors); Group III (2 appearances–21 authors). Group I consists of Andrew B. Whinston and D. Klingman at the University of Texas - Austin, S. Belardo at SUNY - Albany, and W.A. Wallace at RPI. Group II authors are J.V. Hansen, C.W. Holsapple, R. Jaikumar, M. Jarke, S.K. Kassicieh, S.M. Lee, J.D.C. Little, N. Phillips, M.G. Singh, and R.H. Sprague, Jr. Twenty nine of the 35 frequent contributors are currently affiliated with academic institutions in the United States, 3 with academic institutions in the United Kingdom, 1 with an institution in the Netherlands, and 2 in France.

On the basis of our English-language journal database, we can draw several conclusions.

(1) Researchers in the United States play a predominant role in developing and publishing DSS applications and in shaping the direction of DSS applications research.
