# Leading U.S. universities and most influential contributors in decision support systems research (1971–1989)

Sean B. Eom

Southeast Missouri State University, Cape Girardeau, MO, USA

Sang M. Lee

University of Nebraska, Lincoln, NE 68588, USA

This paper identifies the most influential contributors in the DSS area in the U.S., examines their contributions, and reviews the institutional publishing records at the leading U.S. universities, which are actively publishing DSS research. To measure the influence/contributions of leading universities and contributors, we used the bibliographic citations of the publications on the specific DSS applications. The critical assumption of this study is that “bibliographic citations are an acceptable surrogate for the actual influence of various information sources.” (M.J. Culnan, Management Science 32, 2, feb 1986, 156–172) This paper identifies thirty-two leading U.S. universities with eighty-one of their affiliated members and twenty three most influential researchers. Among the leading U.S. universities identified, two universities are truly outstanding: The University of Texas-Austin and MIT. Regardless of any types of yardsticks which may be applied to measure their contributions, these two universities may be recognized as centers of excellent DSS research in the U.S.A. in terms of the number of research publications, the number of total citation frequencies, and the number of active researchers in the DSS related areas.

Keywords: Decision support systems, Leading research institutions, Influential contributors, Citation analysis

## 2. The research model

Study of decision support systems aims at achieving an important goal, improving organizational effectiveness and efficiency through the specific DSS applications development and implementation. The development and implementation of DSS applications, in turn, are influenced both by the research concerning DSS theory and by contributing disciplines such as management science (MS)/operations research (OR), computer science, organizational sciences, psychology, etc.

Application development, DSS theory, and contributing disciplines. The dark-colored arrows in fig. 1 show that the development and implementation of specific DSS are influenced by the specific DSS application development, DSS theory, and contributing disciplines. Research in DSS areas needs an interdisciplinary approach by the researchers in each contributing discipline and other DSS theorists. The DSS theory in Fig. 1 includes all DSS related research except two areas—specific DSS application development, and the study of the contributing disciplines. Thus, the research in the DSS theory includes the study of each subsystem [1] of DSS (language, knowledge, problem-processing, and interface), planning and evaluation of DSS, integration of DSS into the organization, etc. to name a few. All three of these DSS research areas are continuously interacting with each other. Some researchers may conduct research only in one area, while others are engaging two or more subareas such as developing management science models (contributing disciplines) and developing decision support systems. Therefore, the results of this study include the contributions from each of three subareas of DSS research.

The other critical question for this study was how to measure the influence/contributions of each subarea to the decision support system area. One feasible approach could have been the collection and interpretation of publications based on personal judgment. For example, personal judgment could have been exercised when collecting DSS related publications in terms of journal and article selections, etc. A greater use of personal judgment in this process can be a source of heavy distortion and lead to low reliability of the research results. Therefore, we have minimized the use of our personal judgment in the data collection and interpretation process by using the bibliographic citations of the publications on the specific DSS applications. The critical assumption of this study is that “bibliographic citations are an acceptable surrogate for the actual influence of various information sources.” [2]

## 4. Results

## 4.1. Leading institutions

Fig. 2 lists 32 U.S. universities, publications of which have been cited more than 15 frequencies during the specified period (1971–1989). The decision to set the cutoff point at 15 was arbitrary. Table 1 presents more detailed information about the total number of publications, total number of citation frequencies, and a list of faculty with publications that have more than 5 citation frequencies, along with their number of publications included in our database file. The first numerical column shows the number of the total publications by the faculty at each university. The second numerical column indicates the total number of citation frequencies of the publications at each university. For example, 100 DSS research related publications have been authored or coauthored over the last 19 years by faculty members at the University of Texas (UT) at Austin. Those one hundred publications are cited 179 times by the articles on specific DSS applications. The third and fourth numerical columns represent the total number of publications and their citation frequencies of each faculty member at each university selected. The University of Texas-Austin has 9 faculty members who are active in DSS related research: Professors Charnes, Cooper, Dyer, Huber, Klingman, Lasdon, Mahajan,

Phillips, and Whinston. This table excludes those who published articles that have less than 5 citation frequencies. Undoubtedly, the University of Texas at Austin has published the most DSS related research and has the largest citation frequency in the U.S., followed by Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), Purdue University, the University of Hawaii, and the University of Pennsylvania. ## 5. Conclusion

As the DSS area matures, it is necessary for the DSS area to build a cumulative tradition to become a coherent and substantive field. This paper has contributed to the building of a part of the cumulative tradition by identifying the most influential contributors in the DSS area and examining the institutional publishing records at the leading U.S. universities actively publishing DSS research.

Among the thirty-two leading U.S. universities we identified, two universities are truly outstanding: The University of Texas-Austin and MIT. Regardless of the types of yardsticks which may be applied to measure the contribution of each university to the DSS area, these two universities may be recognized as centers of excellent DSS research in the U.S.A. in terms of the number of research publications, the number of total citation frequencies, and the number of active researchers in the DSS related areas. However, institutional standings are greatly influenced by faculty mobility. For example, the relative standing of NYU and Purdue University could have been much different with the inclusion of M. Jarke and A.B. Whinston.

We hope this research will help categorize the contributions of DSS scholars and representative institutions as a subset of diverse information systems study and allow students and researchers in the field to identify those institutions most active in DSS research.
