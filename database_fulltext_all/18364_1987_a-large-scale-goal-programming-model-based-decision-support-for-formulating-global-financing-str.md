A large scale goal programming (GP) model-based decision support system (DSS) is developed to support the formulation of a global financing strategy for a multinational corporation (MNC). The GP model-based DSS provides a major technological breakthrough by presenting a solution to the critical issues in global financial planning that is an improvement over existing approaches. The DSS allows the financial managers of an MNC to choose an appropriate global financing strategy to satisfy multiple and often conflicting management goals and provides a tool for analyzing the trade-offs among financing costs foreign exchange risks political risks capital structure goal and agency costs.

Keywords: Global Financing Multinational Corporation Decision Support Systems Multiple Criteria Decision Making Large-Scale Goal Programming

## 2 Issues in Formulating Global Financing Strategy for the Multinational Corporation

Goal conflicts in organizations have existed for a long time. It was not until the 1960s, however, that academics began to question the validity of the single goal approach, as exemplified by the stockholder wealth maximization model. A growing number of studies [1,6,7,10,15 16,28] have attempted to recognize organizational impediments to stockholder wealth maximization and the inherent goal conflicts between stockholder, manager, and creditor, and organizational goals for survival. Of these, the “satisficing” approach [4] which is based on the concept of bounded rationality, and agency theory [15,16,28] have received the most attention. The satisficing approach attempts to attain satisfactory levels of achievement for several objectives. Organizational theorists such as Cyert and March [4] state that each organizational group (i.e., stockholders, creditors and managers) is forced to “satisfice” its goals in order for the firm to remain viable

When formulating a worldwide financing strategy, financial managers of an MNCs choose an appropriate financial structure (debt/equity mix) for the firm. Furthermore, as Shapiro [30] points out, “the eventual funding strategy selected must reconcile a variety of potentially conflicting objectives such as minimizing expected financing cost, reducing economic exposure providing protection from currency controls and other forms of political risk, and ensuring availability of funds in times of tight credit”

## 21 The Firm's Financial Structure

A majority of today's financial theorists agree that there is an optima financial structure for a firm [8,29] that minimizes its cost of capital for a given level of business risk considering taxes, bankruptcy costs, and the cost of the agency relationships between stockholders (as principal) and management (as agents). The optimal capital structure is not a single low point but a broad flat area over a wide range of debt/equity ratios on the U-shaped weighted average cost of capital curve

There seems to be a consensus among a majority of finance theorists that a primary concern for MNCs should be the consolidated firm's financial structure, because creditors ultimately look to the profitability and cash flow of the consolidated firm for satisfaction of their claim [8,20,29] After selecting a optimal financial structure for the consolidated world-wide firm, the financial manager should establish the financial structure each foreign subsidiary. But there is no consensus among theorists as to whether subsidiary financial structure should

a conform to the debt ratio norms established in each operating country [32], or

b vary, so as to take a comparative advantage (over local firms) of imperfections in national capital markets [20,30], or

c localize the foreign affiliates' financial structure if debt is available to a foreign affiliate at an equal cost to that which could be raised elsewhere [8]

## 22 Conflicting Financing Objectives

Several objectives must be simultaneously satisfied in selecting sources of financing and in determining the financial structures of the consolidated firm and each unit of foreign affiliates. The issues in global financing decisions stem from potentially conflicting objectives. Each of these objectives is discussed below

## 221 Maintaining the Average Weighted Cost of Capital for a Consolidated Firm

The objective of single objective financing decision framework (e.g. ness [24]), is to minimize the sum of the cost of financing of a consolidated firm Multi-objective decision making (MODM) aims at limiting the sum of the consolidated firm's expected cost of financing (after-tax foreign exchange risk adjusted) not to exceed the TCF\$ The TCF\$ can be obtained by the following computation

TCFS = Total Amounts of Funds required (\$)\* AWCC,

where AWCC is the consolidated firm's average weighted cost of capital over time (used as the basis of project selection)

Determination of the TCF\$ depends on the hurdle rate used to select projects. Projects can be selected on the basis of either the cost of financing available in that year [24] or consolidated firm's average weighted cost of capital over time [27]

## 222 Maintaining an Acceptable Level of Foreign Exchange Risks

Managing MNC's economic exposure to exchange risk, not managing its balance sheet exposure is the key to formulating a global financing strategy. And international diversification of both operations and financing is fundamental to managing economic exposure [8,20], because this approach does not require the superhuman ability to forecast a disequilibrium condition, such as foreign exchange rates

The firm's financing base can be diversified by constructing optimal currency cocktail for each unit of the MNC by choosing sources of funds in more than one capital market and/or more than one currency $^{1}$ The optimal currency mix (cocktail) can be constructed by matching (hedging) and/or by exposure netting (or the portfolio approach) in which two decision variables are used – the number of currencies and the proportion of each currency in the mix. The matching approach may be used to hedge a firm's long-term operating exposure at no cost to the value of the firm by structuring debt financing so that the decrease (increase) in asset earnings is matched by a corresponding increase (decrease) in the cost of servicing these liabilities (see, e.g., [8 20]) Exposure netting attempts to construct a Markowitz efficient portfolio of a group of currencies that has proved to be less vulnerable to foreign exchange gains or losses (through variance and covariance analysis), since the currency of each operating country may not move in the same direction (appreciation or depreciation) (see e.g., [21,22,31])

## 2 2 3 Minimizing the Political Risks

Relevant political risks taken in global financing decisions include currency inconvertibility and repatriation risk. Dividend payments and reductions in committed equity are characteristically more closely controlled by government regulations than are principal and interest payments (see, e.g., [26]). To minimize the losses from political interference in a host country with high political risks, the firm must maintain a thin equity base, using a maximum allowable D/E ratio and capitalization with a large portion of local debt financing

## 2.2.4 Minimizing the Cost of Maintaining Agency Relationships

The recent finance literature has recognized the conflict of interests between the participants in an organization Jensen and Meckling [15] recommended minimizing the costs of the agency relationships $^{2}$ between the principals (stockholders) and agents (managers) by investing in bonding or monitoring arrangements only to the point where the benefits from enforcement just outweigh the costs

According to a survey [26], some parent companies refuse to provide additional financing beyond their initial investment, and managers of foreign affiliates may often be forced to borrow locally, regardless of the cost, to foster good relations with local financial institutions. For the parent company, there is a trade-off between the decreased agency/monitoring cost and increased financing cost In other words, the parent company may decrease its cost of monitoring foreign affiliates by turning over some of its monitoring responsibilities to local financial institutions, but forcing affiliates to borrow at high local costs may, in turn increase the after-tax foreign exchange risk adjusted cost of financing for the affiliate Whether the parent will guarantee the debt of the foreign affiliate may be another important factor in the analysis of the trade-offs (see [30, pp 561–567] for the a detailed discussion of the relationships between agency costs and corporate policy regarding parent guarantees of the debt of foreign affiliates)

## 3 A Multiple Objective Decision Support System Approach

## 31 Definition

A multiple objective (criteria) decision support system (MODSS or MCDSS) can be defined as a computer-based system that support decision makers, utilizing data and models to solve semi-structured problems with conflicting objectives

## 32 An Overview of the MODSS

Today multiple objective decision making (MODM) is an important areas in management science. There have been various techniques introduced for MODM, such as multiattribute utility theory, multicriteria linear programming, heuristic search methods, goal programming, and learning models, of these, goal programming is one of the most powerful and popular Decision making with multiple objectives has been a challenge to management science during the past decade. Therefore, MODSS has become an increasingly important class of DSS (e.g., [3,9,11 12 13,14,23]) Most of these MODSS use goal programming as a principal DSS tool

## 321 The Large-Scale Goal Programming Model as a Powerful Tool for MODSS

The GP model is designed to allocate limited resources (internally generated funds) and select the most desirable sources of financing. Goal programming can analyze the decision maker's multiple aspiration levels, relax some of the model constraints, and incorporate the decision maker's preference system for multiple conflicting goals. Thus GP can transform a decision model into a satisficing model, in which an abstract single objective for the organization is replaced by tangible, measurable goals. This special feature of goal programming allows the decision maker to incorporate environmental organizational, and judgmental considerations into the model through the determination of aspiration levels and their priorities

The decision maker's multiple aspirational goals are expressed in either ordinal (lexicographic) or numerical terms so that they can be analyzed on the basis of their importance lower-ranked (or weighted) goals are considered only after higher-ranked goals have been satisfied or have reached the point beyond which no further improvement is desirable. Although it may not be possible to optimize every goal GP attempts to obtain satisfactory levels of goal attainment that represent the best possible combination of goal achievements [19]

## 322 Forecasting Model

A forecasting model predicts the political stability of each operating country and the changes in average annual exchange rates for the currencies used in the firm's financing decisions. These forecasts are for the period of the firm's financial planning horizon

## 323 Simulation Model

Many MNCs are building a simulation model into their internal reporting requirements in an attempt to anticipate (1) future cash flows within the MNC, (2) the future foreign exchange exposure position by currency of by affiliate, and (3) the effects of exchange exposure on all company operations on a consolidated basis, as well as on subsidiary basis

## 324 Statistical Analysis System

The statistical analysis system (SAS) is designed to derive a variance and covariance matrix between the parent company's functional currency and the functional currencies of each subsidiary and any third currency in the currency cocktail decision. Using variance and covariance analysis to manage global cash and foreign exchange risk has attached increasing attention since Makin [22] suggested using portfolio theory to reduce foreign exchange risks (see also [21])

## 42 Defining Objectives and Goals of Global Financing Decisions

The iterative process of developing a world-wide financing strategy begins with the definition of several potentially conflicting financing objectives. If a goal programming model will be used to design global financing strategies qualitative corporate financing objectives should be translated into goals. There is no clear distinction between objectives and goals, but herein a goal is an objective that has been expressed in terms of its importance and target date (during the next strategic financial planning period) A GP model in this MCDSS consists of an objective function and two types of goal constraints priority fixed ( $p_{0}$ through $p_{2}$ ) and priority interchangeable. The following financing goals are identified in the order of their importance

$P_{1}$ Maintain an optimal range of D/E ratios for the consolidated worldwide firm and maintain a localized optimum range of D/E ratio for joint venture subsidiary B where local government and joint venture partners have placed severe restrictions on debt ratio as shown in row 9 of Table 1

## 431 The Effectiveness of Global Financing Decisions

Designing global financing strategies refers to generating and analyzing alternative ways of financing to achieve corporate goals. Increasing the effectiveness of decision making is an essential DSS objective. According to Bennett [2], increasing effectiveness implies a broadening of the decision maker's focus by expanding the set of open decision possibilities (decision space). Keen and Scott-Morton [17] have suggested that the payoffs of decision support systems are in extending the range and capability of the managers' decision processes to help them improve their effectiveness. The more uncertain and unstable the environment, the greater the need to emphasize effectiveness. Effective decision making maximizes goal attainment (i.e., minimizes non-achievement of goals) for a given set of resources

4 4 2 Expanding the Vertical Decision Space Generating Strategies by incorporating Mangerial Judgment

Another avenue for expanding the manager's decision space is to incorporate “what if” types of managerial queries concerning environmental uncertainty, after choosing a preference system for multiple goals. Assume that the decision maker chose priority structure “A” in figure 2. The GP model-based DSS can obtain the value of uncertain variables either from the forecasting model or by managerial judgment. A manager may override automated financing strategies (on the basis of the two assumed values generated by the forecasting model) to reflect managerial judgmental through “what-if” queries. The GP model-based DSS thus increases managers’ ability to deal with complexity and uncertainty. For example, if the corporate treasurer strongly believes that the Spanish Peseta will depreciate against the U S Dollar by 10 percent, thereby exceeding the original forecast value of 425% in table 1 (under the column heading A), this anticipated change in foreign exchange of the Spanish Peseta would significantly alter the financing strategies (Strategy V).

## 5 Conclusion

The large scale GP model-based MODSS we have presented provides a technological breakthrough in resolving critical issues in the design of a global financing strategy. This research significantly increases the effectiveness of global financing decision making and provides better ways of designing a worldwide financing strategy because of the GP model's ability to analyze the trade-offs among potentially conflicting objectives. Our research also suggests alternative financial structure for foreign subsidiaries. In our opinion, after the financial structure of the consolidated MNC has been established, selecting the appropriate capital structures for each foreign affiliate should not be a simple choice among those three alternatives (see, section 21) Rather, based on the analysis of the trade-offs among foreign exchange risks, political risks, capital structures goals, and agency costs, the decision maker can mix alternatives a and b (in section 21) for an individual subsidiary The development of a large-scale model-based DSS shift the emphasis of global financing from the current reliance on intuition and managerial judgment to an equal mix of decision science models, information systems, and intuition and managerial judgment
