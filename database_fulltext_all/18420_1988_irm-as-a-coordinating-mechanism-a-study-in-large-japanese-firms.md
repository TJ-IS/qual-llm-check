# IRM as a Coordinating Mechanism: A Study in Large Japanese Firms

Osamu Sato

Tokyo Keizai University, 1–7, Minami-cho, Kokubunji-shi, Tokyo 185, Japan

Masahiro Horiuchi

Niigata University, 8050, Ninomachi, Igarashi, Niigata-shi, Niigata-ken, 950–21, Japan

One of the functions of Information Resource Management (IRM) is coordination between the Information System (IS) department and the users. Models of this coordination mechanism are presented, and the results of two empirical studies obtained through questionnaires given to large Japanese companies are introduced. The objectives of this research were: (1) to investigate organizational factors which affect the existence of coordination functions; (2) to find organizational factors which affect the selection of coordination mechanisms. The results indicate that five factors: (1) importance of technical factors, (2) organizational climate (adaptability), (3) progressiveness of IS department, (4) the resources of the IS department, and (5) computer experience of the users, significantly affect the existence and selection of coordination mechanisms. Also they alter the patterns of the coordination mechanisms and create a need for an official IRM function as a coordination mechanism. In other words, the coordination pattern shifts from IS driven through user driven to independent or official committee driven in IRM by these factors.

Keywords: Information resource management, Coordination mechanism, Information systems function, End users, Information systems department, Strategic information systems, Organizational characteristics

1. Introduction

With the adoption of increasingly complex new computer systems by organizations and a growing need for highly skilled employees who can use this new equipment, coordination between Information System (IS) departments and users is becoming more and more important [1]. In order to ensure efficient and effective management of user systems, equipment, and data, Information Resource Management (IRM) emerges as an important coordination function in organizations [13,14]. But little research is reported on the function of IRM. This paper attempts to contribute such vital research. The questions this research wishes to address are:

The first and the last types of coordination mechanisms require no coordination function. So, there are three possible types left: IS department driven, user driven, and independent committee. Of these, the IS department driven type is a coordination mechanism where the IS staff designs information systems under the consideration of technical efficiency with little user input. The last coordination mechanism is an independent organization or committee that keeps equal distance from both the IS department and the users.

In every coordination pattern, some users felt satisfied and others not. 3. The IRM Function as a Coordination Mechanism

As computer technology advances, computer systems enter offices and new OA equipment becomes more complex. We see this in many offices; e.g., telephones or typewriters were rather easy to learn. But new OA equipment like word-processors and computers, are not so easy to learn. Three factors contribute to this:

(1) Technical innovation in computers and telecommunications make new OA equipment multifunctional.

(2) After users master the single-function and easy to use equipment, they want to use higher-level more-complex systems.

(3) Because new OA equipment communicates with other systems through telecommunication networks, users need knowledge of telecommunication systems to use them effectively.

But this trend also generates new problems for office workers, because:

(1) When operations become more complex, their users are limited to a relative few.

(2) Many kinds of CA equipment is introduced into the company without IS department control.

(3) Data and information security control in the user area becomes lax.

Local users tend to consider only their narrow work area and select equipment for their use within this narrow perspective. This contributes to an abundance of varied OA equipment in offices, but they are rather complex to use, so the number of qualified users is limited. Because the traditional IS department generally has a large backlog of projects, they do not assist users effectively and on time. The management of many kinds of OA equipment is thus left to the users, but they often have little knowledge about security management and control.

To deal with these problems, the IRM function emerges as a staff function. Jackson [8] states the objectives of IRM as:

(1) Selection of applications for development, including project feasibility evaluation and the setting of priorities of potential candidates.

(3) Selection of communication facilities.

Conceptually, IRM is a corporate coordination function between the traditional IS department and the users. We investigate the following two questions:

(1) Which factors make the IRM function the coordinating mechanism?

(2) Where does the IRM function emerge and how can it be established as an independent corporate staff function?

The Information Center (IC) concept used by IBM is presented by Hammond [6]; the IRM function discussed here is conceptually the same. In this, the IC is a function of the IS organization. Carr [2] observes that all IC that he investigated are under the direct control of IS management.

For example, Ein-Dor and Segev [4] claim that MIS structure is significantly correlated with organizational structure. We received 197 responses for the IS function and 202 for users. In this questionnaire, we selected 27 variables and grouped them into four subgroups, as shown in table 1. These variables were measured on a five point Likert-scale or with a dummy variable.

## 5. Results of the Discriminant Analysis

(2) IS department driven or user driven coordination mechanism,

(3) user driven or independent committee coordination mechanism,

(4) IS department driven or independent committee coordination mechanism.

Though there were 27 explanatory variables, much of it on a five point Likert-type scale, the data used was large enough. ## 6. Interpretations of Results

We now interpret the results within our conceptual model framework.

## 6.1. The Existence of a Consulting Contact

Organizational environment variables which affect the existence of a consulting contact or window are industry, R&D efforts, and new product development efforts. A consulting contact is likely to exist if the company is a manufacturer, or if the R&D effort is high, or new product development efforts are low.

We believe that these results indicate the importance of technical factors to the organization. First, they are more important for manufacturers than non-manufacturers. Second, the R&D effort is high if they are important for the company. But their relative importance in new product development is not high, because non-technical factors, such as advertising, pricing, design, customer service and market competition, are more important. If technical factors are important to the company, then an organization tend to have a consulting contact for end users.

Some user functional characteristic variables affect the existence of a consulting contact. If users belong to the accounting, personnel, or sales department, then a consultant contact tend to exist. These departments usually begin their EDP at an early stage and have much experience with computer systems. Ein-Dor and Segev [3] report that many data processing units emerge from an accounting department. Personnel departments have been using personnel management or salary computing systems for a long time. Sales departments, also, construct customer management systems or billing systems in early stage of EDP. Such variables indicate long EDP experiences of end users. Gibson and Nolan [5] state that the computer is located in the department where it is first applied – very frequently, in accounting – in its first stage. So, it is natural that these department have experience in computer.

Two IS functional characteristic variables affect the existence of a consulting contact. If the EDP cost per sale is high or software management has wide responsibilities, then a consulting contact tends to exist. The EDP cost per sales is a measure of the resources of the IS department. We find that the larger their resource the larger their power.

## 6.2. The Selection of Coordination Mechanisms

Two organizational environment variables seem to affect the selection of a coordination mechanism. First, manufacturing companies tend to have IS department driven mechanisms rather than user driven ones. Second, if new product development efforts are strong, coordination mechanisms tend to be user or IS department driven rather than by an independent committee. We believe that the relative importance of new product development is inversely related to the importance of technological factors in the company.

If company wide organizational structural changes occur many times, the coordination mechanism selected is user rather than independent committee driven. We have found that frequent structural changes make it hard for companies to organize independent committees and this discourages companies from selecting user driven coordination mechanisms. Also, if job-prescription is strict, the mechanism selected is IS department rather than user driven, or user rather than independent committee driven. The strictness of job-prescription is a measure of the rigidity of the organizational structure or adaptability. Our results indicate that organizations whose structures are strict or static prefer IS department to user driven, or user to independent committee driven. In other words, dynamic organizations select independent committee mechanisms and static organizations select IS department driven mechanisms. Contingency theory claims that environmental characteristics affect organizational structure [9]. Moreover, we claim that organizational structure provides coordination mechanisms.

Five user functional characteristic variables affect the selection. First, if organizational and structural changes in a department seldom occur, the consulting contact tends to be user rather than independent committee driven, or IS rather than independent committee driven. Departments which face an unstable environment must change their organizational structure frequently. Second, if the user function is not accounting or personnel, they tend to select IS department rather than user driven, user of IS department rather than independent committee driven. The same results are found for the sales functions. The accounting and personnel functions, as well as sales, have substantial experience in electronic computing. Users of these functions are more familiar with IS technology and tend to participate more actively in IS implementation. Third, end-users of commercial databases more frequently tend to select an independent committee as a coordination mechanism rather than user or IS department driven coordination mechanisms. Again, this is a measure of user familiarity with computer technology.

As many as five IS functional variables affect the preference of the coordination mechanism. First, an IS department whose EDP cost per sales is high tends to select IS rather than independent committee driven. This cost is a measure of the resources of the IS department: it has more resources, and thus coordination mechanism tends to be IS department driven. Possibly it still holds the power resources. Second, a cost-center rather than a profit-center tends to be user or IS driven rather than an independent function. This is a matter of accounting systems. In Japan, companies which have large and progressive IS departments often separate them into new businesses (subsidiaries). Progressive IS departments often accounts to users and are managed as a profit center. So we believe that an adaptive IS department is likely to select an independent coordination mechanism.

Third, considerations of the IS department and the hardware operation show that if IS department responsibility is wide, the coordination mechanism is IS department or independent committee rather than user driven. Apparently, the responsibility for hardware operation by the IS department is proportional to its resources. Use of Office Automation lets end users manage their own computer hardware and narrows the responsibility of the IS department. This is a matter of political power within a corporation. And finally, if the implementation of data processing at a strategic level is low, then the coordination mechanism tends to be IS department rather than user or independent committee driven. The implementation of data processing at this level is a measure of the level of adaptability of the IS department to requests from users: it indicates the technical or processing capacity of the IS department.

## 6.3. Summary

To draw meaningful implications, we must interpret each answer. We have already showed our interpretation. Now, we sum up our interpretations of each variable and the implications of our research.

Industry, R&F efforts, and new product efforts in organizational environment variables are measures of the importance of technology for each company. Structural changes and strictness of job-prescriptions in organizational characteristic variables are measures of organizational climate (adaptability). Frequent departmental structural changes are a measure of departmental adaptability. The functions of users and the use level of commercial databases are measures of user experience with information systems and related technology. IS department characteristic variables consist of (1) the resources of the IS department, including EDP cost per sales and the responsibility for hardware, and (2) the level of progressive-ness of the IS department, including the use of an accounting system (cost-center or profit-center) and the degree of strategic information processing.

Of course, there are several interpretations of these variables, and they affect the real results, substituting subjective judgment. We believe our interpretations are natural and reasonable.

## 7. Implications of the Research

Table 5 is the summary of the results interpretations. It shows the evolution of a coordination mechanism between the IS department and the users. As the level of affecting variables grows, a coordination mechanism evolves in the direction indicated. We illustrate such directions in table 6, where grows of the level of each variable shifts the coordination function in the directions shown.

If the IS department has richer resources, then political power within the corporation shifts from users to the IS department, and then the coordination mechanism becomes IS driven. The same thing occurs for technical factors. But technical factors affect all constituents in the corporation, so its effect on the shift of coordination mechanisms is smaller than the effect of the IS functional resources.

The result of the existence of a consulting window support this direction. As noted earlier, consulting contact for users is one of the IRM functions. So, the existence of this consulting contact indicates the existence of an IRM function. Comparing this with the results of the selection of the coordination mechanism, we believe that when the importance of technical factors grows, or user experiences of IS are accumulated, or the IS function has enough resources, a consulting window tends to exist. Figure 5 shows such key factors.

Table 7 indicates how the IRM function emerges in organizations. In general, this is based on DP growth. To use complex computer systems effectively, coordination is needed. At the first stage of DP growth, this is performed within the IS function. But as additional computer systems are introduced, such as Office Automation systems, the coordination mechanism shifts to users. Finally, when computer systems become very complex and corporate-wide, integrated management is needed, and an independent and official IRM, functioning as an independent committee or an Information Center, must emerge. Moreover, this path corresponds with user awareness described by Nolan [11], who states than the user involvement in IS projects shifts from reactive to driving force to participatory according to the stage of progress.

## 8. Concluding Remarks

We have investigated the organizational factors that affect the existence of consulting contacts and the shift of its location and the selection of coordination mechanisms. Coordination between the IS department and the users is one of the important objectives of the IRM function.
