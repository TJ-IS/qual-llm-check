# Inter-Organizational Information Systems as Company Resources

Reima Suomi

Turku School of Economics and Business Administration / Sampo Insurance Company Limited, SF-20100 Turku, Finland

Here the concept of an inter-organizational system (IOS) is explored. Reasons are given for considering inter-organizational information systems as valuable company resources, and some potential benefits that could be obtained from these resources are listed. A case example of an inter-organizational system is being presented: it also discusses the benefits that could be obtained from this prototype system and its descendants.

Keywords: Inter-organization information systems, Strategic and operational advantages, Strategic use of information systems, Customer contacts, Telecommunications.

## 1. What is an Inter-Organizational IS?

A well established assumption is, that an information system (IS) is always connected with the one and only organization it serves. This has had some consequences, which have made it easier to control IS:

(1) An organization can always fully control its IS.

(2) The costs of the IS can always be assigned to the single organization, as also can be benefits that result.

A big step away from this ideal picture was taken at the advent of decentralized IS's. Albeit under the same parent organizations, ISs were controlled by separate organization units often having contradictory needs. Complicated procedures had to be developed to assist in the allocation of costs and benefits caused by IS's.

As multitudes of new organizational units developed, it would have been possible to develop and consider the concept of interorganizational information systems (IOS's). But it is easy to assert that the term started with the deregulation of data communications and the development of modern data communications technology.

A diversified and complicated organization surely needs internal data communication. Automation of the flow of routine data is being accomplished in nearly all organizations. But why limit this to the inside of the organization, when we are living in a world of interdependence? Company boundaries are not the only, or even the most meaningful, system boundaries. Therefore, even though internal systems may still be far from totally integrated, perceptive management needs to begin to consider the new possibilities for coordinating data processing outside its own organizational limits [4].

An IOS cannot be managed and organized in the same way as internal systems of the company. Crossing company boundaries causes many difficulties which must be solved, but offers potential benefits. These points are made by Cash and Konsynski, who differentiate between IOS's and DDF's (distributed data processing) [2]:

(1) Whereas DDP is under the control of a single company, an IOS crosses company boundaries. Thus an employee in one company can directly allocate resources and initiate business processes in another company.

(2) With an IOS, in contrast to DDP, the question of government regulation arises as a result of information exchange across the boundaries of separate organizations and hence across separate legal entities.

(3) The IOS facilitator is a player that doesn't exist in DDP. Facilitator is an organizational unit that aids in the development, operation, or use of a network for exchange of formation among participants. The supporting products or services are major ingredient of the facilitator's job.

(4) An IOS frequently has a broader and more significant potential competitive impact than the traditional internal uses of IS technology.

IOS's have developed for several reasons. One way is to divide changes into economic, technological and organizational ones. In these three areas, we see that IOS's are nowadays often considered valuable company resources.

## Organizational Changes

(1) The growing need for inter-organizational contacts

Many new organization forms have been created, these need closer communication between participating organizations; e.g. for joint ventures or clearing houses

(2) Liberalization of the data communications industry

New organizations and enterprises have been able to enter the telecommunications industry. This has dropped the prices of services and given way to new innovative ideas and services based on them. As a proof of this a multitude of different networks are in use today. Quarterman and Hoskins [6] divide computer networks into four types; these are: (1) research, (2) company, (3) cooperative, and (4) commercial networks.

(3) The saturation of information processing in organizations.

The combination of decreased costs and increased capability has resulted in a broad range of internal computer applications. As more and more data is stored, the natural next step is to transmit these data in machine readable form wherever they are needed.

## (4) The increased size of organizations

Big organizations have been able to practice IOS-like telecommunication already between their business units, so they have experience. In addition, they have the needed resources to invest in IOS's and they are also capable to take the risks associated with the use of IOS's.

## Economic Changes

(1) The increased value of information in the company

Enterprises are willing to pay more for accurate and timely information, but this can only be provided with the help of intelligent IOS's. The timeliness of information is especially important today, when we live in a world full of change. This trend is mainly based on shifts in international competition, shrinking geographic separation, and deregulation with more open competition.

(2) The dropping prices of telecommunications Because of technological advances, but also because of the breaking up of regulated industries and other monopolie or cartels, prices for electronic information transfer are dropping on all fronts.

(3) The rising price of the traditional ways of doing things

Normal daily routine becomes more and more expensive in all organizations. The work force is expensive, and so are the routine transactions conducted by PTT.

## Technological Changes

(1) Better hardware makes more intelligent use of IOS possible.

The advent of better hardware is a well known trend needing no explanation here. Among the most important technologies for IOS's are: - increased capability of computer input/output devices,

-better communication media: among others fiberoptic networks and satellites,

-faster and more secure secondary storages.

(2) Better communication software
Telecommunication architectures are available in the product lines of major vendors.

(3) The establishment of standards of protocols Historically, governmental regulations was the primary impetus for establishing standards. But now organizations such as industry associations and industry groups are also introducing standards. Private enterprises find local advantages in setting standards.

In addition to these three change types we now add one more reason for the advent of IOS's: competitive changes. Organization are desperately trying to find new ways to obtain strategic advantage over their competitors. IOS's can provide ways of achieve advantages, either by (1) dropping costs, (2) differentiation of product, or (3) focusing.

These are, of course based on the division of competitive strategies by Porter [5].

At first glance the questions of IOS's may seem to be pure technical. These deserve a lot of attention, but there is no reason to forget that IOS's can provide powerful competitive and strategic tools. An IOS can give a corporation an edge over its competitors, but an IOS can shift the balance of power between the supplier and the buyer, making for a possible unhealthy dependence on either side. [2]

## 2. IOS's as Company Resources.

For a long time IS's were considered purely as technical devices assisting in the control and management of the primary operations of a company. From the point of view of management IS's were an expense and they provided few benefits. They were not considered a company resource, and it was considered, that IS's could be changed as easily as any other machinery. They were not valuable, since they were not an integral part of the primary production facility of the company.

Little by little IS's have become known as a resource. Several reasons can be found:

(1) Information processing is now considered to be a kind of production. IS's are vital in many companies, such as banks or insurance.

(2) The costs of IS's are so huge that they become a capital expense requiring a sinking fund.

(3) Company environments are becoming so demanding and difficult, that it is becoming impossible to manage without good IS.

(4) Data itself is becoming a valuable company resource: it can often be bought and sold.

If we look at the components of a company IS department, different parts of it have been recognized as company resources at different times.

In the early days, computers were recognised to be resources. They were concrete entities needing huge investment. Those organizations with computers had more resources at hand than those without them.

In the second stage, it was realized, that computers could not be run without appropriate program resources. So, computer systems consisting of hardware and software could be seen as company resources.

In the third stage, data was considered a company resource. This happened because of advancements in database theories and concrete database organization. Different data structures could be designed, so that more information could be derived from data bases; data bases often contain implicit information, which can be deduced from the explicit data.

But links connecting IS's were not granted the title "resource" for a long time. Some reasons were:

(1) The links were not under the control of one company, while resources normally are.

(2) Because of changing telecommunication standards and expense, no lasting value could be assigned to equipment on hand.

(3) Decisions and investments on telecommunication links were made on a macro level, leaving little room for companies to manage this resource.

In the last few years iOS's have also been recognized as company resources. This is because:

(1) It is impossible to ignore the fact that some companies are doing better than others because of their use of IOS's.

(2) Companies have to invest great amounts of money in IOS's.

(3) It has been recognized that it is not necessary to have complete control over everything you consider a resource.

## 3.2. Strategic Advantages

The strategic advantages obtainable by the use of IOS's are many and intertwined. They are discussed for several company functions.

## Production

Here, the major strategic benefits can be obtained through cost reductions. Production is usually an internal function, with very little room for IOS's. When a large enterprise integrates many separate production plants, IOS's can be of use, possibly through a company information network.

But the storage and delivery functions are a significant application of IOS's: inventory levels can be reduced, and buyers can be provided with flexible ways to order. A prime example is the case of American Hospital Supply (AHS), a company which has acquired a very strong market position with the use of an IOS, as illustrated for example in [3].

## 3. Why do Companies Build IOS's?

## 3.1. Operational Advantages

Organizations are trying to get both operational and strategic advantage from the use of IOS's. Decisions on whether to build or not to build IOS's are usually based on operational advantage.

IOS's are being built to provide operational advantages due to lower costs and greater efficiency. Barrett and Konsynski [1] speak of three kinds of potential benefits:

(1) cost reduction,

(2) productivity improvement, and

(3) product market/strategy.

The first two incentives are clearly operational, while product/market strategy issues are of a strategic nature. Among the operational advantages are:

(1) reduced paperwork and manual operation;

(2) lower inventory levels;

(3) faster flow of materials and products;

(4) standardization of procedures;

(5) faster information about change in demand;

(6) lower telecommunications cost.

## Marketing

In marketing IOS's are used to concentrate on certain markets or to create differentiation image. Marketing and selling functions can use IOS's to illustrate and introduce the products or services. Such a possibly occurs when there is an IOS provided by a third party, when the services of the IOS are open for all willing to co-operate. As an example, airlines can show their routes and flights in terminals provided to travel agents. Although all inquiries do not lead to a scale, the IOS's make it possible to offer the services or products.

## Purchasing

Here IOS's can support the reception, storage, and distribution of resources by forming reliable, working links to the suppliers. Storage levels can be reduced and order quantities can be optimized by IOS's connecting the suppliers to the internal logistics of the firm. The main focus is again on cost reduction.

## 4. A Case

Sampo Insurance Company Limited, with its cooperating companies, is Finland's second largest insurance company group providing a full spectrum of insurances. It converted from a mutual company to a stock company at the end of 1987. This was accomplished by giving free shares to customers, and operation of unusual character, both in the Finnish stock market and in the insurance industry in general. Sampo's position is especially strong in the private household sector: home, car, and travel insurance. The facts of most importance are presented in figure 2

Sampo's orientation toward mass-insurances with little income per policy causes great pressures towards efficiency of operation. IS's need to be updated regularly, yet much of the work consists of small badly-integrated tasks with little computer support.

One reason for the poor integration is the lack of interorganizational links. Because of this, many tasks are manual ones. IOS's are clearly needed.

Customers are among the first group to consider when looking for potential partners in IOS's. Possibilities occur for consumer groups such as:

(1) Travel bureaus

They provide insurance for travellers, with very varied conditions causing much book-keeping, but bringing in little revenue.

(2) Large account agents for different kinds of insurances

The trend is towards using internal agents; local insurance associations may send bigger companies insurances they themselves do not provide. Insurance-brokers are growing in number. Links to such companies offer great competitive advantages.

(3) Car dealers

insurances to customers for their new cars.

(4) Truck companies
They need insurance for their fleet and the freight they handle.

Operational advantages are a very important incentive for building IOS's. Cost reduction can be achieved through reduced paperwork and postage. Letters and messages for customers or from customers need manual processing, even though the material might be initially produced by the computer. A better solution is to leave the paperwork out of the procedure. As telecommunication costs are decreasing and postage fees steadily telecommunication costs are decreasing and postages fees steadily rising, there is also reason to switch over to electronic data transfer instead of traditional mail services.

Generally, faster flow of materials and reduced inventory levels are not relevant, since insurance companies do not handle physical products. Faster flow of information is important, since money can be collected faster

Standardization of procedures is also a goal, but it is not a major problem, since customers are using preprinted forms when applying for insurances.

As far as strategic advantages are concerned, IOS's will probably maintain connection to customers. So the major functions should be (1) the creation of high switching costs, and (2) the creation of a differentiation image.

The switching costs for a client depend on change to their internal operations. Changing to IOS's involves major investment in computers or software. There is reason to believe that internal operations will change a lot, since customers in the insurance field are eagerly asking for new services provided by IOS's.

In the question of differentiation, of the corporate image, success depends on the creation of the differentiation image on the market situation at the time of publication of new IOS-services.

Because of these pressures, the management of Sampo set up a project to connect to major customers with the help of an IOS (SIOS = the Sampo IOS).

The set of customers were selected from those that often use insurance services. They must also be sure that computer-based connections to the insurance company will be of help and use to them.

Two suitable customer groups were found and one company in each group was selected to take part in the project. The groups were:

(1) Car dealers organizing car insurances for their customers;

(2) Truck companies taking insurances for their fleet and maybe later for the freight they are handling.

In the first case, the customer is actually the buyer, not the car dealer (though the dealer may have a car fleet of its own needing insurance and also for leasing and car-rental services); in second case, the insurance is for the truck company or for the freight on behalf of the material owned by the transportation customers.

In the search for competitive advantage, however, connections to customers offered the best testing-field for IOS's. The two cooperating companies were selected to be in the same town as Sampo, thereby decreasing communication costs when building the systems and of the public telephone lines and during the cooperation phase.

The technical solution was built to have the following properties:

(1) No extra cost should result from the customer use of the system.

(2) The investment in the programs and computers in Sampo should be modular, so that new customer and message types could be added without major difficulty.

## 5. Conclusions: IOS's as a Company Resource in the Insurance Industry

As new enterprises learn about IOS's, they are often willing to try them out without any major cost-benefit calculation to learn what is happening and to get experiences of the technology. It is also usual to have some risky and demanding projects (such as IOS's) in the applications portfolio just in order to stimulate staff in edp-departments. As company resources, IOS's are considered risky but promising ones.

The increased size of customer organizations and the increased volume of the transactions they are handling leads to the use of IOS'; e.g., car leaders find that people are buying more expensive cars and are willing to take out more expensive insurances on them.

The technical effect of IOS's on insurance companies is not yet clear. Cost-analysis calculations are not easy to do. IOS projects are projects that must be conducted in order to keep up with customer claims and general competition. It is clear that, when inter-organizational links are working well, savings in routine work will occur. How big they will be or how well they justify the expense, is not yet known.

On the whole, it seems that IOS's are here to stay. Operational benefits are still far away, but these systems need to be built because of their strategic importance. Factors that favour IOS's in other segments of the economy are working in the insurance industry.
