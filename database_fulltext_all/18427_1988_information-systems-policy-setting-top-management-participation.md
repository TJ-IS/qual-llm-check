# Information Systems Policy Setting: Top Management Participation

Victor L. Pérez

Departamento de Ingeniería Industrial, Facultad de Ciencias Físicas y Matemáticas, Universidad de Chile, Casilla 2777, Santiago, Chile

Different: issues that must be considered when setting an Information Systems Policy (ISP) are presented. These issues arise when the information systems (IS) development process is related to the development of the organizational decision making systems (DMS) that those IS are to support. According to our findings, the planning for an ISP should start with a critical "business process analysis". Based upon these relationships, we argue that top management should participate in the ISP setting.

Keywords: Information systems, Decision making systems, Information systems policy, Information resource management.

## 1. Information Systems Policy (ISP)

## 1.1. Issues to be Considered When Setting an ISP

An ISP involves the definition and evaluation of the criteria and strategies for:

(1) The identification, characterization, design, control, and evaluation of the Information Systems (IS) that are to be developed in the medium and long term.

(2) The determination of the costs, benefits, and expected effectiveness of the ISs that are to be developed.

(3) The determination of the priority of developing those ISs.

(4) The allocation and management of all information resources.

The above considerations indicate that the issues to be focused on when setting an ISP arise from:

(1) The manner we understand the scope and objectives of an IS application.

(2) The methodological framework being used during the development of such an application.

As far as the first issue is concerned, the objective of any IS application is to fulfill a set of users' information needs in order to support their activities [17]. With respect to the second, most IS development methodologies present the following facilities:

(1) To understand those user activities that are to be supported by the target IS [3,7,21].

(2) To identify and to specify the users' information requirements [19].

(3) To develop the data manipulation and usage activities that are to implement the IS [23].

Recent developments in the field are focusing IS development interest on:

(1) A search for more efficiency in IS development and operating activities (e.g., prototype technique [4], four generation software products [16], computerized tools for data administration [20], computer-aided software engineering tools, CASE [10]).

(2) An interest in the identification of those ISs that could increase the organizational effectiveness. Among the criteria and approaches that have been considered are: critical success factors, development of informatics plans, strategic and executive information systems [5], and decision support systems [11].

1.2. Issues Derived from Application of Develop-
ment Methodologies

Numerous IS applications are fulfilling neither the users' information requirements nor the IS applications' expected performance [1,8,12,13,14].

Organizational and users oriented dissatisfactions arise, we believe, from the IS development frameworks being used. According to these, the IS development process is mostly focused upon the identification and specification of a set of users' information needs. The fulfillment of these needs are considered the IS purposes.

In this sense, we argue that the IS development process is seldom related to the characterization of the decision making system (DMS) that is to be supported by the IS. This DMS is visualized as a set of interrelated decision making activities attempting to accomplish one or more of the organization's objectives (e.g., Production Planning and its Control). Thus an IS should not be developed in order to support an information user, but an organizational decision maker. The change in the subject of an IS application not only changes the issues when developing such an IS but also changes the issues in setting the criteria for determining and evaluating both the IS applications and the information resource management (IRM) activities.

Current IS development methodologies do not explicitly consider this change; most of them just present the facilities for the users and the IS analysts to:

(1) Understand the manner in which the decision making activities are currently being carried out.

(2) Identify and specify the information needs required to support these activities.

(3) Design and build computer programs, files, and procedures that fulfill these information needs.

We therefore argue that current IS development methodologies do not explicitly attack the question of identifying the users' information needs. And, because of that, practical experience shows that most IS analysts end up asking the users for their information requirements [18]. Numerous IS development methods guide the users into the process of questioning their information requirements. But they do not guide the users into the process of questioning the effectiveness of their decisions and decision making activities. So, they neither attack the IS design process nor the DMS design process. Because of this, they do not consider the possibility that the resulting IS application ends up supporting an ineffective DMS.

At most, some IS development methodologies allow the users to formalize and to specify their current decision making activities. In doing so, they allow the users either to improve the understanding of those activities (and, hence, improve the process of identifying their information needs) or to eliminate some of the problems existing in the implementation of decision making activities. Furthermore, IS development methodologies do not explicitly consider the fact that the effectiveness of the IS development process depends upon the effectiveness of the decision making processes it is to support.

In summary, if we are to develop effective IS applications, the development process must depend upon the development and performance of the DMS to be supported [9].

## 2. Top Management Participation in ISP Setting

The general characterization of IS development involves consideration of the objectives, restrictions, and specific way of implementation of the higher level system in which the IS will be a part [2,6,15,22]. Such a higher level system is a specific decision making system (DMS) within the organization. The target IS must provide information to support all DMS endogenous decisions.

The effectiveness of the IS development process has to be measured from two different points of view. On the one hand, the performance of the IS development process must be effective with respect to the general objectives of any development process. On the other hand, the performance of the resulting IS has to be effective with respect to its own objectives; i.e., the provision of information required by the decision makers belonging to the DMS and supported by the IS. In order to achieve these two objectives, we have to either formalize the current DMS or carry out the development of a new DMS. The development (or the formalization) of the DMS, then, is a first step towards the development of the target IS.

If we extend the argument, we can apply it to the ISP setting. It should start with a critical "business process analysis". The end result of such an analysis would be the setting of an organizational wide decision making systems policy (DMSP). It is very similar to the ISP introduced at the start of this article, except that it deals with an expansion to DMS rather than mere IS.

The content of this policy is needed when determining the criteria for IRM within the organization.

Top management should participate in the definition of criteria for determining:

(1) Which IS applications should be developed in the organization.

(2) The objectives and restrictions of the decision making systems (DMS) to be supported by those IS applications.

But this is not easily accepted by the organization's top management. It is not even fully understood by the information users and IS analysts. This is because the considerations:

(1) Require the involved decision makers to evaluate and question their own methods. And this is hard to carry out [24]. There are cases in which these methods are non documented, subjective, inconsistent, and ineffective. This situation worsens in the case of inexperienced and non specialized decision makers, and ill structured decision making activities.

(2) Move the users and IS analysts out of their normal framework (i.e., searching for quick results from the development and operation of traditional data systems).

## 3. Conclusions: Implementation of these Considerations

The implementation of decision making considerations requires that top management question and (re)design their current DMSs. These activities are important within the establishment of any decision making systems policy (DMSP). In turn, it must be a first step towards the formulation of an ISP within the organization.

We notice how often the information users and the IS analysts take for granted, either explicitly or implicitly, the complete understanding of the DMS that is to be supported by an IS. We observe the tendency to accelerate the IS development process through the use of new tools (e.g., fourth generation software packages, DBMSs, prototypes). These do not explicitly attack the questioning of current DMS, merely of IS implementation.

We argue that the traditional setting of an ISP places emphasis on both a user who defines and uses information, and an IS analyst who takes decisions about the development and implementation of the IS. Top management participation in the ISP setting must concentrate emphasis on a user who is an organizational decision maker. As such, he(she) is continuously searching for the development of more effective DMSs.
