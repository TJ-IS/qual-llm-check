# Information and Modeling Resources for Decision Support in Global Environments

Raja K. Iyer

ITM/KPP/TIEC Cooperative Project, College of Preparatory Studies, Institut Technologi MARA, Section 17, Shah Alam. Selangor, Malaysia

Decision support through information and modeling resources is crucial for strategic decision making in a global environment. This paper describes how decision support systems (DSS) were developed, implemented, and utilized in an organization to evaluate strategic options for foreign direct investments in manufacturing facilities. The paper shows how the process of decision support was implemented and integrated into the company's long range strategic and annual profit planning processes. The approach to decision support can be adopted by other firms to aid international investment planning and operations.

Keywords: Decision support systems. International investment planning.

## 1. Introduction

Organizations striving to develop global participation strategies to be competitive and profitable while serving worldwide markets face a multitude of complex, unstructured, and uncertain decision making and task environments. Political conditions, host government policies, economic scenarios, market and competitive forces, fluctuating exchange rates, technology differences, labor market differentials, restrictions on repatriation of funds, and cultural differences are illustrative of the complexities and uncertainties faced by executives in formulating policies and action plans for global strategic management. Information and modeling resources must therefore be mobilized and managed. This is true even for companies which may not desire to invest in facilities abroad, because global firms are competing in the companies' national markets (for example, Zenith and other American firms vie against Sony, Matsushita, and other firms in the U.S. consumer electronics industry).

This article describes how Onan Corporation, a leading U.S. manufacturer of electrical generator sets and industrial engines mobilized its information, modeling, and other organizational resources to develop and implement a comprehensive decision support system (DSS) for international investment planning, as part of the company's global participation strategy formulation.

Many studies dealing with the international investment planning problem have been reported. however they suffer from several shortcomings. Most prescriptive studies [5,6,12,13] deal with limited aspects of the international investment planning decision. Empirical studies (for example, Dunning [7], which provides an excellent survey of empirical studies) discuss why firms invest abroad based on investments already made. Case studies deal with either one country and many products, or one product or industry and many countries [10] at a time.

## 2. The Global Participation Strategy Problem

Onan Corporation was initially interested in evaluating the feasibility of manufacturing in Canada in response to Canadian governmental policies relating to local content requirements. However, a close examination of the company's worldwide competitive and strategic posture revealed that a more global participation strategy was essential. This was due to several factors including declining market share in growth markets, presence and strength of global competitors, and possible competitive advantages from direct participation in specific regions of the world.

The strategic questions and the formulation of strategic options proceeded as follows.

Why foreign direct investment? This most fundamental issue deals with the need for foreign direct investment in the context of the company's current operating results in a global market place, and its long term organizational goals and strategic posture. The study developed and utilized a practical taxonomy which integrated the theories of foreign direct investment [7] into the company's strategic planning framework consisting of Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats (so-called swot analysis). The taxonomy provided the executives with a structured approach to determining the need for foreign direct investments. Furthermore, the taxonomy was also instrumental in organizing vast amounts of information and organizational resources into a decision support perspective.

Where foreign direct investment? This strategic question involves the assessment of political, economic, financial, and operating risks in all free world countries and, in conjunction with market size and growth potential in each country, select target countries for further evaluation. Based on country assessment information from Business International Corporation and the subject company's personnel in various regions of the world, 21 countries were identified as the target countries.

What portfolio of foreign direct investments? To develop a portfolio of foreign direct investments for a strategic plan, the executives must address the following issues: What products to produce in what country? What forms of ownership? What levels of production – ranging from assembly of components to manufacture of the entire product? When should a specific offshore venture commence during the planning period, subject to the availability of capital and other organizational resources? To address these strategic questions, information systems were established and simulation and evaluation models were implemented through the Interactive Financial Planning System (IFPS).

As shown in Table 1, the combinations of the above strategic questions lead to a total of 5040 strategic options, based on 21 selected target countries, 3 forms of ownership, 8 product lines, 2 levels of production, and 5 years in the company's strategic long range planning period. Fig. 1 shows the framework which was utilized to develop and implement the Dss for the global participation strategy formulation process at the subject company. ## 3. Implementation of Decision Support for Global Participation Strategy Formulation

At Onan Corporation, a comprehensive DSS was developed and implemented to support the complex and unstructured decision process of global participation strategy formulation. Table 2 shows its major components. They include extensive databases and models to support the strategic choice, tactical planning, and operational implementation and integration phases of decision making.

Strategic choice included target country selection and strategy selection. The first step involved the selection of countries with acceptable levels of risks in general instability, ownership/control, operations, and funds transfer. For the selected target countries, strategy selection involved the evaluation of strategic options relating to ownership, product lines, level of manufacture, and the timing of the offshore ventures. The strategic options were evaluated and ranked according to priorities in the context of multiple corporate objectives, resulting in portfolios of foreign direct investments.

Components of the DSS for global participation strategies

Operational implementation and integration:

• Corporate policies and procedures for the implementation of proposed offshore ventures

\- Integrating new venture(s) into the existing corporate structure

Tactical planning involved the evaluation of operational risks for the selected strategic options. The evaluation of risks included sensitivity, what-if, and breakeven probability analyses for factors such as expropriation, profit repatriation controls, foreign exchange fluctuations, and price controls which may likely be imposed by some national governments. Breakeven probability [11] is the tolerable probability of a risk factor, such as expropriation, at which the discounted cash flow from a venture is equal to the investment in the venture. If the projected probability of the risk factor is less than the breakeven probability, then the cash flow is likely to be positive, otherwise negative. This approach proved to be a good, managerially understandable and acceptable method to quantify most of the operational risk factors.

To support operational implementation and integration, the DSS included components to evaluate strategic options for integrating proposed new ventures into the existing or planned corporate organizational and financial structure. One of the methods for evaluating these involved the ranking of the selected strategic options in terms of their effect on multiple corporate objectives. The ranking procedure led to the distillation of the 5,040 strategic options into portfolios of foreign direct investment options, which were then considered along with other projects during the strategic planning and annual budgeting process.

Fig. 2 shows how the DSS was implemented and integrated into the company. It should be noted that successful implementation of the DSS was partly due to the congruence of the stages of the international investment planning process with the stages and the timetable of the company's strategic and profit planning process.

At Onan Corporation, all strategic options were evaluated through IFPS simulations. These simulations were based on vast resources of information obtained internally within the company as well as from several external sources, as summarized below:

Country Information

• Political, economic, and financial information

\- Risk, opportunities, operating conditions

• International economic forecasts

Product Information

\- Product-process definitions (company bill-of-materials)

\- Data on material, direct and indirect Labor, and specification of capital equipment, land, building, and warehouse needs

\- Pricing strategies based on market intelligence information

Market Information

\- Market size and growth estimates for each product line in each selected target country, and the nearby regional markets

\- Market share estimates based on ownership and level of manufacture strategies (corporate and field personnel)

\- Sources for market information include company records and personnel, as well as external market intelligence firms (such as Economic Research Associates, Economist Intelligence Unit, Industry Associations, Government Publications, and others)

Financial Information and Assumptions

\- For the company and the offshore venture, develop schedules of required operating expenses: administrative, manufacturing, engineering, marketing and sales, employee-related, and miscellaneous.

• Financial assumptions:

Assets - Cash on hand, accounts receivable, inventories (raw materials, work-in-process, and finished goods)

Liabilities - Accounts payable, accrued labor overhead, accrued expenses, long-term debt

Total Net Investment = Assets - Liabilities Corporate Discount Rate as % After Taxes

\- Ownership parameters:

Wholly-Owned (Company - 100%)

Joint venture (Company - 49%,

Partner - 51%)

Licensing (Company - 0%,

Licensee - 100%)

The pro forma financial results of the 5,040 strategic options were compared with the corporate financial and general objectives. Those strategic options which did not meet the corporate objectives were eliminated from further evaluation. The remaining strategic options were further considered in the context of specific financial and operating risks, such as expropriation, control on profit repatriation, anticipated price controls, fluctuations in foreign exchange rates, and others. These evaluations were based on the concepts of breakeven probabilities, as discussed in international finance literature [3,11]. The DSS also included a heuristic procedure to rank each strategic option in terms of its ability to dominate the other options in terms of meeting the corporate objectives. It also led to the distillation of a portfolios of international investment options, as shown in Table 3. The portfolios were designed to organize the strategic options into strategic groups as felt necessary by the executives. Finally, the DSS included extensive menus and command files to ensure user-friendly access to information and model bases. The command files also include capability for analysis at any level of detail, including consolidated evaluation, as desired by a decision maker. The latter can be performed at the country, region, or global level, at the product level for a country, region, or global strategy, or at any desired level based on the various strategic questions.

## 4. Benefits from the DSS

The DSS described here provided several benefits to the complex process of global participation strategy formulation. The major ones are as follows:

1. The approach provided a structure to the decision process and helped managers to identify and organize information resources and to evaluate strategic options for global participation objectively using models and sensitivity analysis.

2. Through the application of a decision support framework, we identified the types of data, information, and models needed for a comprehensive and structured approach to the global participation strategy formulation process.

3. Through the case study of successful implementation at Onan Corporation, a feasible, appropriate, and effective decision support framework was developed for a complex task environment, such as the foreign direct investment decision.

4. The DSS prescribes alternative strategic options but leaves the final choice of strategies to the managers. The prescriptive nature of the DSS differs from the descriptive nature of most studies reported in the international investment literature. Our approach introduces structure to ill-structured tasks, reduces complexities through information and models, extends managers' information processing and problem solving capabilities, and provides prescriptive, rather than merely descriptive, tools under the managers' control (see [3]).

5. The approach to DSS development can easily be adopted to other manufacturing firms that wish to mobilize their information, modeling, and other resources for global participation strategies.

6. The DSS provided all benefits expected by senior executives, as studied and reported by Alavi [2]. It provides assistance to executives in identifying problems and in fast, real-time analysis of current problems and opportunities. It provided information processing and retrieval capabilities and assistance in interpreting the information and suggested decision alternatives or enables the executives to evaluate them through capabilities for sensitivity analysis and by asking “what if” questions. These DSS capabilities led to increased confidence of the executives charged with the complex decision task of developing global participation strategies for increased profitability and global competitive advantages.

## 5. Summary and Conclusions

We have demonstrated how an organization mobilized its information and modeling resources to provide comprehensive and structured decision support to a complex and unstructured decision making task environment, namely a strategy formulation for global participation. The approach described here may be adopted by other organizations needing to formulate strategies for international investment planning, etc. At Onan Corporation, the DSS triggered and sustained the effective mobilization of information, modeling, and other organizational resources for complex decision making at the top management level, leading to improved decision making and communication at all levels of the organization – some of the best measures of effectiveness of information and management science resource management and utilization in improving global competitive advantages and profitability.
