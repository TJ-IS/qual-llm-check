# The Impact of Fourth Generation Languages on Systems Development

Mary Sumner

Management Information Systems, Southern Illinois University at Edwardsville, IL, USA

Robert Benson

School of Technology and Information Management, Washington University, Pullman, WA 99164, USA

The purpose of this study was to identify the nature and scope of systems development projects in which 4GL's are being used, to compare these projects with projects in which procedural languages are being used, and to describe the benefits and limitations of 4GL's. Project managers with experience managing both traditional and 4GL projects returned questionnaires describing the size, cost, duration, scope and characteristics of these applications. The results indicated that project managers made a clear distinction between the types of projects that are appropriate for development using procedural languages and 4GL's. Procedural projects had higher transactions volumes, cost more, required more person-days, and were generally larger in scale. In contrast, 4GL projects had more variable outputs, less security requirements, less chance of audit, and less complexity. The perfect 4GL project was an inquiry and reporting system with less structured outputs, dynamic scheduling requirements, and low transactions volumes. MIS professionals recognize the benefits and limitations of 4GL's and are generally making good decisions about their use.

Keywords: Fourth generation languages, Systems development, Application development aids, Prototyping.

## 1. Background

Organizations today are facing a tough set of systems development problems. The tremendous demand for computer-based information systems has created application backlogs often ranging from two to six years. The shortage of application and systems programmers continues to be a constraint [2].

Methods of improving the productivity of the application development process include the use of software packages, the support of end-user application development, and the use of fourth generation languages (4GL) [12]. While these tools have been used extensively to support end-user computing, there is limited evidence of their use in systems development projects conducted by MIS professionals. 4GL's can be used to create programs for many applications with substantially fewer lines of code than COBOL. Features like on-line data dictionaries, libraries of re-usable modules, and pre-programmed functions can speed up the application building process. These tools can also effectively support the prototyping approach.

## 2. Objectives of the Study

A number of organizations have accumulated experience in using 4GL's over the past four to five years. This study is designed to learn the characteristics of projects in which 4GL's have been used and to provide guidelines about the types of projects in which they can be used effectively.

The specific objectives are:

(1) To identify the nature and scope of systems development projects in which 4GL's are being used.

(2) To compare the characteristics of projects using 4GL's with the characteristics of projects using procedural languages.

(3) To compare the systems development practices in projects using 4GL's with those using procedural languages.

(4) To describe the benefits and limitations of 4GL's in systems development.

## 3. Procedures for the Study

The sample for this study consisted of 300 organizations in four cities listed in the Directory of Top Computer Executives [22]. The MIS directors in each organization received a preliminary letter explaining the purpose and scope of the study. Approximately a week later, each director received two questionnaires, one asking about the characteristics of a project using a fourth generation language and the second asking similar questions about the characteristics of a project using a procedural language. They were asked to distribute the questionnaires to project managers responsible for the two types of projects.

Each questionnaire asked about project characteristics, such as cost, duration, size, and type. Respondents were asked to describe their applications, including scope, data volume, users, data retention, scheduling requirements, level of security, level of audit, system complexity, and business significance. The questionnaire also dealt with application development practices, including the time spent on the phases of the systems development life cycle, the distribution of responsibility between MIS professionals and users, and the quality assurance procedures being practiced. Respondents were also asked to describe the success factors and pitfalls of using 4GL's and to identify the characteristics of projects which they felt were suitable for them.

## 4. Findings of the Study

Thirty-three respondents with experience in both 4GL and procedural language projects returned both questionnaires. An additional ten respondents returned only procedural language questionnaires or indicated that their organizations had no experience using 4GL's in MIS systems development. This represented a response rate of 14%. The low percentage of project managers returning both 4GL project and procedural project questionnaires may indicate that many of their firms were not using 4GL's in production data processing projects.

## 5. The Fourth Generation Development Environment

A variety of different fourth generation tools was used by MIS developers. FOCUS, used in about one-third of the projects, was the most frequently reported tool. NOMAD, MANTIS, and NATURAL were used by several respondents, and seven other languages were mentioned by at least one respondent.

A number of studies have pointed to lack of training and to programmer resistance as factors preventing acceptance and effective use of 4GL tools. However, training of project staffs was considered “moderately adequate” by most respondents. In addition, the attitude of project staff members toward the use of 4GL’s was considered “supportive”.

## 6. Characteristics of 4GL and Traditional Applications

In developing the Application Approach Worksheet designed to assist application developers in selecting an appropriate approach, Shomenta, Kamp, Hanson, and Simpson evaluated the characteristics of applications which required traditional vs. 4GL development [21]. They recommended traditional development for organization-wide systems development projects with high data volume, well-define outputs, multiple workstations, and moderate to high system complexity. Projects in which security was important, audit was expected, and automated data recovery to failed transaction was necessary were also good candidates for traditional development.

One of the strengths of 4GL development is its capacity to build prototypes, or applications that can be continuously refined until users' requirement are met. Prototyping with 4GL's works well when users are uncertain about their real needs. Prototyping should be used, Jenkins argues, when an application is primarily a reporting or decision support system, or when system use is irregular [5]. Good opportunities for prototyping also exist in systems which have less complex logic, are interactive, and which are more concerned with the presentation and formatting of data [4,17].

However, prototyping with 4GL's is not suitable in all projects. When a project involves the automation of a well-defined system, when there are large data volumes, where application complexity is great, and when computations are complex, prototyping will probably not work well. Prototyping will also not work well when the information systems staff is not willing to develop prototype builders and when users are not willing to invest time in development.

In this study, project managers were asked to describe the number of users, workstations, and locations; application scope; data volume; data pertinence; data retention; data recovery procedures; and system life expectancy of their projects. Respondents were also asked to provide scheduling requirements, variability of output, level of security, level of audit, data currency, complexity of program logic, and application significance.

## 7. Application Development Practices

Fourth generation languages can be used for prototyping, followed by implementation using a procedural language; they can be used to generate code for an application once requirements have been specified; they can be used to support queries and reports for ad hoc systems development; and they can be integrated throughout an information engineering process involving information planning, data administration, and development.

There is some evidence that 4GL's can be used to speed the application development process. When prototyping is used during analysis, Alavi reports, the process of specifying requirements is shorter. When 4GL's are used during the implementation phase, productivity increases [7,8].

Respondents were asked to report on the approximate percentage of time spent on each of the phases of the systems development life cycle of 4GL systems; their estimates are shown in Table 16.

Similar percentages of time spent on various phases of the life cycle were reported also for the projects in which procedural languages were used.

Fourth generation language users did not indicate that their use saved time, particularly in detailed design and implementation. Project managers reported that they saved 25 percent of the time normally spent in detailed design and 31 percent of the time usually spent in implementation. These findings would support the view that 4GL's were primarily being used to support coding and testing in systems development projects.

Another impact of 4GL development is the re-distribution of responsibility for systems development. Users, it is felt, can work with system builders to generate and refine prototypes using 4GL's. The MIS managers responding to this study were asked to identify whether MIS professionals or users were primarily responsible for each of the steps in the systems development process in 4GL and procedural projects.

Users were responsible for problem definition and feasibility assessment in most of the projects reported, but MIS professionals were responsible for most of the other project phases. Details are given in Table 17.

## 8. Benefits and Limitations of Fourth Generation Tools

A list of productivity impacts and drawbacks of using 4GL's was developed, and respondents were asked to rank these impacts in terms of their importance.

## 8.1. The Benefits of Fourth Generation Tools

One of the main benefits cited for 4GL's is reduction in application development time. Productivity increases over COBOL development range from between 5 to 1 to 300 to 1. Case studies also report that 4GL's have been used to speed the development of systems that are urgently needed [11].

Prototyping facilitates user involvement in systems design and makes it possible to match system features with user expectations in less time than traditional methods [9]. Prototyping can decrease the time spent in analysis and design because user requirements are satisfied more rapidly [19].

Another benefit attributed to the use of 4GL's is a decrease in the time spent on the debugging and maintenance phases. Coding, testing, and debugging comprise about half of a total program's cost. Maintenance, an ongoing concern, can be alleviated by using program generators as to invoke coded modules stored in libraries and to combine them on demand to produce and to enhance applications.

Respondents were asked to rank the impacts attributed to 4GL tools in order of their importance. A rank of "1" was to indicate the most significant impact. The results were used to calculate an average rank score for each of the statements. The findings are shown in Table 19. The major positive impacts cited were consistent with the impacts most frequently described in case studies and articles.

## 8.2. The Limitations of Fourth Generation Languages

MIS professionals have consistently expressed the view that 4GL's cannot provide the performance necessary to drive major transactions systems. Although 4GL's may save time, they require 50 to 150 percent more CPU and disk resources. Others warn that 4GL's advertised as being appropriate for production data processing often run about one-third as fast as COBOL.

Another argument against 4GL's is that they foster poor design methods and lead to the development of poorly documented systems. Fourth generation language systems developed by MIS professionals were being fully documented and these tools were fully integrated into the existing life cycle methodology, which explains why these were not viewed as drawbacks. Much of the criticism about poorly documented systems applies to the use of 4GL's by end-users, not by MIS professionals.

The lack of integration of 4GL's and procedural languages may not have been a major concern because 4GL tools were primarily being used to support query and reporting subsystems. Lack of integration among 4GL products was probably not viewed as a concern because most MIS organizations had standardized on the use of one 4GL.

## 9. Elements of Project Success

Although there are reports of project successes and failures in trade literature, very few articles identify the characteristics of systems suitable for 4GL development. In one article, Cullum suggests that management must be willing to implement a project in small pieces and to use an iterative design approach to apply a 4GL successfully. Moreover, success requires adequate machine resources, a thoroughly knowledgeable development staff, and complete documentation of the final version.

When the respondents were asked if the use of 4GL's in their projects was a success, 29 of 33 said yes, one said no, and the others did not have enough experience to know. Respondents said that speed was the major reason for project success. Eight of the 33 managers reported that they were able to complete their projects in a shorter time frame with 4GL's. A number of respondents gave the ability to prototype application requirements, to meet user requirements, to support ad hoc reporting needs, and to change preliminary designs as reasons for project success.

The final question asked respondents to name three characteristics of an application development project which would make it suitable for development using a 4GL. When these answers were summarized, the major characteristics were situations where ad hoc reporting and inquiry were needed, where prototyping was desirable, where there was urgency, and where transactions volumes were low. Fourth generation tools could effectively support the development of independent, self-contained systems and of highly changeable, dynamic systems. When users were highly involved in interacting with an information system and were willing to become involved in its development, a 4GL tool could be used effectively. See Table 21.

A number of respondents pointed out that simple applications with low to moderate complexity made good 4GL projects. Other respondents noted that small-scale projects supporting a single user or a few users could be handled successfully. Only two respondents stated that experienced programmers and a development team open to new ideas were important factors.

## 10. Summary

The results of the study illustrate that project managers have made a clear distinction between the types of projects that are appropriate for development using procedural languages and for 4GL's. Procedural projects cost more, required more effort, had higher transactions volumes, had more users, and were generally larger in scale. In contrast, 4GL projects had more variable outputs, less security requirements, less chance of audit, and less complexity. The “perfect” 4GL project was an inquiry and reporting system with less structured outputs, dynamic scheduling requirements, and low complexity; standalone applications with low transactions volumes were “good” candidates.

Although most of the respondents indicated that 4GL's helped to speed development, their use did not alter the traditional systems development life cycle methodology. Full documentation and quality assurance procedures were being followed for both types of projects. User participation in systems development was primarily felt during the problem definition and feasibility study phases of the life cycle for both 4GL and procedural language projects.

## 11. Issues and Recommendations

The experiences suggest some issues relating to 4GL development, mainly dealing with project selection and system development methodology.

## 11.1. Project Selection

One serious deficiency of MIS management is the lack of recognition that different projects require different managerial approaches [18]. MIS managers and users need to examine alternative development approaches and to select methods based upon project characteristics. The successful experience of project managers may have been due to selection of projects with characteristics which made them appropriate for 4GL development. When system requirements were less well-defined, 4GL's effectively supported a prototyping approach.

## 11.2. Systems Development Methodology

Although the MIS professionals realized that the use of 4GL's made it possible to speed up systems development activities, the characteristics of the projects were quite different from those of procedural projects. The improvement in application development time could have been attributed to the difference in the nature and scope of the 4GL projects. In other words, if a 4GL had been used in the high transactions volume production systems development projects, the productivity impacts would probably not have been so obvious. Since 4GL's were not used in large projects with high transaction volumes, it could not be determined if large traditional projects would have been impacted by the use of 4GL's.

The current systems development life cycle was not deliberately altered for 4GL's. They were being plugged into the life cycle to speed up certain phases, such as prototyping and code generation. Fourth generation tools might have a greater impact on application development if a systems development methodology incorporating their use were introduced into MIS organizations.

The effective use of 4GL's requires a software development environment which combines new technology with training, procedures, and top management support. The environment must support experimentation through pilot projects. Tools, workstations, and technical support personnel must be readily available to support the environment. If a programmer has to move to a new workstation and to spend hours obtaining access to databases needed for 4GL projects, then MIS management is not fully behind 4GL development.

Although application development productivity can be improved with the use of 4GL's, their use needs to be coupled with other productivity strategies, such as developer workstations, team approaches to testing, and subroutine sharing. Re-usable libraries can be utilized effectively only if programmers are already trained in structured systems design methods.

## 12. Conclusion

In summary, if suitable projects are selected for 4GL development. MIS organizations may experience considerable success with 4GL's. Evidence indicates that project managers are deliberately selecting projects with characteristics which make them suitable for 4GL development. However, greater productivity improvements may occur if MIS organizations use systems development methodologies which support 4GL use and develop effective organizational strategies supporting their use.
