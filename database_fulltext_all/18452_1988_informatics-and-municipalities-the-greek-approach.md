# Informatics and Municipalities: The Greek Approach

Alexander B. Sideridis \*

Informatics Laboratory, Agricultural University of Athens, 75 Iera Odos street, 118 55 Athens, Greece

The design philosophy of a project aimed at the introduction of data processing into Greek municipalities is described. The various factors that influence important decisions were selected and analysed so that the end product would gradually convert manual services in local administration to fully automated procedures. Environmental factors at the national and local level, communicational flows and interconnections, and experience gained in the field were carefully considered and combined with organizational constraints in making specific recommendations for the development of a coordinated and standardized national Local Authorities Information and Organizational System (LAIOS).

Keywords: Information systems, MIS planning, Local authorities, Local governments, Municipalities, Data processing.

## 1. Introduction

Greek municipalities enjoy autonomy in certain areas of local administration, especially in providing a sewage collection for its area.

Several factors lead to a slow decentralization of processes in local government during the seventies. These included: increased awareness of the importance of the role that the local governments could play in solving local problems and the pressure exercised by a public faced with the bureaucracy resulting from a central administration, in conjunction with the problems due to variations experienced in each region of the country.

De-centralization has become a major issue which undoubtedly plays an important role in recent developments in Greek politics. The local governments have responded very well to this and the majority have showed a responsible attitude.

Decentralized procedures increase the responsibility of the local governments and the autonomy of local authorities, but, at the same time, reveal the inadequacy of the classical organizational structure in dealing with an increased number of problems and a higher volume of incoming and outgoing information. Efforts made by some local governments to introduce informatics and automation tools in their services have been unsuccessful and this has discouraged others. The failures were mainly due to lack of experience, ill-defined formulation plans, and confusion in using new technology for non-standard procedures.

The Greek Ministry of the Interior, among its other duties, is also responsible for the decentralization of the central government's activities and the coordination of those in local administrations. During the past two years, it has been planning a Management Information System (MIS) that will meet the information needs of local government.

This system is intended to support automatic decision making; the various procedures will be developed in stages and added to it. Thus, the information system will be gradually integrated into a decision support Local Authorities Information and Organizational System (des-LAIOS) [11,12].

The des-LAIOS, at a typical local government level, must satisfy the strategic, managerial, and planning needs of:

\- participating organizations of citizens, operators and administrators for every procedure [v,2];

\- meeting the users and operators requirements; and

\- feeding back to the system all the necessary information, so that new processes can be researched, explained, developed, and adapted.

## 2. LAIOS architecture

Several methodologies have been proposed for planning an MIS [2,3,5]. For LA IOS a three stage model [1,8] has been adopted to cover the major functions. The three stages are:

-- strategic information systems planning, including preplanning and formal systems planning;

\- organizational restructuring, information requirements analysis, systems development, and implementation; and

\- systems administration by the municipality and allocation of both MIS applications and operational resources.

The overall structure of the LAIOS consists of different applications at various levels of organization, such as operations, management control, and planning, as well as application of service support to the public and various management activities in local authorities, mainly those requiring control and decision making. An architecture for an Information system of this kind, should allow long range development, but also provide responses to various short range information system needs.

The system architecture should include all today's efficient information tools and facilities, such as data bases and model bases linked by a powerful decision support mechanism in the local environment (see Fig. 1).

Telecommunication software could extend the domain of application of des-LAIOS to a regional level that covers the needs of municipalities and administration of a prefecture. ## 3. Factors in LAIOS success

It is evident that a detailed plan of LAIOS should include specific targets and involve a long-range time-table. The plan should not underestimate the difficulties due to limited experience in the development of integrated MIS, the organizational obstacles which develop in the course of implementing those systems, and vulnerability due to considering implementation of a comprehensive LAIOS as having its own goals or objectives. It should also emphasize basic factors, the various technical and nontechnical ones, upon which LAIOS successful implementation will depend. The most important of these factors are:

\- it must have, throughout its implementation, continuous political support,

\- there must be municipal participation in the MIS planning phase.

\- there must be DP expertise available at a local level and purchasing of appropriate software and hardware [8].

Of course, these presumptions cannot guarantee successful development and application of a complex MIS for local authorities [7]. For example, municipal participation in the development of the system during the MIS planning phase cannot ensure that complete information requirements have been collected, since further decentralization often necessitates redetermination of information volumes and flows. Thus, the managerial staff cannot predict their future information requirements, since they may expand with decentralization. In addition, because of insufficient training, they cannot perceive the qualitative difference in available information due to the new automated procedures. As a result, there is a clear need to define processes for systematically determining information requirements of today and the future. For this reason, it has been proposed that a committee of users and experts should be established to regulate LAIOS and evaluate its performance on a regular basis.

All these problems make it difficult to produce an optimum system design and a successful implementation of LAIOS, since the number of alternative architectures is large. Thus, selection among alternatives is a problem.

## 4. Implementation Strategy

## 4.1. The LAIOS Time-table

LAIOS integrates computer applications and the way decisions are made and feeds back information to the various operational systems. These systems are classified into:

\- Operational Support Systems (OSS) designed for the automation of daily activities. The OSS provide all basic accounting (financial, personnel administration, etc) and various citizen services;

\- Management Control Systems (MCS) to provide access to population and industry registers, social services, land use profiles, budget management, and local tax collection; and

\- Planning Systems (PS) designed to assist management in evaluation of future alternatives.

The OSS have been given first priority. The first phase of the program started last year and OSS have since been analysed and application software developed under UNIX and MS-DOS. The development and distribution of these packages to the data processing centres of the interested municipalities will be controlled by a special public enterprise called the “Hellenic Agency for Local Development and Local Government (EETAA)”; its board of directors includes members of unions in the central municipalities.

The time-table of the program for the full operation of the OSS, MCS, and PS is subject to availability of necessary funds. The S-shaped curve, with which implementation is often represented, shows the variation of data processing expenditure over time [10]. The first turning point shows the end of the initiation stage, i.e., the completion of the preplanning and formal system planning phase; the second turning point occurs at the start of integration of the OSS, MCS and PS; the third point represents the end of LAIOS consolidation (see Fig. 2).

Since the availability of skilled personnel is an important factor, a training and recruiting program has been devised. This encompasses re-training of existing management and administrative personnel and encouraging computer scientists from local universities to participate in the project. ## 4.3. Implementation Issues

The technical characteristics of LAIOS and the implementation methodology for the identification of the hardware and software needs of the individual local authorities are fully described in a recently published study [4]. This study is based on a detailed investigation of the present characteristics and information processing needs of the local administrations. The investigation covered practically all local authorities of the country and it was carried out through personal interviews on sight and detailed questionnaires [12]. These questionnaires were completed by more than 95% of all local authorities. The accuracy of the data collected has been certified by the local councils.

The findings and recommendations of the study involve:

\- the overall architecture and technical details of LAIOS,

\- the demographic characteristics for an appropriate classification of the local authorities,

\- the type, structure and technical specifications of the computer systems required by each category of local authority,

\- the appropriate methodology for the development of the necessary application software,

\- the bodies which will provide the necessary technical support, and

\- the detailed implementation plan and costs involved.

These have been fully adopted by the Ministry of the Interior and put into practice.

Thus, the software of the most important to the local governments, operational support systems, has already been developed and is at the moment under test. It is expected that it will soon be available to 50 pilot municipalities selected according to certain social, economic and technical criteria. At the same time hardware purchasing for the pilot local authorities is well under way.

## 5. Conclusion

LAIOS is intended to provide better service to the public through the design of an MIS for local governments. Towards that end, reorganization of local administration is necessary; this is to include the efficient use of new information tools.

From our analysis we conclude that, under LAIOS, it is possible to handle the administrative needs of local governments. The detailed programs and the methodological steps proposed for the implementation of the system allow the progressive development of a highly effective information system suitable for today's decentralized local government with autonomy and self-sufficiency.
