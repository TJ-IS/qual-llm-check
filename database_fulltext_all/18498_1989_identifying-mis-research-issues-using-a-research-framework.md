# Identifying MIS Research Issues Using a Research Framework

Narasimhaiah Gorla

Computer and Information Science Department, Cleveland State University, Cleveland, OH 44115, USA

A research framework is important in identifying the research areas of a discipline. A comprehensive framework is proposed to identify the MIS research issues: a $5 \times 5$ matrix; it has MIS core activities as one dimension and MIS allied fields as the other. The previously proposed frameworks are mapped into this. Some example research hypotheses are made using the framework, and it is used to determine the areas with inadequate research. The statistics for each of the 25 cells for the period 1979–1987 are obtained using Dialog, a computer-aided database. A regression analysis is used to find the growth of each cell of the matrix: most is done in the technological issues of system design and construction. The areas with the least research effort are economic aspects of MIS use/effect, economical aspects of information system operation, and quantitative analysis in MIS management.

Keywords: Information Systems - Models and Principles - General, Frameworks, Management Information Systems, MIS, Research Hypotheses.

## 1. Introduction

Management Information Systems is an applied field and is interdisciplinary in nature. Much of the subject matter has come from other fields. Information systems lie at the intersection of three main disciplinary branches: exact sciences (such as general systems theory, control theory, decision theory, management science, statistics), technology (e.g. electrical engineering, computer science), and social and behavioral sciences (e.g. sociology, cognitive psychology, management theory, organization theory, economics, political science, organizational behavior) [1]. The content of MIS changed as the supporting fields grew. For example, with the advent of fourth generation languages and relational data base systems, prototyping has become a prominent systems development methodology.

Although the influence of allied fields on an area is a common phenomenon, this is especially true in the case of MIS. Hence it is important to distinguish the activities of the MIS from those of allied fields and to have a framework which is broad enough to encompass various MIS activities.

A research framework has the benefit of identifying research issues and the relationships among these issues. It aids both researchers and practitioners in describing the scope of MIS research [2]. A research framework should ideally possess the following characteristics: (i) it should be concise but complete, (ii) its represented research issues should be mutually exclusive, and (iii) it should identify the fruitful research areas yet to be explored.

The objectives of this paper are:

(i) to propose a comprehensive research framework which encompasses most of the research areas of MIS;

(ii) to use the framework to identify the neglected MIS research areas, which need more effort.

The proposed framework is a $5 \times 5$ matrix with one dimension being MIS core activities and the other allied fields that interact with MIS. Previously proposed frameworks are mapped into the proposed framework. Research distribution is shown across the cells, using data on MIS research publications.

## 2. Previous Research in MIS Frameworks

A number of research frameworks have been proposed in the past. Each of these provide an important vehicle for research in the specific MIS areas.

Mason and Mitroff [14] suggest a program of research in MIS by identifying five general considerations for any MIS design. These are: psychological type, class of problem, method of evidence, organizational context, and modes of presentation. They point out that much of the previous research has assumed one psychological type, one class of problem, one or two methods of generating evidence, and one mode of presentation. Their model presents alternative subcategories within each variable. The model focuses on the use and effectiveness of information in decision making.

Gorry and Scott-Morton [8] state “the framework we develop here is one for managerial activities, not for information systems. It is a way of looking at decisions made in an organization”. They categorized managerial decisions into operational control, management control, and strategic planning on one dimension and into structured, semistructured and unstructured decisions on the other. Clearly the emphasis of this framework is on managerial decision making.

More recently, Nolan and Wetherbe [17] presented an MIS research framework. The authors classify the MIS field into five categories using the systems approach: MIS input, MIS technology, MIS output, MIS feedback, and MIS environment. The authors themselves state that the drawback of their framework is that it is extremely broad. Subjectivity is involved when trying to fit the MIS literature into these categories, because they are too general.

Mock [16] identifies five classes of variables that have an effect on the performance of the decision maker: individual/psychological variables, organizational/interpersonal variables, sociological/environment variables, and information variables and their subcategories. Many of these variables are uncontrollable from the system designer's point of view. The model is mainly concerned with the behavioral aspects of decision makers and the effect of behavioral variables on the system design.

Thus most of the past work describe detailed analysis of MIS research in specific areas.

## 3. Development of the Framework

Core activities of MIS: According to Gordon Davis [6], a Management Information System is an integrated man/machine system for providing information to support the operation, management and decision making functions in an organization. MIS research is the systematic investigation of the development, operation, use, and/or impact of an information system in an organizational environment. There are several similar descriptions of MIS available. Nunamaker et al. [18] in their report of the ACM curriculum committee on Information Systems describe the activities that are normally performed by Information Systems graduates in organizations as a basis for an MIS curriculum. These activities include assisting in defining/planning information systems, eliciting information requirements for applications, assisting in designing the systems, implementing information systems applications, and managing information system development and operation.

Also, the primary objective of conducting research in MIS and the developing M.I.S. curriculum is to aid in improving the Information System function in industry. Thus the core activities that characterize Information Systems research are systems definition/analysis, systems design/construction, systems operation/maintenance, systems management/planning, and systems use/effect.

Information Systems is inter-disciplinary and lies at the intersection of certain branches and sub-branches: organizational behavior (behavioral science), computer science, operations research, economics, and functional management. This list represents the fields having high interaction with MIS core activities. It is justified by considering the type of research normally published in top quality MIS journals. Scott Hamilton and Blake Ives [9] listed in their paper the top 10 MIS journals. They are Management Science, MIS quarterly, Communications of the ACM, Decision Sciences, Transactions on Database Systems, Information & Management, Sloan Management Review, Academy of Management Journal, Computing Surveys, and Accounting Review. These journals normally publish MIS research oriented towards operations research or simulation, behavioral sciences, management disciplines, and accounting. Management disciplines and accounting are covered in the proposed framework as functional management and economics/costing. Functional management represents the branches of business administration such as accounting, marketing, finance, production, etc.. We are interested in the intersections of these fields and MIS. We will explore the linkages between MIS core activities and allied fields to describe MIS research issues.

## 4. Description of the Proposed Framework

Our M.I.S. research framework can be represented as a matrix with MIS core activities as one dimension and MIS allied fields as the other dimension (Table 1). Each MIS activity includes several sub-activities as indicated below:

System definition/analysis:

Feasibility study, information requirement analysis, logical and physical system description.

System design/construction:

Computerized system design, database design, programming, testing, procedure development.

System operation/maintenance:

Conversion, training users, system implementation and maintenance, operation.

System management/planning:
Information systems planning, Management of

EDP resources, project management, MIS policies and ethics.

System effect/use:
Evaluation of Information systems, impact of information systems.

The other dimension of the framework is the set of allied disciplines which consist of the following sub-areas:

Behavioral Science/Psychology: Organizational behavior, sociology, cognitive psychology, group dynamics, social psychology, political science.

Computer Science/Computer Engineering: Computer hardware, software, mainframe, mini, and micro computers, office technology, robotics, computer networks, fourth and fifth generation languages, artificial intelligence, database management systems.

Operations Research/Quantitative Analysis: Linear and non-linear programming, integer programming, PERT/CPM, inventory models, stochastic processes, probability theory, simulation.

Functional Management (Accounting, Production etc)
Accounting, Finance, Production, Purchasing, Personnel, Marketing, Sales, Strategic Management, Middle and Operational Management.

Economics/Costing:
Demand, supply, game theory, pricing, and econometrics.

## 5. Significance of this Framework

Our proposed framework covers most of the MIS research issues. The significance of the framework is as follows:

(i) Through the two dimensional structure, it is easier to describe the relationship among the research issues.

(ii) The framework can be used to form research hypotheses concerning the MIS issues at the intersecting cells.

(iii) The distribution of current MIS research among the cells can be obtained, identifying the cells where there are research deficiencies.

## 6. Research Issues with MIS Research Framework

MIS Core Activity Research: The research issues corresponding to the MIS activity of System Design/Construction are: system design, programming, testing, and documentation; they are basically computer implementation issues. Those research issues related to behavioral sciences can be formalized into research hypotheses and are suited for hypothesis testing. Some example hypotheses are:

(1) System designs that are produced by designers who are more adaptable tend to be more flexible and loosely coupled than those produced by the non-adaptable designers.

(2) Programmers who are involved in both design and programming will have higher job satisfaction and thus produce better quality systems than programmers involved only in programming.

The research hypotheses relating to the computer science discipline are mostly of a technical nature. Some research hypotheses could be:

(1) The optimum database fragmentation design on micro computers is different from that on mini computers, and from the design on mainframe computers.

(2) The structured program development approach results in lower development cost than an unstructured approach.

(3) The use of fourth generation languages will increase programmer productivity over lower level languages; program debugging is faster with the use of fourth generation languages than with lower level languages.

The issues related to Operations Research/Quantitative methods are of a prescriptive nature. Most prescribes the values of variables, so that either cost is minimized or benefit is maximized. Example research questions are:

(1) What is the optimum inventory order quantity below which the orders are to be generated automatically by a computer?

(2) What are the optimum locations for data and programs in information system networks for the least communication costs?

(3) What is the optimum packaging of the system modules for the least overall response time?

Some issues related to Functional Management areas are:

(1) Design of interface between functional management information systems.

(2) Integration of office automation and accounting information systems.

(3) Will the use of artificial intelligence in DSS design result in quality systems?

The questions related to economics/costing are:

(1) What are the characteristics of systems for which custom development is cost effective? What are the characteristics of systems for which the use of prepackaged software is cost effective?

(2) What are good methods of charging the users for system development? What method is best suited for a particular type of organizational environment?

(3) In what situations is it economical to use distributed rather than centralized database design?

MIS Allied Field Research: Computer Science/Engineering is taken as an allied field for discussion of the research. MIS issues related to this field are mostly technical.

The research issues of system definition/analysis are characterized by the effects of new computer technology on the information requirement analysis process, and the impact of hardware and software advancements. Some research hypotheses are:

(1) The use of interactive fourth generation languages and end user computing will produce more precise system definition than third generation languages.

(2) The use of prepackaged software for automating system development will result in less precise information requirement specification than manual processes, such as interviewing, questionnaire use, etc.

(3) Development and use of an expert system to specify information requirements in unstructured problems will result in more precise system definition than conventional methods.

The research issues of system operation/maintenance are characterized by the effect of advanced computer technology on the data processing operations and information system maintenance activities. Hypotheses are:

(1) The response time in decentralized data processing operations is less than in centralized operations.

(2) Information systems with natural language interfaces are easier to operate than systems with conventional interfaces.

(3) The use of prepackaged training software will provide better training of system users than conventional training by instructors.

The research issues of system management/planning are characterized by effects of varied computer hardware and software on data processing resources management, computer center management, data processing center policies, etc. These include:

(1) Policy development regarding the purchase of compatible hardware and software in line with the long range plan of the information system center.

(2) Differences in MIS manager's management strategies in the modern systems environment (with end user computing and micro computers) and the traditional systems (centralized batch processing mainframe computers).

The research issues of the cell with information system use/effect are characterized by the following:

(1) In an end-user computing environment, the more experience the users gain, the better the quality of their system.

(2) As user friendly languages developed by software companies grow, the number of application programmers required by commercial organizations decrease.

## 7. MIS Research Distribution and Research Growth

The MIS research statistics were obtained using Dialog Version 2, a computer-aided Database. ABI/INFORM, File #15, because of its management orientation, was used to get research statistics in Behavioral MIS, Functional MIS, and Economics of MIS. INSPEC, File #13, because of its technical and quantitative orientation, was used to obtain computer science oriented MIS and quantitative MIS research statistics. The number of research articles published each year in each cell of the framework during the years 1979–87 were obtained. A regression analysis was used to estimate the research growth in each cell of the framework and to determine the corresponding significance levels.

## 8. Summary and Conclusions

A Comprehensive MIS research framework has been presented in the form of a $5 \times 5$ matrix; one dimension consists of MIS core activities, while the other dimension consists of MIS allied fields. Some research hypotheses were formulated using the framework. MIS research statistics were obtained for each of the cells of the framework for the years 1979 through 1987, using a computerized Database. Using these statistics, MIS research growth rates were obtained for each cell using a regression analysis.
