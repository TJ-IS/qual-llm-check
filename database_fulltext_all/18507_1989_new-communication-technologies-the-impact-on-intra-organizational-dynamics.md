# New Communication Technologies: The Impact on Intra-Organizational Dynamics

Steve D. Ugbah

Department of Marketing, School of Business & Economics
California State University, Hayward, CA 94542, USA

Sue Dewine

School of Interpersonal Communications, Ohio University, Athens, OH 45701, USA

This study empirically investigated the impact of new communication technology (NCT) on intra-organizational dynamics. Specifically, it examined whether the introduction of NCT had any impact on organizational variables such as perceived information adequacy or abundance, perceived general organizational climate, and the nature of NCT utilization in the workplace. Managers more than non-managers reported a positive change in perceived organizational climate and information adequacy, and also reported using NCT more for task-related purposes.

Keywords: New Communication Technologies, Computer Mediated Communication Systems, Intra-organizational Dynamics, Organizational Climate, Information Adequacy.

## 1. Issues and Definitions

With the “settling-in” of new communication technologies (NCT) such as videotext, teletext, teleconferencing, interactive computing, and facsimile in organizations, focus has now shifted from merely examining the systems design factors affecting the implementation of these technologies in the workplace, to the impact NCT have on intra-organizational dynamics. Undoubtedly, as we approach the twenty-first century, the challenges facing designers will be those of successfully integrating the individual and NCT into the organization. Numerous assertions have been made about the major effect on the intra- and inter-organizational communication dynamics $[5,8,9,12]$ . Some of these organizational processes include the alteration of organizational climate, information adequacy, and job content.

In this paper, organizational climate implies a set of attributes than can be perceived about a particular organization and/or its subsystems and which may be induced from the way the organization deals with its members and environment. An organization is the rational coordination of activities of a number of people to achieve some common purpose or goal, through division of labor and function, and through a hierarchy of authority and responsibility. It may also refer to a company, a specific unit in a company, a governmental body, or a business unit.

Information adequacy is used to express the extent to which individuals within organizations perceive being sufficiently informed about instrumental and social aspects of their organizational life. Therefore, in the context of NCT, information adequacy implies an increase to a sufficiently high level of information acquired about aspects of an organization as a result of the introduction of NCT. Components of information adequacy or abundance include: organization-wide matters, personal performance, policies and benefits, and social information.

The intent of this study was to examine the impact of the introduction of NCTs on intra-organizational dynamics. Specifically, it examined (a) the relationship between perceived information adequacy, and task-related and socio-emotional uses of NCT, (b) the relationship between perceived general organizational climate, and task-related and socio-emotional uses of NCT, and (c) the relationship among perceived information adequacy, perceived organizational climate, uses (task-related and socio-emotional) of NCT, and position level.

## 2. NCT in Organizational Settings

NCT are computer-based technologies that involve relatively separate components manageable through traditional mechanisms. Such systems include transaction processing, data management, and configuration of hardware for internal purposes. The data processing function is responsible for meshing the components with the company's activities. However, all this changes when radical changes occur in the way business is conducted. Since effective and efficient information management seem to be keys to business success, an organization's business plan must interact with its technology plan. Consequently, an array of computer networks is generally now in place, requiring management to look out from the technology to the environment instead of concentrating on the relatively parochial concerns that are inherent in organizational information processing.

Some studies have indicated that NCT promote decentralization and enhance organizational communication $[10,19,20]$ . In addition to facilitating the dissemination of information to geographically dispersed groups, NCT perform an integrating function by merging training design, production, and evaluation technologies into broader areas of management. These activities are predicated upon the users' perceptions of the adequacy of the information they receive about relevant aspects of their jobs and of any change in the climate of their organizations since the introduction of NCT.

## 3. Information Adequacy / Abundance

The integration of communication and other organizational variables has been a source of concern for organizational theorists $[16,21,25]$ . This integration function, in the form of organizational information processing capabilities, is characterized by the gathering, interpreting, and synthesizing of data $[17]$ . The effectiveness of organizations depends upon their ability to match information processing requirements with their information processing capacity $[26]$ . This match is a function of the interplay of the internal and external systems of an organization. The external system involves aspects of the system dictated by the environment, whereas the internal system refers to the elaboration and development of relationship by system incumbents.

Most people view organizations as pyramids, in which position is marked by hierarchical status and horizontal function. NCT not only allow us to see organizations more abstractly as systems for information processing, but they have become vehicles for organizational redesign. The elimination of spatial constraints and redistribution of data have created new couplings between previously separate entities, thereby blurring the boundaries between units and between the organization and its environment. Along with this redefinition of lines of communication is the issue of the adequacy of the information that organizational members get through NCT to become more productive on the job.

## 4. Organizational Climate

Organizational climate has always been an elusive concept. Nevertheless, its effect on organizational productivity cannot be overestimated. The nature and growing trends towards large size, complexity, and demand for greater efficiency have required organizations to engage in “communication performance” at unprecedented levels of excellence [11]. One of the main demands, therefore, is the increasing necessity for an organization climate compatible with the psychic needs of its members. Communication skills and techniques are important factors to be considered in creating an effective organization. However, a more crucial factor is organizational members’ perceptions of the “climate” [18] created by management, and the clarity with which responsibility is defined. A poor climate could produce physical and emotional symptoms of stress which would adversely affect individual and organizational performance. It is pertinent to mention that preoccupation with the proper functioning of NCT has precluded focus on another issue: the NCT must also function to befit the style and organizational climate of its users.

## 5. Uses of New Communication Technologies

It is a truism that managers and professionals spend a large proportion of their time in communication-related activities $[1,22]$ . However, with the proliferation of NCT, both technical and non-technical organization members also have access to communication systems. The type of use of NCT and its purpose indicate the nature of organizational information processing.

It has been assumed that managers and other professionals use NCT for predominantly task-related communication. Some researchers, however, have observed significant socio-emotional use $[13,33]$ . In spite of insights into its pattern of use, particularly task-related use, which includes scheduling meetings and appointments, and monitoring the progress of projects, the socio-emotional significance is still controversial $[14]$ . Not all communication within an organization is task-related, neither is the use of NCT. Such social activities as keeping in touch with other organizational members, participating in entertaining conversations, and playing games, are commonly found.

## 6. The Study

## 6.2. Hypotheses

In assessing the impact of NCT on intra-organizational dynamics, the following hypotheses are tested:

$H_{1}$ : Perceived information adequacy will be structurally related to both task-related and socio-emotional uses of NCT; organizational members who utilize NCT for both purposes will report a change in their levels of information adequacy on organization-wide matters, their performance in the organization, organizational policies and benefits, and social information.

$H_{2}$ : Perceived general organizational climate will be moderated by both uses of NCT.

$H_{3}$ : The internal system variable, position level (managers vs. non-managers), will have a moderating effect on perceived information adequacy, perceived general organizational climate, and uses of NCT, with managers reporting more use for task-related purposes than non-managers.

## 6.2. Research Method

## 6.2.1. Sample and Procedures

One hundred and twenty-two NCT users from four organizations participated in this study. They included two government agencies in Ohio, a university library unit and a medical laboratory unit of a hospital, both in Pennsylvania. The common thread that ran through all these organizations was that they all employed modern information technology, which included: central data processing, computerized messaging systems, use of facsimile transmission, and a system of distributed terminals. The criterion for choosing an organization was that it utilized at least one of the following functions: messaging, conferencing, bulletin boards, etc. All the organizations employed more than one function in their electronic communications.

## 6.2.2. Measurement

Participants were administered a questionnaire designed to elicit responses on their perceptions of information adequacy, organizational climate since the introduction of NCT, and whether or not they used NCT for both task-related and socio-emotional purposes.

Information adequacy was operationalized as the composite raw scores on 18 items-9 reflecting instrumental dimensions of an organization and the other 9 reflecting the social dimension [2,3,4,6,7]. These items were scored on a seven-point Likerttype scale. General organization climate was operationalized as a composite score on a 20-item instrument [15]. Task-related and socio-emotional use of NCT was operationalized utilizing 16 items [24]. Both general organizational climate and uses were anchored on a five-point Likert-type scale. Items used to measure information adequacy, general organizational climate, and uses of NCT could not be presented here due to space constraints.

## 6.3. Results

## 6.3.1. Hypothesis 1

Results indicated a strong structural relationship between uses of NCT variables (task-related and socio-emotional) and perceived information adequacy variables (organization-wide), personal performance, policies and benefits, and social). Root 1 canonical variates were highly significantly correlated ( $R_{c}=0.88$ , $R_{c}^{2}=0.77$ ; chi-square = 260.32, $df=8$ , $p<0.00$ ). Total redundancy for uses of NCT variables (Set 1) given perceived information adequacy variables (Set 2) was 0.80; and total redundancy for Set 2 given Set 1 was 0.58.

Root 2 canonical variates also indicate a significant correlation. However, the redundancy index for Root 2 indicated that the two sets contained little overlapping information regardless of direction. Total redundancy for Set 1 given Set 2 was 0.07; and total redundancy for Set 2 given Set 1 was 0.10. Since the interest of this study was the degree to which the two sets spanned the same measurement space (redundancy), the analysis was terminated with Root 1.

Specifically, organizational members who indicated that they utilized NCT for both task-related and socio-emotional use also tended to feel adequately informed about organization-wide matters, personal performance, policies and benefits, and social matters since the introduction of NCT.

## 6.3.2. Hypothesis 2

Results of this analysis tended to indicate that general organizational climate was positively correlated with task-related use $r=0.51, p<0.001$ , and moderately correlated with socio-emotional use $r=0.39, p<0.001$ of NCT. Stepwise multiple regression indicated that task-related use $R=0.51, R^{2}=0.26, \text{Std. Err.}=16.00, \text{Beta}=0.43, F=17.79, p<0.01$ appeared to play a more significant role than socio-emotional use $R=0.39, R^{2}=0.15, \text{Std. Err.}=17.09, \text{Beta}=0.12, F=21.64, p<0.01$ in explaining perceived general organizational climate. Both variables accounted for 26 per cent of the common variance.

## 6.3.3. Hypothesis 3

One-way MANOVA indicated a significant relationship between position level (managers vs. non-managers) and the study variables (Wilks' lambda = 0.80, df = 7/109, F = 4.01, p < 0.001) on the research variables. Univariate tests indicated that managers differed from non-managers regarding their perceptions on all the study variables (p < 0.01), except socio-emotional use of NCT. An examination of the cell and marginal means indicated that both managers and non-managers were receiving less than desired information on personal performance, policies and benefits, and social aspects of their organization since the introduction of NCT. Furthermore, managers more than non-managers reported experiencing some change in the general organizational climate as a result of their introduction.

## 7. Discussion and Implications

From a pragmatic perspective, several implications of these findings may be stated. First, the general proposition that NCT is used for predominantly task-related but not precluding socio-emotional communication has been supported by the findings. It seems that the climate of the organization affects its communication processes. With the introduction of NCT, the status of “information brokers” is also changing. The accessibility of NCT to both technical and non-technical users seems to be responsible for this. Since managers were the main non-technical component of the organizations, it is no surprise that they perceived some change as a result of the introduction of NCT.

Second, the maintenance of interpersonal relationships in high information technology environments did not seem to pose any problems, since organizational members made conscious efforts to maintain interpersonal relationships.

Finally, other issues may perhaps offer some explanation. First, organizations operate under the false assumption that NCT are functionally automated rather than just an upgrading of the everyday office environment. This may offer some explanation regarding the managers' perceptions of the impact of NCT. Second, NCT are essentially a communication tool for performing mechanized routines, such as visual display of messages while the user does the editing or revising. Third, so far NCT have been used for predominantly task-related work though socio-emotional use has also been demonstrated. The reasons for these observations are not far-fetched when one considers the nature of NCT. They are an integration of software and hardware which collect and process data; inherent in this is the problem of understanding the events represented on the screens. The data being processed are flawed because they exclude sensory information, feelings, and context, which are necessary for accurate perception in organizational life.

Although generalizations beyond the organizations under study should not be made, this study has demonstrated the intrinsic value of introducing new communication technology. Any meaningful discussion of the results is tempered by the obvious limitations of survey research. However, as an integral aspect of office automation, NCT are perceived as affecting intra-organizational communication processes. It is evident that NCT is changing intra-organizational dynamics and these changes are perceived as positive by managers.

## 8. Suggestions for Further Research

This study did not make any cross-organizational comparisons. Since the organizations surveyed in this study utilize different forms of new communications technologies, it is also quite conceivable that they would react to their particular systems in a unique manner. Therefore, a potential research area exists in making such cross-organizational comparisons.

Second, the ex post facto nature of this study precludes it from attempting to establish a cause and effect relationship. A longitudinal study starting from the initial installation of NCT can be monitored from the point of conception to final implementation, then the impact of such systems on intra-organizational dynamics can be more readily discerned. The authors of this paper are currently engaged in that endeavor.

Finally, the data analyses tools used in this study, for example, the canonical correlation analysis and the analysis of variance techniques also do not lend themselves to establishing a causal relationship. Other analytical tools such as recursive path analysis may be better suited to establish this causal relationship.
