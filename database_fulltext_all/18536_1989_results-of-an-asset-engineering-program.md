# Results of an Asset Engineering Program

M.E. Swanson and S.K. Curry

GTE Data Services, Inc., One East Telecom Parkway, Tampa, FL 33637, USA

In the past decade, many software development companies have investigated the reuse of assets, while others established asset management programs. GTE Data Services (GTEDS) entered into a corporate Asset Management Program in 1986, which is dedicated to the development and implementation of reusable information system components (assets) that are consistent with the corporate architectural framework, and that provide for productivity improvements. An asset is defined as a qualified entity that, through its reuse, improves quality, provides a competitive edge, and reduces software development and support costs. Probable categories for asset classification include user interfaces, system software, data resources, network gateways, and end user computing facilities. We identified the three key ingredients for the success of our program to be: (1) quality of assets, (2) availability of information regarding the assets, and (3) continual addition and development of assets. In answer to these requirements we developed a Candidate Asset Checklist, which serves for asset nomination and assurance of the quality of those nominated assets; an on-line Asset Shelf Catalog System, which is a faceted classification and rapid retrieval mechanism for asset information; and, assets are now being developed within the Asset Management Program's department. We have realized a cost savings in excess of \$2,000,000 since the program's inception, and currently have 227 assets on the shelf.

Keywords: Reuse, Software asset, Candidate Asset Checklist, Architectural framework, Cost benefit analysis, Asset Shelf.

In 1899, Henry Ford experienced serious problems with a deliverable date for the first commercial vehicle to be used by the United States Post Office. Not only was he slipping with that deliverable, but his partners had made an announcement that a number of “phaeton automobiles” would be ready in a short period of time. All of these vehicles were custom built and the inexperience of his assembly staff was a minor problem compared to the development problems being encountered. [1]

By 1908, Ford had established mass production of his automobiles through simplification of design, precision machining, carefully timed speed, continuous motion, and use of the most ingenious labor saving mechanism . . . standardization of parts and component reuse!

By 1986, the Ford Motor Company was capable of building millions of automobiles a year for less than \$10,000 by using “off the shelf” components. In the same year, Lambroghini custom built a few Jalaphas (which sell for \$90,000) for their lower income clients. Those purchasers who want custom built automobiles must endure a lengthy build time, costly and often inaccessible maintenance, and pay top dollar in purchase price.

In its infancy, the software development industry primarily consisted of companies that “custom built”, but many are now moving toward standard “off the shelf” components.

## 1. Initial Step

For the past decade, major software development companies have investigated the reuse of assets. [2] Some are continuing to investigate its feasibility while others have implemented reuse programs. GTE Data Services (GTEDS) is one of these; it entered into a corporate Asset Management Program that has proven to be both innovative and cost effective.

A software asset is an entity that, through its reuse, improves quality, provides a competitive edge, and reduces development and support costs. Assets are not limited to program code, procedures, or techniques. A few examples of probable categories for software asset classification are:

\- User Application Components

\- User Interfaces

\- System Software

\- Data Resources

• Distributed Processing Components

• Network Gateways

\- Communication Facilities

• Network Management

\- Application Design/Development Tools

• End User Computing Facilities

Companies do realize cost savings through reuse of these assets; however, to be fully effective, an efficient program for maintenance, support and retrieval is required. [3] Though GTEDS has reused assets in the past, they were limited and the process was uncontrolled. A corporate Asset Management Program was necessary to efficiently utilize these assets and to realize the full impact of potential cost savings. These programs require an organization dedicated to the development and maintenance of reusable assets. Proper management requires:

(a) identification and classification of existing assets;

(b) modification of the assets, as necessary, to a more generic state;

(c) development of new assets;

(d) structured help/support for users; and

(e) administrative control, including an inventory catalog and its company-wide distribution.

Based upon the findings of a special Task Force formed in 1985, GTEDS formed a group known as Information Asset Engineering (IAE). This group is responsible for implementing the corporate Asset Management Program. The program now has a catalog of 227 assets, which are currently available for use in the Tampa, Florida facility and will be available to other GTE locations in the near future.

## 2. Task Force Findings

The Task Force consisted of high level executives from GTEDS, and was formed to study the impact of assets on the future of GTEDS technology. [4] At the conclusion of their efforts, three potential asset types were identified: Software Tools; Shared Data Base; and Shared Applications Software. Based upon the financial prediction of these asset types, a corporate asset program was recommended. Task Force findings were that:

\- Joint planning across department function boundaries was the key to product development performance.

\- The program was critical to ensure quality products in an efficient and time saving manner.

The long range goal was asset development, storage, and reuse support. [5]

The architectural framework for asset management was defined as follows:

"A consistent set of rules implemented by reusable technologies and controlled by management policy, or philosophy, that defines the way to manage a task in an organized manner in support of the business mission."

This requires standard protocols, standard interfaces, standard design, and standard assets. The architecture did not specify the way they were to be used. The Task Force defined architectural objectives required to:

\- Simplify development.

\- Reduce development costs.

\- Improve responsiveness.

\- Improve effectiveness.

\- Facilitate new market thrusts (maximum leverage of assets).

\- Provide technological flexibility.

\- Provide a foundation for integration of multiple projects over time.

The Task Force also determined three major criteria for implementation of a corporate Asset Management Program:

(1) Acceptance of the idea

\- Reduce operational costs.

(2) Commitment of resources (3) Willingness to accept change within the corporate culture.

## 4.2 Phase II

Phase II (July 1986) organization requirements are shown in Figure 4.

The workplan, developed during Phase I, primarily consisted of re-evaluation: (a) to determine if previously rejected assets would have reuse value with slight modifications and/or enhancements and, if so, (b) to determine the amount of effort it would require to bring them up to reuse standards.

During this time, IAE realized the need for system documentation that detailed the way to reuse and integrate individual assets. This would also cover general asset description, input to asset, detailed functional description, output from the asset, and environmental requirements, such as operating system and hardware.

The necessary changes to the assets were identified. In some cases, a well functioning routine was rewritten into a language supported by the architecture, and in other cases the routine was repackaged to be easier to use. Code was also modified to increase its quality.

The need for an asset catalog emerged as the assets were identified and qualified. The catalog design is based upon software classification research. [7] The Asset Shelf Catalog System (ASCS) is an on-line, faceted classification and retrieval mechanism for asset information. The ASCS has been developed and beta tested with implementation scheduled for September 1988, pending completion of maintenance tools development.

## 4.2.1 The Faceted Classification System

The Faceted Classification System allows the user to qualify his/her need, to state a broad request, and then “zoom” into a precise request. The facets are those attributes through which a search is conducted.

When a request is made, and the values of the attributes (facets) are specified, the system conducts a search of the database and supplies the requestor with a list of all available assets that possess the requestor's specification.

When dealing with faceted classification, less specifications provides more listings of potential assets, and more specifications give less potential assets, but those items listed will meet all requirements.

An overview of a faceted classification system resembles a diamond; the assets being the center and the various attributes being the facets.

The values of the attributes are reached by traveling through the attributes toward the center of the diamond.

Faceted classification for software and information allows the user to specify the desired asset type, the attributes the asset must possess, and the values of the attributes. While designing the database for the ASCS, IAE performed an analysis to determine the attributes (facets) that seemed most essential for system searches. ## 4.3 Phase III

The Phase III organization is very similar to Phase II, but it consists of 5 functional areas: Asset Support, Asset Acquisition, Configuration Management, Promotion, Funding, and Asset Development.

Asset Support involves Administration, Data/Telecommunications, PC Assets, Retrieval Systems, and Single Function/Service Routines; it has responsibility for help, training, maintenance, enhancements, and coordination of brokered support. Asset Acquisition has responsibility for new asset development, brokered development and existing asset qualification. Configuration Management is responsible for catalog management, library management, order and delivery handling, and record keeping. Promotion includes asset reuse through deployment, information announcements, advertising, and press releases. [8] Funding deals with the financial model, brokered development funding, reuse metrics and budget tracking and control.

With the Phase III organization in place, IAE has moved into asset development within the IAE unit, as well as brokered to other systems development.

## 4.3.1 Three Approaches for Developing an Asset

In order to achieve good results, IAE found that a multi-dimensional implementation strategy was necessary (see Figures 7 and 8).

Vertical implementation occurs when existing assets of numerous functional types are provided for repeated reuse within a project. Horizontal implementation is when an asset is generic enough to be reused by multiple projects. Cross environmental implementation adds the dimension of crossing multiple hardware technologies.

The cross environmental implementation is especially important in solving the problems of a heterogeneous technical architecture. For example, an Application Communication Manager asset was developed to allow DEC, TANDEM, and IBM peer servers to route transactions within a network.

## 4.3.2 Design for Asset Development

Both IAE and brokered asset development are performed according to Development Standards.

For assets to be accepted, they must have certain characteristics. These are the “Reusability Factors”, which include:

Multiple reuse - The candidate must be able to be potential reused at least twice.

Functionality - Regardless of size, scope, or complexity, the function should be general. If an asset candidate has features that are specific to a certain application, it is difficult, if not impossible, to reuse.

Architectural Support – The asset candidate must support the Telephone Operations Information Management architecture.

For all developments, IAE conducts asset design, work plans formulation, and quality assurance reviews, such as follow-ups and inspections. Examples of the types of assets being developed are the Batch Run Control Manager; Flexible Format Print; and Application Communication Manager.

As new assets are developed, IAE promotes them throughout the company to ensure reuse. Additionally, education on reuse is an ongoing priority.

Vendor/Freeware is treated in the same way as any other asset. If a user inquires about the availability of the function, information is supplied by IAE. However, the user must handle any licensing requirements, and support must be negotiated with the vendor.

## 4.3.3 Funding

Asset Management is funded as a corporate project. There is no specific charge-back to users; maintenance and support is funded and has a cost of operations account. The development of assets and the process are funded primarily through a Research and Development account as an experimental corporate program. Any remaining expenses are funded through cost of operations.

The Financial Model supplies a 10 year projection, which determines the need for asset inventory based on the average KLOCs in production, the projected productivity/inflation factor within the company, and the percentage of reuse objectives for each year. Costs of development, maintenance, and integration are included to find the financial benefits of asset reuse. Spin-off reports from the financial model include manyear requirements for development, manyear requirements for support, average life of an asset, percent of assets built by a project team, and percentage of assets built on speculation.

## 5. Achievements to Date

Approximately 320 assets have been on the Asset Shelf since inception of the program. However, many of these have been disqualified due to either low quality or lack of reuse potential. There are currently 227 qualified assets on the shelf, but this is changing due to addition of new assets and retirement of ones that reach “shelf life”.

During 1987, 38% of the current assets had been used. As a result of this, the 1987 projected reuse factor was changed from 5% to 14%. The factor is calculated by dividing the lines of code reused by the total lines of code (produced by the company). The Financial Model savings prediction for 1987 was \$1,500,000.

## 6. Future Plans

A formal progress evaluation will be conducted. Statistics will be generated on identification vs qualification, qualification vs reuse, and number of assets developed primarily for reuse vs reusable assets developed during project development. The more assets acquired and reused, the greater the cost savings.

Enhancements that are scheduled in the future include:

Table Maintenance: Analyze catalog using metrics and fine tune the catalog tables used for asset queries. Provide an integrated set of catalog maintenance functions to guide the coordinator through the necessary steps when adding, changing, and deleting assets.

Integrated Asset Source: Provide a means of viewing asset source statements (code and/or text) directly from the catalog asset list and catalog detail screens.

Metrics / Prioritization: Design and implement a set of reports on catalog usage statistics (usage analysis and catalog maintenance). Design and implement a means of ordering the catalog asset list by reuse potential.

IAE's 5 Year Business Plan predictions include reaching a $50\%$ reuse goal. This would result in savings of over \$12M with over 2M LOC on the shelf. Over 58,000 man-hours will be required in the year 1992 to reach this goal.

## 7. Conclusions

In summary, we have implemented a corporate Asset Management Program. In 19 months, IAE (a) acquired assets that existed within the company and were suitable for reuse, (b) began independent and brokered development, so that assets are available prior to need, (c) developed an Asset Shelf Catalog System, (d) developed a Candidate Asset Checklist to assist in qualification, and (e) has begun development of a Design Review Checklist to be used as a guideline during development for reusability.

The ASCS was available to over 1,000 users in 1988 and assets will continually be added. Assets that outlive their usefulness will be “retired”.

In 1987, based upon one time use, $38\%$ of the current assets were used. This changed the Financial Model's reuse factor prediction from $5\%$ to $14\%$ . However, year-end results showed a $22\%$ reuse factor, which included reusable integrable code, and production and consistency tools. Overall savings in 1987, were in excess of \$1,550,000 due to the application of reusable components.
