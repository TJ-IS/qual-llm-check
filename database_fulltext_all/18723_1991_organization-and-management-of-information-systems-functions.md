# Organization and management of information systems functions

Comparative study of selected organizations in Bahrain $^{1}$

Emdad H Khan \*

Department of Computer Science The University of Bahrain, Isa Town, Bahrain

This study evaluates and compares the organizational structure management and communications strategy of IS functions in selected organizations in Bahrain. The IS activities fit, with some variations, into Zmud's traditional IS roles model. The IS departments of two of the organizations maintain a successful project and work oriented matrix structure for software development functions and the usual functional structures for computer operation units. Two other organizations use the traditional functional structures, as they are primarily involved in acquisition, implementation, and maintenance of overseas developed application software. These operations are carried out with difficulty, as they become fire-fighting. The four organizations were fairly successful in maintaining the desired communication within their various IS sub-functions. Some of them face difficulties in appointing and retaining qualified and experienced staff. With some exceptions, the organizations maintain no established steering committee that can formulate IS policy and plan and prepare project selection and resource utilization guidelines. The relationships of each IS department with its users during routine activities were satisfactory. Each organization encourages the user departments in using PC-based fourth generation software tools. But these organizations are a long way from adopting Zmud's evolving IS roles and organizational model. The findings also suggested that Nolan's growth stages are applicable to these firms

Keywords Information systems management, Information systems organization, Managing systems development, Nolan's growth stage model, Evolution of information systems, Computing in developing countries

## 1 Introduction

Information is a vital asset in every modern organization. The information systems (IS) department is required to design and develop computer-based information systems (CBIS) to meet the total information needs of an organization and be able to provide the functional users with timely, accurate and useful information services for its operation, control, and decision-making activities. The effective organization, management, and utilization of the IS resources of an organization is of paramount importance

This paper discusses the current state-of-the-art situation of organization, management, and coordination strategies of IS functions in four selected organizations in Bahrain. The study also assessed the effectiveness of utilization of available hardware, software, and manpower resources. It should assist the organizations in devising plans, specifying application portfolios, and noting the growth areas in each organization

## 2 Conceptual Framework for the Comparative Study

The various approaches to the organization and management of IS functions in order to attain desired organizational objectives and policies have been broadly described in the literature $[2,3,25,22,29,30,31,33]$ . In describing our case studies, we shall apply the IS research model proposed by Ives et al $[15]$ , which delineates the main components of the development, operation, and use of information systems and their organization environment. A pictorial model of this framework is used to identify the scope and limits of the case study. The study considered (a) the IS development environment, (b) the IS operation environment, and (c) the user environment and process Based on these, we have developed a conceptual framework to investigate and compare the IS functions of the four organizations

First, we describe the status of IS manager, together with the formation and functioning of steering committees in the organizations. The top management of an IS department must be able to unify and meet the diverse requirements of the various user departments by ensuring an unbiased flow of information to all users. The manager of the IS department should hold the status of a vice president and be responsible only to the top management of the organization. The IS department should be organized as a staff department within the broad objectives of the organization [16]

Communication between the three management areas, comprising general, departmental, and IS management is shown in Figure 1. It is essential if an effective and efficient environment for information services is to be established and maintained [6]. The triad, IS department manager, user department managers, and the general management should be actively involved through a steering committee for co-ordination at the policy, applications and technical levels [10,32]

Second, we studied the organization structure currently followed by the IS functions in each enterprise. The selection of an appropriate organization structure contributes to the success of the IS department. The structure chart would reflect the hierarchical levels and part of the formal communication channels. The chart will help to assess the organization methods, processes, and job responsibilities of each functional sub-unit, along with the degree of their co-ordination

The traditional, centralized IS functions - systems development, operations, administration, and technical services can be organized in a variety of ways, depending upon the size and nature of the business organization [26] The initial functional organization structure is based on grouping the IS staff in a hierarchical arrangement according to the specialized professional functions, such as systems analysis and programming In the alternate applicational or product-oriented organization structure, the IS professionals are grouped according to the organization functional sub-units (or departmentalization), such as accounting, marketing, and production The third, matrix structure, tries to overcome the disadvantages of the other two structures while retaining their advantages The flexible project oriented matrix structure improves the motivation to work, facilitates formal and informal communication among team members, and encourages user participation

An organization having a large and complex information systems department finds it difficult to rely solely on any of the three traditional structures. For these organizations, a decentralized, user-oriented, evolving information systems structures may be more appropriate [1,17,21]. Zmud suggested an evolving IS functions organization structure with sub-units – systems development, delivery systems and operations, support and information center, research and development, technology diffusion cell, planning, internal auditing and administration. In assessing and comparing the current IS organization structures, we shall use these traditional as well as evolving information systems roles and organizational design models

Third, we considered the co-ordination and pattern of communication within the IS functions of each organization. These affect the quality of information services provided to fulfill the organization's objectives. The flow of communication and the degree of co-ordination are influenced by the structural forms, organization culture, and style [7,8,9,20,23,24]

Proper co-ordination mechanisms and channels for communications must be established to facilitate smooth handling of jobs and assignments by various functional units and project groups. These may take the form of continual and informal communications among team members side-by-side with formal communications. The informal interactions contribute towards the creation of a better working environment. By allowing each team member in a project to discuss and express freely his/her opinion, a sense of enthusiasm results and the motivation for work improves

Fourth, the effectiveness of the relationship of user departments with IS departments was also investigated. The ability to maintain effective relationships with the user departments improves their ability to meet the objective of providing vital information services. Thus, the IS department must establish and maintain effective communication with the user departments of the organization. The IS department must consider the expectations and needs of the users. Effective relationships with user departments can be maintained by adopting a user participative approach [13,14] or by applying the administrative model with an interaction strategy [22,24] for the system development process. The IS department should provide users with training, up-to-date and relevant documentation for each system, and maintain good quality controls for data entry. The IS unit needs to maintain effective operation schedules for providing the users with timely reports and information

Fifth, we analyzed the current situation regarding the procurement of PC hardware and software resources and end user computing (EUC) activities. Support for the evolution of EUC will widen the user base, increase user computer awareness, and finally help to reduce pressure on the IS department. The availability of a low cost but powerful PC with user friendly fourth generation software development tools will embed EUC more deeply in the organization's operational, tactical, and strategic activities. The IS department should play an important advisory and monitoring role for the growth of EUC by guiding the user departments in obtaining their own hardware and software [12]

Sixth, after analyzing the various aspects of the IS functions, we identified the current growth stage in each organization. This assessment provided guidelines for improving organization planning, resource utilization, and development of control functions. Nolan's growth model [27,28] provided us with an evolutionary tool to assess the current growth status of IS functions, prevailing features, its environment, and application portfolios. It is a contingency theory that can be used both in the diagnosis of the current stage of growth and in planning the changes necessary to move in a controlled way to the next stage. Some observers doubt the validity of the stage hypothesis as an explanatory model [4], but it is still one of the most widely used paradigms for managing organizational IS evolution [18]

The six-stage growth model is shown in Figure 2b, it is expanded from the original four-stage one [11] of Figure 2a. The model correlates organizational IS problems with the familiar S-shaped growth cycle Nolan also proposed an alternative model in which changes in technology eliminate the maturity stage With the introduction of new hardware, software and systems design methods, the organization starts on a new growth cycle, as shown in Figure 2c, depicting the ability to control technology diffusion

## 3 Research Design

## 31 Overview of the Organizations Surveyed

Bahrain is a small island country having a number of established industrial, financial, and business organizations. In some of these, IS departments are mature, while in others they are relatively young. The author conducted some preliminary field work and identified nine firms which agreed to participate in this project. An important factor in the sample selection was that the organizations be large enough to have the structural and behavioral characteristics of established organizations. Furthermore, the IS department had to be reasonably large and employ at least ten IS professionals. Four private sector organizations were selected for in-depth study. The characteristics of the organizations were as follows

(a) One general trade and business firm with a young IS department (GTDY),

(b) One general trade and business firm with a mature IS department (GTDM),

(c) One finance and banking organization (FINC), and

(d) One aluminum refining and marketing industry (INDA)

Thus, the sample included different types of organizations with varying experience of IS usage. They have been using computers for a variety of applications. Although the number of organizations studied is small, they represent about 20 percent of organizations using CBIS in the country. A summary of their activities and computer systems resources are shown in Table 1. The sample of four is insufficient to be used statistically to prove any “facts” but is still indicative of the trends in our small country. This is mainly, therefore a case study of typical larger private businesses in Bahrain. The remaining five organizations were excluded as the they were government controlled, public sector organizations. Further work with the remaining public sector organizations [20,34] would show similar trends, but are likely to be more formalized in using PC-based end user computing. The public sector organizations are non-profit utilities and the government provides required finance and support. The author believes that this case study would provide an insight into organization, management, and usage of CBIS in a developing country

## 32 Methodology and Data Collection

The data were collected through personal interviews with the managers, systems analysts, programmers, and operation supervisors of the IS departments. The interviews were based on a set of twelve structured questions. These determined the structure of the IS department, corporate IS and PC strategy and policy, procurement of hardware and software resources, application portfolios, software development and maintenance, user participation, training and documentation, etc. The information was gathered between April 1988 and September 1989

## 43 The Coordination and communication within the IS Functions

We shall now consider the instrument of coordination and the flow of communication within the IS functions in each organization, as shown in Table 4

(a) GTDY The ISD follows a functional structure and allows formal and informal interaction processes. It follows the principles of chain-of-commands, line-of-accountability, and responsibility. The functional units submit a weekly report, and the manager holds meetings with IS staff, as required. The staff are allowed to discuss their problems freely and informally with the manager Cross-communication and interaction between analysts and programmers are encouraged

According to Zmud's co-ordination mechanism, the ISD follows formal rules and procedures, and direct supervision, together with informal and dynamic co-ordination between groups. Considering the roles and functions of GDC, the co-ordination and communication processes may be considered appropriate

(b) GTDM The DP unit, allows formal and informal interaction among staff engaged in a development project. The two managers, maintain cross-communication and, at times, interchange responsibilities to meet organizational objectives. The managers discuss each project with the analysts and accordingly constitute temporary project teams. The analysts assign jobs to programmers, who in turn submit progress reports to the analysts with an advance copy to the manager. Each project team member can express his views freely. Sometimes the managers also assign jobs directly to the programmers

According to Zmud's criteria, the DP unit applies direct, but flexible, supervision with a chain-of-command structure, liaison roles, continual and interactive planning between work groups, physical work-groups rearrangement, informal, dynamic and multidirectional roles for managers. This flexible communication mechanism is considered beneficial while improving the motivation for work. However, the programmers within the DP unit felt that the managers should consult them, along with the analysts, in selecting new projects and team members

(c) FINC This firm, like GTDM, follows a formal and informal interaction strategy The project manager discusses each new project with all members in regular monthly meeting For a particular project, a programmer or an analyst can act as the leader who submits the formal progress report to the project manager The work-loads in a project are distributed by mutual consent, and all team members are considered to be equal partners Thus the CAP unit follows a flat structure, where each member is involved in each phase of the project

This organization, according to Zmud's model, maintains informal, dynamic and multi-directional co-ordination, and continual, interactive planning between work groups, and manipulation of work group power relations. The CAP section is considered to be highly successful in maintaining effective communication among IS staff and creating a congenial working environment

(d) INDA This follows a rigid functional structure and allows only formal interaction processes The DP superintendent communicates with the lead analyst, who in turn coordinates with the system designer The system designer supervises the work of programmers, while the programmers can discuss project work among themselves Similarly, for upward communication, the progress reports or problems are passed on in the reverse order

This DP department, according to Zmud's mechanism, provides static, direct supervision through formal rules and procedures. Because of the limited nature of software maintenance and routine operation activities, this formal interaction mechanism is working smoothly

## 45 Growth and Support for PC-Based End User Computing

Each organization has recently established a small PC support unit within the IS department. The PC units have been created to guide users in the selection and procurement of PC hardware and software. Each PC unit has only one or two IS professionals. The units are yet to attain the status of an established information center (IC). The hardware used is primarily IBM-PC, or compatible clones. The software packages used are primarily word processing, database management, spreadsheet, etc. These perform tasks, such as document preparation, data capture, query and report generation, and data analysis activities. A fully functional IC (with analysts, programmers, and hardware and software consultants) is yet to be established to provide training and support services for end users. Only one of the organizations, FINC, is currently taking this route

## 46 Current Growth Status of IS Functions

The Nolan growth model is used to determine the current growth stage of each organization. The analysis could help management to decide what should be adopted (1e planning the changes needed) to move to the next stage in a controlled and organized way. The current growth stages of each organization are shown in Table 2

(a) GTDY The pattern of application portfolios, organization structure, control and co-ordination processes indicate that GTDY is currently in growth stage 2, contagion. The benefits of computer applications are firmly established and new application areas are identified, these are at different stages of implementation. Consequently the budget and staff of ISD show a rapid increase. The formation of an IS committee to formulate IS policy and planning could facilitate movement to stage 3, control. The organization is currently negotiating with an outside consultant to audit and evaluate the effectiveness of the existing applications, management procedures and utilization of resources, and setting up a charge-back system for ISD services. These are some of the characteristics to be found at the beginning of growth stage 3, control

(b) GTDM GTDM is found to be located between growth stages 3 and 4. It is the most mature (13 years) of all four organizations, and is currently trying to integrate various applications through a central administration unit. Furthermore, this firm is evaluating hardware and software resources and considering giving each line department and sponsored company some mini-computers linked by a network so that each department will be responsible for managing its own IS resources. This will replace existing large IBM hardware in three DP centers. The firm is also looking into the possibility of replacing COBOL-based applications on the large mainframes by a DBMS-oriented new application package for the proposed mini-computer environment. From this viewpoint, we can consider the organization to be in process of moving to growth stage 5 data administration, or to repeating growth stage 1 initiation, due to technology shift. However, the organization has not considered using the service of any external consultant to audit or assess independently the performance of the DP center or discuss the proposed changes

(c) FINC Although established for only five years, this organization is about to cross stage 2 and enter stage 3 It is strongly committed to PC technology and fourth generation tools for developing applications Thus, we can say that this firm is about to enter new growth stage 1 initiation The CAP unit has some difficulties in handling internal software development along with external consultancy services The organization is currently internally auditing and evaluating the organizational structure and effectiveness of management procedures for the IS functions

(d) INDA The current growth status of this company is between stages 1 and 2. It has limited application portfolios and, as expected at the initiation stage, is not cost effective. The learning curve is essential in the process of moving to the next stage contagion. Currently management is studying the feasibility of enlarging the application areas to include accounting, administration, and personnel. This is an indication of the organization's commitment to information technology

This firm is in process of negotiating with an external consultant to audit the IS functions and to advise on the development of new applications

## 5 Conclusions

We investigated the organization, management, and interaction strategy of IS functions of four business organizations. Each is reasonably successful in meeting the organizational objectives of providing information services. The IS group in each organization was organized as a staff department. However, the chief executive of the IS department rarely held the status of vice president

The organizations followed no broad management standards and procedures, nor were there any established information policy or project selection criteria. We can conclude that each organization has some strengths and weaknesses. There are a number of areas where improvements can be suggested. Each organization should establish a corporate IS steering committee with three way representation – users, general management, and IS functions. This committee should formulate policy and plan the organizational IS needs, project selection, and resource utilization guidelines

Each organization's IS activities fit, with some variations, into Zmud's traditional IS roles model Our study revealed that each organization has adopted a different structure to handle IS functions No one of the three traditional structures – functional, applicational or matrix – is used uniquely This is partly because of the differing IS requirements of each, and also the fact that each is at a different growth stage Two of the organizations (GTDM and FINC) have adopted project-oriented matrix structures for system development functions and functional structures for the operation units The other two (GTDY and INDA) prefer to use the traditional functional structures, as they are primarily involved in acquisition, implementation, maintenance of software, and routine information services Thus, all four organizations have almost similar operational functional structures

Two organizations (GTDM and FINC) have developed all their own application software, and maintain strong and successful in-house system development units. The other two (GTDY and

INDA) use external custom-built application packages developed overseas with minimal user involvement Lack of proper system documentation and limited knowledge on the part of local staff about the packages has made the system learning curve long, producing unreasonable delays in system implementation Software maintenance takes up a substantial amount of the time and manpower resources of the IS departments At times, each organization has had to call the original overseas consultant to fix software bugs

One of the weaknesses of general management is their habit of procuring ready-made software packages without proper analysis of real user requirements. Proper study and planning should be undertaken before engaging an outside consultant to provide application packages. A local software house might be better, or else the overseas consultant should be asked to develop and implement the software locally, preceded by detailed analysis of user requirements. For quality assurance, the corporation should also ensure continual user involvement and co-ordination during the development and implementation process

The study found that each organization was fairly successful in maintaining desired communication mechanisms within the various IS subfunctions. The organizations, using in an in-house software development technique encourage a continual, formal and informal communication channel between project team members. Formal interaction processes within the organization are also working smoothly

The organizations are in general affected by the shortage of qualified local IS professionals and consequent dependency upon overseas manpower that normally contracts to work for a period of one or two years. A number of organizations find it difficult to procure and keep qualified and experienced staff

The user relationships for routine information production and distribution were found to be satisfactory. One of the organizations, FINC, because of bitter past experience in using third party turnkey software, follows a user-participative approach in system development. It holds discussion with users through project committees in each phase of its development and utilizes user opinion as a feedback control mechanism. Similar project committees should be formed in other organizations to guide, review and monitor software project development activities A user participative approach with active user roles in each phase of the life-cycle should be promoted

Each organization supports user departments in using the PC-based fourth generation software tools. But they have not, as yet, established a formal Information Center (IC) to coordinate end user computing activities. One of the organizations, i.e., GTDM, has assigned one IS subunit to monitor technology and software development and to investigate the potential of applying the new technology in its environment. GTDM also uses a partly distributed system for IS functions. However, it is still a long way for any of these organizations to embrace Zmud's evolving IS roles and organizational model

The study showed each organization has reached a different growth stage of Nolan's model. Only one organization (GTDM) may be considered as being at growth stage 3–4, two of them at stage 2–3, and the last (INDA) at stage 1–2. The information systems activities of all four organizations are confined to traditional transaction processing systems (TPS), i.e., accounting, payroll, stock control, etc. However, some of the organizations are looking into the feasibility of introducing administrative management systems (AMS) and decision support systems (DSS) in selected areas. The study should provide guidelines for the orderly and smooth development of a future user participative design and implementation scheme for the organization's information resources
