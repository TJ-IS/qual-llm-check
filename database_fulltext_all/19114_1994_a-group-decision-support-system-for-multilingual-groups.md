# A group decision support system for multilingual groups

Milam W. Aiken, Jeanette S. Martin, Joseph G.P. Paolillo and Ashraf I. Shirani

University of Mississippi, University, MS, USA

Communication in multilingual groups is very difficult. Even if all participants in the group know a common language, it may be a first language for some of the group and a second language for others. Communication in such situations is not equal for all group members. A multilingual Group Decision Support System (GDSS) allows all members to communicate in their native languages, eliminating the group's linguistic problems. This paper describes a prototype multilingual GDSS that provides a high degree of translation accuracy while providing other benefits of Group Decision Support Systems, such as anonymity, parallel communication, and automated recording of the discussion.

Keywords: Group decision support systems; Electronic meeting systems; Electronic brainstorming; Multilingual groups

However, two recent developments in microcomputer technology may greatly increase the productivity of multilingual groups. The first development, Group Decision Support Systems (GDSSs) using microcomputers in local area networks, occurred in the mid-1980s. These systems typically allow meeting participants to exchange written comments anonymously and simultaneously, resulting in decreased meeting time, increased group decision quality, and increased participant satisfaction with the meeting process $[8,12,13,17,18]$ . The second development, microcomputer software for automatic language translation, is a relatively new phenomenon. These two developments combined (a GDSS which provides automatic translations of all written comments generated) may yield an order of magnitude increase in productivity in multilingual groups [2]. This paper describes a prototype multilingual GDSS that can be used to overcome barriers. First, we describe typical communication problems that arise due to differences in group members' language. Second, we describe a multilingual GDSS developed at the University of Mississippi to enhance communication in these groups by providing automatic natural language translation. Third, we discuss the results of an experiment using Spanish-speaking and non-Spanish-speaking individuals to test electronic brainstorming within a multilingual group.

## Lingual barriers to communication

Multilingual groups frequently find communication difficult. The traditional solution to the communication barriers of multilingual groups has been to include human interpreters. One firm that specializes in providing human interpreters for multilingual meetings, Linguistic Systems, Inc. of Cambridge, Massachusetts, reports that an international business conference can be held in 12 languages and with an audience of unlimited size. However, this multilingual conference still suffers from the inherent limitations of oral meetings: (1) Only one person at a time may speak (serial communication), (2) the “minutes” of the meeting must be recorded and later transcribed, and (3) many participants do not participate because of shyness (resulting from the lack of anonymity).

Numerous studies have demonstrated how Group Decision Support Systems (GDSSs) are superior in many cases to traditional, oral meetings. A GDSS is a computer-based system that supports groups of people engaged in a common task through an interface to a shared environment [7]. Most GDSSs provide group members with parallel communication (all group members can exchange comments simultaneously), automated record keeping (all comments are stored on a disk file), and anonymity (no participant can tell where comments originated). For certain types of tasks (those which require group members to exchange comments or preferences) and with groups larger that eight people, GDSSs have been demonstrated to reduce the meeting time [14,16] and increase productivity and satisfaction [15]. Although typing is slower than speaking, reading is generally faster than listening [11,19], and the written, electronic media promotes more carefully-worded communication [3,4,5]. A complex interrelationship of group, task, context, technology, and process variables affect the subsequent meeting outcome as depicted in Figure 1 [6].

A multilingual GDSS extends to multilingual verbal meetings what traditional GDSSs provide monolingual meetings. Even though automatic translation programs, in the near future, will continue to lack 100 percent accuracy, the integration of these programs with Group Decision Support Systems remain the most promising area of implementation for effective multilingual communication. A similar multilingual GDSS has been suggested with the exception that bilingual or multilingual facilitators would take an active role in the translation by either manually translating each comment or editing the output from automatic translators $[9]$ . Using this system, the meeting facilitator or translator would have to be discreet in maintaining anonymity during the process. In addition, several translators may be required for large groups to keep pace with the rate of information generation. The multilingual GDSS prototype described here eliminates the concern about circumventing anonymity, because there is no human intervention in translation. Also, each participant has a dedicated automatic translator and so a single translator is not overburdened by comments from multiple group members.

## The multilingual GDSS prototype

The multilingual GDSS prototype consists of three software tools: a brainstorming program, an idea consolidating program, and a ranking program.

The brainstorming program allows meeting participants to exchange comments anonymously and simultaneously while the computer system records their comments. For example, Figure 2 shows a sample English speaker's brainstorm screen with an edited comment during a session focusing on the new European community. Figure 3 shows the comments translated for the German speaker and a message from the group facilitator. Another example is provided in Figure 4 which shows a Spanish speaker's brainstorm screen featuring instructions on its use. This screen is from a multilingual group meeting focusing on parking at a university.

Although the translations are generally very good, translation errors can occur due to misspellings, poor grammar, slang, euphemisms, and idioms. The system handles words that cannot be translated by enclosing them in brackets in the translation. ## A multilingual GDSS experiment

An experiment was conducted using the brainstorming software described in the previous section. A group of nine students (three spoke Spanish and six spoke English) informally exchanged comments using the brainstorm program equipped with Spanish-to-English and English-to-Spanish translation programs. A total of 59 comments were exchanged (24 Spanish and 25 English) in 20 minutes. Spanish-speaking students saw only Spanish comments on their screens (similar to Figure 4) and the designated English-speaking students saw only English comments (similar to Figure 2). The language translation programs automatically added the English or Spanish translations to the appropriate screens. The students then were asked to evaluate the grammatical accuracy and understandability of the comments. As shown in Table 1, Spanish-speaking students reported on average that 6.6 comments were grammatically incorrect (26% of the comments translated from English) and one comment was misunderstood (4% of the comments translated from English). English-speaking students reported on average that 10.7 comments were grammatically incorrect (55% of the comments translated from Spanish) and 3.5 comments were misunderstood (15% of the comments translated from Spanish). Many of the errors in translation occurred because the originators misspelled words in the source comments.

In a separate study of the translation programs, objective, independent reviewers were asked to evaluate the grammatical accuracy and understandability of 100 comments translated from Spanish to English and 100 comments translated from English to Spanish. The English reviewers rated the Spanish-to-English grammatical accuracy at 46% and the understandability at 95%. The Spanish reviewers rated the English-to-Spanish grammatical accuracy at 75% and the understandability at 98%. The increase in accuracy is due in part to all of the source comments being spelled correctly.

Several cited studies have already demonstrated the advantages of GDSSs over many verbal meetings including anonymity, parallel communication, automated record keeping, decreased meeting times, increased effectiveness, increased satisfaction, etc. Although participants in this study expressed enthusiasm for the technology and satisfaction with the meeting, it sought only to investigate the degree to which participants in a multilingual meeting can understand each other when automatic translation is provided for them. Further experiments on multilingual groups using a GDSS with automatic translation will attempt to duplicate results obtained with monolingual groups.

The results of this study support our hypothesis that a multilingual group can use a GDSS with automatic translation effectively. The accuracy of translation (as measured by whether or not group members understood the translations) ranged from a low of 85% understood to a high of 96%. More studies must be conducted to replicate these results (especially because of the small sample size), and the research must determine exactly how accurate translations must be for an effective and efficient meeting. We believe that this research adds one more dimension to the substantial literature on Group Decision Support Systems and Communication.

## Conclusions

The problem of multilingual communication is of major importance due to global competitiveness and the need for multinational corporations to communicate with people of different national origins. Businesses are constantly urged to compete globally, but lingual barriers to communication hinder this expansion.

This paper has described a prototype multilingual Group Decision Support System (GDSS) that provides groups with anonymity, automated record keeping, parallel communication, and translation of comments among languages. A multilingual GDSS allows better communication and consequently greater group productivity and satisfaction with the meeting. At the same time, nonverbal communication problems may be reduced or eliminated. Future research utilizing the GDSS will provide additional information about multilingual group communication and the effects electronic technology have on such groups.
