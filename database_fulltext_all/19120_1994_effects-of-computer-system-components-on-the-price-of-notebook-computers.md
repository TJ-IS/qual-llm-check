# Effects of computer system components on the price of notebook computers

Albert L. Harris, Dinesh S. Dave \*

Department of Decision Sciences, John A. Walker College of Business, Appalachian State University, Boone, NC 28608, USA

## Abstract

Several studies have been conducted to quantify the relationship between price and power of computer systems. Among the earliest and well known is that of Grosch, which dealt with mainframes. Recently, authors have made similar studies for desktop computers. However, some of the latest technology is in notebook computers. They are becoming more powerful, while size, weight, and prices continue to drop, and they are becoming one of the fastest growing segments of the computer market. This paper develops a model that identifies the computer characteristics that most significantly influence their price. The model can be utilized to perform price comparisons and in purchasing decisions for managers buying notebook computers.

Key words: Notebook computers; Price/performance of computer systems; Pricing model for notebook computers; Managerial pricing decisions

## 1. Introduction

Many researchers have analyzed the relationship between the cost of a computer system and its power and system components. Grosch [5] formulated the initial price/power relationship, Grosch's law, which stated that the speed of a computer system increases in proportion to the square of its cost, giving rise to significant economies of scale. The validity of Grosch's law was tested in a study by Knight [9]. Knight defined computer power as a function of system components (e.g. memory, compute time, and input/output time). He established a single value for the power of a system, and confirmed Grosch's law by determining the relationship between price and certain computer components and power. Of course, many have suggested that the major factor of price at that time was IBM's marketing policy. Cale et al. [1] reviewed previous efforts as quantifying the relationship between the cost of a computer system and its power, and developed a model that related system price to component characteristics, specifically memory and available disk storage. They concluded that: Grosch's law was not valid, computer price is the best single measure of computing power, and the measurement of a computer's power is work specific.

Sircar and Dave [13] analyzed the relationship between the cost of a computer and various computer system components. They reanalyzed the relationships created in the above mentioned studies and developed a reliable model relating cost to memory size, MIPS, machine cycle time, and year in which the machine was introduced.

They divided computer systems into four different classes: IBM machines, IBM plug-compatible machines, IBM competitor machines, and super minicomputers. Dave and Fitzpatrick [4] developed a model that identified the factors that most significantly influence the price of desktop computers. Their model was based upon various computer characteristics such as clock speed, RAM, inclusion of hard disk as a standard feature, effect of time, type of microchip, and availability of optional hard disk drive capacity. Recently, Harris and Dave [7] developed two distinct models, based on the clock speed of a microcomputer, to estimate the price of 486-based desktop microcomputers. Their models identified computer components that directly influenced the price of 486 desktop microcomputers.

The notebook computer market is one of the fastest growing segments of the computer industry. Notebooks are getting more powerful, with hard disks, VGA displays, multiple ports, and Windows capability almost universal. Many possess 486 microprocessor power, color screens, improved batteries, large hard disk capacities, and a variety of ports, connections, and accessories. When we add the fact that notebook computer prices are dropping rapidly while size and weight continue to get smaller, we have one of the most sought after items, especially for people who have to cram a computer into an already full briefcase or carry one for any length of time.

The purpose of this paper is to report on the development of a reliable model that can identify the system components that influence the price of notebook computers. Given these system components, the model can be used to assist managers in conducting a price/performance analysis and making purchasing decisions for the best notebook computer for their needs.

## 2. Methodology

## 2.1. Data collection

In many cases, research efforts in the field of information systems are limited because of rapidly advancing technology, resulting in a scarcity of meaningful, accurate, and timely data. Fortunately, a timely set of data was found for this effort to establish a data base covering a reasonable subset of notebook computers. The data were obtained from a reliable single source of Computer Shopper [2]. The data set contained information and values for system components on a variety of notebook computers, including: retail price, CPU type, clock speed (MHz), RAM included, RAM maximum, type of ports available, size of hard disk included, screen type, battery type, weight, and dimensions.

Comparative data were collected for 167 different notebook computers marketed by 66 different vendors (name of the company and model were included in the data set). The data included notebook computers from hardware manufacturers (IBM, Tandy, NCR, etc.), mail order companies (Dell, Gateway, MidWest Micro, etc.), and companies using discount or retail outlets (Leading Edge, Epson, Compaq, etc.). Very few observations were eliminated because of missing values of computer system components. As a result, 162 notebook computers were used in the analysis. ## 2.2. Development of the model

To determine the effects of computer system components on the price of notebook computers, a model was developed that directly related the notebook computer's price to system component characteristics (i.e., CPU type, clock speed, RAM included, RAM maximum, etc.). Of different linear and nonlinear models tried [10, 3, 11, 8], the model that demonstrated the most consistent results and the highest predictive capability was:

$$
\begin{array}{l l} \text {Price} = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} (\text {CPU}) + \beta_ {2} (\text {SPEED}) \\ & + \beta_ {3} (\text {RAMM}) + \beta_ {4} (\text {DISK}) \\ & + \beta_ {5} (\text {SCR1}) + \beta_ {6} (\text {BAT}), \\ \text {where} \\ \text {Price} & = \text {Base System Price (  )} \\ \text {CPU} & = \text {Type of CPU} \\ \text {SPEED} & = \text {MHz} \\ \text {RAMM} & = \text {RAM Maximum (Megabytes)} \\ \text {DISK} & = \text {Size of hard disk included (Megabytes)} \\ \text {SCR1} & = \text {Screen type} \\ \text {BAT} & = \text {Battery type} \end{array}
$$

The coefficients $\beta_{0}, \beta_{1}, \ldots$ are the parameters to be estimated using SAS [12]. Other characteristics, such as RAM included, type of ports available, screen type, and weight, were not part of the model. This was because the coefficients derived for such measures were not statistically different from zero in any of our alternative models.

The model was based upon six computer system components. Table 2 shows the results of the model run on all observations.

To check the overall validity and utility of the model, a hypothesis $H_{0}=\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=\ldots=0$ was tested against the alternative hypothesis $H_{a}$ : At least one of $\beta$ s is non-zero. The value of F-statistic was found to be 51.57 with a p-value of 0.0001. This value of F-statistic strongly supports the alternate hypotheses; indicating that at least one of the $\beta$ 's is statistically non-zero. Also, the value of $R^{2}$ was found to be reasonably high, which indicated that about 66.5% of the variation was explained by the model. Further, the model was validated through several statistical tests; e.g., the severe problems of multicollinearity and heteroscadasticity were verified through correlation analysis, VIF test, and residual analysis. The values of VIF test were found to be between 0 and 1.44, which indicates that the model is free from multicollinearity. Furthermore, the analysis was performed to plot residuals against predicted values and residuals against independent variables. The results of these plots indicated the absence of heteroscadasticity. Also, Cook's D test was used to check for outliers and the results did not indicate any severe ones.

## 3.2. Analysis of the independent variables

In order to understand the model, an analysis of the independent variables is warranted. Screen type contributed the largest $\beta$ value of 737. Basically, three screen types are used in notebook computers (LED monochrome, passive color, and active color); apparently the inclusion of a passive color screen increases the price of the notebook by approximately 737 while an active color screen further increases the price by \$737. From a visual effect standpoint, the attractiveness increases from a monochrome screen, through the paler passive-matrix screen, to the active-matrix color display.

It is not surprising that the superior nickel metal hydride (NiMH) battery increases the price of the notebook computer over a Nickel cadmium (NiCd) battery. The model showed the difference to be approximately \$249. The NiMH battery is a type of rechargeable battery that delivers more power than a NiCd battery of the same size and weight and is free from nuisance known as “NiCd memory,” which forces users to either sacrifice battery life or run their notebooks all the way to shutdown before recharging.

The third largest influence on notebook computer price is CPU type, with a $\beta$ value of 153. A year ago, the beta value might have been significantly higher, but the price of 486-based computers of all types has fallen drastically. There still is a difference in price between 486-based and 386-based notebook computers.

According to the model, the difference between a 25 MHz and a 33 MHz notebook computer should be about \$156. This is a realistic difference between the two CPU speeds. Likewise, the differences between hard disk capacities included seems realistic. Given the model, the difference between 80 MB and 120 MB disk drives (the two most frequently included disk sizes in notebook computers) would be about \$172.

Overall, the variables seem representative of the pricing differences between various notebook computers and realistic in the marketplace. As a result, the usefulness of the model is greatly increased. Table 3 shows the predicted and actual prices of representative notebook computers. All of the characteristics are found in the selected notebook computers of Table 3. The predicted prices were compared with the actual prices and generally found to be close.

## 3.3. Analysis of predicted prices

In the complete list of notebook computers, thirty-eight of the vendors marketed more than one type. The analysis of the predicted prices (Table 5) showed that, of these the retail price tended to be, as a group, either over or under the predicted prices, with half (19) being under. For example, the predicted prices of all of Compaq's and IBM's notebook computers were lower than their retail prices, while the predicted prices of AMS's and MidWest Mico's notebook computers were higher. In general, the vendors with predicted prices lower than retail prices were the "name brand" vendors-Compaq, Epson, IBM, Sharp, Texas Instruments. These vendors may also offer additional services, software, or unique features. Vendors whose predicted prices are greater than retail prices are presumably using a low price strategy. Table 4 shows vendors with multiple models of notebook computers.

The predicted prices for notebook computers available from mail order companies (e.g. AMS, Bi-Linc, BTI, Bondwell, Imperial, Keydata, Mid-West Micro, Sager, etc.) were consistently above the retail prices. This is a very plausible marketing strategy, indeed some of the “name brand” vendors (e.g. IBM, Compaq, etc.) are turning to mail order marketing as well to sell their products. It will be interesting to see if they can compete in the mail order business with seemingly higher prices.

## 4. Conclusions

Many studies have focused on analyzing the relationship between the cost of a computer system and its power. Several studies have successfully quantified the relationship between price and power of computer systems. The previous studies, however, did not focus on notebook computers. Notebook computers are the market phenomenon of the nineties. They are getting smaller, weigh less, and contain more capabilities. In some organizations, they perform double duty – as portables and as desktop machines. Their importance in the computer industry is increasing.

In this paper, we have developed a reliable model to conduct a price/performance analysis and to estimate the price of notebook computers. Given the information of CPU type, clock speed, maximum RAM, hard disk capacity, screen type, and battery type, a potential buyer of notebook computers can generally compare the price of competing machines.
