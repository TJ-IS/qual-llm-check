# ISDN implementation in the United States and Germany: A cross-country assessment ☆

Vincent S. Lai $^{a,*}$ , Bernard Reeh $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Department of MIS / MS, College of Administrative Sciences, University of Alabama in Huntsville, Huntsville, AL 35899, USA $^{b}$ Department of MIS / MS, College of Business Administration, University of Texas, Arlington Box 19437, Arlington, TX 76019, USA

## Abstract

This paper discusses a survey of the subscribers to ISDN in the United States (US) and Germany to assess their experiences with the installation and use of this technology. The results indicated that ISDN has been more widely accepted as a network solution in Germany. In the US, ISDN adoption rate and implementation extent were low. Lack of government support, inconsistent implementation standards, uncompetitive tariffs, and ineffective marketing strategy were considered obstacles for growth of ISDN in the US.

Keywords: ISDN; Diffusion of innovations; Information technology; Technology management; Networks; Data communications

## 1. Introduction

Significant progress has been made in the last decade in enhancing the performance and capabilities of telecommunication services. With the advent of the integrated services digital network (ISDN), existing communication services can be integrated to provide more efficient information movement and network management. ISDN is not a true innovation but the result of digital technology being used in public networks to improve service offerings. ISDN is an architecture that allows simultaneous transmission of digitized voice, data, and/or images through standard interfaces over existing twisted-pair telephone wires. Its benefits include line sharing, session multiplexing, high throughput, virtually error-free transmission, local access to packet switched networks, automatic number identification, sophisticated and automated diagnostics, and fast call setup. Applications requiring transmission rates from less than 300 bps to more than 2 Mbps can use this technology. Also, this technology allows flexible interconnection and interoperation with all other networks and equipment.

ISDN has already been introduced around the world with various degrees of success. Europe and the Pacific Rim countries are deploying national networks and have well-delineated plans to evolve into its subsequent technologies (such as Broadband ISDN). While impressive progress is being made in other countries, the US has been slow in ISDN implementation $[4,8]$ . The delay in its implementation has been frustratingly long for users looking for new communication capabilities. Up to now, all the US has accomplished is a series of ISDN islands, a far cry from an ubiquitous digital network. The reasons for greater success in some countries than others has been widely discussed $[10,18]$ . Although the answers are not yet confirmed, it is clear that national telecommunication policies and country-specific characteristics play key roles. An evaluation of the relationship between the ISDN diffusion process and the local telecommunication policy thus becomes important.

The objective of this paper is to answer the question: who is leading in ISDN deployment, the US or Germany? More specifically, this study explores the following issues:

1. What is the extent of ISDN implementation?

2. How much has it been used in performing intra- and inter-organizational communication activities?

3. What are the major ISDN applications?

4. What is the general attitude of its users towards this emerging network technology?

5. What are the factors, if any, that caused differences in ISDN implementation extent, usage level, applications, and satisfaction level?

## 2. Background

## 2.1. United States

Since the divestiture of AT&T in 1984, the telecommunications industry in the US has shifted from centralized federal control to decentralized state control. This introduced real competition into long distance services and telecommunication equipment. In 1986, the first ISDN features were introduced. In early 1988, the first commercial services were offered. Since then, implementation and initial service rollouts of ISDN have moved steadily forward.

The major ISDN suppliers in the US can be divided into two principal groups: network carriers (local and long distance) and equipment vendors. Of the network carriers, AT&T and the seven Regional Bell Operating Companies (RBOCs) have been very active in ISDN trials and implementation. The primary suppliers of ISDN equipment (such as terminal adapters and switches) are AT&T and Northern Telecom. They account for more than 85% of switching equipment in the network carriers' central offices [6]. In 1990, it was indicated that more than one-third of the infrastructure needed for ISDN implementation is already in place [2]. Predictions optimistically placed this percentage at the ninety percentile in 1997, meaning the existing ISDN islands could be linked to form an ubiquitous national network.

## 2.2. Germany

The development of the German telecommunication infrastructure has been characterized by the monopoly position of the Deutsche Bundespost (DBP). In the late 1980's, the telecommunication functions were organizationally detached from the DBP and unified within a new publicly-owned organization, called Telekom. The traditional monopoly position has resulted in a supply-driven development of the German telecommunication infrastructure. Based on this, Telekom provided initial pro-active support for ISDN, which included the provision of expertise and initial funding.

Telekom has expressed its ISDN strategy in various media since the beginning of the 1980s. In 1989, a committee was formed with members appointed by the government of Nordrhein-Westfalen (NRW) to define and coordinate ISDN research activities [16]. One of its major tasks was to adjust Telekom's implementation efforts to the actual needs of potential ISDN users. This also helped foster the exchange of implementation experiences by supporting pilot projects and collecting, analyzing, and communicating derived application-oriented knowledge.

ISDN has become a normal service in the German telecommunication infrastructure since its commercial introduction in 1988. By the end of 1993, 70% of all German territory and almost 100% of the Western parts of the country had access to ISDN services [1] and more than 1 million ISDN B-channels were sold. This constitutes the highest ISDN subscriber base for any country in Europe [7].

## 3. Research methodology

A survey approach was used to collect data about ISDN implementation in the US and Germany. Most of the questionnaire was adapted from previous studies in innovation, diffusion, implementation, and information management. Additionally, specific questionnaire items were developed to collect data relevant to the objectives of the study, along with descriptive information. To ensure the consistency and reliability of the new questionnaire, two ISDN adopting companies in Dallas (Texas) were used as pilot sites for testing the instrument. This provided estimates of time required for the test completion and also resulted in the addition, removal, and rephrasing of several items. After revising the survey instrument, one of the authors, who is a native speaking German, translated it for use in Germany.

To test the validity of the instrument, a full scale pilot test was administered to the MIS Directors of fifty-two ISDN adopting organizations in the US and 100 in Germany. The names of the US organizations were acquired from Goldstein's list [5], while the names of German organizations were captured from the 1992 German Directory of ISDN Subscribers [15]. Twelve organizations in the US and twenty-six organizations in Germany responded. Reliability analyses for multiple item measures were conducted to assess the questionnaire's content and construct validity. The resulting Cronbach's alpha for each variable was higher than 0.7 in both countries, suggesting significant reliability of the questionnaire.

After the validation of the instrument, the modified questionnaire was mailed to the MIS directors of the selected organizations in the two countries. Whenever a company's MIS Director was not listed, the survey was mailed to the position, rather than the person holding this position. In Germany, the samples were 500 organizations randomly chosen from the 1992 German Directory of ISDN Subscribers. In the US, a larger sample size from all Business Week 1,000 companies were chosen because there was no national directory available. Many studies [3,11] argued that larger firms, such as Business Week 1,000 companies, are more innovative due to their ability to absorb more risk. Therefore, they would be more inclined to adopt ISDN to satisfy their communication requirements. The Names of the MIS Directors were drawn from the Directory of Top Computer Executives and the Corporate Technology Directory.

## 4. Analysis of results

From all responses, forty-one organizations in the US and seventy-two organizations in Germany were found to have implemented ISDN. They had chosen either BRI, PRI, or a combination of both services and installed different numbers of ISDN lines (ranging from less than 100 to more than 1000). The responses from these 113 organizations were used to answer the research questions for this study.

## 4.1. Respondent profile

Sample profiles by industry in the two countries are shown in Fig. 1. The largest number of respondents in the US came from the manufacturing industry, representing more than 36 percent of the responding companies. Such a distribution was fairly representative of the distribution among Business Week 1,000 firms. In Germany, the largest number of ISDN users was in the computer/DP services (25.0%), trailed by manufacturing (16.7%) and trade (16.7%) industries.

## 4.2. Extent of ISDN implementation

In the US, most ISDN adopters (39%) had installed less than 100 lines. Only two companies had more than 1,000 lines. Between the two services, thirty-six companies (87.8%) had adopted BRI service, whereas three (7.3%) adopted PRI, and two (4.9%) adopted both.

In Germany, ISDN services were adopted by both small and large organizations. This diversified user could be due to Telekom's marketing strategy to promote ISDN as a low cost and flexible network to satisfy diverse telecommunication needs of smaller organizations [17]. Our survey found that no German companies employed more than 1,000 ISDN lines, with most of them (79.2%) having installed less than one hundred lines. In the US, sixty-two companies (86%) had subscribed to BRI, while only eighteen (25%) had adopted PRI.

## 4.3. Extent of ISDN usage

As indicated in Table 1, neither the BRI or PRI services were utilized at, or near, capacity in the US organizations. Only one U.S. respondent indicated utilization of more than 45% of his company's ISDN capacity. In Germany, 54.8% of the companies indicated that they had utilized at least 45% of the total BRI capacity, with 16.1% of them operating at more than 90% of their capacity.

As shown in Table 2, no organization in the US had utilized BRI to perform more than 45% of its internal communication activities. The utilization of PRI in intra-organizational communication activities was even more disappointing. As for Germany, ISDN was used more heavily. There were 41.9% BRI users using BRI to handle more than 45% of their local communication activities and 72.2% of PRI users used it to handle more than 45% of their local communication activities.

## 4.5. Attitude of ISDN

Apparently, users in Germany were more satisfied with their ISDN services than users in the US. On a seven-point Likert scale (1 = very dissatisfied and 7 = very satisfied), German users scored an average of 5.81 (standard deviation of 1.15) while U.S. users 4.52 (standard deviation of 1.34). Not only were the German users more satisfied, they also believed that ISDN was superior to other networks. An analysis of ISDN expectations suggests that they fall into three categories: performance, functionality, and management. Performance includes data rate, throughput, transmission error rate, network availability, and data security. Functionality includes type of service and flexibility of transmission. Management involves network maintenance and network administration. Overall, the Germany respondents were more satisfied with the ISDN service.

The survey also indicated that both countries' respondents were quite satisfied with the functionality and management features (with means above 5.0), but less impressed by the physical performance of ISDN. Interestingly, all ISDN users were concerned with the security and data rate features, which were ranked very low for this technology. The explanation may be that ISDN is still employing twisted-pair wires for data transmission, and this is susceptible to signal disturbances and low data carrying capacity. However, evolution from ISDN to BISDN should reduce the problems as fiber optic cable is used.

## 5. Discussions of results

On the basis of our empirical findings, ISDN was found used more in Germany than the US. Possible explanations are differences in government policy, national standard, tariff, and marketing strategy.

## 5.1. Government policy and support

Many researchers argue that direct government intervention in the process of innovation may distort the market $[14]$ and cause disastrous consequences for both technological innovation and economic growth $[12]$ . However, our empirical findings have indicated that government intervention and financial support was one of the key factors that pushed ISDN to success.

In Germany, the government-owned Telekom played a significant role. It initiated programs to support the development of ISDN applications, publication of promising ISDN trail results, and exchange of implementation-related experiences. To ensure rapid diffusion of ISDN, Telekom has not only implemented a favorable tariff structure, but has also offered direct financial support. In an attempt to draw more subscriptions from small businesses, a total of only 888 DM (\$522) was charged for the installation of a new basic ISDN access and procurement of four ISDN telephones. If an organization needed additional ISDN peripherals, Telekom would furnish additional subsidies. These included 2,000 DM (\$1,176) for small ISDN-based PBXes and 888 DM (\$522) for ISDN PC-adapter cards. During the first year of the ISDN implementation program (between mid-1989 and mid-1990), the financial support from Telekom was between 30,000 DM (about \$17,650) and 500,000 DM (about \$294,120) per organization [19].

In the US, there was no government policy to promote ISDN services, not to mention financial support to adopters. The antitrust decree in 1984 led to the rejection of a public network's monopoly and the breakup of AT and T. Although the RBOCs are still monopolizing local communication services, they are limited to reselling other companies' products, that may have discouraged them from investing and innovating. In 1988, the US government's National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) sponsored the formation of the ISDN Users Forum. Its mission is to ensure that the implementation, development, and applications of ISDN meet users' application needs.

The lack of government intervention and deregulation policies in the US contributed significantly to the maintenance of a market for the telecommunications industry and have caused many institutional barriers. The existence of 'ISDN islands' is evidence of a lack of government efforts to coordinate and streamline the overall implementation of ISDN. However, it is important to recognize that increased attention must be paid, by either the government, communication carriers, or both, to the formulation of national telecommunication policies.

## 5.2. National standards

In 1984, the CCITT formulated a series of ISDN recommendations, called the I-Series, which specified the direction for ISDN development. However, the proposed standards allowed a great deal of latitude in implementation, which led to a proliferation of divergent interpretations, resulting in product configuration complexity (e.g., of interface and protocols). To control and alleviate the problem, each affected country was supposed to promote its own consistent set of standards, defining access to ISDN and its functional performance. This would minimize ISDN development efforts and maximize product and system compatibilities within each country.

In Germany, efforts and resources were devoted to ISDN standardization. In order to speed up the progress of national and European ISDN standards, Germany and three other European countries –France, Italy, and the United Kingdom –cooperated to accelerate the setting of a common ISDN specification and to plan interconnection of their ISDN networks [13]. The enthusiasm of Germany in ISDN is both for standardization of the main ISDN equipment (interface, functional performance, and hardware development) and in several specific ISDN applications to accelerate deployment. It was because of these efforts that Germany has accomplished positive results in its ISDN implementation.

Unlike Germany and other European countries, the US is awaiting standardization. Lack of standardization has affected the market. This, combined with the competing interests of carriers and vendors, has left users confused about the future directions and prospects. One possible way to overcome the technical obstacles is to put national interests ahead of such conflicts. In 1990, there was finally a joint effort when ISDN carriers, vendors, and users decided to determine their own fate by implementing the national ISDN-1 standard. The subsequent recommendations of ISDN-2 and ISDN-3 standards showed the determination of the US to achieve success in ISDN.

## 5.3. Tariffs

Telekom in Germany laid down the conditions and charges for BRI services as early as 1986. One year later, the monthly and traffic charges for PRI were decided. The ISDN tariffs were to be service-independent and correspond to the current telephone tariffs. The tariff provide confidence to all stakeholders (i.e., subscribers, manufacturers, and Telekom).

In the US, the details of ISDN tariffs still have not been worked out among the telephone companies. However, the carriers are expected to follow the telephone tariff structure that has already been established. In the meantime, ISDN subscribers are charged twice as much (by most carriers) for their services as for their regular telephone lines. In order to resolve the issue, all RBOCs have announced their plans for tariff implementation. Only then will the US be in a position to develop effective marketing strategies to increase its chance for ISDN implementation success.

## 5.4. Marketing strategies

The German Bundespost has conceived ISDN as a network for the mass market. It is to be marketed as a low-cost transport service in parallel with other networks, like Datex-P, Datex-L, and permanent data lines. To ensure the economical feasibility and service accessibility of ISDN, Telekom has provided direct financial support to ISDN subscribers and interworked ISDN with the telephone, Datex-L, and Datex-P networks. Additionally, systematic introduction and marketing strategies were established to promote ISDN. These included advertising ISDN in a wide range of publications since the early 1980s. This strategy contributed significantly to the public confidence needed for long-term ISDN planning: the internal marketing strategy was to position ISDN as a focus of advice, or even the best networking solution for specific types of applications.

Unlike Germany, the US does not have an unified force to market ISDN services. Most of the ISDN services and products are marketed by network providers and equipment vendors. They have often projected their business and products into their description of ISDN, leaving the potential end-user confused about the benefits, scheduled availability, and price/performance relationships of ISDN services. The US network providers have not established a preference policy for their sales strategy. Decisions have been left to the customers.

Why network providers in the US did not push for ISDN services? In a competitive environment, the market is demand driven, and new products appear only if they can meet some real or perceived demand of customers. According to a survey conducted by

Lai and Guynes [9], some of the largest organizations in the US rejected ISDN because its services were replicable and expensive. These organizations also expressed their disappointment with the efforts ISDN providers made in promoting ISDN. When customers do not demand ISDN services, and suppliers, seeing no opportunity to earn a profit, will not attempt to devote efforts in pushing ISDN services.

## 6. Conclusions

Our empirical findings reaffirm the presence of a difference in the extent of ISDN implementation and diffusion between the US and Germany. In Germany, ISDN has been more widely accepted as a network solution to satisfy the organization's communication needs. In the US, the adoption rate and implementation extent of ISDN were still low. Lack of government support, inconsistent implementation standards, absence of tariffs, and ineffective marketing strategy have been proposed as the prohibiting factors for acceptance of ISDN in the US. A more comprehensive model must be developed to capture the variations in diffusion of ISDN among all countries (particularly Pacific Rim countries) and to assess accurately the effects of such variables as government policy and cultural factors.

The findings of this study are useful in understanding the current status of ISDN implementation in the US and Germany. Additionally, they suggest significant managerial implications for designing effective strategies for ISDN introduction in other countries.

He received his Ph.D. in Information Systems from the University of Texas at Arlington, M.S. in MIS from the University of Arizona, and B.S.S. from the Chinese University of Hong Kong. His articles have appeared in Information and Management, Data Base, Information Systems Management, and many others. His current research interests include database design, network  
management, and technology management.
