# Expert system applications in business: A review and analysis of the literature (1977–1993)

Bo K. Wong $^{a,*}$ , John A. Monaco $^{b}$

$^{a}$ Youngstown State University, Department of Management, Williamson College of Business Administration, Youngstown State University, Youngstown, OH 44555-3071, USA

$^{b}$ Youngstown State University, Pittsburgh Technical Institute, 635 Smithfield Street, Pittsburgh, PA 15222, USA

## Abstract

A survey of expert system (ES) business application papers published between 1977 and 1993 indicates that an increasing amount of ES research is being conducted for a diverse range of business activities. The classification of literature by (1) year of publication, (2) application area, (3) generic problem area addressed, (4) problem domain, (5) level of management, (6) level of task interdependence, (7) means of development, (8) corporate/academic interaction in development, and (9) technology integration provides some insights in the trend. Implications to ES developers are discussed.

Keywords: Expert systems; Artificial intelligence; Business application; Literature review; Literature analysis; Decision support systems; Neural networks; Technology integration; Joint venture; Task interdependence

## 1. Introduction

Over the past two decades, expert system (ES) technology has emerged as an important development in the field of information science. Once merely experimental in nature, ESs have evolved to become practical tools used in aiding the decision making of many businesses. As this evolution has occurred, a growing literature on the topic of ESs has appeared, with a considerable portion focusing on actual development for business applications. The goal of this paper is to examine this historical trend and to explore potential research areas in the future.

## 2. Research methodology

To identify those journal articles which describe specific ES applications, an extensive search was conducted of the literature. Much of the methodology used in conducting this search was taken from the work of Eom, Lee, and Ayaz [15]. Both the scope of the bibliography and the criteria used in selecting articles are presented in Appendix A. Readers should be cautious in interpreting the results of this research, since the findings are based on data collected only from journal articles. The results will therefore not include all the actual ESs. Second, we have only reviewed academic/professional journal articles only. Conference proceedings and doctoral dissertations are excluded, as we assume that high quality research is eventually published in academic/professional journals. Also many foreign journals are not included in our review since they are not within the scope of our computer/manual searches.

## 3. Classifications

A total of 380 articles were used in this study. As each article was reviewed, it was classified as follows:

1. Year of publication,

2. Application area (accounting/auditing, finance, human resources, information systems, marketing/distribution, production/operations, and strategic management),

3. Generic problem area addressed (monitoring, planning, prediction, diagnosis, design, interpretation, debugging, control, instruction, and/or repair),

4. Problem domain (structured or semi/unstructured),

5. Level of management (operational, tactical, or strategic),

6. Level of task interdependence (personal support, group support, or organizational support),

7. Means of development (programming languages or ES tools/shells),

8. Corporate/academic interaction in development (independent effort or corporate/academic joint venture), and

9. Technology integration.

Table 2 shows the research emphasis within each application area; e.g., in production/operations, the emphasis has been on developing applications for production scheduling/planning and quality control. This is not surprising since these two areas present some of the most important problems for operations managers and high potential for improved problem resolution through the use of ES technology [43]. In addition, Kusiak and Chen [33] found that ESs, combined with traditional operations research techniques, such as scheduling algorithms and mathematical programming algorithms, are very successful in addressing these two areas.

In the area of finance, relatively more ES applications have been published in the field of insurance underwriting. Insurance companies cannot depend solely on mortality tables. Years of experience are required to master fundamental insurance decisions. ES can be used to help even inexperienced underwriters rate the financial and medical risks to insurance companies concerning their customers' life, accident, and disability insurance policies [20].

ESs have been extensively used in a variety of areas in information systems, especially in information retrieval. ESs' capabilities allow them to help users retrieve information in a more effective and efficient manner [21].

## 6. Classification by problem domain

Managerial and other business decisions can generally be categorized as structured or semi/unstructured. About 89.2% (339 articles) of the applications were found to be designed for semi/unstructured decision analysis, while the rest were designed to address structured problems. This is not at all surprising. Many business decisions are semi/unstructured, partially due to a certain degree of uncertainty in the knowledge. According to Giarratano and Riley [23], the sources of errors that create uncertainty can be ambiguity, incompleteness, incorrectness, measurement, systematic errors, random errors, and reasoning. Techniques such as classical probability theory, certainty factors, and/or fuzzy set are incorporated into many ESs and have greatly enhanced their ability to address relatively unstructured problems.

## 7. Classification by level of management

We further classified the applications by year according to their managerial decision level, as shown in Table 5. Approximately 59.2% of the applications (225 articles) were for operational level decisions and 33.4% (127 articles) were developed for tactical decisions, while only 7.4% were strategic level.

Generally, operational level problems are good candidates for ES development, especially for production and operations. Much has been written about the potential of ESs for strategic management $[5,27,31,47,48,65]$ , but only 28 articles were found; this suggests that the authors are wrong or that the potential has yet to be realized.

## 8. Classification by level of task interdependence

The ES applications were also classified in terms of the type of decision making for which they were designed. These can be represented by three levels of increasing organizational task interdependency, as suggested by Thompson [72]. The first occurs on the personal level, which includes ES applications designed to support a single user or a single class of users in a task that is distinct and relatively independent of other tasks. A second is seen at the group level. This focuses on applications designed to support groups who are engaged in separate yet highly interrelated tasks. Finally, some decision making exist at the organizational level, with diverse operations and individuals.

Of the 380 applications reviewed, 67.1% (255 articles) involve personal decision making. About 31.3% (119 articles) are group decision making, while only 1.6% are designed for organizational decision making.

Organizational support often refers to problems requiring somewhat generalized expertise. The six areas are capital budgeting $[52]$ ; marketing planning $[66]$ ; information retrieval $[82]$ ; staff placement $[16,71]$ ; and staff scheduling $[32]$ .

## 9. Classification by means of development

Generally speaking, ESs can be developed either through the use of programming languages, such as PROLOG or LISP, or ES tools/shells, such as VP-Expert [79]. Our survey found that 40.8% (155 articles) of the published ES business applications have been developed by using ES tools/shells. The other were developed using programming languages.

Table 6 shows the distribution of ES application articles by this means of development and year. There appears to be no prominent increase in the use of one means of development over the other.

ESs have been developed by academic institutions, business-related institutions, or through joint efforts. As shown in Table 7, the distribution by the means of development was classified by the types of development institutions. The results seem to indicate that there is no preference for either academic or business-related institutions to choose the means of ES development.

## 10. Corporate / academic interaction in ES development

Our survey found that a vast majority of the ESs published (343 articles, or 90.3%) were the result of the research efforts of either academic or practitioners. Only 9.7% were the products of joint ventures.

There can be several advantages to such joint ventures. By combining resources, joint efforts can reduce the cost of development and provide good academic exercises. For example, through combined financial contributions, the Air Force Office of Scientific Research, Westinghouse Electric Corporation, and Carnegie-Mellon University developed ISIS, a successful ES for factory scheduling [19].

By uniting academic expertise with corporate knowledge, an ES project may have less risk and hence a better chance of success. Blue Cross and Blue Shield (BC/BS) of South Carolina, in a joint effort with the University of South Carolina, successfully developed an ES for insurance claims review [84]. BC/BS reduced its risk by establishing milestones that allowed them to stop the project at the end of each phase if a problem arose.

Joint research efforts can also reveal a “middle ground” for ES applications development; projects that historically may not have been considered “sophisticated” enough for academic research can still be important to organizations. Hence, corporate/academic joint ventures could help produce more “real world” applications.

## 11. Classification by technology integration

The integration of ESs with other technologies, such as DSSs, neural networks, or robotics, can improve the applicability of ESs in addressing various types of problems. However, our survey found that only 4.7% (18 articles) of the published research describe the development of ESs that are integrated with one or more other technologies. These include ESs that are integrated with DSS [14,51,64,67], neural networks [1,10,58,59], robotics [11,56], imaging [7,45,54], CD-ROM LAN [4], voice interface [42], data base management systems [60,63], and natural language interfaces [17]. Possibly ES technology is still in an embryonic stage in business application and there may be technical difficulties in integrating business ESs with other technologies.

## 12. Profile of ES business application development

During the period covered, 380 articles on ES business applications were published. These articles were authored by a total of 779 persons. Of these, approximately 67.1% (523 persons) were affiliated with 172 different academic institutions. The remaining 32.9% (256 persons) were affiliated with 103 nonacademic or business-related institutions. Approximately 72.7% of the academic institutions (125 institutions) were in the US. Eighty-two of the 103 business-related institutions (79.6%) were US.

Approximately 20.5% of the articles reviewed (78) concern ES applications that were actually implemented in business. The rest were in the prototype or developmental stage.

## 13. Implications to ES developers

Developers should not overlook the fact that ESs can address more than one generic application; “real world” applications usually involve multiple decision processes. An advanced manufacturing system, for example, presents processes of production planning and engineering design and control. If the focus is on replacing a single human expert’s knowledge, it is likely that ES developers may ignore the potential of developing an ES that integrates multiple decision making in order to address more than one generic area. Such a narrow focus can reduce the overall success of the system. ES developers should strive to avoid such pitfalls.

## 14. Implications and suggestions for future research

Our analysis of the ES application literature has revealed several unaddressed topics. In particular, we suggest that future research should address the development of organizational and strategic level ESs that integrate decision making throughout an organization, and the integration of ESs with other technologies.

14.1. Development of organizational and strategic level ESs

A vast majority of the published articles on ES applications addressed personal level or group level problems. Also, most were on ES for operational or tactical level decisions.

Expertise is hard to \_extract from humans, especially for highly unstructured, complex, and wide domain problems. Also it is subject to errors due to perceptual and judgmental biases [75].

These limitations may explain the lack of organizational and strategic ESs. Many researchers have already recognized their importance [6], but integration have to be cross-functional (horizontal), cross-planning (vertical), business-level, or global.

The challenge is thus to develop organizational level ESs, while finding ways to integrate the decision making of these systems among functional, planning, business, and global units of organizations.

## 14.2. Integration of ESs with other technologies

One way of achieving integration may be to consolidate ESs with other technologies that are more capable of integrated decision making, such as DSSs and neural networks. Researchers have addressed such potential integration $[9,30,68,74,87]$ . In particular, Turban and Watkins $[77]$ have proposed two models. The first involves integrating an ES with one or more of the components of a DSS: the database, the model base, and the interface. This would allow the ES to act as a consultant to the DSS; alternatively, it could act in support of the ES. A second model for ES/DSS integration involves utilizing the ES as an additional but separate component of the DSS.

Such integration could overcome some of the limitations of stand-alone ESs in the realm of strategic level management, since DSSs are generally better equipped to handle broad, ad hoc, or unique problems, than are ESs. Goul, et al. [25] found that using a system based on ES/DSS integration could improve a decision maker's performance in a strategic planning environment by helping identify problems and providing relevant theoretical models.

The integration of ESs with neural networks may also have potential for improving the quality of decision making in organizations, since neural networks surpass ESs in some circumstances that do not involve experience $[69]$ . In many unstructured decision environments, particularly those that involve classification, associative memory, or clustering, neural networks offer distinct advantages over ESs $[28]$ . In fact, ES/neural network integrations have been successfully utilized in some application areas. The ES can be employed to train neural networks, control information flow through several neural networks, and analyze the responses provided by the neural network, etc.

## 15. Conclusion

The future of ESs should see increased integration, both from the development of ESs which are capable of making strategic/organizational decisions and from the integration of ES technology with other existing or developing technologies. As advancements are made in AI technologies and computer-based related systems, there should be new opportunities to apply existing ES technology for business ES research in the future.

## Scope of the bibliography

The first and most important step in the literature retrieval process was a search of the ABI/INFORM data base. Both mainframe-based and CD-Rom versions of this data base by UMI/data courier were searched for the period covering January 1970-December 1993. This step provided access to abstracts of articles from over 800 different business-related journals world-wide. By using the descriptors “artificial intelligence,” “expert systems,” and “knowledge-based systems,” we were able to retrieve 2,770 abstracts for review from the specified twenty-four year period.

Next, we manually searched the Business Periodical Index (BPI) for the period of January 1970 through December 1993. The BPI indexed 343 business-related periodicals. Only articles from journals not included in the ABI/INFORM data base were considered for further review.

The next step involved a reference search of various textbooks on ESs and related topics. A total of thirty-six textbooks was examined: Alty and Coombs [2], Buchanan and Shortliffe [8], Chorafas [12], Coombs [13], Forsyth [18], Gevarter [22], Harmon and King [27], Hayes-Roth, Waterman, and Lenat [29], Holsapple and Whinston [31], Leigh and Doherty [34], Levine, Drang, and Edelson [35], Liebowitz [36], Manheimer [37], Martin and Oxman [38], McGraw and Harbison-Briggs [41], Michie [44], Mockler [47], Mockler [48], Mockler [49], Mockler and Dologite [50], Negoita [53], Olson and Courtney [55], Pigford and Baur [57], Reitman [62], Simons [70], Turban [73], Turban [74], Turban [75], Turban and Liebowitz [76], Vasarhelyi [78], Waterman [80], Waterman and Hayes-Roth [81], Weiss and Kulikowski [83], Winston and Brown [85], Winston and Prendergast [86], and Zahedi [87].

The textbook search, together with the ABI/INFORM and BPI searches, provided articles from a wide variety of journals. However, it was decided that an additional eleven journals should be searched individually. The decision to include these additional journals was based on two reasons: (1) several journals known to publish ES articles were not included in the ABI/INFORM data base nor BPI, and (2) many of these additional journals are recognized by management information system (MIS) experts as being important to the field of MIS [24,26]. These eleven journals are Artificial Intelligence, Communications of the ACM, IEEE Software, IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering, IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, International Journal of Man – Machine Studies, International Journal of Production Research, Journal of Management Information Systems, Journal of Manufacturing Systems, Ω: The International Journal of Management Science, and Expert Systems: International Journal of Knowledge Engineering. Each of these journals was manually searched for the same twenty-four year period.

## Selection criteria

Every article retrieved through the process described above was carefully reviewed before making a decision regarding its inclusion in this survey. We required each article to discuss the prototype or development of an ES application for business. This requirement eliminated many of the articles retrieved from the ABI/INFORM data base, since the descriptors used produced abstracts from numerous articles that did not describe business applications. In addition, we examined the research methodology of each article retrieved. Only those articles based on rigorous research methods were included.
