# Introducing The Journal of Strategic Information Systems - the new approach to information systems management

Information Technology (IT) needs to be taken seriously, its management needs to be integrated into the mainstream activities of executives and we need to do much more in ensuring that IT 'delivers the goods' by providing the business and social benefits that we seek from it.

These are messages that we have heard for a number of years now. But it still seems to be the case that many managers do not take IT seriously: in fact some dismiss the topic as an irrelevance. In business schools around the world, it is often the case that IT, IT strategy and information management are considered at best as optional topics unworthy of being included in the core programme. And where they do receive greater attention, it is often the case that a discussion of information and IT issues is not integrated into other business topics. And while great concern is being expressed over the amount of expenditure on IT, our methods of measuring — and harvesting — benefits remain rudimentary.

The Journal of Strategic Information Systems seeks to do something about this sad state of affairs. It will provide a forum for debate on these and associated issues. Recognizing both the strategic importance and potential of information, it will present papers from both the academic and business worlds which will draw attention to these key issues, and provide practical lessons in dealing with them from both state-of-the-art research and current experience.

The Journal will provide an international, balanced and integrated perspective on such topics as:

\- organizational re-engineering and the role of information systems and technology in this context;

● the justification and evaluation of information systems, and strategies to ensure that benefits ensue from such investments:

● the business implications of emerging ITs:

\- human resource issues associated with the integration of information systems issues with business;

● the implementation of business systems strategies and the management of change;

● the determination of strategic information requirements and the development of information systems that meet these requirements.

An international perspective is assured by the considerable effort taken to build an editorial board of established academics and practitioners from around the world. Balance will be provided through both research papers and practical case studies, and through an objective evaluation of the worth and contribution of IT in business and society. Integration will be a theme which permeates each of the contributions to be found in the Journal. For example, it is not our intention to consider the topics associated with IT in isolation but very much as part of the business and management processes.

The format for future issues of the Journal will be a viewpoint paper by a leading figure in the world of business and/or academia, followed by a number of research papers and case studies. In addition, book reviews will be included to provide our readers with information on those books and reports which may be useful additions to one's personal or organizational library. The usual length of the Journal will be 64 pages. This is a special launch issue with a slightly shorter page length.

The integration of IT within business and management processes is taken up in this issue by David Lei and Marion Sobol whose paper looks at ways and means of closely coordinating manufacturing activities with information systems and networks. Mikko Ruohonen's paper deals with the necessity to take into account the viewpoints of different managerial groups when undertaking strategic information systems planning studies, while the attention of Blaize Horner Reich and Sid Huff is on the customer when considering strategic information systems. The two case study papers, by Tony Bidgood and Bob Jelley, and by David Targett deal with two quite different but nonetheless extremely topical subjects. The former describes an approach to developing an information architecture following an analysis of corporate information needs, while the latter looks at a Pan-European initiative which is investigating the role of IT-related topics in business education as we move towards the millennium.

The Editorial Board and the publishers hope that you find this a rich mixture of ideas and comment which will make the Journal of Strategic Information Systems essential reading, whether you are in the world of business, government or academia.

Professor Bob Galliers, Warwick Business School, University of Warwick, UK, December 1991
