Keywords: business performance, IT strategy, IT innovations, scoping

The Development Bank of Singapore (DBS Bank) undertook a major business planning exercise in the mid-1980s, as part of its overall strategy to gain a distinct competitive advantage in the banking industry. The Bank adopted a strategy to rely on information technology (IT) to support its business strategy growth by concentrating equally on all areas of banking, namely retail banking, corporate banking, investment banking and treasury operations. Systematic and well-planned approaches in using new and emerging technologies for its multifarious banking and trade financing operations has put DBS Bank in the forefront of the banking industry. In 1992, DBS Bank won the National IT Award in the private sector: the citation from the National Computer Board, Singapore read, 'In today's competitive banking world, DBS Bank stands out as the pre-eminent bank in Singapore and the region. DBS Bank's strategic investment in IT and the close collaboration between the bank's business and IT functions will help maintain its leading position' (see Appendix A). Launched in 1990, the National IT Awards give recognition to organizations in Singapore which have excelled in achieving superior business performance through the strategic application of IT.

According to DBS Bank's chairman Mr Ngiam Tong Dow, 'Our mission for the 1990s is to leverage on our pre-eminent position in Singapore to be a leading bank in the Asia-Pacific region. Towards this, there can be no deviation from the underlying essentials — a dedicated and able core of management and staff, a commitment to productivity and strong computer support'.

## DBS Bank — an overview

Singapore is a small island about 42 km in length and 23 km in breadth. Singapore became a fully independent and sovereign nation in 1965 on separation from Malaysia. Today, Singapore's GNP per capita is second only to that of Japan in the Asia-Pacific region. IT has played a significant role in the economic development of the country. The World Competitiveness Report 1990 ranked Singapore No 1 in the 'Extent to which computer-based IT is effectively used' and the 'Number of top managers understanding and using IT' among the 34 OECD (Organizations for Economic Co-operation and Development) and NIEs (Newly Industrializing Economies) that were studied. According to a survey by the National Computer Board, Singapore, over 80 per cent of establishments with more than 10 employees view computers as having powerful impact on management and strategic operations.

DBS Bank was established as a fully licensed domestic commercial bank in 1968 as part of Singapore's industrial development policy. Owned jointly by the Singapore government and private shareholders, DBS Bank is listed on the Stock Exchange of Singapore. It has overseas offices in New York, Los Angeles, London, Tokyo, Osaka, Seoul, Taipei, Hong Kong, Jakarta, Beijing and Bangkok. As at the end of 1993, DBS Bank had a total of 45 domestic branches and 162 automatic teller machines (ATMs). DBS Bank is the largest bank in Singapore in terms of profits, assets and shareholders' funds. Growth of the DBS Bank in the past five years is given in Table 1. The DBS Group reported an after tax profit of almost S\$300 million in 1992. Its deposits account for nearly 30 per cent of the total deposits in the banking industry in Singapore, while its loans and advances account for nearly 22 per cent. It offers a wide range of financial services in corporate and consumer banking, investment banking, international banking and treasury services. The Bank also provides through its various subsidiaries many other specialized financial and related services such as insurance, stockbroking, venture capital investment, real estate development and corporate advisory services. Its major competitors are the Overseas Chinese Banking Corporation (OCBC) and the United Overseas Bank (UOB).

## IT planning in DBS Bank

IT planning in DBS Bank is a conscious activity. The organizational chart given in Figure 1 brings out the Bank's reliance on IT. The Data Resources and Systems group is responsible for the overall IT planning, as can be seen from Figure 2. The Technical Support Group is responsible for all the operational aspects of computing. The Technology Planning Group is entrusted with the task of keeping abreast with emerging technologies, and educate the users on the impact of recent IT developments on banking services, such as the applications of artificial intelligence, image processing etc. The two Application Groups work on all the main areas of banking, namely retail banking, ATM/telephone banking, investment banking, corporate banking and treasury operations. DBS Bank has been very successful in using technology to gain market share and productivity improvement. It relies extensively on IT for strategic business growth. Following the major planning exercise in 1983 to understand the information requirements, it was imperative that DBS Bank expands its IT applications considerably in order to retain its competitive edge in the banking industry in future. Realizing the potential offered by the developments in computers and communication technologies, DBS Bank chose a number of innovative IT applications in retail banking, investment banking, corporate banking and treasury operations. The choice of computer systems to support such systems was also critical. DBS Bank chose to continue its relationship with IBM. All the existing applications were easily upgraded onto the new platform. Many computer professionals were recruited to undertake the development of new systems. The software development staff strength in DBS Bank more than tripled from 50 staff in 1985 to more than 150 by 1992. The Bank's philosophy is to exploit the main strengths of its technological infrastructure to meet the needs of all its corporate and retail customers.

Management of IT projects has been very crucial for the successful IT applications in DBS Bank. For every IT project, a senior user management staff at the level of a Vice-President is chosen as the project leader. It is the Vice-President who owns the project, and not the IT group. All activities and interactions between the user group and the IT group are coordinated by the Vice-President in charge of the project. Project management is crucial in ensuring that systems are developed on time and within budgets. The key to successful project management at DBS Bank is scoping. Every project is designed to produce some deliverables at the end of nine months. 'Nine months is a natural waiting time for anything good to happen', says Mrs Yee Jee Hong, Senior Vice-President in charge of Data Resources and Systems. This period of nine months is divided into three subperiods: three months for planning and design, three months for development, and three months for training and implementation. All long-term projects are divided into cycles of nine months' duration. This approach to systems design and development has paid rich dividends to the Bank, as the Bank is able to announce new products as per a time schedule.

## IT applications in DBS Bank

DBS Bank started using computers to support its operations in 1978 with a medium-sized IBM/370, and has since then been aggressive in using IT. In the early stages, computers were primarily used for routine data processing activities and in the area of retail banking. In 1985, DBS Bank chose a number of innovative applications for computerization, resulting from the planning exercise the Bank undertook to understand the information requirements in each of its areas of operation. A few IT applications which were pioneered by the Bank are given below — see Appendix B for a brief write-up on a few other IT applications.

## Autobank cashier's order equivalent

In the area of retail banking, DBS Bank was the first bank in Singapore to offer ATM facilities to its customers to get cashier's orders in order to apply for new shares. During any new share issue, all the Banks' branches were attracting a large number of customers. People often had to wait for hours to purchase cashier's orders for their applications of the new shares. With the new system, any DBS customer with an ATM card can make a purchase transaction in a few minutes at any time of the day or night by responding to simple queries on the ATM screen. The required payment will be automatically deducted from the customer's account and a cashier's order printed on the ATM terminal itself. An added advantage was that if the customer was not successful, he did not have to go to a branch to return the cashier's order. His account would be updated automatically, saving him another wait and a trip to the Bank.

The Bank also benefited from the application in many ways. First, a large float was generated when customers who purchased the cashier's orders had to deposit their money into their accounts but the money was not withdrawn until the underwriters presented their cheques after the last date for the application of allotment of new shares. Second, the system also attracted new customers. Those who wanted to benefit from the application had to open an account with the bank and get an ATM card to use the facility. Third, more companies chose DBS Bank to help them in their listings on the stock exchange because they believed they would get a higher subscription rate with this convenient feature provided to potential investors. In fact, DBS Bank was the largest underwriter of initial public offerings in 1991 — a market share of 60 per cent.

## Autolink

In the area of corporate banking, DBS Bank announced Autolink in 1988. With Autolink, the Bank's corporate customers could connect their PCs to the Bank's systems to check on their balances. It enabled the corporate customers to have better cash management. They did not have to go to the branches to check on transactions. Autolink supported most of the enquiry functions. A new system, DBS Corporate Plus which is an extension of Autolink was later introduced. Using this system, executives can authorize transactions remotely by entering their authorization code through a cordless device. This is somewhat similar to authorizing purchases through credit cards. This facility is currently offered only to the Bank's corporate customers. This cordless device is relatively inexpensive, small in size, and the technology not over sophisticated. It is gaining more and more acceptance from corporate clients.

## Share custody system

In the area of investment banking, DBS Bank introduced a share custody system. This was introduced at a time when the stock market was attracting more foreign funds from banks and individuals to invest in the local bourse. The Share Custody System is an online real-time system for processing, monitoring and reporting on transactions which include (a) receipts, withdrawal and registration of share scripts, (b) statements of contracts resulting from sale and purchase of shares, and (c) dividend income and rights distribution. The system offers stringent security controls for segregation of duties and supports extensive reporting for management, statistical, operational and audit purposes. This system was developed by the Bank's own systems staff on the IBM mainframe. Some foreign banks such as Bangkok Bank and Hong Kong Bank have now become interested in this system. This system is helping DBS Bank to build strategic alliances.

## CPF investment account

Another pioneering application on investment banking was announced by DBS

Bank when the Central Provident Fund (CPF) investment fund was introduced in 1985. Under this new scheme, CPF-holders could use their CPF funds to invest in selected stocks and shares in the Singapore Stock Exchange. DBS Bank was the first to have a computer system to support this scheme in all its branches. The Bank won 50 per cent of the market share with this system.

## Autolinking facilities

DBS Bank has been equally innovative in the area of delivery systems as well. Autobanker is a 24-hour service available to the Bank's ATM customers. This service offers complete banking services round the clock, such as updating personal particulars, obtaining a statement of all transactions in the current month, requesting cheque books, applying for demand drafts etc. Besides cash withdrawal and deposits, ATM customers can also use their own telephones through which they can enter their authorization code to gain access to their accounts with the bank for their banking needs.

## Car quota bidding system

Another innovative application in delivery systems is the use of ATMs for submitting applications for the allotment of new vehicles, known as the car quota bidding system. In Singapore, there is an upper limit on the number of private vehicles on the road, and so each individual/dealer wishing to buy a new car has to submit his bids as per the government policy and obtain a Certificate of Entitlement (COE). DBS offered its ATM customers a facility to enter their bids electronically on the ATM screen, and thereby saved the trouble of getting and filling up forms and sending them through the postal system. Motor dealers, who do not have ATM cards, could use Autoview (a service supplied through Teleview on television) to bid on behalf of their customers. DBS Bank was the first bank in Singapore to introduce this system because of its well-developed infrastructure, and gained about 25 per cent of the market share over two to three months.

## Conclusion

DBS Bank has been consistently using technology to package new products and services to meet the specialized banking needs of all its customers. Cooperation from the top management and active involvement of all the end users are some of the key factors in the successful applications of IT in DBS in its pursuit for excellence. The Bank's mission for the 1990s is to become No 1 in the Asia-Pacific region.
