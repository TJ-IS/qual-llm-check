# Information technology enables business process reengineering at YCH DistriPark (Singapore)

K V Ramani

Professor, Indian Institute of Management, Ahmedabad 380015, India

Robert Yap

Managing Director, YCH Group, YCH DistriPark, Singapore

Francis Pavri

Senior Lecturer, National University of Singapore, Singapore

The Yap Chwee Hock Distribution Park (YCH DistriPark) in Tuas, Singapore started as a passenger transportation company in the 1950s, changed its business to cargo transportation in the 1970s, and is today offering 'total logistics'. Information technology (IT) has played a significant role in the business process reengineering at YCH in transforming the company to its current status as a regional leader in offering total logistics. Total logistics at YCH starts with operating a group of central distribution centres or warehouses with storage systems tailored for each client's products, handling chemicals for DuPont, electronics for AIWA, medical products for Roche and so on. It then adds a package of services like container haulage, international sea/air freight forwarding, inventory control, etc. Three major IT applications devised by YCH, namely the Warehouse Management System, Freight Management System, and the Logistics Management System are integrated with the country's electronic data interchange networks TRADENET and PORTNET in providing total logistics. In 1992, YCH won the National Computer Award in the Local Enterprise category, in recognition of achieving superior business performance through strategic application of IT.

Keywords: business process reengineering, total logistics, EDI networks, IT strategy

Landscaped greenery and wide roads greet the visitors as they enter the S\$20 million, 7.8 hectare complex of warehouses in the Yap Chwee Hock Distribution Park (YCH DistriPark) in Tuas, Singapore. The YCH DistriPark is equipped with a high density racking system and a S\$5.4 million state-of-the-art storage and retrieval system. It maintains an inventory worth S\$200 million at any point in time. But what strikes visitors most are the sophisticated computerized systems of storing and retrieving cargo which range from chemicals to electronic products. In 1992, YCH won the National Computer Award in the Local Enterprise category; the citation from the National Computer Board, Singapore stated that 'IT is a key component of YCH's business strategy, creating increased value for its clients and providing YCH with the differentiating factor in the highly competitive transportation industry' (see Appendix A).

Singapore is a small island about 42 km in length and 23 km in breadth. Singapore became a fully independent and sovereign nation in 1965 on separation from Malaysia. Today, Singapore's GNP per capita is second only to that of Japan in this region. IT has played a significant role in the economic development of the country. The World Competitiveness Report 1990 ranked Singapore No. 1 in the 'extent to which computer based IT is effectively used' and the 'number of top managers understanding and using IT' among the 34 Organization for Economic Co-operation Development (OECD) and Newly Industrializing Economies (NIEs) that were studied. According to a survey by the National Computer Board, Singapore, over 80 per cent of establishments with more than 10 employees view computers as having a powerful impact on management and strategic operations to gain competitive advantage.

## Business process reengineering at YCH

Established in 1955 as a small local passenger trucking outfit, YCH transports made a strategic move in 1977 to change its business to cargo transportation. Subsequently, YCH expanded its business to include warehousing and freight forwarding. By 1991, YCH had established itself as Singapore's premier logistics operator. Total logistics at YCH starts with operating a group of central distribution centres (CDC) or warehouses with storage systems tailored for each client's products, handling chemicals for DuPont, electronics for AIWA, medical products for Roche and so on. It then adds a package of services like container haulage, international sea/air freight forwarding, IT support, and computerized inventory control. A list of services offered by the YCH group can be found in Appendix B.

The growth of YCH business can be seen from Table 1. The group's turnover increased from S\$1 million in the late 1970s to S\$50 million in 1993. Current projections call for the turnover to grow to S\$75 million by 1994 and S\$100 million by 1995. Its client list includes 12 multi-national corporations (MNCs), while its nearest competitor serves only two MNCs. The Group aims to become an MNC itself eventually as Singapore gears itself for an enhanced role in the global economy. Guided by the Group's slogan, 'Your Partner in Total Logistics', its business strategy is to forge strategic alliances with its world class multi-national clients, thereby becoming an integral part of their regional and international operations.

## Information technology strategy at YCH

IT has been fundamental to the expansion of the YCH business from a passenger trucking company to offering total logistics. 'Had it not been for my previous venture in the computer trade, I would not have used IT so widely in my business,' admits Mr Yap. Yap's involvement in computers began with Kompusat, set up in 1982 as a joint venture with one of the Royal family of Negeri Sembilan. Mr Yap undertook the distributorship of Eagle computers in Singapore. This gave him an excellent opportunity to understand the power and flexibility of computers, even though his computer business was a failure. Kompusat was sold off in 1986 and the remaining inventory of computers incorporated into his company for word processing and accounting purposes. This simple move triggered a PC-based computerization process that catapulted YCH transport into the ranks of today's progressive local companies known for their strategic IT applications.

A major pillar of the organization is its conscious decision to exploit technology as a means of maintaining a high standard in productivity. The importance of IT at YCH is clear from the YCH organizational chart given in Figure 1. YCH ploughs back 15 per cent of its net profits in IT investments every year. With revenues expected to reach S\$100 million in a few years, the company relies heavily on IT to help it handle more clients, transport larger volume of goods, make better use of manpower, and increase productivity.

## Case studies of YCH's clients

In this section we describe the experience of some of YCH's clients who have benefited extensively.

## The DuPont experience

YCH designed and customized a computerized inventory system and a WMS to meet the specific requirements of DuPont. Flow of information between the warehouse personnel is facilitated by a 'Warehouse Data Communication System' which is a fully computerized real-time monitoring system. These systems support the use of sophisticated high rise stacker in relation to narrow aisle pallet racks, and have led to dramatic improvement in space usage and turnaround time for DuPont. Under this operation, DuPont pre-informs YCH on their incoming shipments to YCH. This enables YCH to plan the inbound logistics operations to collect and store the shipment. The outbound logistics operation to DuPont's customers is triggered via instruction from DuPont. These operations are handled by the integrated WMS, LMS and FMS. WMS handles the picking of goods from the warehouse. Full container load (FCL) shipments are handled by the LMS whereas less than a container load (LCL) shipments are handled by the FMS. Coordination between land transportation and the booking of containers for sea transportation are done online via the EDI network PORTNET. Outward declaration of goods is done via the Trade and Development Board's TRADENET. Such application of modern technologies at DuPont Regional Distribution Centre has helped to reduce human error and give rise to better monitoring of goods movement. As YCH is able to provide the entire logistics value-added activities, DuPont is able to treat YCH as a 'black box' to handle their needs.

## The AIWA experience

AIWA has benefited extensively by using the LMS and WMS at YCH. For AIWA, YCH picks up goods from AIWA's local manufacturing plant in specially designed trucks. Upon arrival at the CDC, the goods are unloaded, and their quantity and locations are keyed into the AIWA inventory control system. When an order is received from Tokyo, an operator climbs into a stacker equipped with a computer terminal which will tell him how many of each item are required and on which aisle and on which rack they may be found. The retrieved stock is then left on the floor to be picked up by a forklift equipped with a similar computer and taken to the container or truck in the loading bay. Locating cargo stored deep inside the warehouse used to take up to 45 minutes but now, with computer support, this job is done in less than 5 minutes and with fewer workers. Any request from AIWA, ranging from from inventory checks to status of shipment, is satisfied at the touch of a few buttons in the YCH computer; soon AIWA will be able to access the YCH computer directly. YCH also has projects underway that will integrate AIWA manufacturing data with YCH's inventory software, thereby eliminating the need to re-enter data. By moving into YCH DistriPark, AIWA has been able to reduce its space requirements for housing its goods from 30 000 m $^{2}$ spread across three locations to about 10 000 m $^{2}$ under one facility.

## The Roche Chemicals experience

Roche Chemicals is a beneficiary of the Integrated Logistics Management System, integrating all three modules — WMS, FMS and LMS. Roche has relocated its physical Asia–Pacific regional distribution centres from Hong Kong to YCH DistriPark in Singapore. This strategic relocation is made possible through YCH's forward integration from a mere transport company to a high-tech total logistic operator. Electronic data communication between Roche in Hong Kong and YCH in Singapore controls the goods movement from Europe to the Singapore hub for eventual redistribution to the Asia–Pacific region. Roche uses LMS to process logistics requests between the Singapore port and its warehouses at YCH DistriPark. WMS processes the receipt, storing, and picking of goods within the warehouses. Finally, all the freight forwarding requirements are taken care of by the FMS. The electronic link between the Roche CDC at YCH and the Roche regional office in Hong Kong has enabled both parties to eliminate duplicate data entries in their exchange of documents. This has resulted in significant manpower savings, faster throughput and accurate document exchange. Roche in Hong Kong is able to log-on to the YCH system to do detailed real-time enquiries of their stocks and status of their goods movement. With this arrangement, Roche is able to instruct YCH to receive, store and deliver their goods efficiently while YCH is able to feedback to Roche on the details of their shipment electronically. This symbiotic relationship is forged through mutual long-term commitment so that YCH is able to plan and strategize the use of IT to serve Roche's business needs better.

## Conclusion

YCH has been able to achieve its present success because it believes in teaming with its clients to take advantage of business opportunities through strategic and innovative use of IT. The mission of YCH is to become the No. 1 total logistics operator in the Asia-Pacific region.

## National IT Award (Local Enterprise) — a citation

YCH Transport started as a trucking business in the 1950s. Today, it is a sophisticated provider of integrated logistic service. IT is a key component of YCH's total logistics concept which offers clients a comprehensive range of services, including transportation, warehousing and freight forwarding.

The highly automated YCH DistriPark provides a collection of modern distribution facilities with integrated IT systems to offer clients the highest level of service.

The firm's Warehouse Management System optimizes the management of cargo in the warehous. It allows the storage, tracking and collection of cargo in the most optimum fashion resulting in increased operational efficiency. Use of wireless bar code readers for stock tracking provides the quantum leap in warehouse productivity and also improved accuracy.

The Logistics Management System coordinates the freight forwarding, fleet management and fleet scheduling functions at the DistriPark. Through this system, YCH has been able to improve its responsiveness to customers and to commit to guaranteed shipping lead times. For faster clearance of clients' goods, YCH has established EDI links to government bodies through networks such as PORTNET and TRADENET. Clients also have access to the status of their shipment by dialling into the YCH system from their office.

IT is a key component of YCH's business strategy, creating increased value for its clients and providing YCH with the differentiating factor in the highly competitive transportation industry.

## Appendix B

## YCH Group profile

The Yap Chwee Hock (YCH) Group comprises companies specializing in a variety of key logistics functions such as warehousing, transportation, international freight forwarding, bonded services, logistics management and consultancy.

## Warehousing

YCH's warehousing and storage facilities are located in the prime industrial area (Tuas) along Singapore's notable distribution belt (Pasir Panjang) and well served by highways and major trunk roads. Its facilities boast of several features: (i) state-of-the-art storage and retrieval equipment such as narrow aisle racking system, high rise stackers, and warehouse data communication system; (ii) strong IT applications for fleet, traffic, warehouse and logistics management functions; (iii) expertise to design customized warehousing and distribution facilities, and so on.

## Transportation

Reputed to be one of Singapore's most experienced transport operators (since 1955), the services that YCH provides are both comprehensive and effective. YCH's unique five-year fleet renewal policy ensures that its clients are consistently supported by the latest equipment and machinery. Besides, YCH boasts of (i) a modern fleet of prime movers, chassis, containers and lorries; (ii) advanced software systems for accurate and effective fleet and traffic management; (iii) EDI such as PORTNET for online access to shipping information; (iv) expertise in customizing specialized transportation equipment to meet clients' varied needs, and so on.

## International freight forwarding

One of the major strengths of YCH is its wide-ranging network of major sea and air freight agencies around the world. With over 40 agencies and links to most of the major shipping lines of international repute, its comprehensive sea and air forwarding services are both efficient, prompt and reliable. In this area, YCH has to its credit (i) EDI such as TRADENET for access to trade-related information; (ii) comprehensive range of support services such as door-to-door delivery, customs clearance, shipment tracking, warehousing, packing/unpacking, documentation, insurance, etc.

## Logistics management and consultancy

YCH's management and consultancy comprises a group of highly trained and experienced logistics specialists. Working in multi-disciplinary task groups with professionals from other fields such as business development, information technology, operations research, etc, YCH is able to manage its clients' projects from incubation to final completion. Its expertise is also available on an external consulting basis for a wide range of functions: (1) requirements study; (ii) long-range logistics planning; (iii) feasibility study; (iv) logistics software design and development, etc.

## Bonded services and regional operations

Its bonded services aim to facilitate the expansion of major corporations who are seeking to participate in this fast growing Asia-Pacific region. YCH, with its concrete plans to regionalize in phases throughout most of the major Asian cities, is therefore well positioned to support its clients. Through its network of affiliated companies and alliances in this region and internationally, the YCH team works with each client to develop customized bonded warehousing and transportation needs on a partnership basis. Its services include: (i) feasibility study; (ii) business intelligence study of industry; (iii) project management, and so on.
