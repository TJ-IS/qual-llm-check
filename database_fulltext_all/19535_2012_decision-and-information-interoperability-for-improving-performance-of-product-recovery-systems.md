# Decision and information interoperability for improving performance of product recovery systems

Jitendra Madaan <sup>a</sup>, Pradeep Kumar <sup>a</sup>, Felix T.S. Chan <sup>b,</sup>⁎

## a b s t r a c t

Enterprises around the world are employing product/resource recovery practices to overcome global competition, meet heightened environmental regulations, and seek additional pro<sup>fi</sup>t making opportunities. These factors in addition to the inherent cost and complexity of recovery processes are due to multidimensional factors and relationships associated with the quality, variety, timeliness, demand changes, and logical processing of product returns. A Reverse Enterprise System (RES) perspective will give scope to develop a <sup>fl</sup>exible system that can handle products with various options and greater return volumes and variability. It explores the various recovery functions and product lifecycle stages and suggests some key business and performance measurement strategies that can be employed to be successful in returns handling. We observed that different recovery nodes act in a relatively autonomous fashion to decide what type and level of <sup>fl</sup>exibility to use, what and when the information is required to be shared for improving decision performance and obtaining bene<sup>fi</sup>ts from the recovery process. Finally, this paper proposes a semi/partially and fully <sup>fl</sup>exible decision model that facilitates decision and information interoperability functions from the perspective of an enterprise engaged in or planning to be engaged in product recovery processes.

## 1. Introduction

Ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently designing and operating product recovery systems to handle reverse <sup>fl</sup>ows, have been gaining academic as well as practitioner's attention. This interest in product recovery, especially the broader concept, reverse logistics, is not surprising considering the hidden potential, i.e. improved customer image, input data for development initiatives, raw material and spare parts. Determining the requirements of synergy between decision and information systems is required to properly support product returns processes, but has been identi<sup>fi</sup>ed as a gap in theory by many researchers [2,17,23]. Studies have demonstrated a loss of information associated with a product after selling is one of the major obstacles for recovering value from returns [25]. Therefore, a systematic approach for modeling product recovery decisions, and in particular, the interoperable role of product information in improving the effectiveness of product recovery decisions, is essential [10,32]. The use of information technology with the emerging mail-order, online purchases and after-sales services, such as the maintenance of guaranteed products, has contributed to the increase of returns. Later with the growing consciousness on environmental issues, enterprises call for gradually reducing their consumption of resources and the amount of waste materials produced. Further, sustainability theories increasingly support the enterprise's role in extending the “end of used” or disposed products back to the producer, in a way of Extending the Producers Responsibility, i.e. EPR [19,32], leading to the need for conceptualization of a robust product recovery as an enterprise system.

Enterprises are now becoming aware of the importance of focusing their efforts on activities surrounding the return and processing of unused products. They seek to restructure, reorganize, support and plan these activities so as to make the present forward and reverse supply chain systems more <sup>fl</sup>exible and ef<sup>fi</sup>cient. With an adequate integration of <sup>fl</sup>exible product recovery activities, in an economic or environmental context, organizations will be able to notice a double effect. First, while focusing efforts on the returns of products and their processing, competitive decision and information strategies can be set up which, at various levels, will contribute to a better performance of current activities in the supply chain, concentrated until now primarily on the distribution of new products. Secondly, an innovative <sup>fl</sup>exible reverse enterprise system can improve the effectiveness of product recovery decisions under uncertainties. The aim of this innovative product recovery system is to ensure clean and adequate distribution of recovered products along with the normal operation of the forward <sup>fl</sup>ow of products as shown in Fig. 1. This <sup>fi</sup>gure depicts cyclic synergy between the individual forward and reverse <sup>fl</sup>exibility contributing towards enterprise <sup>fl</sup>exibility leading to improvement in overall enterprise performance. In contrast to the forward system, the Reverse Enterprise System (RES) supplies the means of ef<sup>fi</sup>cient planning, managing and controlling relevant information from the consumption point to the starting point, so as to counter the overall production cost of the supply chain [5].

This study looks further and demonstrates a systematic interoperability approach for modeling product recovery decisions, and in particular, synergizes the role of product availability and quality information in improving the effectiveness of decisions in RES. Later a <sup>fl</sup>exible product recovery model is proposed that can demonstrate the effectiveness of product information and decisions in RES under uncertainties [16,24,28].

On re<sup>fi</sup>nement these proposed strategies could further be used while choosing the recovery option for a returned product while designing a reverse enterprise system. Although this research focuses on product recovery decisions, it is noted that the models and methods developed can be applied to other decisions made during the forward <sup>fl</sup>ow.

## 2. Requirement for interoperable decision and information model for RES

One of the most serious problems that the enterprises face in the execution of a RES is the requirement of interoperable and robust decision and information systems [14]. RES is typically a boundaryspanning process taking care of returns between enterprises or of the same enterprise, thus proposing a model that has to work across system boundaries adds additional complexity to the problem. Therefore, <sup>fl</sup>exible decision and information interoperability in a RES seems much more important. Returns information captured should be integrated with forward supply chain information to achieve optimum planning and reduction of costs. The whole support network can then be designed in a way that can serve both forward and reverse product/information <sup>fl</sup>ow ef<sup>fi</sup>ciently. This is in line with the concept of designing an information system with an integrated product recovery decision system. The synergy of such information and decisions is the foundation and prerequisite for an RES that runs with high quality [4]. The aim of this section is to illustrate the motivation for an interoperable decision and information model to improve the performance of the product recovery system. The impact of information visibility is well manifested by the bullwhip effect in forward supply chains [13]. Similar to forward supply chains, if decisions are well synchronized with information, it can play an important role in effectiveness management and also in improving performance of the product recovery process [6,8,21].

Further, to establish the requirement for the Decision and Information Synergy (DIS) to improve the performance of an RES, we therefore categorize these requirements for a model according to the feasibility criteria proposed by Krikke [12] (i.e., technical, economic, and ecological), associated with product recovery decisions and information. This discussion leads to a <sup>fl</sup>exible product information and decision model that can help in conceptualizing the evolution of information associated with a product, and manner in which it will be encapsulated by RES [9].

## 4. Interoperable decision and information model for improving recovery performance

While every system has features of aggregation and dependency in order to assemble several units together with certain purpose, then to make them and become a system with a certain function [29]. Product <sup>fl</sup>ow in a supply chain is that same integration of upstream enterprises, i.e. Forward Supply Chain (FSC), and downstream enterprises, i.e. Reverse Supply Chain (RSC) regarding the core enterprise(s) as the center. Therefore, the aim of the interoperable DIS $( \mathrm { i . e . }$ sharing of transformed actionable information between the nodes) is to improve appreciation of the value <sup>fl</sup>ow by means of effective information integration and support between forward and reverse partners, in order that all resources, technology, organization and information related to the enterprise's business activities are integrated effectively to form an overall advantage [11,27]. To achieve higher <sup>fl</sup>exibility we require greater levels of decision information sharing.

Thus, there may be a need to arrive at judicious combination of <sup>fl</sup>exibility and decision information sharing for each system through explicit modeling of <sup>fl</sup>exibility and DIS. An analytical model that computes DIS delays in RES will allow user to quickly compute the value of reducing these delays.

Therefore, we de<sup>fi</sup>ne DIS in terms of the expected bene<sup>fi</sup>t achievable with the available information and decision sharing. Information is said to be complete when the state of the product can be known with certainty [31]. This ‘information’ in the context of the product recovery decision can be de<sup>fi</sup>ned as follows:

Let E represent the information already available to the decisionmaker. $\mathrm { w h e r e {  } } \tilde { E } = \mathrm { E _ { k } } : ( \mathrm { k } = 1 , 2 , . . . . , \mathrm { n } )$ represents the set of product parameters.

Here e represents the list of parameter values that is measured in <sup>\~</sup>E that support the decision regarding the state of the product $\left( \mathrm { { h } _ { i } } \right)$ Therefore, decision-maker is said to have information regarding the state of the product for the purpose of making the product recovery decision if product observes all the parameters in the set <sup>\~</sup>E, given by the expression:

$$
\text { where } \rightarrow \mathrm{p} (\mathrm{h} _ {\mathrm{i}} | \tilde {e}, \mathrm{E}) = \left\{\begin{array}{l}1, \text { if   } \mathrm{i} = \mathrm{j}\\0, \forall \mathrm{i} \neq \mathrm{j}\end{array}\right.
$$

If E represents the set of information already available to the decision maker the expected outcome that can be obtained when the decision-maker chooses the best recovery option for a product after obtaining enough information such that its state is known with complete certainty is given by the expression:

$$
\text { where } \rightarrow \mathrm{EU} _ {\mathrm{E}} = \sum_ {\mathrm{i} = 1} ^ {\mathrm{Nh}} p (h i | \mathrm{E}. \max _ {\mathrm{dj}} [ \mathrm{u} (\mathrm{h} _ {\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{d} _ {\mathrm{i}} ]
$$

It provides the expression for the expected bene<sup>fi</sup>ts that can be obtained with DIS delays. This leads to the expected bene<sup>fi</sup>t from obtaining complete information associated with a product returns. The expected bene<sup>fi</sup>t by reducing delays and selecting best recovery decision on time, EBI can be expressed as the difference between the expected bene<sup>fi</sup>ts that can be obtained when the decisionmaker chooses the best recovery option without delay (since its state is known with complete certainty) and the expected bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of making the decision with delays (since its state is not known with complete certainty).

$$
\text { where } \rightarrow \mathrm{EBI} _ {\mathrm{E}} = \mathrm{EU} _ {\mathrm{E}} - \mathrm{EU} _ {\mathrm{E}} (\mathrm{d})\tag{1}
$$

Essentially, above Eq. (1) presents a special case for previous expression, where $\tilde { E }$ reduces delays. The expected bene<sup>fi</sup>t of reducing DIS delays associated with the product information depends on the probability distribution that the decision maker has attributed to the status of product received and quality of information available. The maximum expected bene<sup>fi</sup>t that can be obtained from reducing DIS delays related with a product recovery is given by the expression:

$$
\text { where } \rightarrow \mathrm{EBI} _ {\max} = - \frac {\varepsilon_ {1} \varepsilon_ {2}}{\varepsilon_ {1} + \varepsilon_ {2}}\tag{2}
$$

Here, $\varepsilon _ { 1 }$ and $\varepsilon _ { 2 }$ denote the DIS delay penalties incurred due to erroneous decisions. Combining Eqs. (1) and (2) we can deduce maximum bene<sup>fi</sup>t function in terms of penalties associated with DIS delays. Where the decision ${ \sf d } _ { \mathrm { e } }$ has to be taken for best recovery option with delay on the basis of the available information E is given by the expression [22]:

$$
\text { where } \rightarrow d _ {e} = \left\{\begin{array}{l}d _ {1}, \text { if } p \left(h _ {1} | E \geq \frac {\varepsilon_ {2}}{\varepsilon_ {1} + \varepsilon_ {2}}\right)\\d _ {2}, \text { if } p \left(h _ {1} | E \leq \frac {\varepsilon_ {2}}{\varepsilon_ {1} + \varepsilon_ {2}}\right)\end{array}\right.
$$

This observation will be further clari<sup>fi</sup>ed through simulation results shown in Section $5 ,$ which plots the maximum expected cost bene<sup>fi</sup>t that can be achieved from complete product DIS as a function of the delay at varying operational <sup>fl</sup>exibility levels.

## 6. Conclusion

This paper has demonstrated the impact of different levels of information sharing, timeliness of decision sharing, impact of <sup>fl</sup>exibility and the role of DIS delays and penalties on overall system cost performance. We observed that different nodes act in a relatively autonomous fashion to decide what type and level of <sup>fl</sup>exibility to use, what and when the information is required to be shared for improving decision performance and obtaining bene<sup>fi</sup>ts from the recovery process. A reverse Enterprise System can be looked on as an extended enterprise situation with coordinated nodes willing to share more information with good timeliness and <sup>fl</sup>exibility, and generate cost reduction of up to 45% from the recovery process.

Similarly, looking at RES in the virtual enterprise context, one expects a further decrease in information sharing but a greater use for <sup>fl</sup>exibility for dynamic product recovery conditions. This study examines product recovery operations of a manufacturing supply chain where there is a dominant node, so that the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) often plays a supervisory role and DIS delays are minimized. Here, we show how the ready availability of information regarding the critical product parameters, indicating the extent of usage, can improve the decision effectiveness in the product recovery process. This indicates that the decision integration across the chain is a critical factor for ensuring effectiveness of the recovery process. The series of studies presented in this paper show the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of DIS delays in various <sup>fl</sup>exibility type scenarios. This study has illustrated the importance of DIS, which in turn affects the ef<sup>fi</sup>ciency of product recovery operations. The low margins and increasing volatility of returned products make timely information and decision making a high priority. Additionally, useful tools such as radio frequency are helpful. New innovations such as two-dimensional bar codes and Radio Frequency Identi<sup>fi</sup>cation Devices (RFID) may soon be in use extensively. Technologies like bar coding and RFID help in making product recovery operations more effective and responsive [20,30].
