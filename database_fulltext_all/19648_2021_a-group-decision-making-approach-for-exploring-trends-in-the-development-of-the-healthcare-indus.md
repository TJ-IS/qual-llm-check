## Abstract

The increased awareness of the importance of global healthcare which has formed an inseparable relationship with the quality of hum res earchers to pay attention to trends in the development of the healthcar appropriate development strategy recommendations. The modified Z-numbers decision-making trial and evaluation laboratory (called the modified Z-DEMATEL) technique is adapted to construct the mutual influential relationships and prioritize these trends. The Z-number method optimizes the conventional fuzzy numbers and increases the reliability of expert evaluations to reflect the confidence of the evaluation environment under uncertainty. The modified Z-DEMATEL provides a complete linguistic evaluation and its membership functions, and generates a set of influence weights of the criteria. Real healthcare development trends in Taiwan are taken as an example to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model. The results show that the internet of things and telemedicine are important future trends in Taiwan. This paper provides useful and reliable management suggestions to help the healthcare industry promote innovative technologies.

Keywords: Healthcare industry; Group decision; Z numbers; DEMATEL

## 1. Introduction

With societal development, comes the awareness of the importance of improving and updating healthcare. The quality and breadth of healthcare services have profound impacts on the people’s health. Within the medical system, the hospital is often seen as on of the units consuming the major proportion of medical resources. How to reduce the wastage of medical resources and improve the efficiency of hospital services is essential [1]. Therefore, the development of the healthcare industry and encouraging innovative R&D capabilities are goals common to governments of all countries [2]. Advanced countries are facing the problem of an $\mathbf { a } _ { \mathbf { \mu } _ { \infty } } ^ { \mathbf { \dot { \mathbf { \alpha } } } \bullet \mathbf { \tilde { \mathbf { \alpha } } } \mathbf { \Phi } }$ population, so that many healthcare services are in short supply. The healthcare industry must pay attention to providing rapid services to patients, while ensuring accurate diagnoses, reliable resource control, and efficient management of medical systems. Furthermore, the healthcare industry needs the support and integration of many industries to develop more diverse medica services. These issues interlock and have complex interactions with one another [3]. More advanced countries have incorporated new technologies: three-dimensional (3D) printing, artificial intelligence (AI), virtual reality (VR), radio frequency identification (RFID), the internet of things (IoT), and

blockchain into the healthcare industry [4-19]. The technologies mentioned above require cross-industry cooperation. Unfortunately, the existing healthcare development studies discuss how to integrate information technologies with healthcare services individually. There has been no comprehensive exploration of the developmental trends in the healthcare industry, nor has the dependence of these development trends on each other been discussed.

The analysis of the mutual influential relationship among the development trends of healthcare technology is a typical multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) problem. MCDM is a decision analysis method based on soft computing, which has demonstrated excellent analytical performance in a complex evaluation environment [20]. It is difficult for researchers to use historical quantitative data to construct a decision model for things that have not yet happened. It is feasible to establish a systematic assessment framework based on the extensive experience of practitioners and the expertise of experts [21, 22, 23]. Mardani et al. [3] identifies the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) method as one of the most frequently e healthcare field. However, although the AHP assumes that there is no influence between factors, that is, the assumption of independence, these medical and healthcare development trends (MHDTs) should complement one another through mutual influential relationships. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to determine the mutual influential relationship among these MHDTs as well as their development priorities.

Common MCDM methods used to explore the mutual influence among factors include the interpretive structural modeling (ISM) [24], the decision-making trial and evaluation laboratory (DEMATEL) [25], and the criteria importance through inter-criteria correlation (CRITIC) techniques [26]. The CRITIC method treats the correlation coefficients of the variables as input values. However, when the number of samples is too small, it is difficult to accurately reflect the correlation among variables. In ISM, the variables 0 and 1 are used to construct the influential network relationship of the factors. Although its operational process is simple, the potential management information is insufficient. Only the influential relationship is obtained without the degree of influence. DEMATEL optimizes the evaluation performance of ISM for construction of the network influential relationship and influential degree among the factors as an influential network relationship map (INRM) [23].

This study proposes a modified Z-DEMATEL technique, which not only considers the uncertainty of the decision environment, but also easures the reliability (confidence) of experts in the evaluation. This method provides a complete linguisti valuation level and Z-number membership functions. Furthermore, the model is an expansion of the work of Lo et al. [27] who generated a set of criteria/projects/trends influence weights through the modified Z-DEMATEL indexes of total influence. The modified Z-DEMATEL can be used to obtain the causality and priority of each project/trend, which can be applied to formulate the most appropriate development trends for the healthcare industry. At present, there has been very little research on the Z-DEMATEL technique. The existing Z-DEMATEL studies only discuss the interdependence of the criteria but do not explore how to obtain the criteria weights. The modified Z-DEMATEL proposed in this study does not require complicated calculations like the DANP (DEMATEL-based ANP) does, but only needs to determine the total influence to generat the criteria weights. In addition, this study compares the conventional DEMATEL and fuzzy DEMATEL technologies. The results show that the proposed model improves the shortcomings of the above two techniques, thereby more accurately transforming expert judgments into computable quantitative data. In summary, the contributions and characteristics of this study are described below.

i. This study identifies and compares eight MHDTs in the healthcare industry in Taiwan to support decision-makers and practitioners in formulating the optimal strategy for future development. ii. The proposed model determines the mutual influential relationships and prioritizes the eight MHDTs. The INRM can identify which MHDTs are causal affecting healthcare development. iii. The modified Z-DEMATEL considers the uncertainty of the evaluation environment and the confidence of the experts when generating a set of criteria influence weights.

## 2.2. Combination of AI and healthcare technology $( C _ { 2 } )$

The development of AI can help to organize thousands of healthcare records into systematic knowledge. This technology has the ability to learn and analyze quickly and can constantly correct itself to build better predictive models. Computer systems are used to calculate and simulate the characteristics of human thoughts and behaviors. These technologies are widely used in health examinations, disease diagnosis, and surgical procedures [8]. Characteristic data can be stored digitally to help medical personnel reduce the cost of building physical records. AI can improve the efficiency and accuracy of patient visits and can even understand people’s physical state to make the most appropriate dietary control recommendations. AI can provide accurate and rapid treatment judgments for complex operations and can be used to assist in planning, checking and formulation of follow-up procedures to minimize medical risks and maximize treatment efficiency [7]. The initial cost of AI is high, but it effectively improves the quality of healthcare services. In the long run, it can reduce the need for manpower and medical institutions. For example, smart medical systems can be adapted to meet urgent healthcare needs in rural areas. Moreover, it can facilitate discussion of the commonality of common and rare diseases, and even connect the world’s top medical teams to help treat patients with rare or complex conditions [30].

## 2.3. Application of VR to assist with patient rehabilitation and physician education (C<sub>3</sub>)

With advancements in hardware and software, the application of VR is becoming more popular. This technology simulates a real-world environment, allowing users to enjoy an immersive experience [9]. In medicine, VR allows the use of innovative technologies and ideas for patient rehabilitation and physician training, including robotic surgery, surgical simulation, and phobia treatment. It can assist physicians to practice medical procedures within a limited time in order to increase their practical emergency situations [10]. VR technology can be used to examine the diseases of patients from multiple perspectives, greatly improving the efficiency of shared decision-making. Physicians can use VR simulations to better communicate with patients about medical plans and rehabilitation procedures [11] which can also help ease uneasiness over treatment.

## 2.4. Installation of sensors and global positioning system (GPS) to obtain physiological information in real time (C<sub>4</sub>)

The development and application of biosensors and trackers to improve healthcare systems and infrastructure is one of the most challenging and compelling goals today. Biosensors can effectively detect and record features with great sensitivity [15]. Biosensors, trackers, and GPS are used mainly to collect the patient’s physiological status and location data. Such devices could read the data every hour and then record them in the medical database allowing patients to measure physiological data on their own to replace the intervention of medical personnel. Even after the patient leaves the hospital, medical staff would still be able to monitor the patient’s physical condition remotely [13, 14].

## 2.5. Promotion of retail clinics and urgent care centers $( C _ { 5 } )$

Retail clinics, designed mainly to provide preventive health services such as health checkups, vaccinations, treatment of minor diseases and development of related health plans, are usually set up in centers can handle fractures or minor burns, and can provide immediate care for patients of all ages. Similar to non-emergency medical procedures, retail clinics and urgent care centers have become alternatives to accessing hospital emergency services. However, patients with major life-threatening conditions still need to be transferred to hospitals for treatment [6]. The advantages of retail clinics and urgent care centers are that they have longer hours of operation compared to general clinics, and medical expenses are also relatively low. Retail clinics are usually located in places with convenient

transportation, and the waiting time for patients to see a doctor is also shorter than in general hospitals.

## 2.6. Development of telemedicine to reduce the cost of consultation $( C _ { 6 } )$

Telemedicine refers to situations where the medical staff care for the patient remotely, analyzing electronic patient-related information to provide professional medical knowledge. Such an approach can reduce the frequency of patients having to go to the hospital and improve the efficiency of visits for patients with reduced mobility or in remote areas [12]. Telemedicine is not merely used for patient diagnosis, it also includes rehabilitation and care. In addition to being more convenient for the patient, medical staff have more time to interact with patients at a distance, and it also reduces the risk of cross infection. In addition, smaller hospitals can use telemedicine to immediately contact large medical institutions, and discuss and consult with multiple professionals to counter the shortage of healthcare resources in remote areas [31, 32].

## 2.7. Integrating IoT into healthcare services $( C _ { 7 } )$

IoT covers a wide range of software technologies, including cloud storage, messaging and communication technologies, and cloud computing. The increasing demand for healthcare service efficiency and experienced treatment has led to the rise of the medical internet of things (MIoT) [16, 33]. The MIoT mainly provides remote monitoring of health conditions, which could for example, assist the elderly in life care, identify and manage chronic diseases, control medicines for special diseases, reduce emergency waiting times, and facilitate the monitoring of medical resources.

The difference between this trend and trend 4 (Subsection 2.4) is that MIoT combines wireless body area networks and RFID to collect relevant medical data for storage in the cloud, effectively eliminating the movement of medical personnel and patients. The IoT supports service management, because all available medical information can be stored in the cloud, and cloud computing used to offer reliable healthcare advice [17]. However, IoT assisted medical services can only be used for common diseases. For rare diseases and unusual symptoms, it is still necessary to rely on doctor consultation.

## 2.8. Incorporation of blockchain technology into the medical cash flow system (C<sub>8</sub>)

The blockchain allows network data storage, verification, transmission and communication through distributed nodes. By relying on algorithms to encrypt all transaction data, it solves the most worrying security trust problem encountered in the IoT. There is no need for third-party intervention, only a consensus between users is required to conduct transactions, which lowers the cost. The blockchain to analyze and communicate healthcare-related information [18].

In addition, the blockchain provides a database platform for healthcare. Patients could establish a dedicated blockchain account to record medical information. This platform would allow patients to share healthcare information with hospitals, insurance companies, and research institutes without personally going to all the hospitals that they have visited to apply for medical records. If the information and cash flow systems of insurance companies and hospitals can be integrated, patients and insurance companies can more systematically pay medical expenses to the correct medical unit [19].

## 3.2. Modified Z-DEMATEL technique

The DEMATEL technique can identify the mutual influential relationship among factors, through a structural INRM, to help decision-makers understand which are the main factors affecting others, and which are the affected factors [36]. The conventional DEMATEL use crisp values to describe the influence among the criteria. However, in an evaluation environment that is both complex and full of uncertain factors, it is difficult for experts to use crisp values to reflect their true judgements. Many fuzzy theories have been combined with the conventional DEMATEL to consider uncertainty. Unfortunately, general fuzzy theories ignore the degree of confidence of the experts in the evaluation value. The modified Z-DEMATEL uses two fuzzy parameters, the evaluation value, and the reliability of the evaluation value, to alleviate this problem. Thus, not only can we know the reliability of the decision-making group in the evaluation, but also the form of triangular fuzzy numbers for calculation to avoid any loss of information. In particular, the modified Z-DEMATEL can generate a set of influential criteria weights and determine their priority. ## 6. Management implications

Healthcare is an emerging industry with huge market potential. Healthcare products, nutritious foods, medical appliances, health management and consulting are all closely linked to human health. People today not only want to preserve their health, but also hope to prolong life. Taiwan has listed as one of the countries with an aging population, and the government actively promotes long-term care 2.0 policies to meet the needs for healthcare services. The relationship between future development trends is discussed in depth in this article. Expert-based information is converted for quantitative analysis through soft calculation methods to construct a systematic decision-making model, which is effective for practical applications [39]. A MCDM model is proposed to identify the mutual influence of MHDTs on each other. Management implications obtained from the results of the analysis are discussed below.

First, according to Table 7, the top two most important development projects are IoT (C<sub>7</sub>) and telemedicine (C<sub>6</sub>) (with influential weights of 0.141 and 0.138, respectively). The application of the IoTs in healthcare involves many projects, including Ambient Assisted Living (AAL), Internet of m-Health Things, and Community Healthcare. Each service will cover information and the Internet, resource sharing, cross-connections and agreements between heterogeneous devices, indicating that the IoT is at the core of the future development of hea lthcare. Mackichan et al. [33] and Sadoughi et al. [16] showed that the integration of IoT can greatly improve the efficiency of healthcare services to meet the medical needs of more people. Telemedicine is the second most important healthcare development project which is consistent with the results of Dullet et al. [31]. They concluded that the development of telemedicine can reduce patient wait times, transportation costs, environmental pollution, and so on. In Taiwan, the gap between urban and rural healthcare services is very large. Patients in rural areas often have to spend a lot of time travelling to hospitals in larger centers. Consequently, the majority of users of healthcare services are urban residents. Telemedicine could solve the problem of access to healthcare services in remote areas. For medical institutions themselves, the reduction in onsite patient density could reduce the

demand for medical staff, thereby increasing operational flexibility for the organizations. Moreover, take the novel coronavirus outbreak (COVID-19) as an example, where habitual gatherings and cross-border travel have caused serious human-to-human infection. At this time, the diversification of healthcare services reveals becomes more important. Telemedicine can provide patients with access to medical consultation while avoiding the gathering of a large number of individuals in clinics and hospital and elimination the danger of cross-infection.

Second, as can be seen in the INRM presented in Figure 1, the main factors affecting the overall healthcare development system are AI and healthcare technolog $( C _ { 2 } )$ , real time patient’s physiological information $( C _ { 4 } )$ and IoT $( C _ { 7 } )$ . These three projects not $^ { \mathrm { 5 n l } } .$ y have a mutual influential relationship with one another, but also significantly affect the others. This result reflects the current state of the global healthcare environment. The integration of the IoT is the most important and the core to the system, as sensing and positioning must rely on wearable and related hardware devices. After the sensor collects a converted into useful information provided to the medical institution. In other words, $C _ { 2 } , C _ { 4 }$ and $C _ { 7 }$ are interlocking technologies, and the lack of any of them will make the intelligent healthcare system incomplete. Other projects are reliant on the support of $C _ { 2 } , C _ { 4 }$ and $C _ { 7 } .$ . For example, it is difficult for groups that cannot express physical and mental sensations (e.g. infants, small children, people with severe disabilities) to communicate effectively with medical staff, so data such as body temperature, pulse rate, and blood glucose levels must be obtained through sensors. In addition, both retail clinics an telemedicine need a strong knowledge system to help determine the patient’s condition [5].

Taiwan has a sound health insurance system, and people can access plentiful and convenient healthcare services by paying their health insurance fees regularly. By prevention through the concept of prediction, the probability of people getting sick can be reduced.

## 7. Conclusion

The development of the healthcare and medical industries has affected the quality of human life and played a significant role in lengthening human life spans. This study proposes a modified Z-DEMATEL method that considers the mutual influential relationships among healthcare development projects and shows how the model can be used in real-world decision-making procedures. All development projects should be interrelated to one another, so MCDM methods using independence assumptions such as AHP or BWM are not applicable. In addition, in order to more accurately convert the judgment of the experts, the Z-numbers theory is combined to consider the degree of confidence in the expert evaluation process. The extended management implications of this study may have effects on governmental and medical institution decision-making process in relation to healthcare development. First of all, the sequence of th development project and the weights of the projects are important information for decision-makers. Decision-makers can assign a reasonable budget for each project based on the proportion of the weights. Furthermore, the mutual influential relationships of all projects reflected in the INRM help to find key causes in the process of healthcare development, thus reducing the occurrence of failures and errors.

This model can be extended to the use of MCDM problems in other industries, especially for problems where the interdependence of the criteria and their ranking need to be established, for example, for the development of anti-epidemic policies, the sustainable operation of enterprises, the evaluation of renewable energy, etc. Although this study has contributed to the fields of healthcare development and MCDM, there are still some limitations that must be overcome. There may be other healthcare development projects that have not yet been discussed. In the future, other projects can be added to expand the breadth and depth of this method. Furthermore, even if the proposed modified

Z-DEMATEL can integrate the opinions of multiple experts, there are some other methods for integrating expert judgment that can be considered in future research, such as rough set theory. Finally, there are currently 14 experts participating in this research. To reduce the problem of sample bias, future research needs to increase the number of samples.
