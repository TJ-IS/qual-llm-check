## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Host-guest interaction Room rental market IT-enabled sharing economy Social interaction theory Customer repeated bookings Xiaozhu.com

## A B S T R A C T

The short-term room rental online platforms have boomed in the era of digital business acceleration, as they offer excellent opportunities for interactive behavior between host and guests. However, pursuing customer repeated bookings is a challenge on these platforms. How to increase customer repeated bookings in the short-term room rental market? We examine theoretically and empirically this crucial research question. Drawing on the theo retical foundations of the social interaction theory, we identify and study three host-guest interactions that can affect customer repeated bookings: overall interaction, individual interaction, and offline interaction. We conduct a combination of empirical methods, including text mining and econometrics, using longitudinal data of 36,072 order records and review contents from 5002 listings and 2181 hosts in six cities Xiaozhu.com, which is a prevalent short-term room rental services provider in China. We find that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) and individual host-guest interaction (the existence of individual interaction, absolute text length, and text similarity) increase customer repeated bookings. We also discover that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and similarity) on customer repeated bookings.

## 1. Introduction

The IT-enabled sharing economy (in short, sharing economy) has developed rapidly and gradually permeated all walks of life. As the market scale of related industries expands continuously [12], the sharing economy connects resource providers and demanders through Internet, thereby significantly revamping the idle resources for potential financial benefits [18]. In recent decades, the sharing economy has been further ushered into many fields, especially the tourism industry, which has gained many users’ momentum. With the mounting demand for online customized tourism services, a short-term rental that can provide diversified accommodation has developed significantly. According to a recent report by iiMedia Research, the scale of online short-term room rental users reached 233 million in 2019, and it is expected to exceed 304 million in 2020 [20].

Short-term room rental refers to a service that connects people with idle property capacity and facilities with users who need accommoda tion [31]. As a non-standardized accommodation service, short-term rentals allow consumers to experience unique accommodation experiences at different places [6]. Unlike the traditional hotel industry, guests of short-term room rental services are provided with a wide range of online listing types and personalized autonomous choices. At the same time, it emphasizes the experience of regional cultural customs and the warm and comfortable feeling of occupancy. Pragmatically, online platforms such as Airbnb and Xiaozhu provide users with a short-term rental service through which the host can publish idle listings, and the guest can consult the host online regarding the details of the listing and then book the property through the Internet.

Most of the prior research has starkly focused on customers’ moti vation and intention to use short-term room rental services [25.34]. Besides, two additional blocks of research can be identified in prior research on this topic: examining the host behavior (e.g., [14]) and the impact of short-term room rental services on society [16]. Notwith standing, there is a paucity of research exploring the role of the inter action between hosts and guests through the online-offline process and how this host-guest interaction can affect customer repeated bookings [13]. To build trust and reduce uncertainty, online platforms enable users to participate in the entire transaction process, including selling, buying, comparing, and sharing information about products and ser vices [31]. Specifically, Xiaozhu (one of the leading short-term room rental online platforms in China) encourages hosts and guests to develop interactive behaviors in the platform’s usage. From the lens and focus of Information Systems (IS) research, we examine how online and offline host-guest interactions affect customer repeated bookings.

To pursue our research goal, we embrace the social interaction the ory that studies how individuals interact with each other in a larger social structure (e.g., [27]). As individuals take in various roles in different scenes, their behaviors would be driven by their roles. In light of the three pairs of roles between hosts and guests before, during, and after the stay, this paper identifies three types of host-guest interactions: overall online interaction, individual online interaction, and offline interaction. The overall interaction reveals the hosts’ interactions with all potential customers and happens before the room’s booking. It is an online pre-order host-guest interaction assessed through online reply rate, acceptance rate, and confirmation time. The individual interaction represents the interactions between a host and a certain guest after a booking. It is an online post-order host-guest interaction, and it is assessed through the existence of interaction, timeliness, text length, and similarity. Finally, offline interaction is the face-to-face host-guest interaction during the stay.

We use a combination of text mining and econometric methods to analyze deeply and rigorously gauge the impact of host-guest interaction on customer repeated bookings using longitudinal data of 36,072 order records and review contents from 5002 listings and 2181 hosts in six cities on Xiaozhu.com. Text mining technology is first employed to extract interaction text features [45]. In particular, the word embedding model, which maps words and phrases to vectors for convenient calcu lation [28], is used to train the word vector and text feature vector, and the similarity value is calculated based on the cosine similarity algo rithm. We find that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) and individual host-guest interac tion (the existence of individual interaction, absolute text length, and text similarity) increase customer repeated bookings. We also discover that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of indi vidual interaction (existence, text length. and similarity) on customer

repeated bookings

## 2.1. IT-enabled sharing economy and short-term room rental

While sharing is not a cutting-edge fashion per se, the sharing economy is a surfacing phenomenon incepted in the Internet age [2,10]. This embryonic social phenomenon has piqued mounting attention in recent years. Despite several different definitions of sharing economy can be found in prior research, there are two commonalities in the sharing economy practices: 1) the temporary access non-ownership models of goods and services utilization, and 2) relying on the Internet to carry out the transactions [2,3].

Sharing economy businesses have sprung up in recent decades as a controversial approach to the traditional approach of planning, modeling, and doing business. The sharing economy’s business model is a triadic business model that consists of service providers, customers, and service enablers [18]. The double-sided customer relationship framework can help the service enablers take effective measures to keep all the stakeholders involved in the business profitable, satisfied, and loyal for the long-term interests.

## 2.2. Social interaction theory

The social interaction theory includes a mass of literature about how individuals connect with larger social structures and interact with others [38]. According to this theory, interaction is the motivation of the social behavior of individuals. It affects intimacy with other members and trust in the community, which can further determine purchase intention. The social commerce environment provides participants with rich usergenerated content and varied patterns of interaction, which can maxi mize the convincingness of word-of-mouth marketing [7]. Moreover, customers’ acceptance of interactive activities, such as recommenda tions and consultations, depends heavily on users’ intimacy. As a result, the sellers who benefit most are those who have access to more cus tomers [36], which can be maximized using social platforms such as social media [9].

Interaction is a critical element for an initiative to be regarded as the sharing economy (e.g., [18]). Interaction is the exchange of information, services, and emotions between people, which will affect people’s perception, and then lead to corresponding behavior. There are various types of host-guest interactions that happen in the short-term room rental platform. Existing literature focuses on the host’s response to potential customers’ online consultation [27], feedback on orders or requests of bookings [41], or the impact of the reply on user’s comments. It is rational to expect that the interactions between the host and the guest can affect guests’ behavior.

The interaction is not only reflected in the number of responses but also the quality of interaction. The quality of content will affect users diagnosis and adoption of the information [32]. In particular, the length of the text can affect the content’s perceived usefulness. Thus, the reply’s length is a reflex of the service quality, which would help the host receive more orders. Furthermore, the nature of the interaction is the mutual transmission of information [40]. The information supplier de livers the information to the information recipient through the inter mediary, such that the recipient generates corresponding feedback based on the information obtained. The process of information trans mission completes the interaction [40]. It has a much greater impact on customers’ attitudes and behavior intentions than the traditional forms of marketing that emphasize one-way communication (Castillo et al., forthcoming).

The social interaction theory has been widely used in various aca demic disciplines and research topics, such as the housing demand and supply, urban development, unemployment, and the economy [38]. It focuses on human behaviors, environmental contexts, and the social cognitive process behind the actions. One social aspect of social inter action that has received scant attention is the issue of social roles. So ciety is a network of connected individuals and institutions, such that different individuals have various social roles in society. The role is people’s subjective expectation of individual status [35]. In different positions and scenes, individuals will have different interactive behav iors with others based on their roles. Individuals will build multiple kinds of interactions in social networks based on their different roles. In the short-term room rental market, the resource provider and the demander will take the host’s roles, the guest, and the temporary roommate, and will interact based on their roles’ understandings.

Response to customers’ reviews is a typical form of online interac tion. Currently, prior IS research on digital retailers’ feedback mainly focuses on online reputation management, especially the response to negative evaluation. Consumers often post their experiences in com ments after shopping. To improve customer engagement and satisfac tion, firms go the extra mile to reply to their comments [7]. Due to the comments’ openness, these comments can be seen by other potential users, thus building online word of mouth and digital reputation.

Studies related to comments and replies have largely addressed the impact of replies on customers’ purchase intention and satisfaction. A retailers’ feedback, interpretation, and service compensation have an impact on the service quality remedy of online transactions, the exis tence of replies and the number, length, timeliness, and readability of replies have been confirmed to have positive effects on online sales volume and user satisfaction (e.g., [21]). Hosts and guests can also have offline interactions when they share physical living places. Offline interaction may have beneficial effects on relationship development, such as improving the sense of belonging and familiarity. The inter woven nature of online interaction and offline interaction provides a cling experience and may shape strong bonds. Thus, strong offline interaction may enable lasting ties in the online environment between hosts and guests. All these arguments require to be tested empirically, something that we aim to do in this study.

## 2.3. The proposed research model and hypotheses development

## 2.3.1. Conceptualization of key constructs and the proposed research model

Drawn on the social interaction theory, this study focuses on three types of host-guest interactions in the short-term room rental market (please see Fig. 1): overall interaction, individual interaction, and offline interaction. The overall interaction reveals the hosts’ interactions with all potential customers and happens before the room’s booking. It is an online pre-order host-guest interaction. The individual interaction rep resents the interactions between a host and a certain guest after a booking. It is an online post-order host-guest interaction. Finally, offline interaction is the face-to-face host-guest interaction during the stay.<sup>1</sup>

Generally, interactions at the group and individual levels capture different features of a user’s interactive behaviors. Thus, the three types of interactions represent a broad range of interactive behaviors between hosts and guests. Before placing an order, the potential renter can con sult with the host about detailed information concerning the order (i.e., the room or the home). It indicates the host’s overall interaction pattern at the group level with all potential guests, including consultations and responses to order requests. It can be seen as the overall interaction with other members of the community. This interactive behavior is covered in this study through the concept of overall interaction. During the stay, there are times when the guest rents a shared room and live together with the host in the same home. In this case, they will meet in-person to interact face-to-face [22]. This interactive behavior is covered in our study through the concept of offline interaction [22]. After a completed order, the host and the guest can comment and reply about the customer experience [3]. This interactive behavior is covered in our study through the concept of individual interaction.

Our proposed research model (Fig. 2) suggests two central proposi tions. First, overall interaction and individual interaction can increase customer repeated bookings. Second, the effect of individual interaction on customer repeated bookings can be substituted (moderated nega tively) by offline interaction. We examine overall interaction through (online) reply rate, acceptance rate, and the confirmation time. Indi vidual interaction is studied through the existence of individual inter action, timeliness, text length, and text similarity.<sup>2</sup> Offline interaction refers to the host-guest face-to-face interaction when they share the host’s house or room. Our key endogenous variable is customer repeated bookings that refer to the customer repeated booking of the same host in the same city in the room rental platform. The effects on our endogenous variable are controlled by listing attributes and host attributes.

## 2.3.2. The relationship between overall interaction and customer repeated bookings

The relationship between overall interaction and customer repeated bookings is examined through three effects and hypotheses that argue the influence of reply rate, acceptance rate, and the confirmation time on customer repeated bookings. Considering the information asymmetry in the online market, the online platforms’ information cannot always completely satisfy customers’ needs and expectations. The online plat form allows potential customers to interact with organizations and in dividuals to acquire additional information and reducing uncertainty risk. On the short-term room rental platform, customers can use online chat to get in touch with the host and ask for specific information before placing an order. If the host can promptly answer these questions and provide detailed information, it would be much easier for customers to make the booking decision faster. In this way, the customer will feel that the host takes care of him/her. It is an effective way to improve service quality, and the potential guest can predict and anticipate customer satisfaction. The online reply rate is one of the ingredients of the hostguest overall interaction that may encourage to book a room on the platform. This behavior and argument are valid in both initial and subsequent bookings. We expect a positive influence of the reply rate on the customer’s repeated bookings. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis H1. There is a positive relationship between online reply rate and customer repeated bookings.

We also expect that the acceptance rate and the confirmation time affect customer repeated bookings. The host can accept or refuse a booking order after a customer places it. Except for availability, the decision is also up to the host’s personal preference and the guest’s risk assessment. When the host has a relatively high acceptance rate, the guest can expect that the order is more acceptable for this host, which is conducive to reducing uncertainty and increasing the guest’s expecta tions the order will be accepted [44]. Therefore, in the short-term room rental platform, a high acceptance rate can promote purchase behavior and influence positively customer repeated bookings [32]. Therefore, we hypothesize that:

Hypothesis H2a. There is a positive relationship between acceptance rate and customer repeated bookings.

The waiting time for order confirmation will also affect customer behavior. Timely confirmation of an order is a positive interaction be tween the host and the guest [44]. A shorter waiting time for confir mation will improve the guest’s order experience and positively impact their decision-making. It is rational to expect a negative effect of confirmation time on customer repeated bookings. The shorter the confirmation time, the greater the customer repeated bookings. We thus hypothesize that:

Hypothesis H2b. There is a negative relationship between confirma tion time and customer repeated bookings.

## 2.3.3. The relationship between individual interaction and customer repeated bookings

Our proposed research model includes H3-H6 related to the effect of individual interaction on customer repeated bookings. We argue that the existence of individual host-guest online interaction (H3), timeliness (H4), the text length (H5), and the text-similarity (H6) between comment and reply will increase customers’ repeated bookings. In the ecommerce platform, customers can share their positive or negative experiences of products or services in online reviews and express their satisfaction or suggestions to the merchants (e.g., [24,46]). Reviews have a powerful impact on customers’ purchase decisions [19,43]. On the short-term room rental platform, it allows the host to respond to the comments. This mutual comment mechanism is a means of interaction based on the online reviews system, affecting consumers’ behavior (Hu et a., 2019). Especially in the case of negative feedback from consumers, comment management, like an apology by merchants, is an effective measure for improve customer satisfaction and prevent the possible adverse effects of customers’ online complaints [17], which is conducive to alleviating negative emotions of consumers and improving their satisfaction and loyalty [21]. The existence of individual interaction has been confirmed to have a significant favorable impact on hotel products sales and satisfaction. On the short-term rental platform, hosts can respond to comments, solving specific problems, or welcoming the guest. This is an active feedback behavior that may affect guests perception of service quality. And then it improves their satisfaction and loyalty to the host. Consequently, we theorize that:

Hypothesis H3. There is a positive relationship between the existence of individual interaction and customer repeated bookings.

The timeliness of reply is an important reflection of their interactive enthusiasm. When merchants respond to comments promptly, they can better reflect their willingness to follow up on user’ feedback [44]. It has been argued that the shorter the time between the comments and the reply, the stronger the impact on the promotion of hotel performance. A quick response can improve users’ purchase experience and enhance users’ satisfaction [21]. Likewise, a timely reply to the guest’s comments can improve their living satisfaction. Such interactions can enhance the overall experience, leading the guest to choose the same host for the next stay. We therefore hypothesize:

Hypothesis H4. There is a positive relationship between the in teraction’s timeliness and customer repeated bookings.

For online communication, the longer the content is, the more useful it may be to consumers [37], which is more conducive to solving con sumers’ problems. It has been argued that the increase in reply length reflects the improvement of feedback quality and can have a promoting impact on hotel performance and customer satisfaction (e.g., [27]). On the short-term room rental platform, the longer the content of hosts responses to comments, the richer the information it is supposed to be expressed in the interaction. It also expresses the positive attitude and effort of the host about the interaction with potential guests. As a result, the link between the host and the guest becomes stronger, and the guest’s behavior can also be influenced positively. In this paper, the absolute text length is the word count of the reply, and the relative text length is the ratio between the interactive text of the host and the guest’s comment text. We argue and expect that the absolute and relative text length positively increase customer repeated bookings. Therefore, it is logical to hypothesize that:

Hypothesis H5a. There is a positive relationship between the absolute text length of reply and customer repeated bookings.

Hypothesis H5b. There is a positive relationship between the relative text length of reply and customer repeated bookings.

The quality of interaction is determined by the text’s length and the content itself and its features. The guest’s comments would vary ac cording to their own living experience. In return, the comments received by the same host can be customized or standardized (i.e., based on the software development principle of modularity). Hosts who are willing to participate in the interaction actively provide customized feedback ac cording to every individual comment [33]. In this case, comments and replies share text similarity (a positive sentiment and emotional connection) when the host customizes them. On the contrary, if the host responds to all comments in the same way (i.e., standardized and identical messages to all customers), it may not answer the guest’s questions or handle the guest’s negative emotion. We argue that the host’s message customization to the customer’s comment, assessed as the text-similarity between comment and reply, will improve customer experience and enhance customer repeated bookings. We, therefore, hypothesize that:

Hypothesis H6. There is a positive relationship between the textsimilarity between comment and reply and customer repeated bookings.

## 2.3.4. The moderating effect of offline interaction in the impact of individual interaction on customer repeated bookings

Social interactions are generally categorized into online interaction, supported electronically without requiring physical meetings, and off line interaction, which is defined through face-to-face interactions. Usually, these two are regarded as mutually exclusive. Yet, in many situations, they can coexist within the same community. Under this condition, they can provide communication to increase information flow and encourage each other’s development. Sociologists have argued that offline relations affect online behavior. Interweaving online and offline interactions allow intimate connections to be developed and maintained. The mixture of online and offline interactions - compared to the purely online interaction - among members in an online community can reduce the prevalence of problems the entire community faces, like trust and engagement. If the guest chooses an entire house, considering the advanced technology used currently, such as auto-lock and selfcheck-in and out, the most probable scenario is that the host and the guest will not meet in person (offline). On the other hand, when renting a shared room, the guest may live with the host (i.e., the offline inter action). Face-to-face (offline) host-guest interactions will improve customer experience and engagement. This offline interaction can sub stitute individual interaction (existence, timeliness, text length, and similarity) and their positive effects on customer repeated bookings. Thus, offline host-guest interactions can diminish the positive impact of individual online interactions on customer repeated bookings, suggest ing a negative moderation effect or a substitution effect. We, therefore, hypothesize the following:

Hypothesis H7. The effect of existence, timeliness, text length, and similarity on customer repeated bookings will be negatively moderated by offline interaction

## 3. Research design and research methodology

## 3.1. Data collection

This study focuses on the online platform Xiaozhu.com as the research context. Xiaozhu.com, which was launched in 2012, is one of China’s largest short-term room rental platforms. Xiaozhu’s online platform connects hosts and guests through accommodation services. Hosts and guests can interact online through the platform. According to their needs, guests can filter listing searches on the platform. Several listing attributes are displayed, such as price, area, pictures, location, availability, and past guests’ comments. Information about the host, such as historic orders and other listings from the same host, is also available. Potential customers can start online conversations with the host when help is needed.

We collected the customer repeated booking data from Xiaozhu using a Python program for all the orders in six cities in China ranging from September 2012 to November 2017: Beijing, Shanghai, Guangz hou, Shenzhen, Chengdu, and Chongqing. There are three types of listing on the Xiaozhu: the entire house, shared room, and sofa bed. Since more than 97% of the listings are entire houses and shared rooms, and orders on sofa beds are uncommon, we focused on Xiaozhu-driven rentals of an entire house and shared rooms. After the data cleaning process, the proposed research model was tested on a dataset of 36,072 order records from 5002 listings and 2181 hosts.

## 6. Discussions and core conclusions

## 6.1. Research goal’s follow-up and summary of findings

The IT-enabled sharing economy is booming as more customers seek unique, authentic, and personalized services instead of traditional ex periences. The short-term room rental market and its platforms are an excellent example of the IT-enabled sharing economy’s goodness and opportunities [25]. However, given these platforms’ specificities, keeping customer loyalty is a real-world challenge that can benefit from theorization and empirical analysis in IS research. How to increase customer repeated bookings in the short-term room rental market? This is the cutting-edge research problem and question examined in this study. Drawing on the theoretical foundations of the social interaction theory, we identify and study three host-guest interactions that can affect customer repeated bookings: overall interaction, individual interaction, and offline interaction. The overall interaction reveals the hosts’ interactions with all potential customers and happens before the room’s booking. It is an online pre-order host-guest interaction assessed through online reply rate, acceptance rate, and confirmation time. The individual interaction represents the interactions between a host and a guest after a booking. It is an online post-order host-guest interaction, and it is assessed through the existence of interaction, timeliness, text length, and text similarity (i.e., message customization). Finally, offline interaction is the face-to-face host-guest interaction during the stay by sharing the house or the room.

We conduct a combination of empirical methods, including text mining and econometrics, by using longitudinal data of 36,072 order records and review contents from 5002 listings and 2181 hosts in six cities on Xiaozhu.com, which is a prevalent short-term room rental services provider in China. We find that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) and individual host-guest interaction (the existence of individual interaction, absolute text length, and text similarity) increase customer repeated bookings. We also discover that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and text similar ity) on customer repeated bookings. We find that the online reply rate, the acceptance rate, and the confirmation time explain customer repeated bookings. Once individual interaction happens, the absolute text length and text similarity increase customer repeated bookings. Our study proves that customer repeated bookings in short-term room rental platforms can be increased by replying with longer posts to customers. Text length is associated with the quantity of information provided by the host. and it also expresses the positive attitude and effort of the host about the interaction with potential guests. This study also shows that text similarity between comment and reply enables customer repeated bookings as guests understand the similarity (i.e., message custom ization) as a continued emotional link with the host. This result suggests that message customization adds value and increases customer repeated bookings.

This study discovers that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and text similarity) on customer repeated bookings. This offline interaction refers to the face-to-face interaction between the host and guest when they share a house or a room. Interestingly, this study suggests that this off line interaction can be used as a substitute for developing online indi vidual interactions and writing and customizing long online comments. Individuals are social, and the Chinese community is social. Face-to-face (offline) host-guest interactions will improve customer experience and engagement and can be used as a substitute for the individual online interaction to increase customer repeated bookings.

We do not find support for the effect of timeliness (an ingredient of individual online interaction) on customer repeated bookings. Consis tently, offline interaction is not a substitute for this effect. The expla nation for this result can be contextual and only applicable to Xiaozhu and China. Unlike the importance of short time between guest’s comment and host’s replies in Western room rental platforms (e.g., Airbnb) to encourage customer repeated bookings, it seems this variable is less relevant in China as customers understand that hosts do not work full time in providing this service. Further IS research on this effect guarantees the debate, discussion, and theory building.

## 6.2. Incremental contributions to IS research

The paper examines an interesting research problem, i.e., explaining the variance explained of customer repeated bookings in the IT-enabled short-term room rental market, where loyalty does not seem to be the common pattern. In this sense, theorizing and showing robust empirical evidence on this research problem can make a very useful contribution to both the IS research community and the real-world. This research topic has been examined mainly in IS and Tourism Management research [42]. This prior research has mainly focused on the definition of IT-enabled sharing economy and short-term room rental platform and its business model, the behavioral patterns of usage of hosts and guests (e.g., [23,26,29,34]; Tussvadiah and Pesonen, 2018), and the impact of sharing economy platforms on tourism and hospitality activities in cities (e.g., [15]). A significant body of prior research use surveys, qualitative methods, and field experiments (e.g., [11]) and has been focused on the Airbnb platform.

Differently, drawing on the social interaction theory, we examine the impact of host-guest interaction on customer repeated bookings using a large-scale granular data investigation in the Chinese platform Xiaozhu. This study focuses on and identifies three types of host-guest interactions in the short-term room rental platform: overall online interaction, in dividual online interaction, and offline interaction. It theorizes and provides robust empirical evidence on how these types of host-guest interactions influence customer repeated bookings. Specifically, we explain and demonstrate that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) and individual host-guest interaction (the existence of individual interaction, absolute text length, and text similarity) increase customer repeated bookings. Our central thesis and evidence also indicate that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and similarity) on customer repeated bookings. This is the primary in cremental contribution to IS research.

Second, this paper draws on the social interaction theory to study individuals’ behavior under various scenes. The social interaction the ory suggests that an individual’s behavior is dominated by his/her role in a certain community or position [40]. We highlighted that online and offline interactions between the host and the guest affect repeated purchase behavior differently. While there are studies on the effect of social interaction in a broader research context, they have yet to classify the differences in online and offline interactions due to the scene diversity. Aside from the respective impact of online and offline in teractions between the host and the guest on repurchase behavior, this paper also explores the effect of the offline interaction on the influence of online interaction on customer repeated bookings. This paper offers an alternative and novel perspective on the social interaction theory by investigating the interplay of various interactive behavior in diverse situations in the IT-enabled sharing economy. This is the second contribution of this paper to IS research.

## 6.3. Lessons learned to IT and business executives

The IT-enabled sharing economy has provoked fierce competition and reduced the information asymmetry between organizations and customers. Keeping customer loyalty is more challenging than ever. Short-term room rental platforms are not an exemption to these trends. How to increase customer repeated bookings in the short-term room rental market? We examine this cutting-edge research problem and question and provide three crucial lessons learned to IT (platform) ex ecutives and business executives (hosts). First. this research shows and explains the existence of three host-guest interactions that can affect customer repeated bookings: overall interaction, individual interaction, and offline interaction. The overall interaction reveals the hosts’ in teractions with all potential customers and happens before the room’s booking. It is an online pre-order host-guest interaction. The individual interaction represents the interactions between a host and a guest after an online booking. Finally, offline interaction is the face-to-face hostguest interaction during the stay by sharing the house or the room. We find that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) increases customer repeated bookings. Hosts can increase customer repeated bookings through a higher online reply rate and acceptance rate to the guests’ requests and a shorter confir mation time.

Second, hosts can increase customer repeated bookings through in dividual online interaction with guests. Once the existence of individual interaction happens, the absolute text length and similarity increase customer repeated bookings. Hosts can increase customer repeated bookings by replying longer posts to customers and customizing their replies to guests’ comments and questions. Customers do not like stan dardized messages and answers. Text length reflects the quantity of content and information provided by the hosts and is interpreted as a signal of the host’s positive attitude and effort. Customized messages connect hosts and guests emotionally and enhance customer experience. Finally, this study suggests hosts go the extra mile in the offline in teractions with guests when they share a house or a room. Offline host guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and similarity) on customer repeated bookings. These lessons learned are valuable for hosts. They are also extremely useful to platform executives to understand the behavioral and inter action patterns between hosts and guests.

## 6.4. Concluding remarks

With short-term room rental services becoming a feasible choice for travelers, the sharing economy has become increasingly popular in the hospitality market in the era of digital acceleration [4,8,30]. We examine how to increase customer repeated bookings in the short-term room rental market in the sharing economy. We use a combination of text mining techniques and econometric models on a large-scale dataset with 36,072 orders and reviews from 5002 listings and 2181 hosts on Xi aozhu.com. We find that overall host-guest interaction (online reply rate, acceptance rate, confirmation time) and individual host-guest interaction (the existence of individual interaction, absolute text length, and text similarity) increase customer repeated bookings. We also discover that offline host-guest interaction is a substitute for the effect of individual interaction (existence, text length, and similarity) on customer repeated bookings. Quo Vadis? Who wishes to take the relay?
