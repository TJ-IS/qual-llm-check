## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Mobile devices Review helpfulness Consumer evaluation Review elements Mediation analysis Response period

## A B S T R A C T

Although extant papers have investigated the direct impact of mobile devices on the review helpfulness, little i known about its indirect impact. In this paper, we address this issue by examining the mediating roles of con sumer evaluation captured by both basic and optional review elements as well as the moderating role of response period. Combining a dataset from TripAdvisor with advanced mediation models, we find that the use of mobile devices significantly influences the content of review elements. which indirectly results in the change of review helpfulness. In detail, review extremity and text negative sentiment positively mediate the effect of mobile de vices on review helpfulness, as opposed to the negative mediations of text length and multidimensional ratings. Moreover, comparisons of their effect sizes reveal that text length and multidimensional ratings are responsible for the lower helpfulness of mobile reviews. Drawing on the theory of temporal distance, our moderationmediation model reveals extending response period can be an effective way of curing the low helpfulness of mobile reviews. Our results deepen the extant understanding about the impact of mobile devices and provide practitioners important implications regarding the design of review elements and the policy of managing reviews.

## 1. Introduction

The prevalence of Mobile Internet facilitates consumers to increas ingly adopt mobile devices, such as smart phones or tablets, to take part in online activities. According to the investigations from Statista, 85% of consumers are more willing to record and share their consumption experience on the mobile devices. To cater for this consumption habit, many firms develop mobile applications (simply, APP) to earn extra mobile traffic for their products or services. For example, McDonald guided consumers to order meals on their APP by sending exclusive coupons only consumed on the mobile platform. Distinguished from the nonmobile devices, extant literature has found that two unique char acteristics of mobile device usage, i.e., the use cost and the portability [25], are reshaping consumers’ behaviors including perception of mar keting information [15,36], shopping decision making [16], and postshopping evaluations [25,43].

A similar effect also arises in the area of consumers’ cognition for the review value. Mariani, et al. [39] provided the initial evidence that mobile device usage decreases the number of helpful votes a review receives. Further, Ransbotham, et al. [43] and Grewal and Stephen [16] conducted a series of rigorous empirical studies and found that mobile device, viewed as the peripheral cue, influences consumers’ judgment for the review helpfulness. However, these studies only account for the direct effect of mobile devices. Given that extant literature has demon strated using mobile devices leads to the change of consumer evaluation behaviors [2,26] and these changes should be related to the review helpfulness [21], we indicate there is an indirect link between mobile device usage and review helpfulness. It is valuable to study in that it can deepen our understanding regarding the impact of mobile devices by clarifying the interplay between the adoption of mobile devices, con sumer evaluation behavior, and review helpfulness.

The prerequisite of identifying this indirect effect is to fully capture the consumer evaluation behavior. Unlike prior literature using basic review elements (review rating and review text) as the representative of consumer evaluation behaviors [18,43], considering a fact that many review websites introduce various optional review elements to enrich the helpfulness, we integrate two widely applied ones into our research framework, i.e., review photos adding more diagnostic value to online reviews and thus improving review helpfulness [38,53] as well as multidimensional ratings enhancing rating informativeness and helping consumers better match their preferences with product attributes [46]. Taken together, the first goal of our paper is to explore how consumer evaluation $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ basic and optional review elements) mediates the effect of mobile devices on review helpfulness. Theoretically, our empirical results reveal the heterogeneous effects of review elements on mobile vs. nonmobile devices, and practically, provide relevant guidance to prac titioners about how to make the appropriate designs of review elements and to implement effective policies of managing online reviews which contribute most to their desired outcomes.

A comprehensive understanding of the above mediating relationship also requires to consider the moderating effect of response period. Response period (or temporal distance) between product consuming and review writing is associated with consumers’ causality judgments [30,52]. That is, a long response period softens consumers’ opinions and decreases the likelihood of being disturbed by extreme sentiments [7,57], especially negative ones. As a remedial measure, the effective ness of response period has been widely proved in the area of marketing and information systems [7,30,37]. Noteworthily, Kim and Hwang [26] proposed an association between response period and the characteristics of mobile devices. Alternatively, the portability of mobile devices en ables consumers to review hotel stay without the limitation of place and time, further decreasing consumers’ response period greatly. Thus, we expect that their interaction may alleviate the difference caused by adopting different devices. However, few papers extend the effective ness of response period to the context of Mobile Internet. To resolve this issue, our second goal is to examine whether the response period can alleviate the difference in the helpfulness of mobile reviews and nonmobile reviews.

To answer the above research questions, we collect the dataset from one of the largest online travel agencies in the world, i.e. TripAdvisor, on 122,187 online reviews from February 2012 to January 2019. 30,840 reviews (25.24%) are from mobile devices and 91,347 reviews (74.76%) are from non-mobile devices. To address the potential unbalanced sample, we employ a propensity scoring match (PSM) method to pre process the dataset. Besides, we use the multiple mediating models proposed by Preacher and Hayes [42] to measure the mediating and moderating effects. Bootstrap confidential intervals help us examine the significance of the mediating effect. Our empirical results first confirm that, on average, using a mobile device has a negative effect on review helpfulness. Results of mediation analysis show that review extremity and text negative sentiment positively mediate the effect of mobile de vices on review helpfulness, as opposed to the negative mediations of text length and multidimensional ratings. By comparing their effect sizes, we can conclude that text length and multidimensional ratings are responsible for the lower helpfulness of mobile reviews. Besides, the results of the mediation-moderation model reveal that the response period significantly moderates the effect of mobile devices on the con tents of review elements, indirectly linking to the change of review helpfulness. A series of robustness checks support the above results.

Our research contributes to the extant literature in several ways. First, beyond prior literature only investigating the direct effect of mo bile devices [39,43], our paper studying the indirect effect by studying how using mobile devices influences the review helpfulness by altering evaluation content. Second, by integrating optional review elements, i. $\mathrm { e } _ { \cdot , }$ review photos and multidimensional ratings, into our research model, we can better capture the consumer evaluation and generate guidance on how to design these review elements on both platforms. Third, consistent with existed literature [7,30,31], our paper demonstrates the effectiveness of extending response period in alleviating the difference of review helpfulness caused by adopting different devices. Our study extends the temporal distance to the area of Mobile Internet. At the end of paper, several management implications are also provided for hotel managers and web developers, respectively.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. We introduce relevant literature in Section 2. We then establish hypotheses in Section 3. In Section 4, we explain the research context and related dataset. In Section

5, we adopt two mediation models to examine our proposed hypotheses. We conclude our paper in Section 6.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Impact of mobile devices on review helpfulness

To avoid information overload, many platforms, such as TripAdvisor and Yelp, develop a function of “Likes” to identify review helpfulness. Therefore, to better identify the review helpfulness, extant literature empirically examined the impact of various factors on the review helpfulness, including reviewer-related (e.g. reputation [8], expertise [23], and profile image [28]) and review-related (e.g. rating [41], characteristics of review text [5,12], and photos [38]) factors. With the online review systems advancing, new determinants of review helpful ness have emerged consistently. Of these, the impact of mobile devices has attracted scholars’ attention. Several papers provided initial ana lyses about the effect of mobile devices on the review helpfulness. Mariani, et al. [39], collecting 1.2 million online reviews from Booking, compared relevant mean values and found that mobile reviews, on average, receive 0.042 helpful votes less than nonmobile reviews. Sim ple mean comparisons suffer from heavily biased effect from endoge neity. Further, to obtain an accurate causal relationship, Ransbotham, et al. [43] and Grewal and Stephen [16] conducted a series of natural experiments or randomized experiments to conclude mobile device adoption significantly relates to the change of review helpfulness.

However, the direct effect of mobile devices examined by the above literature only captures the partial effect of mobile devices [43]. Ac cording to the extant literature, we indicate there is an indirect effect on review helpfulness. A rich literature has disclosed that mobile device usage produces different effects on the review content generation. Specifically, Ransbotham, et al. [43] find that the review content created on nonmobile devices is more affective, more concrete, and less extreme than on mobile devices. Jong, et al. [25] also indicated that the relative ratio of extremely positive and negative reviews on mobile devices is significantly higher. Given that there is a significant association between the change of review content and review helpfulness [21,50], we conjecture that different review contents caused by mobile or nonmobile devices influence review helpfulness. Naturally, an indirect relationship is forming: “the use of mobile devices – the creation of review content – the evaluation of review value".

One extant study investigated the indirect effect of mobile devices. Ransbotham, et al. [43] found that the content of online reviews is different when created on mobile devices and these differences in con tent modify the review helpfulness. However, from the perspective of methodology, Ransbotham, et al. [43] separately conduct regression models on the stage of both review content creation and review value evaluation. Without the application of the mediation model, we cannot establish the indirect relationship between mobile devices and review helpfulness. From the perspective of review elements, the review con tent examined by Ransbotham, et al. [43] mainly concentrates on basic review elements $\mathrm { i . e . , }$ review rating and review text, whose conclusions are hardly generalized to today’s review systems that have introduced various optional review elements. Our paper tries to compensate for the aforementioned drawbacks and extend our understanding of the indirect impact of mobile devices.

## 2.2. Representatives of consumer evaluation

Consumer evaluation has become important reference information for firm’s product plan designing and other consumer’s shopping deci sion making [35]. Thus, how to comprehensively capture consumer evaluation is key to conduct subsequent empirical studies. Prior litera ture mainly uses numeric overall rating from one-star to five-star to represent consumer evaluation in that they are simple and easily un derstood [25,43]. But Schneider, et al. [46] indicated there are three potential different types of biases (i.e., self-selection and reporting bias, social influence bias, and dimensional-rating bias, deviating) deviating review rating from the normal value. To avoid this issue, some re searchers resort to the review text. A series of review text characteristics have been employed including text length [43] and review text senti ment [18] to measure consumer evaluation. With the review system advancing, various review elements have been introduced to improve the credibility and informativeness of online reviews. In this sense, the traditional metrics using either review rating or review text hardly capture the potential change of consumer evaluation, which generate the measure error biasing the results of empirical studies.

To this end, our paper integrates review photos and multidimen sional ratings into our research framework and documents their inter play with mobile device usage and review helpfulness. The reason why we focus on these two variables is that they alleviate the drawbacks of overall rating and review text. In detail, given a product consists of multiple attributes [50], single dimension rating (overall rating) easily leads consumers to replace the evaluation for entire product with the evaluation for detailed product attribute. Multidimensional ratings can decrease the dimensional bias of overall rating [6,46]. Moreover, in contrast to tedious textual information, picture information can provide more expressive and concrete, and perceived content vividness for consumers, decreasing the cognitive burden and adding diagnostic value to textual reviews [38,53]. Relevant conclusions of both variables can help us understand consumer evaluation better and provide theoretical guidance for the design of review elements.

## 3. Hypotheses development

In Fig. 1, our hypotheses development is composed of three parts: First, according to the extant literature, we abstract two characteristics when using mobile devices: the use cost and the portability (Section 3.1). Second, we use basic and optional review elements to capture consumer evaluation behaviors. We expect that the above characteris tics exert heterogeneous effects on the review creation, which indirectly changes consumers’ cognition for the review value measured by review helpfulness (Section 3.2). Third, we propose the moderating role of response period to check their effectiveness in curing the difference caused by adopting different devices (Section 3.3). To test our proposed research framework, five hypotheses are developed: (1) The first four hypotheses mainly document the mediating effects of review elements. (2) The last hypothesis investigates how response period moderates the above mediation relationship.

## 3.1. Characteristics of mobile device usage

A proliferation of mobile devices has reshaped consumer behaviors from cognition for marketing information [15,36] to shopping decision making [16] to post-shopping evaluations [25,43]. Researchers have summarized two major differences between adoption mobile vs. nonmobile devices [11,34]: (1) Use cost. The small-sized screens of mobile devices lower consumer’s efficiency of inputting words and searching information [11]. That is, ceteris paribus, finishing the same review writing task requires mobile users to devote more effort than nonmobile users [25]. (2) Portability. The small-sized mobile devices enable consumers to create and access information where they are and whenever they want [43]. Relying on the high portability of mobile devices, Ghose, et al. [15] proposed a novel “trajectory-based” targeting strategy for mobile recommendation and examined its effect on con sumer shopping decision making. Luo, et al. [36] draw on contextual marketing theory to investigate how different combinations of mobile targeting determine consumer response to mobile promotions. Natu rally, we expect that both characteristics of mobile devices affect eval uation content and subsequently produces different review helpfulness. Next, we illustrate relevant hypotheses.

## 3.2. Mediating effects of basic review elements

To simplify our explanations, we decide to integrate H1a, H1b, and H1c into one section since both rating extremity and text sentiments show consumer attitude for products or services. H2 is a separated section for text length reflecting consumers’ devoted effort of writing reviews.

## 3.2.1. Rating extremity and text sentiments

Review rating intuitively reflects consumer evaluation of products [18]. Mudambi and Schuff [41] defined one-star as the low rating indicating a negative evaluation of products and five-star as the high rating reflecting a positive view of products. Due to our dataset covering various levels of hotels, this method may generate bias. For example, if a luxury hotel owning a high reputation receives a four-star rating, it can be viewed as a consumer’s dissatisfaction with her/his hotel stay. In contrast, for the budget hotel, a four-star rating may mean a positive evaluation. To better capture consumer experience about hotel stays, we use rating extremity defined as the difference between review rating and the average hotel rating. Moreover, many consumers also write a large volume of emotional words to detail their feelings in the review text [18]. Hence, the positive and negative sentiment of review text are crucial indicators revealing consumer attitude to their hotel stay. For brevity, we use the term, i.e., consumer attitude, to represent these three factors.

The high portability of mobile devices enables consumers to post reviews without the limitations of time and place [11]. In the hotel in dustry, the context of our study, after checking out, consumers can use mobile devices to record their experience immediately. In this short time interval, consumers clearly and accurately recall consumption details occurring during the hotel stays, easily boosting consumers’ moods [43]. Hence, mobile consumers tend to express a stronger and more obvious attitude toward hotel stays. The distinguished attitudes help readers better perceive the quality of hotels and support their selection decisions, further leading to higher review helpfulness. This positive relationship between consumer attitude and review helpfulness has been proved by the extant literature. For example, Mudambi and Schuff [41] indicated that extreme ratings easily attract consumers’ attention than moderate ratings since consumers expect that this kind of review con tains valuable information to avoid poor hotels and to improve decision quality. In terms of review text, Han [18] found that strong positive and negative sentiments are rare or unexpected and easily cause consumers attention and drive them to give a helpful vote. In summary, we make the following hypothesis (see Fig. 1):

## 3.2.2. Text length

As illustrated in Section 3.1, mobile devices own a characteristic of high use cost that lowers the typing speed and decreases the desire of inputting more words. Therefore, compared to nonmobile review text, mobile review text has fewer word counts [43] and contains “fewer details of products. and fewer details about how and where the product was used in specific contexts” [41]. Given mobile review text containing low informativeness hardly provides dependent and valuable reference information for consumers’ hotel selection [50], consumers possibly refuse to give a helpful vote, inducing lower review helpfulness. In sum, we make the following hypotheses (see Fig. 1):

H2. Text length negatively mediates the effect of mobile devices on the review helpfulness.

## 3.3. Mediating effects of optional review elements

## 3.3.1. Review photos

To satisfy consumers’ demand for photography, electronics manu facturers launch a series of mobile devices equipped with advanced cameras, such as iPhone 12 and Galaxy S21. Strong photography func tions and customized APP enable consumers to upload photos to their reviews without switching devices. In addition, the high portability of mobile devices allows consumers to overcome the physical drawbacks that using nonmobile devices need fixed desks and wireless access [43]. Both merits drive consumers to choose mobile devices as the platform for posting reviews containing photos; In other words, the likelihood of mobile reviews containing photos is much higher than nonmobile ones.

As common sense indicates that “a picture is worth a thousand words”, mobile reviews with more photos are likely to provide more reference information and improve review helpfulness. Extant papers have examined the information value of review photos. For example, Garrod [10] found the merits of photography as a technique for inves tigating the perception of residents and tourists of a destination. Ma, et al. [38], introducing a deep learning algorithm, proved the necessity of encouraging tourists to post review photos related to their hotel stays. Based on the above illustration, we make the following hypothesis (see Fig. 1):

H3. Review photos positively mediates the effect of mobile devices on the review helpfulness.

## 3.3.2. Multidimensional ratings

Most online review portals have adopted the single-dimensional rating system reflecting the overall product ratings. However, recent research suggests that this kind of rating system cannot comprehensively show information on different product attributes [3] and easily suffer from the dimensional bias [33]. To avoid these drawbacks, a multidi mensional rating system, which provides ratings on different product dimensions, has been developed. Taking hotel rating systems as an example, consumers could rate on sleep, room, location, value, and service of hotels; prospective consumers can better fit their preferences to hotels and increases the confidence of their choices [6].

Although multidimensional ratings are useful in solving online shopping uncertainty, adopting-mobile-devices consumers may refuse to fill in multidimensional ratings because (1) the higher use cost of mobile devices lowers their desire of writing detailed reviews and (2) writing multidimensional ratings requires consumers to devote more cognition capability such as recalling every aspect of hotel stay and transforming them into star ratings. Alternatively, the likelihood of mobile reviews contains multidimensional ratings is smaller than that of nonmobile reviews. Plus the positive effect of multidimensional rating on review helpfulness [27], the use of mobile devices indirectly de creases the review helpfulness. Thus, we make the following hypothesi (see Fig. 1):

H4. Multidimensional ratings negatively mediates the effect of mobile devices on the review helpfulness.

## 3.4. Moderating effect of response period

Temporal distance, stemming from the psychological literature, is defined as the temporal proximity between two physical events [7]. Li, et al. [30] indicated that individuals can use temporal distance to build the potential causality between physical events. In the context of long temporal distance, individual perception of causality will be weakened, even vanquish. For example, assuming a waiter carelessly sprinkles soup on your new dress, you are likely to give emotional evaluation at that time because of this service failure. However, as time elapses, when you comment on this restaurant again, your evaluation of the restaurant may not be worse as the initial one in that long temporal distance attenuates the causal relationship between service failure and evaluation.

The effectivity of temporal distance has aroused the attention of researchers. Extant literature found that temporal distance is able to alleviate negativity bias of online reviews [30,57], moderate the social influence of prior review rating on subsequent review rating [31], and affect consumers’ posting intentions [7]. Due to the high-portability of mobile devices, the causality between consumption experience and following evaluation can be moderated by temporal distance. However, few papers extend the effect of temporal distance to the area of mobile devices. Thus, to fully capture the relationship between mobile devices and review elements, our paper strives to investigate the moderating effect of temporal distance.

To make our hypothesis understood easily, we use another term, i.e., response period, to replace temporal distance and define it as the in terval time from checking out of hotels to posting online reviews. We expect that the response period has a significant moderating effect on the relationship between mobile devices and review elements. In particular, the use of mobile devices allows consumers to record realtime thoughts and opinions before, during, and after their hotel stays [43]. From the angle of sentiment, when extending the response period, consumers have enough time to retrospect their hotel stays and there fore are less likely to be affected by extreme emotion [7,30]. Thus, consumer preference tends to be more moderate. From the angle of memory, after a long response period, consumers tend to forget some details occurring during their hotel stays and weaken the causal rela tionship between consumption process and online evaluation [7], which makes the content of review elements briefer. In contrast, using nonmobile devices to post reviews usually costs consumers much time during which consumers’ evaluation behaviors have been already moderated [43]. If extending the response time on the nonmobile de vices, we cannot receive the same effect size as the mobile devices do. Thus, we make the following hypothesis (see Fig. 1):

H5. Response period significantly moderates the relationship between mobile devices and review elements.

To examine our proposed hypotheses, we conduct empirical studies which can be divided into three parts, including data collection, data procession, and empirical models. Fig. 2 presents our research frame work. In this section, we illustrate data collection and data procession in detail.

## 4.1. Research context and data

We collect the dataset from TripAdvisor, one of the largest online travel agencies in the world. The proliferation of Mobile Internet en courages TripAdvisor to develop mobile application. To identify whether hotel reviews are from mobile devices or nonmobile devices. TripAdvisor designs a phone-like icon: Containing this icon means that this review is from a mobile device, otherwise from a non-mobile device. This design, thus, provides us a convenient setting to observe the dif ference in review helpfulness from different platforms.

Like other review websites, TripAdvisor provides two basic review elements, i.e., review rating in the form of five-star and review text allowing consumers to record the details of hotel stays by words. These two review elements are required to write, otherwise, hotel reviews cannot be submitted to the review system successfully. Besides, to enrich the review contents and to improve the reference value, two optional review elements are developed, i.e., review photos where consumers can upload no more than 9 photos, and multidimensional ratings including six-dimensional ratings (i.e., sleep, value, clean, service, location, and room). Basic and optional review elements provided by TripAdvisor enable us to observe their mediations.

We program a python-based web crawler to collect hotel reviews of Hong Kong. Our study period is from February 2012 to January 2019 during which mobile devices and relevant mobile applications began to popularize, providing abundant samples for the subsequent empirical studies. Hong Kong is one of the most popular travel destinations covering hotels from budget to luxury and attracts travelers from all over the world [40], greatly decreasing the potential sample selection bias. Furthermore, to alleviate the biased effect from missing variables, we collect all visible signals that are identified as the determinants of review helpfulness by extant literature. Next, we explain focal variables one by one.

## 4.2.2. Review elements

As illustrated in the prior section, we use the rating extremity to reflect the extent to which consumers enjoy the hotel stay. ## 4.2.3. Response period

To ensure the authenticity of reviews, TripAdvisor displays the date when consumers leave hotels and write reviews, which gives us a good chance to see how consumer evaluation is moderated by response period. Our control variables are gathered from the identified variables in prior literature. They can be divided into three categories: consumerrelated, review-related, and hotel-related control variables. ## 5.2.1. Multiple mediation model

To examine the mediating effects of review elements and the moderating effect of the response period, we adopt the multiple moderation model where the subscript i denotes consumer and subscript j denotes hotel.

$$
\int \text { element } _ {i j} = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} \cdot \text { mobile } _ {i j} + \boldsymbol {\beta} \cdot \text { control } + \varepsilon_ {i j}\tag{1}
$$

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} h e l p f u l _ {i j} = \delta_ {0} + \delta_ {1} \cdot m o b i l e _ {i j} + \delta_ {2} \cdot e l e m e n t _ {i j} + \boldsymbol {\delta} \cdot \mathbf {c o n t r o l} + \varepsilon_ {i j} \end{array} \right.\tag{2}
$$

We follow the procedures outlined by Hong and Pavlou [22]: First, displaying the estimates for each relationship. In detail, Causal Steps Approach developed by Baron and Kenny [4] indicated that if estab lishing the mediation relationship, the following conditions must hold: (1) the independent variable must affect the mediator $( \beta _ { 1 }$ is significant); (2) the mediator must affect the dependent variable $( \delta _ { 2 }$ is significant). Although this method is easy to understand, it suffers from shortcomings when used in the multiple mediator context [42]. For example, if two mediators act in the opposite direction, then their indirect effects may be canceled out. To address this problem, Shrout and Bolger [48] suggested that bootstrapping methods can be extended to solve the question regarding multiple mediations. Thus, second, we use the bootstrapping method to check the significance of mediating effects. In detail, the bootstrapping confidence interval (CI) is used to calculate the indirect effect through mediators are derived by soring the k values of $\beta _ { 1 } \cdot \delta _ { 2 }$ from low to high [4]. Extant literature has proved the effectiveness of the bootstrapping method in examining the significance of mediators [42].

## 5.3. Robustness check

We conduct several robustness checks to rule out alternative expla nations. First, one might be concerned that our treatment effect, i.e., the use of mobile devices, suffers from sample selection bias caused by in dividual characteristics and results in an inaccurate estimate. For example, Sarker and Wells [45] documented that individual character istics are identified as important determinants influencing the imple mentation and acceptance of mobile devices. To reduce this concern, an endogenous binary-variable model estimated by full maximum likeli hood is adopted [19]. Results of the endogenous binary-variable model are consistent with prior results, supporting our hypotheses (see Ap pendix C).

Second, the zero value accounts for nearly 50% of our dependent variable (review helpfulness) shown in Fig. 7(a), which possibly gen erates an unbalance sample and biases our coefficients of main model estimated by standard negative binomial regression. Thus, we apply zero-inflation negative binomial estimation to re-estimate our focal co efficients. In contrast to zero-inflation negative binomial estimation, the Vuong test (z = − 3.09 and − 3.13) show that standard negative binomial estimation is better (see Appendix D).

Third, Fig. 7(b) displays the distribution of response period. We find most consumers choose to post hotel reviews in the current month of their hotel stays. To highlight the curation of response period, we transform continuous variable into binary one (=0, consumers write reviews in the month when they stay hotel; =1, otherwise). The focal estimates are significant but show a stronger influence magnitude. It means that motivate consumers to write immediate reviews can accen tuate the negative effect of mobile devices on the content of review el ements, indirectly decreasing review helpfulness (Appendix E).

Fourth, some hotels are popular, increasing the number of readers and, at the same time, increasing the likelihood of being given helpful votes. To alleviate this concern, we build two variables to control this effect based on the measure from Ransbotham, et al. [43]. We include “the natural log of the number of prior reviews for the hotels”, hotel reviews, and “average likes received by other reviews for the same ho tels”, hotel popularity, into our mediation model. Results (Tables 2 and 3, Appendix F) are consistent with our main models.

## 6. Summary and discussion

## 6.1. Theoretical contribution

Based on a rich dataset from TripAdvisor, we empirically study the mediating effects of review elements on the association between mobile device usage and review helpfulness as well as the curation of response period. Our paper makes the following theoretical contributions: First, although previous literature illustrates the direct relationship between mobile devices and review helpfulness [39,43], its indirect link has not received proportional attention. Our findings contribute to the current literature by displaying how consumer evaluation operates alongside mobile devices to affect the review helpfulness. In detail, we find that, under the influence of mobile devices, consumer evaluation contents tend to be altered, then affecting the reference value of reviews. For example, review extremity and text negative sentiment positively mediate the effect of mobile devices on review helpfulness, while text length and multidimensional ratings make opposite mediating effects. By comparisons of their effect sizes, the low helpfulness of mobile re views is potentially caused by text length and multidimensional ratings. These results disclose the interplay of mobile device usage, consumer evaluation, and consumer cognition for the review value, further deepening our understanding about the impact of mobile devices.

Second, prior papers mainly use basic review elements, such as re view rating and text, to measure consumer evaluation [18,43]. With the review system consistently introducing various optional review ele ments [6] to improve the reference value of reviews, this type of mea sure cannot represent consumer evaluation behavior. Moreover, there is a dearth of papers exploring optional review elements on different platforms. To fill in these two research gaps, our paper focuses on the effects of review photos and multidimensional ratings on the mobile vs. nonmobile platform. Our mediation models show that, in the mobile context vs. nonmobile context, multidimensional ratings generate het erogeneous effects on review helpfulness but review photos not. Therefore, our work extends the existing research about studies of optional review elements to the area of Mobile Internet.

Third, existed literature has widely demonstrated the effectiveness of response period in reducing negativity bias [7,57]. Following this line, our paper tests the effectiveness of response period in alleviating the negative effect of mobile devices on review helpfulness. Our results show that response period significantly moderates the mediating effects of review text and multidimensional ratings on review helpfulness and further changes review helpfulness. In other words, extending response period can decrease the difference between mobile and nonmobile re view helpfulness. Our conclusion sheds a new insight of the curation of response period to the area of Mobile Internet.

## 6.2. Management implications

This research also offers crucial management implications for how hotel managers effectively utilize online user-generated content. First, the heterogeneous results from mobile vs. nonmobile devices suggested that there is no one-size-fits-all information management strategy on the Mobile Internet. In detail, although the prevalence of mobile devices may prompt the growth of online reviews [43], our results indicate that the helpfulness of mobile reviews would decrease a lot. Thus, to fully utilize the value of online reviews, managers should place more weight on nonmobile reviews to better obtain consumer evaluation and to support subsequent product improvement plans. Second, given that the effectiveness of text length and multidimensional ratings in curing the lower helpfulness of mobile reviews, managers can guide consumers to devote more effort to the generation of review content by sending coupons and giving membership rewards. Third, our results find that shortening response period lowers the reference value of reviews. Thereby, we oppose a common action of hotel managers encouraging consumers to make an instant review when checking out.

Our study also provides a series of rich guidance to website de velopers about how the review systems should be designed differently on the Mobile Internet. From the perspective of the platform, due to the high use cost of mobile devices, developers should design some func tions to improve the input speed and input accuracy, such as adding speech input. Optimizing the structure of mobile webpage can present more information and decrease the use cost of searching and locating information. From the perspective of review elements, our mediation roles of review elements reveal that review text and multidimensional ratings are responsible for the negative effect of mobile devices on re view helpfulness. To this end, website developers (1) can set a limitation of word count to improve the quality of mobile reviews, (2) can set multidimensional ratings as a required review element rather than an optional one on the mobile platforms.

## 6.3. Limitations and future work

This paper is not without limitations, and future extensions are desired. First, our moderating variable is only measured by months due to the privacy policy of website. It would be interesting to collect data about respond period measured by day and compare their moderating effect size. Second, Xu, et al. [55] provided evidence that tablets influence digital commerce. However, our dataset does not distinguish the specific types of mobile devices; Thus, a potential future study is to examine whether tablets exert different impacts on the review helpful ness. Third, consumer-related factors, such as anonymity, are likely to influence review content [18]. Therefore, it would be worthwhile to investigate the effect of consumer-related variables on online evaluation behavior in future research.
