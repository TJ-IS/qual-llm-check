## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Customer engagement Social media Product lifecycle Discourse logic Argument frame Popularity

## A B S T R A C T

More than 4 billion unique users are now using different social media platforms. This provides firms with a new avenue to connect and converse with their targeted customers in a dialogue fashion and create better customer engagement. Although customer engagement has been studied, no study to date has focused on the concept of customer engagement in social media through the lens of users' participation in (co)creation of content on social media. To that end, this study strives to conceptualize and validate the concept of customer engagement with a social media post, proposing a research model which investigates the effects of popularity, discourse logic and argument frame on customer engagement with a social media post. This study also investigates to what extent those effects vary by the product lifecycle stage and social media platform type, using secondary data extracted from Instagram and Twitter on a group of products (i.e., vehicles) at different lifecycle stages to address its research objective. This study's findings would help firms make better decisions on enhancing their customer engagement on social media platforms.

## 1. Introduction

Social media platforms enable their users to build their virtua communities while sharing their ideas or thoughts [1]. These abilities have motivated billions of individuals across the globe to join at least one of the social media platforms. Recent reports estimate that by January 2021, the total number of unique users across various social media platforms has reached 4.2 billion, which shows a 13% increase compared to the previous year (i.e., 2020) [2]. This report also states that each social media user, on average, spends 2 h and 25 min per day browsing her/his social media page(s). Moreover, Tankovska [3] notes that, in 2020, the daily number of shared stories on Instagram has reached 500 million, almost three times the number of stories shared in 2018.

Social media use allows a firm to control its information broadcast, connect and converse with its targeted customers in a dialogue fashion and create better customer engagement [4]. According to a study by Buffer, 2019, the significant majority of marketers (i.e., 73%), who participated in that study, believed that social media marketing is the most effective means for firms to promote their products [5]. Steven Brill, CEO of NewsGuard, says that “Social media moves and shapes the public dialogue and companies operating within it are responding more to their impact on society” [6]. A firm's presence in social media could potentially strengthen its engagement with customers [7]. A higher level of customer engagement allows a firm to increase its website traffic, sales, customer satisfaction, loyalty, and consequently decision quality [8–10].

Various groups of scholars and practitioners have vastly studied the concept of consumer engagement and also pointed out guidelines that a firm could put into practice to increase its customers' engagement [11]. However, there is a continued dialogue among scholars and practi tioners of Information Systems and Marketing on the accuracy, validity and reliability of the means used to measure consumer engagement, especially in the context of social media [9,12]. Conventionally, con sumer engagement entails customers cognitive and physical connections with a brand [13]. In the online environment, firms use interactive ap proaches to increase their customers' physical experiences with their products/services. As opposed to physical experience enhancement, customer engagement through social media allows a firm to impact their customers' emotional and cognitive nuance through motivational drivers such as customer reviews or social media posts [13]. In this re gard, customer engagement refers to making customers voluntary have contributions to a brand's image, beyond any physical transactions [14]. Recent studies confirm that customers engaged with a brand on social media are three times more likely to remain loyal to a brand [13]. In the context of this study, customer engagement reflects a customer's willing ness to participate in the dialogue related to a specific product [11]. While increasing consumer engagement is critical for firms, there is still a lack of understanding of how to conceptualize, validate or measure consumer engagement in social media in the context of users' partici pation in content (co)creation for a product. Therefore, as the first objective, this study strives to define, conceptualize, measure, and vali date the concept of customer engagement in social media in the context of users' (co)creation of content related to a product, as a proxy to measure the actual customer engagement with that specific product.

Traditionally, firms have employed various methods to enhance customer engagement [15], among which is the use of renowned public figures known as influencers to promote their products [16]. According to Source Credibility Theory [17], a promoting message's credibility depends on the receivers' perception of the influencer's trustworthiness and reliability. Renowned influencers are popular and have many fans who follow them, especially on their social media pages. Extant litera ture purports that an influencer's popularity enhances the credibility of her/his promotion, positively impacting the potential customers' deci sion to acquire a particular product (e.g., [18]). Moreover, extant literature also mentions that popularity is a proxy to classify social media users as influencers versus ordinary users [19]. That said, this study considers the number of followers of a user in social media as a proxy to portray her/his popularity (i.e., being an influencer or an actual user).

Extant literature also finds a promoting message's argument quality as another factor shaping and enhancing customer engagement [20]. According to the Source Credibility Theory [17], a promoting message's argument quality is evaluated by its trustworthiness and attractiveness. This study proposes that the argument quality of a message in social media depends on two primary attributes of discourse logic, reflecting trustworthiness [21], and argument frame, representing attractiveness [22,23]. Discourse logic shows whether an influencer, in her/his mes sage, endorse a product providing objective evidence instead of her/his subjective opinion. Argument frame reflects positivity, negativity, or neutrality of the tone of the influencer's message/recommendation regarding a product. Hence, this study's second objective is to inspect the extent to which popularity, discourse logic, and argument frame affect customer engagement in social media.

This study argues that the importance of popularity, discourse logic, and argument frame on consumer engagement may depend on products lifecycle stages (i.e., introduction, growth, maturity, and decline) [24]. According to Armstrong et al. [25], the type of media, promotion and messages effectively endorsing a product varies by its lifecycle stage. Therefore, as the third objective, this study explores how a product's lifecycle stage moderates the proposed relationships outlined in our second objective.

Furthermore, users' demographics vary among social media plat forms [26]. For instance, most users on Instagram (i.e., 80%) are less than 30 years old, compared to Twitter, in which most users (i.e., 60%) are above 30 years old [22]. According to Leone [27] customer engagement varies by platform. For instance, among the social media platforms, Instagram has the highest level of engaged social media users (i.e., 7%) interacting with a social media post, while Twitter has the least engaged users (i.e., less than 2%) with a social media post promoting a product. Therefore, customer engagement and its antecedents could vary by social media platform, and, thus, in the post hoc analysis, we examine whether the relationships shown in the research model vary for Instagram and Twitter as the fourth objective. This study will specifically focus on social media posts on Instagram and Twitter since they are among the most used social media platforms by firms to promote their products [22].

This study relies on Source Credibility Theory [17] and the

Systematic/Heuristic Persuasion Model [28] to explore the above research objectives. We particularly utilized these theories because they enable us to understand whether popularity, which represents a pro moter's credibility, discourse logic that is systematically evaluated, and argument frame, which is heuristically evaluated, affects social media engagement. Secondary data containing promotive messages for prod ucts in different lifecycle stages is scrapped, extracted, and analyzed from Instagram and Twitter to address the above research objectives.

## 2. Literature review and theoretical background

## 2.1. Customer engagement

Traditionally, scholars and practitioners of Marketing have studied the concept of customer engagement, its definition, barriers, drivers and antecedents [11,29,30]. Particularly, Marketing literature suggests “customers' interactive and co-creative actions entails customer engagement” [13]. Kumar and Pansari [31] define customer engage ment as the level of connectedness between a customer and a firm, stating that a higher level of customer engagement enhances a firm's competitiveness. Vivek et al. [32] expand the above definition, asserting that customer engagement also involves “events and activities engaged in by the customer that are not directly related to search, alternative evaluation and decision making involving brand choice”. According to Roberts and Alpert [29], the main driver of enhanced customer engagement is the alignment between a firm's value proposition and its customer experience activities. Roberts and Alpert [26] also define five different stages an engaged customer goes through to connect with a product, with each of these stages building on the previous one. These stages are: product purchase transaction, continued repurchases, read iness to purchase other lines of products, recommending to others, and finally becoming a brand's advocate. To summarize, customer engage ment in the marketing literature lingers around a firm's ability to con nect with its customers. It is worth noting that the above stages differ from those in a product lifecycle. A customer goes through the above phases of engagement with a product at any product lifecycle stage.

The widespread use of social media introduced new opportunities for firms to connect with their customers directly. It also attracted various information system scholars' attention to study the concept of customer engagement in social media [33–35]. According to Cabiddu et al. [36], firms can plan three types of customer engagement strategies: persistent, customized, or triggered. The persistent customer engagement strategy outlines the plans for continued discussions with the customers. The customized customer engagement strategy portrays the goals for a tailored customer interaction based on prior knowledge of customers' interests, preferences, conversations, and relationships. The triggered customer engagement strategy is applicable when a firm plans for customer-initiated events to explore and identify new value-creation opportunities. All in all, engaging with customers in social media en ables a firm to have more loyal customers [37], to increase its perfor mance, sales and referrals [38], and to improve its brand awareness [39].

Extant literature traditionally offers various methods to conceptu alize and measure customer engagement [40], mainly grounded in surveys focusing on how physically customers connect and engage with a product. With the emergence of new technologies, such as recom mender systems, online consumer reviews and social media platforms, the focus of customer engagement studies leaned towards behavioral interactions of customers with a product [13]. Having said that, the definition of customer engagement also changed to “customers making voluntary resource contributions that have a brand or firm focus but go beyond what is fundamental to transactions” [14]. While the definition has been changed, the methods to measure customer engagement in social media remained the same, relying on surveys [40] which is a proxy to customer engagement. However, based on the above definition, customer engagement can be measured by how customers react to a social media post. Therefore, there is still a lack of understanding of how to conceptualize, validate, and measure the concept of customer engagement in social media through the lens of customers participation in content (co)creation. According to the extant literature, common factors on social media platforms reflecting engagement with a post include the number of likes, comments, and shares [41]. In that sense, this study proposes, conceptualizes, validates, and measures the concept of customer engagement in social media as a multidimensional forma tive construct. Our proposed customer engagement construct comprises the total number of likes, comments, and shares of a social media post. The new offered method in this study allows a firm to directly measure to which extent their current or future potential customers are responding or will respond towards their marketing efforts. It also enables a firm to update its marketing strategies and plans accordingly in a timelier fashion, resulting in a higher customer engagement level since cus tomers can perceive the changes as they occur just in time.

## 2.2. Source credibility theory

Source Credibility Theory [17] offers a theoretical explanation about how a message is assessed for its credibility. According to this theory, the receiver evaluates both the messenger and the message's credibility, attractiveness, trustworthiness, and quality. According to this theory, a receiver first estimates the messenger based on her/his attractiveness, reliability and trustworthiness [42]. According to McCroskey and Jen son [43], a credible messenger is an individual with the following five characteristics: competence, character, sociability, composure, and extroversion. Competence refers to the qualification and expertise of a messenger in the domain area of the message. Character portrays a messenger's sympathy and selflessness. Sociability illustrates a messen ger's temperament and friendliness. Composure shows a messenger's excitability. Finally, extroversion refers to the extent to which a messenger is frank, bold and fair, debating a resolution [39]. In the general public's view, renowned public figures who have these charac teristics are deemed to be more trustworthy, attractive and credible [44]. One way to measure credibility in social media is by the popularity of a social media user [45].

A receiver also evaluates the message's quality based on two factors of discourse logic and argument frame [46]. Discourse logic shows whether a social media user has provided objective evidence endorsing a product instead of subjective opinion on her/his social media post, reflecting the trustworthiness of a post. Argument frame illustrates the user's tone reflecting her/ his positive, negative or neutral opinion about a product offered in a social media post, portraying the attractiveness of a post.

## 2.3. The heuristic / systematic model of persuasion

The Heuristic / Systematic Model of Persuasion (HSM) [28] explain to what extent a receiver of a message processes it. According to the HSM, there are two different information processing streams: systematic and heuristic information processing [47]. Systematic processing is used when an individual thoroughly processes all the available information and cues in an argument (e.g., the reasoning behind the statement or the person who is arguing), using deep thinking and intensive reasoning. As opposed to the systematic processing of information, heuristic infor mation processing requires less deliberative information processing. Heuristic processing of information is generally automatically used by an individual. When heuristic processing is applied, an individual only pays attention to noticeable cues of information in an argument, such as an arguer's characteristics, the number of reasons presented in a state ment, or positive or adverse reactions of an arguer [43]. The heuristic/ systematic model of persuasion has been vastly used by various Infor mation Systems scholars [48,49]. For instance, Wathen and Burkell [50] have used HSM to identify the factors that affect website credibility. Their study states that assessing a website's credibility starts with high level processing of information. At first, individuals examine the pe ripheral cues such as the appearance, design, or reputation of a website. Then, they go through a thorough low-level analysis of information. In the end, the credibility results in an interaction of the outcomes of both high and low-level information processing $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ heuristic and systematic processing of information)<sup>1</sup> [46].

HSM and Source Credibility Theory are two theories that go hand in hand. They are mainly used together to analyze the credibility, trust worthiness and attractiveness of presented information. This study as sumes that the heuristic processing of information is used when an individual responds to a social media post's argument frame or an influencer's popularity because the positive or negative tone or the number of followers can be evaluated quickly by a social media user. On the other hand, the systematic processing of information is used on oc casions when a social media user thoroughly looks for objective evi dence in a post (i.e., objective discourse logic). Therefore, the user should spend a significant amount of time validating the influencer's facts in the post.

Extant literature also shows that a higher level of customer engage ment leads to more brand awareness, recommendations, referrals, sales, and profit [37,56,57]. Popularity is also a proxy to measure a promoter's credibility and provides higher reachability of the message to a larger audience [55]. Hence, if a social media influencer, with many social media followers, provides a social media post about a product, a higher number of social media users is expected to react to that post. ## H1. : A higher level of popularity for a message poster is positively asso ciated with customer engagement in social media.

Discourse Logic entails whether a social media post promoting or disapproving a product contains objective evidence versus subjective opinion about a particular product [60,61]. According to Source Cred ibility Theory [14], to deem a message (e.g., a social media post) cred ible, an individual (e.g., a social media user) assesses it based on the two merits of trustworthiness and attractiveness. A social media post's discourse logic represents the trustworthiness attribute, while a social media post's argument frame represents the attractiveness attribute of a message's credibility, respectively.

According to Source Credibility Theory, a message is deemed to be trustworthy if the messenger supports her/his statement by objective and tangible evidence(s). However, evaluating whether a statement is objectively supported is timely. According to HSM [28], to assess central cues of information in a statement, a receiver spends more time evalu ating it using systematic information processing. Therefore, a social media user would spend a significant amount of time assessing whether the social media post is trustworthy (i.e., supported by tangible evidence (s) instead of holding the subjective opinion of the messenger). If the social media user finds a post trustworthy, she/he is more likely to follow its recommendations [62]. According to Coleman [63], trust worthiness, as the primary driver of customer engagement [64], en dorses a higher level of social exchange discussions, leading to a higher customer engagement level. Therefore, an objectively supported social media post is expected to increase customer engagement, attracting more likes, shares, and comments. Hence, it is hypothesized that:

## H2. : A higher level of objectivity of a social media post's discourse logic is positively associated with customer engagement in social media.

Argument Frame expresses the tone (i.e., positivity, negativity or neutrality) of a post on social media, reflecting the attractiveness attri bute of a credible message [22]. As a peripheral cue, heuristically measured, extant literature finds positively written social media posts to be more attractive compared to the neutrally or negatively written ones, drawing the attention of more social media users to participate in social exchange discussions [65]. Moreover, positively written social media posts encourage other social media users to interact with a brand, creating a broader social media community and higher customer engagement in social media [66]. Additionally, customers perceive positively written social media posts promoting a brand to be more attractive while finding them more authoritative than negatively or neutrally written social media posts. According to Source Credibility Theory [14], a higher level of attractiveness leads to a higher perception of the message's quality and credibility. As stated earlier, a message's credibility (e.g., in a social media post) is the primary driver of customer engagement. Hence, it is expected that the positively (i.e., promotive) written social media post brings more customer engagement on a product in social media, attracting more likes, shares, and comments, comparing to a neutrally or negatively written one. Hence, we hypoth esize that:

## H3. : A higher level of positivity of a social media post's argument frame is positively associated with customer engagement in social media.

A product lives through different periods, referred to as Product Lifecycle stages. These stages are classified as introduction, growth, maturity and decline [24]. To effectively endorse a product, the choice of media, promotion, and messages depends highly on the products' life cycle stage [25]. A product that has been newly introduced to the market has a low demand [67], given customers less familiarity with it [68]. Therefore, the marketing objective is to disseminate information about the new product and to present it to the general public as much as possible [69]. One way to increase customer engagement for a product at an introduction phase is to select a brand ambassador whose voice would be seen/heard among a broader community [70]. The primary role of the brand ambassador is to increase the social media users awareness about a product's attributes [71]. The entire idea of choosing an ambassador is to increase brand awareness and encourage other so cial media users to get passionate about engaging and promoting such a product on their social media platforms [70]. Therefore, the introduc tion stage's best marketing strategy is to hire renowned public figures to promote and disseminate information about the newly introduced product since they have a higher number of followers and a higher po tential to increase a product's market reachability [72]. Accordingly, it is expected that at the introduction stage, customer engagement in social media would be higher for those social media posts shared by famous renowned figures compared to products in later lifecycle stages. Hence it is hypothesized that:

## H4a. : Product lifecycle moderates the positive relationships between popularity and customer engagement in social media such that the impact of popularity on customer engagement increases when a product is in the introduction stage of its lifecycle compared to other stages.

Customers at the growth and maturity stages presumably have become significantly familiar with the product, and it is presumed that the demand for the product should have risen to its highest level [73]. In these two stages of the product lifecycle, customers will pay attention to the details provided and spend much time reading about the product details [74]. Based on HSM, customers systematically process informa tion about a product to evaluate its quality and credibility at the growth and maturity stages. Therefore, it is anticipated that a social media post that is objectively supported brings the attention of many social media users who are likely to acquire the product. Accordingly, it is expected that customer engagement in social media would be higher for those posts supported by tangible objective evidence(s) at the growth and maturity product lifecycle stages compared to products in other lifecycle stages. Hence, it is hypothesized that:

H4b. : Product Lifecycle moderates the positive relationship between discourse logic and customer engagement in social media such that the impact of discourse logic on customer engagement increases when a product is at the growth or maturity stage of its lifecycle compared to other stages.

A product would be at its decline stage when demand starts going down. This stage lasts until the product is completely retired from the market [67]. At this stage, firms try to send positive promotive messages to customers to gain their attention back to the product and convince and attract them to acquire it [67]. Therefore, positively promotive messages (e.g., positively written social media posts) are more likely to attract potential customers (e.g., social media users) attention [75]. Therefore, exposure to positively promoted social media posts is pre sumed to attract a significantly higher number of social media users attention when a product is at its decline stage of the product lifecycle [21]. Accordingly, customer engagement in social media would likely be higher for those posts that are supported by positively-framed argu ments at the decline product lifecycle stage compared to any of the other stages. Hence, it is hypothesized that:

H4c. : Product Lifecycle moderates the relationships between argument frame and customer engagement in social media such that the impact of argument frame on customer engagement increases when a product is at the decline stage of its lifecycle compared to other stages.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Data collection

This study uses secondary data, scraped using web-scraping tech niques from two social media platforms of Instagram and Twitter to test the research model and its associated hypotheses. The scrapped unpro cessed data included posts' textual content. which was subsequently used to extract discourse logic and argument frame. The number of likes, comments and shares of a post, as formative indicators, was scraped to conceptualize and measure customer engagement in social media. Moreover, the number of followers of those whose posts were scraped, indicating popularity. The general theme selected was social media posts regarding vehicles. The main reason for this choice of product was that there are precisely distinguishable vehicles at various lifecycles stages. In this regard, to test the moderating effect of the product life cycle, four groups of vehicles were identified: (1) self-driving (i.e., introduction stage), (2) full electric (i.e., growth stage), (3) hybrid cars (i.e., maturity stage), and (4) gasoline/diesel cars (i.e., decline stage) [76]. The introduction stage is when a product endeavours to get noticeable attention through promotions or words of mouth on social media [69]. An example of a product at its introduction lifecycle stage is self-driving cars. At the growth product lifecycle stage, the product's visibility increases, mainly through similar introduction stages market ing incentives. An example of a group of vehicles at the growth product lifecycle stage is full electric cars [69]. The maturity product lifecycle stage is when a product reaches its highest profitability and attention. Mainly at this stage, manufacturers roll out new features to amend thei customers' interests. An example of this group of products is hybrid car [69]. A product at its decline stage has lost its profitability. Marketers mainly prepare a sentimental positive marketing campaign to delay the farewell of a product from the market. An example of a vehicle at this stage is gasoline/diesel cars [69].

According to the Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT) [77], people use different social media platforms to satisfy different needs. For instance, according to [78], people choose Instagram to socialize, entertain, or seek information about a particular product. On the other hand, Twitter is primarily used for sharing ideas [26]. Accordingly, to satisfy this study's fourth objective, data was scraped from both Insta gram and Twitter. These two social media platforms were chosen because various firms primarily use them to increase customer engagement in social media [79,80].

Moreover, the type of social media users between these two plat forms varies a lot. While on Twitter, the majority of users are older than 30 years old (i.e., 60%), on Instagram, the majority of users are less aged less than 30 (i.e., 80%) [23]. This significant difference in users' demography on these two platforms implies that these two age groups have different needs and wants. Therefore, firms should adopt a signif icantly different approach to increase customers' engagement in social media between these two platforms [81]. Hence, in pursuit of our fourth objective, we would strive to understand any differences between the relationships shown in Fig. 1 for Instagram and Twitter.

Having said that, in this study, we collected social media posts that met the above criteria for a period of four years, from 2016 to 2020. Our final dataset yielded 3324 posts, where 1583 social media posts were extracted from Instagram, and 1741 social media posts were extracted from Twitter. Table 2, provided in Section 5.1, illustrates the summary of descriptive statistics of our collected dataset.

## 4.2. Text mining to extract discourse logic and argument frame

Following the extant literature's recommendations, two different classifiers were implemented to extract discourse logic (i.e., objectivity vs subjectivity) and Argument Frame (i.e., Positivity, Neutrality, or Negativity) of social media posts. This study followed the recommen dations of Hajj et al. [82] on subjectivity analysis. Developed in Python, a classifier using a supervised support vector machine model, employing the parts of speech (POS) tagging technique, was implemented to determine whether the text of a social media post is objectively written or subjectively inscribed. The classifier was programmed to extract features related to subjectivity or objectivity written in a social media post [83]. The implemented code looks for quotations, past tense verbs, third-person pronouns, dates, and numbers to determine objectivity.

Argument Frame: ranges between − 1 to 1, representing negativity, neutrality, or positivity of the tone of a social media post.

Subjectivity is determined, detecting imperative and present tense verbs, comparative adjectives, first and second pronouns, and excla mation or question marks [75]. The implemented classifier contains a lexicon, a text processor, a trainer system, and a predictivity accuracy testing system [76,78], employing the outlined algorithm.

This study follows extant literature's recommendations [22,23,85–88] to extract the textual portion of social media posts' argument frame by a sentiment analysis technique to extract social media posts' polarity. There are different variations of sentiment anal ysis techniques. The choice of sentiment analysis technique varies the level of analysis and algorithm type [89]. Based on the level of analysis, sentiment analysis techniques are either concerned with determining the polarity of a given text (i.e., document-based sentiment analysis) or determining the polarity of a given text towards a specific feature (i.e., feature-based sentiment analysis). There are also three general groups of algorithms for sentiment analysis to extract the polarity of a given tex tual document: lexicon-based, rule-based, and machine learning [90]. Lexicon-based and rule-based algorithms use a lexicon to navigate the polarity extraction [19,84]. A machine-learning algorithm uses a deep learning approach to determine and classify a given text's polarity based on a set of criteria of interest [90-92]. This study used a machinelearning document-based sentiment analysis classifier implemented in R, using Sentiment Library, detecting the overall argument frame or polarity of the textual portion of a social media post. This classifier could classify a text, based on its polarity, into three categories: positively written, neutrally-written, or negatively-written.

Two of the authors independently read and assigned a discourse logic, and an argument frame for a randomly chosen 200 social media posts sample to cross-validate the results. To assess the agreement be tween the two raters, we computed Cohen's kappa. Results showed interrater reliability of 0.91 for discourse logics and a 0.90 inter-rater reli ability for argument frames, significantly higher than the accepted threshold of 0.6 [93]. Furthermore, to resolve discrepancies between the authors, two authors commutatively redid the above process to assign a discourse logic and argument frame for the chosen sample. Finally, we checked and compared the rated discourse logic and argument frames assigned by the raters versus the algorithms employed. The compassion results revealed a 93% accuracy rate for the discourse logic classifier and a 91% accuracy for the argument frame classifier. These two accuracy rates are significantly higher than the accepted threshold of 80% offered by the extant literature [22]. Hence, we concluded that both classifiers' outputs are reliable and can be used for further analyses. Fig. 2 depicts the research methodology employed in this study.

As shown in Fig. 2, this study took an explanatory approach relying on secondary data to explain how customer engagement in social media is shaped. Since this study aimed to understand how the textual component of social media posts brings customer engagement, the appropriate approach was to use a mixed-method approach, including quantitative and qualitative research methods. As explained earlier, the qualitative method was used to analyze social media texts and transform the textual document into quantitively measurable values to be used for further analyses. As suggested by the extant literature [94,95], the chosen textual analysis techniques matched this study's research ob jectives. In line with the explanatory research approach, the quantitative method was also used to explain to what extent the proposed hypotheses supported by the theories affect customer engagement with a social media post.

## 7.1. Theoretical contribution

This study extends the extant literature on customer engagement by conceptualizing and validating the concept of customer engagement in social media through the lens of social media users' participation in content (co)creation. This concept can be used as a proxy to measure the current or potential future customers' engagements with a product service.

This study also extends the literature by introducing and validating popularity, discourse logic, and argument frame as the main factors that impact consumer engagement in social media. Specifically, we extended source credibility and HSM theory to the context of consumer engage ment in social media. Our findings showed that a product promoter's popularity is a significant determinant of increasing customer engage ment in social media across social media platforms.

Moreover, this study extends the literature by examining the role of product lifecycle on enhancing consumer engagement when using a heuristic cue (here argument frame, popularity) or systematic cue (here discourse logic). The findings showed that some of the above correla tions vary by the stage a product is at its lifecycle. Our analyses show that popularity increases customer engagement with a social media post for a product at the introduction stage. For a product at the growth stage, we found that discourse logic (i.e., objectivity) is the only factor increasing customer engagement with a social media post. Finally, As the product matures, the argument frame's effect in enhancing customer engagement becomes more important, especially for products at their decline stage.

Finally, this study extends the literature by providing evidence that the effect of the factors affecting customer engagement in social media varies by the social media platform. Specifically, it was found that the antecedents of customer engagement in social media vary by platform. For instance, while on Instagram, subjectively and positively written social media posts bring more customer engagement for a product, on Twitter, social media users get engaged more with objectively and neutrally written social posts. This is a novel and interesting finding that shows the importance of social media type on understanding customer engagement. It moreover illustrates the need to study further the addi tional factors that impact customer engagement in different social media types, which could have a potential impact on firms' gaining competitive

advantage.

## 7.2. Practical contributions

This study also makes several practical contributions. As the first practical contribution, this study provided metrics to measure customer engagement in social media. Although on a practical level, indices such as Klout exist to show how social media users respond to post, no specific metrics can be found to measure and evaluate customer engagement with a promotive social media post. Having said that, this study argues that the number of likes and shares on a social media post as a good proxy to measure customer engagement with a social media post. This finding allows firms to measure how customers are responding to their customer engagement campaigns.

As another practical contribution, this study showed that renowned public figures' social media posts attract customers' attention and encourage them to participate in a social discourse regarding promoting a product on social media. This would assist firms to select influencers helpful in enhancing customer engagement properly.

As the third practical contribution, this study also showed that pro motion and marketing strategies should vary by a product's lifecycle. Specifically, it was found that for a product at the introduction stage, popularity plays a significant role in enhancing customer engagement in social media. For newly introduced products application of this finding enables firms to enhance the attention of more social media users to their products. Moreover, for the products in growth or maturity phases, providing factual pieces of evidence enhances customer engagement. This finding implies that to increase customer engagement with a product, the promotion strategy should shift towards using objectively written social media posts. Moreover, for products at the decline stage, a positive argument frame increases customer engagement. This specific finding implies that providing positive promotive social media posts for a product at its decline phase can bring back social media users' atten tion to the product. These last two findings empower firms to avoid spending their time, money and effort on plans that do not bring value for them.

Finally, as another practical contribution, it was found that firms need to have different types of strategies for their social media posts across various platforms to enhance their customer engagement. These two findings help firms adjust their marketing strategies, plans and spending to increase customer engagement and, accordingly, sales and profit.

## 7.3. Limitation and future research

This study only focused on two primary social media platforms of Instagram and Twitter. Therefore, the generalizability of its findings across other social media platforms needs attention. Moreover, thi study only selected posts regarding a high involvement product $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ vehicles). This study's results need to be validated by other research studies for different products or services types. Furthermore, in this study, we only used social media posts written in English for the sake of expertise. The classifiers used by this study should be adjusted for different cultures and languages to validate the results of the current study. Finally, in the present study, we only identified three antecedents for customer engagement in social media. Moreover, we just used sec ondary data for this study to validate the proposed hypotheses. Future research should find other customer engagement antecedents while employing other research methods to check the current study's findings generalizability.

## 8. Conclusion

A significant number of unique individuals worldwide are using so cial media. Hence, many firms strive to utilize social media to enhance customer engagement, accordingly their sales, profit and customer loyalty. This study attempted to define, conceptualize and validate the concept of customer engagement in social media through the lens of customer (co)creation of content on social media. To that end, it was found that customer engagement in social media is shaped and can be measured by the number of likes and shares of a social media post. Moreover, the current study identified three main antecedents of customer engagement: popularity, discourse logic and argument frame. While focusing on social media posts related to four different categories of vehicles at different product lifecycle stages, this study used second ary data from two platforms of Instagram and Twitter to validate its research model and its associated hypotheses. Having said that, it was found that the effects of the identified antecedents of customer engagement in this study vary between the two chosen social media platforms. While on Instagram, social media users engaged to a greater extent with a positively written social media post that is subjectively supported by a promoter of a product, on Twitter, social media posts that are objectively supported bring more attention. It was also found that these effects vary by a product's lifecycle stage. Across both Instagram and Twitter and for a product in its introduction stage, popularity of a promoter plays an essential role in increasing customer engagement with a social media post. For products at their growth or maturity phases, in general, and across both Instagram and Twitter, discourse logic becomes the most imminent factor in increasing customer engagement with a social media post. Finally, products in their decline phase require more positively written posts to increase social media users' engagement.
