# Understanding users’ trust transfer mechanism in a blockchain-enabled platform: A mixed methods study

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Blockchain Platform mechanisms Trust transfer Behavioral intention Mixed methods

## A B S T R A C T

This study aims to identify and investigate the various trust targets and their transfer mechanism in a blockchainenabled healthcare mutual aid platform. We first conduct a qualitative online interview toward initial users in the platform and identify three types of trust (i.e., trust in technology, trust in members, and trust in platform) that play salient roles in promoting users’ usage intentions. We then develop a preliminary theoretical model based on the interview findings and conduct a survey to test our research model. The empirical results suggest that three platform mechanisms, namely member credibility. blockchain certificate. and structural assurance. significantly facilitate the three trust targets, which subsequently enhance individuals’ behavioral intentions. A mediation analysis further demonstrates that the three trust targets partially or fully mediate the influences of three platform mechanisms on behavioral intentions through different path relationships. Our study contributes to the extant literature by uncovering the nomological network among three specific trust targets and explicating their antecedents as well as behavioral outcomes in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applications.

## 1. Introduction

Blockchain technology is recognized as one of the most prominent technology innovations by academics and practitioners [1–3]. Over the past decade, blockchain has been widely applied in the healthcare, finance, and supply chain management arenas [1]. In particular, decentralized finance (DeFi) is the most exploited field in the blockchain technology-enabled business ecosystem, and a variety of blockchainenabled DeFi applications—exchanges, lending, insurance, and even derivatives—emerge in parallel to the existing financial systems. Ac cording to the official report of ICBC,<sup>1</sup> there are 420 blockchain infor mation and service providers registered in China, of which 72 firms provide blockchain-enabled DeFi applications, accounting for 17% of the total. Specifically, in the field of DeFi insurance, Xianghubao, a healthcare mutual aid platform launched in October 2018 in China, has successfully applied blockchain technology to protect transaction secu rity and ensure the authenticity of healthcare claim information. Compared with traditional technologies such as enterprise information systems and social media applications, a key feature of blockchain technology is that it allows the global disintermediation, decentralization, and transparency of online transactions among various parties [1]. As a result, understanding individuals’ reactions toward the emerging technology application, especially their trust formation mechanism, has become a research focus in the information systems (IS) literature [4–6].

Despite great attention having been paid to trust issues in blockchain-enabled applications, to the best of our knowledge, the na ture of the relationship between blockchain technology and trust is still unclear [4,5]. Specifically, there are two different streams of research findings in the literature. One category of studies posits that blockchain technology is trust-free, and it could create an immutable, consensually agreed, and publicly available record of transactions, which makes institutional trust and interpersonal trust unnecessary in the blockchainenabled platform [5,7]. For example, Notheisen et al. [8] reported that blockchain technology replaces the institutional-based trust mechanism with a tamper-free, autonomous transactional system. Another stream of research emphasizes that blockchain technology could facilitate (instead of replacing) the development of institutional trust and interpersonal trust, and the trusting relationship could jointly explain users’ adoption and usage of blockchain-enabled applications [7]. For instance, Ying et al. [9] posited that blockchain technology creates a trustworthy environment that enables information transparency in the entire network and guarantees the immutability of data. Hughes et al. [10] indicated that blockchain technology could significantly enhance members’ trust since the technology enables immutability of informa tion and could easily detect any identified fraud. There is a call from IS scholars to uncover the nature and formation mechanisms of initial trust in the novel context of blockchain-enabled applications [2,5,10].

Overall, this study aims to develop a theoretical model to compre hensively understand users’ trust formation mechanisms and behavioral intentions in the blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platform. Specifically, our study has two research questions: 1) What are the trust targets and formation mechanisms in the blockchain-based healthcare mutual aid platform? 2) How do the multi-targets of trust influence in dividuals’ behavioral intention in the blockchain-based healthcare mutual aid platform? Notably, we focus on initial trust since it is a sig nificant prerequisite for users’ behavioral outcomes, especially when the user is confronted with a novel technology application (e.g., blockchain) as well as a high degree of decisional freedom [2,11]. Following the procedure of a sequential mixed methods design [12], we first conduct an exploratory qualitative investigation to comprehensively understand users’ initial trust beliefs and their formation mechanisms in the context of a blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platform. We then develop a research model based on the qualitative analysis results and trust transfer theory, and employ a quantitative survey to uncover the trusting relationships and examine their joint influences on users behavioral intentions in the platform. The sequential mixed methods approach allows us to gain a rich understanding of the salient trust targets and reveal their nomological network relationships in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applications.

## 2. Theoretical foundations

## 2.1. Trust and trust transfer theory

Trust is conceptualized as a target’s psychological state to accept vulnerability based upon positive expectations of another target’s intention or behavior [13]. In the context of e-commerce and social commerce, trust is considered a significant factor that facilitates an in dividual’s transaction behavior by reducing perceived uncertainty and risk [14–16]. Recently, trust in blockchain-enabled applications has received increasing attention in the context of financial services [3,17]. the sharing economy [5], supply chain [6,18], knowledge-sharing [19], online shipping, and prediction markets [20,21], and online gaming [22]. As shown in Table 1, the extant studies mostly consider trust as a single target and focus on trust in technology. To our knowledge, few studies have explored the relationships among multi-targets of trust in the context of blockchain-enabled applications [5].

Trust transfer theory refers to a cognitive process in which a person’s trust in an entity can be transferred to another related target entity [23]. According to trust transfer theory, the nomological network among trust targets depends on their similarity, proximity, or belongingness [23,24]. When users positively assess the relationship, they tend to build trust beliefs and subsequently transfer from one trust target to another [23,25]. Recent studies have indicated that trust transfer exists not only in the online environment but also in the transition from web to mobile contexts [25], from online to offline [26], and from offline to online [24]. For example, Lin et al. [27] indicated that trust in online service could be transferred to users’ initial trust in a mobile environment.

Despite trust transfer theory being widely applied in e-commerce and mobile commerce, our understanding of the various trust targets and their formation mechanisms in the context of a blockchain-enabled application is still limited. In contrast to traditional user-facing tech nologies such as social media applications and enterprise information systems, blockchain technology is a non-user-facing digital technology that does not require direct interaction with users [2]. Given the ubiquity of information technologies and the frequent interactions be tween humans and technologies, the traditional trust paradigm has shifted from interpersonal and/or institutional trust to trust in tech nology [28,29]. There is a call for more empirical studies to uncover the nomological network relationship among various trust targets in the emerging context of a blockchain-enabled application [2,30]. Trust transfer theory provides an appropriate theoretical foundation for an indepth understanding of the various trust targets and their formation mechanisms.

## 3. Research methodology: the mixed methods design

A mixed methods design is particularly appropriate where the context nature changes and scholars cannot draw significant insights from existing theories in the IS literature [12,31]. Considering that this study aims to explicate the trust targets, antecedents, and behavioral outcomes in the novel context of a blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platform, we adopt a mixed methods approach to provide a better understanding of users’ trust beliefs and formation mechanisms in the platform. Following a sequential mixed methods design, we first conduct a qualitative investigation to explore the trust targets and an tecedents, and then employ a survey to quantitatively examine their path relationships. Based on a parallel sampling strategy, we draw different samples from the same underlying population for the quanti tative and qualitative studies [32,33]. We collect data using both semi structured investigation and structured survey, and analyze the empir ical data following a sequential qualitative-quantitative procedure [33].

## 3.1. Research setting: the healthcare mutual aid platform of Xianghubao

Xianghubao provides peer-to-peer healthcare mutual aid service to a pool of members to pay for claims of critical illness. In other words, members who have certain critical illnesses or life-threatening injuries are entitled to receive lump-sum cash payouts, which are shared equally by all other members. Notably, Xianghubao platform is one of the first online healthcare platforms that implement blockchain technology to support the mutual aid service. On the one hand, “Claim Publicity” service contains detailed claim records with end-to-end traceable, transparent. and tamper-proof information (e.g.. detailed information including the number of members who have shared the payment, number of claims, and the corresponding case details, such as details of illness). As such, users can browse this function whether they are Xianghubao members or not. When clicking a specific claim, users can find out the presence of a blockchain technical signal and keep track of blockchain-enabled identifiers and the e-certification center. Blockchain establishes a peer-to-peer network where each participant in the network has access to a shared ledger. The transactions and historical records cannot be removed or altered from the ledger to ensure infor mation reliability and capital security. The application of blockchain technology prevents information tampering and provides in-depth access to aid information and capital flow. Further, the “\$0 to join” service allows users to join the platform as a member without premiums or upfront payments. Before entering the mutual aid agreement, the plat form will show users detailed insurance policies, including the collective claim-sharing clause, the scope of claim, membership rules, and guar antee details. The applicants are qualified to join the platform after confirming member identity, member payment account information, member credit, and body health status. Once signing the agreement, the smart contract and consensus algorithms of blockchain technology enable the mutual aid cost to be evenly shared by each member, and the fees are automatically deducted from members' accounts when claims occur. Specifically, the 14th and 28th of each month is the apportion ment day. The yearly maximum out-of-pocket cost for members is fixed, i.e., no more than 188 RMB (\$29.05 US) per year, and any excessive amount to meet the funding target will be covered by the platform.

We selected Xianghubao as the research setting since it is one of the most recognized digital platforms having implemented blockchain technology in China. Moreover, trust is essential in such a mutual aid context. For example, joining Xianghubao platform not only requires users to trust in the platform’s ability and credibility with regard to claim verification and payment process, but also to trust in the group of members to behave in a considerate and respectful manner (e.g., providing authentic claim information). Also, users may rely on block chain technology to fulfill their needs, such as improving the efficiency of the claim process and creating a trusted transparent environment, while assuring the integrity and immutability of data. Therefore, the healthcare mutual aid platform of Xianghubao is a relevant and appro priate context for our study.

## 3.2. Study 1: Qualitative investigation

## 3.2.1. Research method and sample

We first conduct a qualitative investigation to better understand in dividuals’ initial trust beliefs and formation mechanisms in the context of Xianghubao. We selected Xianghubao as the target platform since it is recognized as one of the first and representative online platforms that implemented blockchain technology in China. In particular, 58% of actual users on Xianghubao platform are young adults. Since our study focuses on users’ initial trust, we target our sampling at individuals who have no previous experience on the platform. Specifically, the samples for the qualitative study are drawn from college students of a university in China. In total, 44 individuals (21 females and 23 males) were invited to participate in the study. We collected semi-structured data using an online survey. A brief description was first provided to the participants to inform them that Xianghubao is a leading blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platform in China. We then provided each participant a task sheet and asked them to complete all the predefined tasks to ensure that they understood the operation mechanisms, the core functionalities, and the salient features of the blockchain-enabled plat form. The participants were asked to answer several open-ended ques tions after one week of experience, as illustrated in Appendix AI.

## 3.2.2. NVivo analysis

We conducted a content analysis of the semi-structured qualitative investigation in NVivo 11.0 following a three-stage iterative procedure [34,35]. In the first phase, the research group<sup>2</sup> carefully reviewed the participants’ answers, adopted open coding to identify the key concepts, and summarized them in different categories. In the second stage, the research group continually compared and analyzed the initially identi fied categories, and then summarized categories with common charac teristics into more general themes. In the third phase, the research group used selective coding to synthesize the centralized themes into higherlevel general categories [34,35]. A group meeting was held in the final stage to discuss the inconsistencies of coding results and build consensus, as suggested in the previous literature [34,35]. The exem plary quotations, sub-themes, general themes, and response rate during the three-stage coding process are illustrated in Appendix AII and Ap pendix AIII.

As illustrated in Appendix AIII, when asked about their trust beliefs on Xianghubao platform, three significant sub-themes of trust were identified from the case evidence, namely trust in technology (59.1%), trust in platform (52.3%), and trust in members (36.4%). Furthermore, when asked why they think Xianghubao platform is (is not) credible, 59.1% of participants talked about structural assurance. Additionally, 70.5% of participants mentioned the mechanism of member credibility when asked whether they trust in members on the platform. Notably, when evaluating the blockchain technology implemented on Xianghu bao platform, 36.4% of respondents emphasized the mechanism of the blockchain certificate. In terms of the future plans regarding the plat form usage, 65.9% of participants clearly demonstrated that they were willing to use the platform for healthcare mutual aid service in the near future.

We followed Venkatesh et al.’s [12] approach to examine the reli ability and validity of the qualitative data. First, to ensure design val idity, we selected suitable interviewees who are potential users of Xianghubao platform and conducted a semi-structured investigation based on an open interview protocol. Second, we integrated the quali tative data with trust theory to enhance the credibility and depend ability of the research findings. Third, we adopted a triangulation protocol where each researcher independently coded the semistructured data and corroborated the identified themes with other re searchers to ensure the inferential validity of the qualitative findings.

## 3.3. Study 2: Quantitative investigation

Drawing upon trust transfer theory and the qualitative investigation results, we developed a research model linking the three trust targets, antecedents, and behavioral outcomes in the blockchain-enabled plat form (see Fig. 1). We then conducted a quantitative investigation to uncover the nomological network among the constructs.

## 3.3.1. Hypotheses development

3.3.1.1. Trust in members, trust in platform and behavioral intention. Drawing on social trust lens, trust in members is established in the process of social restrictions and institutional environment [36]. This view is consistent with “trust in generalized others” where the target of trust is the entire community of members, which further affects in dividuals’ assessments, beliefs, and behaviors [15,36]. Based on a social trust lens, trust in members can be viewed as an antecedent belief (i.e., a confident expectation) that reduces social complexity and vulnerability, thereby encouraging users to engage in the “trust-related behaviors”, such as intention to share knowledge in the online community [37] and transaction intention with the community of sellers [15].

In the context of Xianghubao, trust in members refers to users’ beliefs that members on the platform will engage in proper behaviors [38]. When users believe that members of the platform can provide reliable information and keep promises and commitments, they tend to have a positive assessment of members’ behaviors and are more likely to use the Xianghubao platform instead of abandoning it [39]. The statement is echoed by the qualitative investigation. For example, one of the re spondents (P19) mentioned, “I tend to participate in the platform, because I believe that other members are trustworthy and will not be racketeers”. The above analysis leads to the following hypothesis:

H1. Trust in members is positively associated with behavioral inten tion to engage in the healthcare mutual aid platform.

Trust in platform refers to perceived integrity, reliability, and ability of the platform provider [13,16]. Trust in platform is identified in the extant literature as a salient factor for individuals to use an IS [13,16,40,41]. For example, Shao et al. [16] argued that trust in the platform significantly promotes usage intention in the mobile payment platform by reducing opportunistic behaviors.

In the context of Xianghubao, trust in platform refers to users’ belief that the platform will behave with integrity and credibility [15], which plays an important role in affecting users’ behavioral intention [2,42,43]. When users believe that the platform would act in their best interests and is competent in satisfying their needs, they tend to form a high level of trust belief. Consequently, users would be more likely to participate in the Xianghubao platform if they trust in the platform [44]. The argument is corroborated by the qualitative investigation. For instance, respondent P3 stated, “I am willing to participate in Xianghubao platform since it is credible and reliable, and can guarantee the effectiveness of the mutual aid process.” The above analysis leads to the following hypothesis:

H2. Trust in platform is positively associated with behavior intention to engage in the healthcare mutual aid platform.

3.3.1.2. Trust transfer mechanism. Drawing on Muir’s trust network framework [45] and trust transfer theory [23], a network of trust con sists of different trust targets, and one trust target (e.g., interpersonal trust) can be transferred to another trust target (e.g., institutional trust) in different contexts [23,30,46]. The cognitive-based trust literature posits that when people believe other platform members are trustworthy and there is not much social uncertainty, their trusting beliefs toward the platform will form quickly $[ 3 0 , 3 6 , 4 7 ]$ . Therefore, trust may transfer from trust in members to trust in platform.

In the context of Xianghubao, Sesame Credit<sup>3</sup> and basic health criteria<sup>4</sup> are set up as a signaling mechanism for evaluating members credibility to ensure members fulfill promises in the platform. Users with low credit and inappropriate health conditions are prohibited from joining the platform. These strict access conditions enable users to believe that members of the platform are qualified and well managed. This is beneficial to reduce users’ social uncertainty and increase their trust in the platform. During the qualitative investigation, one of our respondents (P6) explained that “The Xianghubao platform selects trust worthy users by setting thresholds, which makes people more reliable in transactions. This is conducive to facilitating the credibility of the platform.” While another respondent (P30) mentioned that: “Trustworthy people will voluntarily and honestly abide by the pre-set rules, which will effectively avoid the occurrence of fraud on the platform and make the platform more secure and reliable.” The above analysis leads to the following hypothesis:

Therefore, trust transfer may occur from trust in blockchain technology to trust in the community of members.

In the context of Xianghubao, trust in technology refers to users belief that blockchain technology can provide reliable technical support to fulfill the health mutual aid service [48]. 3.3.1.3. Member credibility and trust in members. The social trust liter ature [36,47] indicates that generalized interpersonal trust (i.e., trust in members) develops gradually through social restrictions. In other words, appropriate social restrictions are more likely to detect and sanction betrayal so that people may not behave opportunistically, which is beneficial to engender interpersonal trust. For instance, mem ber credibility may inspire interpersonal trust because a credibility assessment screens a group of members to include common character istics, such as high social status, high credibility rating [52], and trust worthy profile [53].

In the context of Xianghubao, member credibility refers to a user’s assessment of the credibility of community members manifested in several rigorous access restrictions [54]. First, the registered member must achieve a Sesame Credit of 650, which reflects a trustworthy profile and good personal credit. Additionally, users must satisfy basic health criteria for registering as a new member and pass through iden tity verification before joining the platform. These rigorous access set tings are identified as salient signals representing the level of trustworthy members in the online healthcare platform. As such, users tend to form a trusting belief in other members [53]. The argument is affirmed by one of our respondents (P3) in the qualitative investigation, who stated, “the sesame credit of members ensures the reliability and authenticity of Xianghubao membership.” Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

H6. Platform mechanism of member credibility is positively associated with trust in members in the healthcare mutual aid platform.

3.3.1.4. Blockchain certificate and trust in technology. The notion of certificate originates from cryptography, and it is viewed as a standard mechanism by which parties in commerce identify each other [55]. In the context of e-commerce, a certificate is recognized as a “digital cer tificate” issued by a reliable certificate authority. The digital certificate provides users a sense of secure connection [55,56], which serves as the proxy for quality assurance [56,57], as well as the verification of system safety [58]. Prior research has shown that the implementation of digital certificates, such as TRUSTe’s certificate of the privacy policy, could reduce individuals’ perceived transaction uncertainties and increase their trust beliefs [56,58,59]. Following this theoretical assumption, issuing certificates using blockchain technology enables users to get a verifiable, tamper-proof version of anti-counterfeiting traceability tags quickly and easily [60], thereby encouraging the formation of trust in technology.

In the context of Xianghubao, we define a blockchain certificate from a user’s perception, that is, the extent to which a user believes that the platform displays clear signals and openly proves their blockchain technology support [26]. A blockchain certificate is issued by a thirdparty certificate authority in collaboration with Alipay based on users certificate application requests. Each time a claim payment is settled, a blockchain certificate including block information and certificate au thorities will be released to all members. The blockchain certificate sends a clear signal to users that the mutual aid process (i.e., basic in surance information, details of illness, and claims amount, etc.) is open to all the members without any opportunity for tampering. Conse quently, the implementation of a blockchain certificate creates a trust worthy environment characterized by transparency and immutability [60]. During the qualitative investigation, one of the respondents (P1) mentioned that “…the blockchain technology redefines how credit is generated in the network...the usage of blockchain technology makes the information credible.” The above analysis leads to the following hypothesis:

H7. Platform mechanism of blockchain certificate is positively asso ciated with trust in technology in the healthcare mutual aid platform.

3.3.1.5. Structural assurance and trust in platform. Originated from McKnight et al. [13]. institution-based trust is defined as trust that is based on institutional structures, such as contracts, regulations, and third-party guarantees [13,61]. That is, people tend to use whatever information is displayed on a platform (e.g., self-proclaimed assurance, satisfied customer endorsements, or third-party assurance) to make initial trust inferences toward a platform [11,13,29,61]. Previous liter ature has reported the positive influence of structural assurance in enhancing users’ trust in e-commerce [13,29,61].

In the context of Xianghubao, structural assurance $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . g . } } ,$ statements and policy guarantees) plays a significant role in stimulating in dividuals’ trust in the platform. Specifically, official statements signal that online transactions can be conducted in a secure manner in the platform [13,62]. These institutional cues can help individuals make a cognitive evaluation of the platform’s ability and credibility, especially for individuals who are not familiar with it $[ 1 3 , 6 1 ]$ . Furthermore, explicit and detailed policies of the platform $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . } } \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g . } } ,$ , sharing clause of collective claim) ensure the reliability of claim-sharing transactions [62]. These protective rules and policy guarantees signal that the plat form is professional, competent, and reliable in handling the healthcare mutual aid service. This is beneficial to increase users’ trust belief in the platform [50], as illustrated by one of the respondents (P41) in the qualitative investigation, who said, “The platform provides a transparent feedback policy for each health mutual aid case to protect the rights and interests of users, making the platform more reliable.” Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

H8. Platform mechanism of structural assurance is positively associ ated with trust in the platform.

## 3.3.2. Research method and data collection

We conducted a free simulation experiment to empirically examine the proposed research hypotheses and address our research objectives. The free simulation method is widely adopted in IS literature as it is appropriate for researchers to study emerging technology without being constrained by the timing issue or the state-of-the-art of technology [14,30,50]. Additionally, the free simulation experiment is advanta geous in ensuring the participants form meaningful perceptions by naturally experiencing realistic situations and reducing biases from memory lapses [14,30]. Considering that our study aims to explore the initial trust formation in the emerging blockchain-enabled platform, we selected the free simulation experiment to control and rule out potential interferential factors, such as familiarity with an existing system [30].

We selected undergraduate students enrolled in an introductory Management Information Systems (MIS) $\mathrm { c o u r s e } ^ { 5 }$ at a well-known uni versity in China. A total of 247 students participated in the experiment, with 52.7% female and an average age of 20.27 (ranging from 18 to 23). Including students in the sample frame is appropriate when similar meanings could be attributed to the tasks for them [63]. The students are familiar with blockchain technology to some degree since they have learned the basic concepts and characteristics of blockchain technology in the MIS class and thus are able to comprehend the Xianghubao case that embeds blockchain features. Therefore, the simulation experiment is particularly appropriate for students since it enables them to experi ence the real environment of the study. Furthermore, student subjects are appropriate for theory exploration and building when the focus is on uncovering the internal theoretical mechanisms instead of theory generalization [63]. Hence, the use of student samples is consistent with our research motivation, that is, to explore the trust-building mechanism in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applications.

A pre-investigation was conducted to ensure none of the participants had any prior usage experience of Xianghubao platform. An online self declaration investigation was conducted prior to the formal experiment. First, we set up a question of “have you ever used Xianghubao?” with two response options (i.e., “yes” and “no”), to confirm the prior usage experiences of the participants. Second, we attached a formal invitation letter with the questionnaire to briefly explain the purpose of this study, and also, stated that the participation is voluntary and students could receive course credits for taking part in the investigation. Finally, 220 subjects who had no prior usage experience volunteered to participate in the experiment.

Before the experiment, the teaching assistant randomly assigned the 220 participants into eight sessions, with 30 to 35 participants per ses sion. The participants in the eight sessions were invited to take part in the simulation experiment in eight different time periods at an appoin ted small conference room. A small session design is convenient for ar ranging task content and helps the participants better understand the assigned tasks independently. After reading the explanation of the predesigned tasks, each participant was asked to complete a task list in the following order within one week: (1) read through the application information and register in Xianghubao platform; (2) understand the operation mechanisms of Xianghubao and experience each functionality in the platform; (3) check the detailed claim information and find out the blockchain technical label; (4) write up the case report and log out of the platform. Participants who had finished the task list were asked to complete an online survey after a one-week usage experience. To guarantee the validity of the responses, we included an attention check in the survey and asked the subjects to select a specific answer $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ strongly disagree with the question). Respondents who provided an incorrect answer to the attention check question were removed from the sample. After deleting seven invalid responses, we obtained 213 valid responses for statistical analysis.

## 3.3.3. Instrument design

We referred to the extant literature to design the instrument and measured each construct using a seven-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). We adapted from Johnson’s [48], Pavlou and Gefen’s [15], and Teo and Liu’s [38] research to operationalize the three trust targets, respectively. We measured struc tural assurance following Kim and Benbasat [64], and referred to Ven katesh et al.’s [65] work to operationalize behavioral intention. Member credibility was primarily drawn from the construct of source credibility (e.g., information credibility from Fan & Lederman [53]). Specifically, we adapted the items to the specific context of Xianghubao. We incor porated platform reputation [13,16], transparency $[ 6 6 , 6 7 ]$ , anonymity [67], and familiarity with blockchain technology in the structural model to control their potential influences on trust targets and on behavioral intention, and added demographic variables $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ gender, monthly in come) as control variables of behavioral intention. Because the con structs of blockchain certificate and familiarity with blockchain technology are emerging constructs identified in our study, we developed three items for the construct of blockchain certificate based on its definition and a review of previous literature (e.g., third-party assurance seal from Lee et al. [68]), and three items for familiarity with blockchain technology based on its definition and Gefen et al.’s measurement of familiarity with the e-vendor [14]. To ensure content validity of the newly developed instrument, we conducted a pretest with two block chain experts and sixteen users of Xianghubao, and adjusted several items based on their suggestions [69]. We also performed a pilot study with the 44 participants who took part in the preceding qualitative study, and revised one item of structural assurance and one item of trust in platform based on the feedback from the respondents and the psy chometric test of the constructs. The measurement items are described in Appendix B.

## 3.3.4. Structural model analysis

We employed SmartPLS v3.2.1 to examine the proposed hypotheses and the explanatory power of the research model. SmartPLS is suitable for theory exploration and prediction [70]. Additionally, our sample size of 213 strictly meets the common standard for PLS analysis-10 times the larger construct number affecting the same construct [14]. Following a two-step analysis procedure, we first examined the measurement model to test the reliability and convergent and discriminant validity of the constructs. As noted in Table 2, the Cronbach’s alpha and the composite reliability for each construct are greater than the benchmark of 0.7. In addition, the average variance extracted (AVE) from each construct is higher than the recommended threshold value of 0.5. The analysis re sults indicate adequate reliability and convergent validity of the mea surement model.

We assessed the discriminant validity of the constructs based on the approach of Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT). Specifically, HTMT is calculated by comparing the ratio of the between trait-correlations to the within-trait correlations [71]. As illustrated in Table 2, the HTMT values are all below the recommended threshold of 0.85, suggesting a good discriminant validity of the measurement model [71].

## 4. Discussion and implications

## 4.1. Discussion of findings

We adopt a mixed methods approach that combines qualitative and quantitative studies to uncover the antecedents, transfer mechanisms, and behavioral outcome of various trust targets in the context of blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platforms. Such an approach offers greater validity to model specification and model validation compared with a single qualitative or quantitative study [32,33]. Research findings obtained from the quantitative study are comple mentary to the qualitative study. Overall, our study advances and ex tends the extant literature by adopting a sequential mixed methods approach in the emerging context of blockchain technology. The trian gulation of qualitative and quantitative data can help develop a deeper and comprehensive understanding of the specific trust targets and an tecedents, and offer rich insights regarding the trust transfer relationship in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled platforms.

We obtain several salient research findings from the mixed-method study. First, our study responds to the call for exploring and confirming the trust network in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled appli cations [30]. Although blockchain technology is denoted as a “trust-free technology” that can eliminate the need for institutional trust and interpersonal trust [8,75], some scholars point out the possibility that blockchain technology may actually fall short of replacing institutional trust and interpersonal trust [5] and call for systematic research into the notion and formation of blockchain technology-enabled trust [9,10]. Based upon the qualitative and quantitative empirical findings, we find that three different trust targets exist in the blockchain-enabled plat form, which jointly affect users’ behavioral decisions in the platform. The research findings echo the sentiment of previous literature [5,9,10] that it is important to explicate the influence of trust on users’ behav ioral outcomes in the novel context of blockchain-enabled applications. Second, our study reveals the antecedents and outcome of the three trust targets. Despite an increasing focus on trust issues in blockchain-enabled applications [4,6], there is scant literature that uncovers the trust building antecedents. Our study addresses this gap by identifying three platform mechanisms (i.e., member credibility, blockchain cer tificate, and structural assurance) that directly impact the three trust targets. Consistent with social trust and institution-based trust theories $[ 1 3 , 1 6 , 3 6 , 4 7 ]$ , the empirical results reveal that interpersonal trust $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ trust in members) develops gradually through member credibility, while institutional trust (i.e., trust in platform) is formed based on structural assurance. Notably, we find that blockchain certificate is beneficial to building trust in technology, thus extending traditional trust-building mechanisms in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applica tions [13,15,16]. Third, we uncover the mediation effect of three trust targets between the three antecedents and behavioral intention. Spe cifically, we find that trust in technology indirectly increases users’ trust in the platform through trust in members, while trust in members indirectly enhances users’ behavioral intention through trust in plat form. Previous literature primarily focused on the direct influence of trust on behavioral outcomes [46,48,50], while our study uncovers both direct and indirect influences of three different trust targets on users behavioral intentions (e.g.. [6]). Therefore, we reinterpret the nomo logical network relationship among different trust targets and uncover their sequential effects on behavioral intentions in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applications.

## 4.2. Theoretical implications

This study makes three major theoretical contributions to the IS literature. First, this study enriches the trust literature by identifying the role of trust in blockchain technology. Although trust has received attention from IS scholars in the context of blockchain-enabled appli cations $[ 4 , 6 ]$ , it remains rather unclear how blockchain technology can be used to enhance trust. Different from prior IS research that focuses on institutional trust and interpersonal trust [30], our study emphasized the importance of a third trust target – trust in the blockchain technol ogy – which is conceptually distinct from and equally important as trust in platform and trust in members. The empirical findings can uncover the diversity of trust targets and provide insights for the conceptuali zation and measurement of trust in the emerging context of blockchain enabled applications.

Second, this study extends trust transfer theory by uncovering the nomological network among various trust targets in the context of a blockchain-enabled application. Although trust transfer has been stud ied in the contexts of e-commerce and mobile commerce [28,29], our understanding regarding the specific trust targets and their relationships is still limited in the context of blockchain-enabled applications [30,75]. This study addresses this gap by developing a nomological network model among trust in technology, trust in members, and trust in plat form. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the first studies that introduces trust in blockchain technology in the theoretical model and uncovers its relationship with interpersonal trust (i.e., trust in members) and institutional trust (i.e., trust in platform). The empirical findings can extend trust transfer theory in the emerging context of blockchainenabled applications.

Third, this study provides a comprehensive picture of the trustbuilding framework by uncovering the formation mechanism of multitargets of trust. Previous IS work in trust-building mostly focused on the structural mechanisms to build a trustworthy environment [13,15,16], while key antecedents of interpersonal trust or technocentric trust are often overlooked. Given the salient socio-technical characteristics across the new applications of blockchain technology, our study extends the trust-building framework in the specific context of blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platforms. Particularly, we uncover that three platform mechanisms, namely blockchain certificate, structural assurance, and member credibility, are salient antecedents that engender the three trust targets. The empirical research findings can enrich our understanding of a trust-building framework in the emerging context of blockchain-enabled applications.

## 4.3. Practical implications

Our study can provide guidelines for administrators of the healthcare mutual aid platform. First, platform administrators need to recognize the importance of users' trust perceptions in affecting their proactive behaviors. Specifically, given the disintermediation, decentralization, and transparency features of blockchain technology, the implementa tion of such technology is beneficial to handle complex mutual re lationships, involving sharing of payment information or mutual investment details. Therefore, the administrators could adopt block chain technology in the platform to release, govern and share public records of past transactions. This is beneficial to build users’ trust and mitigate their potential concerns in the platform. Notably, even though the idea of a fair and decentralized peer-to-peer mutual aid platform is tempting, its setup, organization, and governance is a challenge that cannot be solved by the use of blockchain technology alone. Platform operators and developers must be aware of other platform mechanisms (i.e., structural assurance, and member credibility) to create a credible institutional or social environment. In addition to trust in technology, operators and administrators should pay attention to interpersonal trust (i.e., trust in members) and institutional trust (i.e., trust in platform). The three trust targets jointly affect users’ behavioral intention in the blockchain-enabled platform.

Second, the platform administrators should establish appropriate and rational platform mechanisms to boost the formation of the three trust targets. For instance, service providers need to provide stringent access restrictions, including upload evidence, financial verification, health declaration and regular background, to prevent fraud and in crease the credibility of each admitted member. These strategies are especially effective in enhancing users’ trust in members. During the mutual aid process, service providers should establish and release pol icies related to mutual aid procedures and include significant penalties for inappropriate behaviors within these policies. The implementation of such policies and safeguards can help foster users’ trust in the plat form. Meanwhile, blockchain-enabled technical safeguards should be established to ensure the immutability of transaction information and protect the archived information from being deliberately altered. This is beneficial to increase the transparency and justice of transactions and enhance users’ trust in technology. Furthermore, after each mutual aid transaction, service providers should offer guidelines to enable users to be aware of the specific transactions through the release and tran scription in the blockchain certificate (e.g., basic information of insured, details of illness, claims amount, etc.). These strategies can effectively increase users' trust beliefs, which further facilitate their behavioral intention in the platform.

## 4.4. Limitations and future research directions

This study has some limitations that provide opportunities for future research. First, our study was conducted in a representative blockchainenabled healthcare mutual aid platform in China. Although we followed a rigorous mixed methods approach to identify and validate the pro posed theoretical model [33], future work is needed to assess the generalizability of our results to other cultures. Second, we conduct a free simulation experiment and collect data from subjects without any prior usage experience to examine their initial trust formation and behavioral intention in the blockchain-enabled platform. Although users do not have direct experience when forming initial trust beliefs [28], their subsequent trust in blockchain technology and willingness to continue using the blockchain-enabled platform may be contingent upon time. Therefore, future studies can conduct a multi-time-point longitudinal study toward a broad range of populations spanning across different age and experience levels to explore and depict the dynamic of trust in the long-term use of the blockchain-enabled plat form. Third, this study focused on three salient platform mechanisms that build the three trust targets based upon the qualitative investiga tion. Future studies could delve into other salient technology features of blockchain technology, such as disintermediation, transparency, immutability, and anonymity, and examine their influences on users trust and subsequent behaviors. Last but not least, users’ acceptance and usage of emerging technology may be contingent upon their social networks and individual characteristics [76]. As such, future studies can incorporate elements of the users’ social network (e.g., size) as contin gency factors in the research model to examine their influence from the social capital perspective.

## 5. Conclusions

This study uncovers the specific trust targets and their formation mechanisms in a blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platform. Leveraging a mixed methods design, we conduct a qualitative study and collect data from individuals who are new to the platform. Nvivo anal ysis identifies three different trust targets, namely trust in technology, trust in platform, and trust in members. Furthermore, we find that three platform mechanisms, specifically blockchain certificate, structural assurance, and member credibility, are salient antecedents that drive the three trust targets. A theoretical model is proposed to uncover the an tecedents, transfer mechanisms, and behavioral outcome of the three trust targets. A follow-up study is conducted to examine the proposed research model using a quantitative investigation. We employ a free simulation experiment and collect 213 valid responses from users who have experienced Xianghubao platform. Structural equation modeling is used to examine the path relationships between the constructs. Results support most of our hypotheses, i.e., three platform mechanisms posi tively influence the three trust targets, which further enhance users behavioral intention in blockchain-enabled healthcare mutual aid platforms.
