# Bigger from a distance: The moderating role of spatial distance on the importance of traditional and rhetorical quality signals for transactions in crowdfunding

Mohammad Hossein Tajvarpour <sup>a,\*</sup>, Devashish Pujari <sup>b</sup>

<sup>a</sup> School of Business, State University of New York at Oswego, 7060 State Route 104, Rich Hall 106, Oswego, NY 13126, United States <sup>b</sup> DeGroote School of Business, McMaster University, 1280 Main Street West, DSB 205, Hamilton, ON L8S4M4, Canada

## A B S T R A C T

This article investigates the moderating effect of geographic distance on the importance of traditional and rhetorical quality signals (i.e., language-based signals communicated through the narrative) for the success of crowdfunding campaigns over the Internet. We argue that the information gap between backers and in novators rises with the increases in the geographic distance between them, even for transactions that occur over the Internet. Drawing on signaling theory, we argue that since distant backers face higher levels of information asymmetry compared to nearby backers, the value of traditional and rhetorical quality signals are amplified for them. Interestingly, we show that the interaction of spatial distance and less costly rhetorical signals, such as positive psychological capital, is the same as the interaction of spatial distance and traditional costly signals such as past success experience and endorsement. An analysis of 114,276 Kickstarter crowdfunding campaigns, launched during 2009–2018 in more than 150 countries, confirms our propositions regarding the interaction of geographic distance and quality signals Our results remain consistent in multiple robustness tests.

## 1. Introduction

The internet has changed the way that firms, customers, and in vestors interact. In today's world, many geographic boundaries have been removed and firms can transact with customers and collaborate with partners who are thousands of miles away from them. It is of utmost importance to understand how customers and investors transact over the Internet and whether the Internet has removed all distance-related barriers. In this study, we show that although the Internet has removed many access barriers for distant transactions, the informational barriers are still there, even for online trades. We then provide remedies including traditional and rhetorical signals to reduce this barrier.

Crowdfunding has emerged as a method of collecting funds over the Internet, with millions of dollars' worth of successfully funded projects [1–3]. What is more interesting than the success of these projects in collecting millions of dollars of funds from thousands of backers is the huge spatial distance between the founders of these crowdfunding campaigns and their backers. For instance, the creators of Oculus Rift, virtual reality headsets, located in Long Beach, California, received support from places as close as California, other US States and Canada, to very distant countries such as the United Kingdom, Germany, France, and Japan, with an average backer distance of 5000 km from the creators. Observing this huge range in geographic distance between backers and innovators creates a unique context to study the role of the Internet in removing barriers of transactions between distant parties. The role of the Internet is acknowledged as an important facilitator of transactions [4,5] and is expected to reduce or even eliminate geographical boundaries. Crowdfunding by its very definition occurs over the Internet [4] and is believed to remove geographic barriers and democratize innovation and access to capital [6]. Crowdfunding over the Internet has removed many geographical barriers by enabling transactions between distant backers and creators, but it has not completely resolved the distance-related information gap yet [7]. This information asymmetry issue could be more prominent for transactions of innovative ideas in reward-based crowdfunding where the trade is happening for a product that is yet to be developed. Fund seeking ven tures often rely on signals that act as indicators of quality which implicitly or explicitly certify the value of a venture [8]. In this study we investigate and show that the value of quality signals is not equal for crowdfunding projects that receive funds from nearby backers versus projects that receive funds from distant backers. We argue that the in formation gap rises with increases in the geographic distance between backers and creators [7,9–11], even over the Internet. In this study, we not only investigate the main effect of geographic distance between backers and creators on the success of crowdfunding campaigns over the Internet, but also empirically test the moderating effect of geographic distance on the importance and influence of traditional and rhetorical quality signals. Rhetorical quality signals are language-based quality signals [12] that are reflected in the project narrative description [13] and are generally less costly compared to traditional signals [14]. We argue that as the average distance between backers and the creator grows, the information asymmetry between backers and the creator increases [7,9–11]. We further contend that the increase in information asymmetry gives distant backers an information disadvantage compared to nearby backers. Traditional signals of quality and rhetorical, less costly signals of quality are more valuable for the more informationally disadvantaged backers. Thus, signals of quality should be more valuable and influential for the success of projects that receive funds from distant backers than for projects that receive funds from nearby backers. Crowdfunding is perceived to help innovators connect with distantly located supporters [15], but research has revealed that geographic dis tance still negatively affects crowdfunding transactions [16–18]. In this study we aim to investigate the traditional and rhetorical signals as remedies for reducing the information gap that geographic distance creates. Past studies in this context have been limited to one country [18], one product category [15] or were limited to peer-to-peer crowdlending [17] and charity crowdfunding [16] where the innovativeness of the campaign or product is not important. None of the past crowd funding studies have investigated the interaction of quality signals and spatial distance. In doing so our study investigates the interaction be tween spatial distance and quality signals, in one of the most compre hensive reward-based crowdfunding studies. To the best of our knowledge, our analysis, by investigating 15 different campaign cate gories from 156 countries, is one of the most comprehensive studies of its kind in reward-based crowdfunding.

Our study contributes to crowdfunding research and the signaling theory domain by showing how geographic distance as a proxy for in formation asymmetry, moderates the effect of traditional and rhetorical signals on the performance of crowdfunding campaigns over the Internet. The rest of this paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we review the literature on signaling theory, how it relates to crowdfund ing, and the role of geographic distance on the value of signals in the crowdfunding domain. In Section 3 we propose our hypotheses, and we present our research methodology in Section 4. Then we present our analysis, results, and robustness tests in Sections 5 and 6. Finally, we conclude the article with a discussion of the study's implications and limitations in Section 7.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Quality signals

Signaling theory has been the most prominent theory in explaining crowdfunding dynamics [19,20]. The literature on information eco nomics [21–23] emphasizes the importance of signals in reducing in formation asymmetry between two sides of a transaction [9,24]. The imbalanced access to knowledge occurs when one side of a transaction lacks the required information about the quality or trustworthiness of the other party [24]. Since some details are private and unavailable to all parties involved in a transaction, an issue of information asymmetry develops between the side that has the information and the other side that could make better decisions with that knowledge [25]. Under conditions of information asymmetry, fund-seeking firms often rely on signals that implicitly or explicitly certify their value, and investors can interpret them positively as signals of quality [8]. When we discuss quality in this study, we mean the unobservable capacity of the inno vator to meet promises to backers [25]. In crowdfunding, information asymmetry is an important issue [15,20].

## 2.2. Geographic distance

Geographic distance escalates information asymmetry between trade parties such as backers and campaign owners in the crowdfunding context. Past research [6,26,27] has examined the effect of geographic distance for offline transactions between buyers and sellers [28,29], and on trade frictions between investors and their investment. Studies have shown the presence of home bias, due to the difficulties of evaluating and monitoring foreign firms compared to local firms [30].

Past studies on the effect of geography on crowdfunding have been limited to the peer-to-peer lending sector [16,17] or categories such as music [15], which are not generalizable to all products or other crowdfunding typologies. In this study, we not only examine the effect of distance itself on the success or failure of crowdfunding but investigate the moderating effect of geographic distance on the value of traditional and less costly signals as well. Geographic proximity brings an infor mation advantage, while geographic distance creates information dis advantages [9]. Thus, the importance and influence of indirect measures of quality (i.e., signals) is much greater for distant investors because they face higher levels of information disadvantage risk. The important research in this area, the major findings, and the contributions of this study are shown in Table 1.

An adverse selection issue is evidently present in international ex changes [31], and crowdfunding over the Internet [4] is a good case for international exchanges that take place between parties that are hun dreds of miles apart. Geographic distance between buyers and sellers is a proxy for the level of information asymmetry between them [32]. An alysts in locations close to the investment opportunity enjoy an infor mation advantage over distantly located analysts [33] because they have tacit knowledge that cannot be easily acquired by remote analysts. Geographic distance creates, or at least increases, information asym metry. It is therefore plausible to argue that the prominence of signals increases as the average distance between backers and the innovator increases. Our proposed conceptual model is presented in Fig. 1.

## 3. Hypotheses development

## 3.1. Geographic distance and crowdfunding

As discussed in the literature review, the effect of physical distance on bilateral trade has been the subject of numerous studies and its negative impact on international trade is well established [28]. Physical distance reduces the probability of an investment by Venture Capitalists (VCs) and this is because of the locality of information flow [34]. Geographic distance also negatively affects investment-based crowd funding decisions [7,16,17]. Thus, we safely recognize geography as a proxy for information disadvantage that reduces the chances of funding. Risk of failure in reward-based crowdfunding is very high because the campaign owner has no obligations in case of delay or failure to implement the project [35]. This risk will be higher for more distant backers that are already at an information disadvantage compared to nearby backers.

Crowdfunding backers are generally considered to be less sophisti cated investors [8] and the preference for investment in nearby projects (i.e., home bias) is even higher among such unsophisticated investors [26]. Since distant backers are less willing to invest in projects than backers nearby, we expect crowdfunding campaigns that are collecting funds from distant backers to have a lower likelihood of achieving their funding goal compared to campaigns that are collecting funds from backers nearby.

Hypothesis 1. Geographic distance between the innovator and backers is negatively associated with the likelihood of success of crowdfunding campaigns.

## 3.2. Geographic distance and the importance of signals

Geographic distance increases information asymmetry between backers and campaign owners [7]. Higher geographic distance results in higher information asymmetry which in turn strengthens the impor tance and value of quality signals. Geographically distant backers are at an informational disadvantage compared to proximate backers. Since they are facing higher uncertainty about the quality of the project, distant backers have to rely more heavily on the signals that are pro vided on the campaign webpage. Spatial and social vicinity increase the likelihood of information flows among individuals [36] and this means that it is easier and more likely for geographically proximate backers to receive or acquire information about a campaign compared to distant backers. Since more proximate backers have other sources of acquiring information about the crowdfunding campaign [7], they will be less sensitive to the information that is conveyed by quality signals. On the other hand, remote backers have limited, if any, access to external sources of information other than the Internet [7], and thus are at an informational disadvantage. Since remote backers are less informed than proximate backers, they will be more sensitive to the information that is provided through quality signals. Therefore, as a consequence of their elevated levels of information disadvantage, distant backers will rely more heavily on quality signals compared to backers who are geographically closer. Below, we discuss the moderating role of geographic distance on three costly signals and one less costly signal in crowdfunding.

Since we are aiming to test the moderating effect of geographic distance on quality signals, we must pick signals that are equally visible, and more importantly, equally understandable to all backers around the world. Only by testing the interaction of geographic distance with those equally understandable and visible signals, can we tell if signals have different value interpretations for distant versus nearby backers. Otherwise, it is next to impossible to infer whether the change in the effect of a quality signal with geographic distance is the result of its understandability or the result of the geographic distance. For example, an entrepreneur's affiliation with a university that is famous for its innovative and entrepreneurial activities in South Korea may not be equally understandable to backers from Europe and vice versa. Such signals are not good candidates for our study. For this reason, we care fully and cautiously selected signals that are equally understandable by all crowdfunding backers around the globe.

For the less costly signal of quality, we used the positive psycho logical capital language and for the traditional costly signals we used endorsement, past success, and preparedness (spelling errors).

According to the signaling literature the traditional costly signals vary in observability, veracity, and fit [25]. Observability refers to the clarity and visibility of the signal [25]. Observability is of utmost importance in noisy environments such as crowdfunding platforms [12]. Veracity is about the honesty and genuineness of the information conveyed by the signal [25,37]. Fit is about the extent of the association between the signal and the unobserved characteristics that are communicated through the signal [25,38].

The endorsement is by a reliable third party (Kickstarter staff), thus it is a very genuine signal benefiting from high veracity [39]. Endorsement signal is very observable and is high in fit and veracity. Endorsement is associated with the quality of the current campaign, so it is a very fit signal. Many first-time creators receive the endorsement as well as experienced creators.

Past success is very observable in the campaign's page and is high in veracity since it cannot be misrepresented, but it is not high in fit. The creator's current campaign may be way more ambitious than her past successfully funded campaigns. The project may also be very different with regards to the required skills compared to the creator's previous campaigns.

Preparedness (spelling error) signal benefits from high fit since it conveys the preparedness of the current campaign, it is also genuine since the writing skills cannot be faked by low quality creators, but it lacks observability compared to the other two traditional signals. Although the campaign description is very visible, receiver attention (i. e., the backers' willingness and ability to vigilantly scan the campaign and spot the spelling errors) [25] is not very high in crowdfunding.

## 3.2.1. Geographic distance and the importance of past success

Past experience is a form of human capital, and human capital is an important signal of quality [20] that is highly related to venture success [40]. Human capital is used by start-ups to signal their potential and to secure funds from VCs [41] or from crowds in crowdfunding [14]. Having a history of successfully launched past campaigns is likely to be very helpful for the campaign owner to signal her quality and experi ence. Since on platforms such as Kickstarter, the information about past success or failure of a creator is publicly visible and is equally mean ingful to all backers, it is an appropriate signal to test its interaction with geographic distance.

Spatial distance increases the information asymetry between backers and creators [7], and more distant backers face higher levels of infor mation asyemetry. Past success history signals the creator's experience. Having a history of successfully collecting funds reassures the distant backers that the campaign owners are capabale and have the required skills to deliver on their promises. Nearby backers will be less sensitive to this signal as they have other sources of information about the campaign while distant backers will be more sensitive to it. If this argument is true we expect to see a positive interaction between geographic distance and past success history.

Hypothesis 2. Geographic distance between backers and the inno vator(s) moderates the relationship between past success signals and the likelihood of success of crowdfunding campaigns in a way that increases in geographic distance strengthen the association between past success signal and the likelihood of success.

## 3.2.2. Geographic distance and the importance of expert endorsement

An endorsement from the Kickstarter staff can be considered an endorsement by credible experts in the crowdfunding context. Endorser credibility refers to whether the recommending source has the relevant expertise and can be trusted to provide independent judgment [42–44]. Expert endorsements are effective in mitigating the risk that consumers face [45] and endorser credibility is known to affect purchase intention [44,46]. Firms that receive endorsement by underwriters with high reputational capital can persuade more distant investors to contribute [9]. Kickstarter has both credibility and expertise in the field and thus a campaign's likelihood of success is elevated if it receives a “Project We Love” badge from the Kickstarter staff [4,47,48]. Since only high-quality campaigns can earn the badge and this internal expert endorsement is visible on the campaign's webpage, it can be used by backers as a signal to differentiate between high versus low quality projects. Accordingly, endorsement by external experts is an effective way of reducing infor mation asymmetry [39] between creators and backers. Being experts in assessing the quality of crowdfunding campaigns, Kickstarter staff pro vide reassurance to backers by endorsing a project. Backers may feel confident that the campaign will deliver the rewards if it is endorsed by the Kickstarter staff. This signal is especially effective for distant backers who face higher levels of information disadvantage and have no other source of information except the campaign page.

The argument about interaction of geographic distance and signal should also hold true for internal expert endorsements. Since distant backers are less informed by other sources of information, more distant backers should be reliant on internal expert endorsement as a signal of quality.

Hypothesis 3. Geographic distance between backers and innovator(s) moderates the relationship between expert endorsement and the likeli hood of success of crowdfunding campaigns in a way that increases in geographic distance strengthen the association between expert endorsement and the likelihood of success.

## 3.2.3. Geographic distance and the importance of preparedness signal

The creator's preparedness is an important aspect that investors consider when making their decision to support a project or idea [49]. Preparedness in crowdfunding can be seen by individuals in the quality of the project's textual description [50]. The very first encounter of the backers with a project's webpage is the textual description of the project. Having a flawless text can signal quality, whereas a text that has spelling errors and typos can indicate unpreparedness [4,19]. A project description without spelling errors is a signal of high crowdfunding project quality [50]. Text quality is a signal of project preparedness in crowdfunding studies and its association with success has been shown empirically [4].

If the backers read the campaign narrative and find spelling errors, they will be more cautious in supporting the campaign as it shows that the campaign creators have not put enough time and effort to prepare the project [19]. Distant backers are more sensitive to these spelling errors since they have no other way of checking the campaign quality and preparedness, but the campaign's webpage. Since distant backers face higher levels of information gap and are more sensitive to signals, we expect the negative effect of unpreparedness to be higher among distant backers.

This signal is visible to all backers and is understandable to all, regardless of their geographic location. Accordingly, we expect the preparedness of textual description to show the same interaction with geographic distance as other signals. As for other signals of quality the importance of a well-prepared text should be valued highly by distant backers who have limited information and face higher uncertainty compared to nearby supporters.

Hypothesis 4. Geographic distance between backers and innovator(s) moderates the relationship between preparedness signal and the likeli hood of success of crowdfunding campaigns, in a way that increases in geographic distance strengthen the association between preparedness signal and the likelihood of success.

## 3.2.4. Geographic distance and the importance of PsyCap

Positive psychological capital (PsyCap) refers to the existence of four elements, namely hope, optimism, resilience, and confidence in a person or in an organization [14,51]. Recent studies suggested positive PsyCap language as a less costly signal of quality that is associated with success in crowdfunding [14]. PsyCap refers to having the required confidence and energy to succeed at work, being optimistic about the present and the future, and having hope and being sufficiently resilient to pursue goals and bounce back when faced by challenges [52]. Positivity is considered as an important factor in organizations. Positive psychology literature suggests that this kind of capital can improve a company's performance and thus can be a source of competitive advantage [53]. Positive PsyCap language, reflected in the description of a campaign, can signal to the backers that the innovator behind the campaign is confident about their work, optimistic about the project's future, hopeful and resilient, and would not give up when faced with difficulties [14]. For example, in the sentence below the creators are using a positive PsyCap language that signals their confidence, resilience, and hope to the backers.

“We are adept in making video games and we are confident that we can effectively use the funds. The timing for this project is very propitious and we are optimistic that with the level of devotion and determination in our team we can deliver our commitments to the backers.”

Positive PsyCap language indicates the presence of positivity in an organization [14] and is a signal to backers of this competitive advan tage [54].

PsyCap language is a rhetorical signal that shows the mental pre paredness of the creators. When reflected in a campaign's description, it has a positive association with the likelihood of a campaign's success. This effect grows as the geographic distance between backers and innovator increases.

Hypothesis 5. Geographic distance between backers and innovator(s) moderates the relationship between positive PsyCap language and the likelihood of success of crowdfunding campaigns, in a way that increases in geographic distance strengthen the association between positive PsyCap language and the likelihood of success.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1. Sampling and data collection

We collected data of the universe of all Kickstarter projects that started between 2009 and 04-21 and 2018-07-16, excluding canceled and suspended projects. Following previous studies, we only considered those with project size of equal or above \$5000 and below \$1,000,000 [4]. In our robustness tests, we show that our results are robust even after removing the minimum project size restrictions. We also removed projects that collected more than 1000% of their target goals, since such campaigns are considered outliers as suggested by past studies [55]. To be able to access data about the location of backers, we limited our sample to projects with more than nine backers. We used Heckman's procedure to make sure that this factor in our sample did not affect our results. After removing observations with missing data (case-wise), 114,276 complete observations remained totaling for 2.2 billion dollars of funds. Creators are from more than 150 different countries. Fig. 2 shows a choropleth map of the distribution of projects by their country of initiation.

## 5. Analysis and results

The correlations matrix of our sample is provided in Table 3. For our analysis all continuous variables are log transformed to correct for skewness [14] and all continuous variables included in the interactions are mean centered [61]. To test for multicollinearity we used the generalized variance inflation factors [62,63] and the estimates of GVIF^1/2df (analogous to VIF for regular linear models) are all below 3, suggesting that multicollinearity is not an issue in the analysis. Since we are using a large dataset, we provided effect sizes as well as coefficient pvalue s for our interactions [64].

## 5.1. Logistic regression, Heckman's two-step method

Considering that the dependent variable is binary, and following other studies in crowdfunding [19], we used logistic regression to test the suggested hypotheses. Our variable for the geographic distance is only available for projects with more than nine backers. This may cause selection bias because projects that have more than nine backers may be systematically different from those that have less than nine backers. To account for this matter in our sample, we used Heckman's selection model procedure [65]. At the first stage, which is the selection model, a probit model is used to estimate the likelihood of a campaign reaching nine backers. From the selection model stage, we calculated an Inverse Mills Ratio (IMR) which we then used to control for the selection bias in the second stage which is the outcome model.

The first step is a probit model with the dependent variable being dichotomous if the campaign has more than nine backers and zero otherwise. In this first step we include all projects regardless of them having 9 backers or not. This model includes all the independent vari ables that may affect a project reaching more than nine backers. Lacking better criteria, and following previous studies [19,66], we employed a category level exclusion criteria using two categories; Arts and Games. These two variables are dichotomous showing whether a campaign be longs to the Arts category or to the Games category. The gaming cate gory has a very active community of online users around the world [19], therefore it is reasonable to assume that it is easier for campaigns in this category to at least reach ten backers. On the other hand, the Arts category is very niche, and each campaign has its own very selective audience, therefore it must be rather difficult for this category to reach the requisite number of nine backers. The correlation of the two cate gory variables with the success dummy and the binary variable for reaching more than nine backers supports the idea of using them as exclusion criteria. ## 6. Robustness tests

## 6.1. Robustness 1: cultural distance

Since cultural distance may also be a variable affecting success or failure of crowdfunding campaigns we controlled for the effect of cul tural distance as the first robustness test.

Similar to previous studies [67] using Hofstede's cultural dimensions [68], we calculated a composite index of the Euclidean distances of the six dimensions of culture $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ uncertainty avoidance, power distance, individualism, masculinity/femininity, indulgence, and long term orientation) for each pair of creator country and backer country. Finally, since we have ten backer countries for each campaign, an algebraic weighted average of all cultural distances of each of the ten backer countries and the creator country (with respect to the number of backers per country) was measured and used as the cultural distance index.

## 7. Discussion, limitations, and future research

## 7.1. Discussion

Two key specifications of crowdfunding transactions are that they happen between geographically distant parties [15] and that there is a problem of information asymmetry between backers and creators [20,71]. Our study investigated crowdfunding from these two aspects and showed the important interaction between these two elements. In our study we investigated and suggested traditional signals [24] and less costly signals of quality [12,14] as possible remedies to this informa tional gap. It is very interesting that a rhetorical quality signal $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ positive psychological capital) can be as effective as traditional quality signals, such as endorsements or past success.

In the absence of proper remedies to the problem of adverse selection, some otherwise advantageous transactions between buyers and sellers might not take place and one remedy to this problem is providing quality signals [9,72]. In our estimation, the average geographic dis tance between backers and creators is around 2500 km and has been increasing over past years. This physical distance between creators and backers is very high compared to the average distance between investors and entrepreneurs in traditional funding sources such as VCs [27]. The main way that crowdfunding entrepreneurs can reduce the information asymmetry issue is through providing quality signals [19]. Since the distance between backers and creators has increased due to advance ments in technology, it is very important for crowdfunding campaign owners to understand the interaction of geographic distance and quality signals. Our study addressed this question by investigating the moder ating effect of geographic distance on the importance of quality signals. Findings of this study have both practical and theoretical value by showing the importance of signals for the success of projects that plan to get funds from distant international backers.

From a theoretical point of view, our research adds to the signaling theory literature and crowdfunding research by showing that the value and influence of signals increase with geographic distance between trade parties. Previous crowdfunding research have investigated the importance of costly quality signals [19], less costly and rhetorical sig nals [12,14] and the interactions between different types of signals [12,14], but we add to the literature by proposing hypotheses and empirically testing the interactions between geographic distance and different traditional quality signals including past success, endorse ments, and less costly quality signals such as PsyCap. It is very inter esting that firstly geographic distance creates an information gap even in virtual settings over the Internet. Second, our finding that traditional signals can be effective in reducing the geographically related infor mation gap over the Internet is of important value for the literature. Third, this study shows that less costly signals that have been highly neglected by scholars can indeed be informative and are valuable to distant backers who are at an information disadvantage. This further supports this new and burgeoning literature that at least in settings where the stakes are not very high, such less costly signals are extremely valuable and perform in the same way that costly signals do.

From a practical point of view, our study provides influential sug gestions that help innovators increase their chances of success in modern fundraising settings in which the spatial distance between them and their prosumers is very large. Crowdfunding by definition takes place over the Internet [4,5] and is believed to reduce geographic hurdles for early stage entrepreneurs seeking funds. It is supposed to remove geographic biases and democratize capital [6]. Previous findings suggest that crowdfunding eases geographic constraints, but not completely [6]. Our research shows that although the Internet has removed transaction barriers between distant parties, it has not vet removed the informa: tional barriers between them. In this study, we have shown that not only are signals very effective ways of achieving success in crowdfunding, but, equally importantly and interestingly, this positive effect of quality signals is elevated for projects that are collecting funds from more geographically remote backers as well. Entrepreneurs will benefit from this study by understanding the value of quality signals even in virtual settings where the physical distance is assumed to be non-relevant. Our study interestingly shows that in these virtual settings geographic dis tance still creates an informational barrier that needs to be overcome using quality signals. Furthermore, our study shows that less costly signals of quality can be used by innovators to mitigate the information gap for distant backers in the same way as traditional quality signals. It is of huge value for a new innovator who does not have a history of past success to know that she can use the textual description of her page to signal other aspects that are valuable to customers and thus make up for the lack of other very costly quality signals. It is of clear value to newbie innovators to know that the wording of their narrative can act as a signal of quality and reduce the geographically created information gap. As shown in our results, positive PsyCap language as a less costly quality signal can be as effective as having a history of previously successful campaigns. Innovators can benefit from this finding and put more time, effort, and focus on their narrative to attract supporters from more distant locations.

## 7.2. Limitations and future research

Since our study includes 15 different product categories among many countries, our results have high external validity and are generalizable to other reward-based crowdfunding settings. Although our study is comprehensive with regards to product categories it is limited to the reward-based type of crowdfunding with regular unsophisticated backers. Future studies can extend our work and investigate the effect of quality signals on distant backers in pecuniary based crowdfunding platforms and among more sophisticated investors.

We suggest future studies to investigate the following important questions. First, future studies can investigate the interactions of spatial distance and signals for pecuniary crowdfunding settings such as equitybased and real-estate crowdfunding [73]. Second, it is important to for future investigators to study the value of signals for distant sophisticated and distant regular backers.

We expect the strength of associations identified in this research to be even higher in settings such as equity-based [20] or real-estate crowdfunding [73] where financial stakes are higher. With equity or royalty crowdfunding, backers have pecuniary expectations from their investment and the information asymmetry issue is much higher than reward-based crowdfunding because the goal is not just to make a product but to create a profit generating company [71].

The next important question to address is whether distant sophisti cated investors are affected by quality signals the same way that distant regular backers are. The answer to this question can be of utmost importance especially for investment-based campaigns such as equitybased and real-estate crowdfunding. We expect sophisticated investors to be more sensitive to costly signals such as past success and endorse ment, than regular backers. We expect more sophisticated investors such as those who invest in equity-based and lending-based crowdfunding to respond differently to signals that are provided by distant creators. Many of the equity-based backers are accredited investors [74] who are more knowledgeable and sophisticated than the regular backers. It will be very interesting for future studies to compare the behavior of regular and accredited backers with regards to signals provided by distant crowdfunding creators. The signals that the accredited investors pay attention to may be different from the signals that a regular backer finds informative. It will be very important to compare the effect of different signals among regular and accredited investors.
