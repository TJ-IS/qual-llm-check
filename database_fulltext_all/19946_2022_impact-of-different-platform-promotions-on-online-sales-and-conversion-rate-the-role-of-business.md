## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: E-commerce platform Online promotion Sales Conversion rate Business model Product line length

## A B S T R A C T

Facing fierce competition, e-commerce platforms are implementing various promotional strategies to attract consumers. Based on real-world transaction data from the largest online retailer in China, JD.com, this study builds a three-level hierarchical promotion structure and evaluates the impact of different platform promotions on sales and conversion rate. We find that monetary promotions have a stronger impact on sales than gift promotions. Among monetary promotions, standard promotions are more influential than coupon promotions And among standard promotions, direct and quantity promotions are more effective than bundle promotions. Moreover, we find that the platforms' business model (i.e., reseller or marketplace) and product line length have significant moderating effects on the impact of quantity promotions on both sales and the conversion rate, while the moderating effects on direct promotions are only observed on its impact on sales. Our findings offer guidelines for e-commerce platforms to implement efficient promotion strategies to improve their performance by generating online consumer purchase intention and behavior based on the business model and product line length.

## 1. Introduction

In the e-commerce era, e-commerce platforms are rapidly developing and facing fierce competition [36]. An e-commerce platform serves as an end-to-end software solution that enables online retailers to operate their business, including listing products, making transactions with consumers, coordinating fulfillment, and supporting after-sales services [48]. The three key characteristics of e-commerce platforms include the employment of digital information and communication technologies, collection and use of transaction data, and network effects among users in the online community [11]. For e-commerce platforms to enhance their financial performance, increasing market share through generating more sales and a higher conversion rate is the key. Online promotions provide an effective approach to achieve this objective [67].

Compared with brick-and-mortar stores, which launch most of their promotional events around holidays, platforms implement promotional efforts and strategies consisting of more types and at a higher frequency. Promotional information is valued by consumers to affect their purchase behavior [34,65]. In this study, we examine and compare the effect of five types of promotions launched by platforms, including direct, quantity, bundle, coupon, and gift promotions. In detail, direct pro motions refer to the direct markdowns of the price. Quantity promotion indicates that consumers can have a lower total price if they buy two or more units of the same products compared with the price when they buy them separately. Bundle promotion means consumers can earn a price discount by buying multiple items together. Coupon promotion refers to consumers obtaining a price discount through using digital coupons. Gift promotions indicate that the platform will provide free gifts with orders to consumers. Although promotions can increase sales [18,21], given the many promotion options that platforms can choose, platforms naturally face the following question: Which type of promotion results in the greatest increase in sales, especially when consumers can choose from several options?

In addition, is the increase in sales always associated with the improved online consumer conversion rate? The consumer conversion rate is defined as the percentage of purchasers over the total number of visitors to the product webpage [59]. The conversion rate is a key factor in a platform's financial success [16]. Many successful e-commerce platforms make significant efforts to maintain and increase the con version rate. For instance, the average conversion rate of Amazon is more than 10%, which is much higher than the average online consumer conversion rate, 1% to 4% [7]. Online promotions can attract traffic and motivate consumers to view a product webpage [20]. However, it is possible that only a small fraction of consumers actually make pur chases, resulting in a low conversion rate [9]. The reasons for a low conversion rate include the slow response time caused by internet congestion during promotions due to the high clickstream and the in efficiency of information communication between sellers and con sumers. The low conversion rate may have a negative impact on platforms' financial performance. Thus, when implementing promo tional activities, there is an urgent need for platforms to take into ac count the conversion rate [28].

The effect of platforms' various types of promotions on online sales and the conversion rate may also be affected by how platforms sell (i.e., the business model) and what they sell (i.e., product line length). Regarding the business model, platforms can serve as a reseller that purchases products from suppliers and resells them to consumers (e.g., Bestbuy.com, Macy.com, Target.com); as an intermediary marketplace in which suppliers can list the products that they have to sell (e.g., Tmall, eBay); or as a hybrid platform combining both of these modes (e.g., Amazon.com, JD.com, Walmart.com) [57]. The role of platforms affects their operational procedures, such as fulfillment and return, which can be observed and experienced by consumers. This in turn may affect consumers' perceptions of the platforms and the associated effect of promotions on their purchasing behavior. In addition, platforms need to consider what they sell by determining which brand should be listed. Various brands differ by their product line length, which refers to the total number of stock keeping units (SKUs) in a brand [4,52]. The product line length may affect consumers' perceptions of the promotion and their consequent shopping behaviors.

Although the problems above have received much attention in practice, little research has been conducted regarding the impact of different online promotions on sales and the conversion rate, as well as the moderating role of the business model and product line length, which is the objective of our study. We build a three-level hierarchical promotion structure. Promotions can be classified as monetary pro motions and gift promotions. Monetary promotions can reduce con sumers' monetary cost per unit of product, including direct, quantity, bundle, and coupon promotions, while gift promotions cannot reduce monetary costs directly. In addition, monetary promotions can be clas sified into standardized promotions and coupon promotions. Standard ized promotions mean that the promotions can be automatically applied to the order that contains the promoted products, including direct, quantity, and bundle promotions, while coupon promotions involve a redemption process, sometimes with time and product category re strictions [54,68].

Correspondingly, we raise four research questions. First, how do various types of online promotions affect sales? Specifically, we conduct a comprehensive comparative analysis of the impacts between monetary and gift promotions. Within monetary promotions, we further compare standardized promotions and coupon promotions. Second, different from sales, which shows the actual consumption, the conversion rate, which captures the percentage of consumers converted, is one of the most important factors in the efficiency metrics. Considering the importance of the conversion rate for e-commerce platforms, we seek to identify its influential factors and propose our second research question: What are the impacts of different promotions on the conversion rate, and do different promotions differently affect the conversion rate and sales? Furthermore, because a platform can distribute products through different business models (e.g., as a marketplace and as a reseller), our third research question is as follows: What is the moderating role of a platform's business model in affecting the effects of various promotions on sales and the conversion rate? Finally, considering that brands may have different lengths of product lines, we raise our fourth research question: How does product line length moderate the effect of various types of promotions on online sales and the conversion rate?

To answer these questions, we use data from JD.com, which is the largest online retailer in China. Our panel data set contains 8355 stockkeeping units (SKUs) from JD.com from March 1 to March 31, 2018. Our findings suggest that various types of promotions have different impacts on sales and the conversion rate. We find that monetary promotions have a stronger effect on sales than gift promotions. And among mon etary promotions, standard promotions are more influential than coupon promotions. In addition, among standard promotions, direct and quantity promotions are more effective than bundle promotions. Further, we find that the business model and product line length have significant moderating effects on the impact of quantity promotions on both sales and the conversion rate, while the moderating effects on direct promotions are only observed on its impact on sales.

The main contribution of this study lies in the following aspects. First, this study is among the first to investigate a hierarchical structure of the online promotions launched by the platforms and finds the different effects of these promotions. Second, we contribute to the pre vious literature by revealing that various online promotions have different effects on two of the most important outcomes affecting ecommerce platforms' financial performance – sales and conversion rate. Third, we provide guidelines for e-commerce platforms by finding the role of their business model decisions (reseller or marketplace mode) and brand selection, differed by the product line length within a brand, in affecting the impact of various online promotions. In this way, plat forms that sell products with different product line lengths in different business models can use efficient promotional strategies to improve their performance by increasing sales and the conversion rate.

The rest of this study is structured as follows. Section two reviews the relevant literature. Section three proposes the hypotheses. Section four introduces the data and variable construction. Section five analyzes the data and discusses the results. Section six discusses the theoretical and managerial implications. Section seven concludes this study and pro poses directions for future research.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Various types of promotions

The academic literature has investigated promotion effects in terms of different types of promotions, including direct discounts (e.g., [32,53]), quantity discounts (e.g., [2,71]), coupons (e.g., [37,44]), promotional games (e.g., [40,45]), reward programs (e.g., [31,56]), and rebates (e.g., [15,61]). Some previous studies (e.g., [12,30,66]) compared the effects of different types of promotions on consumer purchasing behavior. For example, Kivetz and Zheng [30] compared the effects of direct price promotions and quantity promotions on gener ating consumer purchasing behavior. They found that the relative magnitude of the effect depends on the nature of products, that is, whether they are hedonic or utilitarian products. Chen et al. [12] compared coupons and rebates on consumer purchasing behavior. They found that consumers typically use coupons in their purchase process; however, consumers redeem rebates only when they have a high mar ginal utility of income. Most previous studies, such as the two above, compared the effects of two types of promotions. This study contributes to the stream of research on the impact of promotions on sales by building a three-level hierarchical promotion structure and providing a more comprehensive comparison of the effects of various types of promotions.

Compared with the numerous studies examining the impact of pro motions on sales, only a few studies $( \mathbf { e } .  g . , [ 1 6 , 2 3 ] )$ have examined the effect of promotions on the consumer conversion rate. Di Fatta et al. [16] focused on products listed on the e-commerce websites of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and found that higher product quality or price promotions increase the consumer conversion rate. However, the effect is more significant if SME retailers use these two strategies separately compared to using them as a mixed strategy. Hock et al. [23] found that winning a discount from promotional games increases con sumer spending and the conversion rate. Our study contributes to pro motion research by identifying the most effective promotional strategies in increasing the conversion rate and by comparing the effects of various types of promotions on sales and the conversion rate.

## 2.2. The role of the business model

In e-commerce, a platform can play the role as a reseller or a marketplace, as examined in previous studies (e.g., [35,57]). Hagiu and Wright [22] found that the optimal tailoring of marketing activities plays an important role in determining the performance of a platform as a reseller or marketplace. The existence of a spillover effect of promoting certain products on the sales of other products listed on a platform en courages the platform to adopt the reseller mode. In addition to the reseller and marketplace modes, Tian et al. [57] explored the hybrid mode of platforms and pointed out that the hybrid mode is especially appropriate for a platform when it faces a moderate order fulfillment cost and when the intensity of competition among suppliers who list on the platform is at a moderate level. Li et al. [35] examined the impact of platform-performance investment on platforms' decisions of the business model and found that the decision depends on the royalty rate, invest ment efficiency, and various costs.

Previous studies have discussed the impact of promotions on online sales in the reseller mode [10,25], the marketplace mode (e.g., [41,67]) and the hybrid mode (e.g., [28,63]). For example, regarding the reseller mode, conducting an empirical study of an online fashion retailer, Hu and Tadikamalla [25] found that the impact of promotions on sales depends on the age of products. Thus, they proposed solutions with regard to the optimal time to promote new products. Regarding the marketplace mode, Zhang et al. [67] conducted an empirical study on the Taobao platform and found that promotions have both a short-term effect on doubling the sales of promoted products and a long-term effect on increasing consumer engagement in viewing and purchasing prod ucts on the platform. Regarding the hybrid mode, using data from Amazon, Jiang et al. [28] concluded that the impact of price promotions on online sales is higher if products have higher substitutability and consumers have more consistency in terms of their valuation of the products. However, when examining the impact of promotions on sales, most previous studies have not specifically compared the reseller or marketplace mode. Our study fills this literature gap by explicitly examining the moderating role of the reseller and marketplace modes in affecting the effects of various promotions on online sales and the con version rate.

## 2.3. The role of product line length

Previous studies have examined both producers' optimal decisions regarding product line length and the impact of product line length. Regarding the determination of the optimal product line length, Dra ganska and Jain [17] included product line length decisions in the consumer utility function and determined the optimal length based on “what-if” experiments. Hopp and Xu [24] used a Bayesian logit model to determine the optimal product line length with pricing decisions. They found that the optimal product line length depends on the degree of modularity and the risk attitude of the producers. In terms of the impact of product line length, the literature has examined its impact on con sumers' perceptions and actions and companies' performance. Many previous studies (e.g., [4,17,70]) claimed that consumers perceive that brand with a long product line length have higher brand quality due to the greater expertise of the providers and their higher commitment to the specific category, which makes consumers generate a positive perception toward the brand. In this way, product line length can affect consumers' choices and purchase behavior [60]. In addition, product line length can also increase companies' competitive advantage and brand sales, and it has a positive relationship with brand performance in the long run [3,4]. Although the aforementioned studies have investi gated the impact of product line length on consumer behavior and companies' performance, how product line length plays a role in the effect of various online promotions is still unexplored. In this study, we investigate the moderating effect of product line length on the impact of various types of online promotions on sales and conversion rates.

## 3. Hypothesis development

## 3.1. Impact of different platform promotions on online sales

Consumers often conduct cost-benefit analysis to make their pur chase decisions [72]. Higher utilitarian and hedonic values and lower costs can increase consumers' utility and thus generate more sales. Various promotion types can reduce consumers' costs per unit of the product; however, they can have different levels of attractiveness to consumers, causing consumers to evaluate them differently and thus have different effectiveness [30]. Monetary promotions (promotions that reduce product prices), such as standardized and coupon pro motions, lower the monetary cost per unit that consumers incur to ac quire products. Therefore, such promotions better meet consumers budget constraints and consequently motivate them to purchase more. Comparatively, gift promotions (promotions that offer gifts) trigger consumers to have positive emotions toward purchasing, but they do not directly lower their costs per unit of products. The effect of gift pro motions depends on the relevance of the gifts to the promoted products, the attractiveness of the gifts, and the characteristics of the gifts [6].

Among monetary promotions, coupon promotions, rather than standardized promotions, including direct, quantity, and bundle pro motions, often need to be redeemed by consumers [42]. However, consumers' redemption behavior depends on coupon convenience, such as whether the coupon can be applied for a large range of products and the expiration date of the coupon [68]. In addition, coupons may be less visible than standardized promotions because consumers need to manually find and clip coupons from one of the following places including the product detail webpages, the promotional landing web pages, and coupon mall, which is a specific section of the platforms to distribute coupons to consumers [49].

Among standardized promotions, bundle promotions are less effi cient in generating more sales than direct and quantity promotions because bundle promotions may not always be attractive to consumers. The reason is that a product that is bundled may be auxiliary and may not be as needed as the focal products that consumers originally inten ded to purchase; furthermore, consumers have to pay extra to acquire the additional product to receive the benefits of the promotion. There fore, based on the discussion above, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1. Monetary promotions have stronger positive effects on sales than gift promotions (H1a). Among monetary promotions, standardized promotions have stronger positive effects on sales than coupon pro motions (H1b). Among standardized promotions, direct and quantity promotions have stronger positive effects on sales than bundle pro motions (H1c).

## 3.2. Impact of different platform promotions on the online consumer conversion rate

Once consumers have accessed a website, promotions increase their likelihood of deciding to purchase in several ways. First, price pro motions lower the cost per unit of product incurred by consumers and enhance their utility, thus making it easier for consumers to make their purchase decisions [13]. Second, once consumers visit a product web page, a promotion stimulates a positive psychological state for con sumers that strengthens their cognitive feelings and facilitates their deep immersion in the online shopping process, that is, flow [46]. Flow constitutes consumers' greater willingness to engage in the online environment and enhances their purchase intentions, thus increasing the conversion rate [26]. Third, obtaining promotion information when browsing a product's webpage can make consumers generate positive feelings of being excited and lucky. Such positive feelings can generate impulse buying behaviors, which contribute to a higher conversion rate. Finally, consumers may worry that if they miss the current promotional opportunity, prices in the future will increase, which also stimulates their purchase intentions [39].

Among different types of promotions, monetary promotions can directly reduce the cost per unit of product, and thus we conjecture that they are more effective in generating a higher conversion rate than gift promotions. In addition, standardized promotions can be applied more straightforwardly compared with coupon promotions, which need to have a redemption process that generates hassle costs and are less attractive [33]. This makes coupon promotions less likely to convert more consumers than standardized promotions. Moreover, compared with bundle promotions that are preset by the platform, direct and quantity promotions can be more efficient in converting consumers because consumers have more flexibility to choose products. Therefore, based on the discussion above, we propose the following hypothesis:

H2. Monetary promotions have stronger positive effects on the con version rate than gift promotions (H2a). Among monetary promotions, standardized promotions have stronger positive effects on the conver sion rate than coupon promotions (H2b). Among standardized pro motions, direct and quantity promotions have stronger positive effects on the conversion rate than bundle promotions (H2c).

## 3.3. Moderating roles of the business model and product line length

When a platform plays the role of a reseller, consumers can make transactions with the platform directly. Compared with the marketplace mode in which the platform serves as an intermediary, the reseller role of the platform can reduce the parties involved in the transaction pro cess, alleviate the marginalization issues that each party tends to have as a result of maximizing its own interests, strengthen the contractual supplier-buyer relationship, and thus increase consumer trust and the effectiveness of promotion [55]. In addition, in the reseller mode, the platform takes ownership and has total control rights over the products in terms of their operations and marketing, such as fulfillment, pricing, promotion, and consumer services [22]. This may be perceived by consumers as a quality guarantee, which makes promotion more attractive to consumers. Thus, consumers will have fewer concerns about discounts, which sometimes may indicate a lower quality of the product [43]. In this way, promotions can better generate consumers purchase behavior in the reseller mode than in the marketplace mode.

A longer product line serves as a higher quality cue [4,17,70]. This is because brands with long product line lengths often have a higher commitment to the category and have higher expertise and competence [5,38]. This quality signal can be successfully received by consumers to enhance their favor toward this brand [4]. Thus, the promotions for products that are in long product lines are more attractive to consumers because consumers tend to perceive these products are with stronger brands [8]. Based on the preceding discussion, we propose the following hypothesis:

H3. The positive effects of promotions on sales are stronger for prod ucts sold in the reseller mode than for those sold in the marketplace mode (H3a). Among products sold in the reseller mode, the positive effects of promotions on sales are stronger for products with a higher level of product line length within the brand (H3b).

Compared with third-party sellers, consumers often have more fa miliarity with and trust in a platform serving as a direct seller because of their previous purchase experience with the platform and the platform's larger size and higher reputation. Thus, the promoted products that are in the reseller modes tend to increase consumers' intention to visit their webpages to purchase, rather than only visiting product webpages for information browsing, and this leads to higher conversion rates [50]. In addition, consumers have a stronger relationship with a platform serving as a direct seller because the platform is more stable and is the party with which they make direct transactions. Thus, the promotion information is more attractive and visible, which generates consumers impulsive behavior and loyalty behavior such as frequently repeated purchases, thus increasing the effectiveness of promotion [69].

A brand with a longer product line length enhances consumers perceived quality of the brand [4,17,70]. A finer distinction among products in the product line, such as having more color, function, size, and style options of the products, is likely to convey category expertise and core competence of the brand [5]. In this way, when the product within a longer product line length is in promotion, the brand effect could be amplified, which increases the effectiveness of promotion. In addition, a longer product line length can cater to a wider range of consumer preferences. Therefore, when consumers browse the infor mation of the promoted products that are in longer product lines, they have more flexibilities to choose and are more likely to find their desirable attributes to better fit their preferences [70]. This reduces their hesitations to purchase after they browse and acquire the product in formation and thus increases the conversion rate to a larger extent. Based on the preceding discussion, we hypothesize the following:

H4. The positive effects of promotions on the conversion rate are stronger for products sold in the reseller mode than for those sold in the marketplace mode (H4a). Among products sold in the reseller mode, the positive effects of promotions on the conversion rate are stronger for products with a higher level of product line length within the brand (H4b).

## 4. Data

Our data are obtained from JD.com, which offers its transaction-level data for the 2020 MSOM data-driven research challenge [49]. JD.com is the largest online retailer in China. In 2020, its net revenue was \$114.3 billion, and it had over 471 million annual active customers [27]. The dataset provides detailed information, including prices, promotions, and sales for each SKU and each order in a specific product category on JD. com from March 1 to March 31, 2018. SKUs refer to the items of stock, which are specific to the product attributes including color, function, size, and style [51,58]. Our dataset shows that the average standard deviation of the promotion rate for each SKU during each day is 0.006, indicating the daily promotion received by different consumers is rela tively stable. Therefore, based on the original data, we construct a panel data structure of 8355 SKUs by calculating the daily sales and the daily average promotions of each SKU

The descriptive statistics of our main variables are listed in Table 1. The dependent variables in our study include daily sales $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ Sales) and the daily conversion rate (i.e., Conversion). Sales represents the daily sales amount of each SKU, with an average of approximately RMB 1230. Conversion is measured by the ratio of the number of product purchases made in a day and the number of clicks of the product's webpage on the same day. We find that the average conversion rate is approximately 7%.

Our main independent variables include five common types of pro motions on JD.com: (1) direct promotions, (2) quantity promotions, (3) bundle promotions, (4) coupon promotions, and (5) gift promotions. Specifically, (1) a direct promotion represents a direct price reduction in the list price, as shown on a product's webpage. (2) A quantity promotion is used to encourage consumers to purchase more, and it can take different forms, such as “receive an RMB 100 discount if spending over RMB 199” or “buy 2, get 1 free”. (3) A bundle promotion can be used if consumers buy a “prespecified bundle” of SKUs within an order. For instance, an iPad and its pencil can be purchased as a bundle, which is cheaper than purchasing them separately. (4) Coupons can also be applied to orders. They can be clipped by consumers from various pla ces, such as the webpage of a product and the “coupon mall” (a specific section on JD.com for coupon distribution). JD.com also sends person alized coupons to consumers based on their past activities. (5) A gift promotion means that consumers can obtain a free gift if they buy a certain set of SKUs.

In addition, products with gift pro motions account for approximately 5% of our sample. To evaluate the hierarchical impacts of promotions on sales and the conversion rate, we classify the first four types of promotions as monetary promotions, in which promotions take the form of money, whereas the last type of promotion is classified as a gift promotion. Among monetary pro motions, direct, quantity, and bundle promotions can be further classi fied as standardized promotions, which are automatically applied once certain criteria are met. Different from standardized promotions, cou pons have to be manually clipped by consumers before making a purchase.

The moderators in this study include the SKU product type (i.e., Reseller) and product line length (i.e., Length). For the product type, we define Reseller = 1 if a product is distributed under the reseller mode. Otherwise, Reseller = 0 if a product is distributed under the marketplace mode. In general, products in the reseller mode usually have higher market shares within the category, and JD.com has full control of these products, such as inventory management, pricing, and customer service, whereas products in the marketplace mode are managed by third-party sellers in the marketplace. According to Table 1, products in the reseller mode account for approximately 11% of our sample. Products distrib uted under two different modes compete for market share on JD.com by adopting different pricing mechanisms and activities. Consumers can easily see the business model of each product, and thus, the business model may have a moderating effect on the impact of promotions on sales and the conversation rate. In addition to the business model, we construct a moderator (i.e., Length) that captures the product line length in the brand by calculating the number of SKUs in the same brand with the focal SKU. We find that the average product line length is approxi mately 245.

The control variables in this study include Price. Weekend, and Holiday. Specifically, Holiday represents the Women's Day on March 8 (i.e., JD Queen's Day), during which JD.com launches a series of promotional activities. We observe that the average daily sales are significantly higher during holidays than during nonholidays. In addition, Weekend is used to capture the effect of time on sales because our data show that average daily sales are higher on weekdays than on weekends. All correlations between each pair of independent variables are less than 0.50, indi cating that there are no significant multicollinearity issues.

## 6. Theoretical and managerial implications

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

The findings of this study contribute to the existing promotion literature in three ways. First, our study contributes to previous comparative studies of the different impacts of various types of pro motions (e.g., [30,66]) by providing a more comprehensive framework for comparison using a three-level hierarchical promotion structure. Our comparison includes monetary and nonmonetary promotions, stan dardized and nonstandardized monetary promotions, and direct, quan tity, and bundle promotions within the standardized monetary promotion type.

Second, our study examines the impact of different online pro motions on consumer purchasing behavior from the perspective of the conversion rate, enriching the existing literature, which has mainly focused on the effects of promotions on sales (e.g., [18,37]). We show that promotions not only increase the sales amount, as measured by sales, but also enhance sales effectiveness, as measured by the conver sion rate. More importantly, our study compares the promotion effect between sales and the conversion rate and finds that direct and quantity promotions are most effective in increasing sales, while quantity pro motions are most effective in increasing the conversion rate. This finding reveals the different mechanisms of influence of promotions on con sumer purchasing behaviors.

Third, our study contributes to the promotion literature by investi gating how the effects of various promotions on sales and the conversion rate vary by the business model and product line length. In terms of the business model, even though previous studies (e.g., [22,57]) have investigated the platforms' operational difference between different business models, very few studies have differentiated business models when examining the promotion effect. Our study contributes to the existing literature by investigating how the platform reseller and marketplace modes affect the promotion effect on sales and the con version rate. Thus, it provides a framework for the comparison of pro motion effects under different business models in the e-commerce context. In terms of product line length, e-commerce platforms face various brands that can be chosen from to list on their website. Our study extends previous studies (e.g., [60,70]) finding that product line length affects consumer purchase perception and behavior by examining the moderating role of product line length in promotion effectiveness. Our findings emphasize that promotions have a stronger effect on generating sales and the conversion rate when a product line length is longer within its brand. This result reveals the interrelationship between product line length, promotions. and consumer purchase behavior.

## 6.2. Managerial implications

The findings of this study provide important managerial implication for platforms to benefit from increased sales and conversion rates. First, e-commerce platforms should understand that the effects of promotions depend on the types of promotions. Platforms can increase the perceived benefits of promotions by offering more monetary promotions with direct or quantity discounts. In this way, consumers can perceive more benefits with direct savings compared with gift promotions that offer free auxiliary items. Platforms should also strive to enhance the effec tiveness of coupon promotions by improving the perceived convenience of coupon redemption [42]. While various types of promotions can bring benefits to the platforms and some types of promotions may be more effective to increase sales, platforms should also consider both the explicit and implicit costs of various types of promotions when designing and implementing these promotions. For example, coupon promotions incur costs from the design of the coupons and operations of the coupon mall [49], and bundle promotions may need efforts to design the algo rithms to bundle the qualified products to apply the bundle promotions [19]. Platforms thus should have an overview of their promotion strat egy to implement their optimal actions based on both the benefits and costs of various types of promotions.

Second, platforms should not use promotions only as a publicity stunt. Only attracting consumer attention and crowds is not the ultimate purpose and may lead to negative consequences such as internet congestion. That is, platforms should set the objective of their pro motions by aiming to enhance both the result and efficiency of their promotions in terms of sales and the conversion rate. In other words, platforms should make their promotions attractive from both perspec tives – encouraging consumers to visit and stimulating their purchasing behavior. In this way, both the clickstream and sales can be increased.

Third, our study highlights the need for platforms to consider the business model and product line length when designing promotion strategies. Platforms nowadays often adopt the hybrid mode by being both a reseller and a marketplace to sell their products [57]. Our find ings suggest that to amplify the promotional effects, given limited re sources, platforms should set priority to promote the products that are in the reseller modes. In addition, to enhance the promotional effect, platforms should promote products with a longer product line length to utilize the brand effect. Specifically, platforms can choose to promote certain products to utilize the potential positive spillover effect. That is, these promoted popular products can attract consumers to visit the platforms and browse the information of other products, which can bring more benefits to platforms.

## 7. Concluding remarks

Based on detailed promotion data from the largest online retailer in China, JD.com, this study builds a three-level hierarchical platform promotion structure and evaluates the impacts of online promotions on sales and the conversion rate. We find that the promotional effects differ by the types of online promotions. In addition, the platforms' business model, in terms of being a reseller or marketplace, and product line length play a moderating role in affecting the effects of online pro motions on sales and the conversion rate. The findings of this study provide guidelines for platforms to better utilize the positive effect to increase their online sales and conversation rate by implementing effective promotional strategies and choosing the optimal business model and brand to sell.

This study has several limitations. First, the investigation of pro motions at the SKU level may lose some information about individual transactions and customized promotions. For example, the promotions to loyal and new consumers may be different from regular consumers, which provides an interesting direction for future research upon data availability. Second, our analysis only estimates the impact of pro motions on sales based on the data of consumers who actually made the purchases. Upon data availability, future studies can incorporate the observations for those consumers' clicks that do not lead to an order and thus investigate how different types of promotions affect consumers decision about whether to purchase and how many to purchase. Third, when evaluating the impact of coupon promotions, due to data limit, we are not able to distinguish the sources to obtain coupons. Coupons from different channels such as the products' detailed webpages or the coupon mall may have different impacts on consumer purchase behaviors, which can be another direction for future research. Last, due to the data limit, all of the SKUs in this study are from one category during a onemonth sales period. Upon data availability, future studies can extend the scope to explore and compare the short-term and long-term pro motion effects across different categories of products.
