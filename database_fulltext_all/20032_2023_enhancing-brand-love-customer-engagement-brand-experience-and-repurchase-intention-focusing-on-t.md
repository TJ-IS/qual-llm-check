# Enhancing brand love, customer engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention: Focusing on the role of gamification in mobile apps

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Mobile technology Gamification Brand identification Self-expressiveness Brand love Customer engagement Brand experience Repurchase intention

## A B S T R A C T

Advances in and increased access to mobile technology have prompted businesses to embrace gamified appli cations, particularly to enhance customer engagement. Thus, it is important to highlight not only the antecedents and consequences of customer engagement but also the critical role of gamification. This study aims to examine the causal relationships among brand identification, self-expressiveness, brand love, gamification, customer engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention in the context of Nike Run Club, a gamified running app available at the Apple and Google Play stores. It also explores the mediating effect of consumer engagement on the association that brand love and gamification have with brand experience and repurchase intention. Addi tionally, it analyzes the moderating effects of gamification on the relationship between brand love and customer engagement. The hypotheses are empirically tested by analyzing data collected from 526 respondents on a Nike Run Club app. Results confirm that both brand identification and self-expressiveness significantly and positively influence brand love, which in turn significantly and positively affects customer engagement and, subsequently, brand experience and repurchase intention. Further, this study demonstrates that customer engagement acts as a mediator to improve brand experience and to motivate the customer to repurchase the brand. In addition to showing the impact of gamification on brand love, brand experience, and repurchase intention, this study reveals the moderating effect of gamification on the relationship between brand love and customer engagement. These findings offer deeper insights into brand identification, self-expressiveness, and gamification as significant influencing factors, which can be helpful to brand managers in enhancing brand love and customer engagement. Additionally, this study demonstrates that gamification is the most effective approach to increasing brand love, strengthening customer engagement, and improving brand experiences and repurchase intention.

## 1. Introduction

Customer engagement has obtained extensive attention in the mar keting literature, given its relationships with various key indicators of brand performance such as purchasing behavior [1,2], brand loyalty [3,4], and sustainable competitive advantages [5]. Companies such as Starbucks, BMW, and Procter & Gamble have incorporated customer engagement in their strategy programs [6]. Some studies (e.g., [7,8]) have confirmed that brand love is a vital antecedent of customer engagement. Carroll and Ahuvia [9] describe that in numerous cir cumstances, consumers' love for a brand is no different from their interpersonal love, thus making brand love a critical indicator of customer engagement. However, this raises the question of whether there are other factors complementing brand love in better predicting customer engagement [8]. This question prompted this study to explore the new trend of gamification as a possible influencing factor. Deterding [10] depicts gamification as the usage of game designs in non-gaming contexts. More specifically, gaming elements such as thinking, me chanics, and design techniques are applied to non-game contexts to improve the value of information systems and technologies [11]. Yang, Asaad, and Dwivedi [12] indicate that gamification has no space and time limitation in brands' products or services and is a new-aged plat form to distribute branding messages. In other words, gamification al lows marketers to generate value by promoting customer engagement through the use of intelligent marketing messages.

Increasing technological access and mobile technologies have encouraged companies to accept and develop gamified applications [13]. The widespread adoption of mobile technology makes it well suited for gamification. In fact, this has become a common and even essential feature in various mobile services provided by companies looking to better attract, entertain, engage and retain customers. Mobile gamification is particularly helpful in attracting consumer attention in regions with significantly high levels of mobile usage. Businesses are embracing gamification to create consumer value through effective communication. Gamification is also able to foster customer engage ment behaviors [14], not limited to purchasing, that contribute to business performance [5].

A literature review reveals the lack of effective theoretical frame works and practical exploration. Scholars have acknowledged the pos itive influences of customer engagement on behavioral intentions in the tourism [15], retail [16], and mobile apps [17] sectors. However, few discuss the principles driving its success. Moreover, while most studies highlight the benefits of customer engagement, few discuss its uncertain and unpredictable nature. Researchers' understanding of why applica tions engage some users but not some others is limited by the lack of a theoretical framework. Fredrickson's [18] broaden-and-build theory proposes that contexts satisfying individuals' basic psychological needs promote engagement. To address the aforementioned gap in the litera ture, this study draws on the broaden-and-build theory to examine the role of gamification in encouraging customer engagement with mobile apps.

Existing studies confirm that gamification is positively related to consumer engagement [12,19], brand love [20,21], brand experience [22], and continuance intention [23]. However, Koivisto and Hamari [24] indicate that most of these studies broadly examine the influence of gamification or apply it as a research context. Therefore, detailed mar keting research on gamification remains scarce. To bridge these gaps, this study aims to investigate the antecedents and consequences of customer engagement using the gamified Nike Run Club app. It builds a theoretical model to explore the influence of gamification on brand love, customer engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention to ward branded products as well as the impact of brand identification and self-expressiveness on brand love. It also analyzes consumer engagement as a mediator and gamification as a moderator.

This study makes several theoretical and practical contributions to the literature, particularly research on consumer engagement. Bitri´an et al. [19] propose that engagement is an abstract and context-specific construct. Ho and Chung [17] highlight the developing nature of research on engagement with mobile apps as well as its antecedents and consequences. Similarly, Kim and Baek [25] indicate that research on customer engagement in the mobile environment remains limited. Their studies focus on identifying the specific features of apps, such as func tionality, ease of use, privacy and security, and interactivity, that drive user engagement. However, few studies investigate the effect of gami fication. Thus, this study expands on existing research to examine gamified mobile apps and how certain game elements may enhance consumer engagement.

Numerous studies (e.g., [26]) have applied performance indicators to measure user engagement. These measures, albeit useful, do not address specific users' behaviors [27]. This study, therefore, applies Hollebeek, Glynn, and Brodie's [28] consumer brand engagement scale to the context of gamified mobile apps. The scale is a self-reporting measure, and the findings will help enhance the present understand ing of measuring customer engagement. This study adopts a holistic view of consumer engagement to offer valuable insights for commercial mobile app developers and operators.

Finally, this study offers key implications for the gamification liter ature. First, the underlying mechanisms that explain how gamification engages users in general and mobile app users, in particular, are not yet fully understood. There is limited empirical research in the field, and these studies are subject to crucial limitations. More specifically, few studies draw on sound theoretical models to explain the effects of gamification features [11]. The present theory-driven study will advance the existing knowledge about mechanisms through which gamification impacts customer engagement in the context of mobile apps and will provides valuable insights into the use of gamification.

Second, this study sheds light on how gamification facilitates individual interactions with mobile digital service offerings, thus highlighting ways for brands to encourage consumer engagement with gamification. This study also shows how businesses can apply gamification as a tool to engage customers and drive brand love, brand experience, and repurchase intention.

## 2. Literature review and hypothesis development

## 2.1. Brand identification

Since consumption is considered a social act, some studies (e.g., [29]) adopt a social identity viewpoint to explore the link between companies and consumers and that between brands and consumers. According to Ekinci, Sirakaya-Turk, and Preciado [30], individuals can convey their social identity to a social group via their identification with a brand. Social identity theory [31] defines brand identification as the degree to which consumers recognize themselves as sharing selfdefinitional attributes with a brand, and such identification develops the psychological state for a committed, meaningful, and deep rela tionship that venders are progressively pursuing to construct with con sumers [32]. Consumers' brand identification can be examined from individual and social viewpoints. In the former, brands highlight con sumers' personalities, beliefs, and values [32], and in the latter, they serve as communication channels for consumers' aspirations and selfstatus [33]. In social networks, consumers tend to socially expose their actual selves [34] and choose brands they believe have the same values. Thus, those with stronger brand identification are anticipated to be more likely to participate in pro-brand activities, such as understanding the products and goals of a business, defending a brand's reputation, and showing other types of brand loyalty [35].

## 2.2. Self-expressiveness

Park, Eisingerich, Pol, and Park [36] state that brands must offer their consumers the means to communicate and strengthen their actual or desired identities [37], which is also recognized as self-expressive benefits. Escalas and Bettman [38] suggest that self-expressive benefits prompt consumers to generate a positive and consistent self-identity. Japutra, Molinillo, and Wang [39] show that animal lovers derive selfexpressive benefits from Body Shop's products due to its Forever against Animal Testing campaign. Carroll and Ahuvia [9] describe selfexpressiveness of a brand as “the degree to which the specific brand enhances one's social self and/or reflects one's inner self.” Sarkar and Sreejesh [8] conceptualize it as the degree to which a consumer feels that a brand increases his or her perceived social identity or social self. Identity theory suggests that undertaking self-expressive physical ac tivities contributes toward individual well-being [40].

## 2.3. Brand love

Brand love remains an interesting topic in literature on the con sumer–brand relationship [41], the findings of which can help brand managers develop and strengthen brand love among consumers. Brand love is explained by Ahuvia [42] as comprising attachment, passion, positive valuation and emotions, and statements of love for a brand. Carroll and Ahuvia [9] define brand love as “the degree of passionate emotional attachment a satisfied consumer has for a particular trade name.” They further propose that brand love is a construct which is useful for predicting or elucidating post-consumption behaviors among satisfied consumers. Carroll and Ahuvia [9] draw on self-inclusion the ory to show that consumers experience brand love when a brand ach ieves an actual, desired, and high level of combination with consumers sense of self and when they begin an emotional linking with that brand. Albert and Merunka [43] describe brand love as enduring, deep, and irreplaceable, and that it generates positive perceptions about the brand.

## 2.4. Gamification

Rodrigues, Oliveira, and Costa [44] describe gamification as the application of design processes, principles, and systems to engage, motivate and affect people, whether individually or at the group or community level. Gamification has become synonymous with rewards [45], and businesses are adopting related mechanisms to increase user engagement and encourage certain behaviors [46]. Nobre and Ferreira [22] position gamification as an innovative branding tool to promote consumer interaction and participation in brand co-creation experi ences, all of which are essential to managing brand-based innovation. Xi and Hamari [47] propose that gamification can positively affect brand engagement and is an effective tool for brand management. Torres et al. [21] confirm that gamification can be an effective strategy to enhance customer–brand relationships. Kaur, Lavuri, Parida, and Singh [48] show that gamification can significantly affect social interaction and, in turn, affect customer purchase intentions. However, Koivisto and Hamari [24] indicate that research on gamification and marketing is still at a nascent stage. Thus, it is important to broaden our understanding of gamification and, more specifically, of factors that help businesses enhance consumers' brand love, engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention and encourage the effective use of resources to maximize profits.

## 2.5. Customer engagement

Engagement helps build and strengthen long-term relationships with consumers, thus significantly increasing the competitiveness of a busi ness [49]. To elaborate, engagement warrants consumer commitment toward an active connection with an explicit market offering. Patterson, Yu, and Ruyter [50] conceptualize engagement as the extent of the cognitive, emotional, and physical attachment of users to a serviceproviding organization. Vivek, Beatty, and Morgan [51] take a behav ioral viewpoint, defining customer engagement as “the intensity of an individual's participation and connection with an organization's offer ings and activities initiated by either the customer or the organization.” A firm's design normally tries to increase customer engagement in order to boost the likelihood of success [52]. The influences of customer engagement on organizational performance take both direct and indi rect forms, including brand advocacy, cost reduction, sales growth, and superior brand co-creation experiences [28]. Thus, customer engage ment can predict and explain consumer behaviors [53], including brand experience and repurchase intention [54].

## 2.6. Brand experience

Brand experience implies consumers' consumption and purchase experiences with a brand or company, and also to the influence of a brand on non-consumers [55]. Ong, Lee, and Ramayah [56] state that a service provider is an actor, and a buyer is considered a guest in the experience creation context. Consumers pursue not only tangible ben efits offered by an organization's brand but also related brand experi ences [56]. Brakus et al. [53] describe brand experience as “sensations, feelings, cognitions, and behavioral responses evoked by brand-related stimuli that are part of a brand design and identity, packaging, com munications, and environments.” Brakus et al. [53] conceptualize brand experience as comprising four dimensions: sensation, feelings, cogni tion, and behavioral responses. Jones, Comfort, Clarke-Hill, and Hillier [57] suggest that the management of consumers' brand experiences has become critical in the retail environment, given retailers' endeavor to connect with consumers and obtain a competitive advantage.

## 2.7. Hypothesis development

## 2.7.1. Brand identification and brand love

Yang et al. [12] indicate that strong interactions can improve brand identification in gamification contexts. The more a brand helps con sumers identify with that brand, the greater the consumer's affection and even passion and affection toward that brand. In other words, they can experience “positive emotions from satisfaction at the lower-intensity end to joy and pride at the higher-intensity end of positive emotions” [58]. But if a brand fails to get them to identify with it, consumers may suffer negative emotions such as embarrassment and even fear or anger. Strong identification with a brand helps create positive feelings, particularly brand love [8,59]. The literature demonstrations that such brand identification is a crucial indicator of brand love $[ 7 , 5 9 , 6 0 ]$ . Hence, the following hypothesis is put forward:

## H1. Brand identification positively influences brand love.

## 2.7.2. Self-expressiveness and brand love

Albert, Merunka, and Valette-Florence [61] put forward brand love is developed through consumers' “self-image motives and self-esteem motives.” Carroll and Ahuvia [9] confirm that brands which are selfexpressive significantly influence brand love; in other words, con sumers tend to love brands when they see them as more self-expressive. Hwang and Kandampully [62] show that connection of a brand with one's self-concept boosts brand love. This means consumers' love toward a brand increases when they identify the brand as expressing significant aspects of their selves. Thus, the study presents the following hypothesis:

## H2. Self-expressiveness positively influences brand love.

## 2.7.3. Brand love and customer engagement

Round and Roper [63] describe brand love as “the main function of branding and of growing importance as a provider of stability and authenticity in a rapidly changing world.” Batra, Ahuvia, and Bagozzi [64] state that consumers are passionate about brands that they love, and are strongly inclined to maintaining their affinity. Consumers with love toward a particular brand show more willingness to engage with it [8]. A beloved brand increases extrinsic rewards and also offers intrinsic rewards [64]. Bergkvist and Bech-Larsen [7] find that brand love is a significant antecedent of engagement. Thus, the following hypothesis is put forward:

## H3. Brand love positively influences customer engagement.

## 2.7.4. Customer engagement and brand experience

Roy, Eshghi, and Sarkar [65] propose that consumer communication can occur through both uncontrolled (word of mouth) and controlled (firm sponsored) forms. Consumers can help improve brand experiences by sharing information with others on, for example, online engagement platforms [66]. Customer engagement can facilitate satisfying brand shopping experiences [67]. Numerous studies (e.g., [53,68]) examine the relationship between customer engagement and brand experience. Islam et al. [54], in particular, reveal that customer engagement significantly impacts brand experiences. Consistent with Islam et al. [54], the following hypothesis is developed:

H4. Customer engagement positively influences brand experience.

## 2.7.5. Customer engagement and repurchase intention

The marketing literature highlights customer engagement as a pre dictor of consumer purchase [69]. Jang et al.'s [2] study on an exercise app shows that customer engagement influences subsequent product purchases. Eisingerich et al. [1] find that customer engagement is positively associated with actual purchases. Cheung, Pires, Rosenberger, Leung, and Sharipudin [70] confirm that customer engagement posi tively impacts repurchase intention. Thus, the following hypothesis is put forth:

## H5. Customer engagement positively influences repurchase intention.

## 2.7.6. Mediating effect of customer engagement

Companies must realize the nature of consumer–brand relationships reflected in constructs such as brand love to establish relationships with their customers [71]. Albert et al. [61] propose that consumers' brand love is influenced by one's self-image. Aaker [72] explains that con sumers are more likely to create stronger emotional bonds with brands whose image is congruent with their concept of self. In other words, brand image is a type of brand personification and, thus, could reflect one's self-image [73]. Brands that are perceived to enhance one's selfimage [74] are likely to report higher consumer engagement. Studies have delineated the positive relationship between brand love and word of mouth, a form of consumer engagement [9]. Bergkvist and Bech-Larsen [7] state that brand love is an essential driver of engagement. Customer engagement can facilitate a satisfying brand shopping expe rience [67] and positively impact repurchase intentions [70]. Thus, this study empirically tests the mediation effects as follows:

H6. Customer engagement fully mediates the effect of brand love on brand experience.

H7. Customer engagement fully mediates the effect of brand love on repurchase intention.

In addition, previous studies have confirmed that gamification add value by promoting and maintaining engagement intention $[ 1 , 1 2 , 7 5 ]$ Customer engagement can also facilitate a pleasant brand shopping experience [67] and positively influence repurchase intention [70]. Hence, this study tests empirically the mediation effects, as follows:

H8. Customer engagement fully mediates the effect of gamification on brand experience.

H9. Customer engagement fully mediates the effect of gamification on repurchase intention.

## 2.7.7. Gamification and brand love

Nobre and Ferreira [22] show that gamification benefits many brand lovers and can be used to obtain deeper insight into consumer behavior. Hwang and Choi [76] suggest that gamification encourages positive evaluations of an object. Hofacker et al. [13] mention that gamified activities involving point systems and special promotions are perceived as a new and interesting way to influence consumer preferences. Hsu and Chen [20] state that gamification adds value and increases satis faction, thus increasing brand love. Thus, the following hypothesis is put forth:

## H10. Gamification positively influences brand love.

## 2.7.8. Gamification and customer engagement

Gamification is a marketing strategy employed to boost customer engagement [1] and enhance marketing success [13]. Eisingerich et al. [1] assert that gamification principles can cause customer engagement. Gamification can be thought of as added value that promotes and maintains participation [75]. For instance, Yang et al. [12] show that gamification affects engagement intention. Therefore, the following hypothesis is put forth:

## H11. Gamification positively influences customer engagement.

## 2.7.9. Moderating effect of gamification

Koivisto and Hamari [24] indicate that the gamification mechanics applied to a system or service facilitate psychological experiences such as enjoyment and engagement. These gamified experiences further promote activities and behaviors supported and motivated by gamifi cation. Yang et al.'s [12] marketing-based research suggests that gami fication affects consumers' engagement intentions and attitudes. Eisingerich et al. [1] describe ways in which service providers have applied gamification to increase customer engagement. Torres et al. [21] find that gamification experiences evoke positive emotions that could increase satisfaction and contribute to building brand love. The current study thus sets forth the following hypothesis:

H12. Gamification has a moderating effect on the relationship be tween brand love and customer engagement.

## 2.7.10. Gamification and brand experience

Mobile gamification needs to take into account consumers' restricted cognitive resources and offer game-like experiences with low entry difficulty [13]. Gamification is likely to influence customer experiences, and thus marketers are critical in the decision-making process [13]. Nobre and Ferreira [22] consider gamification a new branding tool that encourages consumer interactions and sharing in brand experiences. Hsu and Chen [20] confirm that gamification enhances consumers' sense of enjoyment. Thus, this study puts forward the following hypothesis:

## H13. Gamification positively influences brand experiences.

## 2.7.11. Gamification and repurchase intention

Bitrian ´ et al. [19] suggest that gamification encourages customers to frequently revisit an app. Hofacker et al. [13] show that a gamified mobile platform can affect a crucial set of retailing outcomes such as consumer entertainment, accelerated repurchase, consumer retainment, and in-store engagement. Zhang, Shao, Benitez, and Zhang [77] confirm that gamification has a significant positive effect on behavioral in tentions. Thus, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H14: Gamification positively influences repurchase intention.

## 3. Methodology

## 3.1. Sample and data collection

This study administered a questionnaire survey to users of the Nike Run Club in Taiwan. Four post-graduate students were trained as in vestigators to comprehend the survey content and respond to any questions participants might have. The respondents were requested to read an informed agree form prior to taking the survey. The survey contained two filtering questions to exclude data for respondents who had not purchased a Nike product or used the Nike Run Club. Re spondents who answered “yes” to “Have you ever purchased a Nike product?” were asked a second question, “Have you used the Nike Run Club app in the past six months?” To increase the number of high-quality responses, this study rewarded respondents who answered all the survey questions. Next, all duplicate responses were removed and useable re sponses were retained for analysis. There were 526 usable ones. The sample characteristics are displayed in Table 1. ## 4. Analysis and results

## 4.1. Common method bias

This study conducted two tests to minimize any effect of common method bias (CMB). The first test followed Podsakoff, MacKenzie, Lee, and Podsakoff [82]. In line with Iacobucci and Churchill [83], items were revised and pretested to avoid language problems. Respondents were requested to respond honestly about survey-related difficulties [84]. Further, Harman's one-factor test was run to identify the potential for CMB in the data [82]. All observed variables were able to load with a single factor in the unrotated exploratory factor analysis. The results confirmed that the single factor does not elucidate a majority of variance (32.58%). The second test was a confirmatory factor analysis consistent with that of Chang and Zhu [85]. All items were modeled as indicators of a single factor, and the results revealed poor fitness. Method biases were assumed to be substantial if the hypothesized model fits the data [86]. Thus, the results of both tests showed that CMB was not a significant problem in the current study.

A confirma tory factor analysis (CFA) was made to test the scales' validity. As Table 2 shows, each construct's Cronbach's alpha is >0.7, achieving the recommended value [87]. ## 5. Discussion and contributions

## 5.1. Discussion of results

This study's results indicated that (i) brand identification and selfexpressiveness increase brand love, (ii) customer engagement with mobile apps is facilitated by brand love and gamification, and (iii) brand experience and repurchase intention are influenced by customer engagement with mobile apps and gamification. In addition, the results confirmed the mediating role of customer engagement and the moder ating role of gamification. These results are compared with those of previous research.

In line with the extant literature, this study confirmed the impact of brand identification on brand love [9], the effect of self-expressiveness on brand love [7,59,60], and the influence of gamification on brand love [20]. Brand identification, self-expressiveness, and gamification have a significantly positive impact on brand love, thus supporting H1, H2, and H10 and prior research. These findings make critical contri butions to gamification research, which has only partially investigated the dimensions of brand identification, self-expressiveness, and brand love [9]. Thus, it can be argued that brand identification and selfexpressiveness capture more love toward gamification in mobile apps. Brand love, on the other hand, encourages love toward the gamified mobile app itself, although it enhances brand identification and self expressiveness.

This study also showed that brand love (H3) and gamification (H11) directly influences customer engagement, and both brand love and gamification have an indirect impact on brand experience (H4 and H13) and repurchase intention (H5 and H14) via customer engagement. This study goes beyond existing research, which supports the relationship between the positively affective nature of brand love and gamification within gamified experiences [20], to demonstrate that brand love and gamification help create brand experiences and encourage repurchase intentions. In addition, studies have highlighted the mediating role of customer engagement (H6, H7, H8, and H9) in various settings [16,92], and this study confirmed this mediating role in the context of gamification.

Customer engagement facilitates brand experience and repurchase intention. The customer engagement literature has primarily focused on social media [4] and online brand communities [47]. This study expanded on the literature by demonstrating the importance and sig nificance of customer engagement with gamified systems used for brand marketing. The findings revealed that increasing customer engagement is key to the use of gamification for brand marketing because it facilitates the transfer of brand love and gamification experiences to brand marketing outcomes outside of the system, which this study deems as brand experience and repurchase intention.

In addition, this study found that gamification has a significant moderating impact between brand love and customer engagement (H12). Thus, gamification strengthens the positive effect of brand love on customer engagement. The finding validates gamification theory as a driver in the context of mobile apps, thus contributing to both the in dustry and academia focused on designing mobile apps.

There is a severe lack of quantitative evidence for the effectiveness of gamification as a brand marketing approach [47]. While customer engagement in gamification affects related outcomes in commercial settings [1], it remains vital to examine if customer engagement con tributes to brand marketing outcomes. The present findings confirmed that customer engagement enhanced via brand love and gamification improves brand experience and repurchase intention. The study, thus, addresses the issues and gaps in prior systematic reviews of gamification and brand marketing [47].

## 5.2. Theoretical contributions

This study makes three crucial contributions to the literature. First, it enriches the mobile apps literature by examining the impact of emerging gamification technology on customers' brand love, engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention. Earlier studies mainly investi gated utilitarian technologies broadly designed to improve the effi ciency of a given action [93,94]. However, the utility-driven lens of prior mobile apps literature (e.g., [94]) is not geared toward capturing

(continued on next page)

the recent use of entertainment-oriented technology (i.e., gamification) in the mobile apps context [19]. This study addresses the knowledge gap by exploring the influences of context-specific gamification benefits on customers' brand love, engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention from a gamification perspective and a broaden-and-build theoretical lens.

Second, this study advances our understanding of customers' brand love and gamification by revealing the mediation mechanism of customer engagement. Despite previous mobile apps literature having addressed different psychological outcomes, most studies focus on perceived value, perceived trust [95], perceived ease of use, and perceived enjoyment [96]. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the first studies that examine customer engagement from a psychologi cal perspective and investigate its mediating role in the association that brand love has with brand experience and repurchase intention, as well as that of gamification with brand experience and repurchase intention. The research findings can extend the engagement literature from a psychological perspective and investigate insights on the mediation mechanism between brand love or gamification and brand experience and repurchase intention in the mobile apps context.

Third, this study expands on the broaden-and-build theory by introducing the conceptualization of gamification mechanics in the context of mobile apps in the theoretical model. This study applies a sports brand app (Nike Run Club) as a significant gamified situational mechanism and reveals its moderating effect on the relationship be tween brand love and customer engagement. Yang et al. [12] show that gamification not only increases customers' brand love, engagement, and behavioral intentions but also is integral to a brand's success. The results offer insights for further research to explain gamified situational affor dances in the formation of post-adoption behaviors toward mobile apps from a gamification mechanics lens.

## 5.3. Practical contributions

This study also offers key practical implications. It provides brand managers with guidelines to use gamification mechanisms to increase customers' brand love, engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention in the mobile apps context. The findings suggest there are key components of the gamification mechanism that brand managers must integrate into a game design, including task and completion, progress tracking, and achievements. Thus, brand managers must know that game content and not just game mechanics contributes to successful customer experience environments and, thus, customer engagement and repurchase intention. The challenge for brand managers is to use existing brand content to develop gamification mechanics that seam lessly incorporate key functionalities to create a gamified environment. Simply transposing badges, points systems, and leaderboards may lead to superficial emotions and engagement behaviors, undermining desir able brand-related outcomes, as evidenced in this study. Successful ap plications require a thorough consideration of ways in which gamification contributes to outcomes such as brand love, engagement, brand experience, and repurchase intention.

Second, brand managers should be aware that using gamification as a technique first engages customers with brands and further contributes to higher performance outcomes such as brand experience and repurchase intention. In other words, a gamified mobile app may first attract customers by enhancing the depth of engagement, but it may not ensure that users demonstrate customer retention unless they believe that the gamified apps meaningfully engage them. From a gamification design perspective, this study suggests that brand managers should apply gamified strategies to create a meaningful brand experience and increase repurchase intention by facilitating cognitive, emotional, and behavioral engagement.

Finally, brand managers must be aware that brand love can be increased by brand identification. The effect of brand identification on brand love should convince brand managers of the importance of creating familiar proximity between their brands and consumers by stressing a brand's personality or values in company communications. The current findings suggest that self-expressive brands are likely to be more loved, and boosting these facets of their offerings will further strengthen this emotional reaction in consumers. Brands with selfexpressive benefits will have greater potential to enhance consumers love and encourage engagement behaviors. When customers are more strongly engaged with a brand, they will have a stronger brand experi ence, thus enhancing repurchase intentions.

## 5.4. Limitations and future work

There are a few inherent limitations to this study, suggesting areas for future investigation. First, it collected data from only one Nike Run Club in a single country with customers' self-reported data, which limits generalizability to other countries. Examining the same hypothesized relationships in other countries would broaden the evidence on the roles of customer engagement and gamification in brand experience as well as repurchase intention. Second, this research model's hypothesized re lationships among the constructs were only looked at in the sports in dustry. Future studies could thus investigate these relationships in other industries to increase the external validity of the model and confirm whether these relationships are valid in other industries. Third, the snapshot research nature of the present research limits the ability to infer causal relationships, so a longitudinal dataset may be applied to validate these in the future. Finally, only the moderating role of gami fication was tested and not other factors. Previous studies suggest other possible variables, such as personality traits [97] and price [98], and thus future research might use these important factors as the modera tors. Despite these limitations, the current study suggests significant theoretical as well as practical insights which contribute to consumer behavior research.
