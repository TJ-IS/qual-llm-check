# How do organizations leverage social media to enhance marketing performance? Unveiling the power of social CRM capability and guanxi

Keywords: Social media use Social CRM capability Guanxi Marketing performance

## A B S T R A C T

Drawing on information technology-enabled organizational capabilities theory, we argue that how and when social media use affects marketing performance depending on the mediating role of social customer relationship management (CRM) capability and the moderating role of guanxi. Using a sample of 194 Chinese agricultural firms, we employed a combination of complementary techniques: advanced analysis for composites–structural equation modeling (ADANCO-SEM) and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA). In ADANCO-SEM analysis, we empirically verify the full mediating role of social CRM capability in the association between so cial media use and marketing performance and identify the condition for this indirect effect (i.e., when the guanxi levels are high). Moreover, guanxi plays a dual moderating role—it exerts a substitution effect when social media is used to build social CRM capability, and it exerts a complementary effect when social CRM capability is leveraged to improve marketing performance. FsQCA analysis further reveals the different config urations of social media use, guanxi, and social CRM capability that can lead to high marketing performance in different firm sizes. The findings complement those from ADANCO-SEM analysis vis-à-vis the conditions for the formation of marketing performance. This study contributes to the emerging literature on the business value of social media by theoretically exploring and empirically validating the mechanisms and boundary conditions by which social media affects marketing performance.

## 1. Introduction

It is of the utmost importance to maintain functioning of agricultural firms as they provide essential agri-foods for maintaining the population in China. Social media plays a crucial role in enabling agricultural firms to achieve digital marketing success [1]. It can reshape marketing pro cesses of across all customer life cycle stages, thus enhancing marketing performance [2] - a critical metric for evaluating the effectiveness and outcomes of marketing activities. Specifically, in customer acquisition, social media provides agricultural firms with a cost-effective means of promoting their brands, allowing them to engage a broader consumer base. In customer conversion, social media can promote customer pur chase and communication efficiency in marketing by facilitating twoway interactions [3]. In customer retention, social media enables agricultural firms to actively respond to customer feedback, enhancing customer loyalty through comprehensive after-sales services [4]. How ever, despite the increasing use of social media for digital marketing, agricultural firms encounter chronic challenges related to limited digital literacy, content creation difficulties, and low traffic acquisition and conversion rates [5]. The level of digitalization in China’s agricultural firms was only 25.4% in 2021, which was significantly lower than that of the US and developed countries in Europe [6]. This indicates that agri cultural firms have yet to fully harness the potential of social media and realize its value, highlighting the need for guidelines on how social media can be effectively deployed to improve marketing performance.

Although prior studies have demonstrated the potential associations between firms’ social media use and performance, the findings are contradictory—both positive and negative [7,8]. The possible reason is that the mechanisms and boundary conditions of firms’ social media use that affect performance remain ambiguous. Technology itself is a necessary yet insufficient condition for firms to succeed in social media marketing strategy [2]. It has been found that some firms achieve greater success from social media use compared to others, attributable to superior social customer relationship management (CRM) capability [9]. It reflects the competence of an organization in enhancing customer relationships by integrating social media technology with customer facing activities, including processes, systems, and technologies [10]. Social CRM capability allows firms to strategically leverage social media technology to increase the effectiveness of their marketing activities [2]. Information technology (IT)-enabled organizational capabilities theory proposes that IT can create business value by developing organizational capabilities [11]. In this sense, social media serves as an enabling technology to enhance social CRM capability thereby improving mar keting performance. Given the growth in social media use and the mixed outcomes generated, exploring the mediating mechanism of social CRM capability is essential to opening the black box in this link and help agribusinesses realize business value from social media.

Moreover, previous studies investigating contingency factors in the impacts of social media use on firm performance have focused starkly on customers and industry characteristics [3,12], neglecting the moder ating role of guanxi. However, beyond the technology itself, firms require diverse resources to successfully realize the value of social media. As a social resource, guanxi can complement technical resources in value creation [13]. Guanxi is a close and universal interpersonal relationship built on high-quality social interactions and mutual benefits [14]. A high level of guanxi can optimize social media-enabled CRM processes by providing firms with access to unique marketing resources (e.g., knowledge, information, and policy support). This value is further enhanced in agribusiness, where barriers like limited finances and lack of technical and market information are common. However, the closed nature of guanxi also conflicts with the openness of social media and CRM [15], potentially interfering with social media use to improve the standardization and transparency of customer relationships. Given guanxi’s significant and complex role in reshaping social media’s value creation process, exploring its moderating effects enables understanding the boundary conditions affecting the process. This helps firms ratio nally deploy both technological (social media) and social (guanxi) re sources to build social CRM capability and enhance marketing performance.

In addition to understanding the correlation between social media use and marketing performance, it is critical to consider the complexity and dynamics of synergies between relevant resources and capabilities in realizing this correlation. Specifically, the availability and profit ability of resources varies across firms of different sizes [16]. This im plies there are potentially multiple pathways leading to high marketing performance. It’s imperative to reveal the multiple configurations for improving marketing performance, and elucidate how firms can opti mize outcomes with minimal inputs based on their resource endow ments. Therefore, we pose three research questions:

RQ1: How does CRM capability mediate the relationship between social media use and marketing performance?

RQ2: How does guanxi moderate the pathway from social media use to marketing performance?

RQ3: How does the combination of organizational resources and capa bility vary based on firm size to achieve high marketing performance?

To address these interrelated research questions, drawing on the IT enabled organizational capabilities theory, we develop a research model to examine the mediating role of CRM capability and the moderating role of guanxi in the association between social media use and marketing performance. This study contributes significantly to the literature in three ways. First, we contribute to the literature on the business value of social media by uncovering the full mediating role of social CRM capability in the influence of social media use on marketing performance and by identifying the boundary condition for this indirect effect, which depends on the magnitude of guanxi. Second, this study sheds new light on social media research by unraveling the dual moderating roles of guanxi in the process of leveraging social media to realize business value. Third, this research combines complementary techniques to provide in-depth insights into how social media use enhances marketing performance, thus extending IS research by advancing methodological diversity across research paradigms.

## 2. Theoretical foundations

## 2.1. IT-enabled organizational capabilities theory

IT-enabled organizational capabilities theory is a vital bridge theory that elucidates how IT can create business value by developing organi zational capabilities [17,18]. Organizational capability is the ability of firms to continuously learn and innovate by using IT resources and knowledge to achieve IT business value [19]. These organizational ca pabilities include platform digitization capability [20], social media capability [19], and business agility [18], to name a few, which are critical for firms to obtain competitive advantages from IT.

In this study, IT-enabled organizational capabilities theory provides a mature framework for delineating how social media use affects mar keting performance through social CRM capability. First, existing research argues that social media use is a specific IT capability involving the utilization of distinct IT resources (i.e., social media) in organiza tional processes in pursuit of competitive advantage [3]. Second, extant studies have widely proven that social CRM capability is a critical organizational capability for establishing and maintaining the positive relationships between firms and customers in a social network envi ronment [8], which is critical for marketing success [9]. Third, mar keting performance represents the performance of a firm in terms of sales growth, market share, and market and product development and is an essential source of competitive advantage [21,22]. Specifically, social media use as a unique IT capability can promote two-way conversations with customers and establish and maintain close relationships, thereby enhancing firms’ ability to capture the evolution of customer needs and improve customer loyalty, and growth [16]. In this sense, social media use can optimize the customer management process, which is vital for enhancing marketing performance. Therefore, this study builds on ITenabled organizational capabilities theory to articulate how social media use can strengthen marketing performance by developing social CRM capability.

## 2.2. Social media use

Social media use refers to the extent and intensity to which firm deploy social media technologies to manage organizational processes [10]. It provides firms with new opportunities to engage with their target audiences, enhance their brand visibility, and foster customer loyalty [12,23]. Social media has significantly expanded the channels of communication between firms and consumers, enriching marketing strategies and serving as a vital conduit for the transformation from traditional to digital marketing [2,24]. The marketing literature focuses on two types of social media use, owned and earned social media use [25]. The former refers to communications created and shared by a firm through its own online social networking assets, such as Weibo brand pages and WeChat business accounts. The latter refers to entities outside a firm, typically consumers, creating and distributing business-related content through social media. The current marketing literature starkly focuses on earned social media to reveal how consumer social media use (e.g., social sharing, commenting, liking, and following brands) affects purchases, market opportunity identification, and corporate profits [26–30]. Despite growing research adopting an owned social media perspective to explore the potential association between firm social media use and performance, findings remain mixed [7,8]. Moreover, literature on contingent factors in the association focuses on technology resources, traditional marketing, and customer traits [8,12,31], neglecting social resources. The mechanisms and boundary conditions underlying firms’ social media use that affect performance remain ambiguous. This study therefore seeks to bridge the research gap and provide a more comprehensive understanding of this field.

## 2.3. Social CRM capability

Social CRM capability reflects the competence of an organization in enhancing customer relationships by integrating social media technol ogy and customer-facing activities, including processes, systems, and technologies [10,32]. Its essence is in enhancing the value of customer relationships through social interactions and in leveraging social data, which requires firms to possess substantial resources and capabilitie across various dimensions, including social media use, customer in sights, content creation, and interaction management [2,9]. By advancing their social CRM capability, firms can realize clustered customer management, leverage data insights to identify and attract potential customers, and effectively foster customer loyalty and mar keting growth. Existing studies have focused predominantly on social CRM-related technologies [8,33], rather than exploring the role of social CRM capability. Second, while studies have examined the moderating role of organizational characteristics and technology resources on the effects of CRM technology/capabilities on performance [8,10,16,33], they have neglected social resources. The understanding of its role in both phases remains ambiguous: social CRM capability formation and yielding outcomes. Furthermore, the majority of studies have been conducted in Western and Indian contexts, with limited investigations in the Chinese context [34]. This limitation hinders the generalizability of research findings and discovering its relevant boundary conditions. Therefore, it is critical to analyze how social media use influences social CRM capability building and subsequent marketing performance effects, and how the unique social resource of guanxi in China reshapes thi process. ## 2.4. Guanxi

Guanxi refers to close, exclusive interpersonal relationships built on high-quality interactions and mutual benefits [13,14]. Guanxi originates from a collectivist society, represents a ubiquitous social resource in China. Unlike Western social networks, guanxi networks are closed and exclusive, usually formed between people with common background and high barriers to entry to prevent competitors [15]. Western social networks are more open, with members entering and exiting voluntarily based on conscious social accounting [36]. Compared with the trust and commitment relationship driven by the legitimacy logic in Western cultures, guanxi is more driven by morality and social norms. Guanx helps firms exchange benefits, overcoming institutional barriers and instability from regulatory changes [13]. Given its pervasive influence, guanxi dominants business activities in China [15].

Previous research has explored the role of guanxi in granting firms access to unique resources such as knowledge, information, and policy support, achieved through the facilitation of cooperation and trust among organizations [37]. Guanxi was found to be instrumental in building organizational capabilities, encompassing channel and responsive capability [15], innovation capability [13], and e-commerce capability [14]. However, certain studies have shed light on the dark side of guanxi, notably its high maintenance cost arising from reciprocal obligations and collective blindness [15]. Guanxi may limit a firm’s openness to scan the external environment, leading to collective blind ness, and thereby diminishing incentives for continuous learning and innovation [38].

Existing literature on social media marketing has focused primarily on exploring how guanxi, at the individual level (between sellers and buyers), can enhance consumer trust and purchasing behavior, rather than considering guanxi as a firm-level resource [39–41]. Furthermore, these studies highlight the positive effects while neglecting the potential negative effects of guanxi in social media marketing, despite its potential dual roles. On one hand, as guanxi affects the acquisition of resources, it should supplement firms’ efforts to leverage their social CRM capability for improving performance (i.e., a positive moderating effect). On the other hand, guanxi may exert a substituting effect on social media for building social CRM capability (i.e., a negative moderating effect), given their similar utility on establishing the capability. In addition to exam ining the supplementary role of guanxi, exploring its potential substitute effect on social media use is needed to help firms deploy guanxi rationally.

## 2.5. Marketing performance

Marketing performance is a critical metric for evaluating the effec tiveness and outcomes of marketing activities [21]. It enables firms to monitor marketing operations and improve decision making, which are highly significant to the development and competitiveness of firms [42]. The current literature on the impact of corporate social media use on performance primarily focuses on performance reflecting customer mindset, including brand awareness, purchase intent, customer satis faction and loyalty [12,25,32], or centers on accounting performance with a single metric of sales revenue [8,31]. Relying solely on a sales metric can hinder effective linkage of marketing activities to firm-level accounting performance due to this process involving multiple links and potential introduction of “noise” [21]. Thus, assessing diverse as pects of marketing performance allows for the amalgamation of strengths and complementing weaknesses inherent in each metric. In this study, we evaluate marketing performance using composite metrics that capture distinct facets of marketing outcomes: customer-level per formance (acquisition and retention), product market performance (market share), and accounting performance (sales growth). Prior research widely take this method to assess the outcomes of marketing actions and related capabilities [22,43]. Hence, it is applicable to assess the marketing performance contributed by social media use and social CRM capability in this study.

## 3. Research model and hypothesis development

Based on IT-enabled organizational capabilities theory, we develop the following conceptual model and hypotheses (see Fig. 1). We argue that social media use can enhance marketing performance by building social CRM capability. Guanxi may weaken the impact of social media use on social CRM capability, but it may enhance the effect of social CRM capability on marketing performance. We consider three control variables: firm size, ownership, and social media experience [3].

## 3.1. The mediating role of social CRM capability

From an IT-enabled organizational capabilities perspective, social media use can enhance firms’ marketing performance by enabling firms to develop social CRM capability. First, social media provides abundant information from which consumer insights can be obtained and through which changing demands can be met through precision marketing. This process broadens the communication channels of firms, enabling deep consumer interactions and facilitating customer relationship building and development [8]. Second, social media allows and motivates cus tomers to become active participants in customer relationships [44]. Through multilevel interactions, firms can improve consumers engagement and loyalty, cultivating brand dependence and emotional identity. Furthermore, promptly responding to consumer feedback via social media enables firms to provide superior customer solutions and extend customer relationship life cycles through collaboration [32]. Therefore, social media use can build social CRM capability by reshap ing firms’ CRM philosophies and processes.

We also believe that social CRM capability enabled by social media can enhance marketing performance. Specifically, social CRM capability enables firms to understand consumer needs by closely social interacting with consumers [16]. This provides a foundation for improving products and marketing positioning. Based on social CRM capability, firms can optimize their marketing strategies, attracting consumers’ active social sharing and continuously expanding brand influence [9]. It also enables firms to effectively utilize social media to cope with customer relation ship crises, avoiding negative word-of-mouth and customer asset losses, thus laying the foundation for long-term firm development [45]. Social media use can increase the effectiveness of firm marketing activities through social CRM capability, thus improving marketing performance. We therefore hypothesize that:

H1. Social CRM capability mediates the relation between social media use and marketing performance.

## 3.2. The moderating role of guanxi

Guanxi may negatively moderate the influence of social media use on social CRM capability. First, social CRM pursues transparent, open, and universal customer relationships [36]. Firms with high guanxi levels may prioritize individual sentiments and mutual benefits in customer service and resource allocation rather than establish a systematic and standardized CRM process through social media to cultivate social CRM capability [46]. On the contrary, firms with low levels of guanxi tend to rely on systematic processes to manage customer relationships, which can be advantageous for leveraging social media to improve work effi ciency and customer service quality. Second, both guanxi and social media use contribute to the development of social CRM capability by cultivating customer connections and improving customer experiences [47]. Therefore, the simultaneous pursuit of high-level guanxi by firms may increase their marketing costs when using social media to build social CRM capability. Basing on these considerations, we hypothesize the following:

H2. Guanxi negatively moderates the relation between social media use and social CRM capability.

Guanxi can positively moderate the relationship between social CRM capability and marketing performance. First, a high level of guanxi can compensate for the limitations of social CRM capability in terms of relationship depth and stability. By providing emotional or material support, firms can easily cultivate and maintain customers’ emotional identification and loyalty [36]. This, in turn, significantly improves customer retention, brand impact, and overall marketing performance. Second, the mutual benefits of strong guanxi become more apparent, bringing cooperation opportunities and scarce marketing resources to firms [14]. For example, firms may receive government support in funding, taxation, or land use as a result of guanxi, amplifying the ad vantages of social CRM capability in enhancing marketing performance. We then hypothesize the following:

H3. Guanxi positively moderates the relation between social CRM capability and marketing performance.

## 4.2. Data collection

We conducted a survey in China to test the research model. The reasons for choosing Chinese agribusinesses as the research subjects are as follows. First, As a world’s major agricultural producer and agricul tural trading nation, China feed nearly 22% of the global population, with over \$334 billion in agricultural imports and exports in 2022. Chinese agribusinesses play a critical role in protecting lives and health domestically. Second, Chinese agribusinesses have been undergoing drastic marketing environment changes recently. Climate change and regional conflicts have exacerbated challenges for agribusinesses regarding marketing [1]. Due to the dependence on nature and discontinuity of output, traditional distribution channels that dominate agricultural product sales tend to falter during turbulence [18]. The perishability and vulnerability of agricultural products exacerbate the subjectivity of customers’ perceived quality [3], further hindering on line marketing and after-sales services. Though numerous Chinese ag ribusinesses struggle by clinging to antiquated business models, some actively leverage social media to expand marketing channels. To address current challenges, Chinese agribusinesses seek enhanced marketing performance through varying levels of social media adoption. This context offers an apt setting to evaluate our research model. Third, Chinese agribusinesses exhibit variability in resources and capabilities, especially in guanxi with key stakeholders such as distributors and of ficials [14], providing variability for investigating guanxi’s potential moderating role. Last, choosing a single industry averts cross-industry confounding effects and aggregation bias.

We obtained a sample pool of leading Chinese agribusinesses with the support of China’s Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs. We randomly selected 1000 agribusinesses and contacted them to identify a middle or senior manager as the key informant who thoroughly un derstands the process and performance of the social media marketing. Our government provided a letter of recommendation to encourage these companies to voluntarily participate in this investigation. The letter conveys the importance of the survey and the recognition and support for their participation, which stimulated their interest and motivation, and help promote the level of involvement. Besides, we conducted a telephone visit during the survey period to encourage re spondents to complete the survey. A total of 478 agribusinesses accepted our invitation. We sent emails with the survey webpage link to the ag ribusinesses that accepted our invitation (a total of 478). After two follow-up phone calls, 253 questionnaires were returned. Finally, we obtained 194 valid questionnaires. To assess nonresponse bias, we per formed t-tests comparing the means of early and late respondents for each firm characteristic. The t-test results reveal no significant differ ences between the two groups, including ownership (t = − 0.773), firm size (t = 0.720), and social media experience (t = 1.135). The results indicate the potential exclusion of nonresponse bias.

Table 3 reports the characteristics of the sample. Privately owned firms constituted most of the sample firms (56.2%), and most companies had fewer than 1000 employees (84.0%). Therefore, the sample was composed primarily of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Regarding length of social media use, 71.1% of the responding firms reported over four years.

We made several measures to limit the common method bias. First, we kept the respondents’ identities to be anonymous, and they were informed that there were no correct or incorrect answers. Second, the questionnaire items were arranged randomly to avoid respondents to identify the dependent and independent variables. Third, this study performed the marker variable technique to test for common method variance (CMV), as described by Malhotra et al. [48]. The mean value of correlations between the marker variable (number of hours watching TV per week) and the other key variables was only − 0.057, <0.1. The dif ferences in path coefficients between the original and CMV-adjusted models were minor (i.e., Δr = 0.001), indicating that CMV was not a significant concern in this study.

## 5.1. ADANCO–SEM analysis

We used partial least squares structural equation modeling method to test our model because it is suitable for estimating a composite research model [49,50]. Previous studies have identified two types of constructs: latent variables and artifacts [49]. Latent variables assume a causal relationship between indicators and constructs. They cannot be directly observed, but observable indicators can measure them [51], such as personal attitudes and purchase intentions. Artifacts are artifi cially created, theoretically justified products that serve a goal [18], such as IT capability and organizational ambidexterity. Because of the constructivist nature of constructs, recent literature suggests oper ationalizing artifacts through composite constructs [52]. In a composite model, indicators do not lead to constructs but combine to form them. In the proposed model, social media use, guanxi, social CRM capability, and marketing performance, as theoretically justified constructions, were considered composite constructs. These constructs are artifacts not existing in nature but created by executives and companies [49]. Thus, the composite construct suits this study. ## 5.1.4. Endogeneity test

We used the IV method to identify the causal relationship between social media use and social CRM capability. Firms with strong social CRM capability tend to use social media because they usually have more resources and are more customer oriented. This reverse causality may lead to endogeneity concerns. Therefore, the IV method was used to address endogeneity issues arising from reverse causality. We selected mimetic pressure on firms to use social media as an IV because firms facing intense imitative pressures are more likely to use social media. At the same time, mimetic pressure from their industry is unlikely to lead directly to the development of high or low firm capability; it needs to work by influencing the resource utilization processes of a firm. We measured mimetic pressure using a scale adapted from Lin et al. [3]. The regression results also indicate that the relation between mimetic pres sure and social CRM capability was insignificant $( \beta = 0 . 0 4 1 , p = 0 . 8 2 ) .$ Thus, mimetic pressure is a reasonable instrument that satisfies the correlation and independence assumptions. We used Stata 16 to run the IV regression analysis. The results indicate that firms facing intense mimetic pressure had greater extent of social media use $( \beta = 0 . 3 1 2 , p <$ 0.001). ## 5.2. Exploring the role of the firm size: fsQCA analysis

Exploratory analysis is based on fsQCA aimed at addressing RQ 3: How the combination of organizational resources and capability vary based on firm size to achieve high marketing performance? FsQCA is a configuration analysis method based on set theory and fuzzy algebra [54]. It allows researchers to examine the interactions between elements characterized by messy and nonlinear properties [55], identifying combinations of conditions that lead to specific outcomes. Thus, fsQCA helps to explore multiple pathways to achieving high marketing per formance. FsQCA analysis examines how the configurations of organi zational resources and capabilities for achieving high marketing performance vary based on firm size. Fig. 4 depicts the relationship between ADANCO-SEM and fsQCA analysis.

## 5.2.1. Calibration

Before executing fsQCA, we calibrated all variables to fuzzy sets, with values ranging from 0 (indicating full non-membership) to 1 (indicating full set membership). We defined three anchor points to determine the membership of variables (fuzzy scores = 0.95, 0.05, and 0.50), representing full set membership, full non-membership, and the crossover point, respectively [54]. Following Chen and Tian [56], we calibrated the variables according to the three anchor points above, as this allowed us to match the actual sample distribution. Firm size was calibrated to a clear set, where 1 represents a large firm (>500 em ployees), and 0 represents SMEs (1–499 employees).

## 5.2.2. Identifying necessary conditions

We conducted a necessary condition analysis of marketing perfor mance. If the consistency coefficient is higher than 0.9, a condition is usually regarded as necessary for the result [14]. The consistency value of all conditions was <0.9, which indicates that no single condition is necessary for achieving high marketing performance.

## 5.2.3. Identifying sufficient solutions for marketing performance

We applied the fsQCA algorithm to generate a truth table of $: 2 ^ { 4 }$ rows.

To refine the truth table and select meaningful combinations, we set the frequency cutoff to 1 and the consistency threshold to no <0.8 [54]. Frequency is the number of specific causal combinations. Consistency measures the approximation of the causal relationship expressed by the condition combinations and the solutions, similar to the significance concept in statistical models [55]. The proportional reduction in inconsistency (PRI) applied to avoid the simultaneous subset relations of a combination in the result and result negation. We adjusted the six-row truth table to 0 with PRI values below 0.7. Finally, we obtained the truth table satisfying the requirements and three solutions. In the fsQCA re sults, three different solutions were produced: a complex solution, a parsimonious solution, and an intermediate solution. We determined the core and peripheral conditions of each solution by examining the parsimonious and intermediate solutions [55]. The fsQCA analysis re sults for achieving high marketing performance yielded three solutions with 0.645 coverage and 0.872 consistency, indicating that the combi nation of conditions for achieving marketing performance has high interpretability and reliability. The final results are shown in Table 8.

Specifically, Solutions 1 and 2 showed that social media use and guanxi are core resources in firm marketing, and either with large or ganizations that have developed social CRM capability can enhance marketing performance. Namely, large firms can achieve high market ing performance through either path $( \mathrm { i . e . , } S \mathrm { M U ^ { \ast } S C C }  \mathrm { M P \ o r \ G X ^ { \ast } S C C }$ → MP). By contrast, Solution 3 worked for all firms, but it was the only path for SMEs to achieve high marketing performance. Solution 3 in dicates that SMEs not only need the core resources of social media use and guanxi but must also use social media to cultivate social CRM capability and then leverage guanxi to maximize the value of social CRM capability and thus achieve high marketing performance.

In summary, exploratory analysis provides additional insights based on fsQCA, identifying three configurations that can enhance marketing performance. (i) These configurations confirm the findings of ADANCO SEM analysis. All three configurations emphasize the core condition role of social CRM capability in achieving high marketing performance, thus supporting the conclusion of H1 regarding the critical role of social CRM capability. In addition, configurations 1 and 2 indicate that for large firms, high marketing performance can be achieved by investing in either social media or guanxi to develop and cooperate social CRM capability. This finding also confirms H2 that social media and guanxi can substitute each other. (ii) The fsQCA findings likewise extend the ADANCO-SEM analysis. The three configurations reveal that firms of different sizes differ in their strategic configurations for using social media to gain competitive advantages. In other words, rather than investing in each resource, large firms can choose the optimal strategic configuration based on resource availability. Therefore, the insights obtained from the mixed-methods design demonstrate the value of combining two complementary techniques (i.e., ADANCO-SEM and fsQCA) to analyze our research model.

## 6. Discussion and conclusions

## 6.1. Contributions to IS research

This research makes the following theoretical contributions. First, the study elucidates the mechanisms of using social media to enhance marketing performance by revealing the mediating role of social CRM capability. It also reveals that the pathway from social media use to marketing performance via social CRM capability depends on the level of guanxi. Specifically, when the strength of guanxi reaches a certain high threshold, social media use can significantly enhance marketing performance via the full mediating role of social CRM capability. However, when the guanxi level is low, the indirect effect of social media use on marketing performance is nonexistent. These findings provide nuanced insights into the mechanisms through which social media use improves marketing performance. The results extend litera ture on social media business value by identifying guanxi as a boundary condition for leveraging social media to enhance marketing perfor mance through social CRM capability. Moreover, by identifying the moderator role of guanxi in such a process, this study enriches the ITenabled organizational capabilities theory and reveals its boundaries. Additionally, this study broadens the scope of the IT-enabled organiza tional capabilities theory by exploring it in an agricultural context.

Second, this study sheds new light on social media research by identifying the dual moderating roles of guanxi in leveraging social media to realize business value. This research delineates the moderating role of guanxi in refining the ambiguous understanding of existing research on boundary conditions in the influence of social media use on firm performance. Our work complements previous empirical studies that have distinctly focused on industry and channel factors as moder ators in social media value creation by exploring a social resource (i.e., guanxi) as a contingency factor [3,57]. Specifically, we uncover the dual moderating roles of guanxi $( \mathrm { i . e . } ,$ , substitute and complementary) in the value creation process of social media use, which weaken the effect of social media use on social CRM capability and strengthen the effect of social CRM capability on marketing performance. These findings refine the knowledge of guanxi and develop an integrative understanding of how firm performance can be cultivated through social media use.

Third, this study provides subtle and rich insights into the literature on social media business value by adopting a mixed-methods design. We first utilized the ADANCO-SEM technique to reveal the mediating mechanism and boundary condition by which social media use improves marketing performance. Subsequently, exploratory analysis used fsQCA technique to identify three configurations that lead to high levels of marketing performance from a causal asymmetry perspective. It further uncovers the heterogeneity of firm size in the strategic configuration of using social media for competitive advantages. Thus, fsQCA results validate and complement the ADANCO–SEM analysis results by offering an in-depth understanding of the nonlinear and synergistic effects of social media use, social CRM capability, guanxi, and firm size in enhancing marketing performance. The findings also extend previous studies that primarily used SEM methods to explore the influence of social media use on performance $[ 3 , 1 9 ]$ . By combining complementary techniques to answer our research questions, this work enlightens future studies through methodological innovation in numerous research paradigms.

## 6.2. Lessons learned for practitioners

This study has several managerial implications for firms regarding social media marketing. First, this work shows managers how social media can be leveraged to improve marketing performance, and high lights the vital role of social CRM capability. Therefore, firms should adopt social monitoring mechanisms to increase the frequency of in teractions and response speed with customers, meet increasingly com plex consumer needs, and maintain and deepen customer relationships by enhancing consumer satisfaction and loyalty. At the same time, we have revealed to managers the critical value of investing in social media to build social CRM capability. They need to deepen their engagement with social media applications, adjust business processes to adapt to digital marketing models, and achieve success in digital marketing by effectively deploying marketing resources.

Second, our study provides firms with guidance on effectively deploying guanxi resources in social media marketing by revealing the dual moderating role of guanxi in the process of social media value creation. Specifically, firms should devote their limited resources to social media use rather than guanxi during the stage of social CRM capability development, as they are competing for resources. Until their social CRM capability is fully developed, firms may lack sufficient power to efficiently allocate and deploy both resources simultaneously. Thus, investing in guanxi at this stage may waste their resources. We also unveil the complementary effect of guanxi on social CRM capability and present that guanxi is the key condition for the existence of the indirect effects of social media use on marketing performance via social CRM capability. Facing evolving customer needs, firms must focus on build ing and maintaining high-quality guanxi with their stakeholders. This way, firms can better access scarce marketing resources, give full play to the effectiveness of social CRM capability. The findings are extendable to countries with similar culture than China, such as Japan, South Korean and Spain, where guanxi is important.

Third, the fsQCA results also reveal to managers how different sizes of firms can achieve high marketing performance by allocating resources and capabilities. The findings will make managers aware of the necessity of social CRM capability and guiding firms in selecting appropriate strategic configurations based on their realities (e.g., resource avail ability). For SMEs, they need to clarify their positioning, actively develop existing resources, combine the advantages of social networks and guanxi to cultivate customer relationships, optimize costeffectiveness. Large firms should leverage their resources and scale ad vantages, choose their areas of expertise (e.g., social media or guanxi), focus on improving the effectiveness of resource use, and continuously consolidate and expand their competitive advantages. This finding helps avoid the misconception that more resources are always better. If access to specific resources is limited or costly, firms can adjust or choose alternative paths to attain marketing performance. The findings enable firms to optimize resource allocation and achieve strategic flexibility.

## 6.3. Limitations and future opportunities for IS research

This research has several limitations. First, we collected data from Chinese agribusinesses which allowed us to control for cross-industry confounding but may limit the generalizability of our findings. Future research could examine other industry backgrounds to test the research model’s robustness. Second, this study examined the moderating role of guanxi but did not consider other possible contingency factors, such as human capital. Future research could explore how these variables moderate the value-creation process of social media use. Third, we investigated the influence of social media use on social CRM capability. Future studies focus on its effect on other capabilities like organizational agility given social media’s potential to enhance quick responses to uncertainty. Fourth, we utilized survey data to measure marketing performance, which may raise concerns about subjective bias. Thus, future research could use official financial data to measure marketing performance.

## 6.4. Conclusion

Motivated by a desire to understand how social media can be used to enhance marketing performance, we developed and tested a research model of the relationships between social media use, guanxi, social CRM capability, and marketing performance. We conducted a mixed-methods design to identify the underlying mechanisms and boundary conditions through which social media use influenced marketing performance. Our work serves as a building block for future studies in this important and growing area.
