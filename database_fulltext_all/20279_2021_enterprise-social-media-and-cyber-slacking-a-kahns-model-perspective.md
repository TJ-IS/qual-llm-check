## Abstract

This study investigates the associations between enterprise social media (ESM) use, psychological conditions, and cyber-slacking in the workplace and examines the mediating role of psychological conditions between ESM use and cyber-slacking. The study utilizes employs a cross-sectional design, testing the developed research model with 277 ESM users from Chinese enterprises. The study results suggest that ESM use stimulates various psychological conditions that discourage cyber-slacking behaviors and significantly mediate the associations between ESM use and cyber-slacking.

Keywords: Cyber-slacking in the workplace; enterprise social media; psychological meaningfulness; psychological availability; psychological safety

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise social media (ESM) is defined as “web-based platforms that allow workers to communicate or broadcast messages, indicate or reveal particular coworkers as communication partners, post, edit, and sort text and files linked to themselves or others, and view the messages, connections, text, and files communicated, posted, edited and sorted by others” (Leonardi et al., 2013, p.2). ESM offers great capabilities to employees, such as the possibilities to build, enhance, and maintain their social relationships at work (Rathi & Given, 2017). Employees use ESM to communicate and establish social interaction through which they can attain social, psychological, and practical support (Leonardi, 2015). ESM also enables employees to share knowledge (Treem & Leonardi, 2013; Zhao et al., 2020), enhance crossboundary communication and decision-making (van Osch et al., 2015), stimulate their creativity and agility (Cai et al., 2018; Ding et al., 2019), improve management practices, and accelerate innovation and collaboration (Leonardi, 2015). Companies like Google, Microsoft, and IBM are investing their resources in building ESM to support information sharing and cooperation between employees to improve their work performance (Lu et al., 2015). ESM provides a psychologically safe environment for coworkers in which they can establish interactive relationships and seek solutions to difficult problems (Cai et al., 2018). Slack is a well-recognized and extensively used ESM platform that has been widely adopted by organizations to provide new forms of communication and collaboration among employees (Leonardi, 2015). A recent report has indicated that the daily users of Slack have exceeded the 12 million mark (Statista, 2020), and it is one of the most prevalent ESM platforms with a total market value of \$17 billion (Consumer News and Business Channel [CNBC], 2020).

Despite the burgeoning scholarship on ESM use and its outcomes (e.g., creativity, agility, and innovation), much space remains to explore ESM behavioral dynamics, such as cyber-slacking (Cai et al., 2018; Ding et al., 2019; Vitak et al., 2011). “Cyber-slacking” refers to the personal use of the Internet for non-work purposes among employees, which is considered counterwork behavior (Alharthi et al., 2019; Luqman et al., 2020). Scholars have argued that the proliferation of Internet connectivity has resulted in cyber-slacking behavior in the workplace, which poses a significant productivity challenge for employees as well as organizations (Luqman et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2018). Cyber-slacking in the workplace can be minuscule and take just minutes (e.g., personal emails and online shopping), or it can include spending much more time (e.g., gambling, listening to music, and watching movies) that may significantly reduce employee productivity (Vitak et al., 2011). Scholars have claimed that the rate of cyber-slacking in the workplace is about 60–80%, which results in an approximately 30–40% decline in productivity (Alharthi et al., 2019). We agree that ESM not only offers social and psychological benefits but also allows declines in work productivity due to cyber-slacking, making it important to explore how ESM can help employees personally engage in the workplace while reducing such deviant behavior (Leigh & Melwani, 2019).

The review of prior literature on ESM suggests various gaps and limitations. First, the literature perhaps over-emphasizes the positive outcome of ESM use while neglecting its negative outcome, namely, that ESM undermines sensing market changes (Turban et al., 2011), induces social and informational overload that undercuts productivity (Chen & Wei, 2019), and creates emotional exhaustion that hampers innovation (Tang et al., 2019). To bridge this gap, the current investigation focuses on ESM’s adverse outcome, that is, cyber-slacking behavior related to ESM use.

Second, prior literature has mainly focused on performance measures, such as agility performance (Cai et al., 2018) and innovative behavior (Tang et al., 2019). In comparison, the outcome variables of the negative side of ESM use are rarely studied. For example, to the best of our knowledge, cyber-slacking behaviors as outcome variables are never studied in the context of ESM. Organizations are in dire need of better understanding and developing strategies and initiatives to reduce cyber-slacking in the workplace. Prior literature has suggested that organizations have enforced constraints to reduce cyber-slacking behavior (Ali-Hassan et al., 2015; Mercado et al., 2017), including prohibiting the use of certain social media applications, such as Facebook and YouTube (Ali-Hassan et al., 2015), an Internet surveillance program (Mercado et al., 2017), blocking access to certain websites (Koay, 2018), and imposing hefty fines (Khansa et al., 2017). However, scholars have suggested that close monitoring and strict policies are likely to disturb the trust balance between employees and employers (Lee Jr. et al., 2017) and lower their satisfaction level (Cheng et al., 2014). This ineffectiveness leaves an urgent need for employee-friendly strategies to reduce cyber-slacking related to ESM use in the workplace. The cu nt investigation is thus timely and relevant since it helps managers minimize cyber-slacking with non-intrusive strategies.

Third, recent literature has emphasized the associations between ESM use and psychological conditions (e.g., psychological meaningfulness, safety, and availability) (Cai et al., 2018). Scholars have argued that certainty among employees that psychological resources are available, which helps them handle market dynamics, should be regarded as agility performance (May et al., 2004). However, the associations of psychological conditions with deviant behavior at work, such as cyber-slacking, remain unknown. Prior literature has also not yet examined the mediating role of psychological conditions between ESM use and cyber-slacking. Examining all these associations is important because the seminal literature on organizational psychology (e.g., Kahn, 1990) suggests that psychological experiences in the workplace shape employee behavior. The current study addresses this gap by examining the mediating role of psychological conditions between ESM use and cyber-slacking utilizing Kahn’s framework, a popular theoretical lens from organizational behavioral literature, to better understand the proposed associations.

The current study addresses these open research gaps in the ESM literature and proposes two main research questions (RQs). RQ1. Does ESM use share a positive association with psychological conditions, and does psychological conditions share a negative association with cyber-slacking behavior among employees who use ESM? RQ2. Do psychological conditions mediate the associations between ESM use and cyber-slacking behavior? The study proposes a research model based on Kahn’s framework (Kahn, 1990) using a cross-sectional research design to test whether using ESM stimulates desirable psychological conditions (i.e., psychological meaningfulness, psychological availability, and psychological safety) and whether they in turn reduce cyber-slacking. A total of 277 ESM users from China participated in this study.

The main contributions of the current study are the following: First, it contributes to the timely, ongoing debate on whether ESM inhibits or facilitates cyber-slacking behavior (Zhao et al., 2020). This investigation also highlights the essential yet neglected research emphasis on the negative side of ESM. Second, the study’s results may be of use for organizations, ESM service providers, human resource specialists, and policymakers to explore how deviant behaviors at work, such as cyber-slacking, can be reduced by focusing on the psychological conditions of employees. The study results provide insights into how ESM use contributes toward these psychological conditions. Third, the current investigation significantly contributes to the limited theoretical foundations of the ESM literature by, for the first time, utilizing Khan’s theoretical framework to understand the negative side of ESM.

## 2. Background literature

## 2.1 Enterprise social media

Organizations are progressively actualizing applications based on social networking because it offers socialization, shared trust development, and socio-emotional relationships (Luqman et al., 2020). In this regard, organizations are increasingly adopting ESM to improve employee performance, as its use is linked to profitable outcomes (Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016; Zhang et al., 2019). ESM is a form of webbased, intra-organizational correspondence, coordination, and cooperation (Cai et al., 2018; Leonardi, 2015; Zhang et al., 2019). ESM is a collaboration tool, and its popularity is only growing with time.

ESM has many advantages, including (a) creating online communities and forming trust between employees (Leonardi, 2015; Zhong et al., 2012; Zhao et al., 2020), which help identify coworkers with similar backgrounds, interests, and activities to increase peer awareness, build mutual reliance and friendship, and contribute to recruitment (Ding et al., 2019); (b) promoting the flow of valuable resources and generating knowledge and information (Ali-Hassan et al., 2015), including information sharing, team management, and collective intelligence (Ellison et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2019), and allowing employees to learn in different domains to increase their learning abilities (Treem & Leonardi, 2013; Cai et al., 2018); (c) allowing the strategic use of information, that is, sharing, posting, editing, and deleting information to enhance employees’ knowledge-based collaboration and social networking ties (Ali-Hassan et al., 2015), such as coworkers viewing, posting, editing, and sorting text and files communicated, posted, edited, and sorted by anyone else in the organization (Leonardi et al., 2015); (d) improving the productivity of employees, such as recording every interaction and communication, so that they are available at any time (Ding et al., 2019). All these advantages suggest that implementing ESM is important.

Prior ESM literature has focused on the broader advantages of ESM with respect to the alliance between technology and organizational change (Cai et al., 2018; Luqman et al., 2020; Treem & Leonardi, 2013). Scholars have argued that organizations are apprehensive about the effectiveness of ESM and claim that it is a waste of money and time for not aligning with organizational needs and functions (Leigh & Melwani, 2019). Similarly, others have found that ESM induces unproductive use of online resources without effectiveness (Tang et al., 2019). The academic community thus has a different understanding of the effectiveness of ESM that requires further investigation. Similarly, scholars have also reasoned that research on the advantages of ESM is growing, yet empirical investigations are missing that critically examine the merits and demerits of ESM use with respect to work performance (Leonardi, 2013, 2015; Zhang et al., 2019). The current study addresses this gap by investigating whether and how ESM use can reduce deviant employee behavior, that is, cyber-slacking.

## 2.2 Cyber-slacking

“Cyber-slacking” is Internet use during work hours for personal reasons or entertainment (Ugrin & Pearson, 2013). Cyber-slacking was first introduced related to phone usage for personal reasons at work, which affects the productivity of employees (Alharthi et al., 2019). Many organizations provide Internet to their employees, but they spend it inefficiently on non-work-related activities (Ugrin & Pearson, 2013). Cyber-slacking is a contemporary deviant behavior instigated by technological development. Scholars have reasoned that young executives are more likely to be involved in cyber-slacking (Ugrin et al., 2008). Similarly, employees of high status are more likely to engage in cyber-slacking than others (Vitak et al., 2011).

Cyber-slacking has a spectrum of minor to serious types (Blanchard & Henle, 2008; Koay, 2018). Minor cyber-slacking includes visiting websites for sports news, sending or receiving emails, and browsing online shopping portals. In comparison, serious cyber-slacking involves more intensive activities, such as downloading and watching pornographic movies, playing Internet games and gambling, and continuously chatting on social media platforms (Mercado et al., 2017).

Scholars have also emphasized the other types of cyber-slacking behavior besides the major and minor cyber-slacking. Lim (2002) categorized cyber-slacking into two types; first, slacking on the Web (e.g., reading the news and surfing for shopping) and second to check and send non-job-related emails. Blao et al. (2004) added a third type, that is, interaction with other people via software, to Lim's classification (2002). Mahatankon et al. (2004) also suggested three types of cyber-slacking: e-commerce, information search, and personal communication. Later, Ramayah (2010) suggested four types of cyber-slacking, namely, personal communication, access to personal information, personal downloads, and personal ecommerce. Scholars suggest that newer types of cyber-slacking are proposed with time due to the emergence of social media platforms (Doorn, 2011). The four categories of cyber-slacking suggested by Doorn (2011) are social, informational, leisure, and virtual emotional activity. Scholars have employed various terms to explain the non-productive internet use at the workplace. However, they reasoned that serious cyber-slacking signifies deliberate behavior that violates organizational norms and poses a threat to an organization (Leigh & Melwani, 2019). Furthermore, cyber-slacking is a cause of distraction at work, reducing the cognitive resources of employees (Rajah & Lim, 2011).

Prior literature has examined the factors that drive cyber-slacking in the workplace, which include poor organizational justice (Lim & Teo, 2005), techno-stress (Güğerçin, 2019), and information overload (Luqman et al., 2017). Similarly, prior literature has examined the negative implications of cyber-slacking in the workplace, such as wasting time, losing productivity and revenue (Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016), distracting from work-related activities (Tang et al., 2019), undermining the norms and culture of the organization (Sheikh et al., 2019), and threatening the security of email systems and networks (Hernández et al., 2016).

Scholars have cautioned that not all cyber-slacking activities are harmful to employees or their organizations; prior literature has also indicated that cyber-slacking offers various advantages, such as developing personally, coping with stress, and alleviating boredom, fatigue, and stress, which helps improve employee productivity, satisfaction level, and overall recovery (Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016; Reinecke et al., 2014), enhance employee productivity and performance (Cai et al., 2018; Tang et al., 2019), develop essential work skills, and enhance knowledge (Leonardi, 2015; Zhang et al., 2016).

The existing body of literature on cyber-slacking in the workplace has considered either negative or positive performance-related outcomes. To the best of our understanding, no prior study has examined the empirical associations among ESM use, psychological conditions, and cyber-slacking behavior. The current study suggests that ESM use stimulates psychological conditions that ultimately reduce cyber-slacking. These findings pave the way toward the development of non-intrusive strategies or mechanisms to mitigate cyber-slacking at work.

## 3. Theoretical background and hypotheses development

The present study examines whether ESM use stimulates psychological conditions that mitigate cyber-slacking (Figure 1). Scholars have argued that ESM use in organizations has resulted in an increased focus on the psychological conditions of employees (Cai et al., 2018; Kahn, 1990). ESM use at work is indeed positively associated with these psychological conditions, namely, meaningfulness, availability, and safety, and increases employee innovation (Treem & Leonardi, 2013; Zhang et al., 2016). The study utilizes Kahn’s (1990) framework, a popular theoretical model from organizational psychology, to better understand the psychological conditions associated with the use of ESM. Kahn’s framework suggests that psychological conditions drive the extent to which people are psychologically involved in their work in an conditions: meaningfulness, availability, and safety.

Psychological meaningfulness defines employees’ feelings and their work’s key objectives and signifies the work motives or goals in relation to an individual’s own ideals and standards (May et al., 2004). Psychological meaningfulness is an essential, effective state that influences worker performance (Cai et al., 2018; Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016; May et al., 2004). Employees who are high in psychological meaningfulness are likely to spend more time and effort developing their capacities to handle unpredictable situations (Ashrafi et al., 2019; Cai et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2018). Psychological safety is the degree to which employees feel safe and comfortable expressing themselves without fear of negative consequences for their self-images, statuses, or careers (Kahn, 1990). Furthermore, because of psychological safety, employees feel safe in the workplace to express their opinions (Cai et al., 2018; Kahn, 1990). Psychological availability is the belief that employees have the physical, emotional, and cognitive resources to engage themselves at work (Kahn, 1990). Scholars have argued that employees with low psychological availability experience a lack of energy that distracts them from work-related activities (Güğerçin, 2019; Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016). Confidence in the availability of resources also improves employee performance because it enables them to generate good ideas (Halbesleben et al., 2014).

## 3.1 ESM use and psychological conditions

The review of extended literature on ESM use and organizational psychology suggests that ESM use boosts psychological meaningfulness, safety, and availability and that these psychological conditions mitigate the risk of cyber-slacking at work. The associated literature and corresponding hypotheses are discussed below.

## 3.1.1 ESM use and meaningfulness

The prior literature on strategic management and organizations suggests that employees with high psychological meaningfulness are more likely to be committed to their organizations (Hutter et al., 2017). A committed employee is also l ess likely to engage in deviant behavior at work (Pryce-Jones, 2011). Scholars have also suggested that employees receiving admiration and motivational support at work are more likely to concentrate more on work instead of non-work activities (Pryce-Jones, 2011). Similarly, ESM literature suggests that employees tend to feel their work is more meaningful when they see high achievers in their organizations effectively using ESM for work reasons (Treem & Leonardi, 2013). Scholars have positively linked ESM use with meaningfulness as a psychological condition. For example, ESM enables employees to express themselves to make a difference at work and feel that their work is significant (Cai et al., 2018); consequently, they feel worthy and valuable (Lee Jr. et al., 2017). Furthermore, ESM offers employees a platform to introduce ideas, self-expression, questions, and creativity (Leonardi, 2015). Therefore, in the current study, we hypothesize that ESM use is positively linked with meaningfulness.

H1a: ESM use is positively associated with psychological meaningfulness.

## 3.1.2 ESM use and safety

Kahn (1990) suggested that psychological safety owes its existence to attributes that make an individual responsive to work rules and regulations, social relationships with the group and management, and organizational norms. Similarly, organizational elements, connections with other people, and group work and organizational environments affect employees’ psychological experiences and can make them feel safe (Lee Jr. et al., 2017; May et al., 2004; Zhang et al., 2018). Scholars have argued that the supportive relationship of an employee with management, team members, and coworkers keeps them relaxed, which ultimately increases their psychological safety (May et al., 2004; Leonardi, 2015; Cai et al., 2018). Prior ESM literature suggests that ESM use also results in psychological safety because it is associated with building social and instrumental relationships (Leonardi, 2015), expanding network size (Ding et al., 2019), connecting, communicating and collaborating with peers to resolve work-related problems (Ali-Hassan et al., 2015), reducing stress, and solving work-related concerns (Halbesleben et al., 2014). Consequently, we hypothesize a positive association between ESM use and psychological safety. H1b: ESM use is positively associated with psychological safety.

## 3.1.3 ESM use and availability

Psychological availability is influenced by reductions in physical energy, emotional energy, insecurity, and external lives (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016). Similarly, the organizational literature suggests that an individual feeling recognized increases his or her self-efficacy, which ultimately increases the availability of their resources (e.g., mental capabilities) (Hobfoll, 1989). Prior ESM literature suggests significant positive associations between ESM use and availability. Scholars have shown that employees make use of ESM to share their stories about their achievements and experiences (Wohn et al., 2016). When employees receive likes, appreciation, and gratitude from their coworkers, their social support rises (Ellison et al., 2015), which ultimately increases their resources (Wohn et al., 2016). ESM provides them a platform to access resources critical to their development (Chang, 2018; Wang et al., 2018). Furthermore, they can divert themselves in useful, work-related activities that increase their learning and ultimately improve their energy levels (Kügler et al., 2015). Positive experiences related to ESM use, such as shared interests and commitments, stimulate and trigger employees’ full resources (Reychav et al., 2019). Based on this, we hypothesize a positive association between ESM use and availability. H1c: ESM use is positively associated with psychological availability.

## 3.2 Psychological conditions and cyber-slacking

Cyber-slacking is a form of deviant behavior that depends on various factors, such as organizational justice, work environment, and self-control (Lim & Teo, 2005). Similarly, prior literature suggests that employees learn deviant behavior from their work environment and later justify it as normative (Leasure & Zhang, 2018). In this regard, scholars have linked psychological conditions related to the organizational environment with engagement in deviant behavior, such as cyber-slacking.

Psychological meaningfulness facilitates coworkers’ interactions, as it makes them useful and valuable (Kahn, 1990). Meaningfulness enhances relatedness and intrinsic motivation at work (Hutter et al., 2017; Pryce-Jones, 2011). When an employee feels more meaningful in their work, they become more involved in work-related tasks, significantly reducing cyber-slacking (Lee Jr. et al., 2017). We therefore hypothesize that psychological meaningfulness mitigates cyber-slacking at work. H2. Psychological meaningfulness is negatively associated with cyber-slacking.

Employees experience psychological safety when they feel safe and secure expressing their opinions and ideas in their organizational environment (Pee, 2018). Employees feel more comfortable and actively involved in work and explore novel techniques for improving performance when they are high in psychological safety (May et al., 2004). Furthermore, psychologically safe employees are more likely to be involved and committed in work-related tasks, which ultimately reduces their cyber-slacking (Leigh & Melwani, 2019; Luqman et al., 2020; Mercado et al., 2017). Therefore, we hypothesize that safety is negatively linked to cyber-slacking.

H3. Psychological safety is negatively associated with cyber-slacking.

Psychological availability focuses on the faith of employees that they have enough mental capability, cognitive resources, and motivation to achieve their work-related tasks (Jannesari et al., 2017). When employees feel that they have the exact resources required to perform a specific work task, they feel more satisfied, motivated, and fully available (Reychav et al., 2019). Psychological availability enables an employee to cope with physical, emotional, and cognitive demands in the workplace (Jannesari et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2018). Therefore, the availability of psychological resources enables employees to engage in their work, which ultimately reduces their engagement in deviant behaviors, such as cyber-slacking. Based on this rationale, we propose the following hypothesis.

H4. Psychological availability is negatively associated with cyber-slacking.

## 3.3 Mediating role of psychological conditions

The current study utilizes Kahn’s (1990) framework to explain that the psychological conditions associated with ESM use mitigate cyber-slacking. Furthermore, ESM usage enhances desirable psychological conditions. In this regard, we examine the mediating role of these psychological conditions between ESM use and cyber-slacking behavior.

ESM use provides positive personal resources at work, such as self-efficacy, intrinsic motivation, affective commitments, and meaningfulness (Pryce-Jones, 2011). ESM use is associated with meaningfulness at work, which enables employees to solve their problems and reduce their work stress (O’Neill et al., 2016). Meaningfulness also makes employees resilient toward deviant behavior (Leasure & Zhang, 2018; Leigh & Melwani, 2019). Consequently, it is likely that active users of ESM at work are motivated and engaged with work-related tasks and are less likely to indulge in cyber-slacking. We thus propose the following hypothesis.

H5: Psychological meaningfulness mediates the relationship between ESM use and cyber-slacking.

ESM use at work conduces psychological safety because it helps employees receive quick feedback, share their thoughts, and receive instant responses to their queries through ESM (Pee, 2018). Employees tend to feel safer and more confident in their work-related tasks, which mitigates the risk of cyber-slacking (Alharthi et al., 2019). Based on this, we hypothesize that ESM provides a trustworthy atmosphere that promotes trust and belongingness among employees, which makes them feel safe at work and prevents them from engaging in deviant behavior, such as cyber-slacking.

H6: Psychological safety mediates the relationship between ESM use and cyber-slacking.

Employees with psychological availability are willing or ready to be physically involved, cognitively dedicated, and emotionally linked to their work (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Halbesleben & Wheeler, 2015). Similarly, ESM use helps employees conserve their resources, which are then available to workplace (Halbesleben & Wheeler, 2015). The social and emotional ties obtained by ESM use keep employees psychologically present and committed to their work (Ding et al., 2019; Wohn et al., 2016). Employees high in psychological availability are likely to refrain from cyber-slacking. These studies suggest that psychological availability acts as a mediating variable between ESM use and cyber-slacking.

H7: Psychological availability mediates the relationship between ESM use and cyber-slacking.

## 4. Research methodology

## 4.1 Measurement

All the study measures were adapted from prior literature in the fields of ESM and organizational behavior. The measurement items for ESM use at work were adapted from Ou et al. (2016). The items for the psychological meaningfulness, psychological safety, and psychological availability were adapted from May et al. (2004). Furthermore, we have adapted survey items for cyber-slacking from Lim and Teo (2005). All the measurement items were evaluated using a 7-point scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree).

## 4.2. Sample and data collection

The study utilized a cross-sectional survey design to test the proposed research hypotheses. The study participants were Chinese ESM users employed by various Chinese enterprises. China has witnessed a massive popularity of ESM use in recent years, so Chinese enterprises are broadly adopting ESM as a communication tool and for its cost-effectiveness. Three language experts translated the survey from English to Mandarin Chinese using the back-translation method (Brislin et al., 1973). A pilot study was organized with 30 ESM users to ensure the content validity of the survey questionnaire. In the pilot study, researchers asked the participants to examine whether the measurement items/questions were clear and easy to interpret and answer. Based on the pilot study results, the questionnaire used for the data collection received minor language editing. Researchers distributed a total of 351 questionnaires and collected 300 responses. A total of 23 were discarded for incompleteness. The final sample consisted of 277 responses for the data analysis. Researchers ensured the confidentiality and anonymity of the respondents and sample independence while recruiting respondents by providing a unique identification number to every respondent. The unique number ensured that one person could not return two responses.

Table 1 presents the demographic information of the sample. The age range of the study respondents was 22–40 years; the percentage of female respondents was 44.8%, and the respondents who had experiences using ESM for less than a year comprised 32.5%, 1–2 years 45.5%, 2–3 years 14.8%, and 3–4 years 7.2%.

## 6. Discussion

## 6.1 Summary of Findings

Prior literature has mainly focused on the merits of ESM use, which includes examining the various positive outcomes of using ESM in the workplace; the demerits of ESM use, such as cyber-slacking, is less studied. The current study utilizes a well-known theoretical framework from organizational psychology literature—Kahn’s (1990) framework—to examine the mediating role of desirable psychological conditions (i.e., psychological meaningfulness, psychological availability, and psychological safety) between ESM use and cyber-slacking. The study’s findings suggest that ESM use stimulates these psychological conditions, which later mitigate the likelihood of cyber-slacking at work.

The present study examined the relationship between ESM use, desirable psychological conditions, and cyber-slacking. We developed a research model based on Kahn’s (1990) framework and proposed two main RQs. RQ1 examined the positive associations between ESM use and psychological conditions and the negative association between psychological conditions and cyber-slacking among ESM employees. The results suggested that ESM use was positively associated with psychological meaningfulness, safety, and availability, which is consistent with Kahn’s framework, and we found support for all the proposed hypotheses. (a) ESM use enhances the identities of employees at work; they feel more confident and satisfied, which enhances their work’s meaningfulness (Leonardi, 2015), a result consistent with prior literature (Cai et al., 2018; Lee Jr. et al., 2017; Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016; May et al., 2004). (b) ESM use promotes psychological safety at work by encouraging harmony, social belongingness, and trust among employees, due to which employees tend to experience safe, supportive working environments. This finding is congruent with the existing literature, which also suggests that relationships at work keep coworkers relaxed and motivated, which, in turn, enhances their psychological safety (Ding et al., 2019; Kahn, 1990; May et al., 2004). (c) ESM use stimulates psychological availability because employees use it to exchange success stories and receive gratitude, appreciation, and motivation from their colleagues (Kügler et al., 2015; Mäntymäki & Riemer, 2016; Wohn et al., 2016). Furthermore, ESM use boosts employees’ energy levels and makes them psychologically available for work-related tasks (Pryce-Jones, 2011). This finding is also consistent with the existing literature (e.g., Cai et al., 2018).

The latter part of RQ1 examined the associations between psychological conditions and cyberslacking. The study findings support all the proposed hypotheses. To begin, (a) psychological meaningfulness at work can mitigate the possible engagement in cyber-slacking or other deviant behaviors in the workplace. This is consistent with prior literature, which suggests that employees perceiving their work as meaningful are more likely to be committed and motivated for work-related tasks and less likely to cyber-slack (Luqman et al., 2020; Mercado et al., 2017; Turban et al., 2011). (b) Psychological safety can mitigate cyber-slacking in the workplace, which is consistent with prior literature, suggesting that interpersonal trust among coworkers gained through ESM use fosters psychological safety, which prevents non-work-related tasks, such as cyber-slacking (Leigh & Melwani, 2019). (c) Psychological availability at work can mitigate deviant behaviors, such as cyber-slacking, which is again consistent with literature that suggests employees’ psychological resources enable them to perform work-related tasks and keep them engaged (Halbesleben & Wheeler, 2015; May et al., 2004). Furthermore, the of physical, emotional, and psychological resources helps them manage job exertion, keeps them resilient, and prevents them from engaging in deviant behavior (Hutter et al., 2017; Leasure & Zhang, 2018).

RQ2 examined the mediating role of psychological conditions (i.e., meaningfulness, safety, and availability) between ESM use and cyber-slacking. The study results support the significant mediating role of psychological conditions, which is consistent with the postulates of Kahn’s (1990) framework. The study’s findings can be explained as follows. (a) ESM use is associated with the development of social and instrumental ties with coworkers, which stimulate meaningfulness at work (Lee Jr. et al., 2017), ESM use cultivates open discussions and sharing of observations, which promotes psychological safety (Leigh & Melwani, 2019), and ESM helps employees conserve their limited physical, emotional, and cognitive resources, hence supporting their psychological availability (Jannesari et al., 2017). In this way, ESM use is positively associated with all three psychological conditions. (b) Meaningfulness at work enables employees to cope with challenges and remain engaged (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Halbesleben & Wheeler, 2015; Zhang et al., 2018), psychological safety helps employees be dedicated to their workrelated tasks (Leonardi, 2015), and the availability of psychological resources keeps employees engaged (Ding et al., 2019; Wohn et al., 2016). All three psychological conditions mitigate the likelihood of cyberslacking (May et al., 2014), which is also consistent with Kahn’s framework.

## 6.2 Theoretical contributions

The study has three major theoretical implications.

First, the overwhelming majority of prior ESM literature has focused on positive outcomes associated with ESM use, such as employee productivity, social capital, innovative behavior, agility performance, and creativity. In comparison, the current study examines the negative side of ESM use; in this case, employee engagement in deviant behavior like cyber-slacking. The negative side of ESM use is a neglected domain, so the current study provides a new, fresh perspective in the ongoing debate regarding the positive and negative outcomes of ESM use at work.

Second, the present study extends the limited theoretical foundations of prior ESM literature by utilizing a theoretical framework never used before in the field to better understand the use of ESM in the workplace. Since the negative side of ESM is less studied, the current study borrowed a popular psychological theoretical model from organizational psychology: Kahn’s (1990) framework.

Third, the study’s findings significantly contribute to the limited literature on cyber-slacking among ESM users. The results suggest that ESM use stimulates desirable psychological conditions, such as meaningfulness, safety, and availability, which mitigate the likelihood of cyber-slacking. The significant mediating role of these psychological conditions between ESM use and cyber-slacking suggests that psychological conditions effectively soften the negative influence of such deviant behaviors.

## 6.3 Practical implications

The study’s findings and results offer various practical implications for managers, service providers, organizations, employees, and policymakers. First, the findings suggest that ESM use is positively associated with desirable psychological conditions, so managers should leverage it to promote employee well-being. This could be achieved as follows: (a) managers should mentor and advise employees to set their work goals in ESM; (b) managers should encourage employees to take part in debates to find solutions to shared problems on ESM and collaborate with coworkers, such as broadcasting new features on ESM to allow managers to encourage employees to express their opinions freely; and (c) managers should offer socialization opportunities to employees that allow them to share their ideas and work-related information on ESM to enhance their motivation and energy levels.

Second, prior literature has explored the effectiveness of various intrusive strategies and remedies to control cyber-slacking that are focused on strict policing, monitoring, and punishing. Scholars have linked such intrusive strategies with lower satisfaction, trust, and commitment among employees. In comparison, the present study suggests that instead of intrusive strategies and remedies, organizations can strengthen desirable psychological conditions non-intrusively to mitigate cyber-slacking by (a) providing a friendly atmosphere where employees can share their opinion, as it makes them valuable; (b) promoting supportive relationships among employees and management, since it boosts trustworthiness; and (c) providing liberty for employees to express their views and feel safe and secure.

Finally, service providers should develop ESM features that facilitate internal communication, such as keeping employees informed about their pending tasks and providing management systems for handling contextual factors (e.g., collaboration). Such ESM features are less likely to interrupt employee work and are more likely to enhance their output. Moreover, employees should follow organizational norms while using ESM and act appropriately, as other people expect. Employees should prefer workrelated tasks on ESM and try to achieve their set goals. Furthermore, policymakers should promote a friendly atmosphere rather than strict surveillance to avoid negative consequences and enhance employee motivation, as motivated employees are less likely to indulge in cyber-slacking.

## 6.4 Limitations and Future Research

Despite these insights, the present study has three main limitations. First, the findings are based on employees recruited from Chinese enterprises. China is a growing economy that has a unique culture, so the applicability of this study’s findings to other cultural groups is unwarranted. Second, the current study employed a cross-sectional design, which limits the generalizability and validity of the factors. Third, the study only focused on the three specific psychological conditions proposed by Kahn’s (1990) framework. Many other psychological conditions may act as mediating variables between ESM use and cyber-slacking. We recommend scholars consider the following avenues of future research on this topic. First, scholars should replicate this study’s set-up in other cultural contexts through cross-cultural investigations to enable the scholarly community to gauge the similarities and differences in the associations in different cultural milieus. Second, scholars should utilize other research designs, such as longitudinal surveys, experimental designs, and multi-method studies, to overcome the limitations of selfreported data. Third, scholars should explore other theoretical frameworks to expand the existing set of psychological conditions. In this regard, we emphasize exploring the use of theories such as social bonding theory and leader-member-exchange theory in the context of ESM use. Social bonding and emotional attachment (Kügler et al., 2015) could also influence the psychological conditions associated with the use of ESM. Fourth, we encourage scholars to incorporate variables such as techno-stress (Ragu-Nathan et al., 2008), perceptions of organizational justice (Lim & Teo, 2005), and work–family conflict (Ayyagari et al., 2011) as possible instigators of minor and serious cyber-slacking.
