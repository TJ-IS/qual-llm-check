# A Dual-role Trust Model for Social Commerce: A Guanxi Perspective

Chao Su <sup>a</sup>, Qingfei Min <sup>a,\*</sup>, Eusebio Scornavacca <sup>b</sup>, Zilong Liu <sup>c</sup>

<sup>a</sup> School of Economics and Management, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, Liaoning, China

<sup>b</sup> School for the Future of Innovation in Society and Thunderbird School of Global Management, Arizona State University, Tempe, Arizona, United States

<sup>c</sup> School of Management Science and Engineering, Dongbei University of Finance and Economics, Dalian, Liaoning, China

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Social commerce Dual-role Role theory Guanxi Trust

## A B S T R A C T

Different from traditional e-commerce platforms, friend-to-friend (Fr2Fr) social commerce transactions occur among friends within a private network on social media. In this particular context, users assess their friend with dual roles in the decision-making process: friend role and seller role. Based on role theory, this study develops a dual-role trust model for exploring trust mechanisms within multiple roles. Furthermore, this study adopts a unique Guanxi perspective to explore the behavioral interplay among friends. The moderating effects of Guanxi on role-based trust are tested using a survey of 317 WeChat users. Theoretical and practical implications are discussed.

## 1. Introduction

The fast penetration of social media and ubiquitous mobile payments (e.g., WeChat Pay) facilitated commercial transactions among in dividuals on social media platforms and enabled the emergence of social commerce [1.2]. Without doubts. China is the leading marketplace in the world with regard to social commerce initiatives [1,3]. For example, the Chinese social commerce market reached 185 billion US dollars in 2019 in contrast to 22 billion dollars in the U.S. [4,5]. In this particular context, in the past five years, friend-to-friend (Fr2Fr) social commerce has been growing rapidly and becoming a mainstream of Chinese social commerce [6]. In traditional e-commerce and social commerce, the social network is formed for commercial purposes. Transactions are seller-centered (or brand-centered) and consumers share usage and purchase experience within the online community [7,8]. This type of social network is essentially different from the private social network as it connects unfamiliar consumers with commercial purposes [9]. In contrast, in Fr2Fr social commerce on social media platforms, the network is formed between friends for social purposes and transactions are mainly self-regulated by users [10,11,12]. In addition, friends in private social networks usually have a much stronger relationship based on the continuous social interaction in daily offline and online life [13, 14].

Most of the literature has mainly focused on commercial-purposebased social networks and the facet of commercial transactions among friends in social networks has been relatively unexplored. For example, several studies have aimed to understand consumer behavior mainly by building trust upon product-related cues (e.g., information quality, transaction safety, reviews) and context-related cues in the online community [1,14,15,16]. However, from the perspective of private so cial networks, very few studies have explored consumer behavior by building trust upon the existing private relationship as friends. In addition, the extant literature has not taken into consideration the different roles individuals may play in these platforms. On social media, when friends turn to sellers and buyers, circles of friends become virtual malls resulting in a process of establishing and playing different social roles in the digital platforms. Users switch from the “friend role” created in the ex ante social scenario to the “consumer role” or “seller role” in the ex post commercial scenario [17,18]. While the phenomenon of multiple roles has become more latent and significant in social media platforms, it still has not been explored in depth [18]. Since social media users play various and complex roles, the trust building mechanism has changed. More specifically, trust should no longer be built on the overall perception of the user. It is important to build a role-based trust model that captures the internal dual roles of a user. Role-based trust once again becomes a key concern in Fr2Fr commerce because the conflict between dual roles has now become more significant [19,20,21]. When social media users perceive that their friend is playing dual roles which conflict with each other, users will hesitate how to transfer the trust towards friend role to the trust towards seller role. As Fang et al. (2014) [22] point out, the existing literature has largely ignored the context in which trust is built and thus represents an oversimplification of the context in which trust affects. To better understand the trust mechanism from a role perspective, it is important to further explore how dual role conflict is making it harder to build role-based trust. As a result, this study contributes to existing research by focusing on dual-role trust and more importantly, how role conflict influences this process in the private social network context.

However, when using private social networks in commerce, some latent challenges become salient. First, the fact that “social media is designed for social interaction, rather than commercial transaction” may implicate that social media has relatively weaker institutional protection and a lack of guarantee mechanism for transaction [23]. Second, the online private social network on social media is a reflection and exten sion of offline social context. As a result, these challenges are similar to the traditional Chinese offline market which also exist in a relatively weaker institutional and regulatory environment [24,25,26]. Previou studies have demonstrated that Guanxi plays a pivotal role in the traditional Chinese offline market and transactions [27,28,29]. The Chinese concept of Guanxi is broadly defined as a close and pervasive interpersonal relationship with daily interactions and the reciproca exchange of mutual benefits [25,30,31]. From an individual level, Guanxi is regarded as a social resource that people develop and deploy to attain favors and benefits in a tacit social environment (e.g., a better job placement, an unequal transaction, a special privilege, information, or resource) [32,33,34]. Similarly, in the social commerce context, the online private social network on social media is a reflection and exten sion of offline social context. In this case, Guanxi between friends may challenge their commercial behavior. However, the effects of Guanxi are seldom explored in such a relatively weaker institutional protection environment of transactions on social media (see Table 2 and Table 3). Even though some studies explored the relationships between buyer and seller, these studies usually focused on strangers and have not empha sized the familiar Guanxi between friends [6]. Therefore, considering these similar challenges in an offline-to-online context, Guanxi is very likely to influence commercial behavior and cannot be ignored, espe cially in such representative Chinese online context [23.28.31.35]. In addition, several prior studies, either in an online or offline context, merely suggested that Guanxi and trust may be the antecedent or outcome of each other in a buyer-seller relationship [36,37,38]. They rarely identified the different underlying mechanisms involving trust and Guanxi [28,30,31]. As Xin et al. (1996) [27] pointed out, it is vital to explore the “black box” of complementary, substitute, and interaction effects of Guanxi and trust in the decision-making process. As a result, to address this gap, this study explores how the Guanxi mechanism in teracts with the trust mechanism in the Chinese Fr2Fr social commerce context.

In summary, the goal of this study is to examine how role-based trust interacts with role conflict and Guanxi and then facilitate social media users’ engagement intention with their friends. Specifically, two ques tions guide this study:

Q1: How dual-role trust interacts with role conflict and facilitates the engagement intention?

Q2: How dual-role trust interacts with Guanxi in Fr2Fr social commerce?

This study makes two theoretical and practical contributions to the social commerce literature. First, in contrast to measuring trust as an overall perception of the trusting party, we extend trust to include a person’s inner social roles based on role theory, and investigate how social media users process trust towards the dual roles they usually face in the dyadic social-commercial context. Second, this study extends trust and Guanxi research by examining how Guanxi interacts with trust. In particular, we advance the argument that Guanxi has paradoxical posi tive and negative moderating effects in the trust-building process and the intention to engage in social commerce.

## 2.1. Role theory

## 2.1.1. Role theory overview

Role theory has its roots in the sociology and psychology literature [17,19,20,39]. According to role theory, a role is generally defined as a person’s perception of himself/herself when he/she relates to the sur rounding environment and the people he/she interacts with [18,39]. Each social role is a set of norms, expectations, rights, duties, and be haviors that a person has to face and fulfill [21,40,41]. Overall, the general domain of role theory reveals the characteristic of actors within different contexts, serving as a theoretical foundation for the analysis of behavior. The nonverbal influences of social roles are rather under-represented in the literature; however, it has a significant impact on individual behavior [39].

Role theory has been studied in the fields of organization manage ment, marketing, and online social media (as given in Table 1). It uses role-theoretic analysis to identify and specify the “properties” of different roles and how these roles differ in terms of their expected be haviors, activities, and decision-making patterns.

In the social commerce research field, role theory has not drawn previous studies’ attention, but it provides a unique role perspective to analyze individual behavior. In the dyadic social-commercial context of social commerce, social media users no longer simply play a stable, consistent, and authentic social role when their social network becomes complex and multidimensional [21,39]. They frequently change their self-concepts, expectations, norms, and rules depending on the different contexts $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e } } . \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g } } . ,$ , social and commercial context), distinct social network members $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e } } . \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g } } . ,$ friend and seller) [18,47]. As a result, users are required to manage complex social roles and the associate series of appropriate or compatible expectations and behaviors [18,50]. Therefore, users perceive the role of a friend in the original social context switching to a seller role in the commercial context. The separation and coexistence of dual roles become the basis of the role-based perspective [17,46]. Role-based perspective provides an opportunity to analyze individual behavior from internal motivations. Furthermore, the role-based perspective also provides a more detailed analysis by identifying po tential role-related factors (such as role conflict) that may influence this role-based mechanism.

## 2.1.2. Role conflict

In most circumstances, most of these roles do not conflict with one another [19,21]. However, role conflict occurs in a situation when people fully meet the core expectations in one role and fall short in the other [17,19,51]. Role conflict can be defined as the incompatibility and incongruity in expectations between different social roles [19,20,21]. Previous studies pointed out that role conflict can reduce job perfor mance or job satisfaction in organizations [40,52], and commercial transactions or performance in the marketing (offline) field [20,46]. Similarly, role conflict occurs more easily in the online context because the phenomenon of multiple roles is becoming more latent and signifi cant in social media platforms [18]. On social media, users and their friends are connected with intimate self-disclosure and voluntary social interaction. More importantly, the friend role is supposed to develop from an intrinsic orientation [48,49]. According to Ryan et al. (2000) [53] and Ho (2012) [48], intrinsic orientation means that in a rela tionship both people continually emphasize that the relationship de velops for its inherent satisfaction and has a minimal or nonexistent instrumental orientation. In contrast, previous studies indicated that instrumental orientations emphasize attaining instrumental value from someone, which means that intrinsic and instrumental orientations are usually conceptualized as two different dimensions that contrast with each other [53]. However, these contrasting intrinsic and instrumental orientations may coexist in dual roles in the social commerce context With a social network support, user’s friend will leverage relationship or friendships to develop commercial transactions and use or deplete the value of their mutual social network to promote business [14,54,55]. When the user’s friend (in a seller role) display inappropriate behaviors (such as posting too many advertisements in their friendship circle, asking for too many commercial sharing and reposting, or not consid ering the intrinsic friendship orientation between them), social media users will perceive this friend as excessively capitalizing on the friend ship between them [17,49]. As a result, role conflict between the instrumental expectation from the seller role and the intrinsic expecta tion from the friend role will occur [19,20,21]. In this study, role conflict is defined as the incompatibility and incongruity in expectations between the friend role and the seller role in the dyadic social-commercial context [17,18, 20].

## 2.3. Guanxi

## 2.3.1. Guanxi overview

The Chinese phrase “Guanxi” consists of two characters: “guan” means a gate or a hurdle, and “xi” refers to a tie, a relationship, or a connection [68,69]. Guanxi means “pass the gate and get connected.” In general, Guanxi is defined as personal relationships or connections and it can be applied not only to kinship and friendship relationships, but also to social and commercial connections based on mutual interests and benefits [25,70]. Moreover, Guanxi development usually includes three sequential stages: initiating, building, and using Guanxi with different counterparts at various levels [71].

Guanxi can be viewed from a categorical-dimensional conception perspective and an ego-pragmatic perspective [69]. There are different views within the categorical-dimensional conception of Guanxi. To capture the different types and nature of relationships, some studies have categorized Guanxi by distinct types of ties (e.g., family ties (kinship). familiar persons (friends), and strangers) and dimensions (e. g., sentimental, instrumental, and mixed) [34,72]. A recent study by Li et al. (2019) [73] reconceptualized Guanxi. The authors claimed that the boundary of Guanxi should focus only on familiar ties (pseudo-kinship and friendship ties). Following theaforementioned categorical-dimensional conception of Guanxi, this study proposes that “family Guanxi” would be placed on the extreme right (primarily with sentimental ties): on the contrary. “stranger Guanxi" on the extreme left (primarily with instrumental ties), and “familiar person Guanxi” would be placed in the middle (with mixed ties). This is consistent with the context of this study where friends on social media are in a familiar relationship. As a result. Guanxi should be categorized into familiar ties in this study and it could be governed by a mixture of both instrumental and sentimental logic between friends [63,74]. In the ego-pragmatic perspective of Guanxi, networks and Guanxi ties are perceived as social resources that people develop and deploy to attain favors and benefits (e.g., a better job placement) [32,33,34]. They are also viewed as organizational capital for gaining competitive benefits and business advantages [72,75], improving economic performance by reducing costs [27,76], and getting political support from the government [77]. Ac cording to Chen et al. (2013) [69], there are two streams of Guanxi research. The first stream aims to explore individual and interpersonal levels and it studies the conception, boundaries, measurement, ante cedents, and outcomes of Guanxi [73,78]. The second stream explores Guanxi at the organizational level and emphasizes the effects of Guanxi on performance and sustainable outcomes [31,35,63]. As given in Table 3, the effects of Guanxi have been explored in four areas of the literature: organization management, marketing, social media, and so cial commerce.

In the context of social commerce, Guanxi plays an important role in commercial transactions and social behaviors. We adopt the view in this study that Guanxi should be understood as an interpersonal relationship between friends in an online social context and it consists of three dimensions: mutual understanding, reciprocal favors, and relationship harmony [6,28, 31,37]. By building closed Guanxi between friends, users (buyers) can obtain better products and services, discounts, and other benefits in the purchase process and sharing process. In addition, they may also gain confidence and social benefits [36,81]. However, Guanxi management in social commerce may motivate people to be more likely to view purchase and sharing behavior as a form of Guanxi investment or Guanxi cost for other reciprocal rewards [28,76]. In the Guanxi tie perspective, Guanxi has been viewed and empirically tested as a network of familiar ties [31,35]. When familiar Guanxi ties come into the commercial context, it will affect regular purchases and sharing behaviors [80]. Especially when the institutional trust is weak, the in-group Guanxi is silent to substitute trust and guarantee the transaction [27.29].

## 2.3.2. Similarities and differences between guanxi and trust

To fully understand Guanxi, it is important to acknowledge the key similarities and differences between Guanxi and trust. Several studies have treated trust and Guanxi as components or outcomes of each other [6,28,31]. Both trust and Guanxi emphasize a relationship orientation and cooperation between exchanging parties. However, trust and Guanxi are two distinct concepts [30,31]. While trust is mostly perceived as a western concept, Guanxi is considered to be a Chinese cultural trait and a unique form of relationship management [30,74]. In general, previous studies proved that Guanxi differs from trust in terms of the nature of the relationships: personal vs. impersonal and particularistic vs. universalistic [78]. This means that Guanxi usually takes effects in an informal context, while trust is based on a functional system in a formal context [22,61]. More precisely, Guanxi is extremely network-specific and provides exclusive resources for network members [35,82]. The body of research in trust usually identified several functional systems to reflect the trust condition of the seller, such as review systems, reputa tion systems, interaction structures, and institutional mechanisms. [57, 83.84]. In contrast to the reliance on stable systems of trust. Guanxi is generally a hierarchically structured network of relationships embedded with mutual obligations through a self-conscious manipulation of reci procity in a closed circle $[ 3 0 , 3 1 , 7 8 ]$ . This reciprocal favor usually is not an equal value between members in the exchange process (e.g., the value of giving may be larger than receiving), because both of them may save Guanxi and use Guanxi for potential extra benefit in the future, especially in such a social context among friends. This is a clear contrast to western societies’ emphasis on short-term, symmetrically recipro cated exchange relationships [78,82]. Regarding other differences, previous studies also demonstrated the differences between Guanxi and trust, clearly distinguishing these two constructs [28,31,70,78]. There fore, the different underlying mechanisms between Guanxi and trust give a more effective way to understand Fr2Fr engagement behavior in mixed social-commercial online environments.

## 3. Research model and hypotheses

Research model aims at explaining how role-based trust facilitates engagement between social media users and their friends on social media. It hypothesizes that trust towards the friend role positively in fluences trust towards the seller role, which in turn, influences social commerce engagement intention (Fig. 1 shows the research model and Table 4 shows principal constructs and their definitions). In addition, it poses that Guanxi and role conflict moderate the relationship in this trust-building process and intention to engage in social commerce.

## 3.1. Trust effects

## 3.1.1. Trust towards seller role and engagement intention

Trust is a well-known concept in marketing and IS research [57,84]. As discussed by Chen et al. (2015) [10], participating in social com merce transactions requires consumers to cope with the new commercial uncertainty that their friends (in a seller role) may engage in opportu nistic behaviors. In the purchasing or sharing decision-making process, social media users will consider whether their friend (in a seller role) is fully capable to offer the services and products advertised. Users will rely on all kinds of social and commercial cues to enhance their trust worthy belief towards the seller role [10,58]. For example, to measure the seller’s ability to fulfill the transaction, users will estimate the in formation, pictures, or videos (e.g., for displaying the products, brands, previous transaction records, upstream manufactures, and stocks) pos ted by their friends (in a seller role) [7]. They also evaluate the seller’s integrity and benevolence by interacting with the seller using commu nication tools, and by observing the number of “likes” and the feedback from other mutual friends [10]. Based on these cues, once a perceived trustworthy condition towards the seller role is built, it will generate positive beliefs in the seller's ability. It subsequently reduces the perceived uncertainty and risk of transaction failure caused by the seller’s inability [28]. The enhanced belief in integrity and benevolence can also decrease user’s perception of a seller’s potential opportunistic behavior when dealing with them [31,85]. Thus, a social media user who has more trustworthy beliefs in their friend’s seller role will be more likely to engage in sharing behavior or commercial transactions in this new commercial context [22]. Furthermore, according to trust literature both in e-commerce and social commerce, trust towards the seller, in general, can positively influence the trust-related outcomes, such as commercial shopping intentions, social sharing intentions, and actual transactions $[ 1 0 , 2 2 , 3 1 ]$ . Therefore, we propose that trust towards the seller role has positive effects on social commerce engagement intention:

H1a. : A user’s trust towards the seller role of his/her friend positively contributes to social commerce engagement intention.

## 3.1.2. Trust towards friend role and engagement intention

Usually, the development of online commerce depends on the effectiveness of supporting infrastructure, systems, and institutions, as well as formal and public information in the commercial context [7,8]. However, this may face greater challenges and potential limitations in an implicit social environment because of the lack of universally applicable systems and guaranteeing mechanisms in this social context [9]. In this case, previous studies proved that personal trust in a social context may play an important role in the decision-making process [23, 63]. According to the elaboration likelihood model (ELM), compared to the central route, the peripheral route with a low cognitive process usually costs users less energy to search and process information [86]. When social media platform has relatively weaker institutional protec tion and a lack of guarantee mechanism to support the commercial transaction, social media users hardly identify central cues from a new and unfamiliar commercial context [66]. At this point, if users already trust his/her friend in social life, social media users will consider the trust towards friend role as a key factor in the peripheral route to make the engagement decision. In this case, a perceived reliable image of the friend role in social context can act as a strong certification feature to reduce uncertainty and encourage user to engage in sharing behavior or commercial transaction with their friend [8,64]. From the user’s perspective, trust towards the seller’s friend role can provide users a high-efficiency and low-cost way to participate in commercial behavior with their friend [6]. It helps users to break boundaries between social and commercial contexts [49]. From the seller’s perspective, building a trustworthy friend character in social contexts will be a competitive advantage [62]. No matter which benefits the users want to seek, there is very little doubt that trust towards friend role in a social context may positively influence engagement behavior in a commercial context. Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1b. : Trust towards friend role positively influences social commerce engagement intention.

## 3.1.3. Trust transfer between dual-role-based trust

Trust transfer theory is usually used to explain the logical relation ship between two constructs of trust [87]. According to trust transfer theory, one’s trust in a trusted “proof source” can transfer to another part with which the trustor has little or no direct experience [57,87]. The trusted proof source can be a person, organization, institution, or subiect [311. For example, previous studies demonstrated that trust in a platform can be transferred to trust in the members of that platform, trust in an organization is transferable to an individual, and trust may be transferred between different people and contexts [31,57,85]. In this study, we are interested in the trust-transfer process between the different roles a person has in the context of social commerce. According to trust transfer theory, when a social media user trusts their friends (in a friend role) who serve as a proof source, the trust towards the friend role is transferable to the seller role [10]. Hence, in this study, trust transfer theory has provided the theoretical foundation for further understand ing the trust-transfer process between dual roles. For better under standing the trust transfer process, this section also adopts a role-related mechanism (social character) that helps to explain the logic of the transfer process.

Social character describes an individual’s shared character structure that is formed in a society or a social class [88]. It is established on the foundation of one’s personal way of life, typical social expectations, and functional requirements with regard to socially adaptive behavior. As Foster (2017) [89] discussed, once a social character is formed, it is steady and hardly changes. When social media users perceive that a friend is a reliable and trustworthy person, they may also have this perception of the friend’s character (as a seller role) in the commercial context. Therefore, the social media user will believe that their friend will offer reliable information, exhibit integrity and benevolence, and have the ability to guarantee the product in the transactions. The steadiness of the social character tightens the relationship between the friend role and the seller role. Thus, the user treats the trust towards the friend role as a proof source, and this trust can be transferred to the seller role.

Based on the trust transfer theory and the explanation for adopting a social character, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1c. : Trust towards friend role helps to build trust towards seller role.

## 3.2. Interaction effects of role conflict

When role conflict occurs, previous studies have suggested a few effective strategies to reduce or eliminate role conflict, such as forgoing, curtailing, or terminating the relationships between the dual roles [19]. However, most of the time, even though there is a role conflict, users do not always solve the problem by clearly separating role expectations o breaking up the relationship. Instead, they may be willing to continue a relationship online despite the role conflict, which is consistent with the offline context [17,40]. According to Heide et al. (2006) [17], the flexibility of roles helps a social media user to endure this conflict and will not easily remove their friends from their friends’ list, resulting in a mixed relationship that combines friendship expectations and selle expectations [17.18.19.90]. Even though the relationships can continue when there is a role conflict, this negative effect on the dual roles relationship was existing. According to Zhang et al. (2011) [21], when social media users are under a high conflict condition, they may be easily confused between different roles' expectations. In the context of this study, when role conflict occurs, social media users are perceived to not have the ability to evaluate what set of behaviors is expected to be appropriate in the friend role or the seller role. Hence, these results are in a cognitive strain [19,21]. Therefore, in the trust building process, this complex evaluation process and cognitive strain will make user doubt whether their trust in the seller role can be based on the trustworthiness of the friend role in the social context [51]. This means the transferring effects become less effective in such an indecisive situation. Moreover. as discussed. the trust-transfer process is based on the perceived steadiness of social character, However role conflict describes the incompatibility and incongruity between dual roles. This in; compatibility and incongruity predict that the dual roles do not match well and behave in different ways [17,40]. User will perceive that the character of friend role is not same as the character of seller role when two incompatible roles conflict. This means the steadiness of socia character of a person becomes weaker [90]. Therefore, the trust-transfer process that is based on a weaker steadiness of social character also becomes less effective. Furthermore, according to Grayson (2007) [20] and Heide et al. (2006) [17], when there is role conflict, relationship roles are enacted but “in a weakened or diluted state.” As a result, while hypothesis H1c hypothesizes the role-based trust-transfer relationship, hypothesis H2 suggests that the perceived role conflict weakens the trust-transfer relationship.

H2. : Perceived role conflict negatively moderates the relation ship between trust towards friend role and trust towards seller role.

## 3.3. Interaction effects: guanxi

## 3.3.1. Negative moderating effects of guanxi in engagement process

As outlined in the review section on Guanxi, this study distinguishes Guanxi and trust. The unique reciprocal norm of Guanxi can provide a theoretical foundation to explain the moderating effects of Guanxi on the relationship between trust (towards seller role) and engagement inten tion. As previously discussed, in a Guanxi network, it is reciprocity that plays a salient role in maintaining Guanxi [73]. Under this tacit norm of reciprocity, all members spare no effort to save Guanxi and use it for extra benefit in the future. In the social commerce context, when the norm of reciprocity in Guanxi is high, social media users may perceive that even though a seller may partake in opportunistic practices (e.g., being dishonest about the product quality), this will not happen to him/her. This is because the instrumental ties of familiar Guanxi are at work. He/she believes that the seller is looking for reciprocity to attain extra benefits from him/herself in the future – e.g., benefits related to this transaction such as reposting the ad in the user’s social network, and more importantly, other benefits related to career, and business. Simi larly. considering the sincere feeling of familiar Guanxi (in sentimenta) dimension), he/she believes their friend in a seller role will not be opportunistic with him/her. Both parties are aware of the tacit role: when a member disregards or fails to keep the rules of exchanging reciprocal favors and feelings in this transaction, he/she is regarded as having lost reputation and future exchange opportunities with all the members in the Guanxi network. For example, I have a friend named Tom, who is my colleague. I know that he may sell some low-quality products. But I do not worry about that possibility when he sells prod ucts to me because he recently asked me to help him with his job. If he does not provide good products and services to me, I will believe tha what he does is not repaying the favor, maintaining feelings, and does not follow the reciprocity rule between the two of us. If so, I will neve help him again and the existing Guanxi breaks up. Both of us know thi tacit rule and it should be strictly followed in our interactions. There fore, when a user makes a purchase decision. the effects of the reciprocal norm from Guanxi are all working together to guarantee them against potential risks in completing a transaction. Also, from the perspective of the seller, this strong reciprocal norm, high risk of network expulsion, and great desire for future benefits from Guanxi will constrain thei behavior and drive them to make more efforts to ensure a successfu transaction [69]. Therefore, in the engagement process, social media users may rely less on measuring the effects of trust towards the selle role This means that given the high reciprocity of Guanxi the rela: tionship between trust and engagement intention becomes weak [24, 29]. Thus, different levels of instrumental Guanxi conditions reflec more important boundary conditions on the relationship between trust and engagement behavior. Similarly, some prior studies have explored the negative moderating effects of Guanxi between trust and trust-related outcomes in the offline context. For example, in an offline B2B context. Yen et al. (2016) [24] argued that increasing Guanxi could significantly weaken the trust’s influence on financial performance. Following the unique effects of Guanxi and the prior research associated with this topic, this study extends Guanxi effects to the social commerce context and proposes that Guanxi has negative moderating effects be tween trust (towards the friend role and seller role) and social commerce engagement intention. As a result, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H3a. : Guanxi negatively moderates the relationship between trust towards seller role and social commerce engagement intention.

Similarly, the moral obligation of Guanxi also can provide a theo retical foundation to explain its moderating effects on the relationship between trust (towards friend role) and engagement intention. As dis cussed in the development of hypothesis H1c, trust towards friend role is built on the social image and reputation that is elaborately created from the social interactions in both offline and online contexts. It is a unidi rectional perception from a user to his/her friend role. In contrast, the Chinese concept of Guanxi social networks emerge from personal re lationships or personal agreements with mutual confirmation [27,63,71, 91]. For example, Li et al. (2019) [73] pointed out that in Guanxi net works where people know each other well, oral agreement and mutual assurance are emphasized in commercial transactions. In the decision-making process, the social media user will believe that he/she has to fulfill the moral obligations to his/her friend because the familiar Guanxi binds them with mutual feelings as friends. Even if he/she does not purchase directly from his/her friend. he/she is willing to perform the duty by sharing the information in his/her private social network. As friends, especially from the sentimental dimension of Guanxi, the per formance of a mutual moral obligation is the key to maintain a long-term

Guanxi relationship. According to Chen et al. (2013) [69], a Chinese Guanxi network connects all the members together to exchange infor mation and supports every member in getting access to exclusive re sources and thus develop a sense of belonging. A member’s reputation and social status are used to guarantee a promise merely through per sonal word of mouth or personal interaction [69]. In this case, if the social media user disregards or breaks his/her promises and obligations (such as not providing help and blind to the information) in the Guanxi networks, it will result in the loss of respect from this friend. The social media user will eventually worry about losing her/his social bonds with this friend, and the sustaining connection with all the members in the Guanxi network. Thus, several studies predict that the mutual assurance in a Guanxi network (rather than trust) appears to be more critical in the exchange relationship [78] and is usually viewed as being more stable [68,69,78]. This means, in a high Guanxi condition, social media users engagement behavior may rely less on the trustworthy condition of the roles that friends play. Even though a friend in the Guanxi network is not trustworthy enough to motivate the user to engage in the social com merce activities, the duty to perform the moral obligation of Guanxi will guarantee the engagement. Therefore, the trust’s (towards friend role) influence on engagement becomes weaker when their Guanxi condition is high. It means that different levels of sentimental Guanxi conditions reflect more important boundary conditions on the relationship between trust towards friend role and engagement behavior. Similarly, prior studies have explored the negative moderating effects of Guanxi be tween trust and trust-related outcomes in the offline context. For example, Liu et al. (2008) [29] explored that the relationships between trust and opportunistic behavior will decrease when the Guanxi is closer in offline marketing channels. As a result, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H3b. : Guanxi negatively moderates the relationship between trust towards friend role and social commerce engagement intention.

## 3.3.2. Positive moderating effects of guanxi in the trust-transfer process

Previous studies pointed out that Guanxi elements, such as mutual understanding, reciprocal favors, and relationship harmony are mani festations of the norm of reciprocity [31,68]. In the social commerce context, the trust-transfer process is based on the perceived associations between the dual roles in trust transfer theory [57,85,87,92]. When the level of the reciprocity of Guanxi is high, the moral obligation will make the users perceive that their friend’s behavior when in the seller role should match and remain the same as that in the friend role to maintain Guanxi and to obtain more benefits in the future. In this case, by perceiving the match between the dual roles, the association between the roles will be positively strengthened by such a high reciprocal norm [25,28,93]. In this case, social media users are more likely to believe that their perception of trust towards the “seller role” is associated with the trust towards the “friend role” [30,78]. Thus, in a high Guanxi reciprocity condition, users will be more likely to capture the trust re flected by the friend role in the social context and use it as a reference to develop trust towards the seller role in the commercial context. As a result, this strengthened association between the dual roles will make the trust-transfer process smoother. This association also can be strengthened because both of them regard the sincere feelings (in the sentimental dimension of Guanxi) as a solid tie between the dual roles. This is supported by Li et al. (2019) [73], who demonstrated that the trust transfer process improved under a developed Guanxi condition. The authors also proposed that “trust transfer and subsequent oppor tunities for transfer can be improved through trust conversion by deepening the ties bonded into a denser network (a high condition of Guanxi)”.

Furthermore, when the Guanxi between friends is high, social media users believe that the moral obligation mechanism is in place to guar antee that a friend's behavior in the seller role proceeds within the trustworthy context of the friend role. Therefore, users may perceive that this reciprocity mechanism protects them against potential risks – enhancing users’ confidence $[ 7 0 , 7 8 ]$ ]. With a stronger confidence level, users will be more likely to believe that the new unfamiliar “seller role” of her/his friend is related to the familiar “friend role” in the social context [37,81]. The association between the friend role and the seller role is connected and strengthened in this high confidence condition. This implies that, in a high Guanxi condition, the trust towards the seller role will depend more heavily on their trust toward the friend role that already exists in the social context. Both instrumental and sentimental dimensions of Guanxi can reflect important boundary conditions on the relationship between dual role-based trust. Therefore, based on this discussion, we propose that:

H3c. : Guanxi positively moderates the relationship between trust towards friend role and seller role.

## 4. Method

## 4.1. Research design

WeChat is one of the most popular social media platforms in Main land China, with one billion active monthly users [4]. It provides op portunities for users to share information and advertisements with friends, family, and others. “WeChat business” is popular and is becoming one of the most widely used social commerce platforms [4, 94]. WeChat is usually used by vendors to post information and adver tisements of products in their friendship circle or for a target audience in the private chat interface. As a result, WeChat was chosen as the main focus of this study.

An online survey was designed to collect data. The recall method was used in the survey. This technique has been used as a valid approach to collect perceptual data in the marketing and IS literature [22]. It can serve as a retrieval cue to trigger the memory of an experience. In the survey, the WeChat users were asked to recall their most recent trans action experience or a recent advertisement posted by a friend in WeChat. They were also asked to type this friend’s name in the textbox on the survey webpage. Then, they responded to the items regarding this friend. Respondents were instructed to respond to the questionnaire only if:(1) they had relevant prior engagement experiences with their friend, such as shopping and/or sharing commercial information from this friend, or (2) they had a friend who was selling products or/and posting advertisements on WeChat. We believe that the recall method used in this study encouraged the participants to provide their most accurate memories of their perceptions of this friend’s social image and commercial behavior on WeChat.

The questions in the survey were split into three parts. The first part of the survey collected data on Guanxi and trust towards friend role with the selected friend. We emphasized that the questions in this part of the survey were related to social context or daily life. In the second part of the survey, it was stressed that the questions were specifically related to “the selling context of this friend” on WeChat. Before the participants responded to these questions, we asked each of them to focus on the seller role when completing the questions, keeping in mind that Guanxi covers mutual understanding, reciprocal favors, and relationship har mony between them and their friends. In this part, data on trust towards seller role, role conflict, social commerce engagement intention, and some control variables related to the commercial context, such as commitment to the seller role, were collected. These steps aimed to accurately capture trust towards friend role and seller role that should be measured in their “relevant context”. Lastly, the third part of the survey was used to collect data on the demographic variables and other control variables.

## 4.2. Measurement

All the constructs used in this study have been validated by previous research. Some minor changes were made to ensure that the scales used here fitted the present research context. The items of the constructs were measured with seven-point Likert scales ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (7), as presented in Appendix 1.

The measurement of trust towards seller role was adopted from Ou et al. (2014) [31]. Before measuring role-based trust, the role separation phenome on social media was tested in a pilot study. Following the procedure suggested by Ou et al. (2014) [31], we completed the pilot study using a semi-structured interview protocol to guide a panel dis cussion. We wanted to provoke insightful ideas and thoughts on role separation in the group discussions. The interview and the panel dis cussion supported the view that Chinese WeChat users can recognize role separation, that is, their friends are in a friend role in the social context but they switch to a seller role in the commercial context. Spe cifically, trust towards the friend role assesses the user’s perception of the degree of trustworthiness of his/her friend in terms of personal characteristics in the social context. Finally, on the basis of this pilot study, we adapted six items from Ohanian (1990), Butler Jr (1991), and Leung et al. (2005) [62,63,64] to measure trust towards the friend role. Furthermore, the trust towards the seller role was measured with existing scales from Ou et al. (2014) and Colquitt et al. (2007) [31,60] covering the evaluation of trust in the seller role as a second-order construct encompassing the dimensions of integrity, benevolence, and ability. To ensure construct validity in our conceptualization, the items of role-based trust were carefully worded so that trust towards the friend role referred to the perception of the friend role in a social context and daily life in general, and the items of trust towards the seller role referred to the perception of the seller role in the commercial context (e. g., shopping and commercial transactions). As a result, all the items of role-based trust were used in further analysis. The measurement items for the Guanxi construct were adapted from Ou et al. (2014) [31]. Following Ou et al. (2014) [31], Guanxi is a second-order construct measured with three first-order factors: mutual understanding, recip rocal favors, and relationship harmony. Role conflict was measured with items adapted from Grayson (2007), Zhang et al. (2011), and Tarafdar et al. (2007) [20,21,41]. Following Zhang et al. (2014) and Chen et al. (2015) [10,11], social commerce engagement intention was measured.

With regard to control variables, we captured control variables known in the literature to affect trust and engagement intention. We controlled the effects of users’ characteristics based on previous studies that proved they might affect trust in seller role and behavior intention, such as age, education, annual income, number of contacts, and time length of WeChat use [31,85]. Studies have shown that past online purchase experiences and commitment of the seller affect the user’s trust and decision to engage online [22,31]. Thus, we also control the effects of past transactions and commitment. We also collected the data on time by which participants finished the survey and the IP address for contro purposes.

As the original items were in English, the instruments were first translated into Chinese and then back-translated to English. To ensure content validity, this process was performed by three professors who were proficient in both languages [28]. They collaborated to resolve any disagreements or inconsistencies in the translation. A pretest was also administered to 18 students who were WeChat users. The respondents were also contacted for a face-to-face interview so that their opinions on the questionnaire could be collected. After analyzing the respondents feedback, several minor revisions were made to refine the questionnaire.

## 5. Data analyses and results

To test the measurement model, construct reliability and validity were examined. As given in Table 6, the Cronbach’s alpha values of all the constructs were greater than the suggested threshold of 0.70 [95]. The result suggests that the measurement exhibits high internal con sistency. Following Hult et al. (2007) [96], the average variance extracted (AVE) of each construct exceeded the acceptable standard of 0.5 as a measure of convergent validity. Following Chan et al. (2019) [97], the factor analysis was performed and the results revealed that the item loadings were all above the suggested value of 0.7. In the cross loading of constructs, all of the items loaded well onto their own construct and poorly onto other constructs (shown in Appendix 2). Thus, all the results demonstrated convergent and discriminant validity. Taken together, the measurement model demonstrates satisfactory reliability, convergent, and discriminant validity.

We also tested for common method bias [98]. First, following Har man’s single-factor test, the analysis showed that the first factor accounted for 0.38, lower than 0.5, and that common method bias was likely not a serious concern. Second, we tested the collinearity of the constructs and found that the VIF values of all the latent constructs were lower than 3.3. This indicates that common method bias was not a threat to the validity of the findings. Third, to apply the marker-variable technique, we carefully identified variables that would not relate to the phenomena under investigation [99,100]. Therefore, following Podsakoff (2003), we gathered a marker variable (resentment) [99,101, 102]. The results show that the path loadings and model fit values are consistent with the original estimates (given in Appendix 3). Therefore, we conclude that common method bias is not a severe concern overall.

We performed hierarchical regression analyses to test the hypotheses [97,103]. Because three interaction terms were included in the equa tion, we standardized all variables to reduce the potential effects of multicollinearity. The technique of least squares was used with the control variables entered as a block in step 1 (model 1), followed by the main effects in step 2 (model 2), and the interaction and moderators in step 3 (model 3). To test the effects of the trust towards friend role on trust towards seller role, we ran a control effect model and then a main effect model by setting trust towards seller role as a dependent variable in the first stage. We set engagement intention as a dependent variable in the second stage to test the effects of the trust towards friend role and trust towards seller role on engagement intention. Table 7 summarizes the results of these analyses.

In the first stage, we first tested the control variables. Subsequently, we tested the main effects of the trust towards friend role on trust to wards seller role. Specifically, trust towards friend role $( \beta = 0 . 3 0 2 , p <$ 0.001) predicts trust towards seller role, supporting Hypothesis 1c. Furthermore, role conflict $( \beta = - 0 . 1 9 8 , p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ negatively moderates the relationship between trust towards friend role and seller role, whereas Guanxi $( \beta = 0 . 0 9 8 , p < 0 . 0 1 )$ positively moderates the relation ship between trust towards friend role and seller role; therefore, sup porting Hypothesis 2 and Hypothesis 3c. The model explains 70.9% of the variance for engagement intention.

In the second stage, trust towards seller role significantly enhanced social commerce engagement intention, with path coefficients of 0.259 (H1a: p < 0.001). Hence, hypothesis H1a was supported. Trust towards friend role significantly influenced social commerce engagement intention, with path coefficients of 0.224 (H1b: $p < 0 . 0 0 1 $ ). Thus, H1b was also supported. Guanxi significantly negatively moderated the relationship between trust towards seller role and social commerce engagement intention $( \mathrm { H 3 a } \colon \beta = - 0 . 2 6 1 , p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ , but not significant between trust towards friend role and social commerce engagement intention (H3b: $\beta = 0 . 0 3 5 , p > 0 . 0 5 ) ;$ ; thus, supporting hypothesis H3a and not supporting H3b. Among the control variables, shopping expe rience, number of friends, and income demonstrated significant effects on social commerce engagement intention. In sum, the R<sup>2</sup> scores for all the variables explained 65.7% of the variance, and the high factor loadings yielded an adequate goodness-of-fit for the overall dual-role trust model in this study.

## 6. Discussion and conclusion

## 6.1. Discussion of findings

The goal of this study is to examine how role-based trust interacts with Guanxi to facilitate social media users’ engagement behavior with their friends. In contrast to measuring trust as an overall perception of the trusting party in previous studies, this study proposes a dual-role based trust model for exploring the effects of trust mechanisms within an individual’s multiple roles in depth. In addition, Guanxi is a more relevant concept for developing an in-depth understanding of social commerce in China. Our empirical research reveals that Guanxi has a paradoxical positive and negative moderating effect on the trust mech anism. Our examination of its role in relationships involving role conflict and engagement intention reveals several important research findings.

The result of H1a and H1b confirms that both users trust towards the friend role and the seller role positively contributes to his/her social commerce engagement intention. This results of H1a and H1b are consistent with previous trust research (e.g., Lin et al. (2017), Li et al. (2017), Chen et al. (2015), and Chong et al. (2018) [6,9,10,28]), revealing that trust plays an important role in consumer’s engagement behavior. Furthermore, Hypothesis H1c shows that trust towards the friend role helps to build trust towards the seller role. Compared to H1a, the result of H1b is significant and can distinguish the effects of dual role-based trust. The regression results of H1a and H1b indicate that both trust towards the friend role and seller role are important in Fr2Fr social commerce. When H1c is considered, H1a, H1b, and H1c taken together indicate that trust towards friend role in social context differs from trust towards seller role in the commercial context, especially two types of trust can transfer. More importantly, the three hypotheses show a direct and an indirect way that role-based trust mechanism influences consumer behavior: the direct effect from trust towards friend role (H1b), and the indirect effect from trust towards friend role that is mediated by trust towards seller role (H1c and H1a).

The result of H2 demonstrates the value of role conflict that extends the understanding of role interaction in role theory. As Grayson (2007) [20] suggested, role-theoretic analysis helps to identify the “properties” of different roles and helps to understand how roles differ and affect in terms of the decision-making process in a business relationship. As a result, the finding of the moderating effect of role conflict contributes to a better understanding of role-based trust mechanism in social com merce. While previous studies on role theory are rather focused on the offline context, it appears that the online context has made role inter action more relevant to both commercial and social contexts [21,41,46, 51].

The results of H3a and H3c are also supported. The result shows that Guanxi has a paradoxical positive and negative moderating effect with the trust mechanism (in the trust-building process and engaging pro cess). By drawing on the Guanxi perspective, the result explicitly spec ifies the Guanxi context as an important boundary condition to assess how trust influences engagement intention. With this new insight, this study explains the finding that trust has a less important effect on engagement intention if Guanxi is supplving a substitution. The results of three moderating hypotheses indicate that both sentimental and instrumental dimensions of Guanxi have import interacting effects with trust. This study also indicates that trust towards friend role affects more on trust towards seller role under a high Guanxi condition. Moreover, in contrast to our expectations, the result of H3b is not supported. This may be explained by the same social context in which Guanxi and trust (to wards friend role) coexist. Trust towards friend role and Guanxi may not fiercely compete in the same social context and eventually make the effects of substitution nonsignificant. Some previous studies also pointed out that the substitution of Guanxi is not significant when the trust mainly measures goodwill in social interaction [29]. In short, this article highlights a clearer boundary of Guanxi definition and attempt to pro vide new insights and direction for future research on the trust mecha nism and Guanxi mechanism, specifically for social commerce.

## 6.2. Implications for theory

## 6.2.1. Implications of trust based on role theory

Our study makes several important theoretical contributions in terms of trust based on role theory. First, this study contributes to trust liter ature from a role-based perspective. While the phenomenon of multiple roles is becoming more latent in Fr2Fr social commerce, few studies have explored this in the trust building process. As Pavlou et al. (2004) [57] pointed out, the existing literature has largely ignored the context in which trust is built and affected (e.g., institution-based trust in institutional structures [57], individual level trust in buy-seller com mercial context [28], and community-based trust in social context [10]). Therefore, the existing literature has presented an oversimplification of the context in which trust affects. They mainly build trust as an overall perception of the trusting party. To narrow the gap, by drawing on role theory, our findings explicitly conceptualized trust towards the friend role and the seller role to advance our understanding of the specific dyadic social-commercial context. This also contributes to e-commerce research from a role perspective. Perhaps the reason why previous re searchers rarely examined different social roles in online marketplaces is because such different roles were assumed to be nonexistant in most single online transactions between unfamiliar people [31]. Also, the activation and separation of social roles as the basis of role-based trust only happen when there are two different contexts (e.g., the friend role in the social context and the seller role in the commercial context). Even in the social media context, previous studies rarely considered and distinguished these different contexts and unique roles (e.g., Lin et al. (2017) and Chong et al. (2018) [6,28]). Therefore, role-based trust adds to the trust literature by identifying important contextual conditions from a role perspective.

Second, this study also contributes to trust literature by showing a direct and an indirect effect of dual-role-based trust on user’s engage ment behavior. According to Gefen et al. (2012) [84], prior literature rarely distinguishes the boundaries of trust under their premise (e.g., different dimensions of trust are sustained by different conditions). Chen et al. (2015) [10] also pointed that trust towards different entities should play different unique mechanisms on consumer behavior. Therefore, it is necessary to distinguish the effects of different trusts on individual behavior. To narrow this gap, this study contributes to trust research by testing the direct effect from trust towards friend role and the indirect effect mediated by trust towards seller role. Based on a comprehensive review of the trust and role theory literature, this article offers an initial theoretical attempt to extend the novel concept of the role-based trust mechanism to social commerce. Furthermore, social commerce development is closely related to social network expansion. In the initial stage, social networks are built by an acquaintance’s recommendation or word of mouth (WOM) among adjacent nodes, and then they expand and form different multiple layers of social network [58,66,67]. Therefore, exploring the trust building process in the initial stage is the primary foundation of social network expansion. As a result, this study contributes to existing research by extending the initial trust building study from a role perspective in the private social network context.

Third, this study also extends trust transfer theory by adopting a rolerelated mechanism (social character). Most previous studies only used trust transfer theory to explain superficially “how trust can transfer from one entity to another”, ignoring to acknowledge the connection between these two entities (e.g. Chen et al. (2015) and Chen et al. (2015) [10, 85]). A key point regarding trust transfer theory is the relationship be tween proof source and target or a related circumstance. To elaborate the transfer theory, this study extends the cognition of trust transfer by adding role-related mechanism (social character) to explore the rela tionship between two roles. Specifically, this study argues that trust towards the friend role in the ex ante social context is a potential driver of the success of trust towards the seller role in the ex post commercial context on social media with the trust transfer theory.

Fourth, this study also contributes to the role theory by testing rolebased perspective in social commerce. This study proves that the perspective of roles provides a significant impact on social commerce outcomes. More importantly, this article contributes to the role theory by suggesting that the value of social roles and role-based trust mechanisms should be highlighted in social commerce markets. This study also in creases a better understanding of role interaction. It is worthwhile examining other possible role-based factors $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . } } \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g . } } ,$ , role conflict) that can foster segmented dual-role-based trust in online markets. It is an impor tant new implication for theory because role conflict is easily overlooked but deeply influences the role-based decision-making process.

## 6.2.2. Implications of the paradoxical moderating effects of guanxi

Finally, this study also contributes to the literature on Guanxi. Whereas several prior studies have shown that Guanxi and trust may be the antecedent or outcome of each other among buyer-seller relation ship online and offline [28,30,31], there is limited understanding of how the Guanxi mechanism might affect the trust mechanism in transactional decisions [29,79]. To narrow this research gap, this study contributes to the Guanxi theory by providing empirical evidence that Guanxi moder ates the relationship between trust and behavior intention. This finding echoes previous studies on the analysis of the differences between Guanxi and trust [25,31,78]. Moreover, our study uncovers the para doxical moderating effects of Guanxi [22,85]. This finding is aligned with Fang et al. (2014) [22] and Chen et al. (2015) [85]’s studies of institutional mechanisms. Moreover, consistent with the Guanxi litera ture (e.g. Yen et al. (2016), Xin et al. (1996), Liu et al. (2008), and Luo et al. (2007) [24,27,29,79]), our study reveals that Guanxi negatively moderates the relationship between trust towards seller role and social commerce engagement intention. Therefore, this article helps us to better understand Guanxi as a substitution of trust. Especially, a mixture of instrumental and sentimental logics of Guanxi contributes to a better understanding of the interaction relationship with trust. Our study also shows that Guanxi positively moderates the relationship between trust towards friend role and seller role. This is in accordance with Li et al (2019) [73], demonstrating that trust transfer and trust conversion can strengthen the members’ connection in a developing Guanxi network. Taken together, our study enriches the Guanxi theory by suggesting that the effects of the Guanxi construct are not steady and constant in the overall decision-making process, especially affecting both social and commercial contexts. This paradoxical moderating effect also contrib utes the Guanxi literature from the traditional B2G, B2B, and B2C re lationships to more complex social media Fr2Fr online marketplaces.

Furthermore, as Li et al. (2019) [73] pointed, the boundary of Guanxi should exclude blood-based family ties, close kinship ties, and stranger ties. This conception is consistent with the approach taken in our study that the friends on social media are in a familiar relationship, so that Guanxi can be governed by a mixture of both instrumental and senti mental logic. It is different from previous studies that focused on Guanxi in a short time between strangers, such as Lin et al. (2017) and Chong et al. (2018) [6,28]. Therefore, this study further contributes to Guanxi research by making a clearer boundary of its conception in a familiar relationship on social media.

## 6.3. Implications for practice

In addition to the aforementioned theoretical contributions, this study also has important practical implications. It is useful for online marketing managers who want to generate social media strategies in emerging social commerce markets to motivate customers’ engagement behavior and sustained use of the platforms. This study shows that we can understand trust from a role-based perspective. Trust towards friend role can be transferred to trust towards seller role and finally influence social commerce engagement intention. It suggests that social media platforms, such as WeChat, Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram, could capitalize on the unique characteristics of the different social roles that users play. Designers should distinguish different systems and settings to match the corresponding roles. Indeed, the existence of the friend role between familiar friends helps social media users to easily accept another kind of behavior of their friends, such as new transactional behavior in the commercial context. This role-based mechanism can help the designer of social media platforms to implement new functions for users and then obtain business revenue in a short time. What is noteworthy is that the effects of social roles are two-fold. Role conflict may produce negative effects in multi-role contexts. This suggests that the platforms cannot add complex functions and overlap different con texts all the time, such as excessive commercial advertising in the social context. If these functions have to be added for economic benefits, functionalities aiming to reduce role conflicts or intrusiveness should be included in the design. This rationale highlights the critical role of social media design that social media platforms should mainly focus on developing more convenient social functions to facilitate the coexistence of different roles and corresponding contexts. In summary, these find ings support the development of profit-seeking strategies and sound the alarm to avoid increasing inconsistencies and conflicting app function across different scenarios.

This study also shows that Guanxi plays a significant role in social media users’ sharing and shopping behavior. It suggests that to sustain commercial transactions and usage on social media. building Guanxi is important as a key marketing strategy for platforms. Our study shows that Guanxi facilitates the effects from trust towards friend role to seller role because of the benefits of the familiar social context on social media, but it has negative moderating effects in the relationship between trust towards seller role and behavior intention. This suggests that using Guanxi can make the users switch easily and quickly between different contexts, but produce a slow or even stagnant effect in the outcome process. Notably, exerting the functions of Guanxi requires a more sophisticated approach by social media platforms. Designers can trace the relationship data and classify friends at different levels to facilitate the appropriate in teractions. Moreover. designers are required to provide a homelike environment or systems for building Guanxi based on the deep attitudinal trio of constructs of mutual understanding, reciprocal favors, and rela tionship harmony. This study also supports previous studies that some times, under extremely high Guanxi conditions, trust is no longer important to trust-related behavior as Guanxi will provide connections as substitutes for formal institutional support [27]. Therefore, in the design of commercial systems on social media platforms, designers can add some interfaces to show more social information that generated from the user's friend in the private networks, such as how many real friends support and endorse this user. This type of Guanxi's information may be more accurate and reliable than the business information from strangers. Furthermore, this study also has practical implications for other popular social plat forms, such as Tiktok, Instagram, Kuaishou, and so on. Even though Guanxi is a traditional Chinese concept, recent studies in the social media research field have begun adopting Guanxi to different platforms and cultural contexts. This reinforces the view point that Guanxi and other concepts (e.g., trust and dual role) are universal and they still work under the same mechanisms in similar social contexts. For example, Lee et al. (2o05) [30] point out that Gugnxi can be universal because some nuances of Guanxi are intrinsically related to human behavior, though the dem onstrations of affection or instrument may differ across cultures. This car bring implications for practice in different social media platforms and countries with unique cultures. First, from an instrumental perspective of the Guanxi relationship, some studies suggested that the social media platform can design reciprocal systems, such as the “red envelope” function and virtual gift that allows friends to express congratulations and exchange favors on the platform [36,81]. Indeed, it is hard to mention instrumental issues in a sincere affectionate relationship, but monetary incentive and reciprocity can enhance the relationship between social media users. Second, from a sentimental perspective of the Guanxi rela tionship, the relationships between users in other social media platforms are usually at a low-to-medium level, such as fans-celebrities relationship on Twitter or Instagram. This kind of relationship should be deemed as an illusory para-social relationship [105]. Even though it is different from Guanxi, which is mutually confirmed by familiar friends, there is no doubt that the Guanxi sentimental perspective can provide practical inspiration of how to promote the relationship and shorten the psychological dis tance between users. For example, platforms can design more rich communication channels and create kinship atmosphere. A similar identity and same interest are the foundation to build sincere feelings and Guanxi relationship. Such enhancement of relationship will make users feel a deeper emotional attachment to the platform and increase the user’s loyalty. Therefore, based on this general applicability, this research may have particular referential value when studying not only the per sonal Guanxi of Chinese consumers, but also those with different plat forms, cultural, and ethnic backgrounds.

## 6.4. Limitations and future research

While this research makes a positive contribution, it also has several limitations that open up interesting opportunities for future research. First, the empirical study in this article was conducted in a Chinese WeChat context. It is important to acknowledge that we examined the impacts of interpersonal Guanxi and role-based trust with Chinese WeChat users. To investigate the stability of the results, we would welcome studies that examine the effects of Guanxi and trust in the so cial commerce field on other platforms, such as Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram. Second, the research model examines social commerce behavior only from the customers’ perspective. This may limit the uni versality of the model to discern diverse perspectives on social media. Future research could explore the role-based trust model by using a double survey that can match the information reported by buyers and sellers. Lastly, we suggest that future research use a longitudinal study to test the effects of Guanxi and role-based trust, as personal relationship between users and their friends may either remain static or change. Future studies could attempt to test these effects in an evolutionary process in an experiment and explore the different influences of these constructs in the e-commerce or social commerce context.
