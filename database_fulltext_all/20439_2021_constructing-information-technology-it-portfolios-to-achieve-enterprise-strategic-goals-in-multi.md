## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: IT portfolio management (ITPM IT portfolio attribute Data envelopment analysis (DEA)

## A B S T R A C T

This study aims to develop a new methodology in IT Portfolio Management (ITPM) to address the impact of portfolio priorities on IT strategies in a multi-business unit firm. The methodology includes a new ITPM model and uses simulated scenarios to overcome limitations due to insufficient real-world organizational IT portfolio data. The weight scores generated by the proposed ITPM model can enable the firm to create a rational viewpoint to improve its IT investment efficiency while establishing IT priorities to achieve enterprise strategic goals. Such an approach would enable firms to reduce their reliance on CIO intuition in guiding firms’ technology portfolios.

## 1. Introduction

Spending on information technology (IT) forms a significant portion of overall capital investment in most firms [54]. The business value of investments in Information Systems (IS) has been considered as a major research topic in the IS field [63], and many top-performing enterprises are successful due to their implementation of effective IT governance to support their strategies [[19],[35],[79]]. However, though IT in vestments in large multi-business firms have been shown to contribute to strategic and financial obiectives. the impact of IT strategies on IT portfolio priorities has not been extensively studied (e.g., [[15],[39], [40],[69]]). Furthermore, it remains equivocal how a large multi-business unit firm (e.g., Fortune 500 firms) allocates its IT re sources efficiently in IT portfolios to achieve enterprise strategic goals. Hence, the motivation for this research is to open the black box of resource allocation in ITPM in a large multi-business unit firm to provide direction for CIOs (e.g., IT executives) to better allocate IT resources that align with their enterprise strategic goals (e.g., [[50],[78]]).

To contextualize the importance of effective IT investment decisionmaking in a large multi-business unit firm, we consider a few real-world examples. Wal-Mart centralizes the planning, analysis, and prioritiza tion of the IT portfolio to ensure that projects are aligned with the company’s strategic goals and funds them by channeling all IT projects through the CIO office [45]. Similarly, several large multi-business companies, including Scoular, Harrah’s Entertainment, DTE Energy, and Carpenter Technology Corporation, use a centralized portfolio management office to drive IT investment decisions across the enter prises [33]. Given such moves toward raising the importance and centralization of IT portfolio decision-making, there is a distinct need to develop quantitative methodologies that can be used to assist enterprise executives in establishing IT priorities. We propose one such method ology to prioritize IT initiatives to achieve the firm’s strategic goals in a multi-business unit firm. An IT portfolio is defined as a catalog of IT projects used to manage the collection of IT investments according to a firm’s strategy [[1],[20],[46]]. We seek to answer the following two research questions: (1) How decision-makers might measure the effi ciency of IT resource allocation in IT portfolio compositions across different business units in a multi-business unit firm?, and (2) How decision makers might prioritize IT portfolios that align with enterprise strategic objectives to improve the IT investment efficiency in a multi-business unit firm?

Our main contributions towards ITPM research can be summarized as follows. First, our research characterizes IT project portfolios using theoretically pertinent portfolio attributes: project strategy type, project size, budgeted cost, risk, and benefit. Second, the process of portfolio choice requires constant balancing and rebalancing that is contingent on a set of diverse IT portfolio attributes. We generate an “efficiency score” using our proposed ITPM model, also known as the Data Envelopment Analysis/Parallel (DEA/P) model, which can be used for appropriate measurement of the efficiency of a given IT portfolio resource allocation. More importantly, since many IT investment decisions rely heavily on intuition and a leap of faith, the weight scores we generate through the proposed ITPM model can provide CIOs with guidance reallocating re sources to better prioritize IT portfolios to advance a firm’s strategic goals in a multi-business firm. Third, our proposed methodology in corporates a simulation approach to generate various IT portfolio sce narios for investment consideration and overall IT planning by the CIO office [49], which can serve as decision aids for senior practitioners.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the relevant studies in the ITPM area; Section 3 includes the description of the research approach and the Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) Model. In Section $^ { 4 , }$ we develop a new ITPM model that applies the DEA/Parallel (DEA/P) model to the ITPM context and demonstrate our proposed model using a hypothetical example. Section 5 summarizes the findings and points out future directions for ITPM research.

## 2. Literature review

A firm’s resource allocation process involves a diverse set of stake holders who compete for capital investment opportunities across the hierarchy of the firm [78]. IT can be considered as a strategic organi zational resource [72]. The IT function in an organization is designed to support the firm’s IT needs efficiently and is expected to contribute to the fulfillment of the firm’s strategic goals [47]. While the organiza tional structure is one aspect of the IT strategy of the firm, the main components of IT strategy include the role that IT plays in the firm, technical competencies, and IT governance [[26],[27],[47]]. In this regard, the strategic alignment of IT is viewed as the fit between the IT strategy and business strategy that occurs through a series of IT gover nance processes (e.g., [[65],[79]]). Oh and Pinsonneault [55] and Gerow et al. [24] stated that the strategic alignment of IT refers to the level of alignment of the priorities, goals, and objectives of both IT and the business it supports. Henderson and Venkatraman $[ [ 2 6 ] , [ 2 7 ] ]$ highlight various aspects of alignment between IT strategy and business strategy through several practical examples. The alignment of IT in vestments with strategic enterprise goals may enable business flexibility to address abrupt demand changes and provide valuable options for the company [75].

While attempting to enhance this alignment of IT investments with enterprise strategic goals to improve the performance of multi-business firms, technology leaders and business executives are under constant pressure to balance various priorities and appease different stake holders. For instance, there is constant pressure to reduce costs and in crease the value created from their firm's IT resources [[29],[72]]. Due to such pressures, the strategic alignment of IT has often been considered a critical challenge faced by enterprise executives [11]. To shed light on this top management concern, Benbya et al. [8] summarized a stream of IS studies and pointed out that the strategic alignment of IT is regarded as a process of constant adjustments between business and IT strategy across multiple organizational dimensions as well as organizational levels to achieve better organizational performance. Accordingly, the studies increasingly indicate that the strategic alignment of IT is significantly associated with the overall performance of a firm $[ [ 8 ] , [ 1 1 ] ,$ [30],[76]]. As part of overall IT governance, the IT portfolio can be considered the tangible manifestation of an enterprise IT plan, and IT portfolio management (ITPM) ensures that an enterprise’s IT in vestments and resources can improve its strategies [[25],[70]].

Generally, portfolio management is the centralized management of one or more project portfolios to achieve enterprise strategic goals [60], and project portfolio management involves consideration of numerous attributes $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . } } \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g . } } ,$ benefits, costs, and risk) and acts as a bridge between high-level strategy at one end and tactical-level planning and manage ment at the other end [[15],[17]]. Many senior executives have expressed apprehensions about how to create the best overall portfolio of IT investments, and the current trend shows that firms need to govern IT investments at the portfolio level [77], as recommended by Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies (COBIT). In line with insights from IT governance literature, ITPM emphasizes the need to align the portfolio with organizational strategic goals. Implementing ITPM in organizations requires creating value through several internal processes, including operation management, customer management, innovation, human resource management, and regulatory issues [[39], [46]]. ITPM plays an essential role in enabling value creation from IT investments [[32],[59],[61]]. Extending the research of Weill and Aral [80], we define ITPM as the process of managing a collection of IT projects to accomplish a firm’s strategy [[1],[20]], and IT project management is the main tactical level through which IT activity trans lates into usable technology in the enterprise (e.g., [[13],[22]]). While this narrative might suggest treating ITPM analogous to the manage ment of financial instruments and assets, there are certain limitations when we apply this approach. For example, a firm is not generally able to sell or buy an IT project in any type of marketplace [[13],[28]]. Software projects and advanced IT of computer-aided design (CAD)/ computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) may encode intellectual property and create unique firm resources (e.g., [[13],[76]]). Compared with financial services and new project development studies [14], ITPM lacks publicly available historical data to assist IT project portfolio decisions. To better understand how IT can deliver critical services throughout the entire organization $[ [ 6 2 ] , [ 8 3 ] ]$ , the analysis of IT value at the portfolio level can serve as a bridge connecting IT resource allocation at the project level to the performance of the set of investments at the firm level.

Currently, there are a limited number of ITPM studies. Firms, espe cially those with multiple business units, need to develop proper methodologies to select and manage their IT investment portfolios [14]. In response to this need, the objective of the current study is to produce the objective weight scores to demonstrate the strategic priorities driven by IT portfolios in a multi-business firm. This study also accounts for numerous IT portfolios in relation to technology and business opportu nities $[ [ 2 9 ] , [ 5 7 ] ]$ . Thus, business and IT executives can craft the most appropriate IT portfolio to achieve their enterprise strategic goals. The IT portfolio involves different classes of investments [80], and IT in vestment allocations across different business units are implemented through business-unit level IT portfolios to achieve strategic business objectives. Consistent with the suggestions of Aral and Weill [2], Chiang and Nunez [13], and Huang [29], each IT portfolio may be characterized by numerous IT portfolio attributes, including project strategy type, project size, budgeted cost, risk, and benefit. Specific attributes of the portfolio have impacts on business processes and related applications [51]. Due to diverse portfolio selection criteria in practice, additional IT portfolio attributes that are not listed in this section may be considered in the idiosyncratic IT investment decision-making process of each firm.

Projects being planned or under development are likely to vary based on their size, strategic implications, risks, and technologies [13]. A firm with experience in strategic planning of IT will endeavor to carefully select IT projects to reach its organizational goals [34]. Further, along the lines of Weill and Aral [80], firms can consider IT investments as an IT portfolio to better assist their management team in matching IT in vestments to overall strategic objectives [[74],[75]]. Specifically, IT portfolios may be categorized using two distinct approaches. First, they might be classified as one of four IT investment classifications: (1) infra structure investments, which support the shared IT services and plat forms used by multiple applications, (2) transactional investments, which are mainly used to reduce costs or increase output for the same cost, (3) information investments, which provide information for various decision-making purposes, and (4) strategic investments, which can be specifically leveraged to gain competitive advantage. Alterna tively, to extend the viewpoint from Weill and Aral [80], and also in line with key stakeholders at our research sites, IT projects can also be categorized based on their strategic focus (or IT project strategy type): (1) Compulsory (CM) projects address critical compliance or financial reporting issues (these IT projects receive priority in terms of funding or resources), (2) Operating Margin (OM) projects focus on return on in vestment by allowing the business to do its existing processes faster or at lower cost, and (3) Long Term Growth (LTG) projects usually add new capabilities for the business (these IT projects have a significant impact on the existing business processes). The four IT investment classifica tions [80], and our three IT project strategy types, are noted in Table 1.

In practice, in addition to our classifications by investment type and project strategy type, there could be alternative idiosyncratic concep tualizations. For example, a portfolio might adopt project size-based classification, where a certain portfolio can be considered as a compo sition of numerous small IT projects, a small number of large projects, or some combination of both. Similarly, firms may have a large number of different projects with significant variations in project size, making their management more challenging [22].

Any assessment of a portfolio depends on an understanding of the inputs (resources), outputs (business outcomes), and the associated risks to the portfolio. On the input side, budgeted costs are highly related to a firm’s invested resources. Regarding IT portfolio decision making, an IT budget can include either the planned capital for acquiring IT-related resources [[16],[82]] or the planned support to address IT-driven ser vices [[23],[82],[84]]. Thus, a budgeted cost is a key attribute in planning organizational IT investment allocation. This research mainly addresses the following two critical variables of budgeted cost attri butes: (1) Capital Expenditure: According to Shim et al. [66], a capital expenditure budget is the anticipated level of monetary spending that is required to meet the nonfinancial manager’s objectives and allow the division or department to function properly. In the context of the IT budget, capital expenditures include hardware, equipment, purchases of packaged software, and application development [7]. (2) Operating Expense: Operating expense refers to spending by a firm to turn in ventory into throughput [64], including both fixed assets and depreci ated assets [7]. In the ITPM context, operating expense is the total of all IT costs that a firm faces as a result of performing its normal, day-to-day IT operations.

Meanwhile, the risk is perceived as the possibility of additional cost or loss due to the choice of alternatives [30]. According to Symons $[ 7 0 ] ,$ risks associated with IT are often the same as risks to the business, including the resiliency of systems from outages as well as the risks associated with project failures. In particular, the risk is the possibility of an unfavorable outcome of the final project deliverable $[ [ 3 0 ] , [ 4 6 ] ]$ and its financial consequence [58]. A superior IT portfolio produces the greatest investment benefits without exceeding a planned level of in vestment risk [[6],[77]]. Regarding IT projects, both technological novelty and project complexity contribute to the overall technical complexity, associated risk, and challenges to project completion [[73], [81]].

Project benefits can be regarded as the financial or operational im pacts of the project after the utilization of the firm’s resources and may be offset by losses when IT investments fail to deliver the promised benefits [41]. This research mainly addresses the following two critical benefit variables: (1) Expected Return: Expected returns are uncertain ex-ante but they may be intangible ex-post for most assets [31], espe cially products of an IT portfolio. (2) Cost Savings: Firms use multiple mechanisms to achieve cost savings including a common platform for multiple products to improve economies of scale, pooling to reduced inventory holding costs against demand uncertainty, and investments in technologically advanced production equipment [43].

## 3.2. Model description

Decision patterns can be identified and analyzed in a systematic approach to avoid the pitfalls of organizational decision-making processes that are highly complex and unstructured $\left[ [ 1 0 ] , \left[ 4 2 \right] \right]$ ]. Since IT has been recognized as a backbone of operational activities [3], the core responsibilities of ITPM are IT project selection and the optimiza tion of IT resource allocation [14]. Therefore, our research approach is designed to facilitate the CIO office’s IT investment decision-making process while considering various IT portfolios to fulfill enterprise strategic goals.

To ground our research in the practice of ITPM, the research approach includes conversations with business and IT executives, model development, and simulation. Fig. 1 shows that the CIO office identifies a firm’s strategic goals and begins with an assessment of available IT resources that represent the choice set of potential IT initiatives. The firm implements ITPM that aligns with the firm’s strategic goals to derive expected benefits and capture the simultaneous considerations of business units regarding their IT portfolio investment. We summarize numerous IT portfolio attributes (i.e., project strategy type, project size, budgeted cost, risk, and benefit) in the Literature Review section. Input variables include capital expenditure, operating expense and risk; output variables include expected return and cost savings. Both input and output variables may vary in project strategy type and project size.

In the construction and testing of this new model, we collaborated with a Fortune 50 firm located in the Midwestern United States and obtained access to the firm’s IT project portfolio data. In addition to empirical IT project portfolio data, we used Monte Carlo simulations to build numerous scenarios. The hypothetical example demonstrates how to use the proposed ITPM model to measure the efficiency of IT resource allocation in IT portfolio compositions across different business units and establish IT priorities aligned with enterprise strategic goals. Finally, our research approach provides insight into the effects of various IT portfolio attributes on portfolio performance and the weigh scores generated by the proposed ITPM model enable a firm to better achieve enterprise strategic goals.

Ayabakan et al. [3] found that the Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA)-based approach can be used to capture the tradeoffs between a given set of resources (inputs) and expected return or cost savings (outputs) involved in the measurement of IT-enabled production capa bilities. In line with Ayabakan et al. [3], input variables in our IT in vestment context include, but are not limited $^ { \mathrm { t o , } }$ the following: capital expenditure, operating expense, risk, labor cost, material cost, and IT spending. Furthermore, output variables in the IT investment context include, but are not limited to, the following: expected return, cost-saving, cycle time, inventory turnover, on-time delivery, and acceptance rate. To further emphasize the link between the two pro posed IT portfolio attributes (i.e., project strategy type and project size) and the relevant variables in this study, a cluster of input and output variables are shown in Table 4a and Table 4b.

Superior firm performance can be realized through leveraging IT resources to develop firm-specific processes [[3],[52],[53]]. Compared to other production models, Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA), origi nally proposed by Charnes, Cooper and Rhodes [12], affords the con current analysis of a complex mix of relevant attributes of multiple Decision-Making Units (DMUs) [83]. Because the DEA model is a non-parametric and linear fractional programming model, there is no need to include explicit mathematical forms between a given set of re sources (inputs) and strategic objectives (outputs). In this regard, motivated by the non-linear relationships between inputs and outputs, which is common in IT investment [71], the key function of the DEA model is to produce an optimal combined ratio of all the inputs to out puts between 0 and 1, also known as an efficiency score [[4],[5]]. Hence, when the inputs and outputs of IT investments in a portfolio are considered as part of the firm’s production process, we aim to develop the DEA-based model that can obtain precise estimates of the nature and complexity of the relation for IT investments in the ITPM context [[3], [71]].

Maxi mizing business value requires linking the portfolio problem to the challenge of enterprise resource allocation [21]. Hence, our study can be regarded as an extension of prior studies [[3],[71],[80]] to develop a new model based on the features of the DEA model that can combine various IT portfolio attributes to understand organizational perfor mance, as shown in Table 5. Furthermore, the model generates a slack score $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ slack variable), which can represent the level of resource utilization for a given IT portfolio. To improve the efficiency of orga nizational units, the slack variables represent potential further increases in output variables or reduction in input variables. The slack scores can be used as an indication for the leftover portions of inefficient organi zational units. Identification of slack can benefit managers seeking to achieve economies of scale and also illuminate opportunities that exist for better resource utilization.

The general parallel DEA model was developed with the fundamental assumption that a production system operates with multiple indepen dent processes; thus, system and process efficiencies are determined in one linear fraction model. Specifically, while considering, simulta neously, the combined effect of various organizational units within the firm, the parallel DEA model can decompose a complex system into multiple separated processes through the concept of the parallel pro duction system [[36],[37]], which addresses one set of parallel objec tives in the organization $( \mathbf { e . g . }$ , research and teaching in an academic institution).

An enterprise usually consists of various business units, and each independent business unit may have its strategic priority in terms of enterprise IT-driven strategic goals. These strategic objectives can be pursued through the set of ongoing IT projects, the IT project portfolio. To address IT resource allocations in IT portfolios for a multi-business unit firm, this research extends the parallel DEA model [36,37,38] to propose a new ITPM model that applies the DEA/Parallel (DEA/P) model to the ITPM context. The proposed model represents a centralized approach, from the CIO office perspective, to allocate IT resources across multi-business units (multi-divisions) within a firm, and we conceptu ally illustrate three main components of the proposed model in Fig. 2: (1) the ability to manage the diverse strategic objectives of multiple business units, (2) the consideration of the IT project portfolio to realize strategic objectives, and (3) the assessment of an IT portfolio comprised of a set of IT projects.

We next extend the parallel DEA model to develop a new (revised) ITPM model that applies the DEA/P framework to the ITPM context. This represents a setting where a multi-business unit firm operates with multiple independent business units, and each business unit can distribute its IT resources into different IT (project) portfolios via a parallel approach to achieve strategic objectives as shown in Fig. 2. By following the optimization method of DEA/P enumerated in Fig. 3, the business unit level portion shows how the efficiency score $( E ^ { B U } )$ is maximized subject to resource constraints. More details about defini tions of variables in the DEA/P model are shown in Table 2.

## 5. Conclusions

Overall, our results demonstrate the importance and value of taking a central, enterprise-level view of managing the IT portfolio. Our ITPM model can help firms determine where specifically they could invest their scarce resources. Doing so requires a coordinated effort across business units. The results also suggest that the five portfolio attributes, which are employed in the model, affect overall performance. This finding validates, at least through a simplified setting, the value of considering a set of portfolio attributes in assessing the performance of an IT portfolio. Finally, we observe that each business unit must match its IT portfolio to the specific objectives of the business unit to achieve the best performance. This finding suggests that the idea of matching the IT portfolio to the business objectives is a useful perspective for man aging IT investment

The IT portfolio data in our study varies across the following attri butes: (1) Project Strategy Type, (2) Project Size, (3) Budgeted Cost, (4) Risk, and (5) Benefit. Our analysis, using the proposed ITPM model, would enable the CIO office to measure the efficiency of IT resource allocations across business units and generate objective weight scores (the extent to which the business unit’s portfolio focuses on the firm’s strategic objectives) to optimize IT portfolios in a multi-business unit firm. The principle methodology emploved in this research is an ITPM model and simulation, which creates a computational representation of the underlying theoretical logic that links constructs together within a simplified world [18]. While simulation can be used purely for description or exploration, the intent here is to explore what relation ships exist relating key IT portfolio attributes and IT investment effi ciency. In our process of applying our analysis framework, one of our findings is that the strategic emphasis of a particular business unit’s IT projects plays a significant role in moderating the relationship between IT investments and firm performance, which is consistent with Mithas and Rust [53].

Next, we generate the following implications for ITPM research based on our application of the model to the (simulated) data generated based on projects from our Fortune 50 firm (research site). First, while emphasizing compliance and operational issues in the multi-business unit firm, the IT resources in an IT portfolio composed of small varia tions in project size, with projects characterized by low levels of tech nical complexity, may contribute to superior IT portfolio efficiency through achieving customer-oriented strategic goals. Second, our results show that a strong focus on Operating Margin (OM) strategy type in an IT portfolio accompanied by medium variations in project size will benefit cost-saving-oriented business objectives. Furthermore, the CIO office of a multi-business unit firm that embraces a higher level of customer strategy may gain a greater impact on IT investment efficiency by implementing an IT portfolio with medium variations in project size, while reducing the amount of IT resources used for both operation and innovation strategies. Finally, a multi-business unit firm that allocates its IT resources to an IT portfolio composed of projects with high vari ation in size, which involve an emphasis on Long-Term Growth (LTG) strategy type at lower levels of technical complexity, may contribute to superior IT portfolio efficiency through achieving operation-oriented strategic goals.

IT investment decisions are highly integral to every business process. With the increasing embeddedness of IT in the processes of an enter prise, IT investment decision-making should receive significant atten tion in governance and resource allocation processes from a top management perspective (e.g., [[44],[54]]). Our research aims to assist the CIO office of a multi-business unit firm in building an IT portfolio characterized by a set of proposed portfolio attributes that perform well in a given strategic context to better accomplish enterprise business objectives. The ITPM model helps incorporate not only IT portfolio at tributes but also includes the combined economic efficiency of all business units in the firm. More specifically, instead of being determined by CIO’s intuition, the weight scores generated by the proposed ITPM model are based on data, which enables a multi-business unit firm to create a rational view of how much to invest in each strategic objective and thereby improve its investment efficiency according to the specific scenarios faced by the organizational unit.

Although our research recognizes that investment decisions in complex business environments are fraught with political and organi zational challenges [[67],[79]], modeling such challenges is beyond the scope of this paper. Our methodology has the essential strengths of high internal validity and experimentation [18], though we acknowledge its weakness in terms of external validity. Many firms experience a lack of transparency in the prioritization process for IT investments. In this regard, decision-makers (e.g., business executives and IT executives) may employ our proposed methodology, an evidence-based manage ment approach [68], to achieve a higher degree of managerial ratio nality in decision-making processes. Moreover, our ITPM study aims to establish the links among various IT portfolio attributes and provide rational viewpoints for the CIO office to invest in different strategic goals across multiple business units. To do so, here we analyze the IT project portfolio data using the proposed ITPM model from a single snapshot in time. Hence, the inability to fully model the dynamic nature of IT portfolio management could be considered as a limitation of our paper. Further, due to the proprietary and highly sensitive and strategic nature of this data, we had considerable difficulty in getting access to other firms’ IT project portfolio data, and this could limit the generalizability of our simulation. While the specific relationships among IT portfolio attributes are unique to each firm, we submit that the simulation approach described here is generally applicable to any multi-business unit firm.

Lastly, our findings are consistent with the matching perspective suggesting that strategic alignment is achieved by matching the IT portfolio with the organization’s business objectives [[9],[75]]. A firm that methodically matches the composition of its IT portfolio to its business strategy is highly likely to gain benefit through overall portfolio performance.

Overall, we enumerate three main findings of this study: (1) the importance of a centralized approach in IT portfolio management, (2) the need for matching the IT portfolio and business objectives to achieve strategic alignment, and (3) the utility of the portfolio attributes in assessing the value of the IT portfolio.
