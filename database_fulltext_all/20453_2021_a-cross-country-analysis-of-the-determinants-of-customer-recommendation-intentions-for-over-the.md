## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Expectation–confirmation mode Perceived enjoyment C2c interactions Perceived content quality User satisfaction Continuance intentions

## A B S T R A C T

Using data from India and the USA, this research expands the expectation–confirmation model (ECM) to include constructs like neutral confirmation, customer-to-customer (C2C) interactions and perceived content quality as antecedents to perceived enjoyment, perceived usefulness, user satisfaction, continuance, and recommendation intentions. Across both countries, except for the influence of C2C interactions on perceived enjoyment and of perceived usefulness on recommendation intentions, the remaining hypotheses are supported. There are crosscountry differences in the magnitude of the paths from neutral confirmation and C2C interactions to perceived usefulness, and of paths from perceived enjoyment and satisfaction to continuance intentions.

## 1. Introduction

The term over-the-top (OTT) is used to refer to the “programming content that is streamed through the internet to a device where it can be viewed, such as a television, mobile phone, or tablet” ([104], p. 343). In recent years, there has been a dramatic shift from linear broadcasting and cable/satellite to OTT content [77]. This shift is often attributed to the fact that OTT platforms offer consumers anytime/anywhere digital media services, combined with convenience, accessibility, high quality, and affordability [67]. Enabled through the latest embedded technolo gies, online streaming content provided by Netflix, Amazon, and Hulu is consumed more extensively compared with programs shown through most traditional television broadcasters [26, 91]. Revenues from streaming services are expected to reach \$252 billion by 2025 from \$123 billion in 2019<sup>1</sup>; the three largest OTT markets, the USA, China, and India, are expected to command two-thirds of global OTT revenues, with \$68 billion, \$19 billion, and \$6 billion, respectively [18].

The shifts in the evolution of OTT platforms have recently led to a proliferation of studies examining its implications for both firms and consumers ([26]; Kim, [47, 48, 77, 91]). A few studies have also eval uated the use of OTT platforms to predict adoption or consumers willingness to pay (e.g. [48]). However, none of these focuses on the post-consumption dynamics for this technology, whereby consumers not only continue using the service, but also recommend the platform to others.

Understanding the post-adoption behaviour for OTT platforms is relevant to researchers and practitioners [17, 53]. The OTT platforms, with the integration of digital technologies and traditional media, are rapidly disrupting the entertainment business [43, 74]. Such emerging platforms have a strong influence on consumer lives, have evolved the approach of entertainment, and have created a transition in the con sumer adoption and consumption journeys [75, 86]. The traditional televisions are being gradually replaced by a variety of smart devices, with the content creation going beyond a few creators [34]. Faster and cheaper data access has fuelled the demand for OTT platforms, which are constantly leveraging technology to embed the content in an ecosystem of technologies and devices for wider access [17].

The rise of OTT platforms has also caused consumer retention to emerge as a serious challenge for its managers, given there are no major switching costs and consumers have greater control over the content/ platform they want to watch [87]. Beyond quality content, OTT plat forms need to provide user-friendly interface/features, across-devices accessibility, and on-the-go consumption abilities to promote con sumer loyalty [43]. Additionally, the growth of OTT platforms across countries is heterogeneous; for example, the growth in India, while higher than the developed world as well as the global average, is behind that of the Asia-Pacific markets in total size [17, 38]. Like other emerging technologies, OTT platforms are expected to have different diffusion rates across countries, contingent on the development status or culture of the country [10, 19]. Hence, for OTT platforms, the effects of the technology attribute on user continuance and recommendation in tentions, as well as the variations of these effects across countries of different socio-economic status and cultures, remains an extant research gap.

Considering continuance and recommendation intentions as a proxy to consumer retention, this study assumes that retention is less probable if consumers feel that the antecedent drivers of such intentions are unfavourable. In this regard, the application of the expect ation–confirmation model (ECM), with suitable augmentations, is appropriate, since, in the post-consumption domain, ECM has been applied in various high-technology contexts, but not for OTT platforms. Compared with other technologies examined through the ECM lens, like mobile applications, m-wallets, m-shopping, social media, online banking, and e-health [23, 58, 90, 95, 99], OTT platforms are unique. The key features of consumer satisfaction and retention for OTT plat forms pertain to their ability to provide engaging content, as well as personalized recommendations, both enabled by a service that adapts well across device platforms [85]. OTT platforms are a metamorphosis of the traditional media, entertainment, and technology to transition the way content is created, distributed, and consumed, which makes them very different to other user-centred technologies [43].

While applying ECM to this context, the construct of confirmation in the traditional ECM has been replaced with neutral confirmation, owing to the dynamically evolving levels of desired expectations for emerging technologies that lead to positive confirmation [78, 92]. The con sumption of new technologies fulfils user gratifications, beyond basic functionalities, and hence, the study enriches ECM by integrating tenets of uses and gratification theory (UGT). Prior works (e.g. [31, 119]) suggest that elements of UGT, like information seeking, enjoyment, convenience, and social interaction are important for sustained con sumption of emerging technologies. Thus, four variables were adde d/retained to the traditional ECM, which serve as consumption-enhancing attributes, over the adequacy attributes: con tent quality (information), customer-to-customer (C2C) interactions (social interaction), perceived usefulness (convenience), and perceived enjoyment (entertainment). The model was empirically validated across 849 respondents in the two countries, India and the USA. These coun tries depict cultural/economic diversity [10, 11], and are two of the largest markets for OTT platforms.<sup>2</sup>

## 2. Theoretical background

## 2.1. Expectation–Confirmation model

Bhattacherjee [6] discusses the importance of studying consumers intentions towards IT products and/or services from a continuance usage perspective, rather than an initial adoption perspective. The former perspective is considered superior because continued usage re flects a stronger impact on the service provider’s long-term profits [23, 64, 106]. A key factor in ECM theory is confirmation of expectations which is the level of agreement between users’ expectations and the actual performance [64, 83]. In the traditional ECM, it is associated with continuance usage intentions directed through consumption-driven satisfaction and perceived usefulness [92, 106]. Satisfaction is the users’ evaluation of their overall experience with the technology, while perceived usefulness is the post-adoption behavioural belief in a pro duct’s/service’s usefulness, after the assimilation of expected and actual

consumption values ([6]a).

Kettinger and Lee [44] argue that for services that involve complex and simultaneous consumer interactions with multiple service-facets, the confirmation may be an outcome of multiple expectations. Further, they suggest that such expectations can range from normative (desired) expectations to minimum tolerable (adequacy) expectations, creating a zone of tolerance (ZOT), within which the customer is neither dissatisfied (below minimum expectations) nor delighted (above desired expectations). The ZOT indicates the range in which the consumer will depict neutral satisfaction and will not discontinue the product/service usage [92]. This concept has emerged as a critique of the concept of confirmation in ECM, which neither dissociates expectations into min imum and desired, nor considers multiple expectations from a complex service system.

In the case of emerging technologies, desired user expectations are ambiguous due to the several unknown value-neutral technology in gredients [21]. OTT platforms utilize cutting-edge technologies like artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and deep data ana lytics to ensure seamless streaming, in the wake of surging demand.<sup>3</sup> With the COVID-19-induced lockdowns, this demand was even higher and OTT platforms had to leverage these technologies further to keep their backend services online. For example, MXPlayer, an OTT platform in India, deployed the data-saver mode through H.265 encoding and advanced data compression using AI and object recognition technology to keep the stream buffer-free. Similarly, efficient data churning algo rithms for individual subscribers used by Netflix help them curate engaging content as well as generate relevant recommendations. Further, such platforms represent the convergence of a variety of tech nologies, like video-editing, post-production, animations, language mi grations, video-formats, and embedded virtual and augmented reality (VR/AR) to enhance consumer experiences [111]

For such applications, the user evaluates the technology on adequacy expectations, shaped through similar other applications, since the desired expectations evolve with continued gratifying experiences [78, 92]. Hence, we argue that for emerging technologies, the normative (desired) expectations are evolving and the consumer evaluates the technology through two lenses: one, of the known/experienced tech nology ingredients that provide legitimacy to the emerging pro duct/service, and two, of the unknown, evolving, and value-neutral ingredients that provide delightful gratifications [78, 79]. In both cases, the confirmation of the expectations lies in the ZOT since the desired expectations, both for legitimacy-enhancing attributes and the differ entiating value-neutral technologies, are argued to be ambiguous owing to the rapid evolution of the technology.

The desired expectations realign when new features/functions are quickly added, and users decide to re-engage when the performance of such technologies exceeds a basic/minimum adequacy expectation, which is formed through experience with related technologies [78, 92]. Thus, a better measure to measure confirmation for such technologies in the traditional ECM is neutral confirmation, defined as the positive evaluation of technology against adequacy expectations of emerging technologies. Only when such neutral confirmation is combined with other perceivable points-of-difference of the technology, would a con sumer decide to continue with the technology [23, 92].

## 2.2. Uses and gratification theory

UGT explains an individual’s psychological motivations for using a certain product/service to fulfil their personal needs [35, 51]. This theory has discussed motives for people selecting a technology, factors that shape those motives, and post-adoption outcomes from the consumption of the technology [33]. UGT is considered an influential theory that provides a clear understanding of what individuals need, how needs germinate, and how they are gratified through consumption [119]. The theory has been widely in a variety of contexts like mobile devices [54], websites [33], social media [31, 115], and online content [96].

In the UGT, the primary gratifications are information, convenience, entertainment, and social interaction [51]. Information gratification is the extent to which technology provides resourceful information [65]. Modern technologies, like OTT platforms, through their content, can supply a user with insightful information and becomes a primary motive for users to consume them [115]. Useful content, fulfilling the infor mation gratification, creates a positive user attitude towards the tech nology and enhances sharing intentions [31, 96].

Convenience refers to the ease with which users find the consump tion of technology as useful, intuitive, and user-friendly [73]. Extant research agrees that ease of use and convenience lead to the success of products/technologies [50]. Beyond ease-of-use, convenience also im plies accessibility which is the user motivation of technology to get ac cess to the information that is also relevant [112]. Modern OTT platforms not only aim at the relevance of content based on cutting-edge recommendation systems, but also work across devices, adding to the consumer’s mobility [96]. ECM refers to this convenience as post-adoption perceived usefulness.

Entertainment gratification, a primary outcome of OTT platform consumption, is the extent to which users feel that the content on the OTT platform, as well as the technology itself, is fun and pleasurable [65, 77, 91]. Such enjoyable technology provides emotional relaxation to the consumer, which is the emotional outcome of pleasurable con sumption [54, 73]. The relevant construct in this context is perceived enjoyment which is the extent to which using OTT platforms is consid ered enjoyable by users.

Finally. social interaction enables users to connect with others and express their unique social identities [118, 119]. Unlike conventional media, computer-mediated channels, like OTT platforms, need to facil itate inter-customer social interaction as it is a strong motivator for continual platform usage [115]. This is because such communication helps an individual build connection with other users with similar in terests. This work considers C2C interactions as the relevant construct facilitating social interactions.

## 3. Proposed framework

Neutral confirmation of adequacy expectations from embedded technological attributes indicates users’ evaluation of their adequacy expectations against actual performance [23]. From new technologies, like OTT platforms, consumers may not know what to expect from the technology in terms of desirable attributes. Hence, they evaluate the technology with adequacy attributes like bandwidth requirements, streaming quality, platform/software updates, system requirements, user interface, data security, and easy migration across devices, derived from experiences of related technologies like social media, video-streaming websites, and smartphones [23].

Users may experience psychological dissonance when even their adequacy expectations from OTT platforms’ enabling technologies are not met [83]. Once these adequacy expectations have been met, users show evidence of deriving satisfaction and perceived usefulness from the technology [22]. This is because the confirmation of their adequacy expectations assures them of the minimal performance expect ancy/usefulness of the technology, and combined with consumption-enhancing features, they contribute to the satisfaction since users foresee the realization of all expected values [23, 103]. Hence, we propose:

H1a: Neutral confirmation of expectations is positively related to satisfaction.

H1b: Neutral confirmation of expectations is positively related to

perceived usefulness.

Along with adequacy attributes, there are non-utilitarian gratifica tions related to enjoyment, like fun-seeking and entertainment, associ ated with product/service consumption [64]. These refer to perceived enjoyment, which is the intrinsic motivation to consume, as opposed to perceived usefulness which serves as the extrinsic motivation [48, 49]. Gupta, Yousaf, and Mishra [23] argue that such non-utilitarian gratifi cations need to be added to the traditional ECM. Extant research em phasizes pleasure-generating attributes as more significant than perceived usefulness (e.g. [73]). Multiple recent studies (e.g. [2]) have suggested that neutral confirmation is a key antecedent to building perceived enjoyment, since only if users’ adequacy expectations from underlying technologies are met during the actual use of OTT platforms, they are in a position to derive entertainment [30]. Thus, we hypothesize:

H1c: Neutral confirmation of expectations is positively related to perceived enjoyment.

Colm, Ordanini, and Parasuraman [13] suggest that customers may consider fellow customers as preferred sources of information, support or expertise when their knowledge of technology is limited. Social ex periences and the effects of resultant networks are important for users of evolving technologies, since such conversations help shape consumer perception towards their advantages, or otherwise [55]. Users who interact with co-customers become more aware of the capabilities of the underlying technologies and attempt to convert the vicarious experi ences of others to their own since such novelty experiences make the technology more useful and enjoyable for an individual [41, 49, 80]. Extant research in experiential services postulates that co-experiencing services with other customers leads to an enhanced utility, as well as entails unforgettable hedonic experiences [4]. Sharing of ideas and feelings with other consumers about a service, which manifests as C2C interactions, enhances the overall customer value, both utilitarian and hedonic [46].

Such experiences, driven through social interactions, contribute to establishing the individuals’ social status in the referent user group, and in turn, enhance the consumer’s self-esteem, subjective well-being, and happiness [32, 41, 42, 71, 88, 101]. We argue that users of OTT plat forms not only formulate/share their appraisal of services through co-customer interactions, but also feel valued as technology experts in the community of co-users/non-users. These C2C interactions help users form positive evaluations about the usefulness of the underlying tech nology of the OTT service, as well as enhance its overall enjoyability [109]. Therefore, we posit:

H2a: C2C interactions are positively related to perceived usefulness.

H2b: C2C interactions are positively related to perceived enjoyment.

In the context of OTT platforms, perceived content quality is the capability of the platform to provide diverse and high-quality programs as well as information-based services to its subscribers [57, 60]. With the user having limited knowledge of the complex ingredients and ecosys tems that enable the technology, high-quality content for the OTT platforms serve as the visible source of the technology’s utility as well as positive consumption experiences, sometimes transcending users to a state of “binge-watching,” an equivalent of flow for OTT platforms [15]. The theory of flow explains how high-technology service elements, such as the content and the users’ emotional responses, lead to intrinsic enjoyment [60]. This is especially true if the technology is a high involvement one, as such technologies are a major source of user enjoyment [29].

Extant works argue that content relevance, facilitated through a capable technology, enhances the contextual and actionable significance of the embedded knowledge (e.g. [37]). The systems which present credible, interesting and useful content are expected to enhance the perceived utility of the technology [116]. Emerging technologies, like OTT platforms, involve AI/ML-based recommendation systems which aggregate information about a consumer’s preferences, estimate them, and generate appropriate content recommendations [72]. Efficient recommendation systems empower users to discover relevant content which is suited to their specific tastes, reduce the user’s cognitive load, and enable content personalization, which enhances the perceived usefulness of the technology [62]. Thus, we posit that:

H3a: Perceived content quality is positively related to perceived usefulness.

H3b: Perceived content quality is positively related to perceived enjoyment.

Post-adoption perceived usefulness reflects an individual’s belief that the OTT platform underlying technology enables them to get access to relevant content, with great convenience. Users develop consumption intentions toward technologies largely based on their cognitive appraisal of how the technology improves their expected performance; when the technology is found to fulfil those expectations during actual usage, users are satisfied [48, 80].

Perceived usefulness has also been found to be an important ante cedent to behavioural intentions in emerging technology contexts like fin-tech products, digital textbooks, Internet-of-Things products/ser vices, massive open online courses, m-banking, m-wallets, fitness ap plications, and smart wearables [23, 24, 39, 40, 93, 106]. It is apparent that for emerging technologies, like OTT, the providers whose services are both time-saving and enable the accomplishment of users tasks—reflecting the usefulness of the technology—are more likely to have a greater number of satisfied consumers who also want to continue using the technology [93]. The impact of perceived usefulness on satisfaction and continuance intentions is also well-entrenched in ECM [6].

Further, beyond continuance intentions, there is another post-usage behaviour that is as important: recommendation intentions [9]. Compared with word-of-mouth which can have both positive or nega tive valence, and is communicated to a larger audience, recommenda tion is largely positive and remains restricted to a specific group of peers [16]. The recommendation from consumers using an OTT platform en courages others to adopt/subscribe the platform [110]. Such recom mendation is an outcome of post-adoption positive utility obtained by the users, which implies that a positive consumption experience, driven through high perceived usefulness, can motivate users to recommend the OTT platform to his/her peers [8, 116]. Therefore, we posit:

H4a: Perceived usefulness is positively related to satisfaction.

H4b: Perceived usefulness is positively related to continuance intentions.

H4c: Perceived usefulness is positively related to recommendation intentions.

Perceived enjoyment indicates an experience derived from product/ service consumption accompanied by fun/pleasure [73, 94]. Enjoyment is an intrinsic experience that is personally relevant and enables an in dividual to maintain an optimal state of comfort. The importance of such experiences is a key determinant of consumer satisfaction and continu ance intentions [83]. OTT platforms are primarily used for entertain ment purposes, including watching movies/series; hence, perceived enjoyment is a critical factor to ensure satisfaction and continuance intentions in technology use [69].

OTT content providers are increasingly focusing on enjoyable fea tures in platform design and content to serve as a space for nonutilitarian gratification since the effect of perceived usefulness on satisfaction and continuance intentions is smaller compared with that of perceived enjoyment, [61]. The outcomes of perceived enjoyment on satisfaction and continuance intentions have been reinforced across similar technologies, such as smartphones, wearable devices, digital textbooks, IPTV, and social networking [39, 81, 83]. Therefore, we posit:

H5: Perceived enjoyment is positively related to satisfaction.

H6: Perceived enjoyment is positively related to continuance intentions.

Continuance of a product/service is primarily a function of post usage satisfaction [3]. User satisfaction is a post-choice evaluative judgement, where consumers compare product or service performance, quality, and other outcomes with an evaluative standard [113]. Bhat tacherjee et al. [7] indicate that satisfaction is a transaction-specific effect contingent on the immediate usage experiences of technology, and positively impacts a user’s continuance intentions. Studies in related contexts have indicated that satisfaction—an outcome of users’ per ceptions of the value gained from consuming OTT platform con tent—has a positive impact on users’ intentions to reengage with the platform (e.g. [23]). Therefore, we posit:

H7: Satisfaction is positively related to continuance intentions.

For evolving technologies like OTT platforms, customers, especially late adopters, are dependant on the information from those who have already adopted/consumed the technology [13]. This motivates current users to discuss their experiences with others, and not only help them make an informed decision, but also enhance their reputation within a referent group [41, 73]. Such positive social interactions in the form of recommendations (online/in-person) materialize only when the current user is satisfied with the technology and intends to continue using it [84, 102, 117]. In the case of OTT platforms, users can share/recommend the platform or specific content with individuals within their network [82]. Through such recommendations, an individual reflects on the gratifying consumption experiences with the OTT platform, representing their satisfaction and re-engagement intentions with the platform (Xu, Peak, & Prybutok [117]. This encourages prospective users to adopt the platform and watch the recommended content [12]. Hence, we posit:

H8a: Satisfaction is positively related to recommendation intentions

H8b: Continuance intentions are positively related to recommen dation intentions.

The global development of communication technologies has resulted in the widespread diffusion of technology products/services, such as OTT platforms. Digital technology has helped many firms cross national boundaries at minimal cost [68]. Some studies indicate technology adoption might vary across countries due to various macro-level eco nomic/social/cultural attributes (e.g. [36]). The specific attributes of a country, such as its culture, geographical location, or the state of asso ciated technology diffusion, may shape users’ expectations and affect their engagement with such technologies [10, 52, 107]. Sun and Zhang [98] identified these possible country-specific variables as voluntari ness, nature of tasks, technology complexity, the purpose of the tech nology (work/entertainment, individual/group), the gender of the user, related technology experience, intellectual capabilities, and culture.

Khan et al. [45] and Lu [63] argue that the richness/diversity of the content affects user responses differently across countries, which is supported by Yousaf, Amin, Jaziri, and Mishra [118] and Mariani and Predvoditeleva [68]. Furthermore, countries may vary in their risk perception of new technologies, which impacts the adoption of new products/services [108]. However, findings from previous studies on the relationship between a country’s characteristics and level of technology diffusion have been inconsistent [36]. While we argue that some re lationships in the model may depict differences across India and the USA, the primary structure of the model, which is based on ECM and UGT, should remain intact, supporting the theoretical robustness of the model. Hence, we posit:

H9: The overall mechanism of post-adoption consumption should remain invariant across India and the USA, with only subtle differences in certain relationships.

## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Data collection and sample characteristics

The measurement items, anchored on a scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree), were developed from previously vali dated instruments. The items for neutral confirmation (three items) were adapted from Shen, Li, & Sun, [92], items for perceived usefulness (three items), satisfaction (three items), and continuance intentions (four items) were adapted from Bhattacherjee [6]; items for intentions to recommend (three items) were adapted from Alexandris et al. [1]; items for perceived enjoyment (five items) were adapted from Oghuma et al. [81]; items for perceived content quality (three items) were adapted from Shin [94]; and items for C2C interactions (four items) were adapted from Moore, Moore, and Capella [76]. The draft questionnaire was reviewed by three professors and three doctoral scholars at a premier business school in India, who were familiar with the selected context. This was done to ensure content validity and the suitability of the scales. Before the final data collection, the questionnaire was subjected to a pre-test with a sample of 10 respondents to identify the language and/or typographical errors. Table 1 presents the items used for data collection.

For data collection, we secured the email addresses of 5000 (2500 from each country) potential participants, who were OTT platform users at least for the previous three months, with the help of local telemar keting agencies. A retrospective period of three months was taken to prevent any recall errors [97]. Of these, a sample of 1500 (750 from each country) respondents were generated using a random sampling tech nique. E-mail invitations were sent to seek participation, with the pro vision of a surprise gift for a randomly selected respondent. Additional attention filters were added to the questions to ensure a higher rate of valid responses from the sample [89]. The online survey was designed in English since this is a common language across India and the USA. Participants who did not meet the qualifying criteria were dropped following the initial screening. After removing outliers using Cook’s method, a final sample size of 849 (403 respondents in India and 446 in the USA) was obtained [14]. The overall response rate was 56.6% (53.7% India; 59.4% USA).

The Indian sample comprised 51.8% males; 75.2% aged 18–25 and 17.3% aged 26–35 (rest above 35 years); 78.2% who had been using OTT platforms for six months or more; and 83.6% who used either Netflix or Amazon Prime. The USA sample consisted of 47.8% males; 20.2% aged 18–25 and 35.7% aged 26–35; 80.7% who had been using OTT platforms for six months or more; and 92.6% who used either Netflix or Amazon Prime. The data collection process was completed within two months, between January and February 2020. As per Adobe,<sup>5</sup> the increasing global adoption of OTT content is due mainly to millennials (18–35 years), who account for 52% of global OTT sub scribers. Additionally, 80% of millennials have a subscription to at least one OTT service and consume at least seven hours of online video per week. Following the target population of OTT services, 73.17% of the respondents in this study were individuals aged between 18 and 35 years who had held a subscription to an OTT platform for at least three months. ## 4.2. Data analysis

Partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 3.0 was used to evaluate the measurement and the path models. PLS-SEM has been used for multiple studies since it is suitable for data with even non-normal distributional properties [25]. Addi tionally, it is less sensitive to smaller sample sizes compared with covariance-based SEM (CBSEM). According to Wetzels et al. [114], PLS-SEM is more suitable to evaluate complex models since, compared with CBSEM, it is a partial-information method and only estimates a subset of parameters.

## 5. Results

## 5.1. Combined analysis

First, we checked the presence of common method variance (CMV) using the Harman one-factor and marker variable tests [59]; the single largest factor accounted for only 32.41% of the variance in the Harman exploratory factor analysis (EFA), executed with principal component analysis in SPSS21. Next, a theoretically unrelated marker variable, ‘susceptibility to peer influence’, for which data were collected, was found to be relatively uncorrelated to extant constructs, with the highest inter-construct correlation being 0.09. The CMV-adjusted correlations were then calculated for the main constructs; none of the original cor relations was significantly different from the CMV-adjusted counterparts [66].

Next, the measurement model was tested. The convergent validity was examined by assessing the factor loadings, composite reliability (CR), and average variance extracted (AVE) [25]. It was found that all the item loadings exceeded the recommended value of 0.70, the CR values exceeded the recommended value of 0.70, and the AVE values exceeded the cut-off point of 0.50 (see Table 3 for AVE), thereby indi cating that the latent variable explained sufficient variance of its in dicators [25].

To evaluate discriminant validity, which implies that each latent variable is distinct from other such variables in the model, three tests were done. First, the EFA results obtained for the Harman test were reanalysed. A total of eight factors were obtained with neutral confirma tion emerging as the largest factor (32.41% variance) and continuance intentions as the smallest factor (8.69% variance), with the eight factors explaining 77.1% of the total data variance. The factor loadings from EFA are presented in Table 2. All the item loadings were highest for their respective constructs, with relatively smaller cross-loadings, ensuring a rigorous factor structure [70].

## 6. General discussion

Based on the theories of ECM and UGT, this paper proposes a framework that defines the factors that influence user continuance and recommendation intentions for OTT platforms. The findings suggest that the neutral confirmation of users has a strong impact on the perceived usefulness and perceived enjoyment of the application. Also, once a user neutrally confirms the primary performance of the underlying technol ogies of an OTT application, compared with their adequacy expecta tions, the perceived usefulness, serving convenience gratifications, as well as perceived enjoyment, serving entertainment gratification, are enhanced. Neutral confirmation, based on expected basic attributes, is also found to be an initial source of user satisfaction, which is furthe built upon by consumption-enhancing attributes, discussed as C2C in teractions and perceived content quality in this work.

C2C interactions, fulfilling social interaction gratifications, influence the perceived usefulness, which in turn, enhances their satisfaction and continuance intentions with the technology, but not recommendation intentions. This implies that OTT platforms are considered as an effec tive technology providing convenient access, satisfactory, and worthy of further usage, but necessarily not to recommend, if an individual’s social interactions with their peer groups contribute to its consumption. C2C interactions, however, do not lead to perceived enjoyment indicating the relatively little role of social interactions for a consumer’s enjoyment of OTT platforms, which is largely driven by private consumption of con tent and the neutral confirmation of the enabling technologies.

Content quality, serving information gratifications, is found to be a strong predictor of perceived usefulness and perceived enjoyment; hence, OTT platforms need to have high-quality enjoyable, yet relevant and informative, content to generate utilitarian and hedonic experi ences. Such usefulness and enjoyment plays an important role since it creates post-usage satisfaction for the OTT user and influences their intentions to continue using the platform. Satisfaction is known to play an important role in the intent to reuse and recommend a service, and our findings align with this premise. Thus, satisfying experiences are key drivers for users to reengage with the OTT service as well as recommend it to others. Lastly, continuance intentions create recommendation in tentions, which suggest that engaging experiences, fulfilling all user gratifications with OTT platforms encourage users not only to watch more content on them, but also to recommend them and related shows to peers.

The model is robust across both India and the USA despite the cul tural/economic differences of these two countries. In both countries, not only is the measurement model found to be invariant, but all relation ships depict the same pattern of significance/non-significance, which both proves the universal application of the framework. Four relationships are found to be statistically different. Americans derive more perceived usefulness from OTT platforms’ neutral confirmation than do Indians. However, the usefulness derived from C2C interactions is higher in India. This implies that once the adequacy expectations of Americans are met by the OTT platform, they find it to have greater usefulness value. Indians, however, find a platform more useful if it is also discussed/recommended in their peer group—a finding justified by the collectivist culture in India.

Americans also seem to have higher continual intentions with the same OTT platform if they are satisfied with it, unlike Indians, who may still switch. In American society, individuals may be more driven by their own opinions and to have realistic expectations compared with Indians, who may be biased by their social circles and carry unreason able expectations from an OTT platform, be it concerning the content quality or subscription cost. Interestingly, compared with Americans, Indians prefer to continue with an OTT platform if they derive enjoy ment from it. This may imply that Indians prefer content that is light and enjoyable, unlike Americans who may be attracted to serious, storybased programs.

## 7. Theoretical implications

The current research provides valuable academic insights. First, this work proposes an enhanced ECM, with tenets of UGT integrated into it, which examine determinants of continuance and recommendation in tentions for OTT platforms [56]. This work adds to the extant literature that attempts to enrich the ECM through the addition of context-specific variables (e.g. [7, 64, 106]). In the case of emerging technologies, most information systems literature focuses on the initial acceptance of a new product/service, supported primarily by pre-adoption theories and limited focus on the post-adoption consumption process. This work focuses on the post-adoption dynamics of such technologies which lead to enhanced diffusion through word-of-mouth recommendations.

Second, this paper replaces confirmation in the ECM model with the concept of neutral confirmation, a concept derived from confirmation but built-in with a ZOT [5, 44, 106]. Traditionally, confirmation is the user’s evaluation of the performance of an application in comparison with desired expectations. However, in the case of emerging technolo gies, the concept becomes ambiguous, with two levels of expect ations—adequate, encapsulating a consumer’s basic expectations based on related technologies, and desired, the evolving expectations based on continual usage of new technology, the two representing the ZOT [78, 92]. Neutral confirmation is a user’s evaluation of an OTT platform based on adequacy attributes expected of any application, such as a smooth interface, easy accessibility, device compatibility, data security, and cross-platform integration [61]. The other attributes that build over the basic attributes to shape the performance expectations are articu lated separately in this model. We, thus, add value to the traditional ECM by arguing that for novel technologies, like OTT platforms, where the desired expectations are ambiguous, technology ingredients com bined with experience-enablers individually need to fulfil the adequacy expectations for generating satisfaction and continuance intentions.

Third, the model introduces three consumption-enhancing variables for OTT platforms—C2C interactions, content quality, and perceived enjoyment. These variables are derived from the three gratifications of the UGT—social interaction, information, and entertainment, respec tively [51]. C2C interactions emerge as significant in enhancing the perceived usefulness of an application, content quality is found to be significant in enhancing usefulness and enjoyment, and both, in turn, enable satisfaction and continuance intentions. The role of perceived enjoyment suggests that non-utilitarian gratifications, such as fun and entertainment, supplement utilitarian benefits in generating perceived usefulness for emerging technologies [48, 64, 99].

Finally, although national, cultural and socioeconomic attributes are expected to have an impact on user expectations and engagement (e.g. [10, 52, 107]), our findings do not support this. Our work suggests that, while adoption might differ in the case of emerging technologies, post-adoption customer engagement remains invariant. However, the mechanism underlying this is not identical. We find that content quality has a greater effect on enjoyment amongst Indians, and confirmation and continuance intentions have a greater impact on usefulness and satisfaction, respectively, amongst Americans. Thus, we negate the findings of some works (e.g. [45, 118]) which propose that users engagement to such technologies differ across cultures.

## 8. Managerial implications

This paper offers important implications for practitioners. Our model demonstrates that an OTT platform that meets adequacy expectations can generate user satisfaction and continued usage intentions for the platform’s content. Thus, platform managers should focus on gauging and meeting user expectations of basic attributes to keep users “hooked.” Backed by technologies ensuring data compression and frugal bandwidth requirements, features, such as broadcasting live content, high streaming quality and low data requirements, frequent OTT plat form updates, low update time, reasonable system requirements, highquality user interface, integration of the latest technology, high data security, and easy migration across devices should be well-managed to ensure that users find the application easy to use and navigate. Powered by AI and ML technologies, leading OTT platforms, such as Netflix and Amazon, excel in most of these parameters, with streaming available in 4 K resolution, HDR and Dolby Atmos, support across platforms and operating systems (including Nintendo 3DS for Netflix), seamless up dates for the best platform features, intuitive interfaces, and adequate account security with limited logins and notifications in case of unau thorized access.<sup>6</sup>

Next, owing to the proliferation of multiple OTT applications, service providers should emphasize the provision of high-quality content on their platforms, including international shows and movies and platformoriginal content, to maintain audience engagement with the platform. By providing content that is personalized and enjoyable for the audi ence, backed by powerful content generation and recommendation systems, platform managers can be confident that their customers will be satisfied with the services and reengage, as well as recommend the platform to others. For instance, Netflix has a large collection of licensed movies from Hollywood, as well as content from across the world that is localized for the audience. It also has a multitude of English/non-English series, shows, documentaries and specials. Recently, it also added its own original content (e.g. shows, such as The Witcher, Stranger Things, The Umbrella Academy, Sacred Games), which has increased its desirability.

Our model shows that C2C interactions play a major role in making a platform useful for consumers. This implies that despite Web platform and content consumption being a private activity, users are keen to discuss their “watchlists” with others. Based on what they are watching, such interactions create a sense of belongingness for individuals. For instance, discussions around the HBO series Game of Thrones resulted in increased subscriptions to HBO’s OTT platform to watch the series.<sup>8</sup> Our model shows that C2C interactions lead to usefulness, satisfaction and continuance intentions, which ultimately create recommendation in tentions. This relationship serves as a “propagation network,” where a platform/content can potentially lead to further recommendations, creating a virtuous cycle for the platform. Hence, OTT platform man agers should not only employ platform-embedded social media links to enable consumers to recommend their watched content, but also develop recommendations for users based on what their friends are watching. For example, currently, Netflix’s content recommendation works on variables based on a user’s watching habits, and not on what people close to them are watching.<sup>9</sup> However, platforms should develop such peer-based recommendations

Finally, based on the path model differences between the two countries, platform managers in India and the USA should ensure that they provide more comedic and light-hearted content (e.g. stand-up comedy) in countries, such as India, and more serious/functional con tent (e.g. science fiction) in countries, such as the USA. Additionally, Indians are more reliant on inter-/intragroup interactions when select ing a platform/content; hence, platforms should aim towards greater facilitation of such interactions in countries like India, which have strong group dynamics. Furthermore, Americans have stable neutral expectations that, if fulfilled, can lead to higher perceived usefulness. On the other hand, the basic expectations of Indians may continue to evolve; hence, platform managers must ensure that they keep adding features and functions to the platform to continue to meet these expectations. This also requires managers to monitor the expectations of countries, such as India, using their deep video analytics technologies, carefully to ensure the usefulness of, and subsequent satisfaction with, their plat forms, as well as the continuance and recommendation intentions.

## 9. Limitations and directions for future research

This study has several limitations. First, the proposed model has been validated only in the context of OTT platforms. For the current frame work to become an established theory for all emerging technologies, there is a need for additional validation across contexts. Second, the study does not focus on the types of devices through which users access OTT platforms. Users may use multiple devices, such as smart TVs, personal computers or smartphone/tablets, and the experience of the content may vary across devices. Therefore, although the findings apply to all devices, the model can be evaluated across devices with novel constructs, such as user-interface quality and operating system.

Third, the study does not explore the role of socio-demographic and psychographic variables in the creation of satisfaction, continuance engagement, and recommendation intentions. Future research could examine the impact of such factors, as well as the role of socioeconomic factors, such as subscription costs, family opinion, and normative in fluences, on continued usage and recommendation intentions. Finally, while the study demonstrates specific differences in the model across India and the USA, platform-specific technology attributes that drive satisfaction in both countries, such as the neutral confirmation construct and the genre of the content (e.g. comedy, horror, movie, series, or special), are only implied, and were not directly measured. Thus, future studies can introduce platform-based attributes and type of content as antecedents in the model.
