## A B S T R A C T

Along with the development of artificial intelligence (AI), more IT applications based on AI are being created. A personal intelligent assistant is an AI application that provides information, education, consulting, or enter tainment to users. Due to their high levels of cognitive and emotional capabilities, we assume that users can form humanlike relationships with intelligent assistants, therefore, we develop a research model based on the theory of love. Data were collected from users of intelligent assistants through a survey. The results indicate that users can develop intimacy and passion for an AI application similar to that experienced with human beings. These feelings are related to users’ commitment, promoting the usage of an intelligent assistant, influenced by AI factors (performance efficacy and emotional capability), and moderated by human trust disposition

## 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) is intelligence demonstrated by machines in contrast to the natural intelligence displayed by humans [82]. Founded as an academic discipline in 1956, AI has experienced rapid growth in recent years due to advanced computing technologies, big data resources and theoretical understanding [23]. The AI field draws upon computer science, mathematics, psychology, linguistics, philoso phy and many other fields.

Research has recognized the benefits of AI applications across various fields [30, 34, 48, 49, 62, 73, 99, 100]. AI applications may replace human and work forces [20, 68], reduce costs and improve quality levels [15, 79]. Currently, intelligent assistants are becoming important applications of AI. An intelligent assistant often interacts with users in an integrated, conversational manner, offers information ser vices or performs various actions. For example, Siri, a most widely used intelligent assistant software, offers a faster, easier way to perform tasks using Apple devices. Other popular intelligent assistants include Microsoft’s Xiaoice, Baidu’s Duer, and Xiaomi’s Xiaoai. These applica tions work on personal computers or mobile devices (e.g., smartphones and iPads) and are used akin to a personal assistant by human users for information, learning, consulting, and even entertainment. To foster the application and diffusion of AI technologies, it is important to understand people’s use of intelligent assistants.

In past decades, researchers have proposed models and conducted numerous studies to explain and predict user acceptance and use of IT. For example, users’ IT usage is determined by perceptions and evalua tions of technology in terms of usefulness, ease of use, performance and effort expectancy, enjoyment, and playfulness [2, 27, 56, 66, 102]. Usage is also affected or moderated by social influence and the user’s age, gender, and experiences [102]. With the emergence and growth of electronic commerce (ecommerce), research has demonstrated that users may develop trusting relationships with online businesses and commitment to such relationships sustains their usage of an ecommerce system [38, 64]. Recently, researchers have studied the usage of intel ligent assistants based on the above aspects, such as usefulness, ease of use, enjoyment and trust. For example, Moussawi et al. [75] indicated that a personal assistant’s intelligence and anthropomorphism affect users’ adoption through the mediation of perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, perceived enjoyment and trust. Fernandes and Oliveira [33] found that the adoption of digital assistants was driven by functional elements such as perceived usefulness and ease of use, anthropomorphic qualities, such as humanness and interactivity, and relational elements, such as trust.

AI technologies are applied in the design of intelligent assistants to simulate how a human behaves as a conversational partner. Compared with other types of IT, AI-based intelligent assistants are anthropomor phized with humanlike features [69]. With AI functionalities of voice recognition and conversational semantics, an intelligent assistant can recognize human voices and interpret and understand the meanings of words and sentences. In this way, it can converse with human beings and answer their questions as a human does. Thus, when using intelligent software, users may feel as if they are interacting with a human being and are likely to generate humanlike feelings for and relationships with an intelligent assistant [1, 40]. To better understand the adoption and usage of an intelligent assistant, it is necessary to explore it in more depth from this aspect. Some theories, such as Sternberg’s theory of love, have been used to explain interpersonal bonds. According to this theory, love is composed of intimacy, passion and commitment between persons [92, 93, 94]. Currently, among the components of love, commitment has been adopted in information systems research, and it has been found that users may have commitment to an information system or technology, which sustains their usage [72, 73, 83]. Research indicated that intimacy and passion, which is reflected by strong emotional connections and passionate feelings, can be forged between humans and objects as between human beings. For example, marketing research revealed that consumers may generate intimacy and passion for a brand as they would with a person, and the intimacy and passion, different from commitment, will greatly promote loyalty to the brand [22, 108]. For intelligent assistants, which are highly anthropomor phized, it is most likely that users may generate emotional connection and passionate feelings for them in some contexts. However, it is not clear whether and how intimacy and passion can be formed between users and an AI application. In this study, based on the theory of love and research on love between human and objects, we explore how love can form between the users and an intelligent assistant, and particularly we focus on intimacy and passion to investigate their generation, relation ship with user commitment, and effects on usage.

This paper makes contribution to AI research by offering under standing of intelligent assistant usage through the perspective of love. It has been advocated by IS researchers that AI applications be investi gated from new theoretical angles [88, 89]. Different from previous research that studies AI adoption and usage based on traditional factors such as perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, enjoyment and trust, this paper demonstrates that human users may develop human like love feelings for an intelligent assistant, which greatly promotes their usage. Particularly, this paper for the first time introduces the love components of intimacy and passion to relationship between users and IT applica tions, and reveals that the factors, together with commitment, are sig nificant in promoting IT usage.

This paper also makes contribution to design and distribution of AI based applications such as intelligent assistants. Usage of an intelligent assistant can be enhanced by love feelings between human users and the application, which could be promoted by the intelligent assistant’s emotional and task performance capabilities. To further promote the adoption and usage of AI applications, both advanced cognitive computing and affective computing technologies should be applied in design and development of the applications since they may directly improve the usage and enhance users’ acceptance through the feelings of love. Users of high trust disposition are more likely to form love rela tionship with an AI application. Thus, the human trait factor should be considered by service providers in AI-based services.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. AI and intelligent assistants

In computer science, AI research is defined as the study of intelligent agents that perceive environment and take actions to maximize the likelihood of successfully achieving their goals. The traditional focus of AI research is computer technologies that enable machines to simulate human cognitive functions such as learning and problem solving [76,

82, 85].

Unlike scholars of computer science, whose work mainly centers on developing or improving AI techniques, information systems researchers apply AI techniques for management and business problem solving. AI has been explored for its role in decision support and expert systems [6, 7, 26, 39, 41], knowledge management [21, 36], financial forecasting [12, 105, 107], system design [95], technology investment and inno vation [21, 53], supply chain management [70, 77], and big data ana lytics [65]. Research has recognized the benefits of AI applications across numerous fields, including finance [34], healthcare [30, 62, 73], and marketing [48, 49, 99, 100]. For example, recommendation agents or shopbots are widely used in e-business to assist customers with in formation searching and decision making [54, 55]. An increasing number of business companies are using chatbots to enhance the quality and reduce the costs of sales and customer service [61]. In the service sector, robots will take on heavy or otherwise for people unpleasant, difficult, or even dangerous tasks [106].

In addition to the applications of AI to business described above, intelligent assistants are becoming an important application of AI. An intelligent assistant (e.g., Apple’s Siri, Microsoft’s Cortana, Microsoft’s Xiaoice, and Baidu’s Duer) is an AI application that engages in conver sation via auditory methods, provides information, and carries out certain tasks for individual users. Thus, intelligent assistants provide IT functions that make individual users’ lives and work easier, more effi cient and enjoyable. There has been some research on the adoption and usage of intelligent assistants. Moussawi et al. [75] found that perceived intelligence and anthropomorphism are significant drivers of intelligent assistant adoption by impacting users’ perceived usefulness, ease of use, enjoyment, and trust. Fernandes and Oliveira [33] indicated that acceptance of digital assistants is determined by functional elements, including perceived usefulness and ease of use, anthropomorphic design factors, such as perceived humanness and social interactivity, and relational factors, such as trust. Sheehan et al. [90] showed that a digital assistant’s anthropomorphic quality in avoiding or resolving miscom munication will significantly enhance user adoption. Lee et al. [63] discovered that users may have both cognitive and affective trust in an intelligent assistant, such as Siri, and the trust is impacted by the match between users’ gender and Siri’s gendered voice. Hu et al. [47] studied the mind perception of intelligent assistants and found that users perception of an assistant’s competence and warmth significantly in fluence their continuous usage. Turja et al. [101] investigated em ployees’ use of robots based on the technology acceptance model (TAM) [27] and found that use intention was predicted by usefulness, enjoy ment, social influence, and attitude.

Users may perceive IT artifacts as social factors instead of just pro ductivity enhancing tools [3]. Users’ trust in a technology is affected by a technology’s humanness. They are likely to develop humanlike re lationships with technologies that are more humanlike [60]. For example, people may identify with and emotionally attach to robots through teamwork [109]. It is believed that AI applications need investigation from a new theoretical angle and present opportunities for novel theoretical development in information systems research [88, 89]. As discussed above, research has examined the usage of intelligent as sistants based on a traditional theoretical framework and found that it can be predicted by usefulness, enjoyment, social influence, and atti tudes [101]. Compared with other forms of IT, AI-based intelligent as sistants are anthropomorphized with more humanlike features and capabilities [69]. Human users may develop intense humanlike re lationships with intelligent assistants. To understand the usage of intelligent assistants, it is essential to further explore the interactions between users and intelligent assistants from the perspective of inter personal relationship.

## 2.2. Love

Sternberg’s theory of love has been widely accepted by many researchers primarily due to its robustness and high generalizability [92]. Sternberg [94] validated scales to measure love components considering different types of love relationships (e.g., love for a parent, sibling, friend of the same sex, lover/spouse, or ideal lover) rather than considering only heterosexual marital love. The author proposes that loving relationships between persons can be characterized by some or all of three components: intimacy, passion, and commitment. Intimacy re fers to feelings of closeness or connectedness. Passion is derived from motivational involvement between persons and causes different forms of arousal. Commitment refers to the willingness to maintain the loving relationship long term in the future.

In addition to being used to examine relationships between persons, the theory of love has been applied to relationships between humans and objects. For example, Carroll and Ahuvia, [22] defined brand love as emotional and passionate feelings felt for any trademark. Research has also found that brand love, which is composed of intimacy and passion, significantly influences consumer loyalty, and behaviors, such as pur chase and word of mouth [4, 108]. As for antecedents of love between human and objects, research indicated that it is determined by human experience with the object and individual differences. For example, consumers’ love for a brand or product is affected by the perceived utilitarian and hedonic value [11, 50]. Brand love derives from brand experience that consists of sensory, affective, behavioral, and intellec tual dimensions [88]. Individual romanticism personality induces brand love, while materialistic consumers are less likely to develop brand love [83]. Brand love is also moderated by consumers’ attachment style (avoidance and anxiety) [72]. Anthropomorphism is demonstrated to have significant influence on consumers’ brand love [81]. Research also found that human love for an object may change over time, for example, consumer’s love for a brand has a lifecycle and develops over time [87].

Currently, commitment has been used to explain the relationship between users and IT. Users may display trust and commitment toward ecommerce systems, and they have been demonstrated to play a sig nificant role in driving users’ adoption and usage [37, 64]. Malhotra and Galletta [70] found that users may show a commitment to organiza tional IT that sustains their volitional use of the system. AI applications, such as intelligent assistants, are highly anthropomorphized with hu manlike features and capabilities, and can communicate and interact with users in a similar way to human assistants. In addition, intelligent assistants often outperform their human counterparts in high computing and data processing capabilities. They may offer information service and perform tasks more efficiently and accurately than human assis tants. Due to the humanized features of intelligent assistants, it is reasonable to believe that human users may have more affective and passionate feelings for them. Love that is composed of intimacy, passion and commitment can offer a better perspective than commitment only to understand the relationship between the users and an intelligent assis tant. However, current IS research lacks evidence of intimacy and pas sion between users and AI applications. In this paper, to better understand users’ usage of intelligent assistants from the relationship perspective, we include intimacy and passion to investigate how they are generated between users and intelligent assistants, as well as their relationship with user commitment and usage.

## 3. Research model and hypothesis development

As summarized in the literature review, love between human and objects is mainly determined by human experiences with the object and individual characteristics. The research model, as shown in Fig. 1, is developed based on the theory and research of love. The central part of the model contains love components. Since this study focuses more on intimacy and passion, we propose that a user’s intimacy and passion with an intelligent assistant affects the user’s commitment to and usage of the intelligent assistant. Human capital is commonly thought to consist of cognitive skills and non-cognitive (or “soft”) skills [43]. Cognitive skills refer to capabilities of performing tasks, such as syn thesizing information, making diagnoses, and generating forecasts. Soft skills are mainly social skills that comprise the human ability to un derstand and interact with others. The cognitive computing capability to perform tasks and the emotional capability to understand and interact with users are also considered the most important for users’ experience with AI agents [19, 47, 58]. In this study, we propose that an intelligent assistant’s performance efficacy and emotional capability have impact on the user’s intimacy and passion. Trust disposition is an important human trait that governs the quality of human relationship. To under stand the individual difference in forming love with an intelligent as sistant, we propose that the effects of performance efficacy and emotional capability of an intelligent assistant are moderated by user’s trust disposition. The user’s age, gender, education, and length of using the intelligent assistant are included as control variables. In the following sections, we introduce these constructs and hypotheses.

## 3.1. Love: Intimacy, passion, and commitment

An individual can experience love for a consumed object or brand that is as emotional and passionate as interpersonal love [4, 13, 22]. Examples of such a feeling might include a deep desire to play a piano, fantasizing about a sports car, and child’s excitement for a toy, etc. [91].

According to Baumeister and Bratslavsky [14], intimacy involves the mutual disclosure of personal information, a strong favorable attitude toward the other person, and the communication of affection. As a whole, intimacy is characterized by positive emotional feelings of closeness. Such feelings of intimacy exist in the context of a consumer –brand relationship [91]. Brand intimacy spurs a consumer’s willingness to maintain a relationship with a brand [86].

Sternberg [91] stated that passion is largely derived from the moti vational involvement of the partners involved. Passion and intimacy are highly and reciprocally interactive. Arousal is the prime manifestation of passion [14, 92] and refers to various physiological and psychological states of excitement caused by passionate feelings, which are strongly correlated [91].

Anthropomorphism of a product can help generate love [84]. AI applications, such as intelligent assistants can communicate verbally and engage in humanlike interactions with users. An intelligent assistant provides information, answers questions, and offers other personal ser vices to users. Users in turn often feel they are being assisted by a human. Intimacy or close relationships may develop between the user and the intelligent assistant over time. In addition, with the development of AI technologies, applications, such as intelligent assistants have more advanced cognitive and emotional capabilities, inspiring fascination, and passionate feelings for such assistants in users. Prior literature states that an individual’s emotional connection and passion for a brand have positive influences on proximity maintenance with the brand [98]. Ac cording to Shimp and Madden [91], an individual strongly desires to possess a brand when he/she has intimate emotional and passionate feelings for a brand. Thus, we have the following hypotheses:

H1: Intimacy positively influences a user’s use intention of an intelligent assistant.

H2: Passion positively influences a user’s use intention of an intel ligent assistant.

Commitment is defined as “an enduring desire to maintain a valued relationship” [74] (p. 316). It is the attitudinal component of loyalty. Brand passion and intimacy leads to action loyalty through the over coming of obstacles [86], including an individual’s variety seeking behavior, withdrawal from product categories, and changing consumer needs. An individual is motivated enough to overcome these obstacles and remain loyal to a brand when level of brand passion and intimacy is high. The motivation to overcome such obstacles is an important outcome that in turn makes the consumer loyal [13]. Thus, we propose that a user’s intimacy and passion with an intelligent assistant positively influences commitment.

H3: Intimacy positively influences a user’s commitment to an intel ligent assistant.

H4: Passion positively influences a user’s commitment to an intelli gent assistant.

Commitment is related to people’s identifications and sense of obli gation, thus leading to long-term relationship between persons. It has also been found between users and ecommerce systems and demon strated to play a significant role in driving users’ continuous use [64]. More recently, research on user commitment has been extended to other technologies. Malhotra and Galletta [70] found that users may show a commitment to organizational IT that sustains their volitional use of the system. We propose users may have commitment to an AI-based intel ligent assistant, which sustains their use intention.

H5: Commitment positively influences a user’s use intention of an intelligent assistant.

## 3.2. Performance efficacy

AI often outperforms human workers. For example, Luo et al. [69] found that AI chatbots are as effective as proficient workers, and much more effective than inexperienced workers in customer service. AI and robots do not show heterogeneity over time and behave identically in providing services. Such devices are free of human error and fatigue, and respond to their service environments in a highly reliable manner [49]. When people evaluate a product, they typically make judgment based on function [16, 25]. Relative to human agents, AI offers stronger data processing and analytical capabilities [106]. Perceived quality has been related to brand love in past studies [5, 13, 22] and is significantly related to perceived value, which considers benefits relative to sacri fices, and to important consumer psychological and behavioral re sponses [59]. According to the appraisal-emotional response paradigm, perceived value is positively related to consumers’ brand love [10]. When consumers believe they achieve high value from a brand, they are likely to develop emotional connection and passionate feelings for it. An intelligent assistant’s performance efficacy is the capability and com petency to offer information and perform tasks. As users employ an intelligent assistant, they gain much value from the application when it shows high levels of performance efficacy. Their experience with the assistant would be characterized by happiness and satisfaction. Thus, we propose that an intelligent assistant’s performance efficacy influences users’ intimacy and passion for the application.

H6: The performance efficacy of an intelligent assistant positively influences user intimacy.

H7: The performance efficacy of an intelligent assistant positively influences user passion.

## 3.3. Emotional capability

Emotion is a new and key design factor manipulated in AI applica tions. Practitioners incorporate emotions into chatbot design to have chatbots converse with customers emotionally. Empathy has covered a range of emotional states, including caring for other people; emotions that match other people’s emotions; and discerning what the other person is thinking or feeling [80]. It refers to the ability to feel and share another person’s emotions [96].

Empathy has been studied in various business contexts. It is essential to well-functioning interpersonal relationships and significantly related to online interpersonal trust [32]. Emotional capacity is a major distinction of advanced AI applications from previous IT. It is an important human quality in AI applications, which will cause emotional experiences for users and lead them to form an emotional relationship as they might with other humans [1]. An intelligent assistant’s empathic emotional capability to understand the user’s feelings and respond accordingly is critical for the user to view it as human and to build a strong humanlike relationship with it.

H8: The emotional capability of an intelligent assistant positively influences user intimacy.

H9: The emotional capability of an intelligent assistant positively influences user passion.

## 3.4. Trust disposition

Trust disposition refers to the inherent individual propensity to trust or distrust others [57]. An individual’s trust disposition is a stable characteristic that governs how he/she views the trustworthiness of every other entity [71]. People with high trust disposition are inclined to make and maintain relationships with others. Thus, when an individual has a high trust disposition, he/she easily forms close ties with others. Users can trust technologies [60]. When consumers interact with chat bots online, trust plays a significant role [69]. A user’s trust disposition is a determinant of his/her trust in an AI application and affects the development of a relationship between the user and the application.

Thus, when a user has a high trust disposition, it is more likely that he/she will have emotional and passionate feelings for an intelligent assistant if it shows extraordinary cognitive and emotional capabilities.

H10: Trust disposition moderates the relationship between perfor mance efficacy and intimacy. For users of high trust disposition per formance efficacy of an intelligent assistant has stronger effect on user intimacy.

H11: Trust disposition moderates the relationship between perfor mance efficacy and passion. For users of high trust disposition perfor mance efficacy of an intelligent assistant has stronger effect on user passion.

H12: Trust disposition moderates the relationship between emotional capability and intimacy. For users of high trust disposition emotional capability of an intelligent assistant has stronger effect on user intimacy.

H13: Trust disposition moderates the relationship between emotional capability and passion. For users of high trust disposition emotional capability of an intelligent assistant has stronger effect on user passion.

## 4. Research methods

## 4.1. Measures

All the research constructs are measured with multi-item 7-point Likert scales adapted from prior studies, with minor wording modifi cations to tailor them to the research context. The scale for measuring intelligent assistant use intention was adapted from Agarwal and Kar ahanna [2]. To measure intimacy, passion, and commitment, we adapted items from Sarkar et al. [86] and Heinrich et al. [44], who conceptualized and measured brand love with items for intimacy, pas sion and commitment. Trust disposition was measured using a scale from Gefen [37], which measures trust disposition in online environ ments. Items for performance efficacy were adapted from Lu et al. [67], who measure the performance efficacy of service robots. Emotional capability was measured with the Basic Empathy Scale [51], which as sesses the ability to understand another person’s emotional state and to give an appropriate response. The measurement items of the constructs are shown in Table 1.

## 4.2. Data collection

A questionnaire was used to validate the conceptual model. Potential respondents were individuals who had used at least one kind of intelli gent assistant. The survey was drafted in English and translated into Chinese by one author who was proficient in both languages. The survey was then tested with three faculty members and ten students not included in the final data collection phase. Based on the feedback, a few minor changes were made to the survey to make the items more accu rately reflect the measurement intentions.

Data were collected with the assistance of an Internet research company in China. The company keeps basic information of approxi mately one million Internet users in its database. The survey was posted on the company’s website and was accessible for two weeks. In total, 5000 potential respondents were randomly selected from the database of the research company, and then invitation messages were sent to them by email. Repetitive submissions of the questionnaire from the same respondent were forbidden, as verified with hyperlinks and IP (Internet Protocol) addresses. Among the invited respondents, 2471 had not used any intelligent assistant, and 1018 persons answered the questionnaire. After deleting answers with missing items, 1004 valid responses were used for analysis.

In the questionnaire, the respondents were asked to specify one intelligent assistant they used and for how long they had been using it. They were also asked to report their demographic characteristics, such as age, gender, and education. Next, they answered questions about their intelligent assistant usage, intimacy, passion, and commitment for an intelligent assistant; evaluations of intelligent assistant performance and emotion; and trust disposition.

## 5. Data analysis and results

We chose the partial least squares (PLS) method for data analysis, which is a structural equation modeling (SEM) technique that simulta neously assesses the reliability and validity of the measures of constructs and estimates the relationships among the constructs [42]. We prefer PLS over other SEM techniques, such as LISREL (linear structural re lations), because this study investigates users’ loving relationships with AI in an exploratory manner, where PLS is advised as the right approach [42]. In addition, PLS employs a component-based approach for esti mation and places minimal restrictions on sample size and residual distributions [97]. Data were analyzed using SmartPLS 3.0. We followed the two-step approach in examining the measurement and structural models[8].

## 5.2. Structural model

SmartPLS 3.0 was used to test the structural model. We ran two models to test the hypotheses. The first model did not contain any moderating terms. The second model tested the moderating effects of trust disposition. The results of hypotheses testing are shown in Table 5.

The first model explained 64.1% of the variance in use intention, 60.7% of the variance in commitment, 51.5% of the variance in in timacy, and 50.3% of the variance in passion. Intimacy has a significant positive effect on use intention $( \beta = 0 . 1 1 ; p < 0 . 0 5 )$ . Therefore, Hy pothesis 1 was supported. Passion has a significant positive effect on use intention $( \beta = 0 . 1 9 ; p < 0 . 0 5 )$ . Therefore, Hypothesis 2 was supported. Intimacy has a significant positive effect on commitment $( \beta = 0 . 1 7 ; p <$ 0.01), which supported Hypothesis 3. Passion has a significant positive effect on commitment $( \beta = 0 . 3 4 ; p < 0 . 0 1 )$ , which supported Hypothesis 4. Commitment significantly influences use intention $( \beta = 0 . 5 3 ; p <$ 0.001), supporting Hypothesis 5. Performance efficacy significantly in fluences passion $( \beta = 0 . 0 5 ; p < 0 . 0 5 )$ . Thus, Hypothesis 7 was supported. Emotional capability has significant positive effect on intimacy (β = $0 . 5 3 ; p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ , and passion $( \beta = 0 . 5 6 ; p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ ), thereby supporting Hypotheses 8 and 9. However, performance efficacy did not show sig nificant effect on intimacy, thus Hypothesis 6 was not supported.

The results of model 2 suggested that trust disposition has a positive moderating effect on the relationship between performance efficacy and passion $( \beta = 0 . 0 6 ; p < 0 . 0 5 )$ , supporting Hypothesis 11. It has a positive moderating effect on the relationship between emotional capability and intimacy $( \beta = 0 . 1 5 ; p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ . Thus, Hypothesis 12 was supported. It also has a positive moderating effect on the relationship between emotional capability and passion $( \beta = 0 . 1 9 ; p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ . Thus, Hypoth esis 13 was supported. However, the moderating effect of trust dispo sition on the relationship between performance efficacy and intimacy was not significant, which indicated that Hypothesis 10 was not supported.

As for the control variables, gender had a significant negative effect on passion for an intelligent assistant, which implies that males may generate passionate feelings for intelligent assistants more than females. Currently, most intelligent assistants have female voice. According to the flirtation theory, they may have more attraction to male users than to female users. Length of using an intelligent assistant had a significant positive effect on intimacy, which means that if users use an intelligent assistant for a long time, they are more likely to form close relationship with it. Age and education did not have a significant effect on intimacy or passion.

An intelligent assistant’s emotional capacity to empathize with users has a stronger effect on human users’ feelings of love than performance effi cacy. Regarding human factors, trust disposition is an important human personality trait in interpersonal relationships. Persons with high trust disposition easily form relationships with others. The results demon strate that users with high trust disposition are likely to develop both intimacy and passionate feelings with an intelligent assistant when it possesses strong emotional capability, and develop passionate feelings for an intelligent assistant when it has high performance efficacy. However, performance efficacy of an intelligent assistant does not cause user’s intimacy, and the relationship between performance efficacy and intimacy is not moderated by user’s trust disposition. Although intimacy and passion are correlated dimensions of love, the results show that they may have different antecedents in human object relationship. Previous research on love between human and objects are mostly about brand love [4, 108]. Consumers’ intimacy with and passion for a brand co-exist when they have high experience and value from the brand [11, 50]. The results of current study indicate that intimacy and passion may not co-exist between human and an object. For AI applications that are more humanlike, users may have expectation for higher humanlike features.

Only high performance capability is not sufficient for love by users. High emotional capability is even more critical for an intelligent assistant to induce humanlike feelings in users, while assistants with high cognitive computing capability but low emotional capability will more likely be treated by users as mainly productivity tools.

## 6.1. Theoretical implications

This study has theoretical implications for AI research. Different from prior studies on AI adoption, it offers a new understanding of AI adoption and usage by demonstrating that users may form love re lationships characterized by intimacy, passion, and commitment with an AI-based intelligent assistant as they would with a human partner. This implies that interactions between users and AI applications can be further examined from the perspective of interpersonal relationship. In addition to the theory of love, other theories of social relationships, such as those focused on social identification and psychological contracts, could also be applied in research on human-AI interactions to achieve a stronger understanding of users’ engagements with AI applications.

This study has implications for research on IT adoption and usage. As an important and popular research domain, IT adoption and usage have been studied extensively by IS researchers. Theories and models, such as the technology acceptance model (TAM) and unified theory of accep tance and use of technology (UTAUT), have been developed, extended, and widely used to explain users’ adoption and usage of various tech nologies [27, 102]. These tools have been demonstrated to be powerful in understanding IT usage. However, they explore IT as pure objects and perceive usage as mostly driven by benefits, values, and costs. Some factors are influential in the usage of various IT applications across a variety of settings. For example, users’ IT usage is determined by per ceptions and evaluations of technology in terms of usefulness, ease of use, performance and effort expectancy, enjoyment and playfulness [9, 17, 18, 46, 103, 104]. Unlike previous IT applications, AI applications are often highly anthropomorphized and exhibit humanlike features to users, which may lead users to develop humanlike relationships with them. Users’ relationships with and usage of AI applications should thus be studied from new angles with new factors [88]. Although commit ment has been demonstrated to play a significant role in promoting IT usage, it is not clear whether intimacy and passion can boost the usage of AI applications. The results of this paper show that users may develop humanlike love that is composed of not only commitment, but also in timacy and passion with AI-based assistants that possess strong cognitive and emotional capabilities particularly for users with high levels of so cial relational traits, such as trust disposition. Such relationships are critical to their usage of such applications. This implies that to fully explain the adoption and usage of advanced AI applications, prior the ories and research should be extended to cover appropriate social rela tional constructs.

This study also makes contributions to the theory of love, which is robust in analyzing different types of love relationships between people, including a person’s love for a lover, spouse, parent, or friend, etc. [92, 93]. The theory was later introduced to interpret strong relationships between human and objects. Research indicates that consumers may experience brand love, which entails intimacy with and passion for a brand, and that loving relationships promote consumer loyalty and purchases [4, 5, 22]. This paper is the first to apply the theory of love to IS research and finds that users may develop not only commitment, but also intimacy and passion with AI applications. It also finds the new antecedents to intimacy and passion, including the cognitive computing and emotional capabilities of AI applications, and human trust disposi tion. Thus, Sternberg’s theory of love applies not only to interpersonal relationships, but also to relationships between humans and objects (e. g., technology) with humanlike features.

## 6.2. Managerial implications

This paper has implications for the design and diffusion of AI ap plications. To promote the acceptance and usage of an AI application, it is important to enhance loving relationships between users and the application since the loving relationship boosts AI usage beyond the effect of performance competence.

Empathic emotion is considered important for AI applications. Practitioners incorporate empathic emotions into chatbot design to allow chatbots to converse with customers emotionally. The results of this study demonstrate that being empathic is central to users’ love for an intelligent assistant. Emotional capability is regarded as a major distinguishing feature of AI relative to traditional technologies. When designing and implementing an AI application (e.g., an intelligent as sistant), developers should focus not only on cognitive computing and data analytics functions, but also on affective computing or the ability to monitor, analyze and understand users’ emotions. Currently, AI tech nologies are advancing rapidly in terms of being able to analyze and understand human emotions. For example, AI can recognize emotions through face monitoring and can adjust the tone and adopt the right pitch to convey emotions to users accordingly. In this way, AI applica tions may interact with users more emotionally. These technological advances can be applied to the design and development of a new

generation of intelligent assistants.

Performance efficacy shows a strong effect on the generation of passion for an intelligent assistant. To encourage the usage of an intel ligent assistant, it is important to further improve its performance competence. Currently, intelligent assistants can answer questions and provide certain services to users. To enhance users’ perception of competence, it is essential to continue to improve their intelligence, for example, by increasing the capacity of big data analytics and learning. Then, they can offer more personalized and accurate services for higher user satisfaction.

The findings of this work indicate that those with high levels of trust disposition are likely to develop intimacy and passion with intelligent assistants. We suggest that vendors and service providers (e.g., Apple, Microsoft, Google, and Baidu) identify customers with higher levels of trust disposition. Marketing and service personnel should pay more attention to these customers and strive to attract and retain them since they are highly likely to become loyal customers of high value. We also recommend that business managers identify employees with higher levels of trust disposition. Such employees are more suitable for tasks or positions that require considerable collaboration with AI agents, as they may form intense relationships with AI agents in team or group work.

## 6.3. Limitations and future research

This study presents several limitations. First, the findings in this paper may be limited to personal intelligent assistants. The present research could be extended by studying users of other types of AI ap plications. Second, the data were collected from users of intelligent as sistants in China, whose culture differs from those of Western countries, such as the US. Future studies may examine the effects of cultural dif ferences on the relationships between humans and AI. Third, the various types of intelligent assistants have differences in some anthropomorphic features such as image and voice, which may influence users’ generation of love feelings. Future studies will also investigate the effects of intel ligent assistant types on the relationships between humans and AI. Fourth, the data used were mostly obtained from a single source through a survey. Future studies should use quantitative data to measure intel ligent assistant usage.

## 7. Conclusion

This paper investigates the use of intelligent assistants based on the theory of love. The results suggest that users can develop intimacy and passion for their intelligent assistants and that such feelings will enhance their commitment to and use intention of the applications. In addition, such intimacy and passion can be promoted through the design of ap plications with performance and emotional capabilities. Users with high trust disposition will form high levels of intimacy and passion with an AI application when it possesses strong performance and emotional capa bility. This study contributes to research on AI and IT usage. It also has practical implications for the design and promotion of AI applications.
