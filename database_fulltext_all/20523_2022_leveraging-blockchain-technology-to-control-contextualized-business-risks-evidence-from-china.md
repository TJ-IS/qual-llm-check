# Leveraging blockchain technology to control contextualized business risks: Evidence from China

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Blockchain Risk management Opportunistic risk Trust-evoking technology

## A B S T R A C T

The complexity and diversity of socio-economic environments call for a more nuanced consideration of contextualized risks confronting enterprises operating in these environments. By dissecting six cases that suc cessfully adopted blockchain technology in China, we present findings of a grounded theory study into the deployment of blockchain for managing contextualized risks and opportunistic risks. Findings reveal that applying blockchain can augment risk management by controlling opportunistic risk, the latter of which denotes the variability arising from opportunistic practices of internal agents or external parties afforded by their im mediate socio-economic environment. Particularly, we not only identify credibility, predatory, and compliance risk as three distinct types of opportunistic risk stemming from the unique socio-economic environment of China, but we also illustrate how blockchain could be leveraged to deal with such opportunistic risks through trustevoking mechanisms. We discovered that blockchain could evoke trust between individuals-to-organization organization-to-organization, and organization-to-individuals by bolstering competency, fostering benevo lence, and gauging integrity in individual, inter-organizational and intra-organizational contexts, respectively.

## 1. Introduction

China has undergone an impressive economic transformation and achieved unprecedented growth since opening its economy. A recent report published by the World Bank [85] ranks China among the world’s top 10 most improved economies on the ease of doing business. Yet, despite its admirable transition, the Chinese society is firmly rooted and shaped by its long-standing cultural background, culminating in a unique socio-economic environment that is distinct from western soci eties and amplifies the risks of enterprises operating in the country. With about 48% of foreign corporations either failing in or withdrawing from the Chinese market within a two-vear operational window [49], it is unsurprising that even seasoned multinational companies like Amazon, Disney, and Google, have succumbed to the perilous business risk in China. Consequently, scholars have called for greater contextualization in research to elucidate the challenges confronting enterprises operating in China [[16], [34], [39], [70], [94]].

Risk refers to an uncertain event or set of circumstances that, should it occur, will affect the attainment of envisioned goals [67]. Although risks are part and parcel of every forward-looking business decision, thev are also contextualized to a given environment since risks exist as a multidimensional concept that embodies interrelated firm, industrial, and socio-economic considerations [31]. In this sense, we conceive contextualized risks as risks that become prominent due to the embedded socio-economic environments within which enterprises operate. Indeed, past studies have yielded a diversity of contextualized risks associated with doing business in China. Whereas Lusita [47] has alluded to deficient intellectual property protection as a persistent problem plaguing the Chinese economy due to high counterfeit risk, Ethridge et al [22]. contended that the risk of employee fraud is prevalent in China due to intensified competition and unbridled greed.

Containment of contextualized risks is just as critical as controlling general risks, especially in high-risk, high-uncertainty, and low-trust markets such as China [31]. Since risk could present both challenges and opportunities depending on how it is dealt with [82], risk man agement plays an instrumental role in the competitiveness and surviv ability of enterprises. Take Alipay for example, to cope with the pervasiveness of online fraud in China in the early 2000s, the company offered escrow services to mitigate the impact of such risks for Consumer-to-Consumer (C2C) transacting parties. In so doing, Alipay was able to shape consumers’ transactional practices and grow its businesses despite the risks embedded in its immediate socio-economic environment [46].

Yet, despite scholarly consensus on the prevalence of contextualized risks in China, there is a dearth of research on managing these risks for three reasons. First, the concept of contextualized risks was initially advanced by researchers to draw attention to the existence of contex tualized risks confronting foreign investment, with scant attention being paid to how such risks can be controlled (e.g., Jayaraman 2009; [[95]]). These studies tend to conceive contextualized risks as insurmountable external conditions and ignore the possibility of undertaking proactive managerial interventions to control these risks. Haley [31], for instance, draws on a macroenvironment-industry-corporation framework to accentuate the predominance of demand-side, regulatory, and technol ogy risks in China. However, despite attesting to contextualized risk as a multilevel phenomenon that permeates macro-environmental, indus trial, and corporate levels (Haley 2006), this line of work stopped short of providing prescriptions on how these identified risks can be controlled. Second, despite the existence of general risk management frameworks within extant literature (e.g., [14]), these frameworks are oftentimes much too generic and may be inadequate in generating tar geted managerial strategies to control contextualized risks. Third, while enterprises have advocated for the application of cutting-edge technol ogies (e.g., artificial intelligence and blockchain) to cope with general risks [[24],[26]], there is limited progress in comprehending how such technologies can be leveraged to control contextualized risks.

To bridge the abovementioned knowledge gap, we conduct an investigation into multiple cases of blockchain application in China to deliver an in-depth appreciation of how blockchain can be leveraged to control contextualized risks that stem from the latter’s unique socioeconomic environment. Through analyzing multiple case studies covering diverse conditions of applying blockchain technology [88], we endeavor to answer the following research questions: how can blockchain technology be leveraged to control contextualized risks in China? Our choice to concentrate on blockchain is deliberate for two reasons. First, blockchain has been touted as a promising technology for controlling contextualized risks [[24],[26]]. Embracing blockchain will not only lead to standardization in industrial environments [76], but it can also trigger strong network externalities that evoke trust [65] and reduces information asymmetry [[6],[33]] among business partners, both of which aids in controlling contextualized risks. Second, a wave of blockchain-inspired, practitioner-driven innovations has already swept through China, translating into fertile grounds for unraveling the role of blockchain in controlling contextualized risks [[13],[62]].

Our analysis of multiple case studies not only reveals opportunistic risks as a pervasive form of contextualized risk that harms companies operating in the Chinese market by stimulating speculative behaviors among agents, but it also uncovers credibility, predatory, and compliance risks as distinct manifestations of opportunistic risks in such market. By advancing the notion of opportunistic risks to capture the uncertainties arising from other parties’ exploitation of loopholes embedded in China’s socio-economic environment, findings from this study contribute to a nuanced understanding of the formation of contextual ized risks from human-environment interactions. Furthermore, by scrutinizing blockchain applications, we also gain insights into how blockchain could be leveraged to control opportunistic risks through the provision of trust-evoking mechanisms. Particularly, we discovered that blockchain could aid in controlling the aforementioned three types of opportunistic risks by evoking trust between individuals-toorganization, organization-to-organization, and organization-toindividuals through bolstering competency, fostering benevolence, and gauging integrity in individual, inter-organizational, and intra organizational contexts, respectively.

The remainder of the paper is organized into five sections. The following section gives an overview of the extant literature on block chain, risks, and risk management. We then describe our study design and provide information about our case sites. Next, we present findings from our case analyses. Based on comparison and contrast with extant literature, we derived three types of opportunistic risk (i.e., credibility, predatory, and compliance risk) contextualized to China that could be controlled through blockchain technology. We then further theorize how blockchain can control for these three types of opportunistic risk by evoking trust through bolstering competency, fostering benevolence, and gauging integrity. Finally, we conclude with the implications of our findings for theory and practice, highlight the limitations of our work, and identify avenues for future research.

## 2. Theoretical foundation

## 2.1. Risks and risk management: an overview

While risk management is deemed a critical factor in business operation, there is no generally accepted conception of risk. Within extant literature on economics and finance, risk refers primarily to unanticipated variations in business outcomes such as revenues, costs, market share [55]. Managers pay specific attention to controlling the downside of risks to achieve expected corporate outcomes. In the management discipline, risk refer to the possibility of an event, should it come to fruition, would reduce the value of the business [5]. Manage ment scholars have identified different sources of risk and uncertainty [48]. For example, [[96]]state that asymmetry in the information required is a source of risk. Followed by [[97]] argues that even infor mation is fully available, the limited cognitive abilities of decision-makers to fully process that information would also contribute to a source of uncertainty.

Managing risks is critical for every organization since it helps com panies reduce losses and enhance the overall business operation [48]. The concept of risk management for business was developed originally in the mid-1990s and evolved with three main trends. Firstly, risk management has received increasing attention in IS studies, and tech nology has become one of the most important tools for risk management [45]. Advanced technology could help companies model complex risk by providing a better way to control it [[38],[54]]. On the one hand, IT can greatly enhance the information processing ability of organizations as it improves the quality of information that supports the decision-making of managers [[4],[75]]. Leveraging advanced tech nology such as big data allows the company to realize the prevention first instead of taking remedial measures after the risks occurred [[36], [86]].

On the other hand, IT is effective in supporting integrating business processes such as information sharing and coordinating (Rai et al., 2006; [77]). For instance, Tian & Xu [77] proves ERP system is associated with reduced firm risks, and the risk reduction effects were stronger when operating environments of the organization feature higher uncertainty. Social media platform was adopted by B2B sales professionals to over come the potential relationship risks [57]. Among these technologies, blockchain is one of the most promising tools for controlling business risks, including fraud risks [24], supply chain risk [26], etc. While applying cutting-edge technology in risk management becomes a com mon phenomenon [[24],[26]], how the technologies can help in con trolling the contextualized risks is still limited.

Secondly, instead of focusing merely on losses, more scholars have started emphasizing the positive outcomes of risk management. For example, an early study by Gahin [25] proposed a general theory for managing pure risk, which refers to hazards that lead to either a loss or no loss. At that time, reducing the uncertainties and losses is put on top of the agenda for both scholars and practitioners [25]. By contrast, scholars also claim the existence of speculative risk, which consists of the chance for loss or gains [[83],[84]]. Speculative risks identified in the research are associated with business, finance, investment, IT strategy, and politics, where proper risk management always contrib utes to the overall corporation [[20],[83]]. In practice, while pure and speculative risks often interact, organizations are encouraged to pay more attention to controlling speculative risks and eventually increase corporations’ values.

Thirdly, scholars tend to develop an integrated framework that re minds the existence of potential risks rather than providing solutions to control a single specific risk faced by organizations. Previous scholars stressed that enterprise risks are inherent aspects of the corporate strategy, so strategy designs need to consider risk controls [[3],[58], [74]]. For this reason, Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) has been touted as a systematic, integrated approach to managing general risks faced by organizations. ERM refers to a process applied in strategy setting and across the enterprise, designed to identify potential events that may affect the entity, manage risk within tolerate levels, and pro vide reasonable assurance regarding achieving entity objectives [14]. While ERM delivers effective tools for organizations to detect risks and increase risk predictability [20], scant solutions guide organizations to deal with these risks, especially with a contextualized consideration.

## 2.2. Contextualized risks in China

China has experienced an astonishing level of transformation in its economic and geopolitical status over the past 30 years. While China continues on the path towards rapid modernization, academics and practitioners have acknowledged that the Chinese society is still firmly rooted and influenced by its long-lasting cultural background that has largely remained inaccessible to the majority of the global community [[34],[70]]. Furthermore, the vast regional differences and uncontrolled behaviors of regional governments constitute a more complex business climate that is deserving of our special attention [[17],[44]]. Research interests into the Chinese business environment have begun since the 1990s, where multinationals have poured into China. Through China’s rise as a force in global production [43], there are increasing concerns over its contextualized business risks.

The current discourse on China’s contextualized risk can be delin eated into two major streams: cultural and legal challenges. The Chinese law is currently loosely defined, especially with the rapid economic growth, which exacerbates the contextual uncertainties for business operations in China. The complexity in the interpretation of the Chinese law leaves room for the emergence of copycats (e.g., Shanzhai products), thereby deterring innovative firms from entering the market. Other than the problem of copycat, the market supervision system is lagging since the regulation tends to lag behind with the rapid economic growth, especially in financial markets. According to Allen et al [2]., in efficiencies in the Chinese stock markets can be attributed to poor and ineffective regulation. While the central government assumed formal policy-making powers and re-structure stock market regulation, local leaders tended to retain significant influence over the firm-listing pro cess and enforcement of secondary market regulation until 2000 [32].

Chinese culture leans toward collectivism in terms of culture chal. lenges, which advocates for individuals to subsume their interests and goals to the group’s or collective’s [37]. The Chinese indigenous con cepts such as ‘guanxi’ [[10],[42]], ‘face’, [7], and ‘renqing’ (relation ships among people) play important roles in facilitating business relationships [80]. These cultural orientations leave room for opportu nistic behaviors in business transactions, which induces business risks.

Though literature provides insights on the generation of China contextualized risks, most studies focus on identifying and analyzing the evolution of the contextualized risks. It assumes that contextualized risk is deeply rooted in the environment and can hardly be altered or miti gated, thus reminding practitioners to consider contextualized risks when making business decisions. Thus, a dearth of research has attempted to provide potential solutions, especially technological solu tions, to eliminate these risks. Therefore, this paper aims to identify China contextualized risks and offer ways to control such risks.

## 2.3. Blockchain in risk management

While IS scholars are committed to exploring advanced IT solutions for risk management, blockchain technology possesses an innate advantage in assisting enterprise risks management [76]. Blockchain is a distributed ledger database for recording transactions. Technically, in blockchain, data is saved on a growing list of blocks linked through cryptography. As each block contains a cryptographic hash of the pre vious block and a timestamp, the data in any given block can be altered retroactively only if all subsequent blocks were changed correspond ingly, which is hardly possible. Thus, a blockchain is resistant to data modification [76]. In addition, blockchain employed a consensus mechanism to keep the database consistent whenever new transactions need to be validated; therefore, it provides a peer-to-peer networking services that enable decentralized data storage and security.

Blockchain has been touted as a promising tool for controlling business risks for several reasons [[30],[65],[66]]. Firstly, blockchain empowers its participants to avoid risks of central point failures [76]. As the distributed ledger is no longer controlled by a central party but co-maintained by all participants, the collapse of any of them will not affect the operations of others. For example, traditionally, a single cyberattack on the hosting database of an e-business platform would lead to loss of information for both sellers and buyers. A blockchain-based electronic marketplace can avoid this as trading no longer relays on a third-party platform [73]. As blockchain technology provides an open platform that is co-maintained by all participants, risks are distributed to all participants.

Second, blockchain technology can reduce the risks of fraud [[8], [15]]. Since blockchain provides organizations with high-quality data (e.g., timely, consistent, temper-resistant), it allows users to trace the whole operation process and leaves no room for fraud and data manipulation. Practitioners in areas such as finance [35] and supply chain [41] have started exploring the use of blockchains for this reason.

Thirdly, organizations equipped with blockchain technology can avoid the risk of misconduct [[98]]. The rules of the blockchain system are pre-coded; any malicious attempt or unconventional operation to defraud the system would be automatedly rejected. Thus, the blockchain-based system makes it easy to realize automation control and reduces the risk of misconduct to the minimum [1]. Fourthly, blockchain technology can help to improve risk management beyond single orga nizations. Unlike other technologies focusing on inter-organizational risk management, blockchain has shown the potential to build an open consortium that connects business partners and customers, man aging the risks at industrial or social levels [76]. Several scholars have verified the effectiveness of blockchain in managing general business risk. For example, Fu and Zhu [24] have demonstrated that blockchain technology can eliminate fraud risks among business partners and pro vide accurate information for business managers to make decisions. Similarly, Giovanni [26], through a game theoretic model, confirmed that blockchain could mitigate business risks in the supply chain and save transaction costs.

However, in reviewing the previous literature, we find only limited papers that systematically study how blockchain technology can assist organizations in risk management. Moreover, most of the paper stayed in the design phase and lacked empirical evidence ([[99]]; [89]; [[100]]. Neither its risk control mechanism nor its effectiveness has been well addressed. Also, comparatively limited progress is being made on how blockchain technology can assist in coping with contextualized risks. Yet, implementing blockchain technology leads to industrial and environmental standardization, conceived as an effective tool to control contextualized risks. Therefore, filling up these gaps would contribute to blockchain literature and provide industrial practitioners with guidance about how to achieve risk control based on blockchain solutions.

## 3. Methodology

We conducted a multi-case study and grounded theory method to investigate the impact of blockchain technology on risk management in China. Since blockchain technology is applied in a large spectrum of fields, multiple-case design is appropriate because it has subgroups of cases that cover different conditions [88]. More importantly, multiple case studies show a comparative advantage over a single case study and obtain more substantial analytics benefits [88]. The research is con ducted with the instruction of grounded theory for its exploratory nature [[27],[28]]. The grounded theory is adopted for three primary reasons. First, grounded theory is a “theory discovery methodology” [50], thus, the method can promote greater insights into the phenomena (e.g., rapid growth of blockchain development in China) and build up the expla nation theory of how and why such phenomena happened [29]. Therefore, the generative approach seemed particularly useful in our case, given that no theory of blockchain mechanism for business risk control has been established to date. Second, the inductive and contextual characteristics of grounded theory fit with the interpretive orientation of this research [[50],[71]]. Third, grounded theory can incorporate the context’s complexities into the understanding of the phenomenon [59]. Thus, it is a suitable method to analyze the often-complex blockchain technology issues encountered within an en terprise and among enterprises.

## 3.1. Data collection and analysis

Data was collected through various methods: semi-structured interview, documentation review, and on-site observations (Fig. 1). To explore the drivers of implementing blockchain technology to control the risks in China, we started by approaching the companies in China that have successfully implemented blockchain technologies. Specif ically, we began by searching the credible blockchain companies rec ommended by the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology<sup>1</sup> in May 2017. In detail, nine blockchain companies are on the recommendation list. We then collected each company’s public in formation $( \mathrm { i . e . } ,$ press releases and public archives) and filtered their undergoing blockchain projects with two main criteria. First, the blockchain project must range in business applications. Thus, we checked the companies’ websites and removed companies focusing on the technology infrastructure development projects and cryptocurrency projects from our list, leaving seven companies. Second, only the pro jects that have been successfully launched are targeted. We contract the companies through call and email to get the operation information. After this process, one project which ceased the operation was therefore removed. After filtering through the public material, we got six appli cation cases that satisfy our criteria which are located in three business areas: (1) agricultural product traceability, (2) financial market, and (3) commercial procurement and payment. Each market is valued over trillions and is perceived as the market with high risk for business. To avoid repetitive analysis, we picked one illustrative case project of each area and started to contact the relative companies - a blockchain-based agricultural products traceability system, a blockchain-enabled ABS system, and a blockchain-based E-draft system.

Primary data of the three blockchain companies were collected be tween Nov 2017 and Sep 2018. In total, we have obtained 14 semistructural interviews with managers of the three blockchain com panies from Nov 2017 to Sep 2018. Data collection and analysis were intertwined. After each interview, the field researcher took analytical notes to summarize what was learned and constituted emergent conceptual themes. The notes also added new interview questions that need confirmation or further explanation. To enhance the rigor of the research, we followed a replication logic recommended by [88] and further contacted other companies who have already launched similar blockchain projects in China. The second-round interview involved new informants to either confirm or challenge the emerging patterns in the data. Adding additional cases offers diploid analytic power by yielding more robust results [21]. It leads to our final data set that involves 24 interviews with 16 respondents from 6 blockchain projects Table 1. shows the detailed summary for the interview data.

Data analysis process was conducted following the principles of grounded theory [[28],[78]]. The collected data were initially analyzed one by one before the cross-check. Specifically, we adopted the Straus sian coding procedure [[69],[72]]. For each case project, we first use open coding to attach descriptive labels to every interviewee statement to capture core issues and concepts. Next, axial coding is performed to emerge the core category of contextualized risks encountered in each case project. Axial coding involves coding for contextualized conditions, the role of blockchain technology, and application consequences. As a result of axial coding, three key categories of China contextualized risks mitigated by blockchain technology emerged. Afterward, selective coding was conducted to cross-check all data coded under each main category to compare incidents further and refine categories [72]. Coding results within each category were constantly compared and discussed by authors until no disagreement existed [11]. In addition, we employed the processes of theoretical sampling. The initial analysis results were shared with key informants, co-authors, and academic colleagues. Based on their feedback, further data relevant to the themes are collected. Grounded theory emerges when theoretical saturation is achieved [[27], [28]]. Finally, theoretical integration is achieved by discussing the blockchain-based trust-evoking mechanism with trust theory. The three illustrative cases constituting our case set will be introduced in detail with the analysis results in the next section.

## 4. Case analysis

4.1. Illustrative Case 1: blockchain-based agricultural product traceability system

The first case focuses on the scenario of China’s agricultural industry. The illustrative case company (case project No. 1) is an agriculture giant that owns more than fourteen million acres of farmland in northeast China. Rice produced in northeastern China is famous in its high quality as it can only be cultivated one season a year. While the longer growth cycle and intense sunlight result in agricultural products with better quality, they also lead to lower yields than those of regions that support cultivation up to two or three times a year. Thus, the prices of agricul tural products produced in northeastern China are always higher than those of their competitors. To maintain the regional advantages and ensure high quality of rice production, the company has invested massive resources. As the CEO of the blockchain solution provider introduced, “As northeast China is an important grain production base of the country, the government invests heavily in it each year. The investment is mainly reflected in infrastructure building, employing of agricultural tech nologies to enhance its productivity…the farms are equipped with hundreds of sensors and handheld devices, which enable the digitalization of all its pro duction processes.”

However, even though the company continuously enhanced its production management and quality control system to guarantee a sustainable high-quality rice output, they struggled to fight low-quality Shanzhai products in the market. Shanzhai, sometimes also translated as a copycat, refers to business based on fake or pirated products with cheap prices [40]. Normally, shanzhai product duplicates or imitates the physical appearance of other products through an imitation-plus-innovation approach (e.g., adding slight modifications), thus, making them right under the trademark laws [87]. China in Ten words [89], shanzhai is listed as one of the ten most representative words to describe China. It is not just a widespread but also a longstanding phenomenon. The prevalence of shanzhai products poses a significant threat to the original brands. On the one hand, low-quality shanzhai products can damage the brand image of the original certified products. By providing look-alike products with lower quality, customers may feel the brand is no longer trustworthy and switch to other brands instead. It leads to decreases of purchase inten tion and damages the overall brand reputation [81]. On the other hand, even if customers can distinguish between shanzhai products and orig inal certified products, the appearance of shanzhai products can dilute the brand’s market share. By adding innovative features that fit cus tomers’ demand, there are even cases that shanzhai products success fully counterattack the leading brand [[18],[81]]).

Thus, the company faces high credibility risk. Credibility risk refers to the possibility that the company would lose customers as they cannot differentiate its products from shanzhai goods. The concept highlights the dilemma that the company delivered high-quality products, but the market failed to recognize them. In our case, since rice produced from northeast China is superior to the other place in terms of quality and price, unscrupulous producers occupy the market by selling inferior rice with lower prices and similar packages. The existence of shanzhai products leads to unfair competition and will eventually occupy the market share of the certified products. As stated by the CEO of the blockchain solution provider, “The annual rice production in north China is around 10 million tons. However, you could find that the actual sales in the market far surpass production. Shanzhai products or aged rice were mixed into the market by unscrupulous competitors… The bad competition pushes the company to lower their price, which is detrimental to both revenue and brand images”. Credibility risk is neglected by previous literature on shanzhai products. In this field, scholars centered on shanzhai producers and tend to study shanzhai from a relative positive perspective, like identifying the demand side drivers [40] and exploring shanzhai-enabled innovation models [[19],[92],[93]]. There is a lack of studies investigating the credibility risks from the side of companies that suffer from the shanzhai phenomenon and exploring ways to control such risks.

Our case gives an example of how blockchain technology can help the original company control the credibility risk. As shown in Fig. 2, the company has set up a blockchain-based system that integrates all of the supply chain participants (e.g., farmers, agricultural machinery pro ducers, transportation companies, financing institutions, warehouses, and retailers). The system enables customers to achieve real-time tracking by covering 55 key agricultural product-producing nodes. The increasing transparency of production information gives customers extra access to recognize the ‘right’ products from the markets. More over, the system clarifies the right and duties of each party. Consumers can directly trace the source of a product through the system. Once encountering the quality problems, they can easily find out the responsible party to guarantee their rights and interests. As presented by the CEO of the blockchain solution provider, “The company has created a new brand for rice whose producing process was all recorded on the block chain. The new brand requires a close production cycle. In other words, blockchain has integrated all production processes including food processing, logistics, and sales that form an independent ecosystem.”

The traceability feature of blockchain technology plays the main role in controlling the credibility risk. ## 4.2. Illustrative Case 2: blockchain-based Asset-Backed Security (ABS) system

The second case concerns Asset-Backed Security (ABS) business scenario. ABS is a financial security collateralized by a pool of assets such as loans, credit card debts, and receivables. Issuing ABS involves three main parties: the seller. the issuer, and the investor. Sellers are the firms that generate loans and sell to issuers (financial institutions such as investment banks) who act as the servicer, collecting principal and in terest payments from investors. Normally, the seller creates ABS by selling pools of loans to a special-purpose vehicle (SPV) and turning their loans into marketable securities through a process known as securitization. ABS is a popular financing approach chosen by enter prises. Firstly, it opens new sources of funds to the enterprise. Second, it enables the issuing enterprise to remove the debt items from its balance sheet to gain greater flexibility to pursue new businesses. In addition, from the investors’ side, ABS enriched investing tools that enable them to pick up additional yield and augment their portfolio diversification.

The most salient problem of ABS in China is the lagging supervision system. On the one hand, the ABS market is immature in China. The chief product officer of the ABS platform stated: “ABS is quite new to China. Compared with western countries where ABS was introduced more than 20 years ago, the regulation is not yet mature in the Chinese stock market to support ABS business.” On the other hand, the spring up of financial innovations and the rapid growth of the financial market exaggerated the problem of lagging supervision and left room for op portunists to manipulate. As stated by the chief product officer of the ABS financer: “ABS is a very complex financial product with multi-layer designs… Our ABS is issued based on the consumption loan. We (the ABS seller) firstly packaged cash receivables from several micro-loan companies and compounded them into a single ABS product for public investors... For consumption loan assets, it consists of millions of micro-loans, which is very hard to supervise. Previously, financial companies just recorded the asset information via Excel. It is not an up-to-date database but only a summary of statistical data. You are even not sure if the data is authentic.”

The immature financial market of China stirs up predatory risk when issuing ABS. Predatory risk captures the possibility in gaming structure that each participant tried to gain superior power by exploiting advan tages through the environmental vulnerability (in our case, e.g., lagging supervision system). The concept of ‘predatory’ comes from ecology and emphasis that the contextualized risk is transferable through the ‘food chain’. For instance, in our case, the ABS financer plays both predator and prey roles in its financing chain at different stages. At the asset acquisition stage, the ABS financers play the role of prey because the micro-loan companies (original owners of the asset) can take advantage of the lagging supervision system and provide inauthentic information for higher acquisition prices. However, at the ABS issuance stage, the ABS financer becomes the predator to issuers as they have incentives to take the same advantages to bargain a relatively lower financing cost (e. g., portray itself as less risky than it truly is). In other words, the pred atory risk arises as ABS financer plays different roles in the issuance chains, where conflict objectives and managerial incentives are involved.

Previously, scholars in the finance area have explored the various risks that are subjective to ABS as a financial instrument, e.g., credit risk, which refers to the risk that originates from the performance of the underlying collateral pool of receivables [64], and operational risks which refer the risks that caused by reinvestment and liquidity issues. Practitioners also remind that it's risky to fully trust credit ratings from rating agencies due to the mixed history of ABS. However, there is a limited concern related to the risks that are raised from contextualized environments with a dynamic perspective. Moreover, previous literature pays more attention to capturing and model risks of ABS, little effort has been put into solving them because the risk is natural in security. But the predatory risk is different as it comes from the opportunistic behaviors under certain contextualized environments rather than the security it: self. Thus, it leaves the opportunity to control it by successfully man aging the environmental elements.

Blockchain technology is introduced to help the company to control predatory risk. Specifically, taking advantage of the shared authenticity features of blockchain, the company is able to build an inter-institutional distributed data storage and query platform and create an ecosystem that allows participants to achieve higher-level process automation, better risk management, and smoother cooperation (Fig. 2). Once the asset is recorded in the blockchain ledger, the true information would be co-maintained by partners. Therefore, information transfer friction is erased, and manipulation is no longer allowed. In this platform, asset value is calculated based on its real historical price, and the trust among the participants is evoked for blockchain technology. As introduced by the chief product officer of the ABS financer, “It is a consortium block chain, based on which we have built a set of inter-organizational standards for ABS trading. All participants need to follow the standards so that data can be executed synchronously among different entities. Thus, data authority is secured as the ledger is co-maintained and no one can modify the data pri vately.” The CEO of case project five also expressed the same view:“It is difficult for sellers to get authentic information of underlying assets though it is a critical step... (Before applying blockchain) They need to spend a lot of efforts to verify the authenticity of information offline, which not just raise financial burden and causes transaction delays... However, now (after applying blockchain), ABS issuers are actively cooperating with financer to disclose real-time and authentic information... it not just cutting off costs for asset verification but increase their (financer) willingness to believe that they would not be cheated by the asset owners (issuers).”

## 4.3. Illustrative Case 3: blockchain-based e-draft system

Blockchain-based e-draft systems have been developed to remedy inter-organizational trade as the encrypted token offers a novel alter native payment option. Enterprise routine procurement refers to the daily purchasing affairs for the operations of the enterprises, e.g., the procurement of office supplies such as stationeries and computers. Generally, when a business operator is assigned to purchase some daily office supplies (e.g., desks and chairs), the business operator would firstly scout the market to select products and sign a pre-purchase con tract with the merchants based on a negotiated settlement price. Next, the company’s finance department would examine the contract and authorize a bank transfer to the merchants’ account pending the company’s approval process. After receiving the money, the merchants would then deliver the goods. The whole procurement process commonly takes several weeks or even months, which is neither timeefficient nor cost-saving. As stated by the founder and vice president of the blockchain solution provider, “The routine procurement contract of SMEs usually has a relatively small amount. For example, buying office tables and chairs, the amount is less than 5000 RMB… (but)the approval process is complex… before the operator settles the deal, financial staff need to go through the procurement lists and check the price negotiated was reason able…Sometimes, this process would repeat for several times, which causes waste in both manpower and time.”

As routine procurement is common in business operations, even though the cost of auditing and control may go far beyond the cost of failure, the process is necessary to prevent business operators’ unpro fessional behaviors, especially under Chinese cultural norms such as “renqing” and “guanxi”. China is a long-term oriented country where people appreciate values such as guanxi and renqing. For procurement staff, on the one hand, building and using personal connections with company recourses can create and maintain competitive advantages. For example, the staff can obtain “Mianzi” by paying the merchants higher prices. On the other hand, guanxi could negatively impact the firms. The frequent exchanges among closed guanxi parties will be detrimental to the firm as the staff consumes its resource for its benefit. Therefore, to protect the firm’s interests, managers need to take effective measures to fend off the unprofessional behaviors of employees. As claimed by the founder and vice president of the blockchain solution provider, “Generally, the company assigns procurement tasks to their employees, and the employees would then be in charge of the selection of goods and price negotiation. The employee would take it as an opportunity to do a favor for their friends or relatives. For example, a chair may cost only 500 RMB, but reported as 800 RMB by the employee because the suppliers are their rela tives... False invoices are also possible... Although such behaviors are not very costly to the company, as you see, 300 or 500 RMB, but such behaviors need attention and be carefully regulated.”

Strong compliance risk is captured in our case. Compliance risk occurred when the company failed to detect that the business operators purchased the routine products from unauthorized suppliers. Specif ically, business operators might behave dishonestly to obtain personal benefits through the purchasing tasks (e.g., seize the chance to benefit their friends, balling the purchasing price, or taking kickbacks). There fore, the company designs a complex audit and approval mechanism to prevent compliance risks. However, since the routine procurement is not related to core business and features low frequency and low budgets, it seems in this case the company has sacrificed its operational efficiencies to enhance its risk control ability for pursuing merely a small improvement.

Compliance risk is different from fraud risk, and the risk is often mentioned in previous literature when talking about misconducted be haviors in procurement [[51],[56]]. According to Matthew et al [51]. procurement fraud is defined as ‘any fraud relating to a company pur chasing goods, services or commissioning construction projects from third parties.” One typical procurement fraud is employees colluding with suppliers to purchase products at a higher cost or a cheaper quality and receiving a kickback in return. Bribery, bid-rigging, embezzlement, and submission of false claims are other examples. However, non-compliances behaviors induced by guanxi or renqing are different from fraud. First, the motivations of fraud behaviors and non-compliance behaviors are different. Fraud behaviors are conducted with malicious intentions, but non-compliances behaviors could benefit the company. Second, the results for the two behaviors will be distinct. Non-compliant behaviors do not necessarily lead to a detriment. Reminding of the positive power of guanxi and renqing, it is possible that non-compliant behaviors of business operators finally bring the company benefits. Thus, the compliance risk needs to be differentiated from fraud risk and treated separately according to its intentions and potential results.

In 2016, a blockchain-based E-draft project was launched to improve the SMEs routine procurement management, which is a successful case for controlling compliance risk. The blockchain-based application is codeveloped by a technology company and a local bank that their client companies can adopt. As shown in Fig. 3, the system allows the company to issue e-drafts based on tokens authorized by the banks and can be exchanged to RMB at a rate of 1 to 1. The e-draft payment can be customized based on a budget of the organization. For example, as the founder and vice president of the blockchain solution provider intro duced, “The e-draft is programmed by a smart contract, where all the pur chase processes can be controlled. For instance, if the e-draft is appointed to purchase computers, it cannot be used to buy other products, the payment would not be recognized when it pays to food dealers. similarly, all the business rules can be regulated by smart contracts in advances, such as cash withdrawal rules, interest rates etc. … The e-draft system can accelerate the purchases process of adopters and help them save communication costs and fractional costs…moreover, through the system, the whole life cycle of the edraft could be monitored.”

The routine procurement system has been simplified significantly by leveraging the functions of blockchain to achieve the divisible process restriction. In addition, the compliance risk could be successfully managed since business operators’ behavior is strictly limited by precoded laws. The founder and vice president of the blockchain solution provider introduced, “We have developed a mobile App. Once the business operator uploads the contractual information into the App, the smart contract would automatically examine whether the procurement is fully compliant and generate a payment code if all the conditions have been fulfilled. The seller can confirm the corresponding amount of e-draft by scanning the code, and the procurement would be settled immediately.” The blockchain-based application secures the integrity of employees’ purchasing behaviors, thus increasing the trust between the organization and its employees. Specifically, the application allows the manager to trace all procurement purchases. Any misconducted behaviors will be observed immediately. As introduced by the Vice general manager of blockchain solution pro viders, “Now, we (clients) can fully trust our employees, as the blockchainbased E-draft system ensures that procurements are strictly processed ac cording to our manual. More importantly, any misconduct behaviors will be presented in the system, and we can simply find the responsible person... The operation staffs need to report to their managers if they want to conduct noncompliance behaviors but with well-intentions. Sometimes they do bring us (client) some benefits. As all trading information is recorded, if staffs bring some reliable supplier, we can transfer the personal relationship to long-term organizational benefits.”

## 5. Discussion

## 5.1. Theorizing opportunistic risk and blockchain solutions

Although being in completely different contexts, the three illustra tive cases have presented how blockchain technology can effectively control business risks for enterprises. As shown in the case analysis, blockchain can help enterprises manage risks caused by speculative behaviors contextualized to certain phenomena. We define it as oppor tunistic risk, which refers to the uncertainty resulting from other parties opportunistic practices. There are three main characteristics of oppor tunistic risk. First, the generation of opportunistic risk caused by the contextual environment where speculative opportunities exist. Second, the opportunistic risks are caused by speculative behaviors of other parties, leading to a trust crisis for the incumbent company. These parties could be external entities that cannot be directly controlled by the company or internal agents with an inherent conflict of interests. Third, controlling opportunistic risk could result in either loss or gain. In other words, successful control of opportunistic risk can benefit the companies. As summarized in ${ \mathrm { F i g . ~ } } 5 ,$ the unique contextualized envi ronment of China leads to opportunistic behaviors where people manage to take advantage of certain environmental flaws. These opportunistic behaviors, in turn, cause a trust crisis that solidifies the opportunistic risks to the trustees (the focal firm in each case).

By examining three cases, we have identified three salient opportu nistic risks in China: credibility risk, predatory risk, and compliance risk. Credibility risk comes from a trust crisis between companies (organi zations) and customers (individual), where customers failed to value the company or its products objectively due to the interference of shanzhai products. Predatory risk comes from a trust crisis between organizations, where financial institutions cannot completely trust the information disclosed by the other organizations. Compliance risk stems from a deficit of trust between companies (organization) and employees (in dividual), leading to a complicated approval process for preventing incompliance behaviors. Considering that opportunistic risks are mainly generated by speculative behaviors of the other parties that the company can hardly control, our finding shows that managing the trust crisis caused by speculative behaviors has become the key point for the company to prevent the opportunistic risk. Regarding the characteristic of blockchain technology, blockchain is appropriate for these firms to alleviate the opportunistic crisis by providing a series of underlying trust-evoking mechanisms.

In the case of a blockchain-based agricultural product traceability system, the original company can reduce the credibility risk by signaling customers its competency, thus, obtaining the trust of externalities (customers). Competence refers to a company’s ability to fulfill promises made with the consumers [[12],[79]]. Competence-based trust could be built when customers believe that the company has expertise and apti tudes in providing agricultural products with high quality. Blockchain equipped the company with the ability to trace the entire production and distribution process of products. Benefited from the traceability feature, the blockchain-based system extends the boundary lines by concatenating the upstream and downstream enterprises with the end customers. It provides an official channel for enterprises to show their expertise and provide evidence for customers to support purchasing decisions. To customers, although the risk of buying shanzhai products is unchanged, being able to get traceable information could increase cus tomers’ trust in the product, which means they are willing to believe the company even though quality problems are still possible. Thus, even if the environmental problem, shanzhai and counterfeit goods are not solved, the credibility risk faced by the company could be successfully controlled.

In the second case of the blockchain-based ABS system, benevolence is built among financial entities through applying blockchain. Benevo lence in an inter-organizational context refers that a company shows concerns for partners’ welfare to fulfill partners’ needs and demands [[52],[53]]. The shared-authenticity feature of blockchain technology offers a safe channel for companies to transfer information, which sig nals that the company is concerned with its partner’s needs. Conversely, the ABS financer shares their asset data on a blockchain, indicating an open and proactive attitude for cooperation, which eases partners concerns on acts of distrust. On the other hand, the shared authenticity can help partners to reduce costs and human workload. ABS issuance involves multiple financial entities such as custodian banks, rating companies, and law firms. Before applying blockchain technologies, a duplicated effort is commonly found among these entities to verify the information authenticity for achieving mutual trust. The whole process is time-consuming and leads to higher operating costs. What is worse is that China’s lagging financial regulation system has even solidified the requirements of such duplicated efforts. After implementing blockchain technology, institutional partners still need to meet auditing criteria to support mutual trust. However, the effort to reach a certain trust level is considerably eased. Although the contextualized problem still exists, applying blockchain technology helps the company to reduce the pos sibilities of exposure to predatory risks.

In the third case of the blockchain-based e-draft system, the compliance of business operators’ procurement behaviors is extremely enforced through the pre-coded restriction of blockchain technology. Thus, behavioral integrity is greatly assured, which enhances the level of trust. Integrity has been discussed as an antecedent to trust by several literatures [9]. It is defined as the “consistency of an acting entities words and actions” [68]. Palanski & Yammarino [61] further proposed the concept of behavior integrity on a personal level as a “perceived pattern of alignment between an actor’s words and deeds”, which em phasizes honesty and truthfulness. Behavior integrity is positively related to trust as trustors perceive the trustee adheres to a set of prin ciples that the trustor finds acceptable [60]. Blockchain enables the company to pre-code the principles and reinforces business operators compliance. After implementing blockchain technology, business oper ators can no longer act unprofessional or non-compliant behaviors. Moreover, the digital records can help the company transfer the short-time inter-personal guanxi into long-term organizational re lationships. Therefore, the unique environmental problem of unprofes sional behavior is well managed.

As summarized in Table 3, our findings reveal that blockchain can evoke trust by bolstering competency, fostering benevolence, and gauging integrity. These trust-evoking mechanisms are applicable in the context of individuals-to-organization, organization-to-organization, and organization-to-individuals. As the underlying trust issues caused by speculative behaviors are mitigated, the opportunistic risks faced by the company can be controlled. Though previous studies examine technol ogy can enhance trust among a specific party [90], they would rather focus on dyadic relationships between different stakeholders instead of detangling the underlying mechanism of trust enhancement among different constituents. For example, Zavolokina et al [90]. proposed that blockchain can enhance trust between customers and merchants in the car market; Ratnasingham & Pavlou [63] claims that e-commerce technology can improve inter-organizational communication trust. Our research illustrates that blockchain technology, with its features of traceability, shared authenticity, and divisible process control, can potentially evoke trust in all levels of relationships.

Instead of focusing on one specific contextualized risk, our paper presents a comprehensive framework to illustrate the contextualized risks in China, such as the opportunistic risks that result from the opportunistic behaviors driven by the unique business environment. In particular, we have identified three opportu nistic risks induced by China’s rooted culture and social norms: credi bility risk, predatory risk, and compliance risk. Our finding shows that although many studies believe contextualized risks are tightly embedded in certain environments and can hardly mitigate with cor poration strategies, our study finds it controllable. By identifying the causes of various contextualized risks, our finding on opportunistic risks suggests the need to establish a refined typology of contextualized risks that differentiate by the corresponding causes to understand which type of control approach is most applicable. Therefore, this study contributes to the risk literature by highlighting contextualized risks and providing a refined differentiation that implies that symptomatic treatment is needed for the different risks.

Though blockchain is touted as potential technology in resolving multiple business risks, many have cited the need for greater understanding of how blockchain can address business risk in the real world ([[102]]; [91]). This study demonstrated the empirical evidence showing that blockchain effectively controls opportunistic risks. ## 5.3. Implications for practice

This research provides practical experience and useful references for blockchain applications in controlling business risk. First, with our study, practitioners can be aware of the importance of opportunistic risks rooted in mainland China and comprehend the current contextu. alized risks they have to resolve. Specifically, the creditability risk caused by shanzhai severely threatens the food industry and other related industries. Realizing the creditability risk could help enterprises to re-evaluate their competition environment and tailor strategies. Similarly, the predatory risk is prevalent within inter-company’s collaboration, as the cooperation and competition are co-existed in the business context. While collaborators are striving for limited resources to optimize their own benefits, being aware of predatory risk can help the company better understand its situation, which is full of dynamic competition, and detect opportunities and threats in advance. The compliance risk also needs to be taken seriously by enterprises since it could threaten intra-firm management. Once incompliance behaviors occur, enterprises need to diagnose their reasons before taking action, and different treatments are required.

Second, although our cases analysis centered on three specific areas. the finding of this study does not limit to the case industries. Despite the dynamic business environment of China, these three contextualized risks and the solution can also apply to other emerging risks in China. For example, blockchain potentials in controlling contextualized risks can also be applied for copyright protection. As the business environment in China becomes increasingly attractive to practitioners worldwide, our finding reminds potential practitioners of the existence of contextual ized risks and implies that such contextualized risks could be well managed through appropriate technological solutions.

On the other hand, this paper extends the business landscape for blockchain applications to solve opportunistic risks. Specifically, this paper disassembles the technical features of blockchain technology and provides practitioners a detailed guide on blockchain solutions respec tively. It finds that enterprises’ demand for technological functions in different application scenarios is different. Enterprises could seek various technical solutions according to their actual situations. More over, the finding shows blockchain can not only assist the industry of consumption by evoking information visibility but also enhance the enterprises’ competency on firm’s external collaboration and internal management. Therefore, practitioners who have demands on improving interfirm and intrafirm management can perceive blockchain technol ogy as a possible means. Furthermore, since the development of block chain technology is still in its infancy stage and lacks successful applications, practitioners who plan to adopt blockchain technology can have a depth understanding of the technology.

## 5.4. Limitation and future research

Our study shows that applying blockchain technology can assist companies in controlling opportunistic risks, referring to the uncertainty that results in opportunistic practices of other parties caused by the contextual environment. This study also points out the blockchain characteristics that could be leveraged to mitigate such opportunistic risks by evoking trust correspondingly. However, this study is naturally not without limitations. Firstly, when detecting the business risks, we focus only on the Chinese context, which may lead to a limited guiding role for other developing countries. Future studies could take it as a starting point and devote itself to identifying contextualized risks in other countries. Secondly, although this research adopted a multiplecase study, we are still unsure whether the cases were definitive or exhaustive for mapping all types of opportunistic risks in China. Future studies could extend our work by conducting case studies on other case scenarios imprinted with strong Chinese contextual characteristics. Thirdly, this study is driven by research interest exploring how block chain technology could enhance risk management abilities. However, this is unnecessary but only provides one technology option for orga nizations to consider. Future researchers can also explore risk control mechanisms empowered by other technologies.
