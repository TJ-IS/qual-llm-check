# Product information and green consumption: An integrated perspective of regulatory focus, self-construal, and temporal distance

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Green consumption Message framing Regulatory focus Self-construal Temporal distance

## A B S T R A C T

Understanding consumer information processing is fundamental. Previous studies investigated the effect of message framing and self-construal in green consumption using self-reported measures and obtaining contrasting results. This study used the eye-tracking method across two studies to examine the effect of promotion- vs. prevention-framed messages on green consumption. Study 1 confirms that promotion (vs. prevention)-framed information attracts more attention from independent (vs. interdependent) individuals. Moreover, temporal distance strengthens this matching effect (Study 2). Overall, this study uncovers consumers’ processing of messages with different regulatory frames from a visual attention perspective and offers valuable insights about the three-way interaction to promote green consumption.

## 1. Introduction

Consumers’ non-sustainable consumption directly leads to 30–40% of environmental deterioration [1]. Green consumption is key to reducing the impact of human activities on the environment and achieving the goals of sustainable development [2]. The European Union implements sustainable product policies through the EU Ecolabel, Green Public Procurement, Eco-design of Energy-Related Products, and the Energy Label. Information about products’ environmental impact has been acknowledged as a powerful factor in developing awareness and ascription of responsibility for sustainable products and behaviors [3]. However, information on product sustainability is often difficult to process and compare for consumers, thus resulting in confusion and shift from interest to skepticism [4]. Therefore, exploring how to provide sustainability information about a product effectively is relevant for promoting consumers’ sustainable consumption [5].

Motivation is a vital driver for human pro-environmental behavior [6]. Prior studies have investigated consumers’ motivation for green consumption [5][7]. However, presenting information that fits in dividuals’ motivation to adopt green consumption has rarely been investigated. Existing studies have considered gain–loss green infor mation [7], positive–negative product communication information [8], eWOM information about products’ environmental impacts [4], and abstract–concrete feature information of green products [9]. Based on prospect theory, research involving gain–loss message framing [10] highlights people’s approach–avoidance motivation and explores its effect on their pro-environmental behavior [7]. However, another approach–avoidance motivational aspect based on regulatory focus theory [11] that has garnered attention in marketing research [11] is scarcely discussed in green marketing [12], that is, message framing (specifically, regulatory focus) matching individuals’ self-regulatory focus (i.e., promotion-framed vs. prevention-framed information).

Regulatory focus theory [11] identifies two basic modes of in dividuals’ goal orientations, namely, promotion focus and prevention focus. Notably, a person’s promotion focus orientation can be elicited by a message that highlights pursuing positive goals (e.g., “exercising can contribute to the increase of energy”; promotion framing), whereas one’s prevention focus orientation can be induced by a message that emphasizes avoiding negative outcomes (e.g., “exercising can reduce the risk of some cancers and mental illnesses”; prevention framing). In this study, we apply regulatory focus theory to message framing and inves tigate the influence of green product information framing on consumers perceived information attractiveness and green consumption intention. This study advances the literature on green product information pro cessing by focusing on social media contexts, which have received less attention. Social media platforms have become major channels for consumers’ information acquisition and shopping (i.e., social com merce) [4]. Consumers increasingly acquire relevant product informa tion from social media platforms, such as WeChat, Weibo, Tik Tok, and Kwai in China and Facebook and Twitter in the US, where several thousands of users observe interactions between consumers and their comments [4].

Additionally, research shows that green consumption intention de pends on individuals’ personality and situational factors [12]. To address these aspects, self-construal and temporal distance are incor porated into our framework. Self-construal is relevant because egoistic and altruistic motives affect people’s motivation to engage in green consumption [13]. Furthermore, the main beneficiary of green con sumption is not always the consumers themselves but other people or the whole society [2]. Hence, how individuals weigh the relationship between themselves and others, especially how they see themselves as separate from others or as linked with others, can influence their green consumption intention. Self-construal delineates this concept, and two distinguished self-construals have been identified, namely, independent and interdependent self-construal.

Previous research has measured the role of self-construal on green consumption [6], suggesting that people’s self-construal can systemati cally influence green information evaluation [12]. However, the results of these studies are contrasting. For instance, Aaker and Lee [14] reveal that people with an independent self-view are more persuaded by promotion-focused information, whereas individuals with an interde pendent self-view are more persuaded by prevention-focused informa tion (avoidance goal). In contrast, Kareklas and colleagues show that individuals with an independent self-view develop more favorable at titudes toward prevention-focused (as opposed to promotion-focused) environmental appeals, whereas individuals primed with interdepen dent self-view are more influenced by promotion-focused environmental appeals [12]. Moreover, the underlying processing mechanism of the regulatory fit between message framing (based on regulatory focus) and self-construal has received less research attention [15]. Furthermore, previous studies on this topic primarily use self-reported measures (i.e., questionnaires) [16], which is susceptible to subjectivity and social desirability [17]. This study makes a methodological contribution by adopting eye-tracking methods in the study of green product informa tion processing.

We incorporated temporal distance in our framework because green consumption is believed to bring benefits not only to the present but also to the future, potentially influencing consumers’ expectations and out comes about green consumption. According to construal level theory [18], green consumption can differ based on how temporally near or distant such behavior is perceived [19]. Moreover, people with different self-views perceive the passage of time differently [20]. That is, a future event, behavior, or outcome may be perceived by people with an inde pendent self as somewhat distant. However, it is perceived as relatively proximal by individuals with an interdependent self. These distinct perceptions are associated with consumers’ subsequent evaluation, preference, and performance [18]. Thus, temporal distance combined with self-construal may better explain green consumption. The second goal of this study is to uncover how temporal distance might interact with the regulatory fit of message framing and self-construal.

This study contributes to consumer psychology and green product information processing literature in social media settings. First, we provide a better understanding of the role of the regulatory fit of green message framing and self-construal and how this matching influences consumers’ perceived information attractiveness and green consump tion intention. We extend the existing literature by investigating the persuasive effect of green message framing based on regulatory focus. Even though some pioneering studies have explored the regulatory fit of message framing and self-construal by adopting self-reported data collection methods, they obtained mixed findings [12][14]. To resolve these contrasting findings, in this study, we have adopted the eye-tracking method. Eye-tracking is an unobtrusive and powerful method in uncovering the underlying cognitive mechanism from a visual attention perspective. Furthermore, we assume a relevant boundary condition, temporal distance, to reveal a three-way interplay of message framing, self-construal, and temporal distance for guiding the develop ment of effective information strategy via social media to promote green consumption. The findings of this study can provide a more compre hensive and meaningful understanding of green consumption than currently exists.

## 2. Theoretical background and literature review

## 2.1. Message framing in green domain

Connecting consumers by information strategies to achieve better persuasiveness has been widely investigated in marketing research [21], particularly in the areas of green consumption, where scholars have explored the effectiveness of gain–loss, positive–negative, self-- benefit–other-benefit, and abstract–concrete message framing. Scholars have paid much attention to the gain–loss message frame. In gain-framed messages, consumers are provided with positive outcomes if they comply with a particular activity, whereas loss-framed messages warn them about negative outcomes if they fail to comply. For example, Chang et al. [7], Segev et al. [5], and Baek and Yoon [22] examined the persuasiveness of gain–loss message framing in green advertising. Dai et al. [10] explored the effectiveness of gain–loss messages in enhancing people’s green consumption intention. In addition to the gain–loss message frame, the positive–negative frame is also adopted by re searchers [8]. Positively framed messages emphasize the possible ben efits derived from green consumption. However, the definitions of negatively framed messages have been inconsonant. Kulkarni and Yuan [23] and Xue [8] portrayed the negative frame as one that emphasizes avoiding and minimizing possible pain or loss by practicing green consumption. Amatulli et al. [24] described it as highlighting harmful outcomes derived from not complying with green consumption, which is similar to that of the loss frame. Scholars have also looked at the impact of self-benefit (claims that green consumption benefits the consumer themselves) and other-benefit message frames (claims that emphasize the benefits to others or the environment) on pro-environmental behavior [25]. Another message framing dichotomy that has been investigated concerns the abstract–concrete message frame [9]. Abstract claims use unspecific or vague wording and portray green products subjectively and ambiguously, whereas concrete claims contain information with detailed and tangible clues and describe green products in a more objective and specific manner.

Although prior research has investigated the green message strategy from multiple aspects, this study highlights a scarcely discussed perspective of message framing, specifically regulatory focus in the green consumption context [12], to fill this void. According to regula tory focus theory [11], individuals can be promotion- or prevention-oriented when making decisions and taking action in pursuit of a goal [26]. Promotion-focused individuals regulate their attention, attitudes, and behaviors to fulfill their goals of gains and aspirations and strive toward advancement and achievement [27]. By contrast, prevention-focused individuals regulate their attention, attitudes, and behaviors to achieve their goals of duties and obligations, and gravitate toward protection and safety [27]. This distinction between individuals regulatory orientation is important because it affects individuals’ information search goals and processing strategies [26]. Moreover, it ad vocates the idea of regulatory fit theory that a person’s regulatory focus often results in certain preferences, attitudes, and behaviors toward certain goal-pursuit approaches [28]. Thus, a growing body of studies suggests that regulatory orientation could influence an individual’s in formation strategy and subsequent consuming behavior [29].

In consumer behavior contexts, the regulatory fit theory points out that individuals adopt different information processing strategies depending on their motivation. In this study, we focus on conditions that present information by maximizing the opportunities to obtain a posi tive outcome or to avoid a negative outcome. The information strategy (message framing) that fits consumers’ self-regulatory focus is the core argument of the current research. Specifically, information that em phasizes positive outcomes of complying with a green activity (i.e., approach motivation) is labeled as promotion-framed [14], whereas information that highlights the avoidance of negative outcomes due to practicing green behavior (i.e., avoidance motivation) is labeled as prevention-framed [14]. Promotion–prevention message frames appear similar to the gain–loss and positive–negative frames, however, they are conceptually distinct. Promotion-, gain-, and positive-framed messages highlight gains, and loss-framed information and negative-framed in formation defined by Amatulli et al. [24] emphasize loss. However, prevention-framed information still underlines gains but in an avoided description manner.

In marketing literature, the message framing based on regulatory focus has been adopted in studies on website evaluation [14], adver tising message persuasiveness [30], product evaluation [21], the persuasive effect of online reviews [29], and purchase intentions of online food deliveries [31]. However, research involving message framing based on regulatory focus on green consumption has been limited. A few exceptions include Kareklas et al. [12] and Gao et al. [32]. The former examined promotion-framed and prevention-framed infor mation in terms of green advertising appeals, where messages focus on personal health appeals or environmental appeals [12]. The latter focused on people’s prosocial behavior (donation for water cleaning) and investigated the fit between message framing and visual design (cute vs. aggressive) on the effectiveness of self-benefit versus other-benefit appeals. In addition to focusing on message framing based on regulatory focus, the majority of these studies were concerned with the interplay between message framing and individuals’ self-construal.

## 2.2. Self-construal, regulatory focus theory, and green consumption

Research has indicated that self-construal is related to the effects described by regulatory focus theory [14]. Self-construal is defined as the relationship between the self and others and, especially, the degree to which individuals see themselves as separate from others or connected with others [33]. Individuals with an independent self-construal pay more attention to self-reliance and autonomy [34]. They strive to ward being unique, different, and separate from others (e.g., “I am a doctoral student in physics"). By contrast, individuals with an interdependent self-construal value their relationship with others and inter personal harmony [34]. They see themselves as part of a social group and strive toward blending and fitting in (e.g., “I am a member of the physics student community.”). Self-construal was originally conceptu alized as a culturally determined self-schema [33]. Subsequent research has shown that situational primes and contextual factors can make one or the other self-construal temporarily more salient regardless of culture, thus proving that the two self-construals co-exist within an individual [35]. In this condition, the more salient self-construal can direct an in dividual’s cognitive, emotional, and motivational processes, thereby further influencing their perceptions, attitudes, and behaviors [34].

Regarding the relationship of self-construal and regulatory focus, research suggests that one’s “self-construal” may be an antecedent of regulatory focus effects [34]. The theoretical rationale of this contention is goal compatibility [34]. It demonstrates that the independent goal of achievement and autonomy is compatible with a promotion focus (i.e., growth needs). Thus, independent people strive to separate from others positively and focus on possible achievements and gains. The interde pendent goal of adhering to social norms and fulfilling duties and social responsibilities is compatible with a prevention focus (i.e., security needs). People with a dominant interdependent self-construal concen trate on their negative features and are inclined to be concerned with avoiding behavior that might lead to social conflicts.

According to the regulatory fit theory [28], regulatory fit is defined as the enhanced motivational intensity that results when a match occurs between how people pursue a goal and their goal orientation. The theory suggests that when information is presented in a frame that is compat ible with individuals’ regulatory focus, the experience of regulatory fit can increase the intensity of people’s responses; specifically, a positive response becomes more positive, and a negative response becomes more negative [36]. This effect is known as the regulatory fit effect. For example, when consumers experience a regulatory fit, they tend to feel good about their behaviors and have more confidence in their decisions and more positive attitudes toward products, thus resulting in greater (purchase) intention [37]. In terms of goal compatibility, the goal of people with different self-construals is compatible with distinguished regulatory focus orientations. Thus, to satisfy their promotion or pre vention goals, people with different self-construals have different in formation strategies [14]. From the aspect of message framing based on regulatory focus, studies have demonstrated that individuals with an independent self-construal prefer promotion-framed information [34]. By contrast, interdependent individuals are more attracted by prevention-framed information [34].

Several studies on regulatory fit between message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal revealed inconsistent findings. For example, Lin et al. [21] examined the influence of regulatory fit on in formation persuasion by involving the moderating role of perceived risk. They indicated that the regulatory fit effect was evident when the perceived risk was low, whereas prevention-focused information was more persuasive when the perceived risk was high, independent from individuals’ self-construal. Kwon and Sung [29] explored the interplay of online reviews framed by promotion or prevention goals and people’s self-construal under two product categories, namely, digital cameras and hotels. Their results demonstrated that the compatibility of inde pendent self-view with promotion-framed information was effective for both products. However, the reverse condition was supported only for digital cameras, thus suggesting that the regulatory fit effect may vary depending on product categories.

In the realm of green consumption, Kareklas et al. [12] revealed an opposite result for messages with different appeals: promotion-framed (vs. prevention-framed) messages matched with independent (vs. interdependent) self-construal for personal health appeals but prevention-framed (vs. promotion-framed) messages matched with in dependent (vs. interdependent) self-construal for environmental ap peals. By incorporating the third self-construal, metapersonal self-view described by a “universal focus” and featured as an other-focused thinking mode, Kareklas et al. [12] contended that an environmentally sustainable manner is more compatible with universal goals (e.g., pro tecting the environment for everyone’s profit). However, many other pro-environmental or pro-social behavior studies have stated that egoistic and altruistic considerations exist when people evaluate or decide on specific behaviors [13]. Specifically, people with egoistic orientation prioritize myself-related issues and fear the negative influ ence of climate change on their local environment, thus showing a self-benefit motivation. By contrast, altruistic people prioritize issues associated with others and nature and fear the negative influence of environmental degradation on the whole society, thus showing an other/social-benefit motivation. The inspiration for these two motiva tions is contingent on consumers’ features and consumption context [32]. For example, independent self-construal has an egoistic environ mental concern, while interdependent self-construal has an altruistic environmental concern [38].

## 2.3. Temporal distance in green consumption

Construal level theory suggests that objects, events, and individuals can be perceived as being either psychologically near or distant from different dimensions of psychological distance [39]. For example, ac tions may take place in a distant versus near future (i.e., temporal dis tance), may be taken for other people versus oneself (i.e., social distance), may occur at a remote versus close location $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ spatial dis tance), or may happen uncertainly versus certainly (i.e., hypothetical distance). Construal level theory [18] argues that people form more abstract representations (high-level construal) of psychologically distant actions and more concrete statements (low-level construal) of psycho logically near actions. For instance, in the near future, people will describe similar actions in relatively more concrete, means-related terms $( \mathbf { e . g . }$ ., construing the act of reading a book as reading an e-book) rather than more abstract, ends-related terms $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . g . } }$ , reading to create a relaxing mood) [40]. Overall, psychological distance can affect people’s repre sentation, prediction, evaluation, and behavior through the mediating effect of construal levels [41]. Therefore, a growing number of scholars are aware of the value of psychological distance for understanding consumer behavior, especially from a temporal distance perspective.

Given that benefits from pro-environmental behavior can be near or distant, the effect of temporal distance on green consumption has eli cited research attention. Gao et al. [19] examined the influence of public recognition on price sensitivity of green products and considered the moderating role of temporal distance, thus highlighting the relevance of the time factor to maintain the continuity of green consumption. Considering the gain–loss message frame, Chang et al. [7] explored the moderating role of temporal distance on consumers’ attitudes and intention toward green consumption. As evident from this literature review, scarce research has focused on the interrelationship of message framing based on regulatory focus and temporal distance. Mcdonald [42] collectively reviewed relevant studies about whether framing climate change-related events as close (such as happening now, tem poral distance) could encourage climate protection action and revealed mixed findings, thus suggesting further investigation concerning the role of temporal distance in the effect of green message frames on in dividuals’ green behaviors. This study explores the three-way interplay of temporal distance and regulatory fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal.

## 3. Hypothesis development

## 3.1. Regulatory fit of message framing and self-construal

On the basis of regulatory fit theory, self-construal, goal compati bility, and egoistic and altruistic motivation of green consumption, we advocate that green consumption brings self-related and others-related interests for people; hence, it can trigger different considerations of egoistic or altruistic consequences depending on individuals’ dominant self-views. Specifically, the independent self-view emphasizes autonomy and individuality and focuses more on self-enhancement and dis tinguishing from others [33]. Emphasis on their own rights drives them to be more concerned about myself-related issues [38]. For environ mental appeals, when myself-focused consideration is activated, people with independent self-construal are prone to place more weight on consequences for themselves (e.g., environmental degradation will threaten jobs for people like me). This mode is compatible with their independent goal orientation.

Conversely, people with interdependent self-view highlight re lationships with group members and value social harmony and connection with important others [33]. A focus on the maintenance of group harmony increases their concern for other-related issues [38]. For environmental degradation, when their other-focused motivation is triggered, they act more selflessly and consider the effects on other people more. This mode is in accordance with their interdependent orientation. Therefore, although they have been primed with self-construal situationally and exposed to environmental appeals, if independent (vs. interdependent) self-views activate their emphasis on myself (vs. others)-related aspects, the effects of goal compatibility and regulatory fit could provide an impetus for them to meet their promotion (vs. prevention) goals. It results in adopting matched information stra tegies- independent (vs. interdependent) self-view with promotion (vs.

prevention)-framed messages. Thus, we expect to replicate the results of Aaker and Lee [14] in a new context, that is, the green consumption context.

In addition to investigating the regulatory fit of message frame and self-construal, researchers have also strived to uncover its underlying mechanism. One explanation for this regulatory fit effect is that the increased attention and elaboration under conditions of compatibility lead to stronger persuasion effects [15]. That is, people experiencing fit are more engaged and hence more likely to devote themselves to a subsequent task. In a similar vein, engagement and processing fluency is proposed to explain the nature of regulatory fit [16]. However, extant studies have not directly examined the existence of “increased attention and elaboration” [14] or have measured “engagement and processing fluency” by participants’ self-reported measures [16]. Self-reported measures could be susceptible to measurement biases, such as subjec tivity and social desirability [17]. To overcome these issues, this study adopts eye-tracking technology. Eye-tracking is a research technique that focuses on participants’ eye movement tracking in a realistic stimulus-based setting to generate data. This method is considered more reliable because it is based on actual behavior and provides reliable measures of attention to stimuli. This study is the first to attempt to uncover the underlying cognitive process of the regulatory fit between message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal from a visual attention aspect. We posit that the regulatory fit of message framing and self-construal can induce enhanced attention, elaboration, and engagement.

Hence, drawing upon the arguments of regulatory focus, selfconstrual, goal compatibility, regulatory $\mathrm { f i t } ,$ and egoistic and altruistic motivation of green consumption, when individuals’ self-construal and their me/others-related considerations are activated, we propose the following hypotheses:

H1a: Promotion-framed green information will attract more atten tion from individuals with a dominant independent self-construal.

H1b: Prevention-framed green information will attract more atten tion from individuals with a dominant interdependent self-construal. H2a: Promotion-framed green information will lead to higher PI for individuals with a dominant independent self-construal.

H2b: Prevention-framed green information will lead to higher PI for individuals with a dominant interdependent self-construal.

## 3.2. Temporal distance, message framing, and self-construal

The way people look at their self $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ self-construal) influences how they perceive upcoming events in the near or distant future [20]. Two reasons explain this phenomenon. First, given that time is a resource, promotion- and prevention-focus differentially restrict the bounds of temporal imagination. In particular, for independent individuals, their promotion focus inspires goals and aspirations of growth and achieve ment that need more time to implement and complete, developing a distal temporal perspective; the converse occurs for interdependent in dividuals, who develop a proximal temporal perception [43]. Second, interdependent self-construal evokes a more context-dependent cogni tive mode than independent self-construal. People with interdependent self-construal pay close attention to harmonious relationships with others, consider contextualized information and construe relationships between the self and others in concrete and specific social contexts [44]. On the basis of construal level theory, this attention to the “concrete” and “contextualized” is more likely to prompt a low-level construal and a proximal temporal perspective. Nevertheless, independent individuals feel higher levels of self-control and personal agency than interdepen dent individuals, associated with more abstract representations and more distal temporal perspectives [45].

Lee et al. [34] proposed and verified a temporal fit effect, which suggests that people with a more accessible interdependent (vs. inde pendent) self-construal perceive future events as temporally more proximal (vs. distal), and people’s response, motivation, and engage ment toward a task will be enhanced when the temporal distance to the task matches their self-construal. Spassova and Lee [15] explored this temporal fit effect on persuasion, revealing that compatibility between the time frame of an advertisement and the self-construal of the con sumer leads to changes in the persuasive effect of advertising and product appeal. According to hypotheses H1a and H1b, promotion-framed (vs. prevention-framed) information will attract increased attention for individuals with independent (vs. interdepen dent) self-construal. When considering the three-way interaction with the time factor, referring to temporal fit effect contended by Lee et al. [34] and Spassova and Lee [15], we propose that the fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal might be stronger under a condition of temporal fit. Accordingly, we predict that the distant future will elicit independent consumers’ greater perceived in formation attractiveness of promotion-framed information and stronger purchase intention, whereas the proximal future will elicit interdepen dent consumers’ greater perceived information attractiveness of prevention-framed information and stronger purchase intention. Thus, we hypothesize the following:

H3a: Compared with a proximal future, promotion-framed infor mation will be more attractive to individuals with a dominant in dependent self-construal in a distal future.

H3b: Compared with a distal future, prevention-framed information will be more attractive to individuals with a dominant interdepen dent self-construal in a proximal future.

H4a: Compared with a proximal future, promotion-framed infor mation will lead to magnified PI for individuals with a dominant independent self-construal in a distal future.

H4b: Compared with a distal future, prevention-framed information will lead to magnified PI for individuals with a dominant interde pendent self-construal in a proximal future.

## 4.1. Experiment design

We conducted a laboratory experiment within three weeks to test the hypotheses using product guidance information. Referring to Kwon and Sung [29], we selected a durable green product: the solar air conditioner.

## 4.1.1. Message framing based on regulatory focus

To disentangle the interaction of message framing based on regula tory focus and self-construal, given the social media context, guidance information with green product messages and a cartoon illustration was designed for the stimuli of message framing to enhance its attractiveness and interestingness (Appendix A). On the basis of the definitions, the promotion/prevention-framed information was artificially produced by the experimenters. Specifically, the promotion-framed message was organized by emphasizing that the adoption of solar air conditioners could improve environmental health and promote efficient and clean energy use. The prevention-framed message emphasized that solar air conditioners could avoid the deterioration of air quality and energy crisis and reduce the waste of resources. Adapted from Aaker and Lee [14], the manipulation of message framing can be checked by asking participants, “to what extent do you perceive message 1 highlights environmental protection, energy efficiency, and economy?” (promo tion focus), and “to what extent do you perceive message 2 emphasizes the avoidance of air pollution, energy crisis, and wasting of resources?” (prevention focus) by a seven-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree; 7 = strongly agree)

## 4.1.2. Self-construal

We primed self-construal by asking participants to imagine they were competing in a tennis tournament either as an individual player (inde pendent construal) or as a team player (interdependent construal) (Appendix B). Prior research established the validity of this priming method [14]. Additionally, slogans for cheering for the tournament were used to strengthen the priming further. Participants who were randomly assigned to the independent self-construal condition read the message to enhance their own aspirations and to promote their personal growth and accomplishment. The others who were randomly assigned to the inter dependent self-construal condition read the message to show group honor and to promote the community’s power and prestige. To measure participants’ primed self-construal, we used the Self-construal Scale [35], which consists of a 24-item scale. We selected 18 items that were best suited for this study to capture situational self-construal (Appendix C). Participants responded on a seven-point scale (1 = strongly disagree; 7 = strongly agree).

## 4.1.3. Dependent variables

Research has contended that attention can represent people’s infor mation selecting and processing strategies (e.g., which information is more attractive) [17]. To examine perceived information attractiveness toward the promotion–prevention information frame, we used a Tobii Pro-Lab X64 eye tracker to capture participants’ visual attention to green information. This eye-tracking application has been used in recent studies on consumer information processing [46]. A randomization technique was used to eliminate the influence of the habitual reading order (reading from left to right). We set up two types of information presentation to enhance the measurement validity of perceived infor mation attractiveness, namely, promotion frame (left) versus prevention frame (right) and prevention frame (left) versus promotion frame (right). Participants were randomly assigned to each condition to alle viate common method bias [31]. Purchase intention was evaluated by the question [21] “Based on the above information, how likely is it that you might purchase the solar air conditioner shared on Weibo?” along a seven-point scale anchored by 1 (not at all) to 7 (extremely).

## 4.4. Discussion

Overall, the purpose of Study 1 is to confirm the regulatory fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal in the context of green consumption. Prior research has recognized that regulatory-framed information that matches one’s self-construal tends to be more attractive, effective, and persuasive [30]. By situationally priming participants’ self-construal, the results from eye-tracking data support the prediction that promotion-framed information is more attractive and focused for individuals with independent self-construal (H1a), whereas individuals with interdependent self-construal gravi tate more toward prevention-framed information (H1b). Additionally, people with independent self-view report higher PI to products with promotion-focused green information (H2a), while people with inter dependent self-view indicate higher PI to products with prevention-focused green information (H2b).

## 5. Study 2: effect of temporal distance on the match effect of message framing and self-construal

Study 1 verified the regulatory fit of self-construal and message framing based on regulatory focus, as indicated by Aaker and Lee (2001), by revealing that people’s egoistic or altruistic environmental concerns can be activated in such conditions. Additionally, this effect i evident in decision-making scenarios by affecting consumers’ purchase intentions. As mentioned, time is one of the most common factors that result in differences in consumers’ responses and behavior [20]. Hence, Study 2 extends Study 1 and further considers the regulatory fit effect of message framing and self-construal with varied temporal distance. Specifically, we expect that a distant future can strengthen independent consumers’ perceived information attractiveness to promotion-framed information (H3a) and purchase intention (H3b). However, for inter dependent consumers, the proximal future can enhance their willingness to adopt prevention-framed information (H4a) and their purchase intention (H4b). Study 1 is an eye-tracking laboratory experiment with a student sample. To increase the generalizability of our findings, in Study 2, we conducted an online experiment with the general consumers within four weeks. For greater generalizability, further evidence on the regulatory fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal in the green context was also provided by using another green product.

## 5.1. Experiment design

The online experiment was a 2 (temporal distance: distal vs. prox imal) × 2 (self-construal: interdependent vs. independent) × 2 (message framing: promotion-framed vs. prevention-framed) mixed design. Selfconstrual and temporal distance were between-subject factors, and message framing was a within-subjects factor. Participants were randomly assigned into four groups. Similar to Study 1, two dependent variables were concerned, namely, perceived information attractiveness and purchase intention.

## 5.1.1. Message framing

For greater generalizability, another durable green product, a new energy vehicle, was selected as a representative to examine whether the effects found in Study 1 are valid across different durable green prod ucts. Similarly, product guidance information with cartoon illustrations and messages shared on Weibo was designed for the manipulation of message framing based on regulatory focus (Appendix D). Specifically, the promotion-framed green information was organized by emphasizing that the adoption of the new energy vehicles would achieve the goal of improving the environmental health index, making people feel envi ronmentally comfortable and enjoy a subsidy policy. The preventionframed advertisement emphasized that the new energy vehicles could avoid air pollution and exhaust gas emissions and reduce fuel expenses. A similar way to check the manipulation of message framing based on regulatory focus [14] was used by asking the questions “To what extent do you perceive message 1 highlights the environmental protection and additional subsidy?” (promotion focus), and “To what extent do you perceive message 2 emphasizes the avoidance of pollution deterioration and the reduction of fuel expenses?” (prevention focus) using a seven-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree; 7 = strongly agree).

## 5.1.2. Self-construal

Consistent with Study 1, a story and slogans about a tennis tourna ment highlighting “my responsibility” or “our responsibility” to win served as the manipulation of self-construal (Appendix B). However, to increase participants’ involvement, we played an audio message instead of using a text description. A total of 18 items from the Self-Construal Scale [35] were used again to examine participants’ situational self-construal.

## 5.1.3. Temporal distance

Regarding time, we set two levels of temporal distances (tomorrow vs. one year later) as the time context. Similar to prior studies [18], through text description, consumers would be guided to produce different perceptions of the near or distant future. To check temporal perception, participants needed to indicate the time they perceived that they were going to buy the vehicle as near or far by answering the question, “How soon do you perceive the planned purchase time ‘tomorrow’ compared with ‘one year later $\ ? ? ^ { \prime \prime } \left( 1 = \mathbf { v e r y s o o n } ; 7 = \mathbf { v e r y } \right.$ far in the future) [48].

## 5.1.4. Dependent variables

Items anchored on a seven-point scale were used to measure in dividuals’ perceived information attractiveness toward specific regulatory-framed green information [49]: “Based on the above infor mation, to what extent are you attracted to the product guidance in formation?”; and purchase intention [21]: “Based on the above information, how likely is it that you might purchase the new energy vehicle?”

## 5.2. Experiment procedure

A total of 245 citizens in China (44% female, aged 18–55, 50% with bachelor’s degrees and above) were enrolled via online social media (e. g., WeChat) and randomly assigned to the experimental scenario through Wenjuanxing. They were given \$3 as a reward.

At the beginning of the experiment, the participants were informed that it comprised two parts, namely, self-role evaluation and green product evaluation. They were first exposed to role evaluation materials, which aimed to prime specific self-construal. Next, they were introduced to the scenario (including time cue) “Imagine that you (or your family) plan to purchase a new car tomorrow (or one year later) and are inter ested in popular new energy vehicles. The following are two product guidance messages of new energy vehicles shared on Weibo.” The two messages were randomly presented to participants individually. After viewing the first claim, the participants needed to evaluate their perceived information attractiveness and intention to purchase the new energy vehicle by referring to all provided information (i.e., self construal, temporal distance, message framing based on regulatory focus). The same procedure was repeated for the second message. Thereafter, participants were provided with questions related to sociodemographics (e.g., age, gender, marriage and education) and manip ulation checks. Finally, they were debriefed and given a reward. The procedure of the experiment in Study 2 is shown in Fig. 6.

## 5.4. Discussion

The main purpose of Study 2 is to examine the enhanced effect of temporal distance on the regulatory fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal in terms of perceived information attractiveness and purchase intention toward green products. ## 6. General discussion

Study 1 corroborates that when an individual has a more accessible independent (vs. interdependent) self-construal and the information is promotion-framed (vs. prevention-framed), it results in regulatory fit between the way information is construed and the presented informa tion [34]. Eye-tracking results support this notion by showing that more attention was allocated when a fit occurs between message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal. Such a match also con tributes to a higher purchase intention. The findings of regulatory fit ir this study are compatible with previous consumer behavior studies (e.g., [30]). Empirical evidence from these studies has shown that when people encounter regulatory-framed information that is compatible with their self-construal, they are more likely to be persuaded. However, findings of regulatory fit effect in the green context are controversial. Under the condition of environmental appeals, Kareklas et al. [12] re ported a considerably different relationship. In our study, we focus on green consumption conditions, and our findings are consistent with Aaker and Lee [14] but opposite to Kareklas et al. [12]. An explanation for this result could be that people have egoistic and altruistic envi ronmental concerns. and environmental appeals can activate their self or other-related thoughts depending on their more salien self-construals. However, Kareklas et al. [12] emphasized the meta personal self-view and presumed that environmental appeals could trigger people's metapersonal self-view and drive their universal-focused thinking mode. In this study, we attested implicitly (in main experiments) and explicitly (in supplemental experiments) that when processing environmental appeals, people’s egoistic or altruistic motivation can be aroused under corresponding conditions of self-construals. Hence, our study extends the finding of Kareklas et al. [12] that environmental appeals can mainly propel people’s concern about universal benefits. A potential reason for the change in in dividuals’ thinking modes toward environmental appeals is that people have increasingly acknowledged the direct influence of environmenta degradation on themselves. For example, in 2021 alone, several extreme natural disasters have occurred in China: giant sandstorms in northern China (three times from March to May) and intense downpours in Zhengzhou City (in July) and Shanxi province (in October). These event affect the public profoundly and change their attitudes toward nature As elucidated in this study, people are not solely universal-focused when facing environmental appeals. They can generate egoistic and altruistic concerns, and which of the two is predominated is contingent on thei self-construals.

Furthermore, scholars suggest that one possible underlying process of regulatory fit between message frame and self-construal is that compatibility increases attention, elaboration, and engagement; that is, people selectively attend to and engage more on information that matches their salient self-construal [15]. However, research that has directly examined this inference is scant [15], and limited research has primarily relied on consumers’ self-reported statements in question naires [16]. The present study is the first to adopt eye-tracking tech niques to explicitly demonstrate the underlying cognitive mechanisms of regulatory fit from a visual attention perspective. We found that message framing based on regulatory focus that matches individuals self-construal attracts more attention (redder in heat maps), and a longer fixation duration on compatible information reveals increased elabora tion and engagement.

On the basis of the findings of regulatory fit between message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal found in Study 1, Study 2 further highlights and verifies the enhanced effect of temporal distance. Specifically, people with an activated interdependent selfconstrual are likely to perceive future events as temporally proximal because they are more sensitive to the negatives (e.g., negative out comes or goals), which are more salient in the proximal future [50].

Therefore, when confronted with an imminent purchase of green products, they have a stronger perceived information attractiveness toward prevention-framed information and higher purchase intention. When the purchase of green products is still far off in the future, people with an activated independent self-construal tend to be more attracted by promotion-framed information and exhibit higher behavioral inten tion. This enhanced effect of temporal fit is consistent with the findings of previous studies incorporating the interaction of self-construal and temporal distance [20] as well as the three-way interplay of message frame, self-construal, and temporal distance [41]. These studies indicate that temporal fit significantly improves consumers’ evaluation of effectiveness, attractiveness, attitude, and behavior [41]. However, the examination of three-way interaction in extant research is limited and mainly focused on the gain-loss message frame in the healthcare context [41]. Therefore, this study extends the temporal fit effect into a three-way interaction context with the message frame of regulatory focus and expands its application into the green consumption domain.

## 7. Conclusion

As concerns for the environment are growing, green consumption is receiving mounting attention from policymakers, marketing strategists, consumers, and researchers [51]. Across two studies, this study exam ined how marketers can use product guidance information delivered on social media to promote consumers’ green consumption from an inte grative perspective of message framing based on regulatory focus, self-construal, and temporal distance. Study 1 reveals a match between one’s accessible self-view and message framing based on regulatory focus in a green context. Such a match can result in favorable outcomes in the context of persuasion. Study 2 further verifies the enhanced effect of temporal distance on the regulatory fit effect found in Study 1.

## 7.1. Theoretical implications

The present research makes theoretical contributions on multiple fronts. First, this study contributes to the literature by introducing message framing based on regulatory focus in green consumption research [12] and examining the regulatory fit of this message framing and self-construal, thereby extending existing message frames and reg ulatory fit effect in this domain. As a way to promote pro-environmental behavior, message framing has recently elicited research attention [5]. However, the extant research focusing on message frames in the green consumption domain is limited and leaves many issues to be investi gated. On the basis of the rationale of regulatory focus, self-construal, goal compatibility, and regulatory fit, we examined this regulatory fit and confirmed that environmental appeals could induce people’s egoistic or altruistic consideration depending on their dominant self-construal.

Second, we further revealed the underlying cognitive mechanism of the regulatory fit effect from a visual attention perspective by eyetracking technology. It supplies the lack of direct scrutiny into the un derlying process of the regulatory fit effect and extends the limited studies that mainly rely on the self-report approach. The eye-tracking results support the prior empirical inference and indicate the cognitive process of attention allocation, elaboration, and engagement underlying the regulatory fit effect.

Finally, referring to the empirical evidence that temporal distance perception is affected by self-construal [20], this study introduces a boundary condition of temporal distance. It provides a more compre hensive view of the three-way interaction, incorporating the regulatory fit of message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal as well as the temporal fit of self-construal and temporal distance to the study of green consumption. The theoretical foundation in this study provides theoretical reference for future research on green consumption.

## 7.2. Practical implications

This study is important for marketing practitioners because it can help them understand how a message frame based on regulatory focus matches with individuals’ self-construal and how temporal distance can magnify this regulatory fit effect. From a practical standpoint, first, the current research indicates that participants with situationally primed independent (vs. interdependent) self-construal focus more on promo tion (vs. prevention)-framed information which is more compatible with their egoistic (vs. altruistic) motivation. Therefore, by adopting psy chographic segmentation, marketers can tailor their campaigns to suit individuals with different self-construals to enhance green consumption [52]. For example, when composing green product information shared on social media, marketers can combine figures featuring a single person (vs. a group of people) and texts emphasizing oneself (vs. ourselves) to prime consumers’ independent (vs. interdependent) self-construal. Accordingly, marketers can describe green messages based on regula tory focus depending on receivers’ primed self-construals, thereby improving their information strategies more effectively.

Second, our research highlights the enhanced effect of temporal fit. Our findings suggest how practitioners can use the right combination of message framing based on regulatory focus, self-construal, and temporal distance to persuade consumers more effectively. For example, using rich secondary data from online platforms, marketers can analyze their consumers’ chronic self-construals by aiding new information technol ogies, as suggested by Wu et al. [53]. When green information is pre sented in a distal (vs. proximal) context, marketers should call for promotion (vs. prevention)-framed messages to increase the persuasive effect aimed at their identified consumers with independent (vs. inter dependent) self-construal. Therefore, an improved understanding of how to select the right combination in the context of green consumption is particularly crucial, given that consumers’ green consumption re mains relatively limited [54]. Our findings lend marketers variations of message framing based on regulatory focus in conjunction with self-construal and temporal distance to promote green consumption more effectively.

## 7.3. Limitations and future research

Although this research contributes to green consumption theoreti cally and practically, it has several limitations that merit highlighting in future research. First, we did not collect participants’ information related to their green behavior, such as their shopping experience with green products, which may be potential factors influencing their pro cessing of green information and intention toward green products. Future research can supplement related information.

Second, this study focused on message framing from a regulatory focus perspective and set it as guidance information for green products shared on social media. Online reviews considerably influence consumer decisions [29][55], particularly in the social commerce context, where people communicate with their friends and rely more on online reviews shared by their acquaintances [17].

Future research can investigate effective information from online reviews that promote green consumption. Notably, Kwon and Sung [29] investigated online reviews with a message frame of regulatory focus in the conventional consumption domain. However, scholars have started investigating the influence of online reviews on consumer evaluation of products with different environmental impacts considering the role of environmental concerns [4].

Drawing from the existing study, future researchers can examine the green consumption context and discern possible boundary conditions to provide a more comprehensive understanding of potential information strategies in terms of online reviews. In addition, specific green features (e.g., energy conservation, degradability, and carbon footprint) can be highlighted in online reviews, helping consumers better understand and evaluate specific green products.

Third, product category can be considered in further investigations on how information strategy influences green consumption. Kwon and Sung [29] suggested that the regulatory fit effect between message framing based on regulatory focus and self-construal in conventional consumption is contingent on product category, such as durable and temporary products. Hence, whether and how product category affects consumers’ green information strategy merits further investigation.

Finally, temporal distance is the factual distance between the present and the point of a specific event (e.g., tomorrow vs. next year) [56]. According to this definition, we manipulated the temporal distance as the purchase of green products occurring tomorrow or one year later. Future research can continue to examine the three-way interaction found in this study across different contexts. For example, temporal frame is also used to manipulate temporal distance, such as “1644 people die of heart disease every day” (proximal day frame) and “600, 000 people die of heart disease every year” (distal year frame) in Pounders et al. [41]. The time interval between the initial purchase and repurchase (7 days vs. 15 days) is used to manipulate temporal distance by Gao et al. [19]. Thus, future green consumption research concerning temporal distance can formulate ways to present green information combining temporal distance with different manipulation approaches to identify the compatible combination for effective information strategy

to promote green consumption.
