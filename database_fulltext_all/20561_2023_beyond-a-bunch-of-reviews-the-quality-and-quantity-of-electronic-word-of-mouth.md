# Beyond a bunch of reviews: The quality and quantity of electronic word-of-mouth

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Electronic word-of-mouth Online consumer reviews Quality Quantity

## A B S T R A C T

This work proposed an integrated model incorporating review quality and quantity to explore the extent to which consumers adopt electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) information. A linear structural equation model was employed to analyze 435 valid responses and test it against the hypotheses. The empirical results suggest that in terms of e-WOM the influence of review quantity outweighs that of the review quality. Review consistency negatively correlates with informational influence. Consumers are alert to highly consistent reviews and may easily doubt ones that contain similar content. Furthermore, the relationship between perceived persuasiveness and social influence will not be inhibited by confirmation bias.

## 1. Introduction

The Internet has made it easy for consumers to acquire product/ service information, and read and participate in online discussions. This has led to the conceptualization of electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM). otherwise known as online word-of-mouth. Such customer to customer (C2C) information exchanges have been shown to play an important role in decision-making processes [1.2.3]. A recent study has shown that un to 90% of consumers search online for reviews before buying products [4]. Scholars have different opinions on how consumers decision-making is influenced by information-searching. Most studies on e-WOM, in line with Steve Jobs’ belief that “Quality is more important than quantity,” have considered review quality as the most important influence on consumers’ decision-making [5,6,7,8,9]. However, in practice, consumers also care about the quantity of review comments and the quantity of e-WOM is often regarded as the product’s review credibility and popularity [6,10,11]. For example, the data from Statista [12] shows that, on average, consumers read 112 reviews before pur chasing a product/service online. Products with five reviews are 270% more likely to be purchased than those without any reviews [13]. In addition, a study conducted to task 120 adults by comparing pairs of phone cases on Amazon showed that the participants regularly select the phone case with the majority reviews – even though the review ratings for both products are low. Another survey over a greater data set of 400 million customers also discovered that the more reviews a product has, the higher possibility it is that a consumer will buy that product [14]. All these raise the question about whether consumers are more likely to purchase products that have lower ratings but more reviews than products with higher ratings but few reviews.

We proposed an integrated model incorporating review quality and quantity to explore the extent to which consumers adopt e-WOM in formation. Petty and Cacioppo [15] proposed the elaboration likelihood model (ELM), consisting of two main routes, central and peripheral, which affect the delivery of a persuasive message. The central route is logic-driven and employs data and facts to persuade people of an ar gument’s worthiness, and the peripheral route refers to the relationship with positive features, including emotions and celebrity endorsement [16]. In terms of e-WOM, the central route implies the importance of review quality as consumers need to carefully interpret the information to judge perceived informativeness and persuasiveness. The peripheral route stresses the significance of review quantity since intuitive features unrelated to information ontology are usually involved in the associa tion of information, such as the number of reviews or reviewer rankings. In other words, based on this route the individual’s interpretation of information may vary with the quantity of reviews. This study aimed to investigate how review quality and quantity affect consumers through the central and peripheral paths based on the ELM.

Online reviews serve as a kind of social influence. Social influence denotes the process that consumers alter their concepts or behaviors rested on interaction with others who are regarded as possessing similar purchasing experiences or based on experts’ opinions of a product. BrightLocal [17] found that 76% of consumers conceive anonymous online recommendations as being as trustworthy as recommendations from relatives and friends. The social influence theory (SIT) proposed by Kelman [18] shows that after an individual receives the influence of the external society, it will form an important influence on the subsequent behavior. Customers who are particularly satisfied/dissatisfied with a product will express their opinions as recognized by other customers through online reviews. Other customers then learn the reviews as a method of information collection to be notified regarding the product. Such social interrelationship is believed to be equivalent to close dis cussions in the pattern of these interrelationships through online re views. On the other hand, as product referral votes reflect reviewers opinions that are believed to reduce the risk of a social exchange, the volume, rating, and helpfulness of reviews can be employed to assist consumers’ decisions to purchase and facilitate product sales. Zhao et al. [19] differentiated the social influence of reviews on consumers into informational influence and value-expressive influence. The informa tional influence represents the extent to which the individual enhances their understanding of product knowledge by reading reviews. The value-expressive influence represents the degree to which an individual enhances his or her purchasing decision due to the reviews. E-WOM can either deepen consumers’ comprehension of a product or help them obtain the same purchasing experience as the reviewers. Therefore, so cial influence improves consumers’ judgment of e-WOM’s usefulness and promote e-WOM adoption.

In addition, consumers’ preconceptions about products or services, namely, the “confirmation bias,” may play a major role in the decisionmaking process. People have been extensively found to be biased to information persistent with their current beliefs [20]. Consumers are inclined to recognize reviews that affirm their preliminary beliefs as being more helpful, which not only affects what people read but also how they interpret the information [21]. The confirmation bias acts especially a key role when it comes to online search as results pages often show messages with a diversity of perspectives. When consumers observe these messages, they subconsciously filter these messages with their own beliefs or preferences [22]. Confirmation bias can also result in superior perceived helpfulness for positive reviews when the overall product rating is high, and for negative reviews when the overall rating is low. Therefore, the inclusion of confirmation bias helps to clarify the influence of the customer’s own position when viewing the message [23, 24,25].

With the advance of online communications, it is indispensable that the characteristics of e-WOM on adoption are investigated. The theory of interactive media effects (TIME) provides an eminent framework that illustrates the influence of communications on humans according to affordances [26]. According to TIME, media features can play as the catalyst for consumers to prompt psychological correlatives, to be permeated into the media, and in turn to exhibit cognitive, affective, and conative responses. In the field of media interaction, affordances can be seen as perceived properties of the system that spurs users to engage the system in distinct ways [26]. In line with the TIME, the affordance of review quality and quantity can be used to prompt transmission of social influence and e-WOM usefulness, thereby showing action responses [26, 27]. A TIME-based framework was developed to explore the effects of review quality and quantity on e-WOM adoption, which to our knowl edge has not been done in any previous study. This research took actual consumers who have searched for e-WOM as the object and conducted a survey through an online questionnaire. A linear structural equation model was employed for the analysis. This article sheds light on the importance of e-WOM to consumers and the moderating effect of confirmation bias between review quality and social influence. The re sults of this study also provide several practical and theoretical implications.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. Electronic word-of-mouth

As the Internet becomes increasingly popular, more and more con sumers are posting their views on products and services and sharing their purchasing experiences online, helping facilitate consumers’ pur chasing decisions [10]. Accordingly, traditional word-of-mouth has gradually evolved into e-WOM. Hennig-Thurau et al. [28] considered e-WOM as any positive or negative statement completed by potential, actual, or former users regarding a product or company, which can be accessed via various online channels such as forums, blogs, social net works, and review websites. Among these media, online reviews are recognized as the most accessible and popular e-WOM [29,30,31].

Various scholars have studied e-WOM from different perspectives, including the impact of this on brand image [32,33,34]; product posi tioning strategies [35]; consumers’ purchase intention [36,37,38,39]; and trust in e-WOM [40,41,42]. How individuals deal with acquired information affects their decision-making, and Petty and Cacioppo [15] proposed the ELM to explain how individuals manage the information received by e-WOM. The ELM is a dual process theory depicting changing attitudes, which consists of two routes to persuasion: the central route and the peripheral route [28]. On the central route, persuasion will be likely caused by an individual’s careful and thoughtful examination of the true benefits of the information received, such as a review’s quality [5,43,44]. The central route includes a high level of information elaboration, in which the individual receiving the information carefully considers it. With the peripheral route, the amount of review is resulted from an individual’s relationship with positive or negative indications in the stimulus; or through a simple inference regarding the benefits of a recommended position, such as through the amount of reviews of something [10,45]. The indications obtained by the individual taking the peripheral route are in general not correlated to the logical quality of the stimulus.

## 2.2. Review quality and quantity

Review quality has always been considered the key factor in infor mational influence [15]. Researchers have differentiated review quality into perceived informativeness and perceived persuasiveness $[ 6 , 4 6 , 4 7 ,$ 48]. Perceived informativeness refers to consumers’ overall perceptions of information, while perceived persuasiveness represents consumers views on language statements [6]. Zhou et al. [46] proposed that both perceived informativeness and perceived persuasiveness represent a kind of perceived information value as each consumer has different demands and perceptions of information. Even the same information may have different influences on consumers [49]. Both perceived informativeness and perceived persuasion reflect the extent to which information can meet consumers’ needs as well as the ability of con sumers to evaluate and perceive information [6]. To explore what fac tors influence people’s perceptions of different information, review quality has been widely studied in online reviews [6,9,50]; social net works [2,46,48]; interpersonal communication [51]; health information [52,53]; etc.

Review quantity is composed of intuitive amount of reviews and review consistency [7,9,19]. The number of reviews is regarded as important intuitive information in both e-WOM and social media research [6,9,50,54,55]. Major search engines and review websites usually quantify reviews on their front pages, making this an effective judgment indicator for consumers. Individuals thus tend to compare the amount of reviews to the popularity of products. More reviews mean the product is considered to be more popular $[ 6 , 1 0 , 1 1 ]$ . Both purchase risk and consumer anxiety have been found to be reduced when seeing the amount of reviews in e-WOM, even when some of the content is negative [10,11,45]. Meanwhile, review consistency represents the degree to which the content of a perceived review is consistent with the information in other reviews [7]. Review consistency is an important feature that reinforces consumers’ belief that review quantity is mean ingful [19]. When consumers read user reviews for a particular product, they are exposed to a group of people who express their personal opinions of the product. Consumers’ evaluation of a product is typically based on a group of reviewers rather than a single reviewer [19]. When reviews are generally consistent (e.g., most consumers complain about the same thing), consumers will perceive these reviews as credible [5]. Mannes [56] found that people rely more on groups than individuals to update their beliefs. As the size of a group increases, group opinions will be perceived as more accurate in terms of objectivity and representa tiveness [57]. Furthermore, from a psychological point of view, the content of consistent e-WOM will be generally accepted in the virtual community and possess a low possibility of being questioned [58]. Therefore, comment consistency in e-WOM has been regarded as one of the most important intuitive information features in diverse studies [9, 58,59].

## 2.3. Social influence

The term “social influence” describes how society affects the process of acceptance of a set of norms created by people or groups that affect the individual [19]. Previously, scholars have applied the idea of social influence to various fields, including consumers’ online behaviors [19, 60,61]; people’s willingness to engage in health activities [62]; the impact of social influence on consumption patterns [63,64]; interper sonal interaction [65,66]; attitudes toward green consumption [67,68]; etc.

Zhao et al. [19] summarized the social influence of reviewers on consumers as “informational and value-expressive influence.” Con sumers collect relevant information online before buying products while reviewers provide consumers with more details about products to generate informational influence, which mainly occurs through inter nalization. When the information provided is considered helpful and acceptable, informational influence will occur [68]. While browsing e-WOMs, consumers usually take reviewers as the reference group in order to choose products mostly favored by others. This is where value-expressive influence arises. Under the value-expressive influence, individuals will adopt the same behaviors as an individual or a certain group to form a connection. They tend to link their behaviors with the reference group to retain a self-defined relationship as a group member [19]; this action generates an identity. In comparison to utilitarian in fluence, value-expressive influence requires no external rewards and punishments. Individuals will act according to the beliefs or values of the reference group out of the willingness to improve their self-image. For instance, they may buy expensive brands or otherwise partake in conspicuous consumption to conform to the mores of popular and high-ranking groups [69].

Prior to conducting the research, we considered that e-WOM is likely to impose the same social influence on consumers. Because, according to SIT, people tend to revise their perceptions based on information from other members of the outside world [70]. This phenomenon is more common today in the Internet generation. According to the anchoring effects in judgment [71], people may adopt an anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic when making a decision. Con sumers constantly adjust their views based on external information to achieve the final choice. Specifically, the average ratings of other con sumers constitute an anchored initial value and then the focal consumer makes corresponding modifications according to the perceived discon firmation based on his/her consumption experience. The anchoring ef fect of judgments is more pronounced when the searched information is further similar to past experience, and vice versa [72]. Through online reviews, consumers confirm biases based on their own experiences, read the latest product information, and build a sense of identity with re viewers in order to make purchasing decisions similar to theirs. To investigate this idea, this paper divided the influence of e-WOM into two aspects: informational influence and value-expressive influence. Through informational influence, consumers can glean product knowl edge from e-WOM [19], while e-WOM can enhance consumers self-concepts to affect their purchase decisions through value-expressive influence [61].

## 2.4. Adoption theory

Adoption theory is a term used to explain the intentional use of in formation [73]; it includes information usefulness and information adoption. Information usefulness represents the extent to which in dividuals believe that the use of information and opinions will enhance or improve their performance in a certain area [73]. Information adoption represents the degree of acceptability of information after in dividuals have carefully evaluated the content of the information and considered it to be beneficial [74].

Based on the technology acceptance model [75], the intention to adopt a certain behavior/technology relies on the belief and evaluation of the results post-adoption. The usefulness of adopting the behav ior/technology is particularly important to affirm adoption intention [76]. In addition, information that relates to problem-solving will be given priority while people are searching for and selecting useful in formation [77]. Cheung et al. [73] proposed that the usefulness of in formation could increase the likelihood of people adopting it. As the effect of the information may alter from person to person, even infor mation with the same content may have different influences on the re ceivers [2]. Hence, adoption theory has been widely used in the field of information science to explore how users integrate information and adopt it in distinct disciplines such as e-commerce websites [45,78,79]; social media [2,45,80]; e-mail [41]; etc.

In terms of e-WOM, consumers will search for product reviews before making a purchase so as to collect useful information for better decisionmaking. E-WOM usefulness thus is a key influential factor in e-WOM adoption. Based on the previous research, this study defined e-WOM usefulness as how it can enhance consumers’ purchase decision-making [73]. Meanwhile, e-WOM adoption indicates the extent to which in dividuals accept and adopt opinions from the Internet after evaluating the content of e-WOM [74].

## 2.5. Confirmation bias

As a type of cognitive bias, confirmation bias means that individuals favor information that confirms their existing beliefs or attitudes and ignores what contradicts these. Scholars have found that there are two main reasons for confirmation bias. First, individuals are motivated to have a positive attitude toward themselves and their futures. They pay more attention to confirmatory information and selectively search for information consistent with previous beliefs [81,82,83]. Second, when faced with information that does conflict with previous beliefs, in dividuals are likely to feel psychologically uncomfortable [84]; there fore, individuals will have negative preconceptions against such information to enhance the confidence in their beliefs [83,85,86,87]. Related literature has also pointed out that the more individuals believe in their original idea, the greater their confirmation bias will be [88].

In addition to the general psychological research [89,90,91], confirmation bias has been found to exert an influence on other be haviors in daily life, including the personal collection and sharing of information [92,93]; political participation in candidate elections [94, 95]; losses in investment and financing [96]; and erroneous judgment in a criminal investigation [97,98]. Taking confirmation bias as the moderator, Zhao et al. [93] proposed that when there is a high level of confirmation bias, Internet users will be more active to share health-related information on the Internet. Park et al. [99] pointed out that when a company is accused of misconduct, confirmation bias by consumers who expected this to happen will increase the negative perception of the company.

Based on the above literature, it can be deduced that confirmation bias almost affects every individual during the processing of informa tion. It is therefore likely that when consumers use e-WOM to collect information, they will believe and be affected by reviews because of confirmation bias. Confirmation bias arises when information is taken as the moderator between e-WOM quality and social influence.

## 3. Research framework and hypotheses

This paper attempts to use the TIME framework to examine the impact of review quality and quantity [100,101] on e-WOM adoption. TIME explains the propositions that relate affordances, transmission, and outcomes. Affordance accounts for the initial stimulus for in teractions between medium and consumers, mainly through the mes sage feature to enhance the psychological effect of transmission and subsequent outcomes. The framework also illustrates the influence of medium on individuals included in the communication [28], which in turn drives the e-WOM adoption. ## 3.1. Relationship between e-WOM and social influence

In line with the ELM, e-WOM reception can be divided into the central path and the peripheral path. Argument quality, as a variable of the central path, consists of perceived informativeness and perceived persuasiveness. The former represents consumers’ overall perceptions of review quality, while the latter indicates consumers’ views of persuasive review strength [47].

Liu et al. [47] proposed that perceived informativeness can be measured by the real-time performance, relevance, and integrity of e-WOM. Social influence includes informational influence and value-expressive influence, with the former denoting the idea that e-WOM can enhance consumers’ understanding of product knowledge [19]. Madu and Madu [102] proposed that instead of reading carefully, most Internet users browse the information they require. When e-WOM contains relevant information about products or services, it enables consumers to quickly and easily collect information to enhance their understanding of the products. In addition, the more complete infor mation e-WOM provides, the more effective consumers consider the content is, which usually leads to a positive attitude toward e-WOM [9, 23]. In summary, the higher the perceptual information from e-WOM, the more consumers will be willing to refer to the product information provided by this reviewer and internalize it as their own product knowledge. Therefore, it can be concluded that perceived informative ness will have an informational influence.

Value-expressive influence denotes the extent to which a consumer is affected by a group of reviewers as the reference group to promote his/ her self-concept [103]. Bock et al. [104] proposed that when value-expressive influence occurs, individuals pursue to believe and act in a manner the same as those referents. Reviewers who offer online opinions may not be acquainted to the online shoppers. Nevertheless, they can still impose value-expressive influence through social valida tion [105]. Such information will lead to greater persuasion [106], so consumers are inclined to recognize those who provide high-quality arguments, and refer to their opinions to form their own pro duct/service preferences. The high perceived informativeness of e-WOM not only enables consumers to identify information and expect the same experience as a reviewer, but also enhances their confidence in referring to the e-WOM [9]. Hence, perceived informativeness can have a value-expressive influence.

In summary, the perceived informativeness of e-WOM can exert a social influence on consumers that can enable them to quickly grasp relevant product information. Therefore, the following hypotheses were proposed:

H1a: The perceived informativeness of e-WOM has a significant positive effect on informational influence.

H1b: The perceived informativeness of e-WOM has a significant positive effect on value-expressive influence.

Perceived persuasiveness is related to the objectivity and rationality of information (Liu et al., 2019). Within the Internet environment, the persuasive strength of information can directly influence consumers attitudes [23,107,108,109]. Informational influence is expected to be stronger when a consumer is unsure about the right interpretation of reality and/or the proper behavior in a given context and consequently searches to other group members for guidance. Prior studies have found that objective information, namely, information that is understandable and contains specific data. is considered to be more reliable than sub jective information from consumers since the former contains sufficient reasons to support arguments [47]. If e-WOM is regarded as highly persuasive, consumers will consider it effective and take a positive attitude to it, regarding the information they read as credible [23]. The higher the perceived persuasiveness, the more consumers will trust e-WOM product information, which leads to the improvement of their product understanding. Therefore, it can be inferred that perceived persuasiveness will have an informational influence. Additionally, some consumers read reviews before purchasing products/services to reduce their anxiety [45] as it leads them to indirectly confirm that they have made the right choice. If the e-WOM is of high perceived persuasiveness, consumers will be willing to believe that the e-WOM is based on real or actual experience. By observing the reviewers’ decisions and opinions, consumers can get a social identity from the e-WOM that will affect their purchase decisions or they will purchase the same products as reviewers to establish mutual connections [19]. Perceived persuasiveness has been proven to promote consumers’ purchase intentions [11], suggesting that it will have a perceived persuasiveness influence.

In sum, a high level of perceived persuasiveness can better convince consumers of the veracity of the information and opinions they encounter online. It can also encourage consumers to use e-WOM as a source of product information and as a basis for decision-making. In this way, perceived persuasiveness has a social influence on consumers. Based on the above arguments, the following hypotheses were proposed:

H1c: The perceived persuasiveness of e-WOM has a significant pos itive effect on informational influence.

H1d: The perceived persuasiveness of e-WOM has a significant pos itive effect on value-expressive influence.

When a product is widely discussed online, it is likely to have been purchased by many people and its e-WOM is considered to have a reference value for consumers $[ 6 , 1 5 , 1 0 ]$ . A large number of reviews can assist consumers to acquire more information about a product, thereby increasing its popularity and sales [10,110]. Therefore, it can be inferred that the numbers of reviews will have an informational influence. In addition, scholars have believed that the number of reviews of products represents their popularity [45,54,111]. Park et al. [11] discovered that the popularity of products has a positive influence on consumers’ pur chase decisions, reducing their anxiety about making the purchase. By noting the amount of reviews in evaluating the popularity of products, consumers may rationalize their purchase decisions [11]. They may even want to become reviewers by purchasing the product [19]. Therefore, the amount of reviews can have a value-expressive influence.

Based on the arguments above, we felt that the quantity of reviews of a particular product would have a social influence on consumers. A large number of reviews indicates that consumers can gain new knowledge from them. Consumers may also be attracted to products that most people have bought and therefore make the same purchase decision. The following hypotheses were thus proposed:

H2a: Review quantity has a significant positive effect on informa tional influence.

H2b: Review quantity has a significant positive effect on value expressive influence.

Review consistency in e-WOM represents the extent to which pub lished content is reliable and represents the viewpoint of many con sumers [7]. When searching for e-WOM, consumers usually browse multiple reviews. If the content of a review is generally consistent, consumers will consider that the review is credible and useful [1,23,50]. On the contrary, if a review’s content is inconsistent with most of the other reviews of the same product, consumers will feel confused and may consider the inconsistent review to be untrustworthy [19]. Ac cording to the SIT, when reviews are highly consistent it facilitates consumers absorbing and internalizing the opinions of others into their personal understanding [19]. Therefore, review consistency is likely to have an informational influence.

The quantity of online reviews can be employed to evaluate the popularity of the product, while review consistency is considered as reinforcing the confidence of consumers while potentially lessening the concern of risk exposure. People are naturally inclined to believe and follow normative opinions, and when most reviewers have similar opinions consumers will pay more attention to them and accept the views expressed [112,113]. High review consistency relates to the fundamental human desire to follow the majority with one opinion than align with the minority with divided opinions [19]. It can thus be inferred that review consistency will have a value-expressive influence [114].

According to the analysis above, high review consistency can convince consumers that the content of e-WOM represents the values or information generally recognized by the public. This can help consumers to absorb product knowledge as well as generate social influence by following the public’s purchase decisions. The following hypotheses were therefore proposed:

H3a: Review consistency has a significant positive effect on infor mational influence.

H3b: Review consistency has a significant positive effect on valueexpressive influence.

## 3.2. Relationship between social influence and adoption theory

Social influence denotes the process by which an individual’s atti tudes, beliefs, or behavior are altered by the existence or actions of others. According to the adoption theory, information will have different effects on consumer behaviors based on its perceived usefulness [43]. Usefulness is viewed as the main indicator of information adoption and purchase intention [43,71,72] as people tend to interact with useful information and give priority to information with the po tential to solve issues while making judgments and selections [115] E-WOM usefulness refers to how individuals believe that the information they read can enhance or improve their purchase decision-making [116]. When e-WOM has an informational influence, making cus tomers more aware of the quality and characteristics of products or services, they will consider that this content they have read will enable them to make better purchasing decisions. They will then feel positive about the usefulness of e-WOM [19,117]. In addition, the motivation for consumers to search for e-WOM is to seek advice or opinions [2]. Con sumers may form their own preferences by observing reviewers’ par tialities and decisions [19]; or interact with reviewers who share the same values [118] to make the same purchase decisions. Based on these arguments, it can be inferred that the social influence of e-WOM can improve consumers’ shopping performances, thereby enhancing e-WOM usefulness. Therefore, the following hypotheses were proposed:

H4a: Informational influence has a significant positive effect on e-WOM usefulness.

H4b: Value-expressive influence has a significant positive effect on e-WOM usefulness.

E-WOM adoption is defined as the extent to which individuals accept and assume opinions as their own after evaluating content [74]. Con sumers are always exposed to a large amount of e-WOM in the network environment as its usefulness plays an important role in purchase decision-making. Hu et al. [119] found that the information considered to be most useful by consumers will have a greater effect on their pur chase decisions. Several other studies have confirmed that e-WOM usefulness directly affects consumers’ adoption intention [23,43,73, 120]. Based on this theorizing, we proposed the following hypothesis:

H5: E-WOM usefulness has a significant positive effect on e-WOM adoption.

## 3.3. Moderating effect of confirmation bias

According to Nickerson [82], confirmation bias means that people tend to overemphasize information favoring their previously existing beliefs and positions. Generally, people prefer information that is consistent with their beliefs [83,93,121], which affects what they read as well as how they interpret this information [16]. Itzchakov and Van Hareveld [122] pointed out that when people use the Internet to collect information, they actively seek out information that is consistent with their views, attitudes, or expectations. In terms of information-sharing, users like to read and share online articles in line with their interests and avoid articles that conflict with these [123].

Fogg [124] and Cheung et al. [23] found that the existence of confirmation bias affects the credibility of content. With e-WOM, con sumers are more likely to believe the reviews and comments that are in line with their internal thoughts. They may reject inconsistent opinions to reduce the degree of psychological discomfort [84]. Whether it is positive or negative, the confirmation bias of consumers’ selectively ignored information may cause consumers to miss some important e-WOM, resulting in incomplete information about the observed pro duct/service. Hence, it can be inferred that a high level of confirmation bias means consumers will believe the information that supports their internal thoughts. Even if electronic reviews are of a high quality, they may fail to promote consumers’ understanding of products or persuade them to expect a particular experience. The e-WOM therefore cannot provide the reference value, which in turn reduce its informational and value-expressive influences. According to ELM, the moderators should involve both central and peripheral routes. Peripheral routes are a cognitive shortcut for customers to judge information and do not spend too much time thinking. The amount of review and review consistency usually has these characteristics [125]. In the state where information is directly obtained and adopted, it is not easily affected by cognitive bias. The moderating variable does not necessarily have a moderating effect on every central route or peripheral route [126]. Therefore, we proposed the following hypotheses:

H6a: Confirmation bias has a moderating effect on the relationship between perceived informativeness and informational influence. H6b: Confirmation bias has a moderating effect on the relationship between perceived informativeness and value-expressive influence. H6c: Confirmation bias has a moderating effect on the relationship between perceived persuasiveness and informational influence. H6d: Confirmation bias has a moderating effect on the relationship between perceived persuasiveness and value-expressive influence.

## 4. Research design

## 4.1. Questionnaire development and pilot study

We designed an online survey questionnaire for this study. To better comprehend the influence of e-WOM, we centered on review users who recently use e-WOM in the past three days. Similar to previous studies [127,128], we requested each respondent to recall the scene he/she checked such reviews of online Events/Products/Services.

This research adopted the sampling method of purposive sampling, designed online questionnaires, and posted them on social networks (Facebook, Dcard, and PTT) to target consumers searching for e-WOM.

Purposive sampling, at the discretion of the researcher, helps the research focus on a specific topic to investigate [129]. While Facebook is the most popular global social networking service, Dcard and PTT are the most widely visited websites in Taiwan. Krejcie and Morgan [130] investigated the influence of sample size on research activities and proposed that an analysis can reach 95% confidence level and 5% confidence interval if the sample size is greater than 384, when the data set is 1000,000 and above. In this study, we collected a total of 435 valid responses from those respondents who have used e-WOM within the previous three days to ensure the external validity of sampling [127, 128].

The instruments of this study were formed by employing previously validated measures in the literature, involving perceived informative ness [47,73] perceived persuasiveness [47],[109], amount of review [6], review consistency [7], informational influence [19], value-expressive influences [93], e-WOM usefulness [73], e-WOM adoption [74], and confirmation bias [93]. We briefly amended the wording of these items to fit the present research context. All items were evaluated using 7-point Likert scales, where 7 denotes strongly agree and 1 denotes strongly disagree.

To affirm that the meanings of the questions can be precisely com prehended, a back-translation method was employed to convert these items from English into Chinese. Rewording of ambiguous items was also completed to assure clarity. Additionally, to ensure the logical consistency and ease of understanding, the questionnaire was pre-tested by 30 graduate students who had experience in social commerce and one scholar who specializes in information science. The comments gathered from these people offered a basis to revise the construct measures and modify the wording and item sequence. Table 3 lists the measurement items.

## 4.2. Data collection

The collection time was divided into two stages to prevent the concern of common method bias (CMB). During the first stage (November 21 to November 30, 2020: 10 days in total), 247 question naires were collected. After excluding 43 invalid questionnaires, 204 valid questionnaires were acquired with an effective recovery rate of 82.59%. In the second stage (December 21 to December 30, 2020: 10 days in total). The exclusion of 35 invalid questionnaires led to a final 231 valid questionnaires with an effective recovery rate of 86.84%. A total of 435 valid responses were gathered to test against the hypotheses.

The questionnaire design and the conceptual definition of variables were based on the literature. Table 1 gives each construct, the defini tions of the decomposed factors, and the literature resources.

## 4.4. Statistical method

SPSS 21.0 software package was used for the descriptive statistical analysis, and linear structural equation software, AMOS 21.0, was employed to analyze the path coefficients and significance of the hy potheses model. The data analysis consisted of four parts; the first was a descriptive statistical analysis of sample data; the second and third parts were the analysis of reliability and validity; and the final one was the structural equation modeling (SEM) for hypotheses testing.

## 6.2. Amount of e-WOM has a social influence on consumers; review consistency and value-expressive influence are insignificantly correlated

This study analyzed review quantity through the number of reviews and review consistency. Based on the statistical results, H2a $( \beta = 5 8 , p <$ 0.001) and H2b $( \beta = 5 0 , p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ were supported, implying that the number of reviews has a significant positive effect on social influence. Up to now, there has been no research addressing how the amount of reviews affects social influence. This study shows that a large amount of e-WOM can provide consumers with more information about products to exert an informational influence. Aside from the amount of information that can indicate the popularity of products, consumers’ purchase con fidence can be promoted by observing or imitating the purchase be haviors of reviewers, thus exerting a value-expressive influence. More importantly, by examining the significance of the characteristics of each e-WOM, we found that the number of reviews has the strongest effect on social influence. This demonstrates that consumers will not only be affected by the total number of reviews when they search for e-WOM, but the degree of influence is higher than the quality of a review.

However, hypothesis H3a $( \beta = \mathrm { ~ - ~ } 2 9 , ~ p ~ < ~ 0 . 0 0 1 )$ was supported, indicating that review consistency has a significant negative effect on informational influence. This finding is different from that of Zhao et al. [19], which suggests that despite the fact that a high degree of review consistency will allow consumers to draw conclusions quickly, they will not be able to obtain other additional product information from the reviews, resulting in the loss of informational influence. The discrepancy might be explained by the fact that many retailers use rewards or discounts to attract consumers to give them positive feedback and even spend money hiring people to do so, leading to numerous fake reviews. Because of this practice, consumers often think carefully about the credibility of the content of each piece of e-WOM. If the content of re views is similar, consumers may consider that there is a high probability that these have been faked. This makes consumers suspicious of the review content, and the informational influence of e-WOM is reduced accordingly.

Hypothesis 3b $( \beta = - 0 5 , p > 0 . 0 5 )$ was not supported, suggesting that review consistency has no significant effect on value-expressive influ ence. This finding is again different from that of Zhao et al. [19], who suggested that when reviews of a product are consistent, consumers will regard them as a collective opinion or even a social position and will hope to become associated with the reviewers. To explain the discrep ancy, we argue that consumers will question highly consistent reviews as soon as they become aware of the existence of fake reviews. The effect of high consistency on value-expressive influence diminishes accordingly.

## 6.3. Social influence is significantly positively correlated with e-WOM usefulness to affect e-WOM adoption

The statistical results support both hypothesis 4a $( \beta = 3 8 , p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ and hypothesis 4b $( \beta = 5 6 , P < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ , implying that social influence is positively correlated with e-WOM usefulness. Up to now, no study has investigated the correlation between social influence and e-WOM use fulness. The empirical data in this research showed that the social in fluence resulting from e-WOM can improve its usefulness, in which value-expressive influence is the strongest. While the informational in fluence of e-WOM can help consumers increase their familiarity and knowledge of products and services, value-expressive influence makes consumers agree with the suggestions of e-WOM and make the same decision as the reviewers. Therefore, compared with informational in fluence, value-expressive influence can significantly enhance e-WOM usefulness.

Hypothesis 5 $( \beta = 8 9 , p < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ was supported, which indicates that e-WOM usefulness is significantly positively correlated with e-WOM adoption. The results are in line with the adoption theory studies [2,23, 126], which suggest that consumers will be more willing to follow written dictates and suggestions to make their final purchase decision when information is considered substantially helpful.

## 6.4. Confirmation bias may not have a moderating effect between argument quality and social influence

There is no literature on the moderating effect of confirmation bias between review quality and the social influence of e-WOM, and this study reveals that such a moderating effect may not exist. The statistical outcomes suggest that hypothesis 6a $( \beta = \ - 0 . 0 9 , \ p \ < \ 0 . 0 5 )$ and hypothesis 6b $( \beta = \ - 0 . 1 0 , \ p \ < \ 0 . 0 5 )$ were supported, indicating that confirmation bias plays a significant negative mediating role between social influence and perceived informativeness. When e-WOM is of high perceived informativeness, it will generate a stronger social influence. However. confirmation bias will weaken the relationship between perceived informativeness and social influence. Based on the results of this work, we suggest that consumers are usually biased while browsing e-WOM, taking their previous perceptions as facts. No matter how much information e-WOM provides, consumers still tend to accept information that is consistent with their perceptions. Consumers will consider other additional information as having an insufficient reference value and believe that purchasing products may not lead to the same experience. The relationship between perceived informativeness and social influ ence diminishes accordingly.

Hypotheses 6c $( \beta = - 0 5 , p > 0 . 0 5 )$ and 6d $( \beta = - 0 2 , p > 0 . 0 5 )$ ) were not supported, implying that confirmation bias has no significant mediating effect between social influence and perceived persuasiveness. The high perceived persuasiveness of e-WOM can result in a stronger social in fluence, and the relationship between the perceived persuasion and social influence will not be lessened even with the existence of a confirmation bias. The high perceived persuasiveness of e-WOM usually originates from real experiences of the reviewers. Even with a confir mation bias, consumers will consider the information somewhat worthy of consideration. Confirmation bias will then not interfere with the relationship between perceived persuasiveness and social influence.

## 7. Conclusions and implications

## 7.1. Conclusions

This study proposed an integrated model to explore and explain the relationships between e-WOM, social influence, and adoption theory. Our results offer support for the theoretical model and most of our hy potheses, and contribute to the existing research on the validation of decision-making based on the quality and quantity of e-WOM. This study has important findings worthy of attention from those seeking to foster and increase consumers’ information adoption behavior.

First, from the perspective of e-WOM, this study explored the pur chasing decision-making of consumers. After integrating the quality of e-WOM reviews and the amount of reviews through the ELM, in combi nation with the social influence and adoption theories, the importance of e-WOM to consumers and the consumers’ adoption intention was clar ified, confirming the moderating effect of confirmation bias between review quality and social influence.

Second, through the analysis of empirical results we found that the amount of reviews, informational influence, e-WOM usefulness, and e-WOM adoption has the greatest influence on consumers’ information adoption behavior. The larger the number of reviews, the more likely consumers are to obtain additional product information from them, enhancing the informational influence of e-WOM, which in turn makes consumers feel that e-WOM is highly useful and makes them likely to adopt the suggestions of reviewers. This further signifies that consumers are more concerned about review quantity than quality.

Third, the singular finding of this study is that review consistency is negatively correlated with informational influence and insignificantly correlated with value-expressive influence. Unlike previous studies [19], our results suggest that most consumers are alert to highly consistent reviews since they know that these can be faked. Moreover, the results point out that consumers consider that reviews providing both advantages and disadvantages are credible.

Lastly, we found that confirmation bias has a negative mediating effect on the relationship between perceived informativeness and social influence, but no mediating effect between perceived informativenes and social influence. If reviewers only describe the features of a product, confirmation bias will weaken the effect of perceived informativeness on social influence. However, even reviewers propose persuasive and reasonable arguments that conflict with consumers’ initial opinions, the consumers may still consider the reviews as a reference. This means that the relationship between perceived persuasiveness and social influence will not be inhibited by confirmation bias.

## 7.2. Implications for research

The ELM has been widely employed to explain how consumers process information. The research outcomes contribute to the current comprehension of e-commerce by concurrently investigating the rela tive importance of the quality and quantity of e-WOM via the central and peripheral routes, which have not been thoroughly discussed in the ecommerce literature. This work therefore contributes to the theoretical development of the present e-commerce literature by combining the theory of persuasion (i.e., ELM model) with the SIT to differentiate re view quality into perceptual informativeness and perceptual persua siveness. The result of this study also fills the gap in research on e-WOM.

Secondly, while previous studies discuss only the direct relationship between e-WOM and usefulness, this study explains how social influence affects consumers’ decision-making process more comprehensively using the adoption theory. The quality and quantity of e-WOM have an informational and value-performance influence on consumers. Furthermore, value-expressive influence is found to be more effective than informational influence on e-WOM usefulness, indicating that when e-WOM can provide consumers with a sense of identity through their purchasing decisions, its content will be more helpful. The findings of this work can offer researchers insights into the employment of social influence to effectively recognize and explore the important factors of ecommerce websites in a variety of e-WOM contexts from a relational perspective.

Thirdly, confirmation bias has not been thoroughly investigated in past studies regarding e-WOM. To highlight the key role of confirmation bias in the process of consumer information gathering, we used confir mation bias as a moderating variable in ELM. We verified that confir mation bias has a significant moderating influence on the relationship between the perception of e-WOM information and social influence. In general, this work stresses the importance of the simultaneous employment of ELM, social influence, and adoption theory, which have not acquired the attention they deserve from e-commerce professionals, as essential factors for the construction and retention of correlations between business parties and consumers, particularly in e-WOM

contexts.

## 7.3. Implications for practitioners

The research results suggest that the amount of e-WOM reviews has a greater effect on consumers than anything else. Discussion and opinions of products on the Internet are the key to improving purchasing inten tion. Therefore, if a company increases its marketing budget for e-WOM and utilizes forums, this will be an effective investment. However, this study also found that review consistency is negatively correlated with informational influence, but not significantly correlated with valueperformance influence. It can be inferred that today’s consumers pre fer multiview comments, perhaps because some companies have previ ously faked reviews, causing consumers to become more alert to highly consistent e-WOM. Therefore, we suggest that if companies want to conduct e-WOM marketing, rather than paying others for product endorsement or fake reviews, they should invest their attention in driving real consumers to initiate discussions on the Internet. This will hopefully increase sales, as well as avoid the loss of consumer trust.

The empirical results of this study also indicate that the valueperformance influence of e-WOM has a strong effect on its efficacy. This can be explained by the fact that compared to simply helping consumers understand a product, when e-WOM resonates with con sumers the usefulness of it will be greatly improved and consumers will then adopt the suggestions they have read. Many review sites and fo rums have a voting/liking mechanism, allowing consumers to vote on the usefulness of a message, and commenters can get rewards based on the number of appreciative votes or likes. If a reviewer wants to increase their number of votes, in addition to describing product information, they can also think about how to make or strengthen a connection with other consumers to enhance the sense of identity. For example, the reviewer can mention their professional background to interest con sumers of the same profession and make them more inclined to make the same decisions on a product or service as themselves. In this way, a review not only helps consumers make quick decisions, but also allows them to feel that their choices reflect who they are as people. Then the usefulness of the reviews will prompt consumers to vote for reviewers and earn points.

In line with the research results, for consumers with a confirmation bias only a high perception of the persuasiveness of e-WOM can elimi nate their preconceptions. Marketing personnel may wish to invite people who have experienced a product to share their opinions to take part in an advertisement or promotion. If an advertisement contains actual user experience, it can have a higher degree of perceived persuasiveness and sway consumers through confirmation bias to make a purchase through the formation of a sense of identity.

## 7.4. Limitations and future research

Alongside its contributions, this study has limitations. First, the e-WOM discussed in this research is mainly based on surveys posted on professional review websites or social networking sites. The form of e-WOM is constantly evolving, from textual comments and pictures to the current popular videos (such as on YouTube). Therefore, the results of this study may not be applicable to all types of e-WOM. It is recom mended that in the future, scholars explore the impact of different types of e-WOM on consumers. Second, this study only investigated the moderating effect of review quality under different conditions. How ever, consumers’ purchase decision-making process involves many different psychological factors, such as selective memory and empirical learning. Therefore, future research should incorporate other psycho logical variables. In addition, this study focused mainly on the moder ating effect of central path. Future study should include variables that have a moderating effect for both the central and peripheral paths. Finally, the influence of sampling method on the research results remained uncertain. Additional work will be needed to answer this

question.
