# Design shopping as an experience: Exploring the effect of the live-streaming shopping characteristics on consumers’ participation intention and memorable experience

Yu Gu <sup>a</sup>, Xusen Cheng <sup>b,\*</sup>, Jia Shen <sup>c</sup>

<sup>a</sup> School of Economics and Management, Tsinghua University, Beijing,100084, China

<sup>b</sup> School of Information, Renmin University of China, Beijing, 100872, China

<sup>c</sup> Norm Brodsky College of Business, Rider University, Lawrenceville, 08648, United States of America

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords Live-streaming shopping, Stimulus-organismresponse framework, Characteristics of livestreaming shopping Memorable experience Continuous participation intention

## A B S T R A C T

Live-streaming shopping becomes increasingly popular recently. However, little research explored why livestreaming shopping is so successful in continuously promoting sales and engaging consumers. Based on the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework, we explore the effects of five distinct characteristics (information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness) of live-streaming shopping on consumers continuous participation intentions, and investigate the underlving organism from both the momentary states (i e., perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support) and the reflective state (i.e., memorable experience). The results of a multi-method empirical study indicate that the memorable experience is a strong indicator of continued shopping behaviors.

## 1. Introduction

Recently, along with the rapid development of live-streaming tech nology, many e-retailers have adopted live-streaming as an effective tool to improve sales performance [1]. Live streaming is a medium that can record and broadcast in real-time, and deliver live video to an audience over the Internet [2,3]. After applying live-streaming to online shop ping, consumers can learn about products via live videos rather than via text and pictures, which helps them gain richer information and make better purchase decisions [1]. According to a recent report by AliR esearch, Taobao Live, a live-streaming platform of Alibaba, has accu mulated 400 million consumers, and its annual gross merchandise volume has exceeded 200 billion yuan in 2019 [4]. In addition, the percentage of consumers who watched live-streaming shopping has increased by more than 40% in 2020 compared with 2019 [4], showing the big advantage of live-streaming shopping in retaining consumers. Although live-streaming has received plenty of attention in practice, there is a paucity of understanding about live-streaming shopping in the research area, especially about why live-streaming shopping can continuously attract people’s attention and create value [5]. Previous literature on live-streaming mainly focused on the influencing factors of consumers’ live-streaming watching intentions and live-streaming shopping intentions [1,2,6-9]. However, researchers have vet to identify influencing factors and unveil the underlying mechanism of why live-streaming shopping is so successful in assisting e-retailers in continuously retaining and engaging consumers in their shopping journey. As the competition among existing online and mobile shopping platforms is becoming increasingly fierce and new platforms are emerging rapidly, customer retention is becoming increasingly critical to businesses and a better theoretical understanding of this could yield potential guidance for both live-streaming shopping platforms as well as traditional online shopping platforms to refine their strategies to better retain consumers. Therefore, in this article, we seek to identify the distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping and investigate the underlying mechanism of why consumers are willing to participate in live-streaming shopping continuously so as to uncover the reasons for the popularity and success of live-streaming shopping.

In this study, we propose that the essential difference between livestreaming shopping and traditional online shopping is that consumers could gain not only goods or services but also a memorable experience when participating in live-streaming shopping. Consumer memorable experiences here refer to the memorable experiences consumers obtain from their encounters with vendors and other consumers during their live-streaming shopping process, which can be remembered and recalled by them after the live-streaming shopping process [10,11]. According to Pine and Gilmore [10], experiences occur when companies intentionally use services as the stage and goods as props to engage consumers in a way that could create memorable events. Compared with traditional online shopping which is merely aimed at providing services or goods to meet consumers’ shopping requirements, live-streaming shopping goes beyond the function and provides an immersive and memorable expe rience for consumers through visibility, meta voicing, and guidance shopping affordance [1]. Creating a memorable experience for con sumers is more valuable than simply providing services or goods to consumers [10] because creating memorable experiences for consumers could give rise to consumer delight [12,13] and increase consumers revisit intentions, repeat purchasing intentions as well as positive word of mouth (WOM) intentions [14,15]. Consequently, understanding how live-streaming shopping creates memorable experiences for consumers and knowing about the underlying mechanism are of great benefit to e-retailers. By creating more pleasant and memorable experiences for consumers, e-retailers could enhance consumers’ engagement and loy alty to live-streaming, thus improving their sales volume and promoting their business sustainability.

In essence, this paper contributes to live-streaming shopping in three distinct ways. First, to the best of our knowledge, this research is the first endeavor that explores consumers’ memorable experiences during the live-streaming shopping journey, which may serve as a crucial differ ence between live-streaming shopping and traditional online shopping. Second, this research identifies five distinct characteristics of live streaming shopping compared to traditional online shopping, i.e., in formation richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness, which previous studies haven’t elucidated. Third, this research explores the mechanism of how live-streaming characteristics affect consumers’ continuous participation intentions in live-streaming shopping not only from consumers’ momentary states but also from consumers’ reflective states, which have rarely been incorporated together as the organism in previous research.

With the help of real-time videos, consumers could gain more detailed and comprehensive information about the target products than in traditional online shopping. Therefore, infor mation richness, which is an important characteristic of communication media [19], could be a distinct characteristic of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping.

Regarding consumers’ interaction with online vendors, only text-totext written communication is feasible in traditional online shopping, while in live-streaming shopping, verbal responses can be delivered to consumers through real-time videos [1]. When consumers have ques tions about the presented products, they can type in a text, and then vendors could respond verbally and behaviorally in real-time through live-streaming. For instance, when a female consumer intends to buy a dress and wants to know about the length and tightness of it, she can type to ask the live streamer to put on the dress and see the wearing effect from multi-camera angles. Then she is able to imagine the wearing effect on herself and decide whether the dress fits her. Therefore, we propose that interactivity and vividness, which are two important media features studied in previous research [20], can be considered as two distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping [5].

In terms of social presence, no other consumers are present during traditional online shopping, thus traditional online shopping can be regarded as a shopping journey of one’s own. However, in livestreaming shopping, many other consumers watch the live-streaming together and can interact with each other via text, so that consumers may feel a sense of “being there” with other consumers in the same space [1,6]. Hence, social presence can be regarded as another distinct char acteristic of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping, which has been investigated by Ang et al. [6] and Hou et al. [5].

As for the time of popularity, online shopping has gradually become popular since 2005 [21], while live-streaming shopping is attracting people’s attention in recent years and has begun to develop rapidly since 2019 [22]. As a new form of online shopping, live-streaming shopping may not have been experienced by many people and the shopping process during the live streaming shopping is much different from the process of traditional online shopping, which includes real-time inter action with the seller, social interaction with other consumers, limited-time offers and flash sales, etc. Hence, live streaming is a novel method of shopping for consumers. Considering that newness has been documented by previous research as an important technology charac teristic [23–25], we propose newness to be another distinct character istic of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping.

Overall, based on previous literature about live-streaming shopping and communication media $[ 5 , 6 , 1 9 , 2 0 , 2 6 , 2 7 , ]$ , we identify five distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared to traditional on line shopping, i.e., information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness. Among them, information richness, vividness, and newness are three characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared to traditional online shopping that are newly proposed in this research.

Moreover, besides the distinct characteristics, another critical dif ference between traditional online shopping and live-streaming shop ping comes from the experience part. Consumers could merely obtain the experience of purchasing the targeted goods or services and the social interaction experience with the seller through traditional online shopping, which usually contains the consumer’s own feelings and af fections regarding their shopping practices. By contrast, when a con sumer participates in live-streaming shopping, s/he could not only get the experience of purchasing goods or services and the experience of interacting with the seller through the text-to-text communication method, but also obtain the experience of interacting with the live streamer through the face-to-text communication method and the experience of interacting with other consumers in the same live streaming room by asking each other questions, sharing opinions, and providing feedback on products [8]. Therefore, the experience gained from live-streaming shopping would be richer and more memorable than the experience obtained from traditional online shopping. Given that previous research has documented the importance of consumer experience to the success of a business [28,29], the difference in con sumer experience could also be a critical difference between traditional online shopping and live-streaming shopping.

## 2.2. Stimulus-organism-response (S-O-R) framework

Environmental psychologists put forward the S-O-R paradigm to explore the effects of external cues on people’s behaviors [30]. This paradigm posits that multifarious aspects of the external environment can act as stimuli (S) to influence individuals’ inner states (O), which subsequently affect people’s behaviors (R) [30–32]. This theoretical paradigm has been adopted in research related to impulsive purchasing [31,33], retailing [34,35], and s-commerce [32,36], both in offline and online contexts, with much success in exploring consumer behaviors and technology design. Therefore, the S-O-R model might also be adopted to explore the effect of characteristics of live-streaming platforms on con sumers’ participation intention in live-streaming shopping.

Specifically, stimuli (S) manifest in various formats and they usually pertain to the design features of technologies or websites in the online context [37]. Based on previous research on SOR and shopping, and adapting to our study, we regard the five features of live-streaming shopping (i.e., information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness) as the environmental stimuli (S) that consumers interact with during their online shopping process [32].

The organism (O) represents consumers’ subconscious process acti vated by the stimuli, which usually captures consumers’ cognitive and affective systems, and is the underlying mechanism of the effect of stimuli on consumers’ behavioral responses [35,37-40]. Cognitive re actions from the stimuli usually represent consumers’ mental processes when they interact with the stimuli [35,41]. In the IS literature, perceived diagnosticity is one of the most frequent cognitive reactions studied [41,42], which usually captures consumers’ beliefs about the ability of a system to provide helpful information to assist consumers in product evaluation [43]. It has been demonstrated to be important in the online shopping environment, which could influence consumers’ pur chase intention [44] and intention to revisit a website [45]. On the other hand, affective reactions refer to consumers’ emotional responses to ward the stimuli [41]. In this research, we employ perceived enjoyment and perceived social support to examine affective reactions. Perceived enjoyment usually captures consumers’ perception about whether or not the interaction with a system is interesting and fun [46.47], which has also been frequently studied in IS research and has shown to be an important affective component [41]. Together, the first two variables Perceived Diagnosticity and Perceived Enjoyment, capture the cognitive and emotion aspects of design, which have been shown as most effective in previous studies. The last variable, Perceived social support is gaining increasing attention in IS field due to the development of social com merce and live-streaming. Perceived social support refers to consumers feeling of being cared for, being responded to, and being helped by others in a system [48,49]. Previous research also showed that perceived social support could promote consumers’ social commerce intention [32] and continuance intention of social commerce platforms [49]. In sum, perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support used in this model to capture consumers’ cognitive and affective reactions towards stimuli are consistent with past IS literature. They are congruent with leading theories on human cognition and emotions, such as Three Level of Processing [50], on capturing users’ inner states at visceral and behavior levels.

Moreover, the present study considers consumers’ continuous participation intention as the response (R) to the live-streaming shop ping characteristics (S) and consumers’ cognitive and affective states (O) because it seeks to figure out why live-streaming shopping is more successful in assisting e-retailers in continuously retaining and engaging consumers in their shopping journey compared with traditional online shopping [5]. Consumers’ continuous participation intention in live-streaming shopping is a key factor for live-streaming shopping platforms to continuously attract people’s attention and promote busi ness values [5,8,18]. The stronger willingness to continue participating in live-streaming shopping, the higher the possibility that live-streaming shopping platforms could generate sustainable sales transactions.

However, in the live-streaming shopping context, consumers not only obtain goods or services via live-streaming but also gain memorable experiences through interacting with the live streamers and other con sumers. A memorable experience, which refers to the experience that can be remembered and recalled by people after the event has occurred [51], is a concept advocated by Pine and Gilmore [10], who are the pioneers in appealing to companies to “stage an experience whenever they engage consumers in a personal, memorable way.” A memorable experience is a long-term and sustainable memory based on the reflec tion of the events that have happened [52,53]. According to Kim and Chen [54], memorable experience includes four sub-dimensions, namely interaction, enthusiasm, novelty, and learning, which together unveil the essence of memorable experiences. Specifically, interaction refers to a feeling of connection and group identity with other participants [51]. Enthusiasm refers to pleasurable feelings that excite oneself [51]. Nov elty is a psychological feeling of newness resulting from having a new experience, and learning refers to the information, facts, or experiences learned by the consumer [51].

According to the classic theory in human cognition and emotions, “Three Levels of Processing” by Norman [50], there are three levels of processing in the brain, applicable to both cognitive and emotional processing, and they are different yet interconnected levels: visceral, behavioral, and reflective. The momentary cognitive and affective states in this research (i.e., perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support aforementioned), which focus on consumers on-site cognition and emotion during the shopping process, mainly capture the visceral and behavioral level of human cognition and emo tions immediately or soon following the shopping activities, while the memorable experience, which is built on recalled thoughts and pro tracted emotions after the shopping process, mainly captures users inner states at the reflective level that are deep and slow [50]. Although different characteristics of traditional online shopping could affect consumers’ participation intention by influencing consumers’ momen tary cognitive and affective states, the momentary states are insufficient in representing the full range of inner states of consumers to explain their behaviors on live-streaming shopping platforms that can afford much richer experiences and continuously engage consumers. Norman [50] argued that memories last longer than on-site experiences during the period of usage, and reflective memories play a more crucial role to drive consumers to make recommendations and guide their future be haviors [55,56], thus the memorable experiences that are recalled and remembered after the on-site experience should also be included in the “organism” that influences consumers’ continuous participation intention.

Consumer experiences could be classified into three types based on different stages, namely predicted, on-line, and remembered experi ences [57]. Specifically, the remembered experiences are formed based on the selective reconstruction of their on-line experiences [58], so the on-line experiences could influence the remembered experiences. Like wise, consumers’ momentary experience, which is constructed by their momentary cognitive states and affective states, would also influence consumers’ memorable experience, which is consumers’ remembered experience after the shopping process. In other words, consumers momentary internal states could affect their memorable experiences, which could consequently influence consumers’ continuous participa tion intentions in live-streaming shopping. Therefore, in this research, we seek to incorporate the “memorable experience” as the reflective level of consumers’ internal states together with the momentary internal states as the “Organism” of how live-streaming shopping characteristics influence consumers’ continuous participation intention, which en riches the “Organism” part of S-O-R framework by integrating both momentary and reflective organisms.

## 3. Hypotheses development

## 3.1. Conceptual model

Based on the overarching theory of the S-O-R model, we investigate the effects of live-streaming shopping characteristics on consumers continuous participation intentions in live-streaming shopping. Stimu lated by the characteristics of live-streaming shopping, consumers momentary states and their subsequent reflective states may be affected, which could consequently influence their intentions to participate continuously in live-streaming shopping. Specifically, five distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping are identified in this paper, including information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness. Then we explore their impacts on consumers’ momentary states (i.e., perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support) as well as their subsequent influences on consumers reflective state (i.e., memorable live-streaming shopping experiences), which would consequently affect their continuous participation in live streaming. 3.2. The effect of live-streaming shopping characteristics on consumers’ momentary internal states

## 3.2.1. Information richness

Information richness refers to the amount of information embedded in the shopping journey [59], including product features, brand attri butes, usage experiences, application of the crowd, and so on. In live-streaming shopping, consumers can obtain detailed information about the target product through the verbal description and gain first hand experiences of the live streamer [1,16]. Additionally, with the help of real-time videos, live streamers can also respond to consumers questions about the products in time, which provides comprehensive information for consumers. Hence, with the help of live-streaming shopping, consumers could more easily decide the fitness of the prod uct for their needs, so live-streaming shopping could enhance con sumers’ perceived diagnosticity of products. Moreover, it has been proven that social media’s richness could make the communication process more enjoyable for consumers [19,60]. During the shopping process, consumers often tend to search for plenty of information on alternative options before making purchase decisions. Compared with traditional online shopping, live-streaming shopping which has a higher level of information richness is more likely to meet consumers’ needs for information to compare alternative options, which could make con sumers perceive their shopping process to be more enjoyable [19]. Furthermore, with the support of comprehensive information offered by online vendors in live-streaming shopping, consumers’ perceived social support about their shopping process could also be enhanced [61]. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H1a: Information richness is positively associated with perceived diagnosticity.

H1b: Information richness is positively associated with perceived enjoyment.

H1c: Information richness is positively associated with perceived social support.

## 3.2.2. Interactivity

Interactivity in this context refers to the extent to which consumers can interact with and participate in modifying the content of the livestreaming in real-time during the live-streaming shopping process [20, 62]. It encompasses three components, namely active control (i.e., the degree to which consumers have active control over the communication process), two-way communication (i.e., the degree to which the communication is reciprocal), and synchronicity (i.e., the degree to which the communication between consumers and vendors is synchro nized). In traditional online shopping, consumers have no control over the online vendors’ product presentation, whereas live-streaming en ables consumers to convey text-to-face communication with online vendors in real-time so that the vendors can explain the specific ques tions consumers cared about and adjust their product presentation ac cording to consumers’ requirements, promoting the effectiveness of consumers’ information collection [6,20]. Hence, live-streaming shop ping enables consumers to find high-quality products that fit for them more easily and more effectively, enhancing consumers’ perceived diagnosticity of the target products. In addition, the timely and frequent interactions in live-streaming shopping could make consumers more immersive in the shopping process [20]. The greater immersion could lead to the perception that time is passing more quickly, which is closely related to enjoyment [63]. Moreover, previous research also found that users tend to feel a greater sense of enjoyment when they experience more interactive functions in playing video games [20,64,]. Hence, we can conclude that consumers may also gain greater enjoyment from frequent interaction with online vendors via live-streaming. Further more, the responses and support from online vendors can also make consumers perceive that they are cared for, esteemed, and valued by others, which strengthens their perceived social support [48,65]. ## 3.2.3. Vividness

Vividness here is defined as the ability of live-streaming technology to produce a sensorially rich mediated environment, which captures the quality of product presentation [20,66,67]. In live-streaming shopping, live streamers could present the product (e.g. a dress) by trying it on themselves combined with a verbal introduction of it, which provides a comprehensive and vivid display of the product, thereby enabling con sumers to mentally envision their anticipatory usage experience with the product in their future consumption [20]. And through live-streaming, consumers can imagine the tactile, olfactory, and gustatory feeling about the product on the basis of the detailed description and the authentic usage experiences offered by live streamers, which traditional online shopping is less likely to deliver. Consequently, vividness could promote consumers’ perceived diagnosticity of the product. Moreover, similar to the effect of interactivity on perceived enjoyment, vividness could also exert a positive impact on perceived enjoyment because the vivid product visualization could facilitate consumers’ positive emotional experience and make them more immersed in their shopping process [20], which could make them perceive that time is passing more quickly and consequently a greater sense of enjoyment is induced [63]. Furthermore, the vividness of live-streaming shopping could make consumers perceive that they are talking to the vendor face-to-face and they are valued and concerned by the vendor, which enables them to perceive social support from others [48,65]. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H3a: Vividness is positively associated with perceived diagnosticity.

H3b: Vividness is positively associated with perceived enjoyment.

H3c: Vividness is positively associated with perceived social support.

## 3.2.4. Social presence

Social presence is defined as the extent to which the live-streaming platform allows a user to experience the psychological presence of other users during online interactions [68,69]. In traditional online shopping, consumers usually make purchase decisions by themselves without other consumers’ presence, whereas live-streaming shopping enables real-time interaction and communication among consumers, facilitating the information exchange among consumers and thus enhancing information transparency [1]. The more transparent the product information, the more diagnostic the product quality. In addi tion, communicating and chatting with other consumers is more pleasant and interesting than shopping alone, which can also contribute to consumers’ perceived enjoyment [69]. Furthermore, the feedback and support from other consumers can also strengthen consumers feelings that they are being listened to, cared for, and valued by others, thus enhancing consumers’ perceived social support [48,65]. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H4a: Social presence is positively associated with perceived diagnosticity.

H4b: Social presence is positively associated with perceived enjoyment.

H4c: Social presence is positively associated with perceived social support.

## 3.2.5. Newness

Newness captures the degree to which live-streaming shopping is outside individuals’ experience [26]. As a new trend in online shopping, live-streaming shopping emerged in 2016 and became popular in 2019 [22]. The emergence of live-streaming shopping overturns people’s inherent thoughts about online shopping, making online shopping to be similar to offline shopping where consumers can communicate and interact with vendors and other consumers in real-time. Hence, live-streaming shopping offers a novel online shopping experience to consumers, enhancing consumers’ perceived enjoyment. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H5: Newness is positively associated with perceived enjoyment.

3.3. The effect of consumers’ momentary internal states on consumers’ memorable experiences and consumers’ participation intentions in livestreaming shopping

## 3.3.1. Perceived diagnosticity

Following the definition by Jiang and Benbasat [43], perceived diagnosticity in this paper refers to the ability of live-streaming shop ping to convey relevant product information to help consumers’ product evaluation and product selection, which is a paramount predictor of consumers’ intention to use the live-streaming shopping to facilitate their shopping [70,71,72]. Live-streaming shopping enables consumers to obtain comprehensive information about the target product via live-time videos, improving the efficiency in information gathering, helping them in judging the quality and performance of the target products, and thus promoting their decision making, which could enhance the learning dimension of memorable experiences [16,43]. In addition, previous research has also proven that perceived diagnosticity facilitates consumers to reach a mental state of flow [43], inducing the generation of consumers’ positive attitudes toward the shopping process [2], which could enhance the hedonism of memorable experiences. Furthermore, due to the great diagnosticity of live-streaming shopping, consumers are more willing to participate in it in order to acquire more useful information to make better decisions. Hence, the perceived diagnosticity of live-streaming shopping also contributes to consumers continuous participation intentions. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H6a: Perceived diagnosticity is positively associated with con sumers’ memorable experiences in live-streaming shopping.

H6b: Perceived diagnosticity is positively associated with con sumers’ continuous participation intentions in live-streaming shopping.

## 3.3.2. Perceived enjoyment

In this paper, perceived enjoyment is defined as the degree to which consumers believe that the activity of using live-streaming shopping is enjoyable in its own right [46,47]. Perceived enjoyment captures the enthusiasm aspect of consumers’ memorable experiences, facilitating the generation of consumers’ memorable experiences during live-streaming shopping. Additionally, enjoyment has been proven to be an important intrinsic motivation for shopping online [20,73,74]. When consumers feel enjoyable in live-streaming shopping, they are more likely to be engaged in live-streaming shopping, thus their continuous participation in live-streaming shopping would be facilitated. Therefore. we hypothesize:

H7a: Perceived enjoyment is positively associated with consumers memorable experiences in live-streaming shopping.

H7b: Perceived enjoyment is positively associated with consumers continuous participation intention in live-streaming shopping.

## 3.3.3. Perceived social support

Perceived social support in this context refers to the mental and material support consumers gained from live-streaming shopping, making consumers feel that they are cared for, loved, esteemed, and valued [48,65]. Social support is a fundamental psychological protective resource, which can buffer pain and stress as well as provide a sense of physical safety and psychological security [48,75,76]. It may facilitate the building of strong social relationships and develop a strong identi fication with the community [77]. In live-streaming shopping, a con sumer can obtain valuable information, suggestions, and advice from live streamers and other consumers, which in turn enables him/her to feel that s/he is also obligatory to actively participate in the live-streaming shopping and offer his/her opinions to facilitate the communication [78]. Moreover, social support has also been demon strated to be positively related to individuals’ subjective well-being [79, 80]. With greater perceived social support obtained from live-streaming shopping, consumers could build a more intimate relationship and form a stronger group identity with others they meet in the live-streaming shopping, consequently contributing to the interaction aspect of their memorable shopping experiences and improving their continuous participation intention. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H8a: Perceived social support is positively associated with con sumers’ memorable experiences in live-streaming shopping. H8b: Perceived social support is positively associated with con sumers’ continuous participation intentions in live-streaming shopping.

## 3.4. The effect of consumers’ memorable experiences on consumers’ participation intentions in live-streaming shopping

Consumers’ memorable experiences gained from live-streaming shopping could serve as references to guide their present and future thinking and behavior [54]. In tourism-related research, researchers have already proven that memorable tourism experiences about the tourist destinations are a strong predictor of their affective commitment and future destination choice because memorable experiences could strengthen consumers’ intimacy with the destination [57,81]. Likewise, consumers’ memorable experiences obtained from their live-streaming shopping process could also enhance their intimacy and attachment to the live-streaming shopping, which could consequently increase their continuous participation intention. Overall, the more memorable the experiences consumers gain from live-streaming shopping, the higher enthusiasm, involvement, novelty, and learning they will perceive in live-streaming shopping [54,82], which promotes their continuous participation intentions in live-streaming shopping. Therefore, we hypothesize:

H9: Consumers’ memorable experiences are positively associated with consumers’ continuous participation intentions in livestreaming shopping.

## 4. Data and methods

## 4.1. Data collection

We used an online survey to examine our conceptual model. The participants of this survey were consumers who have used livestreaming shopping platforms and participated in live-streaming shop ping. These live-streaming shopping platforms include Taobao, JD, Sina Weibo, Tiktok, Kuaishou, Pinduoduo, and Tencent, which are all pop ular live-streaming shopping platforms in China. The participants were given financial incentives and were guaranteed anonymity. Two authors who have a doctorate from English-speaking countries (UK and USA) translated the original English version of the questionnaire into Chinese first and then back-translated the Chinese version into English to ensure minimum language discrepancy. Two academic experts checked the consistency between the two versions of the questionnaire.

Inappropriate wording issues were corrected. The final version of the questionnaire is shown in Appendix. The first part of this questionnaire asked participants to report their demographic information and some basic information about their live-streaming shopping history. The second part of the survey aimed at measuring the target constructs of this research based on the participants’ last live-streaming shopping experience. The measurements of the constructs were all adapted from previous studies and were slightly modified to fit the research context of this paper. All of them were measured with a seven-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). In particular, we also controlled the effects of some individual difference variables (i.e., technology anxiety and need for interaction) on memorable experience and continuous participation intention in this model in order to control the individual-level variance. Finally, the participants were also asked to share their opinions about live-streaming shopping in some open-ended questions, including “In your opinion, what are the unique character istics of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping?”, “What needs does live-streaming shopping meet for you?”, and “Please recall your most memorable live-streaming shopping experience and list at least five words to describe the experience.” The purpose of the three open-ended questions was to identify characteris tics of live-streaming shopping, consumers’ needs met by live-streaming shopping, and construct dimensions that constituted one’s memorable live-streaming shopping experience, which could ensure the reason ability of the conceptual model and the content validity of construct domains that were predetermined from the literature review [51].

We received 357 questionnaires, but 48 of them were discarded because the participants failed to pass the attention check question or had never participated in live-streaming shopping. In sum, 309 usable responses were used for further analysis.

## 4.3. Common method variance

To test whether the dataset suffers from a common method variance (CMV) problem, we loaded all the variables of our model on one factor [83]. The results showed that the fit of the single-factor model was poor, and all fit statistics were unacceptable (CMIN/DF=4.662, GFI=0.554, AGFI=0.510, NFI=0.610, CFI=0.664, RMSEA=0.109), indicating that common method bias is not a serious problem in this research. We also adopted the marker variable technique to examine common method variance [84]. We considered emotional stability as the marker variable and compared the structural model with and without the marker vari able. The results showed no statistically significant correlation between the marker variable and the constructs in the conceptual model, and the significance of the path coefficients was not changed when the marker variable was added. Overall, our dataset did not suffer from a common method variance problem.

## 5. Results

In this research, we used AMOS 21.0 (IBM, SPSS Inc.) software to analyze the reliability and validity of the measurement model and subsequently tested the hypotheses. Furthermore, as the supplemental data collection and analysis in addition to quantitative data analysis, we conducted content analysis on the responses of the three open-ended questions to further verify the reasonability of our conceptual model and confirm the content validity of the memorable experience construct.

## 5.1. Measurement model

Cronbach’s alpha and Composite Reliability (CR) were used to evaluate internal consistency. As shown in Table 5. the Cronbach's alpha values of our dataset ranged from 0.745 to 0.910, exceeding the threshold of 0.7 and proving sufficient internal consistency. Values of CR, which should be greater than the benchmark of 0.7 to be considered adequate, ranged from 0.756 to 0.910, proving the adequate internal consistency of this dataset once again. Next, we adopted the Average Variance Extracted (AVE) values to evaluate the convergent validity of this dataset. The values for all constructs were > 0.5, indicating that the scales had a good convergent validity.

Finally, the square root of AVE and latent variable correlations were adopted to evaluate discriminant validity. To show good discriminant validity of the dataset, the square root of AVE for each construct should be higher than all the correlations between the target construct and the other constructs. The correlation matrix of the constructs and the square root of the AVE for each construct are presented in Table 6. Although some of the variable correlations are relatively high (e.g., the correlation between two-way communication and synchronicity (0.918), the cor relation between memorable experience-hedonism and memorable experience-interaction (0.922), the correlation between memorable experience-hedonism and memorable experience-uniqueness (0.897), the correlation between memorable experience-hedonism and memo rable experience-knowledge (0.887)), these high correlations are reasonable because two-way communication and synchronicity are both second-order constructs of interactivity, as well as memorable experience-hedonism, memorable experience-interaction, memorable experience-uniqueness, and memorable experience-knowledge, are all second-order constructs of a memorable experience. Overall. the results prove the discriminant validity of the dataset.

## 5.3. Content analysis

Regarding the first open-ended question about characteristics of livestreaming shopping, 35 different words were identified through content analysis using SmartAnalyze, which is a text analysis tool that could count word frequency based on natural language processing technology. When reviewing the responses, we found that different words could be categorized under one theme. Hence, two independent coders who majored in management information systems were hired to indepen dently carry out the content-coding and they verified the final themes through discussions. Through this process, the 38 words were catego rized into six themes (see Table 8), including information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, newness, and experience. The percent agreement of the two independent coders was 97.14%, which could serve as the measure of inter-rater reliability. And the word “introduction” was finally included in the “information richness” construct rather than the “interactivity” construct after discussion. Hence, we can conclude that the five characteristics of live-streaming shopping proposed in the conceptual model can well represent the distinctiveness of live-streaming shopping. Moreover, the experience was also identified as a distinct feature of live-streaming shopping, which supports our argument aforementioned that “the essential dif ference between live-streaming shopping and traditional online shop ping is that consumers could gain not only goods or services but also a memorable experience when participating in live-streaming shopping”.

Regarding the second open-ended question about consumers’ needs met by live-streaming shopping, 20 different words were identified through content analysis using SmartAnalyze. Same as for the analysis of the first open-ended question, two researchers were hired to indepen dently carry out the content-coding, and finally, they verified three themes through discussions, namely perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support (see Table 9). The percent agreement of the two independent coders is 95.00%, and the word “handle queries” was finally included in the “perceived social support” construct rather than the “perceived diagnosticity” construct after dis cussion. Hence, the three momentary internal states proposed in the conceptual model can well underlie the relationship between charac teristics of live-streaming shopping and consumers’ reflective experi ence and participation intention in live-streaming shopping.

Regarding the third open-ended question about construct dimensions that constituted one’s memorable live-streaming shopping experience, 28 different words were identified through content analysis. Similarly, two researchers were hired to independently carry out the contentcoding, and finally, they verified four themes through discussions, namely interaction, enthusiasm, novelty, and learning (see Table 10). ## 6.2. Theoretical contributions

In conclusion, this paper mainly makes three important contribu tions to extant literature. First, to the best of our knowledge, this research is the first endeavor that explores the memorable experience in consumers’ live-streaming shopping journey, which could serve as a crucial difference between live-streaming shopping and traditional on line shopping. Past research explored the differences between livestreaming shopping and traditional online shopping mainly from a technical perspective, such as synchronicity, information carrier, and communication methods [1,6,16]. However, little research explores this difference from a consumer psychology perspective, which is more beneficial for us to deeply understand why live-streaming shopping is so popular nowadays. Hence, by borrowing the concept of a memorable experience in research about live-streaming shopping, we could better understand consumers’ intentions and behaviors and figure out how to promote their participation intentions in live-streaming shopping.

Second, this research identifies five distinct characteristics of livestreaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping, i.e., information richness, interactivity, vividness, social presence, and newness, which previous studies could not summarize and elucidate. Past research about factors influencing consumers’ participation inten tion in live-streaming shopping draws from the perspectives of consumer motivation, broadcaster characteristics, IT affordance, live content, etc. [1-3,6-9,16-18]. However, the distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping have not been summarized. Therefore, this research could serve as the basis for future research, facilitating the development of research about live-streaming shopping characteristics.

Third, regarding the mechanism influencing the consumers’ partic ipation in live-streaming shopping, previous research focused either on the momentary organism (e.g., attitude, perceived value, perceived website effectiveness, etc.) or the reflective organism (e.g., trust, engagement, etc.) [1,2,16]. But this research incorporates both the momentary states (i.e., perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support) and the reflective state (i.e., memorable experience) together to explore the organism of how live-streaming shopping characteristics influence consumers’ continuous participa tion intention, which extends the S-O-R framework with Reflective State incorporated as a key factor. By exploring the underlying mechanism of the effects of live-streaming characteristics on consumers’ continuous participation intention from a comprehensive perspective, we could offer suggestions for live-streaming shopping platform designers about how to strengthen consumers’ participation intention in both the short run and the long run.

## 6.3. Managerial implications

Memorable experiences are unquestionably desired by consumers, and businesses spare no effort to design and promote pleasant experi ences for consumers [10]. This research provides a general framework for both the designers of live-streaming platforms to assist them in creating more memorable experiences for consumers and the designers of traditional online shopping platforms to help them continuously engage consumers. Firstly, platform designers could improve the design of shopping platforms on the basis of the five distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping identified in this research. For instance, they can improve the communication functions of the shopping platform, which could enhance the interactivity of online shopping and offer a better shopping experience to consumers. Secondly, platform designers could develop functions promoting consumers’ perceived diagnosticity, perceived enjoyment, and perceived social support. For example, adopting argument reality technology in online shopping could strengthen consumers’ perceived enjoyment and perceived diag nosticity, offering them impressive and memorable experiences. Last but not least, since memorable experiences could enhance consumers continuous participation intentions, platform designers could also try to create a better experience for consumers. Specifically, managers and designers of such platforms could consider designing the experiences using some of the principles identified by Pine and Gilmore [10] such as: creating themes, harmonizing impressions with positive cues, elimi nating negative cues, mixing in memorabilia, and engaging all five senses, which would be beneficial to increase consumers’ participation intentions.

## 6.4. Limitations and future research

The current research has some limitations. First, it proposes the difference between live-streaming shopping and traditional online shopping. Future work to compare live-streaming shopping versus traditional in-person shopping will further shed light on the role of “Experience” in consumer behaviors and provide insights on designing memorable experiences in online as well as offline settings. Second, this research identifies only five distinct characteristics of live-streaming shopping compared with traditional online shopping. However, some other characteristics of live-streaming shopping may also exist, which future research could continue to investigate. Third, live-streaming shopping is most popular in China nowadays, so we only collect the data of this study in China, thus the results found in this study may not be suitable for other cultural contexts. Fourth, we investigated con sumers’ continuous participation intention rather than their actual be haviors in this study. However, behavioral intentions cannot always translate into actual behaviors. Consequently, future research could further explore the factors influencing consumers’ actual continuous participation behaviors.
