# How social learning drives customer engagement in short video commerce: An attitude transfer perspective

## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Social learning Atmospheric cues Short video commerce Attitude transfer Relationship norms Customer engagement TikTok

## A B S T R A C T

Short video commerce has become increasingly prevalent; however, the factors affecting customer engagement have not been adequately examined. Drawing on social learning theory, attitude transfer theory and relationship norms theory, we explored how social learning behavior from three dimensions of atmospheric cues is associated with customers’ attitudes, how such attitudes mediate these engagement relationships, and also how the relationship norms moderate the mediating effect of attitude. The empirical findings obtained through the PLS-SEM largely support the hypotheses. The fsOCA results illustrate how combinations of antecedents can lead to high customer engagement. This study contributes to the literature on short video commerce, customer attitudes, and relationship norms, and provides influencers and managers with practical strategies to improve customer engagement.

## 1. Introduction

Short videos, an emerging form of social media, have rapidly attracted the attention of individuals, compensating for the lack of social presence of traditional social media content such as text, pictures, and voice [1]. The number of short video users in China reached 1.053 billion by December 2023, accounting for 96.4 % of the total Internet users (CNNIC 2024). In the same year, such vivid features and vast traffic led approximately 3 million influencers on TikTok, one of the most popular applications, to adopt short videos as a tool to sell products [2], resulting in a new form of social commerce, i.e., short video com merce. The revenue of short video commerce in China reached CNy 50.37 billion in the first half of 2021 (about USD 7.79 billion), ranking first among all types of advertising revenues [3].

However, concealed within the prosperity, two challenges quietly surface. First. the short video commerce-induced market influence has enticed numerous influencers and sellers, culminating in a highly competitive landscape [4]. Second, in short video commerce transactions, the transient nature of viewers occurs when switching between influencers, leading to diminished loyalty towards a specific influencer [5]. These challenges continually confront all influencers or sellers, prompting them to ponder on how to foster customer retention for sustainable competitiveness. Previous studies have shown that customer engagement in social commerce could effectively increase customer stickiness, which can further capture the customer loyalty [6,7]. Customer engagement refers to “the mechanics of a customer’s value addition to the firm, either through direct or/and indirect contribution" [8], which holds significant importance to establishing good customer relationships and increasing customer loyalty in short video commerce [9,10]. Specifically, customer engagement includes not only direct transactions such as customer purchase behaviors but also non-transactional behaviors such as customer referrals and suggestions [11]. In this regard, customer engagement becomes a low-cost and vital psychological determinant for influencers and marketers to attract and retain customers in an environment of fierce competition and low switching costs of customers.

There are lots of studies focusing on customer engagement in short video commerce and live streaming commerce, as summarized in Appendix A. Extant literature in live streaming commerce focuses on cultivating customer engagement from the angle of trust [6], customer value [11], interaction [7], and bonds [10]. Little attention has systematically paid to examining the influence of social learning on customer engagement. As is well known, short video commerce is of strong social attribute interaction such as likes, shares, favorites and followers. Customers can learn knowledge or collect information from this plentiful information like product reviews and shopping experiences, which the literature called atmospheric cues in short video com merce [12,13], to make more informed and accurate decisions. The process of learning product knowledge and purchasing experience from these clues is highly consistent with the perspective of social learning theory [14]. In detail, short video commerce offers an integrated environment for customers to observe other individuals and learn information and knowledge of products and influencers without leaving the platform [15]. Simultaneously, live videos are 10 times more likely to generate comments than recorded videos and viewed 3 times longer than regular videos and similar engagement quantity occurs in short videos [16]. Therefore, we speculate that customers’ social learning from short video commerce springing up with some novel atmospheric cues may play a role in driving customer behaviors such as engagement.

However, in the realm of short video commerce, customers face a limited chance to directly engage with influencers and products. The customers’ perceptions of influencers through social learning may be affected by individual psychological tendencies such as their attitudes. Literature on the theory of planned behaviors [17] indicated that atti tude plays a significant role in individual intentions and behaviors. In short video commerce, customer engagement implicates individual behaviors. Therefore, we argue that customer engagement boosted from the social learning may be affected by their attitudes. Besides, according to attitude transfer theory, perceived attitude towards the influencers may transfer to the products, because the products are displayed by the influencers. On the other hand, customers’ attitudes towards the influ encers and products may also be affected by the relationship norms. Relationship norms play a critical role in adjusting individual behaviors [18]. In short video commerce, some customers may desire a friendship-like relationship with influencers, called a “communal relationship”; others may want a purely transactional relationship with influencers, called an “exchange relationship”. We propose that differences in these norms may result in different attitudes towards influ encers and these attitudes may further transfer to the products.

Considering all the above-mentioned factors affecting customer engagement and drawing on social learning theory, attitude transfer theory and relationship norms, we propose our research questions (RQ1): How does social learning influence customer engagement? Specifically, RQ1.1: What effect does learning from different atmospheric cues have on customers’ attitudes? RQ1.2: What effect do customers’ attitudes have on customer engagement? RQ1.3: How can attitude towards influencers be transferred to attitude towards products? Furthermore (RQ2): To what extent do the effects of learning from different atmospheric cues on customers’ attitudes depend on the type of relationship norms that are dominant in their minds?

To address the above two research questions, a hybrid method combining PLS-SEM and fsQCA is adopted. Our results reveal learning from short video comment information and learning from product review information positively affect both attitudes towards the influencer and the product, and then lead to customer engagement. However, learning from guidance information only has a positive effect on attitude towards the influencer. Attitude can be further transferred from the influencer to the product. Moreover, relationship norms moderate the relationships between learning from guidance information and learning from short video comment information with attitude towards the influencer.

Our study makes several contributions. First, our study is among the first that explores social learning behaviors in boosting customer engagement in short video commerce settings. Despite the increasing use of short videos by influencers and brands to advertise products, research on short video commerce is still in its infancy. Previous studies have mainly investigated the effect of short video advertisements on product sales [1,19] and the outcomes of social learning behavior on users’ purchase intention [20,21]. Therefore, investigating social learning behavior in short video commerce could help us to understand how different atmospheric cues affect customers’ attitudes and consequently their engagement. Thus, our study not only enriches the engagement research in short video commerce, but also extends the social learning theory in novel fields.

Second, although customers’ attitudes have been explored and recognized as a driving factor in the context of social commerce [22], very few studies, if any, have examined what role customers’ attitudes play in short video commerce. Thus, our study contributes to attitude theory development in short video commerce. Notably, by differentiating attitudes at the interpersonal level and product level in short video commerce, we can fine-tune the mechanism of the social learning effect on customer engagement, because the influencers in short video commerce play a key role in business performance [23]. Identifying this mechanism could increase the knowledge of customer attitude theory. Furthermore, there is scant research on attitude transfer between two relevant behaviors in short video commerce, thus our study has extended the attitude transfer theory in a new application. In addition, past studies have not established the link between social learning behavior and customer engagement through attitude. Thus, our study examining the mediating effect of the attitude in connecting social learning effect on customer engagement also contributes to the attitude theory development.

Third, we use the relationship norms theory to examine the different cue categories effect on customers’ attitudes towards the influencers. Specifically, the exchange relationships could strengthen the association between learning from task cues and attitude towards the influencers, and the communal relationships could strengthen the association between learning from social cues and attitude towards the influencers. Prior studies have not taken a broader view of social learning by considering the moderating role of relationship norms in understanding customer engagement. Thus, our study contributes to the literature in moderating effect of relationship norms on customers’ attitudes.

The remaining sections are organized as follows. First, we introduce the theoretical background, followed by developing the research model, and hypotheses. Subsequently, we present the empirical methodology and results. Then, we discuss the findings, describe the theoretical and practical implications, and present the limitations with future research directions. Finally, we conclude the paper.

## 2. Literature review and theoretical background

## 2.1. Short video commerce

Short videos are relatively new video media that rely on mobile Internet and intelligent terminals to present content ranging in length from a few seconds to a few minutes. The importance of short videos is evident from the finding that consumers are 89 % more likely to make a purchase after watching a video [24] and that short videos boost product sales in practice [1,19]. Influencer endorsements and short video content are the two main factors driving the success of short video marketing [4,5]. Product advertising and influencer-generated content are usually integrated into short videos [4], which provide customers with information about both the products and influencers.

First, compared to traditional social commerce, commercial short videos are categorized as dynamic displays of products, which are usually vivid and carry more information regarding the products and influencers presented [4]. Along with being eye-catching, video content also allows customers to see how a product looks and feels in real life. This shortens the customer journey and makes it more likely the viewer will make a purchase. Moreover, short video commerce is an integrated context that simultaneously combines commercial short videos, short video comments, and product reviews into one scene or platform, providing a major channel through which customers can connect with influencers and products [3]. Customers can not only share, like, and comment on short videos but also review products and influencers simultaneously, resulting in higher interactivity than traditional social commerce. Second, unlike live streaming, customers can watch short videos anytime and anywhere because they are pre-recorded by influencers who have used the advertised products [1]. Moreover, persistent descriptions from influencers allow customers to gain information and experience regarding the influencers and advertised products [25]. The specific attributes of short video commerce allow customers to acquire new information from atmospheric cues. ## 2.2. Atmospheric cues

Atmospheric cues are environmental elements that marketers can design to increase favorable responses from customers [26]. Atmospheric cues refer to the sum of all visible and audible stimuli for customers [27]. The subfactors involved in atmospheric cues vary according to the research context [12]. Tang and Zhang [28] found that task cues, aesthetic cues, and social cues are the three major antecedents of consumers’ approach and avoidance behavioral intentions. Wu et al. [13] suggested that atmospheric cues on travel applications should include interactivity and vividness. According to Wang et al. [12], atmospheric cues in live streaming commerce involve two primary types and three dimensions: task cues (guidance information) and social cues (parasocial interactions and bullet information).

The subfactors mentioned above can be classified as graphic design or information design, in which graphic design captures the most visual elements in the environment, whereas information design provides ac cess to relevant information about goods and services [29]. Compared with graphic design, information design fosters positive emotions and loyalty in an online context [29]. Therefore, as opposed to graphic design which focuses on elements such as aesthetic cues, information design emphasizes the informational contents. According to Wu et al. [13], vividness and interactivity are two crucial aspects of atmospheric cues. Vividness is the ability to convey information to capture cus tomers’ attention, and interactivity is correlated with mimicking interpersonal communication. Thus, two types of atmospheric cues have been identified in information design that correspond to vividness and interactivity, respectively: task cues and social cues [12,13]. Task cues are utilitarian and related to task efficiency and effectiveness, which include guidance information such as product descriptions and social advertising, and are offered by influencers. In comparison, social cues refer to features such as access to customer reviews and product ratings and reflect the social presence of other customers and sales personnel in online marketplaces [28.30]. Thus, task cues are related to commercial short videos, which reflect vividness and persistent product descriptions, while social cues emphasize interactivity between influencers and customers.

Given the increasing importance of customer engagement for online shopping decisions, it is critically important to understand the relationship between learning from different dimensions of atmospheric cues and customer engagement, so as to help influencers and sellers deploy effective strategies to make commercial profits and attain sustainable competitiveness.

## 2.3. Social learning theory

Psychologists Bandura and McClelland [33] first proposed the term social learning to describe the process of observing, modeling, and imitating the behaviors, attitudes, and emotional reactions of others. Social learning is related to how environmental and cognitive factors interact to affect customer learning and behavior. Observational learning, is a related theory, positing that individuals learn new information by observing the behaviors and actions of others through direct or indirect social interactions [15]. Individuals then form different attitudes and behaviors through social encouragement and reinforcement received. This is known as a social learning process. Specifically, individuals learn acceptable or unacceptable behaviors through observing and imitating others, who fill the role of the model. People observe the model’s various social behaviors and note the reinforcement of those behaviors. If individuals value the reinforcement the model obtains, they will attempt to replicate this type of behavior and obtain similar reinforcement [33]. Observation is an essential heuristic that helps in dividuals overcome information overload when making decisions. Previous studies on e-commerce and social commerce have revealed that customers gather information by browsing the content peer users create, such as that on online forums and in communities and chat discussions [20]. Therefore, we argue that the social learning process of observing others is an essential component of a short video environment, which can elicit customer engagement behavior.

In short video commerce, customers get access to social knowledge and experience by learning from three dimensions of cues. In the learning process, customers become aware of whether the product is of high quality and whether the influencer is credible. Specifically, customers can learn consumption-related knowledge, skills, product infor mation, and influencer information from guidance information, which can be conceptualized as learning from guidance information. In addi tion, customers can learn from short video comment information, such as product selection and referrals from others [20]. We summarized those learning behaviors as learning from short video comment infor mation. Moreover, product review information allows individuals to exchange product feedback and inform each other of their choices with independent perspectives and experiences: the user-generated content from independent and real individuals makes it more credible and useful for customers to learn. We mapped those learning behaviors into learning from product review information. As such, social learning behavior is operationalized into three dimensions: learning from guid ance information, learning from short video comment information, and learning from product review information. ## 2.4. Attitude transfer theory

Attitude transfer refers to user perceptions and attitudes being transferred from one entity to another related entity [34]. Specifically, attitude transfer theory has been applied in social psychology to explain the formation of impressions [35] and in social commerce to understand consumer purchase behavior [22]. For example, customers’ initial product attitudes formed on Facebook can be transferred to the e-commerce site and shape final product attitudes [22]. These attitudes can also be transferred from advertisements to advertised products [36].

This study involves two related entities: influencer and product. We expect a transference of attitude from the influencer to the product and, subsequently, engagement behavior. In our research context, when customers watch commercial short videos, view the videos’ comments and responses, and browse product reviews, they learn about the influencer. Their cognition improves and affects the attitude they form towards the influencer. Their attitude towards the influencer is expected to carry over to the product. As a vital part of our research model, the underlying process of attitude transfer is shown in Fig. 2. However, product review information is more relevant to the product than that to the influencer, resulting in a slightly weaker degree of transfer. Thus. learning from product review information is represented by the dotted lines. Different types of attitudes influence individuals’ behaviors in various ways [22]. However, research is scarce on how attitudes in short video commerce are transferred between the two types of entities. Furthermore, empirical literature exploring whether and how transferred attitudes are associated with customer engagement behaviors is lacking. Thus, attitude is identified at the interpersonal level (i.e., attitude towards influencer) and product level (i.e., attitude towards product), and applied to relate social learning behavior to customer engagement.

Another related concept is trust transfer, which is an effective mechanism for building and enhancing trust [37]. Trust transfer theory refers to the cognitive process by which trust is transferred among entities. This occurs when an individual develops trust in an entity because of the trust they have in an associated entity [38]. According to trust transfer theory, transmission is based on an individual’s assessment of an entity’s similarity, proximity, or belongingness to the trusted referent [39,40]. As a mature and useful analytical framework, trust transfer theory has been widely adopted in social commerce and e-commerce research to examine the underlying transformation mechanism of trust in reducing perceived uncertainty and leading to positive outcomes such as customer engagement [6], online purchase intention [41], repurchase intention [42], and consumer loyalty [43].

Trust transfer can be classified into two types: inter-channel and intra-channel [44]. Inter-channel trust transfer refers to the transfer of trust from one context to another, whereas intra-channel trust transfer describes that between entities in an identical channel, which is the case in our study. The transfer process is the same between trust with attitude transfer, in that both involve transfers from one entity to another related entity; however, the object of transfer is different (i.e., one is trust and the other one is attitude). Trust is conceptualized as the subjective belief that the other party will act in a moral, dependable, and social manner and will not behave opportunistically [45]. Attitude generally refers to the overall evaluation of an attitude object [46]. It is experienced and learned [47], and can also be affected by advertisements [47] and disconfirmation and satisfaction levels [48], which fits the context of this research. Furthermore, according to the theory of reasoned action (TRA) [49] and the theory of planned behavior (TPB) [17], behavioral intention is the main determinant of behavior, and the intention to perform that behavior is determined by a person’s attitude towards it, emphasizing the role of attitude in intention and behavior [50]. In short video commerce, a positive attitude towards the influencer or product is a good starting point for stimulating customer engagement. Therefore, attitude transfer theory instead of trust transfer theory is used in our study.

## 2.5. Relationship norms theory

In interpersonal relationship studies, relationship norms are classified into exchange relationships and communal relationships [51]. The difference between the two is based on the criteria for providing benefit to the relationship partner [51]. Specifically, in exchange relationships, individuals provide benefits to others while expecting to receive commensurate benefits in return: that is. compensation is required. This type of relationship norm is similar to the communication pattern between business partners and emphasizes self-oriented interests. In contrast, communal relationships focus on the needs and benefits of others. Thus, individuals give benefits to others as a demonstration of concern for them without any expectation of repayment or quid pro quo, placing greater emphasis on friendship [52]. The two categories of relationships are initially treated as mutually exclusive [51], but have evolved to be viewed as a combination of both exchange and communal behaviors [53].

Relationship norms play a critical role in evaluating individual behaviors [18] and have been adopted in relationships such as those between consumers and brands [54], influencers and followers [55], and humans and chatbots [56,57]. Furthermore, the moderating role of relationship norms has been confirmed in environments such as service failure [58–60], running apps [61], and AI chatbots [56,57]. In some cases, previous studies have provided evidence of the moderating role of relationship norms. Regarding short video commerce, relationship norms may play a significant role in the relationship between influencers and customers and serve as boundary conditions under which learning from different atmospheric cues is associated with attitudes towards influencers.

Fig. 3 shows the research model of this study, which is based on the theoretical background in the previous section. Specifically, there are two different processes of social learning. One is an external interaction process, and the other is an internal psychological process [62]. In our study, atmospheric cues in short video commerce offer an environment for social learning, and customers can acquire basic knowledge in the process of interaction through guidance information, short video comment information, and product review information. Attitudes towards the influencer and product reflect customers’ psychological processes, among which attitudes can also be transferred from the influencer to the product. Positive attitudes then ultimately trigger customer engagement. Finally, relationship norms (exchange relationships or communal relationships) are related to influencers’ competence or warmth. Accordingly, we examine whether relationship norms moderate the relationships between customers’ social learning behavior and their attitudes towards the influencer.

## 3.1. Social learning and customer attitude

Influencers can post commercial videos to advertise products, and individuals can share information and experience on short video platforms for others to view and learn, which helps other customers know more about the social commerce environment and form an attitude towards the influencer and product [63]. Thus, in this study, we investigate the effects on customers’ attitudes towards the influencer and product resulting from learning from guidance information, short video comment information, and product review information.

First, guidance information is utilitarian and related to task effi ciency and effectiveness [28]. If the cues an influencer offers are directly relevant to task accomplishment, they are categorized as task cues (i.e., guidance information) [12]. Therefore, short video advertisements are identified as guidance information. Similarly, Wang et al. [12] regarded task cues as guidance information, indicating that task cues positively affect customers’ dynamic brand experience in a brand live streaming e-commerce context. As suggested by Tang and Zhang [28], task cues are perceptible information that can assist consumers in accomplishing their shopping goals. According to Shen et al. [64], three aspects of technology attractiveness cues (i.e., task, social, and physical attractiveness) are positively associated with community involvement, which in turn affects social commerce engagement. In practice, short video advertisement (task cues) is an emerging marketing tool, which is quickly and widely accessible. and dependent on customers' personalized needs Influencers create various kinds of videos, such as melodramatic or daily life content, to embed advertised product information. Task cues (i.e., guidance information) flexibly and repeatedly gratify customers’ needs for access to information about both the influencer and the product. This can enhance customers’ satisfaction and cognition, and improve their attitudes towards the influencer and product.

Second, short video comment information focuses on interpersonal interactions and reflects the social presence of other customers and influencers [30]. In this study, customers’ comments on commercial short videos and influencers’ responses are considered as short video comment information. According to Tang and Zhang [28]. social cues imply the possibility for customers to connect with others, hence eliciting connective affordance. Wang et al. [12] provided strong evidence that social cues exert a positive effect on customers’ dynamic brand experience in brand live streaming e-commerce. Wu et al. [13] regarded interactivity and vividness as atmospheric cues, and illuminated their important roles in travel application usage and site revisits through the serial mediating processes of flow experience, perceived usefulness, perceived enjoyment, and attitude. In addition, Fei et al. [30] suggested that social cues in live streaming commerce include herding messages and interaction text, both of which can capture consumers’ exogenous attention. In practice, customers who are satisfied with their experiences may develop positive attitudes [65]. In short video commerce, short video comment information provides opportunities for consumers to interact with influencers, peer customers, or potentially anyone else regarding video content, product, and influencer. Customers initially browse short videos at random and may not know what they need. While browsing other customers’ comments, and communicating with other customers and influencers, customers may acquire social knowledge and social experience. Customers may also gain more information on both the influencer and the product by reading comments, thus enhancing their perceptions of the credibility and trustworthiness of the influencer and product [11].

Finally, product review information consists of content created by consumers who have bought the product, such as reviews of the influ encers and products displayed on the product page or short video page [31]. Thus, product review information is a type of information deliv ered by other consumers about the product, influencer, or brand. Typically, customers read ratings and reviews posted by previous consumers and acquire information, such as the cognition of product parameters, judgment of product quality, and evaluation of the influencer, which is directly related to customers’ attitudes. After the direct social learning process, customers assess whether the product is of high quality and the influencer is trustworthy [66]. Prior studies have validated the positive role of product review information (one dimension of social cues) on consumers’ downloads of software samples [66], behavioral intention [31], and the efficiency of the online shopping process [67]. Moreover, Lee et al. [31] unveiled that the effects of user-generated cues on attitudinal and behavioral intentions interact with system-generated cues. According to Wang et al. [67], learning from word-of-mouth can reinforce consumers’ intrinsic motivations.

Overall, these three dimensions of atmospheric cues provide social experience and information for customers and help them form attitudes towards an object. Thus, we hypothesize the following:

H1. Learning from guidance information is positively associated with customers’ attitudes towards the influencer (H1a) and product (H1b).

H2. Learning from short video comment information is positively associated with customers’ attitudes towards the influencer (H2a) and product (H2b).

H3. Learning from product review information is positively associated with customers’ attitudes towards the influencer (H3a) and product (H3b).

## 3.2. Customer attitude and engagement

Attitude generally refers to an overall evaluation of a particular object, involving positive or negative assessment [46], and has been found to affect customer belief, motivation, and behavior [68]. We distinguish between the attitude objects “influencer” and “product” in the short video commerce environment. In this study, attitudes towards influencers and products are defined as customers’ positive or negative perceptions, and favorable or unfavorable feelings about the influencers or the products, which are formed in their learning process through short video commerce. Furthermore, we define customer engagement as customers’ behavioral manifestation towards influencers or sellers, involving transactional behaviors such as making purchases and non-transactional behaviors such as following the influencer’s account, sharing shopping experiences, recommending quality products, and helping others.

Extant studies have extensively explored the effect of consumers attitudes on their engagement in a variety of fields. For instance, Chu et al. [69] found that consumers’ attitudes towards corporate social responsibility play an important role in their engagement in social media. Pang [70] confirmed that users’ attitudes towards WeChat usage are positively associated with eWOM engagement. According to Arghashi and Yuksel [71], consumers’ favorable attitudes towards augmented reality applications can enhance their engagement. Furthermore, Serenko [72] classified attitudes as explicit attitude and implicit attitude, and found that implicit attitude towards digital piracy exerts a positive direct effect on engagement in digital piracy, while the effect of explicit attitude towards digital piracy on engagement is fully mediated by behavioral intentions to engage in digital piracy. Bailey et al. [73] posited that both consumers’ general attitudes towards social networking sites and attitudes towards marketers’ social networking sites can trigger their engagement with social networking sites.

In short video commerce, customers’ attitudes towards the influencer and product can shape their engagement. If the customer perceives the influencer to be interesting, pleasant, and trustworthy, they are more likely to continue following video updates. Moreover, if customers develop a favorable opinion about the influencer, they will be more inclined to purchase the advertised product, interact more with influencers, or recommend the account to others [74]. In addition, customers’ positive feelings towards the product may boost their intention to purchase and recommend it to others [6]. Furthermore, based on customers’ favorable attitudes towards the advertised product, they may tend to interact with other customers positively, which helps them make decisions and increase their engagement. Therefore, we expect that the more customers develop a positive attitude towards the influencer and product, the more likely they will form an intention to engage in short video commerce. Thus, we hypothesize the following:

H4. Customers’ attitudes towards the influencer are positively associated with their engagement.

H5. Customers’ attitudes towards the product are positively associ ated with their engagement.

## 3.3. Attitude transfer and the mediating effect of attitude towards the influencer

As discussed earlier, the attitude transfer process refers to the mechanism by which a customer’s attitude towards an entity can be transferred to a relevant person or entity through their association [22, 36]. Previous research has applied attitude transfer theory to online environments. Specifically, extant research has indicated how customers’ initial attitudes towards the product advertised on Facebook can be transferred to the linked e-commerce site [22]. Jiang et al. [35] noted that users’ attitudes towards a corporate website can be transferred to their overall image of the company, and this attitude fully mediates the relationship between the two variables. Accordingly, in short video commerce, influencers with professional abilities such as election abil ity, interpretation ability, and professional knowledge can induce customers’ positive attitudes towards the influencer, which can further enrich customers’ knowledge about the advertised product, thereby facilitating their positive attitudes towards it. Thus, when customers watch a commercial video, view short video comments, and browse product reviews, they learn more about the influencers and subsequently develop positive attitudes towards them. Customers may then hold an attitude towards the influencer formed via learning behavior related to the advertised product because of the relatedness between the influencer and the advertised product [36]. This is similar to the findings of prior studies on the mechanism of trust transfer [37,39].

According to trust transfer theory, trust in a known target can be transferred to another relatively unknown target related to the known target [38]. This mature theory has been widely investigated in the contexts of e-commerce and social commerce. Specifically, consumers trust can be transferred from a traditional sales channel to a web-based channel [38], from Internet payments to mobile payments [44], from an online channel to a mobile channel [75], from a review site to a specific reviewer [76], or even from streamers to products [77] and community members [6]. Meanwhile, the trust transfer process also uncovers the mediating effect of trust in general reviews [76] and even trust in streamers [77]. Furthermore, customers’ cognitive trust and emotional trust can be transferred from web payments to mobile payments [78].

In line with these findings, we contend that customers’ positive attitudes towards influencers can enhance their attitudes towards prod ucts, especially when they have limited information or experience with which to evaluate the product. Furthermore, there are sufficient reasons to support that customers’ attitudes towards the influencer mediate the association between social learning and attitudes towards the product. Thus, we hypothesize the following:

H6. Customers’ attitudes towards the influencer are positively associated with their attitudes towards the product.

H6a. Customers’ attitudes towards the influencer mediate the relationship between learning from guidance information and their attitude towards the product.

H6b. Customers’ attitudes towards the influencer mediate the relationship between learning from short video comment information and their attitudes towards the product.

H6c. Customers’ attitudes towards the influencer mediate the relationship between learning from product review information and their attitudes towards the product.

## 3.4. Moderating effect of relationship norms

As discussed in the literature review, two types of relationship norms exist: communal relationships and exchange relationships. The stereotype content model characterizes social groups and interpersonal impressions into two basic stereotyping dimensions: warmth and competence [79], which are in accordance with communal relationships and exchange relationships respectively. Thus, customers who follow communal relationships tend to expect influencers to deliver warmth, whereas those who follow exchange relationships tend to expect influencers to exhibit competence. Similarly, social relationship theory proposes that individuals are inclined to utilize relationship norms to direct their attitudes and behaviors towards their partners [80]. Therefore, customers’ attitudes towards and perceptions of influencers may be affected by the category of relationship norms dominance [56]. By learning from various specific atmospheric cues, customers become aware of whether the influencer demonstrates more competence or warmth. Accordingly, when perceiving warmth from the influencer, focal customers under communal relationships are more willing to develop positive attitudes towards the influencer. In contrast, when recognizing that the influencer is competent, customers following exchange relationships would develop positive attitudes towards the influencer. This means that customers’ attitudes towards or perceptions of influencers rely on the type of relationship that is dominant in their minds during the social learning process.

In practice, when customers follow exchange relationships, they are more likely to be attracted to information concerning commercial relationships that reflect influencers’ abilities and competence. Influencers in commercial short videos often exhibit professional knowledge, sufficient information, and experience with the advertised products to demonstrate their expertise, trustworthiness, and ability (Liu et al., 2022). Influencers tend to be regarded as experts with high competence in offering reliable information and useful advice [81]. In addition, on e-commerce websites, consumers who purchase advertised products often comment on the products or influencers. Especially in situations where influencers advertise for themselves, consumers usually post comments about influencers’ competence and ability, making other customers aware of the influencers and advertised products [5]. When exchange relationships are salient, customers’ favorable attitudes towards influencers will develop through repeated positive comments. Thus, competence-related information about influencers is usually conveyed as guidance information and product review information for customers to learn. Therefore, customers in the exchange relationship condition perceive that the influencer is competent and develop positive attitudes.

Conversely, when customers follow communal relationships, they expect influencers to deliver friendly and caring information, and judge them based on whether they are warm or caring. Furthermore, customers pay more attention to whether influencers concentrate on interpersonal relationships and responsibility to meet their wants and needs [82]. In the comment area of short videos, some customers post comments on the influencer, product, or video content to interact with the influencer or other customers [1]. The influencer often responds to customers in a warm and friendly manner, which is different from the situation with guidance information and product review information, thus developing warm human contact [7]. That is, influencers often provide warmth-related information for customers in short video comment information. Accordingly, customers who have a communal relationship orientation and perceive the warmth of the influence would form favorable attitudes. Thus, we hypothesize the following:

H7a. Exchange relationships strengthen the association between learning from guidance information and attitude towards the influencer.

H7b. Communal relationships strengthen the association between learning from short video comment information and attitude towards the influencer.

H7c. Exchange relationships strengthen the association between learning from product review information and attitude towards the influencer.

## 4. Methodology

## 4.1. Research context

We selected users of TikTok as the target respondents for two reasons. First, TikTok has skyrocketed in popularity, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, not only in China but also overseas. Although various short video platforms exist, TikTok is currently the leader. The number of monthly active users of TikTok reached nearly 700 million as of July 2022, with more than 2 billion downloads globally. Although both amateurs and influencers can create short videos, this study focuses on commercial videos created by influencers, as most short videos posted by amateurs are non-commercial and primarily for recording and sharing life experiences. Furthermore, influencers are digital personalities with a large number of fans and followers, who can influence others [83]. Influencers can be active creators of short video content and have greater marketing power than general users [1].

Second, TikTok is commercialized with several categories of commercial influencers. Most commercial videos are created by influencers and concentrate on selling a single product. These videos link to TikTok stores to help promote sales of advertised products. Furthermore, customers can view the comments on promotional videos and even browse reviews of products and influencers without leaving the platform when it is linked to the e-commerce site through promotional videos. In short, TikTok is a vibrant social commerce platform, and the influencers on it aim to promote products to customers. Thus, TikTok is an appropriate platform for our research context.

## 4.2. Measurement development

The instruments were mainly adapted from existing research where psychometric properties have been established. The references and detailed measurements for the items are presented in Appendix B. A 7- point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree) was used to record responses. These measures were adjusted to fit the context of short video commerce. Social learning behavior in the three dimensions of atmospheric cues was measured using three formative items (frequency, duration. and material quality) based on Chen et al. [20] with some modifications and adjustments to fit the short video commerce context. The measurement items pertaining to attitudes towards influencers and products were adjusted from Silvera and Austad [36] and Ko et al. [84] respectively. Exchange relationships and communal relationships were measured using three items adapted from Li et al. [59] and Ma and Wang [60]. In measuring engagement, we adopted an eight-item scale developed by Wongkitrungrueng and Assarut [11]. Two researchers and three PhD students were invited to examine the readability and clarity of each item. The questionnaire was subsequently translated into Chinese and translated back with their help. A pilot test was carried out to validate the measurement tools, and 30 respondents who had previously purchased products through promotional short videos were invited to complete the questionnaire. In line with the feedback from respondents, some modifications were made to the phrasing of the questions.

## 4.3. Data collection and sampling

To empirically test the proposed research hypotheses and address our research questions, we collected data from an online questionnaire targeting TikTok users in China. The online questionnaire was carried out via the professional online survey website (www.sojump.com). To ensure the representativeness of the responses, both the paid sampling service offered by SOJUMP and social media platforms, such as TikTok and WeChat, were employed to distribute the URL of the questionnaire from July 11 to August 11, 2022. Multiple channels can obtain opinions from diverse commercial short video consumers and enhance the overall reach to general online consumers in China, as demonstrated by Gong et al. [78]. Furthermore, t-tests were conducted to examine data bias. No significant differences were found between the data from SOJUMP and those from the social media platforms. Monetary incentives were offered to each respondent to encourage their participation.

Some data-filtering procedures were implemented to improve the data quality. First, screening questions were employed to ensure that respondents had shopped on this platform. These screeners included the following questions: “In the past two months, how many times have you watched promotional short videos on TikTok?” “In the past two months, how many times have you shopped directly via promotional short videos on TikTok?” and “How long have you shopped directly via promotional short videos on TikTok?” Respondents who answered “zero” were excluded from our research. Other respondents were asked to recall their recent shopping experience on this platform and complete the questionnaire based on this experience. This approach ensures a consistent frame of reference in terms of the reference group of influencers and perceived experience across the proposed constructs in our research model [85]. Second, the respondents were asked to write down the influencer’s TikTok account through the question “What’s the influencer’s TikTok account in your recent shopping experience on TikTok?”. The follower counts for the influencers the respondents mentioned were then collected manually. Following Hung et al.’s [5] criteria and definition of influencers, if the number of followers ranges from 30,000 to 100,000, the influencer is classified as a micro-influencer, while macro-influencers usually have more than 5 million followers. Consequently, we excluded respondents who reported influencers with fewer than 30,000 followers. Third, to prevent the same respondent from repeatedly participating in the survey, we only allowed one entry from a given IP address via SOJUMP’s technical options. Fourth, questionnaires that were completed in less than 7 or more than 15 min were eliminated. Fifth, questionnaires that contained more than 10 repeated answers in a row were dropped to ensure that the respondents answered the questionnaire with due care [86]. Finally, a question (“Please choose 1 for this question”) unrelated to other items but that required attention was placed in our questionnaire to identify invalid responses.

A total of 687 questionnaires were collected, yielding 542 valid questionnaires. The demographics of the respondents and their usage of short video commerce are described in Table 3. Among all participants, 52.77 % were male. To ensure the appropriateness of the sample, we checked the statistics of TikTok users conducted by iResearch [87]. The report indicated that male users were more active in utilizing short videos than female users, with an average ratio of 53.49 %:46.51 %, which is in line with the gender ratio of our survey respondents (52.77 %:47.23 %). In addition, our gender ratio is consistent with previous research investigating short videos [88,89]. Moreover, the ages of respondents ranged from 18 to over 45 years (n = 15, 2.77 %), with the majority of respondents being 18–35 years old (n = 433, 79.89 %), which is the major group of online shoppers [90]. Our results also indicated that the majority (n = 526, 97.05 %) of respondents have been shopping through commercial short videos for more than 6 months.

## 5. Data analysis and results

## 5.1. Overview of the empirical design

The PLS-SEM and fsQCA were combined to test and further explore the relationships in our model because they can overcome each method’s limitations and improve the robustness of the findings. This mixed-method approach has been applied in numerous studies [91,92]. We decided to use PLS-SEM rather than covariance-based structural equation modeling (CB-SEM) because PLS-SEM is applicable to exploratory theoretical development and prediction [93], as is the case in our study. Moreover, PLS-SEM is suitable if the structural model is complicated with many indicators and relationships, as in our research model [93]. Finally, PLS-SEM is appropriate for hypothesis testing when the path model involves one or more formative constructs [94]. Our research model involves not only reflective constructs but also formative ones. PLS-SEM can handle both reflective and formative constructs in a model concurrently [94,95]. Thus, PLS-SEM is the preferred method in our study. SmartPLS 3.3.3 was adopted for data analysis.

We used fsQCA as well to offer an alternative perspective to the PLS SEM results, as PLS-SEM might suffer from the disadvantages of causal symmetry. As a set-theoretic method, fsQCA can be used to investigate the combined effects of antecedents and identify the asymmetric relationships between antecedents and outcomes [96]. In a symmetric relationship, high values for an independent variable are related to high values for a dependent variable, and vice versa [97]. However, symmetric methods cannot reveal the combinations of antecedents that lead to high levels of the outcome. In contrast, asymmetric approaches can identify sufficient and necessary conditions to produce high levels of a dependent outcome by combining specific independent antecedents [98]. Accordingly, extant studies have adopted fsQCA to supplement the findings of research models originally tested by PLS-SEM [99,100].

In summary, these two techniques are based on different principles and have different focuses (Rasoolimanesh et al., 2022). Combining these two methods provides symmetric and asymmetric perspectives, enabling us to explore not only the direct associations between antecedents and outcomes but also the various combinations of antecedents that lead to the same outcome, thereby yielding a more enriching understanding of customer engagement. Hence, in addition to examining the linear effects, this research combines seven factors (e.g., learning from guidance information, learning from short video comment infor mation, learning from product review information, attitude towards influencer, attitude towards product, relationship norms, and length of using short video commerce) to investigate the configurations for generating high customer engagement applying fsQCA 3.0.

Discriminant validity was assessed using the criterion proposed by Fornell and Larcker [102], Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT), and the cross-loadings of the indicators. As described in Table 4, the square root of AVE is greater than its correlation with any other construct, suggesting good discriminant validity of the constructs. The results (Appendix C) show that the factor loadings of each item are higher than the cross-loadings on other factors, providing further support for discriminant validity.

## 5.2.4. Moderating effect analysis

We used multigroup analysis to detect the moderating effect of relationship norms. Following Huang and Ha [58], the scores of exchange relationships were reversed, and then the six items were summed to create a general relationship norm index. Before conducting multigroup analysis, the three-stage Measurement Invariance of Composite Models (MICOM) procedure advanced by Henseler et al. [107] was carried out to test for potential measurement invariance concerns, as presented in Appendix E. First, the exchange relationships and communal relationships had identical indicators, data treatment, and algorithm settings, suggesting satisfactory configural invariance. Second, compositional invariance was assessed utilizing the function of premutation analysis in SmartPLS. The result shows the original correlation between the composite scores is larger than the 5 % quantile of empirical distribution, thus supporting compositional invariance. Finally, step three demonstrates that there are no differences in mean values and variances, indicating a full measurement invariance This means that we can conduct a multigroup analysis [107,108].

Finally, the SmartPLS Multi-Group Analysis (MGA) was used to examine the moderating effect of relationship norms. As presented in Table 7, the empirical findings reveal that exchange relationships positively moderate the relationship between learning from guidance information and attitude towards the influencer, and communal relationships positively moderate the relationship between learning from short video comment information and attitude towards the influencer, which provides support for H7a and H7b. ## 5.3. FsQCA analysis

## 5.3.1. Calibration

In this study, seven variables, comprising learning from guidance information, learning from short video comment information, learning from product review information, attitude towards the influencer, attitude towards the product, relationship norms, and length of using short video commerce were selected as antecedents, mainly based on the following considerations. First, the effects of the first six factors are supported by theoretical and empirical findings. Second, the results of PLS-SEM reveal the length of using short video commerce is positively related to customer engagement while other control variables are not significantly associated with customer engagement. Therefore, the length of using short video commerce is considered as an antecedent variable.

Before running fsQCA, the original data were converted into fuzzy set membership scores for calibration. The calibration criteria include three anchors: full non-membership (value=0), crossover point (value=0.5), and full membership (value=1) [98]. Before calibrating the variables, the items measuring each construct were summed [109]. Then, utilizing fsQCA 3.0, the data were calibrated according to the recommended breakpoints of 5 %, 50 %, and 95 % of the summated measures [98]. Finally, fsQCA transformed the 7-point Likert scale into a fuzzy set ranging from 0 to 1. Furthermore, a column with a membership score of 0.5 was added to 0.001 to ensure that no case was deleted when the fuzzy set was computed.

## 5.3.3. Sufficient conditions analysi

To conduct sufficient conditions analysis, the truth table consisting of $2 ^ { k }$ rows was created and refined, where k is the number of causal conditions. The thresholds of case frequency, configuration consistency, and proportional reduction in inconsistency (PRI) score were set to 2, 0.8, and 0.7, respectively [98]. Finally, the complex solution, intermediate solution, and parsimonious solution were assembled and presented. According to Ragin [98], the intermediate solution and parsimonious solution were applied to combine various antecedents. The results of various condition configurations are shown in Table 9. Each configuration represents a sufficient condition for high customer engagement.

Seven configurations were found to result in high customer engagement. The overall solution coverage and solution consistency are 0.648 and 0.935, respectively, and the consistency of all the configurations exceeds the threshold of 0.9. The findings reveal some interesting conclusions. Regarding customer attitude, the presence of attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product in six out of seven configurations indicates that a positive attitude facilitates customer engagement. Second, the seven solutions also present the substitutive effects between different social learning behaviors. Specifically, learning from guidance information is found to be present in S2, S6, and S7 while is either present or absent in S1, S3, S4, and S5. Learning from short video comment information is present in S3, S4, S6, and S7, while is either present or absent in S1, S2, and S5. Learning from product review information is found to be present in S3, S5, S6, and S7 while is either present or absent in S1, S2, and S4. These configurations confirm that it is not necessary to require all the social learning be haviors to be present to motivate customer engagement in short video commerce. Third, S6 and S7 suggest that experienced individuals might learn more from guidance information, short video comment information, and product review information when shopping through short video commerce. These two configurations also confirm that experienced individuals might be dominated by communal relationships. Finally, comparing S2 and S4, we find that the presence of learning from guidance information coupled with low values (absence) of relationship norms $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ exchange relationships), or the presence of learning from short video comment information and communal relationships can lead to high customer engagement, given that attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product are both high.

## 6. Discussion and implications

## 6.1. Discussion of findings

In short video commerce, previous research is inconclusive about how customer engagement can be developed from the perspective of social learning. Customers learn information from three dimensions of atmospheric cues [20], which may affect their attitudes [11] and, in turn, their engagement. Accordingly, this study investigates the theoretical linkage between social learning, customer attitude, and customer engagement by combining two complementary methods, PLS-SEM and fsQCA. We do this through the lens of social learning, atmospheric cues, attitude transfer, and relationship norms, which offers an interesting perspective to examine the antecedents of customer engagement. PLS-SEM views the effect of social learning behavior and customer attitude as well as relationship norms independently, while fsQCA il lustrates the configurations associated with high customer engagement. Interesting insights are unveiled when comparing PLS-SEM results with findings emanating from fsQCA.

First, the empirical findings of PLS-SEM suggest that customer engagement is driven by their attitudes, which are divided into attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product. Specifically, the two types of attitudes are found to be positively related to customer engagement [69,73]. FsQCA results are consistent with PLS-SEM results in that the presence of attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product in six out of seven configurations reveals that this condition facilitates customer engagement, though this is not a necessary condition for customers to develop high engagement intentions. The results of PLS-SEM and fsQCA concur with prior studies, which found that product attitude on Facebook can foster the likelihood of purchase and recommendation on a linked e-commerce site [22]. It also complements extant research indicating that individuals’ attitudes towards the influencer are associated with their behavioral intentions [111]. Furthermore, both TPB and TRA highlight that attitude is a strong determinant of behavioral intention [17,49], which is once again veri fied in short video commerce.

Second, the PLS-SEM results reveal that the three forms of learning behavior (i.e., learning from guidance information, learning from short video comment information, and learning from product review infor mation) are positively associated with attitude towards the influencer. Furthermore, learning from short video comment information and learning from product review information are positively related to attitude towards the product. The findings are congruent with the propositions of Chen et al. [20] and Riaz et al. [21] that customers social learning behaviors in social commerce will change their cognition and attitude. However, in contrast to our expectations, the results sug gest that learning from guidance information is insignificantly associated with attitude towards the product, suggesting that learning from guidance information would not improve customers’ attitudes towards the product directly. This is inconsistent with extant studies [21]. One possible explanation is that influencers may avoid placing the advertised product in the most engaging spots of the video, attracting customers attention more to the influencers themselves, and less to the advertised product [19]. Another possible explanation could be that the mediating effects may lead to H1b not being supported. This is evident from our results, which show that attitude towards the influencer fully mediates the relationship between learning from guidance information and attitude towards the product, while it acts as a partial mediator in the relationship between learning from short video comment information and learning from product review information with attitudes towards the product. In contrast, the findings of fsQCA suggest that not all social learning behaviors are needed to motivate customer engagement in short video commerce. and there are substitutive effects between learning from different dimensions of atmospheric cues. Furthermore, experienced customers can learn more from these three dimensions of atmospheric cues, which is not obvious from the PLS-SEM results. One plausible explanation is that experienced customers are more familiar with the short video platform and know where to look for information.

Third, regarding relationship norms, there are similarities between PLS-SEM and fsQCA results. The findings of PLS-SEM indicate that the relationships between learning from guidance information and learning from short video comment information with attitude towards the influencer are moderated by relationship norms. However, the moderating effect of relationship norms on the relationship between learning from product review information and attitude towards the influencer is not significant. Specifically, the effect of learning from guidance information on attitude towards the influencer is stronger for customers in the exchange relationship condition. Conversely, the effect of learning from short video comment information on attitude towards the influencer is stronger for customers following the communal relationship norms. S2 and S4 of fsQCA results reveal that if the presence of learning from guidance information coupled with low relationship norms $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ exchange relationship) or if learning from short video comment information and communal relationships are simultaneously present, they may facilitate high customer engagement, which is also coherent with our moderation hypotheses of relationship norms. Furthermore, these findings echo the propositions of several studies [58,59] that customers tend to pay more attention to messages conveying warmth when communal relationships are salient, while they are more attracted to information signaling competence and ability when exchange relationships are salient. However, according to PLS-SEM results, we found no moderating effect of relationship norms on the relationship between learning from product review information and attitude towards the influencer, a new phenomenon that differs from other studies of relationship norms. In contrast, the fsQCA results suggest that high levels of customer engagement result from high levels of learning from product review information and relationship norms (i.e., communal relationships) in causal configurations presented in S5.

Finally, PLS-SEM results also indicate that attitude towards the influencer can be transferred to the advertised product, thus facilitating customers to follow the influencer, purchase products, or even recommend the influencer or commercial short video to others. The result is consistent with Bhattacharyva and Bose [22]. which show that attitude can be shifted between two relevant entities. However, this attitude transfer mechanism cannot be reflected in causal configurations of fsQCA findings. In contrast, fsQCA results (S6 and S7) suggest that experienced customers may follow communal relationships and learn more from the three dimensions of atmospheric cues. Taken together, the comparison between PLS-SEM and fsQCA results illustrates that both approaches are complementary and can be utilized together to improve the range and depth of the solutions available to influencers and managers to better induce customer engagement. The empirical findings of this study offer several implications for theory and practice.

## 6.2. Theoretical implications

This study develops a theoretical model to reveal how social learning from different atmospheric cues affects customer engagement, and this effect is mediated by customer attitude. Further, the mediating effect of customer attitude is moderated by relationship norms. The finding yield several prominent theoretical implications for the existing literature.

First, extant literature has investigated customers’ behavior (like engagement) of short videos through the lens of socio-technical theory [112], affordance theory [88], and self-determination theory [113], while few studies have explored the social learning theory that facilitates engagement behavior from the perspective of atmospheric cues in short video commerce. Thus, our study is the first to investigate social learning from atmospheric cues revealing the differential effects on customer engagement in the context of short video commerce. Specif ically, we conceptualize and decompose the atmospheric cues for short video commerce into learning from guidance information, learning from short video comment information and learning from product review information, and uncover their influence mechanisms on customer engagement behavior in the context of short video commerce. Therefore, this work increases the knowledge of customer engagement through social learning from atmospheric cues.

Second, different from social commerce or e-commerce, the influencer plays an important role in displaying and selling products in short video commerce. Our work adds to existing knowledge of customer attitude by untangling the atmospheric cues-induced attitudes towards the short video influencer and the promoted product. On the one hand, in line with TPB and TRA [17,49], this work emphasizes the vital role of attitude in triggering customer engagement in short video commerce. On the other hand, unlike a univalent view of prior literature [114], we differentiate between attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product, and empirically validate their transformation process in the context of short video commerce. Thereby, this work also extends the attitude transfer theory by revealing the function of attitude transfer in mediating the effects of atmospheric cues on customer engagement in the context of short video commerce.

Third, this work applies relationship norms theory to the context of short video commerce and explicates the boundary conditions of relationship norms for the three saliant atmospheric cues-induced attitudes towards influencers. While relationship norms have been extensively applied to understand service failure and recovery in the consumerbrand relationship [58,59], this work is a preliminary attempt to introduce the theory into the domain of short video commerce. Specifically, we appropriate relationship norms theory to map customers preference for exchange relationships or communal relationships into two basic stereotypes of customers’ interpersonal impressions towards influencers, i.e., competence and warmth, respectively. Our results demonstrate an interesting insight into the moderating role of rela tionship norms and offer some references for future research.

## 6.3. Practical implications

With short video commerce emerging growth, understanding the mechanism that affects customer engagement may reinforce competitive advantages for short video commerce management. This research provides management insights for how influencers and managers can leverage short video characteristics to boost customer engagement.

First, this study uses social learning theory to realize the atmospheric cues valence. That is, learning from guidance information, learning from short video comment information, and learning from product review information have positive impact on customers’ attitudes. The PLS-SEM results suggest that learning from guidance information has the strongest positive effect on attitude towards the influencer (H1a), and learning from product review information has the strongest effect on attitude towards the product (H3b). Thus, influencers and managers should consider strategies to boost social learning which can improve customers’ attitudes. For instance, to stimulate customers’ learning behavior in guidance information, influencers should utilize high quality equipment to show the advertised products to assist customers to see and visualize the real products more clearly. Moreover, to motivate learning from short video comment information (see results of H2), influencers should answer customers’ questions and interact with them frequently to help customers create a sense of belonging [6]. Additionally, managers can offer consumers some gifts or coupons to encourage them to positively share their experiences to foster other customers learning behavior in product review information. However, the substitution between different dimensions of social learning (see results of S2, S4, and S5) indicates that it is not necessary to require all social learning behaviors to be present to motivate customer engagement in short video commerce. Instead, influencers, in general, can either improve their skills to display the advertised products in short videos or interact with customers in the comment area. Whereas, regarding experienced cus tomers (S6 and S7), influencers should not only enhance their display skills in short videos, but also their interaction skills in the comment area, and managers are advised to take incentive measures as well. Taken together, influencers and managers should identify the configurations for high customer engagement and allocate resources and efforts appropriately. In this way, the configurations can help influencers and managers with prioritization as well as cost reduction.

Second. both attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product are essential to customer engagement (H4 and H5). The fsQCA findings indicate that attitude towards the influencer and attitude towards the product are present in six out of seven configurations, which confirms the importance of customer attitude. In the face of the devel opment of short video commerce and the growth of the number of influencers, advertising and promoting products through short videos have become increasingly prevalent. Thus, influencers and marketers should be aware of how to adapt to this new business model and enhance their performance in the competitive environment. For influencers, they can opt to make the short videos more engaging in terms of where and when the product appears, and display ample details as well as adopt creative and entertaining introductions of the product to shape customers’ attitudes [19]. They should also choose quality products before creating a commercial short video, which can reduce the probability of providing problematic products and thus increase customers’ positive attitudes towards the product. Furthermore, since attitude towards the influencer can be transferred directly to attitude towards the product (H6), marketers should evaluate the influencers dynamically and value those with high WOM. These influencers can offer pleasant viewing experiences to customers, and the positive attitude that has been fostered can be shifted to the advertised product.

Third, the PLS-SEM findings indicate that exchange relationships positively moderate the association between learning from guidance information and attitude towards the influencer (H7a), while communal relationships positively moderate the association between learning from short video comment information and attitude towards the influencer (H7b). Simultaneously, the fsQCA results (S2 and S4) verify the findings again by showing the presence of learning from guidance information coupled with low relationship norms (i.e., exchange relationships) or the presence of learning from short video comment information coupled with communal relationships can facilitate high customer engagement. Therefore, influencers can strengthen their interaction and communication with customers to ensure that the communal relationship is dominant between influencer and customer. Meanwhile, influencers can also adjust responses in short video comment areas to ensure that customers follow communal relationship norms, such as friendly and warm responses instead of formal ones. On the other hand, influencers can display their competence, ability, and professional knowledge relevant to products when creating commercial short videos. In this way, the exchange relationship is salient while customers are watching videos, thus enhancing their attitude towards the influencer. The fsQCA results (S6 and S7) suggest that experienced customers are inclined to follow communal relationships, suggesting influencers convey warmth and make customers feel cared for either in commercial short videos (guidance information) or in the comment area (short video comment information).

## 6.4. Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations, which provide some directions for future research. First, the research was limited to general products without a specific brand. Thus, the relationship norms between brands and customers were not considered in this work. Furthermore, the empirical findings might be different if products with well-known brands were explored. Second, this study focused on examining the roles of customers’ social learning behavior on their attitudes. However, trust is another vital factor, especially in a short video commerce environment, and trust transfer theory has been widely explored in similar live streaming commerce and traditional e-commerce contexts. Thus, future research could investigate customers’ social learning behavior from the perspective of trust and trust transfer theory. Third, three di mensions of atmospheric cues and two types of attitudes in short video commerce were identified in our study. Future studies could examine other dimensions of atmospheric cues, such as physical cues as well as customers’ attitudes towards short video commerce. Fourth, this research adopted cross-sectional data. Future research could utilize panel data and econometric models to investigate social learning behavior and customer engagement over time. Finally, the research was carried out with TikTok users in China, and it is unclear whether our proposed framework could be generalized to other cultural contexts or other short video platforms. Since short video commerce is a global phenomenon, samples from more short video platforms or countries are needed to validate the generalizability of our findings.

## 7. Conclusion

The explosive growth of short video commerce motivates this study to gain a better understanding of how social learning impacts customer engagement in the field of short video commerce. The social learning theory, attitude transfer theory, and relationship norms were adopted to explore how customers’ learning behavior, attitudes, and relationship norms affect customer engagement and the combinations of these fac tors that lead to high customer engagement using PLS-SEM-fsQCA methods. The findings of PLS-SEM suggest that customers’ attitudes towards the influencer and the product are the main predictors of customer engagement, and attitude can be further transferred from the influencer to the product. Learning from short video comment infor mation and learning from product review information are positively associated with both attitudes towards the influencer and the product, while learning from guidance information is only related to attitude towards the influencer. The findings also reveal that relationship norms moderate the associations between learning from guidance information and learning from short video comment information with attitude towards the influencer, which further enriches our theoretical framework. In addition, the results of fsQCA further highlight that customers’ social learning, attitude, and relationship norms can be used to improve customer engagement. Specifically, seven overall configurations are available to attain high customer engagement. This work contributes to extant studies by illustrating the relationships and combinations of antecedents that lead to high customer engagement in short video commerce. In doing so, it provides management implications for influencers and managers on ways to enhance customer engagement.
