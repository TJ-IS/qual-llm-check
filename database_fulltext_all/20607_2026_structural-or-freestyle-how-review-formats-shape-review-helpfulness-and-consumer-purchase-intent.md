## A R T I C L E I N F O

Keywords: Online review Structural reviewing Perceived processing fluency Hedonic and utilitarian products Implementational and deliberative mindsets Consumer expertise

## A B S T R A C T

Consumers seeking online reviews for purchase decisions are often overwhelmed by information overload resulting from the review explosion. Many platforms offer an innovative structural reviewing format to allow consumers to compose reviews on several product attributes. Through a field study and two experiments, we reveal the advantage of structural reviewing over traditional freestyle reviewing in generating helpful review and enhancing purchase intention through the mediation of perceived processing fluency. Furthermore, this advantage is more pronounced for utilitarian (versus hedonic) products, for consumers in an implementational (versus deliberative) mindset, and for novice (versus experienced) consumers. Our findings deepen the word-ofmouth marketing literature and offer important implications for practitioners to capitalize on structural reviewing.

## 1. Introduction

Over the past decades, existing literature across different fields such as information systems and marketing has verified that online reviews exert a substantial impact on product sales e.g., [1–3], stock perfor mance [4], and customers’ purchase intentions and satisfaction e.g., [5, 6]. Consumers rely on online reviews to alleviate product uncertainty in online shopping environments. Specifically, numerical ratings (e.g., aggregate star ratings or multi-dimensional ratings) reduce quality un certainty by signaling average product performance, whereas review text mitigates fit uncertainty by demonstrating whether the specific product attributes align with individual preferences [7]. By reducing fit uncertainty, review text facilitates customers in constructing prefer ences effectively [7]. Moreover, since review text conveys richer and more contextualized information, it allows consumers to evaluate a product holistically and plays a dominant role in attribute preference construction and purchase decisions [8,9].

Nevertheless, the abundance of text reviews can also overwhelm customers and create information overload [10,11], which urges con sumers to identify those helpful ones [12]. Review helpfulness refers to the extent to which consumers perceive a review as helpful in facilitating their purchase decision [13,14]. Review helpfulness vote reflects the assessments of its informativeness and diagnostic value. allowing customers to screen and prioritize relevant reviews more efficiently. Accordingly, many digital platforms have devoted themselves to pro ducing helpful reviews and expediting consumer purchase decisions. In addition to review helpfulness, we also examine purchase intentions as another key outcome variable. While review helpfulness captures con sumers’ cognitive evaluation of review quality, purchase intentions reflect their behavioral inclination to act [15]. Examining both out comes provides a more comprehensive understanding of consumer re sponses to review characteristics.

A substantial body of studies have examined the antecedents of re view helpfulness and purchase intention, such as various review char acteristics (e.g., certainty [13,16], arousal [17], informativeness [18,19] emotionality e.g., [5], or narrativity e.g., [5,16,17,20]), and reviewer characteristics (e.g., popularity [21], expertise [13], consensus [19], or trustworthiness and homophily [22]). However, only limited studies have explored how platform-related changes in the presentation of re view texts influence their helpfulness. For instance, the two-sided review format structures reviews into “pros” and “cons” sections (e.g., Booking. com), which shifts consumers’ attention allocation between positive and negative content [23] and improves credibility, which in turn increases helpfulness [24]. More related work lies in the multi-dimensional rating systems, which allow consumers to rate their experience along several pre-specified attribute ratings. Muti-dimensional ratings are deemed to be more helpful, facilitate more efficient matching, and lead to more satisfied purchases [25–28].

Building on this line of work in identifying platform-side factors in general and multi-dimensional ratings in specific, our research focuses on another form of review text presentation-structural review-which prompts users to evaluate a product textually on several prespecified attributes (as illustrated in Fig. 1B). This format contrasts with tradi tional freestyle reviews, where consumers are free to compose their reviews in any way they prefer within a blank field (as exemplified in Fig. 1A). Thus, structural reviews provide an alternative presentation format that may shape both perceived helpfulness and consumer pur chase intention. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, no existing research has investigated such a platform-initiated change, leaving several fundamental questions unanswered: (1) Does structural reviewing lead to more helpful reviews and higher purchase intention for prospective consumers than freestyle reviewing? (2) If yes, what is the underlying mechanism for the effectiveness of structural reviewing? (3) Is structural reviewing especially more effective for certain product types or some types of consumers?

To respond, we draw on the constructive preference perspective [29, 30], which posits that preference construction arises from the interplay between the information environment (e.g., information presentation) and the human information-processing system (e.g., cognitive struc ture). Based on this view, we propose that structural vs. freestyle review text formats shape preference construction through influencing pro cessing fluency. Specifically, structural (vs. freestyle) reviewing en hances both the salience and partitioning of attribute information and aligns with customers’ analytic cognitive structure by organizing in formation clearly along different important attributes. This structure facilitates information processing fluency, which in turn has down stream implications for perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention. We further identify product types, consumer mindsets, and expertise as the meaningful contingencies for the effectiveness of structural reviewing, because such factors qualify whether the struc tured cues as implied by structural format align with consumers’ in. formation needs in preference construction. We test our theoretical framework through one field study and two behavioral experiments (see Fig. 2). Study 1 analyzes real review data from JD.com to establish the main effect of structural reviewing on review helpfulness and its moderation by product type. Study 2 employs a controlled experiment to replicate these effects and demonstrate the mediating role of processing fluency. Study 3 further examines the moderating effects of consumer mindset and expertise. Together, these studies provide converging evi dence from both field and experimental settings.

This research contributes to the literature in several ways. Primarily, we advance the understanding of online review presentation by inves tigating structural reviewing-a platform-design intervention that re mains underexplored. By integrating both review helpfulness and consumer purchase intention as the key outcomes, we uncover the advantage of structural reviewing format over freestyle reviewing format and identify processing fluency as the critical psychological mechanism. Furthermore, we explore various boundary conditions of significant managerial implications, demonstrating that the efficacy of structural reviewing is most pronounced for utilitarian products, novice consumers, and those in an implementational mindset. Our findings deepen the word-of-mouth marketing literature and offer important implications for practitioners to capitalize on structural reviewing.

## 2. Literature review and hypothetical development

## 2.1. Factors influencing review helpfulness

Consumers nowadays rely increasingly on online reviews to guide their purchase decisions [14,31,32]. However, they may be over whelmed by the explosion of online reviews [10,16,32]. To address this challenge, scholars and practitioners have attempted to identify the factors that influence perceived review helpfulness for prospective consumers [33]. While early researchers focused on numerical ratings (e. g., metrics valence, volume, dispersion, etc. e.g., [34–36]), more recent research has shifted attention to review text, which conveys richer and more contextualized information. Broadly, three categories of anteced ents have been identified. First, review characteristics, such as review emotionality e.g., [5], arousal [17], certainty [13,16], and narrativity (the extent to which a review tells a story) [20], have been investigated widely. For instance, emotional arousal has an inverted-U-shaped effect on perceived review helpfulness [17]. Also, the cognitive features mat ter: Reviews with more explained actions (versus explained reactions) or with more attribute-based (versus experience-based) evaluations are perceived to be more helpful for utilitarian products than for hedonic products [37,38].

Second, reviewer characteristics, such as attention focus (self-focus versus other-focus) [14], temporal contiguity (i.e., consumers’ decision to submit a review soon or late after product consumption) [39], or identity disclosure [3], shape review helpfulness as well. More importantly, the effect of platform-related factors seems controversial, with mixed findings, thereby warranting more in vestigations or consideration of situational influence. Several studies find that platforms that explicitly label reviews written via mobile de vices or out of a “free” product trial produce mixed effects on the gen eration of helpful reviews [31,40–42]. Furthermore, platforms have begun to devote increasing efforts to designing effective review systems. For example, some platforms extract multiple keywords using natural language processing technology from reviews and display them at the top, which helps consumers to understand the full picture of the reviews quickly and accordingly increases helpfulness [32]. Our research extends this emerging research on multi-dimensional ratings from the numeric domain into the textual domain by focusing on two different review text presentations initiated by platforms: structural reviewing versus freestyle reviewing. As we elaborate below, we aim to examine the effect of different review text formats on review helpfulness and customer purchase intention.

## 2.2. The effects of online reviews on purchase intentions

Having reviewed the factors influencing review helpfulness, we now turn to another important outcome: purchase intentions. Scholars have identified various review factors (e.g., review content characteristics and source factors) that shape consumers’ purchase intentions. Regarding content characteristics, Jiménez and Mendoza [19] found that detailed reviews (compared with general reviews) lead to stronger purchase intentions. Similarly, content quality (i.e., perceived infor mativeness and persuasiveness) affects purchase intentions significantly [18], as do two-sided reviews [15]. From a source perspective, reviewer expertise, trustworthiness, and homophily influence intention to pur chase significantly [22]. Reviews written on a mobile device (labeled by “via mobile”) also influence consumers’ purchase intentions positively [31] by implying greater reviewing effort on smaller mobile screens. Beyond individual source characteristics, reviewer consensus matters as well: Jim´enez and Mendoza [19] demonstrate that higher levels of reviewer agreement with positive reviews (vs. low levels of agreement) increase purchase intentions.

While the majority of online review research has focused on eithe review helpfulness or purchase intention as the primary outcome, these two constructs capture different aspects of consumer response. Review helpfulness reflects consumers’ cognitive evaluation of review quality, whereas purchase intentions represent their behavioral inclination [15]. They are related but conceptually distinct: A review may be perceived as helpful yet not necessarily lead to purchase intention, or vice versa. Examining both outcomes provides a more comprehensive under standing of how review characteristics shape consumer responses.

## 2.3. Different reviewing formats: structural reviewing versus freestyle reviewing

In the current research, we focus on the structural versus freestyle reviewing enabled by digital platforms (e.g., JD.com, shein.com). Freestyle reviewing is a traditional format in which consumers describe their consumption experience freely in a given blank field. It seldom involves an explicit structure of product attributes for review display, although reviews themselves may demonstrate logical sequences (e.g., first, second, etc.) or event sequences (e.g., the narrativity-the extent to which a review tells a story) [20]. In contrast, structural reviewing en ables consumers to organize their consumption experience and evalua tions along the dimensions or attributes that are relevant to the reviewed product and prescribed by platforms. Therefore, if an individual de scribes a recent dining experience in a restaurant using freestyle reviewing, (s)he may post a review like the one shown in Fig. 1A, or could post a structural review of the same experience, as demonstrated in Fig. 1B.

Structural reviewing not only builds upon but also extends the attribute-based presentation format established in multi-dimensional rating research [25–28]. Chen, Hong, and Liu [25] demonstrate that multi-dimensional ratings facilitate matching between consumers and products by providing information on different attributes of products and mitigating consumers’ product quality uncertainty. Nevertheless, they ignore the greater impact of the detailed, richer information conveyed by review text, which may effectively mitigate product fit uncertainty and assist customers in constructing preferences [7,9]. Thus, our study investigates how this structural presentation of review texts enhances prospective consumers’ processing fluency of textual content. To the best of our knowledge, the current literature has hardly investi gated whether, how, and when structural reviewing (versus freestyle reviewing) influences review helpfulness.

## 2.4. The effects of review text formats on perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention

According to the constructive preference perspective [29,30], con sumers develop their preferences based on interaction between the in formation environment [30] (e.g., information presentation) and the human information-processing system (e.g., basic cognitive principles or cognitive structures). Attribute preference refers to the relative impor tance consumers place on the various attributes of a product in product evaluation or choice e.g., [43,44]. In the online environment, where information presentation is flexible and easily manipulated, consumers preferences are particularly susceptible to how information is presented [8,32]. Particularly, two major factors in information presentation have been identified in the literature: salience and partitioning of attribute information [45,46].

We propose that structural reviewing outperforms freestyle review ing in enhancing review helpfulness by improving both the salience and partitioning of attribute information. Research suggests that product attributes explicitly displayed are likely to receive higher weights than those present in a normal manner [45]. Similarly, attributes presented as separate categories tend to receive greater importance weight than those presented under an umbrella category [46], facilitating cognitive pro cessing. By explicitly presenting different attributes of a product in a list format, structural reviews help consumers acquire and process infor mation [29,30], thereby enhancing preference construction and increasing perceived review helpfulness.

Furthermore, from the perspective of the human informationprocessing system, consumers usually have developed and accumu lated the cognitive structures for a product based on previous experi ence, which they tend to apply when processing new attribute information [47]. Such a cognitive structure tends to adopt an analytical approach, leading customers to decompose products into different parts and evaluate them based on diagnostic characteristics [47]. When reading a freestyle review, consumers may need to go through the text to extract relevant attribute information. In contrast, a structural review text aligns with existing cognitive structures, enabling consumers to locate the performance information on specific attributes, construct attribute preference and achieve more efficient matching, and finally arrive at a more accurate product judgment with less cognitive re sources. This facilitates efficient preference construction and enhances consumer purchase intention.

In summary, we believe that structural reviews improve both the salience and partitioning of attribute information, and help consumers efficiently assess whether the product matches their preferences with less cognitive effort, thus increasing perceived review helpfulness and facilitating purchase decisions. Hence, we propose

H1. Structural reviewing leads to higher perceived review helpfulnes and purchase intention than freestyle reviewing.

## 2.5. The mediation of perceived processing fluency

We further postulate processing fluency [48] as the psychological mechanism accounting for the positive effect of structural (versus free style) reviewing on perceived review helpfulness. Processing fluency refers to subjective feelings of ease or difficulty experienced when pro cessing information about an object [49]. Processing information can activate an experience or feeling of fluency that reflects how difficult or easy an individual feels [50]. High fluency provokes a positive affective reaction [51] and has a fundamental influence on consumers’ judgments [48,51,52]. For instance, if consumers read product information more fluently, they tend to evaluate the product more favorably [53].

Extant literature has consistently revealed the favorable effects of processing fluency in various contexts. For instance, stocks with more fluent names outperform those with disfluent names [54]. Similarly, products with fluent names are more likely to be favored by customers e. g., [55], and highly fluent statements are judged more likely to be true [56]. In the context of online reviews, reviewers written in those easy-to-read fonts are judged as more credible and exert greater impact [57]. Likewise, reviews with single-meaning emoji are considered more helpful through increasing processing fluency [58]. Collectively, it is reasonable to conclude that consumers’ perceived processing fluency of a review may influence its perceived helpfulness and customers’ pur chase intention.

Structural reviewing, by explicitly partitioning evaluations into several attributes, allows consumers to easily extract and evaluate attribute-relevant information. Since product evaluation often involves multiple dimensions [59], consumers generally construct their prefer ence for a product based on its performance in some attributes and the importance they attach to them [26]. Compared to freestyle reviews, structural reviews engender more fluent information processing by providing attribute-related information in a well-organized way. Moreover, as humans are “cognitive misers” with limited cognitive ca pacity in the information process [29], structural (versus freestyle) re views reduce the cognitive overload for consumers performing a cognitively demanding evaluation task. By facilitating the matching between attributes explicitly provided in the review and consumer preferences, structural review helps consumers to make a more informed purchase [60]. Taken together, structural reviewing enhances process ing fluency, which in turn increases perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention. Thus,

H2. The enhanced feeling of perceived processing fluency mediates the positive effect of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing on con sumers’ perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention.

## 2.6. The moderation of utilitarian versus hedonic products

Next, we wonder whether the aforementioned effects of structural versus freestyle reviewing on review helpfulness differ between utili tarian and hedonic products. Utilitarian products are generally cogni tive, instrumental, and functional, with consumption arising from necessity (e.g., microwaves), whereas hedonic products are primarily affective and sensory, consumed for pleasure, fun, and excitement (e.g., designer clothes) [61,62].

Consumers may adopt holistic (versus piecemeal) judgment strate gies when evaluating hedonic (versus utilitarian) products. Since eval uations of a hedonic product tend to depict the enjoyability, excitement, and fun attributed to it [63], they are generally temporally and thematically related. In this case, consumers may think about the rela. tionship between each behavioral event and the previous or subsequent ones [64]. Accordingly, they are expected to form an impression of the reviewed product as a whole e.g., [65]. That ${ \mathrm { i } } s ,$ consumers may base their evaluation on the review as a whole without considering the im plications of any particular attribute description in isolation. In contrast, reviewers tend to evaluate utilitarian products predominantly on char acteristics such as functionality, usefulness, or practicality [63], for which temporal or thematic relatedness is not apparent cf., [66]. Thus, consumers may derive an evaluation of the reviewed product by combining the evaluative implications of the individual attributes mechanistically. In other words, they may perform a piecemeal inte gration of the evaluative implications of each attribute [67].

Given that the nature and evaluation of utilitarian products align more with structural reviews than those of hedonic products, we pro pose that structural reviewing enhances consumers’ processing fluency to a greater extent and leads to higher review helpfulness and purchase intention for utilitarian products than for hedonic products. Structural reviewing explicitly demonstrates reviewers’ evaluation on a product’s key attributes, which enables consumers to integrate and extract the functional information they are concerned without expending much cognitive effort. The congruence between structural reviewing and customers’ cognitive piecemeal judgment strategy for utilitarian prod ucts largely reduces consumers’ cognitive efforts in processing and extracting attribute information, contributing to preference construc tion. In contrast, the experiential nature of hedonic products compel consumers to adopt a holistic judgment approach rather than focusing on specific evaluations along a few attributes, which limits the benefits of process fluency offered by structural reviews. Thus, we contend that the positive effects of structural reviewing are more salient for utilitarian (versus hedonic) products through a more enhanced processing fluency. That is,

H3. Structural reviewing enhances review helpfulness and purchase intention to a greater extent for utilitarian products than for hedonic products, as mediated by processing fluency.

## 2.7. The moderation of consumer mindsets

We are also curious about whether prospective consumers’ mindsets (an implementational versus deliberative mindset) qualify the effect of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing on review helpfulness. Along the customer journey, customers move dynamically and interactively from the pre-purchase stage to the purchase stage, to the post-purchase stage, with customer priorities varying at each stage [68]. Mindset theory ar gues that, in the pre-decisional phase, individuals face a decision about which goal to pursue and are expected to adopt a deliberative mindset [69], which orients individuals to be relatively open-minded, critical, and realistic in available information to achieve certainty, and thus arrive at a good decision e.g., [70]. Once individuals have chosen to pursue particular goals, they proceed to the post-decisional phase (e.g., deciding to buy) and contemplate implementing the decision. This re sults in an implementational mindset [69,71], which leads individuals to pay more attention to implementation-related information [71].

Individuals with different mindsets attend to different information [70]. According to the mindset literature e.g., [72–74], individuals in a deliberative mindset are usually more open-minded to any sources or types of information available. In contrast, those in an implementational mindset tend to be closed-minded in information processing and filter information selectively by focusing on more goal-related information and ignoring goal-irrelevant information [72,75]. Such differences are caused by wider (narrower) visual attention for the deliberative (implementational) mindset [70].

When consumers construct preferences by reading online reviews, this mindset difference matters. Structural reviews organize the con sumption evaluations on different attributes and filter other attributeirrelevant information, which demands a relatively narrow breadth of attention. Consumers in an implementational mindset, who adopt a narrow focus and prioritize goal-relevant attributes and disregard those unrelated [70,72], are more likely to favor structural reviews that match their mindset cf., [76]. This mindset-review match arouses a sensation of rightness and leads to positive behavioral outcomes [77,78]. Such a matching effect is often the cause of processing fluency e.g., [48,79]. Therefore, consumers in the implementational mindset process struc tural reviews much more fluently than freestyle reviews, which induces a sense of ease in metacognition e.g., [51], thereby enhancing review helpfulness and purchase intention.

In contrast, the positive effect of structural reviews may be attenu ated for consumers in a deliberative mindset, who possess a wide breadth of attention and are responsive to incidental $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ taskirrelevant) information. Such consumers may tend to process the in formation without any filtering [69,80–82], rendering them less likely to rely on structural review to construct preferences. Thus, we propose that

H4. The implementational mindset strengthens, but the deliberative mindset weakens, the positive effects of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing, as mediated by perceived processing fluency.

## 2.8. The moderation of consumer expertise

We explore consumer expertise as another contingency that qualifie the effect of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing on review helpful ness. Consumer expertise reflects the extensiveness and depth of domain-specific knowledge acquired through prior learning and expe rience [83]. Such cognitive structure facilitates consumers to adopt an analytical approach to understand a product domain, decomposing products into different parts and discriminating them based on diag nostic characteristics [47]. The more knowledge consumers have in a domain, the more likely they are to apply these structures to understand and assess the quality of a product [84].

For consumers with high expertise, internal cognitive structures outweigh the external cues indicated by structural review texts. Their knowledge base and analytic processing allow them to efficiently pro cess freestyle review information, thereby reducing the informationprocessing benefits that structural reviews can provide. The experi enced customers already possess a set of criteria for decomposing and evaluating products, so the external structure from structural reviews exerts relatively little additional influence on their information process. Consequently, the benefits of structural reviewing on review helpfulness and purchase intention via enhanced processing fluency are lower for the experienced consumers.

Conversely, consumers with less experience lack such internal cognitive structures and are not competent in extracting information from freestyle review texts to construct their preferences efficiently. For them, structural reviews provide an external framework, which com pensates for their cognitive shortcomings. By displaying the information in a clear and partitioned manner, structural review greatly reduces cognitive overload and enhances information processing fluency. This ease of processing facilitates novice customers to better construct pref erences and to perceive the structural review as more helpful when making purchase decisions. Thus,

H5. The positive effects of structural (vs. freestyle) reviewing, through enhanced processing fluency, are stronger for novice consumers than experienced consumers.

## 2.9. Overview of the studies

Fig. 3 depicts the theoretical research model for this article. We examine the proposed hypotheses in one field study and two behavioral experiments. Specifically, in Study 1, we conduct a field investigation on JD.com to verify whether different reviewing formats (structural versus freestyle reviewing) exert a significant effect on review helpfulness votes and whether this effect is moderated by hedonic versus utilitarian product types, providing initial evidence for H1 and H3. In Study 2, we manipulate the structural versus freestyle reviews for the same product with varying (hedonic versus utilitarian) consumption goals. We show that structural reviewing leads to a higher perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention for the utilitarian products than for hedonic ones. We further find that the perceived processing fluency mediates the aforementioned effects, ruling out the enhanced information recall as the alternative explanation. Overall, Study 2 supports H1, H2, and H3. In Study $^ { 3 , }$ we aim to examine the moderating roles of consumers mindset (implementational versus deliberative) and their expertise on the effect of structural reviewing on review helpfulness and purchase intention through processing fluency, verifying H4 and H5.

## 3. Study 1: field investigation on JD.com

Study 1 uses large-scale field data crawled from JD.com, which is the largest online retailing platform (with a yearly revenue of 160 billion USD in 2024) in China. It investigates whether the different reviewing formats (structural versus freestyle reviewing) affect consumers perceived review helpfulness in a real-world setting. Furthermore, we examine the moderating effect of product type that the advantage of structural reviews over freestyle reviews is more pronounced for utili tarian products than for hedonic products.

## 3.1. Data collection and sample description

We selected JD.com as the data source for our field investigation because it is popular among Chinese consumers and has demonstrated the coexistence of structural reviews and freestyle reviews for the ma jority of products. When consumers decide to post a review for a prod uct, they usually leave a rating on the five-star scale and submit a text review below. As shown in Fig. 4, the text area indicates either “Activate the soft keyboard to add tags to facilitate you to generate more helpful reviews” (4A) or “How do you feel? Share with everyone” (4B). In Fig. 4A, when consumers try to input in the texting area, several tags indicating different product attributes or dimensions are prompted to them automatically. It is up to consumers whether to choose some, all, or none of the tags to compose their text reviews. However, in Fig. 4B, no tag is provided to consumers when texting. After reviewing, consumers may upload some pictures or videos of the product. Prospective con sumers can vote for these reviews on JD.com for their helpfulness in informing their decisions. The number of cumulative helpfulness votes is displaved at the bottom of each review.

To serve our research purposes, we selected a wide range of product categories that are popular on JD.com. As defined, hedonic products are generally evaluated based on characteristics such as enjoyability and excitement and are purchased out of desire, whereas utilitarian products are primarily evaluated based on characteristics such as functionality and practicality and are purchased out of necessity $[ 3 7 , 6 1 , 6 2 ]$ . We selected 14 categories of hedonic products (e.g., dolls, necklaces, gui tars) and 14 categories of utilitarian products (e.g., air conditioners, suitcases, mice). For each category, we chose 20 different products, which resulted in a sample of 560 products. We retrieved the most upfront 30-page reviews for each product and got a large-scale sample of 158,650 reviews posted between 2010 and 2022. Finally, our dataset recorded each review’s rating, helpfulness votes, text content, review date, etc.

LIWC captures the cognitivity, which describes the depth and complexity of thinking, by various categories of words including causal words $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . } } \boldsymbol { \mathrm { g . } } ,$ , because, effect, hence), insight words (e.g., think, know, consider), and filler words (e.g., I mean) [85].

Since reviewing contiguity shapes review helpfulness [39], we included it as a covariate, measured by the time difference between the review date and the purchase date $( M = 1 2 . 1 4 , S D = 1 5 . 2 9 )$ . Given that mobile reviewing also influences review helpfulness significantly [31, 40], we controlled for different reviewing channels via either a mobile device (79.35%, 125, 895 reviews) or a PC device. Besides the ratings and texts of reviews, JD.com allows its consumers to upload images as supplementary information; therefore, we controlled for the number of images $( M { = } 1 . 5 6 , S D { = } 1 . 7 1 )$ . We also considered the sequence of a re view due to the declining sequential dynamic of online ratings revealed by WOM literature e.g., [36,89]. Since JD.com displays ten reviews per page, we included the review page and the position on each page for each review. Meanwhile, because it takes time for a review to accu mulate helpfulness votes, we accounted for review age as well, which is measured by the time elapsed since each review was posted (M= 373.81 days, SD= 382.93). Also, the number of product attributes mentioned in either structural or freestyle reviews may shape review informativeness and thus impact helpfulness, so we text-mined the number of attributes by a natural language processing approach and included it as a covariate<sup>1</sup> (M= 3.12, SD= 1.68). Additionally, we added dummies to indicate whether the reviewed product was purchased during the major shopping carnivals (e.g., Carnival 618 or Carnival 1111). Besides the product fixed effects, we also included month dummies to control for the time trend and seasonality of the product consumption. ## 3.3. Estimation results

Since the reviewers decided whether to compose a structural review using the suggested tags or to write a free style review, these reviews may differ systematically in review content, such as., review length and the number of attributes, which in turn affect review quality and help fulness votes. To address this selection bias issue, we adopted the Heckman two-stage procedure. In the first stage, we predicted the pos sibility of a review being structural or free style using the review length and number of attributes, and used the average proportion of structural reviews for the same product category but excluding the current reviewed product till the reviewing date as a variable. The idea was that the prevailing norm in a category might influence a reviewer’s choice to use structural format, but it might not directly affect the helpfulness of a particular review. The first-stage regression results confirm that three variables, review length $( 0 . 0 1 3 , p < . 0 0 1 )$ , the number of attributes $( - 0 . 2 3 2 , p < . 0 0 1 )$ , and the average proportion of structural reviews within the same product category $( 0 . 0 6 7 , p = . 0 6 1 )$ , are predictive of a reviewer’s decision to adopt a structural or freestyle reviewing format, with a significant likelihood-ratio $\chi < ^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize { \sigma u } } } > ^ { 2 } < / ^ { \sigma \mathrm { { u } } \pi } >$ (LR chi2) of 7641.55 $( p < . 0 0 1 )$ ). These demonstrate strong relevance for the variables. Based on the estimation results, we obtained the inverse Mills ratio (IMR) and included it as an additional regressor in the second stage to correct the self-selection bias and predict the helpfulness votes. Since a small number of customer reviews (N= 8220, 5.18%) had both structural and freestyle parts, we removed these reviews and implemented the Heck man procedure. Given that our dependent variable, Review helpfulness , is a count variable with its variance (209.08) greater than its mean (0.79), we adopted negative binomial regression with product fixed ef fects (xtnbreg) in Stata 17.0 for model estimations e.g., [31,90], whose results are demonstrated in Models 1 and 2 of Table 2. The coefficients of IMR are statistically significant (-1.195, p= .170; -1.188, p < .173), justifying that the selection bias might not threaten the validity of our empirical findings.

## Robustness checks

One may argue that instead of the dichotomous classification of re views as either structural or freestyle, these different review pre sentations can be gauged in a more granular measurement of structuredness: freestyle review format represents a zero level of struc turedness, whereas a structural review format with more pre-specified attribute dimensions denotes a higher level of structuredness than one with fewer pre-specified attribute dimensions.<sup>2</sup> Thus, we conduct a robustness check examining the impact of the level of structuredness (i. e., varying the number of attributes) on review helpfulness. The esti mation results in Models 3 and 4 in Table 2 show robust findings that the higher the structuredness, the more helpful a review is, and that this effect is stronger for utilitarian (vs. hedonic) products. These findings extend beyond the general superiority of multi-dimensional ratings over overall ratings identified by the current literature [25,27,28] by showing that the granularity of multi-dimensional ratings and reviews matters as well.

## 3.4. Discussion

Using a large-scale, multi-category sample of real-world data, Study 1 reveals that the structural reviews are associated positively with more helpfulness votes and this positive effect is greater for utilitarian prod ucts than for hedonic products. Overall, these findings provide initial empirical evidence for H1 and H3. However, two limitations of Study 1 should be acknowledged. First, we distinguished different reviewing formats by identifying reviews with at least one tag as the structural ones and those without as the freestyle ones. Due to the large sample size, human coders cannot tell the difference when a consumer wrote a re view in the freestyle format but elaborated the review according to several attributes sequentially. Second, the field investigation is not able to uncover the underlying mechanism for the observed effects of struc tural reviewing on review helpfulness. Next, we conduct a more controlled behavioral experiment in Study 2, aiming to address the aforementioned limitations and generalize these findings in the hotel booking context.

## 4. Study 2: behavioral experiment in hotel booking

Study 1 reveals that structural reviews are generally deemed to be more helpful than freestyle reviews and are so to a greater extent for utilitarian products than for hedonic products. Study 2 designs a scenario-based experiment to address Study 1′s limitations and serve the following purposes. First, it replicates Study 1′s findings in a more controlled experimental setting. It exposes the participants to either a structural review or a freestyle review while keeping the review content consistent. Second, since the consumption of many products can serve both utilitarian and hedonic goals for consumers [91], we adopt the same service (hotel accommodation) as the context and manipulate different consumption goals to avoid the effect of unobserved product heterogeneity in consumer perceptions. Therefore, by following Moore [37], Study 2 manipulates product types by assigning the hedonic versus utilitarian consumption goals. In line with the manipulation by Rocklage and Fazio [5], we instructed the participants to imagine that they were going to choose a hotel either for a conference (utilitarian) or a vacation (hedonic). Third, this study aims to reveal the perceived processing fluency as the underlying mechanism for the effectiveness of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing on review helpfulness. We predict that the structural reviews for utilitarian consumption result in a greater enhancement of processing fluency than for hedonic consumption, which in turn leads to higher perceived review helpfulness. Last, since several key attributes are explicitly highlighted in the headings of structural reviews, structural reviewing may increase review helpfulness perception and purchase intention by enhanced information recall. We aim to rule out this alternative explanation.

## 4.1. Method

We conducted a 2 (reviewing format: structural or $\mathrm { f r e e s t y l e } ) \times 2$ (product type: hedonic or utilitarian) between-subject experiment. We recruited participants from the United States via Prolific, an online panel popular among business researchers e.g., [92], and randomly assigned them to one of the four conditions. We inserted one screening question, “Are you familiar with TripAdvisor? Please select ‘a bit unfamiliar directly,” after the manipulation check questions. We obtained 322 participants $( M _ { \mathrm { A g e } } = 3 6 ;$ 58.01% female) after removing 15 participants who failed to pass the screening question.

Procedure. Participants were exposed to a scenario in which they were planning to go to San Francisco for a business conference (utili tarian-goal condition) or a relaxing vacation (hedonic-goal condition) and needed to book a hotel for the trip. They were subsequently instructed to imagine browsing the available hotels on a popular travel platform. They were then exposed to a hotel image with the fictitious name “Sunrise Hotel,” some primary information $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm { e . g . } }$ , the location, price, average rating, and total number of customer reviews), and a text description. They were then asked to read the hotel review carefully before making a reservation decision.

We adopted a four-star hotel review from TripAdvisor.com and modified the review text to fit our purpose (see the detailed experi mental materials in Fig. A.1 in the Appendix). Under the hedonic con dition, the review described the hotel for vacation purposes, such as the geographic proximity to the popular scenic spots and in-room WiFi for entertainment. Under the utilitarian condition, the review evaluated the hotel for the conference purpose in terms of geographic proximity to the conference venue and in-room WiFi for work. Next, we manipulated the different reviewing formats. For the freestyle format, the review was a freestyle by which the reviewer was telling a story about his/her expe rience with the hotel [20,93]. For the structural format, the review assessed the hotel according to several attributes (location, service, rooms, and amenities and facilities).

After reading the review, the participants were asked to report on the perceived processing fluency $( M = 3 . 8 3 , S D = 1 . 0 7 )$ , review helpfulness $( M { = } 3 . 4 9 , S D { = } 1 . 1 6 )$ , and purchase intention $( M { = } 3 . 6 0 , \mathrm { S D } { = } 1 . 1 1 )$ on a five-point Likert scale. We gauged the perceived processing fluency by a five-item scale adapted from Graf, Mayer, and Landwehr [94] (The process of reading the review is easy/- clear/fluent/effortless/comprehensible [α= 0.94]). We measured the perceived review helpfulness by a three-item scale adapted from Lei, Yin, and Zhang [14] (This review is helpful/useful/informative [α= 0.92]). Purchase intention was gauged by a three-item scale adapted from Lei, Yin, Mitra, and Zhang [95] (Suppose you have no budget constraint on the hotel booking. Based on the hotel information and review, how likely is it that you would book this Sunrise Hotel; how likely is it that you would consider this Sunrise Hotel; and how likely is it that this Sunrise Hotel would be a good choice for you? [α= 0.91]). For the manipulation check of different consumption goals, we followed Kivetz and Zheng [96] to provide participants with definitions of both goals and asked them to indicate whether their booking was hedonic or utilitarian. Finally, they completed several questions for demographic information before ending the survey.

## 4.3. Ruling out the alternative explanation of enhanced information recall

Due to the positive impact of salience on consumers’ information recall [47], one might argue that several key attributes are explicitly highlighted in the headings of structural reviews, thus increasing review helpfulness perception and purchase intention by enhanced information recall. To rule out this alternative explanation, we also measured the participants’ information recall by a five-point Likert item adopted from Kim, Ding, Wang, and Lu [92] (To what extent do you agree that the review of the hotel shown were memorable?) after the screening ques tion. A two-way ANOVA demonstrated a nonsignificant main effect of reviewing format $( F \left( 1 , 3 1 8 \right) = 2 . 6 1 , p = . 1 1 )$ , a nonsignificant main ef fect of consumption goal $( F ( 1 , 3 1 8 ) = 2 . 1 4 , p = . 1 4 )$ , and a nonsignificant interaction effect $( F \left( 1 , 3 1 8 \right) = 1 . 9 1 , p = . 1 7 )$ . Consumers exposed to a structural or freestyle review did not show significant difference in in formation recall of the review. Thus, we have ruled out the possibility that the positive effects of structural reviews can be explained by enhanced information recall.

## 4.4. Discussion

The current experiment, in which we manipulated different reviewing formats and product types, replicated the effects of structural versus freestyle reviewing under a well-controlled condition. We further verified that the structural reviews led to greater review helpfulness and purchase intention regardless of product type. Furthermore, the moderated mediation analysis supported our proposed mechanism that the effect was attributed to enhanced perceived processing fluency and moderated by product types. That is, structural reviewing led to higher perceived processing fluency, which in turn increased the perceived review helpfulness and purchase intention. Meanwhile, this effect was stronger for utilitarian products than for hedonic products. Additionally, we ruled out the other explanation, better information recall, for the advantage of structural reviewing.

## 5. Study 3: behavioral experiment in digital camera purchase

Studies 1 and 2 provided converging evidence on the effect of reviewing formats moderated by product types; however, they seldom considered consumer factors. Study 3 aims to examine whether con sumer factors-particularly mindset and expertise-qualify the effects of structural versus freestyle reviewing. Consumers hold different mindset along the customer journey [68], a deliberative mindset at the pre-purchase stage and an implementational mindset at the purchase stage, so that consumers attend to different information [70] and demonstrate different information processing preferences [73]. Simi larly, customers with varying expertise possess different cognitive structures for processing product-related information. Thus, Study 3 investigates how both contingencies moderate the effect of structural (versus freestyle) reviews. Specifically, we first measure consumer expertise and then experimentally induce the participants to be in either the implementational mindset or the deliberative mindset. We predict that the effectiveness of structural reviews will be stronger for con sumers in an implementational (versus deliberative) mindset and for those with less (versus higher) expertise. Moreover, since Studies 1 and 2 find that structural reviews primarily benefit hedonic (versus utilitarian) products by enhancing processing fluency and review helpfulness, Study 3 uses a hedonic consumption (digital camera purchase) as the research context.

## 5.1. Method

Participants were recruited via Prolific and randomly assigned to one of four conditions in a 2 (reviewing format: structural or $\mathrm { f r e e s t y l e } ) \times 2$ (consumer mindset: implementational or deliberative) between-subject experiment. Twelve participants who did not pass the screening ques tion, similarly to Study 2 (after the manipulation check questions), were excluded, leaving a final sample of 320 participants (M<sub>Age</sub>= 39; 54.79% female).

Procedure. We measured consumers’ expertise in evaluating digital cameras by the two-item scales from Rocklage, Rucker, and Nordgren [84]: “Compared to an expert, how much expertise do you have in evaluating digital cameras?” (1= no expertise at all; 5= a great deal of expertise) and, “Compared to an expert, how would you rate your knowledge in evaluating digital cameras?” (1= not at all knowledge able; 5= very knowledgeable).

The participants were then informed that the survey included two unrelated parts. The first part served to induce either an implementa tional or deliberative mindset in participants by following the procedure of Taylor, Shelley, Gollwitzer, Peter, and M [82]. Under the imple mentational mindset condition, participants were required to focus on how they would plan an overnight camping with friends for the coming weekend and list the important steps to realize it. Under the deliberative mindset condition, participants were instead asked to deliberate on a camping plan with friends and provide a description of the positive and negative consequences. Also, they had to indicate their estimated probability of each consequence as a percentage on a bar. For the manipulation check, participants were asked to indicate their action tendency on a 5-point Likert scale with three items consisting of “I feel determined at the moment concerning the decision at hand,” “I feel committed to a certain course of action,” and “I feel prepared to use specific occasions or opportunities to $\mathrm { a c t ^ { * } } , \mathrm { e } . 8 { \cdot } , [ 7 0 , 7 1 ]$

In the second part, participants read a scenario in which they were going to buy a digital camera for an upcoming camping tour and thus browsed various digital cameras on a popular E-commerce platform. They were subsequently exposed to a camera illustrated by an image and some fundamental details (the screen, zoom, lens, price, average rating, and the total number of customer reviews, etc.). They were then instructed to read a review of the camera carefully before making a purchase decision.

We adopted a five-star digital camera review from Amazon.com and modified the review text according to our context. We manipulated the freestyle and structural reviews (see the experimental materials in Fig. A.2 in the Appendix). The freestyle review included a story about the reviewer’s evaluation of the digital cameras. ## 5.3. Discussion

The present study explored two boundary conditions for the positive effects of structural (versus freestyle) reviewing on review helpfulness and purchase intention. We confirmed that structural reviews were generally more helpful than freestyle reviews. Moreover, the imple mentational mindset magnified, but the deliberative mindset attenu ated, this effect. Similarly, the positive effects of structural (versu freestyle) reviewing, through enhanced processing fluency, were stronger for novice consumers than for experienced consumers. The moderated mediation analyses supported the conclusion that the struc tural reviews facilitated participants in the implementational mindset or with lower expertise engaging in more fluent information processing and, in turn, enhanced their review helpfulness perception and purchase intention. The findings enriched the managerial implications of WOM marketing along the customer decision journey.

We provide threefold contributions to pertinent literature. First, we are among the first to investigate the impact of structural reviewing, which underpins the proactive role of platforms or firms in promoting informative and helpful reviews. Regarding the determinants of review helpfulness, devising reviewing interfaces for users is more controllable by firms or platforms than the content features of review text e.g., [5,17, 37] or reviewer characteristics e.g., [14,98]. Second, we have uncovered that enhanced processing fluency is the underlying mecha nism that explains the higher helpfulness of structural reviews and finally higher purchase intention in comparison to freestyle reviews. ## 6.2. Managerial implications

Our research findings offer actionable insights for digital platforms and marketers aiming to optimize the design of online review systems. While prior strategies have largely focused on incentivizing review generation, our study highlights the critical importance of review format design-specifically, the shift from freestyle to structural reviewing-in shaping downstream consumer behavior. First, our research advocates the adoption of structural reviewing to improve the perceived helpful ness of online reviews and meanwhile uncovers the corresponding mechanism. Given that facilitating consumer decision making with more helpful reviews can ease consumers’ tension caused by information overloading [99,100], firms and platforms have been searching for ways to help consumers create more helpful online reviews, such as allowing reviewers to disclose their identity-descriptive information (e.g., Amazon.com [3]), revealing temporal proximity between consumption and reviewing (e.g., Bestbuy.com [39]), instructing reviewers to indi cate the “pros” and “cons” and even summary tags in the reviews (e.g., Booking.com [24]), or adopting a multidimensional rating system for them to rate on different attributes (e.g., TripAdvisor [25]). We advise platforms to adopt structural reviewing interfaces strategically to drive conversion outcomes in addition to enhanced review helpfulness. Therefore, platforms currently relying on unstructured text boxes should consider implementing attribute-specific prompts to unlock this dual benefit of review helpfulness and sales conversion.

Second, the moderating effect of product types denotes that the positive effect of structural reviewing is more salient for utilitarian products (versus hedonic products). This implies that firms selling util itarian products are strongly advised to take advantage of structural reviewing for their consumers, as it elevates consumer processing fluency to a greater extent. Nevertheless, the application of structural reviewing is still far from sufficient. Only a limited number of popular digital platforms adopt structural reviewing for their users (e.g., JD.com, taobao.com, beeradvocate.com, shein.com). Furthermore, firms on platforms with structural reviewing have not realized their potential value. For instance, in our field data on JD.com, only 16.44% of utili tarian product reviews are structural reviews. Our findings urge plat forms and firms to promote the adoption of structural reviewing, especially for utilitarian products.

Third, our research also offers practical guidelines for firms to access target consumers more effectively by considering both their mindsets in the customer journal and their expertise. The consumer journey can be divided into three stages: pre-purchase, purchase, and post-purchase, which encompass actions from need recognition to purchase comple tion [68,101]. With the rise of omnichannel marketing, consumer touchpoints along the consumer journey have become more diverse, allowing companies to tailor their online reviews to better serve con sumers. For example, consumers in the pre-purchase stage tend to possess a deliberative mindset, considering their needs/goals/impulses and how to satisfy them e.g.,[102]. In this stage, it is less necessary to prioritize structural reviews. Conversely, consumers in the purchase stage are more likely to adopt an implementational mindset, focusing on decision making [71]. As a result, companies can prioritize structural reviews for them, which enhances processing fluency and purchase de cision confidence. At the same time, companies should recognize dif ferences in consumer expertise. Novice consumers, who lack extensive product knowledge, benefit more from structured reviews. Experienced consumers, however, already possess rich cognitive schemas [88] and are less dependent on review structure to form judgments. Accordingly, firms can prioritize displaying structural reviews to novice consumers. By aligning review format with both customer mindset and expertise, companies can serve the prospective consumers better and improve the effectiveness of their WOM marketing, thereby significantly driving prospective consumers’ purchase intentions.

## 6.3. Limitations and future research

While the current research offers a deep understanding of the impact of structural reviewing on consumer perceptions of online reviews, it is undeniable that it has several limitations that offer avenues for future research. First, our results show that structural reviews are perceived as more helpful than freestyle reviews. However, existing research has shown that online reviews with high narrativity are more engaging and persuasive [20]. Narrative reviews are usually generated by freestyle reviewing. Future research is encouraged to consider more fine-grained types of freestyle reviewing. Furthermore, although we focus on how structural reviewing enhances review helpfulness and purchase inten tion by enhancing processing fluency for a single product, scenarios involving cross-product evaluations and comparisons are common. Accordingly, structural reviewing enables consumers to identify trade-offs more easily and make informed choices by improving infor mation comparisons among different products rather than processing fluency. 3

Second, we divided various products into hedonic or utilitarian products based on their functions for most people for the data collection in our field study. However, hedonism and utilitarianism do not exist as opposing ends of a one-dimensional scale [63]. Although hedonic (utilitarian) consumption is prone to be seen as relatively more discre tionary (necessary) in nature, a product (e.g., microwave) can be discretionary to some while being necessary to others [103]. Thus, the classification of products in our field study may not be accurate, as the reviewed products may be consumed out of either necessity or desire. To eliminate this concern, we manipulated hedonic and utilitarian con sumption in Study 2 in a well-controlled condition, which replicated the results of the field study.

Third, although our research considers the downstream effect of structural reviewing (i.e., readers of structural reviews), it may affect reviewers’ writing process and the content they generate (e.g., posi tiveness, objectivity, specificity, review depth, and breadth). For instance, structural reviewing usually provides several attributes of the products as a reference, which gives hints to consumers on how to evaluate a product. Also, whether the total number and different con figurations of the pre-specified attributes in the structural reviewing, which we controlled for in both the field study and two experiments, play a significant role in review helpfulness deserves future studies. Researchers are encouraged to examine whether and how structural reviewing affects the enjoyment of review writing or the content fea tures of structural reviews.
