# Applications development by end-users: can quality be improved?

## Abstract

End-users frequently develop applications to use in their jobs but there is reason to be concerned about the quality of these applications. High error rates have been found in applications developed by end-users in the few empirical studies done. This paper examines one approach to improving application quality which is to provide end-users with training in systems analysis and design methods. The study was conducted via the Internet with real end-users. Though no significant difference was found for some measures of quality, there was a significant improvement in the overall design of an application after an end-user received analysis and design training. This is important information for business managers because a well designed application is more likely to remain accurate and useful over time. q 2000 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: End-user computing; End-user development; Application quality; Spreadsheet design

## 1. Introduction

Many people today know enough about computers and software that they don’t just passively use programs set up for them by MIS professionals; they create their own applications to help them do their job. The term ‘‘end-user computing’’ EUC refersŽ . to people developing software applications for themselves or for others even though they are not trained

MIS professionals. Examples of EUC are when an administrative assistant creates a spreadsheet to calculate and graph variances between budget and actual performance numbers and a manager creates a small database to track projects and employee assignments.

Since the mid-1980s, EUC has become an increasingly common practice in business, for several reasons. First, the advances in information technology have made microcomputer hardware relatively cheap but quite powerful. Second, software has become more user-friendly while offering a wider range of capabilities — giving end-users the ability to perform analyses with spreadsheets, write reports complete with automatically updated graphs and tables, to manage work schedules, professional contacts, and so on. Finally, there is the advantage of acting independently, thereby avoiding communication problems and delays when dealing with the MIS department. Early in the use of computers, end-users found that by developing applications for themselves they could eliminate the problem of explaining to someone else what is needed and they could skip the delay of making a formal request to the MIS department. End-users recognize they may be less efficient in developing their own systems but they feel they save time overall by avoiding a queue for assistance from the MIS department 34 . Therefore, the ad- <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> vances in hardware and software and the freedom to work independently of the MIS department have helped make EUC a common occurrence in most businesses 15,17,19,23,24,33,34 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

There are reasons to be concerned about EUC, however. The lack of quality control, the lack of standards, and the lack of adherence to standards, when they exist, are frequently cited problems <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> <sup>w x</sup> 10,14,19 . Klepper and Sumner 21 found that endusers who develop their own applications were largely unaware of formal development procedures to ensure the integrity of an information system–procedures such as data validation, data security, testing, and documentation. In the late 1980s, Davis 10<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> warned about the risk of using applications that have not been thoroughly checked for correctness and which may not be consistently maintained.

The few empirical studies that looked at EUC applications have found alarmingly high errors rates. For example, Brown and Gould 5 found that 44%<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> of the spreadsheets developed by experienced users had at least one substantive error even though the user<sup>r</sup>developers were confident their spreadsheets were correct. Panko and Sprague 29 report error <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> rates between 10% and 25% in real-world spreadsheet audits. These findings should be a red flag to managers because using spreadsheets is the leading EUC activity 24 . Apart from spreadsheets, Edberg<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> and Bowman 13 also found more errors in database<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> applications created by M.B.A. students than database applications created by senior MIS students.

To help ensure the quality and success of applications they develop, MIS professionals receive training in formal systems development methods. In contrast, end-users typically have no training in analysis and design methods and this may be one reason for the high errors rates in EUC applications. Some researchers have suggested that such training could help alleviate the problems associated with EUC <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 10,20,26,27,37,38 and that is the focus of the current study.

To determine whether the quality of end-user developed applications can be improved, this study examines whether training end-users in systems analysis and design methods will impact the quality of their applications. The research question is: Do endusers develop higher quality applications when they have received systems analysis and design training? There are important implications for businesses whether or not the proposition is supported that training end-users in systems development methods will improve the quality of their applications. If end-users receive systems development training but do not improve the quality of their applications, then managers will want to consider the critical nature of certain applications and whether certain applications should only be handled by IS professionals. If the quality of applications does improve, then businesses will know that training is one way to help ensure end-users develop high quality, useful applications.

## 2.2. End-user

The end-user of interest is that person who adopts and uses information technology to develop applications 3,4,9,35,38 . The characteristics of an end-user,<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> such as work experience and software skills, might come into play when an application is developed <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 28,34,36,38 . Therefore, the current study measures three end-user characteristics to control for their possible affect on the EUC outcomes. These measures are EUC activities, spreadsheet knowledge, and current development skill. Demographic data is also collected.

## 2.3. Task

The task is a particular activity to be done or problem to be solved. In EUC, the task is to develop an application. The task can be characterized by its complexity, requisite domain knowledge such as theŽ need to know accounting principles , task scope, .

structuredness, and interdependence 6,22,30 . The <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> task variable in the current study is controlled. All subjects create two spreadsheets. The two spreadsheet tasks require no special domain knowledge and are relatively simple, narrow in scope, well defined, and independent of other tasks.

## 2.4. Tool

The tool is what the end-user uses to develop an application, such as the software used or the methodology applied. Two tools are important to the current study. First, the software tool is controlled because all subjects are required to use one of two well-known spreadsheet software packages. The second tool is the methodology end-users use to develop an application and this is the manipulated variable in this study. The development methodology is manipulated through training. The treatment group receives training in systems analysis and design SA&D duringŽ . the experiment while the control group receives training on a topic unrelated to SA&D.

## 2.6. EUC outcomes

This study examines two outcomes to EUC. At one level application quality is assessed through three measures: completeness, accuracy, and design. Completeness represents the extent to which an application provides all the requisite information. Accuracy is the degree to which the application is without errors. Design is the extent to which proper design elements, such as documentation, are incorporated into the application.

Another outcome of EUC is at the individual, end-user level. Most IS researchers have assessed whether an application was successful or not through some measure of user satisfaction 2,11,12,18 . End-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> user satisfaction is ‘‘the affective attitude towards a specific computer application by someone who interacts with the application directly’’ 12 . From the <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> user’s perspective, if the application does what he<sup>r</sup>she wants, then it is a high quality system 1 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> This study assesses end-user satisfaction with the Doll and Torkzadeh instrument. There has beenŽ some criticism of the satisfaction construct because of its subjectiveness 25 . However, the benefit of<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> having established satisfaction measures with known reliability and validity makes the use of these measures easy and provides a basis for comparison across studies..

The research question for this study is ‘‘Do endusers develop higher quality applications when they have received systems analysis and design training?’’ The quality of an application is measured through four variables. The research hypothesis for each variable follows:

Hypothesis 1. Applications developed by end-users with systems analysis and design training are more complete than applications developed by end-users without such training.

Hypothesis 2. Applications developed by end-users with systems analysis and design training have fewer errors than applications developed by end-users without such training.

Hypothesis 3. Applications developed by end-users with systems analysis and design training have fewer design defects than applications developed by endusers without such training.

Hypothesis 4. Application end-users will be more satisfied with those developed by end-users with systems analysis and design training than those developed by end-users without such training.

The objective quality measure of completeness is the extent to which all requirements for a specified task were met. Accuracy is measured as the extent to which output was free of substantive errors. Design is measured at the extent to which proper design elements, such a documentation, are included in the spreadsheet. The measure of end-user satisfaction is taken with the well-established Doll and Torkzadeh instrument 12 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

## 3. Research method

The experimental design is the untreated control group with pre- and post-tests 8 . Subjects in the <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> treatment and control groups complete pre-tests assessments of possible covariates and they develop a spreadsheet based on a problem scenario. Both groups receive training, but only the treatment group receives training in analysis and design methods. Both groups complete a post-test spreadsheet.

The target population is the type of end-user who develops his<sup>r</sup>her own applications or who develops applications for other people. The working population consists of volunteer subjects with full-time work experience and experience using spreadsheets. The requisite sample size for this experiment is set at 66 33 in each group . This sample size is deter-Ž . mined by the values set for the level of significance Ž . Ž . Ž . 0.05 , effect size 0.35 , and power 0.80 7 . Con-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> tact with prospective subjects is by postcard, personal request, or e-mail. As an incentive to participate, end-users who complete the study have the option of receiving a \$10 payment themselves or donating the money to a charity.

The experiment is conducted through a World Wide Web site. Subjects complete demographic and two pre-test measures through HTML forms. One pre-test measure uses the EUC activities instrument from Ref. 32 to assess the extent of end-user com-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> puting activities subjects are involved in. A second pre-test measure assesses how well subjects know spreadsheet software. This spreadsheet knowledge Ž assessment was developed over a series of pilot tests and revisions by experienced spreadsheet instructors.. The third pre-test measure has the subject create a spreadsheet based on a problem description and submit it by e-mail or ftp. This pre-test is used to measure subjects’ current skill in developing spreadsheets by assessing the quality of a sample application.

After the subjects complete the pre-test measure they are randomly assigned to one of two training groups. Subjects receive a URL and instructions for downloading a training program. The assignment to Ž a group is handled by a CGI script. The subjects are not aware they are being directed to one of two URLs for the treatment and control groups. The. training module for the treatment group covers the analysis and design steps for developing a spreadsheet. The training module for the control group covers guidelines for when to use spreadsheet or database software for a particular task.

After the training, the subjects are given the task of creating another spreadsheet which is the post-test. ŽBoth the pre- and post-tests require the subjects to create a spreadsheet for a specific scenario. The scenarios for the pre-test and post-test have similarities but the context of the task is different. For both scenarios, the task is characterized as narrow in scope, structured, and independent of other tasks.. Next, the quality of the post-test spreadsheets is assessed for accuracy number of substantive errors ,Ž . completeness inclusion of requisite data and compo- Ž nents , and proper design layout, documentation,. Ž use of cell references rather than literal values, etc..

Another post-test measure is end-user satisfaction. Volunteer students are randomly assigned spreadsheet applications created by the study subjects. The end-user<sup>r</sup>students do four things: 1 read a briefŽ . description of the problem the spreadsheet is used for; 2 use the spreadsheet to enter new data; 3Ž . Ž . answer five questions based on information provided by the spreadsheet this is to assure they complete Ž the first two steps ; and 4 complete the Doll and . Ž . ## 5. Discussion

The purpose of this study was to determine whether the quality of EUC applications could be improved by training end-users in analysis and design methods. The treatment group significantly im proved the design of their spreadsheet applications when compared to the control group. What is meant by design? A well–designed spreadsheet should include a a modular layout–separate sections orŽ . worksheets for documentation, input, and calculations, b documentation that explains what the Ž . spreadsheet is for and any underlying assumptions, and c proper use of cell references in formulas Ž . rather than literal values in formulas. The post-test spreadsheets from the treatment group included more aspects of proper design. The post-test spreadsheets from the control group show these end-users do what most end-users do — open a new spreadsheet and start creating an application ‘‘on the fly’’ without designing a layout or documenting any assumptions or the proper way to use the spreadsheet.

The results of this study do not prove accuracy and completeness were improved for the treatment group’s spreadsheets. Since both the treatment and control groups scored very high on accuracy and fairly high on completeness, there was little variability for these measures and detection of relationships between the dependent and independent variables was problematic. Two likely reasons for the high completeness and accuracy scores are the subjects knowledge of spreadsheet software and the fairly simple spreadsheets subjects were asked to create. The subjects in this study know spreadsheets well since the average score was 86% for the pre-test of spreadsheet knowledge in contrast, the average wasŽ 54% for the same test given to students in a juniorlevel MIS class . The problem scenarios used for. pre- and post-test spreadsheets may have been too simple to give experienced spreadsheet users the ‘‘opportunity’’ to make errors. On a scale of one Ž <sup>w</sup> <sup>x w</sup> <sup>x</sup> very easy to five very difficult , the subjects rated the spreadsheet scenarios an average just under two.. However, to avoid making the experiment too timeconsuming for subjects the spreadsheet scenarios were meant to be fairly simple. The scenarios were also designed so that special domain knowledge, such as accounting or finance, was not required. Certainly, further research using a somewhat more complex model would be a very useful extension of this study.

There are two other points that should be made about the accuracy of the spreadsheets in this study. First, the accuracy scores only reflect original errors, which means an error was counted once in a spreadsheet even though the error could have a cascading effect on spreadsheet output. In many cases, all the output of a spreadsheet was wrong due to one error in data input or in a single formula.

The second point about accuracy is linked to one aspect of design quality — the use of cell references rather than literal values in formulas. The use of cell references in formulas is analogous to the use of variables in programming. If a programmer enters literal values in calculations used throughout a program, then future changes in any of those values would make the accuracy of the program highly questionable. First, the programmer would have to remember that literal values were used and, second, the programmer would have to find any lines of code using a particular value that need to be changed. It is probably not surprising to anyone who has audited spreadsheets that a similar situation occurred in many of the spreadsheets subjects submitted. Subjects entered values in cells of the spreadsheet then frequently they entered the same values directly in a formula rather than typing the cell reference. In the pre-test spreadsheets, almost half 45% of theŽ . spreadsheets from both groups had literal values in the formulas. After the training, 47% of the control group’s spreadsheets had literal values in formulas but the treatment group’s percentage dropped to 29%.

The fact that many end-users created formulas with literal values has important implications for the accuracy of a spreadsheet that will be used over time. If someone opens that spreadsheet weeks later and changes a value in a cell, how will that person know that some formulas need to be changed, also? Even the creator of the spreadsheet is not likely to remember. Other users of the spreadsheet are even less likely to know formulas must be changed if certain values are changed in the spreadsheet. This problem was illustrated in the study when the researchers had students use the spreadsheets developed by the end-users. For the end-user satisfaction measure, student volunteers were given instructions to open a spreadsheet, alter some data, then interpret the output. Only a few students verified the spreadsheet output after they altered some values. Most students assumed the spreadsheet automatically adjusted to a change in an input value.

## 6. Limitations

There are limitations to this study with respect to the sample and the use of an experimental design. First, the sample consisted of volunteer end-users, not a random sample, and this limits how the results can be generalized to the end-user population 8 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Another limitation on external validity is the use of an experimental design, which has been criticized for its artificiality and its lack of bearing on real-life situations 31 . For instance, subjects’ awareness that <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> they are participating in an experiment can affect their responses. Also, the problems presented to subjects may not adequately reflect more complex real-life problems, so accuracy and completeness measures may be unrealistically high.

## 7. Summary

The purpose of this study was to see whether the quality of EUC applications could be improved by training end-users in systems analysis and design methods. A significant improvement in application design was found. There are reasons to believe a well-designed application will remain useful and accurate as it is used over time. Further research is needed to see if the results can be replicated and to see if more complex applications have an impact on quality measures such as completeness and accuracy. The practical implication of this study is that business could benefit from training end-users in analysis and design methods because end-users are likely to apply that training in improving the design of their applications.
