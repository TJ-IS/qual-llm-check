# Critical factors influencing the adoption of data warehouse technology: a study of the banking industry in Taiwan

Hsin-Ginn Hwang<sup>a,1</sup>, Cheng-Yuan Ku<sup>a,1</sup>, David C. Yen<sup>b,</sup>\*, Chi-Chung Cheng<sup>a,1</sup>

<sup>a</sup> Department of Information Management, National Chung Cheng University, Ming-Hsiung, Chiai 621, Taiwan, ROC

<sup>b</sup> Department of Decision Sciences and Management Information Systems, Miami University, Oxford, OH 45056, USA

Accepted 31 July 2002

## Abstract

Previous literature suggests that various factors play crucial roles in the adoption of an information system; however, there is little empirical research about the factors affecting adoption of data warehouse technology, particularly in a single information technology intensive industry. In this study, we used a survey to investigate the factors influencing adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry in Taiwan. A total of 50 questionnaires were mailed to CIOs in domestic banks. The response rate was 60%. Discriminant analysis was employed to test hypotheses. The results revealed that factors such as support from the top management, size of the bank, effect of champion, internal needs, and competitive pressure would affect the adoption of data warehouse technology. The results and conclusions from this study may be a good reference for global banks in these aforementioned countries to establish and develop operational strategies, which in turn will facilitate the implementation in overseas branches.

## 1. Introduction

With keener and stronger competition, enterprises are much more eager in getting immediate and accurate information to make better decisions. Furthermore, with the rapidly growing need for large amounts of information, enterprises’ traditional database is incapable of effectively handling the demands of increasing online information retrieval, access, update, and maintenance. This inability greatly impacts businesses in a way that the management level cannot utilize internal data efficiently and effectively to assist reliable decision-making in a timely manner. As a result, it is such a critical issue for every business to seek for ways and/or means to access, store, maintain, and utilize the massive data efficiently.

Businesses constantly require a database environment with high flexibility, better adaptability, and good support to make a decision. During the past years, academia and industry have continuously offered different solutions to create such an aforementioned environment. One of the possible alternatives is to adopt data warehouse technology. According to Inmon [20], data warehouse technology was described as, ‘‘Collecting data from several dispersed sources to build a central data warehouse. Then users can use appropriate data-analyzing tools to store and analyze needed data.’’ The main application of data warehouse is to process every dimension analysis in internal data by utilizing data survey technology, and it can be a reference of decision-making for enterprises. For example, online analytical processing (OLAP) capability can be a convenient and effective way to query data immediately and to provide various information using multidimensional querying to match with users’ demands.

The banking industry is categorized as one industry with high information demands. Information technology is taken as a key tool to improve the quality of service and to gain a competitive advantage. The Taiwanese banking industry has showed a great interest in adopting data warehouse technology and has started to undertake the challenges of implementing it in order to manage the existing internal and external heterogeneous database. According to the recent research of Wen et al. [40], the adoption of data warehouse technology, which requires huge capital spending and also consumes a good deal of development time, has a very high possibility of failure. Even most of the companies adopting data warehouse technology have successfully accomplished the expected goals established before adoption, and 20% of companies still fail [29]. As a result, it will be critical and essential to have a detailed understanding of critical success factors to ensure the successful adoption of data warehouse technology. Unfortunately, in the area of the adoption of data warehouse technology, most of the research available focused on the technological and operational aspects, and there is very little research to consider the factors in the managerial and strategic levels.

In addition, there is a little prior research to study the key factors affecting adoption of data warehouse technology, especially for the enterprises in Asia. Asia, including Singapore, Taiwan, and mainland China, is a fast-growing market. Many western banks have great interest in establishing Asian branches in cities such as Shanghai, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Taipei. To this end, this study, which reports valuable empirical data on the adoption of data warehouse for the banking industry in Taiwan, can be easily applied to mainland China, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Taiwan, since these are all with the same culture and speak the same language. It is obvious that the results, findings, and conclusions drawn from this study can be a good reference for global banks to establish and develop operational strategies for their eastern branches.

With a systematic examination about the existing situation and future development of data warehouse technology in the banking industry of Taiwan, this study also investigates the factors affecting the adoption of data warehouse technology. The results presented in this manuscript can certainly help those banks, which might wish to adopt data warehouse technology by overcoming potential obstacles, and hence reducing the high risk of failure during implementation. For other industries intending to adopt data warehouse technology, the results and findings from this study can be used as a case study for the adoption of data warehouse technology in the near future. Furthermore, academia can use the findings of this study as a basis to initiate other related studies in the data warehouse area.

## 2. Literature review

2.1. Definitions and characteristics of data warehouse technology

The concept of data warehouse technology was initially introduced by Devlin and Murphy [6]. They suggested the construction of a read-only database that stores historical datum for operating and offers integration tools for users to query and search what they want for decision supporting and analyzing. Inmon [19] perceives that data warehouse technology mainly includes four essential characteristics, ‘‘subject-oriented,’’ ‘‘integrated,’’ ‘‘time-variant,’’ and ‘‘non-volatile.’’ ‘‘Subject-oriented’’ attribute actually means the datum is constructed and integrated in many ways according to different topics. In a traditional system, for example, the custom datum from a POS system might be different from other sale systems. By using data warehouse technology, the custom data are isolated as a single topic no matter what system is used. Furthermore, the data will not be in consistency because of its being integrated, extracted, and/or format-converted by certain tools. Thus, data are all with integration. The characteristic of timevariant is that if the system focuses on a ‘‘real-time’’ attribute, any time variation, as a result, can be very important. It is common that the data stored in the data warehouse also contains the relevant time and interval of time information to make it convenient for later querying. As to ‘‘non-variant’’ characteristic, the data warehouse can store massive historical data by which the process of analysis, prediction, and discovery can be more effective, reliable, and accurate. Once the data are loaded into the data warehouse, any modification, however, cannot be done to ensure the perseverance of best quality.

In recent years, many researchers [10,18,22,26, 30,42] have modified and/or refined the definitions introduced by Inmon [19]. These different definitions have essentially broadened the scope of data warehouse domain but are still consistent with Inmon’s definition on essential characteristics. In summary of those various definitions, data warehouse could be defined as, ‘‘Data warehouse collects daily transaction-oriented enterprise data both internally and externally, and then accumulate, categorize, and store huge historical data for further analysis, prediction, and discovery of data pattern.’’ Those data are more related to statistics, non-modified, and stored in warehouse in a long-term manner. In addition, they are time-oriented, integrated, and can be used effectively for analyzing and decision-making.

## 3. Research methodologies

## 3.1. Research problem and objectives

Given the previous discussion, the investment on data warehouse technology is very expensive and the failure is pretty high compared with other information technologies. Consequently, this study is important for the corporations and/or businesses to understand the critical factors, which will affect the adoption and/or implementation of the data warehouse technology. Furthermore, since there is little research focusing on the managerial and strategic aspects and discussing the banking industry in Asia in general, and in Taiwan in particular, this study is timely and important to provide additional insights to complement the findings from prior research in this subject area.

The research problem of this study can be described as, ‘‘What are the important critical success factors that the banking industry in Taiwan should take into consideration before and during the adoption of data warehouse technology?’’ Furthermore, the research objectives include the following items:

1. To study the critical success factors, which will affect the success before and during the adoption of data warehouse technology.

2. To study the factors that will affect the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry in particular.

3. To study the factors that can be applied to lead to further studies of the adoption of data warehouse technology in the Asian area.

## 3.2. Research model

From the above discussion, this manuscript proposes a research model and related hypotheses about these important factors, which will affect the adoption of data warehouse technology based on the data collected from the banking industry in Taiwan. This research model includes the following three dimensions as discussed earlier: the organizational dimension, environmental dimension, and project-planning dimension as shown in Fig. 1. The relevant factors are grouped in each dimension based on the findings of prior research discussed in Section 2 as well as the results of an opinion survey, which included a group of academic and industrial practitioners from the Taiwanese banking industry. The proposed model groups all the relevant factors into three dimensions and shows all the relationships and proposed hypotheses.

## 3.3. Hypotheses and variables

## 3.3.1. Organizational dimension

For any organization, the adoption of data warehouse technology is a very expensive and complex job in which the size of the organization is an important factor to take into account. Furthermore, the difficulty in learning a new technology will result in resistance from users and will begin to surface during the course of the adoption process. As a result, it is essential to secure top management support and commitment, and encourage users’ enthusiastic participation. By taking all organizational factors into consideration during the implementation, the unnecessary obstacles and barriers will certainly be reduced. In line with the discussion in Section 2, the size of the organization, the existence of the champions, the support of top management, and the internal needs of the organization should all be included in the organizational dimension.

## H1.1. The size of the bank affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

The size of the organization is one of the important factors affecting the adoption of new information technology. The larger the size of the organization, the more resources and capital can be allocated to adopt new information technology [7,25]. Larger enterprises usually have sufficient resources to compensate the accompanied limitations of huge expenses and labor required in the adoption of data warehouse technology. This study builds the first hypothesis by assuming that the size of the bank affects the decision to adopt the data warehouse technology in the banking industry. There is, however, no consensus about the quantification of the size of the organization found in the past researches. To refer to Kimberly’s study [23], the variable ‘‘constituents’’ and ‘‘usable resources’’ in the organization were used as indicators to quantify the size of an organization in this study. ‘‘Constituents’’ represent the numbers of bank employees, and ‘‘usable resources’’ are the capital spending. In the survey to perform this study, respondents are asked to fill in the actual number of bank employees and capital spending. After receiving all the questionnaires, the authors made a comparison with the data obtained from the top 1000 enterprises listed in Taiwan’s leading magazines to minimize the discrepancy of the size of the organization. To measure the organization scale is to contrast the relative scale between banks. Calculating separately the numbers of bank employees and capitals as ‘‘Z’’ scores, we average two $^ { 6 6 } Z '$ scores to be the unit for measuring bank scale.

## H1.2. The existence of champions affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

Previous researches have shown that the existence of champions has a positive influence in affecting the adoption of a new information technology [33,39,43]. Champions often appreciate the adoption of new technology in an organization and usually stimulate their associates and staffs to support their ideas. In most cases, they have a direct and positive influence in terms of the adoption of a new technology. Lacking internal champions will lead to a failure as indicated in numerous researches. Champions play a critical role in providing information, related resources, and needed assistance to the process of adopting new information technology [2,4,12].

This study believes that the existence of champions is one of the key factors, which will affect the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry.

Based on Haley’s research [15], the quantification of ‘‘champion’’ variable can be estimated with a fivepoint Likert scale by using the following four items: the position of champion, the capability to provide related information, the assistance to secure the organizational resources, and the ability to acquire supports from all departments. A ‘‘five-point’’ represents that the factor is most important and a ‘‘one-point’’ means it is the least important one.

## H1.3. Top management support affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

As indicated in a number of researches [12,28], top management support is a key factor affecting the adoption of information technology. The greater the top management support is, the much easier it is for an organization to overcome the difficulty and complexity encountered in the adoption of information technology. As a result, securing the support of top management will lead to obtain necessary assistance related to required capital spending and labors support, and the cooperation to compete for resources in project-planning and development stage [12]. Without the support from top management, it will result in stronger resistance from employees and will become a substantial barrier to the adoption of data warehouse technology [15].

This study builds on the third hypothesis by assuming that top management support affects the decision to adopt data warehouse technology. Based on Haley’s research [15], the quantification of the ‘‘top management support’’ variable can be estimated by using a five-point Likert scale with the following four items: the support from top management for the adoption of new information technology, the attitude toward the information technology, the attitude to users’ satisfaction, and the attitude to employees’ resistance. The most important factor is represented by a ‘‘five-point’’ and a ‘‘one-point’’ is used to represent the factor with the least importance.

## H1.4. The bank’s internal needs affect the adoption of data warehouse technology

Previous studies [33,43] have shown that the internal needs in an organization is an important factor which affects the adoption of information technology.

The adoption of data warehouse technology results from internal needs such as the demands for requiring correct information from a single source more rapidly [39]. It would be helpful to adopt data warehouse technology only after organizational decision-makers completely understand the internal needs to necessitate such an adoption. Hence, this study assumes that internal needs in banks will drive the decision for an adoption of data warehouse technology.

Based on Grover and Goslar’s research [13], the quantification of this variable can be derived with a five-point Likert scale by the following five items classified under the internal needs category: the needs for a timely responding time, the needs to improve service quality, the needs of reducing costs, the needs to provide correct information, and the needs for raising competitive advantages. A five-point is used to present the most important factor, and a one-point is for the least important one.

## 3.3.2. Environmental dimension

In an environment with dynamic changes, organizations must try their best to measure and reduce uncertainties in the surrounding environment, and hence create competitive advantages by actively adopting newer information technology. As a result, the environment itself has a great influence on the utilization of new information technology. Most enterprises will be affected by such competition as raising the operational productivity and efficiency by adopting new information technology. Also, the selection of a vendor can be a factor that affects the adoption of data warehouse technology.

## H2.1. Degree of business competition affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

Enterprises would always like to raise their competitive advantage by adopting new information technology. Thus, they will evaluate and plan the adoption of a data warehouse to maintain the leading edge if their competitors are adopting or have adopted this new technology. Many researches [16,25] have also shown that the degree of business competition is directly associated with the adoption of new information technology. This study assumes that the degree of competition affects the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry.

Using Gatignon and Robertson’s research [11] as a reference to quantify the degree of competition, it can be justified by using the following five terms: a general organizational growth circle, the degree of competition in this industrial environment, the degree of computerization for the competitors, the needs of utilization of new technology to increase competitive advantages, and the degree of universality of the new information technology. A general organizational growth cycle can be divided into four stages according to Nolan’s stage theory [31] based on the level of information technology expenditures. Namely, they are initiation period, contagion period, control period, and integration period. The respondents fill in the questionnaires to match with their own company’s condition and/or status. As to the quantification of the other four items—the degree of competition in this industrial environment, the degree of computerization for the competitors, the need to utilize the new technology to increase competitive advantage, and universality of the new technology—it is again quantified with a five-point Likert scale using a fivepoint to represent the most important factor and using a one-point to represent the least important factor.

## H2.2. Selection of vendors affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

Enterprises should always carefully evaluate the possibility of outsourcing or in-house developing a data warehouse based on their own surrounding situations. If the enterprises decide to use outsourcing to adopt data warehouse technology, extra care should be taken into account in selecting the vendors [22]. The data warehouse technology itself is not only a software package, and the plans proposed by vendors may not be perfectly tailored for an enterprise itself. Thus, an enterprise cannot leave all the implementation plan and operating details in the control of vendors. This study assumes that the selection of vendors affects the decision of adopting data warehouse technology.

According to another study [32], the variable ‘‘selection of vendors’’ can be measured using fivepoint Likert scale by quantifying the following six items: the vendors’ reputation, the relationship with customers, the successful experience possessed, the capability to plan a complete project, the capability with good technology, and the ability to provide sufficient information of market. Weighted average totals can be calculated with a ‘‘five-point’’ that represents the most important factor and a ‘‘one-point’’ to depict the least important factor.

## 3.3.3. Project-planning dimension

Project-planning dimension is associated with resolving all related problems or issues about project analysis, development, management, and control in the process of the adoption. Project-planning is one of the most important factors in the adoption of the data warehouse technology. A good plan can help identify and accommodate the end users’ needs better. It can also reduce the dissatisfaction and discrepancy to manage and use the data gathered from different sources. According to prior research [12,15,40], the project-planning factor can be measured using the following four variables: skills of the project team, the degree of coordination of the organizational resources, the participation of end users, and the assistance from information consultants.

## H3.1. The skills of project team affect the adoption of data warehouse technology

The project team plays an important role in the process of project planning [10,20]. The project team should be composed of those employees who have expertise in the individual, and professional discipline. The members in the project team who possess a strong background and knowledge of the adoption of new information technology and a better communication capability will certainly play a positive role to influence the adoption of data warehouse technology. In addition, it is very important for project team members to have some experience in implementing a data warehouse and large-scale information systems [15]. To ensure successful adoption of the data warehouse technology, the project team members, internal users, and related parties must be continuously informed and aware of the status and latest developments of the project. It is necessary to provide the related educational training to these involved parties. Furthermore, the training should focus on the technology itself as well as the associated management and maintenance of issues. This study believes that the skills of the project team affect the decision of adopting data warehouse technology.

According to Haley’s research [15], the skills of a project team can be quantified with a five-point Likert scale by three items: technical capability, communication capability, and the degree of understanding of the users’ demands. A five-point represents the most important factor, and a one-point indicates the least important factor.

## H3.2. Coordination of organizational resources affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

Past researches [1,15,39] indicate that the adoption of data warehouse technology comes with characteristics such as incurring large expense and consuming much time. This study will assume that the coordination of organizational resources (money, time, etc.) is a key factor which affects the adoption of data warehouse technology.

Using Haley’s research [15], organizational resources will be defined as capital, development time, and labor. As a result, this study evaluates the coordination of organizational resources as if the organization affords abundant capital, sufficient time, and required labor. The weighted total can be obtained with a fivepoint Likert scale to quantify the following three items: the degree of support for needed budgets, the time needed, and the degree of support for needed labor in adopting new technology [15].

## H3.3. The participation of end users affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

Previous researches [8,34] indicate that the participation of end users has a direct impact on the adoption of information technology in an organization. Other related researches [1,12,15,39] in the data warehouse area also indicate that good users’ participation in the implementation and adoption of data warehouse technology can help meet the demands and expectations from various end users. Without the active participation from users, the result proves to be useless [12]. Furthermore, most data in the warehouse proves to be confidential and not every employee has the right to access it. The selection and inclusion of appropriate users in the project team is, as a result, an important task. In addition, the training for users cannot be ignored. Adequate training can help users to explore the needed information positively and in a much effective manner. Furthermore, the project team must evaluate the friendliness of the user interface and functions incorporated in the data warehouse applications. It is important to have a friendly utility to reduce the resistance from end users to use the newer information technology. This study assumes that the participation of users in the adoption stage affects the adoption of data warehouse technology.

According to Hartwick and Barki’s research [17], the quantification to the degree of users’ participation can be evaluated with a five-point Likert scale by the following three items: the degree of users’ participation in the implementation of information technology, the degree of coordination and cooperation between an information department and end users, whether the major work for end user is to identify the information needs. A five-point represents the most important factor, and a one-point is the least important factor, similar to the discussion presented earlier.

## H3.4. Assistance from information consultants affects the adoption of data warehouse technology

The adoption/implementation of data warehouse technology is a time-consuming process, which require large expenses and is associated with high risks. The consultants who have much experience with the adoption of new technology can be invaluable assets to the success of the adoption/implementation of new technology. Information consultants can be used as a supplement to provide ideas and assist organizations that lack the experience to adopt/implement a new information technology in an organization [15]. As a result, this study assumes that information consultants’ assistance in the process affects the decision to adopt the data warehouse technology in the banking industry.

Using Grover’s research [12] to quantify the variables, the assistance from information consultants and the degrees of importance can be measured with a five-point Likert scale by three items: the attitude of an organization toward the information consultants, the degree of expectation to acquire the suggestion from the information consultants, and the degree of expectation to acquire information consultant’s experience and skills. A five-point and a one-point will be used to represent the most and the least important factor, respectively.

## 4. Data collection

## 4.1. Design of the questionnaire

In line with the research model, the design of the questionnaire used in this study also includes the results of the conversation with bank’s top management and the findings from other related research questionnaires. According to Kwon and Zmud’s [24] classification scheme of the technology-adopting situations in the industry, this study categorizes the current situations to adopting data warehouse technology in the Taiwanese banking industry into four types: adopted, implementing, considering to adopt, and abandoned.

## 4.2. Objectives of the questionnaire

The purpose of this study is to identify critical factors influencing the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry in Taiwan. In addition, the current status of the adoption of data warehouse technology in Taiwanese banks is also investigated. The objectives of the questionnaire used in this study are to collect appropriate data for testing the significance of hypotheses proposed and for reporting the current utilization of the data warehouse technology in Taiwanese banks. The initial questionnaire was developed based on the findings from related literature. In order to improve the reliability and validity of this instrument, a panel of experts (including academia and practitioners) was formed to review each question and make necessary changes. After that, a pilot study was employed. The questionnaire was then sent to several IS department managers to answer, and the results were further reviewed by the panel for the improvement. After this two-round process, a final questionnaire was developed.

The questionnaire is composed of six sections. The first section is used to collect basic data on the respondents who answer the questionnaire and the general information about the bank. The second section is actually the major part of this questionnaire. This section intends to collect data related to CIO’s perception about the adoption of data warehouse technology for each dimension proposed in the research model. The next four sections are used to collect data from the following four situations, such as already adopted, start implementing, considering to adopt, and abandoned. The respondent has the option to answer any one of these four sections based on his/ her bank’s situation to use the data warehouse technology. The basic objective of the last four sections is to seek additional information related to the status about the adoption of the data warehouse technology. The data collected from these four sections were used for the descriptive purpose of reporting the current status of data warehouse usage in Taiwan’s banking industry.

## 4.3. A sample of the survey

According to the study in ‘‘Finance and Statistics Monthly Report in Taiwan, ROC (September 1999),’’ there is a total of 52 domestic banks including 47 general banks and 5 small-business banks. This study excludes the Central Trust of China and the Export– Import Bank of the Republic of China due to the special business conducted by these two national banks; and as a result, a sample size of 50 is used in this research. Furthermore, this study does not include foreign-capital banks in the research samples because of its different business operating nature compared with domestic banks and also because of the reason that the adoption of data warehouse technology in foreign-capital banks is determined and controlled by the overseas headquarters. The other ones that are not listed in the samples are farmer cooperatives, fishing cooperatives, and domestic credit cooperatives because of their very small scale. This survey was sent to chief information officers in local banking industries, and follow-up telephone conversations were conducted to ensure the integrity and reliability of the data collected.

## 5. Data analysis and discussion of findings

## 5.1. Data analysis

Fifty questionnaires were mailed to chief information officers in domestic banks, and a final of thirtytwo responded to the questionnaires via a second effort of mailing to solicit a response. After the deletion of two incomplete ones, a total of 30 valid survey responses were included in this study, and the resulting response rate was 60%. To ensure the full representation of samples, this study takes a chisquare test to demonstrate their homogeneity. The result shows that all parts in the questionnaires received after the first and second round of mailing solicitation are of no significant difference. Therefore, the unreturned questionnaires will not create an impact in the accuracy of the research findings. Most respondents are chief information officers who are in either strategic or managerial levels with an educational background of ‘‘bachelor’s degree and above.’’ Their average work experience was 15.27 years, with the longest experience for 30 years and the shortest for 4 years.

## 5.2. Analysis of the current status related to the adoption of data warehouse technology

In terms of the current status of operations related to the data warehouse technology, there are 16 banks with some data warehouse functions or capabilities. In fact, 5 out of 16 completed the process and currently utilize the data warehouse technology; the remaining 11 are in the process of development to adopt/implement data warehouse technology. There are 14 banks that have no implemented data warehouse technology. ## 6. Findings and observations

## 6.1. Findings and observations in the organizational dimension

Prior researches indicate that the larger the size of the organization, the more resources and capital can be secured to adopt the new information technology [7,25]. The capital spending and number of employees are used to measure the size of the bank in this study. From the studies of these aforementioned researches, the strategies to adopt information technologies are affected by factors—the size and industrial category of the enterprise. This study focused on a single industry; the result also indicates that the size of the bank affects the decision whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology. Furthermore, the statistics also show that the discriminative loading and the discriminative coefficient are pretty high. The size of the bank adopting data warehouse technology is larger than the size of the banks not adopting the same technology. Thus, the size of the bank has a great influence on the adoption of data warehouse technology in the Taiwanese banking industry.

For the second variable, existence of champions, the findings are more interesting. Champions in an organization can appreciate the contribution of adopting new technology and can actively persuade the associates, subordinates, and even superiors to support their ideas, and as a result, they should have a direct and positive influence on the decision to adopt a new technology. This study defines the champions as the ones who can be helpful in the enterprises to bring up innovative ideas for new adoption and/or implementation and have the ability to persuade the chief executive officers, department managers, and other associated employees to support their ideas. The result of this study shows again that the discriminative loading and the discriminative coefficient are pretty high. One glitch related to the previous result is the limited difference in discriminative coefficient value to compare the banks with the adoption of data warehouse technology vs. the banks without. Specifically, the effect of the champion factor in the bank’s adopting data warehouse technology is higher than the banks that have not adopted data warehouse technology. Thus, the existence of champions certainly has played a role in the adoption of data warehouse technology.

In many researches, top management support has been a key factor affecting the adoption of a new information technology. Acquiring the top management support usually leads to secure the assistance to acquire abundant capitals, labors, and related resources. As a result, the support from top management will be a strong indication that the adoption of data warehouse technology will go smoothly [12,28,39]. The result of this study shows that the discrimination loading and discrimination coefficient of top management support factor is pretty high, and it reinforces the fact that top management support can obviously affect the adoption of data warehouse technology $\left( P \mathrm { = } 0 . 0 3 6 \right)$ . Thus, the support from top management is an important factor affecting the adoption of data warehouse technology. Lacking this great support from the top management, it will lose the cooperation to acquire the necessary organizational resources, and thus, affect the adoption of data warehouse in the banking industry.

In addition to the factors such as the existence of champions and the support from the top management, the internal needs of a bank is also a key factor affecting the banks’ adoption of new information technology [33,43]. The need to adopt data warehouse technology includes the better capabilities to integrate the bank’s internal data, the ease of acquiring realtime and correct data, and the importance of analyzing future trends from historical data. It would be helpful to adopt data warehouse technology if the organizational decision-makers really buy in the values to satisfy the internal needs to adopt the data warehouse technology. For this factor, the values of discriminative loading and discriminative coefficient are pretty high. Furthermore, it has distinct difference between the banks with the adoption of technology data warehouse and the banks without $( P { = } 0 . 0 9 8 )$ . The internal needs factor from the banks adopting data warehouse technology is obviously higher than the banks not adopting it. As a result, the main reason to adopt the data warehouse technology in banks depends heavily on the internal needs in banks, and those needs may include such items as speeding the reaction, improving the quality of the service, cutting operating costs, providing reliable information in a timely manner, and increasing the relative competitive advantage.

## 6.2. Findings and observations in the environmental dimension

Enterprises always strive hard to raise their competitive advantages by adopting new information technology. It comes as no surprise that these enterprises perform thorough evaluation and assessment about the timing, cost, and underlying opportunity to adopt the data warehouse technology to gain strategic advantages and raise the market entrance costs for their competitors [16,25]. With the keen competition and degree of highly computerized advancement in the banking industry, banks in Taiwan are eager to raise their competitive advantage and improve their productivity and efficiency by adopting new technology. Since Taiwan’s government released the restriction to limit the establishment of new banks in 1991, domestic banks have faced fierce competition. Not long ago, Taiwan’s government also took effort in developing a positive financial policy and incentives to attract new foreign investors, which resulted in even more rigid competition in the financial industry. Namely, these competitions were from various forms of the banks such as traditional financial organizations: trust investment companies, commercial banks, small savings and loans banks, and public banks. From the results of the discrimination analyses, the discriminative loading and the discriminative coefficient are high with the surprising findings that the variables ‘‘degree of business competition’’ are not sufficiently high. A further examination of the two groups with and without adopting the data warehouse technology, the degree of business competition in banks adopting data warehouse, is higher than the ones without adopting the technology. In this study, the findings indicate that the degree of business competition affects the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry.

‘‘Selection of vendor’’ factor also plays an important role in the process of adopting data warehouse technology. Vendors can be helpful to provide complete products, better technological capability and knowledge, and familiarity to particular information technologies to assist in the adoption [22]. Although the local vendors have the capability to provide the required knowledge and assistance to the adoption of data warehouse technology, the implementation and adoption of this technology still needs the expertise of internal in-house functional employees, and the technology must be integrated into and hence be compatible with the existing information systems. Thus, vendors alone cannot implement the data warehouses. In this study, the variable ‘‘selection of vendors’’ has no obvious influence on the adoption of the data warehouse technology in terms of the values of discriminative loading and discriminative coefficient. Furthermore, it also indicates no difference between two groups, including banks adopting and not adopting the data warehouse technology. In summary, the selection of vendors in local banks has no apparent influence on the adoption of data warehouse technology.

## 6.3. Findings and observations in the projectplanning dimension

Prior researchs support that the members of the project team play an important role in adopting the data warehouse technology [12,15,20]. This study assesses the skills of the project team by evaluating three factors: the technical capability of a project team, the communication skills, and the degree of understanding the users’ needs. From the results of this study, the skills of the project team have a positive impact in the process of adopting the data warehouse technology, but it does not affect the decision to the adoption of data warehouse technology.

A great deal of researchs also indicate that the adoption of data warehouse technology is a process which is time-consuming, requires huge capital investment, and is associated with high risks [1,12,39]. The ‘‘coordination of organizational resources’’ factor can reduce unnecessary barriers during the implementation process and also facilitate the ease of adopting data warehouse technology by investing requested labors and capitals in the project [15]. After examining the empirical results, this study found that the discriminant loading and the discriminant coefficient values are pretty low. It indicates that the coordination of organizational resources in the adopting process may not be a dominant factor, which affects the adoption of data warehouse technology in the banking industry in Taiwan.

The participation of end users in the adoption process can assist in better defining needs explicitly and increasing the opportunities of having a successful technology adoption while the system needs are not clear [8,34]. To have a better accessing, retrieval, converting, and storing information from existing database, the successful adoption of data warehouse technology not only needs technologically competent users but also their expertise in the professional or functional disciplines. Since the end users are the ones who will use the data, hence they can provide a better picture about the data in terms of the characteristics, meanings, description, pattern, usefulness, and the relationships with other data. Interestingly enough, in this study, the participation of end users factor does not greatly affect the adoption of data warehouse technology since all of its discriminant indexes are of low significance level.

Based on prior researches, the employment of information consultants will affect the adoption of information technology. The purpose of employing external consultants is to acquire their professional experience and knowledge, which can be utilized to facilitate the implementation and/or adoption. By using the information consultants, one organization can quickly and cheaply utilize all the knowledge (lessons, successes, and/or failures from other companies) in the process of adoption and implementation [12,15]. This study has found out that having assistance from external information consultants is not an important factor to affect the adoption of data warehouse technology.

## 7. Conclusions

## 7.1. Important observation/contribution of this study

Given the fact that a number of businesses jumped on board to use the data warehouse technology, the users need to be careful that this technology may not be a perfect solution for all different organizations to resolve every data-related problem. Consequently, many adoption-related factors must be carefully and fully evaluated before any adoption attempt is made. The adoption of data warehouse technology is not just a simple activity to purchase the required hardware and software, but rather a complex process to establish a sophisticated and integrated information system. The plug and play situation may not be the case to adopt the data warehouse technology. The results of this study indicated that all factors in the organizational dimension and one factor in environmental dimension are important to take into account. Specifically, these factors include support from top management, the size of the bank, the internal needs, the degree of business competition, and the existence of champions. These aforementioned factors have a noticeable impact on the decision to adopt the data warehouse technology in the banking industry in Taiwan.

It comes as no surprise that top management plays an important role in the process of adopting data warehouse technology. If the top management level supports the adoption of the data warehouse technology, the assistance for the required resources will be easily acquired. The project team members, hence, will worry less about the shortage of resources and can focus more of their attention onto the other matters related to the adoption of data warehouse technology. The results of this study actually validate this belief as other prior research did. The top management support is truly one of the most important factors affecting the adoption of data warehouse technology. Specifically, the top management support factor is the most significant, discriminant index to distinguish whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology in the banking industry in Taiwan. Furthermore, this finding is in line with the findings to adopt other types of information technologies.

Data warehouse technology is an expensive, and hence risky, undertaking. Generally speaking, it will cost in excess of US\$1 million to build a full-scale data warehouse application/system. For most domestic banks in Taiwan, it is quite a large expenditure for a single software application (to most CEOs of banks in Taiwan). Given this information, the authors believe that the size of the bank should play an important role in the decision-making processes for the banking industry in Taiwan. In this study, the values of discriminant loading and discriminant coefficient of the size of the bank are ranked as the second highest and with a p-value of 0.076. That is, in addition to the factor of top management support, the size of the bank is the second most important discriminant index. This study found that there is a significant difference in terms of this factor between the banks decision of whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology. The capital spending is higher for the banks to adopt the data warehouse technology than the ones who do not adopt it. This finding, again, illustrates that the banks must have sufficient capital resources to take advantages to adopt the data warehouse technology.

It would be helpful to adopt data warehouse technology only after the organizational decisionmakers completely understand the internal needs to necessitate such an adoption. This study found that the internal needs for banks with the data warehouse technology adopted are significantly higher than those banks that have not yet adopted it. As a result, this study believes that the internal needs also have a great influence on the decision to adopt the data warehouse technology. Champions can be helpful in persuading chief executive officers, department managers, as well as other associates and staffs to adopt the data warehouse technology. In this study, champions are the persons who actively and vigorously promote their personal vision for the adoption of new information technology. The adoption of data warehouse technology may frequently meet some resistance, and champions are expected to diminish such resistance. This study found that there is a difference in ‘‘the existence of champions’’ variables for banks with or without the adoption of data warehouse technology. However, at a 5% level of significance, the analysis result did not support the second hypothesis (since pvalue = 0.141). As a result, this study still believes that the existence of champions has a great influence on the decision of whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology.

All factors in organizational dimension are important discriminant indexes to distinguish either to adopt or not to adopt the data warehouse technology from a practical perspective (all discriminant loading values >0.3). In addition to practical significance, hypotheses of top management support, the size of banks, and internal needs are all supported by discriminant analysis at 10% level of significance.

There are two factors in the environmental dimension. One factor is the degree of business competition and the other one is the selection of vendors.

The discriminant loading of the first factor is 0.335. This means that the degree of business competition is an important discriminant index to distinguish between the groups to adopt or not to adopt. Taiwan’s government recently established many financial policies which have resulted in much stronger competition in the financial industry. Another challenge to the financial/banking industry will be the emergence of foreign-capital investment after joining the WTO. It is of no doubt that the competition in the banking industry will be much keener, and current Taiwanese banks are anxious to use new information technology to strengthen/improve their competitive advantages. Again, this study discovers that the degree of business competition is one of the important factors affecting the decision to adopt the data warehouse technology. As per previous discussion, the data warehouse technology is not only a software package, and the implementation plan proposed/developed by vendors may not be perfectly tailored for banks. Thus, a bank cannot afford to fully depend on the vendors’ suggestion to develop the data warehouse applications/systems. In other words, how to select the right vendor to work with would affect the successful adoption of the data warehouse technology. However, the results of this study did not support this belief in both discriminant loading value and p-value of discriminant coefficient. One possible reason may be that there are not too many vendors of data warehouse technology in Taiwan for banks to select from. Most data warehouse vendors are naturally the representatives of well-established companies from the US, Europe, or Japan. Very few domestic data warehouse products exist in Taiwan’s market. Thus, the factor of selection of vendors is not an important discriminant index.

In line with the literature reviews discussed earlier, the projects of adoption of data warehouse technology are not only an expensive undertaking, but also need to be carefully evaluated and planned for. Although the project-planning dimension deals with some activities at the operational level, the authors believe that it is important for the organizational decision-makers to take it into consideration before the adoption of data warehouse technology. For this reason, the projectplanning dimension is also included in the proposed research model. Factors in this dimension include skills of the project team, the degree of coordination of the organizational resources, the participation of end users, and the assistance from information consultants. The respondents were asked to answer their perceptions regarding the factors included in this dimension based on the adoption of either data warehouse technology (i.e., banks with adoption of data warehouse technology) or other types of information technology (banks without adoption of data warehouse technology). The results revealed that there is no significant difference between these two groups. Although factors of the project dimension are very important for developing both data warehouse technology and other types of information technology, they are not important discriminant indexes to distinguish the decision of whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology. That is, the adoption of data warehouse technology is a strategic level decisionmaking based on the findings of this study.

The results of this study revealed that 16 out of 30 domestic banks have already adopted or are in the process of adopting/implementing data warehouse technology; 12 out of 30 banks are currently evaluating and studying possible adoption. However, only 2 out of 30 banks abandoned the idea to use data warehouse technology. These findings indicated that the adoption of data warehouse technology is favored by Taiwanese banks as a strategic weapon to be more competitive in the future.

As mentioned earlier, the Asian region, including Hong Kong, Singapore, Taiwan, and mainland China, is a fast growing market. These four areas are all with the same culture and speak the same language. Furthermore, business environments are not too much different after mainland China and Taiwan both joined WTO. The findings of this study are very valuable for the western banks that have an interest in establishing Asian branches or are considering to adopt data warehouse technology. In summary, the major factors influencing the adoption of data warehouse technology are top management support, the size of the bank, internal needs, champions, and the degree of business competition.

## 7.2. Limitations of this study

The sample for this study is limited to domestic banks in Taiwan. The current status of the banks may have different opinions and attitudes toward the adoption of data warehouse technology. For example, banks established earlier may have sufficient budgets and resources to invest in the new technology, but their existing information systems may be too obsolete, which will possibly present a strong obstacle toward the adoption of data warehouse technology. For newly established banks, they may be very aggressive to try the new technology but failed to have the adequate funds to realize the benefits.

Further, the perception and vision of the strategic level toward the use of information technology can be another consideration. A number of chief information officers in the banking industry may not have enough appreciation of the adoption of the latest IT because of their educational background and training in non-IT areas. Compared to the US, there are still a large number of IS executives not promoted from the IS area.

Adding to the complexity, the difficulty to evaluate/assess the benefits of adopting data warehouse technology is also a contributing factor that many banks in Taiwan are currently in a ‘‘hold and wait-tosee’’ state and watching the results closely and/or impacts from their competitors who have adopted data warehouse technology. Still, a relatively favorable environment for one bank to adopt the data warehouse technology may not exist for a second bank due to different operating characteristics. Moreover, it is pretty difficult to define a ‘‘favorable’’ environment explicitly for every bank to adopt the data warehouse technology.

In terms of different industries, this study emphasizes the adoption from one single industry—the banking industry. Different industries have different operating characteristics and environments, and as a result, the factors related to the adoption may be quite different. More concerns from the points of view of vendors and information consulting companies may affect the decision itself and/or the process to adopt the data warehouse technology.

For the analyses of statistics, the lowest standard for discriminant analyses is at least 20 samples for one group [14]. Though the reliability and efficiency of this study are in the acceptable range, this study does not reach the standard. Furthermore, the response rate is 60%, which is sufficient to perform an analysis, but there were actually only 30 samples, which is too limited.

## 7.3. Follow-up activities of this study

Two domestic banks in Taiwan, China Trust Commercial Bank (CTCB) and Bank Sino Pac (BSP), were randomly selected to conduct the follow-up activities. Currently, CTCB is ranked by the China Financial Association as the top bank in Taiwan because of its maintaining highest profitability and highest value of total assets in the recent years. BSP, which was founded after deregulation in Taiwan, is also one of the better, newer banks in terms of size of the bank, profitability, management styles, and product innovation.

In order to gain competitive advantages, CTCB has started to improve its business process and aggressively adopted emerging information technology since 1994. In fact, CTCB signed a US\$5 million contract with NCR in the late 1990s to construct the first data warehouse application in Taiwan’s banking industry. The main goal of this aforementioned project was to analyze/track the various behaviors of the customers, and hence, to deliver more diversified products and services to increase the operating efficiency and improve the management of customer relations. CIO and several upper level managers were interviewed. According to them, the critical success factors presented in this study had been seriously considered before the adoption of data warehouse technology.

Firstly, all upper level managers of CTCB committed and participated in the determination of establishing an information strategy/mission for CTCB since 1994. All respondents from CTCB who participated in the follow-up interview agreed that the factor of top management support is one of the most important critical factors to decide whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology. Not only the staff but also all upper level managers were trained to actively use IT to improve the efficiency of business processes and operations. In addition, CTCB has a sufficient number of information system staffs, 400 staff members, which is more than the staff number of most domestic software companies for handling the follow-up maintaining and related implementation activities. Furthermore, joining the WTO has been one of the major goals for Taiwan’s government for the past few years. Executives of CTCB did notice the impacts after joining WTO and took them into consideration. For example, the degree of business competition will be very different after Taiwan’s accession to WTO. As a result, this aforementioned factor has also been considered heavily by CEOs of CTCB as one of the critical factors before the adoption. Some other factors CTCB regarded as important ones to affect the adoption include the capabilities to sustain its competence edge and fulfill the needs of its customers. Selection of vendor factor, which is also supported by this study, proved to be another factor to affect the adoption decision. CTCB chose the experienced leader in the information service industry in Taiwan, NCR, to construct the data warehouse.

BSP is also one of the early adopters of data warehouse technology in Taiwan. Due to the keener competition in the credit card market, BSP applied data marts technology in their credit card department in 1998 and expanding from it to other departments/divisions such as personal consulting, online business consulting in finance and investment departments in the recent 2 years. These implemented data marts technology have provided an invaluable insight for designing/developing new financial products. Except for the satisfaction to internal needs in bank, one of the most important reasons for BSP to adopt data warehouse technology was to gain the competitive advantage in the domestic market. The upper managers of BSP strongly believe that the heavy investment in IT will sustain their competitive advantage. Consequently, BSP supported the entire project of data warehouse implementation. In addition, the MIS department has 122 staff members with an average age around 30 years old, and an intensive training and/or experience in both information technology and business functional areas. They have accumulated sufficient experience in system development, maintenance, and outsourcing management, which can be applied to adoption of data warehouse technology. The selection of vendors is, again, one of the critical factors in determining whether or not to adopt data warehouse technology. BSP chose Ascential Software as its vendor because of the ability of this company to work with many famous banks and the extensive knowledge and/or expertise to integrate information technology into the financial area.

The majority results of the above two follow-up interviews are consistent with the findings of this study. The results indicated that the degree of business competition, the internal and external demands, and the upper level managers’ support are the critical factors to determine whether or not to adopt the data warehouse technology. Upper managers in both banks believe that the investment in data warehouse is a must and definitely will bring in a substantial return. Since these two banks are early adopters, and hence, lacking the champion’s reference to reach the adoption decision, most of the references about the adoption of data warehouse technology were obtained from foreign banks and other industries. In addition, the size of banks and IS departments are also an important factor to determine the decision of adoption. In this study, the variable of selection of vendor is not significant to distinguish two groups. However, the interview results indicated that both banks highly regarded the factor of experienced IS vendors. Indeed, it is one of the main reasons why these two banks dared to invest in adopting data warehouse technology.

## 7.4. Future implications

The results of this study show that more than half of Taiwan’s banks have adopted or are adopting data warehouse technology, and most of the banks who have not yet adopted data warehouse technology at this stage expressed their desire to adopt it in the near future. These banks that are currently not adopting data warehouse technology realize its importance, but the reasons they cited for holding include huge capital investment, lengthy time consumption, difficulty in evaluating intangible benefits, problems with current data management and systems infrastructure, etc.

In conclusion, while this study advances the investigation of factors affecting the adoption of data warehouse technology and current status of adopting data warehouse technology in Taiwan’s banking industry, it is just a first step. Further study can be focused on the areas of these factors, which will affect the effective and efficient maintenance of a corporation’s data warehouse. As new information technology continues to emerge, it will also be beneficial to integrate the data warehouse technology with these newcomers such as customer relationship management (CRM) and enterprise resource planning technologies.
