## Abstract

The Intranet is rapidly leading to new ways of using, sharing and managing information in many organizations. This paper explores the role of Intranet from a new perspective—as a mechanism for supporting decision-making. This paper is divided into two parts. The first part of the paper uses the well-known data-dialog-model framework of decision support systems. It explores how an Intranet can be useful for rapid and flexible access to information required for decision-making. The second part of the paper presents a taxonomy of major types of Intranet-based decision support with illustrative examples. q 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Intranet; Decision support; World Wide Web; Internet; Distributed computing

The World Wide Web WWW 3 has had aŽ . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> tremenduous impact in various fields such as commerce and publishing. ## 2. Intranet-based decision support

A DSS helps decision-makers utilize data and models to solve semistructured or unstructured problems 30 . The quality of support is greatly enhanced <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> if quick and easy access is provided to data and models relevant to the decision. An Intranet facilitates this access by allowing distributed access to desired information, which itself may be distributed throughout the enterprise.

Traditionally, information accessed using a computer has been mostly limited to structured information residing in databases. The access to semistructured or unstructured information, such as memorandums and video clips, using the same computer-based interface, was cumbersome and difficult. If the information is distributed at different locations and is subject to frequent changes, the task of accessing such information becomes even more difficult and time-consuming. An Intranet-based information access provides several advantages when compared to the traditional forms of information access described above. These include the following:

1. More Õariety: Various types of information in different formats and levels of detail can be accessed;

2. Larger Õolume: More information can be accessed in the same time;

3. Greater reach: Information distributed in different parts of the organization can be accessed from any location linked to the Intranet.

The above advantages allow greater exploration of issues related to a decision problem. This can be of immense value to the decision-maker especially when dealing with semistructured or unstructured problems.

A DSS comprises of three subsystems 29 : dialog <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> subsystem, data subsystem and model subsystem. The dialog subsystem serves as the interface between the user and the system. The data subsystem deals with the creation, update and retrieval of data. The model subsystem focuses on the creation, maintenance and manipulation of models. In the remainder of this section, some of the major features desirable for each of the three subsystems are listed. This is followed by a detailed exploration of the role of an Intranet in supporting these features for each subsystem.

## 2.1. Intranet support for dialog subsystem

The dialog system refers to the user–system interface. As far as the user is concerned, the dialog subsystem is the system. Some of the desirable features for the user–system interface include the following 29 : i the ability to handle a variety of <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Ž . dialog styles, ii the ability to accommodate userŽ . actions in a variety of media, and iii the ability toŽ . present data in a variety of formats and media.

The browsers used in an Intranet facilitate the dialog between the user and the system, and hence constitute an important part of the dialog subsystem. The browsers support different types of dialog such as question<sup>r</sup>answer, menus, check boxes, forms and radio buttons. They can be used to provide information as input to the system and also display the output from the system. The hyperlink functionality in the browsers enables the user to navigate through the information and to examine the information at varying levels of detail. For example, a Network Manager can access a high-level view of the corporate network and if interested can drill down to the details of the network interface card used in a computer connected to a LAN. The hypertext functionality of the browser provides users with a greater control of the application, and a greater understanding of the problem and its domain 5 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

The browsers also support the presentation of desired information in different formats text, tables,Ž graphs, images and in different media screen dis-. Ž play, audio and video Fig. 1 . Several alternatives . Ž . are available for display of the information available in non-standard formats. One of the alternatives is to use conversion software e.g., Microsoft Word-to-Ž HTML . Another alternative is to use special viewer . software as a helper or a plug-in application in the browser. However, this implies that more than one viewer may be required for displaying different formats. Another alternative is to convert information in other formats to a common format such as Portable Document Format PDF , and use a single viewerŽ . software for viewing the information. Although constraints such as limited bandwidth may render realtime video access difficult, advances in computer and networking technologies e.g., better compres-Ž sion, streaming video can help overcome such con- . straints.

While the above features can be provided by a stand-alone DSS with a hypermedia user interface, such a system cannot access information available at a remote location. In contrast, the Intranet browser allows access to remote information, in addition to local information, required for decision-making.

Moreover, the user-interface can be enhanced by exploiting additional capabilities of the browser. For example, animation, client-level validation of user inputs using Java applets, for example and visual- Ž . ization, using tools such as Virtual Reality Modeling Language VRML , can significantly enhance the Ž . value of the information presented to the user. Special types of browsers can also be used for visualizing real-time data from multiple data sources 27 . A<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> further enhancement of the user-interface involves embedding the browser with intelligence. For example, rule-based interfaces can automatically adapt to individual user preferences in terms of the presentation of information 7 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

In addition to the above features, the browsers are easy to use, easy to learn and are relatively inexpensive. Moreover, most browsers present the same interface to the users across heterogeneous computing platforms. This greatly improves the productivity of the user since the user can gain access to the required information as and when a decision has to be made without undue concern about the differences in interfaces.

## 2.2. Intranet support for data subsystem

The data subsystem deals with the creation, update and retrieval of data. Some of the desirable features for data subsystem include the following <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 29 : i data management capability, ii the ability Ž . Ž . to access a variety of data sources, iii the ability toŽ . access transactional as well non-transactional data, Ž . iv the ability to handle personal and unofficial data, and v the ability to support rapid and flexible dataŽ . retrieval and changes to data.

An Intranet can support decision-making by providing rapid and easy access to data and database management systems distributed throughout the organization Fig. 2 . The data may reside on remoteŽ . Intranet servers or it may reside in remote databases which can be accessed through the Intranet. One of the major advantages of using the Intranet for database access is that the user need not have a detailed knowledge about the database management system. This is possible due to the availability of several Web-to-database connectivity tools which provide seamless access to the data residing in a variety of databases 34 . An Intranet also provides<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> access to legacy data on mainframes without any need for terminal emulation software 8 . It also facilitates access to data that is extracted and summarized from production databases.

The data itself can be distributed in different locations. For example, each LAN administrator may be required to maintain a database about LAN-related information including hardware, software, and applications. Thus, when a major decision about procurement or upgrade across the entire organization has to be made, the corporate IS manager can access the information residing in the databases corresponding to each LAN. In fact, software agents can access the remote databases and provide a consolidated view of the information to the IS manager. Another example is distributed access using the Intranet to information from expensive network management platforms and from other network devices <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 18 .

Decision-making, especially in upper management levels, relies heavily on data sources outside the organization 29 . An Intranet allows users to<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> access several external data sources during the decision-making process. For example, information about new technologies and about com petitive product<sup>r</sup>service offerings, available on the WWW, can be accessed through the Intranet. This can help the users in making important business decisions about the future of the organization. The Intranet allows similar access to external consultants and to parts of the internal networks of selected vendors and customers e.g., for deciding delivery schedules Ž of products and services, depending on the current inventory ..

While the Intranet can be used to access structured information i.e., transactional data residing inŽ . databases, for example, it can also be used to access semistructured or unstructured information. This includes non-transactional data such as internal memorandums and planning documents. Since such unstructured information can be distributed throughout the enterprise, efficient indexing and search mechanisms are required to locate and retrieve the relevant information quickly.

The Intranet can also be used to access other forms of semistructured information, such as the rationale for past decisions, to help in future decision-making. For example, a specific decision might have been taken after considering various issues, assumptions, constraints and the impact on stakeholders. However, over a period of time, some of the stakeholders may have left the organization, or some of the issues or constraints may no longer be relevant. Hence, when the decision-maker is required to revisit the problem, later on, the rationale and related factors which were used to justify the original decision can be compared with the current situation. The information about the rationale can be in different forms such as the minutes of a meeting or video clips, and can be distributed in different parts of the organization. Tools similar to HyperPKM 32 can be used on an Intranet for searching, accessing and distributing such information.

In addition to facilitating access to remote information, the Intranet also allows access to local information. Thus, personal information available locally on the decision-maker’s computer can be used in conjunction with the organizational information available remotely. The hyperlink feature in an Intranet browser enables the decision-maker to access information available locally or remotely and to seamlessly switch between the two types of information.

For certain decisions, the decision-makers may have to rely on information facts, judgements, etc.Ž . provided by people who are not part of the decisionmaking team. Such people include employees with necessary domain expertise or experience related to the problem. They can advise the decision-makers about the implications of a proposed decision or can propose alternative solutions to the problem. The interaction with such employees, each of whom may be a potential source of information, can be facilitated by the Intranet using, for example, Web-collaboration software.

The above discussion focused on different forms of information access using the Intranet for supporting decision-making. In addition to information access, the Intranet also facilitates different forms of information deliÕery. Information can be customized not only at the source but also at delivery depending on user privileges and need. Thus, a document accessed through the Intranet can be designed to automatically display different views depending on the user. For example, a Sales Manager can access pricing information while the Product Manager can access the technical information from the same document 28 . This is possible by embedding view-re-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> lated information within the document.

A recent trend in information delivery using the Intranet is the development of mechanisms for ‘information push’. Thus, customized information is sent to the decision-maker at prespecified times or when information is generated or updated. The advantage of this scheme is that it allows the decisionmaker to spend more time in analyzing the problem and its alternatives rather than searching for the required information 23 . However, excess reliance on such mechanisms can lead to information overload or can be distracting to the decision-makers and hence require judicious use.

## 2.3. Intranet support for model subsystem

The model subsystem focuses on the creation, maintenance and manipulation of models. Some of the desirable features for model subsystem include the following 29 : i model management capability, <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Ž . Ž . ii the ability to create new models quickly and easily, iii the ability to catalog and maintain a wide Ž . range of models, iv the ability to access and inte-Ž . grate model building blocks, and v the ability toŽ . interrelate these models with appropriate linkages through the database.

Typically, in many large organizations, several models are developed independently by users or groups of users to solve specific decision problems. However, these developments tend to be ad-hoc in nature leading to possible duplication of efforts. Besides, when confronted with similar problems, the decision-makers may not be aware of the existence of models developed earlier, or by others, in the organization. This can lead to a loss in organizational productivity.

An Intranet can help in solving the above problem. It can facilitate communication among the users or groups of users in coordinating the development of new models Fig. 3 . In addition, an Intranet Ž . provides the ability to acquire, distribute and update information about the various models located throughout the organization. This information can be held in a small number of Intranet servers perhaps, Ž on a central repository where developers and users. can exchange information about various decision models in the organization. It has to be noted that only the links to the models may need to be stored in a central repository; the models can be available at different locations. Thus, the central repository essentially provides catalog and directory services for accessing desired models. Intranet servers which provide similar catalog and directory services are available from several vendors, such as Netscape.

The Intranet can also provide access to several model management systems, model development tools and decision models. For example, several systems such as the knowledge-based DSS for computer resource planning 1 have been developed to <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> support some of the decisions made by IS managers. If access to such systems is provided to a larger number of decision-makers using the Intranet, the value of such systems to the organization can be greatly increased. Some of the models can be acquired from external providers and can be customized to suit the decision-making requirements <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 22 . Seamless access to different decision models from within an application using an Intranet can be facilitated by incorporating tools similar to web widgets 10 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

The Intranet also allows some of the models to be linked to appropriate databases. For example, models can be downloaded by the decision-maker through the Intranet and data from a local database can be used for model execution. The models can also be remotely accessed through the Intranet; the models, in turn, can access appropriate databases, which may be in a different location, through the Intranet as well. Thus, the Intranet not only facilitates remote access to models but also allows links between the models and appropriate databases.

The above features of Intranet in facilitating access to models will be very useful for large organizations whose decision-makers are geographically dispersed. The Intranet allows the decision-makers to reuse, and if necessary revise, the models developed by others, thereby leading to quicker decisions.

## 3. Taxonomy of Intranet-based decision support

This section provides a taxonomy of methods for Intranet-based decision support. It attempts to delineate how an Intranet can facilitate decision-making by providing links between the major components: data, model and user. The key dimensions of each component are identified. These dimensions are then used to develop a taxonomy of Intranet-based decision support. This taxonomy is not necessarily exhaustive but is meant to describe the major types of Intranet-based decision support, along with illustrative examples.

Models, data and users are important organizational resources required for decision-making and it is important to utilize and manage these valuable resources effectively. Any form of decision support involving these entities should facilitate enterprise modeling 6 , i.e., the identification of a variety of<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> information sources and the relationships among them. Some of these sources may be external to the organization and it is important to facilitate access to such sources as well. Hence, the location of the information source and the method of information access are considered as important dimensions in the taxonomy presented below.

## 3.1. Data

First, the data component in decision-making is explored. The following dimensions are considered for Intranet-based decision support:

1. Data source: Data can be obtained from internal Ž . Ž within the organization or external outside the organization sources..

2. Nature of data: Data can be structured Že.g., spreadsheet or. Ž unstructured e.g., memorandums ..

3. Type of access: Data can be accessed locally, on the decision-maker’s computer, or from a remote location.

As explained earlier, the location of the data source and the type of access are considered important dimensions in decision support. In addition, as indicated in Section 2.2, decision-making often involves the use of both transactional as well as non-transactional data, and hence the nature of data is considered as one of the dimensions for decision support.

Table 1 lists the different types of Intranet-based decision support with respect to the data component. Types D1 and D2 involve local access while Types D3 to D6 involve remote access. As explained in Section 2.2, the Intranet can be used to access local as well as remote data, although the access may or may not be seamless depending on the format of the data. An example of Type D1 is data access from a spreadsheet or a database in the decision-maker’s computer. Type D2 is similar to Type D1 except that unstructured data in the form of memorandums or letters can be accessed locally.

The benefits of an Intranet to a decision-maker are greater in terms of facilitating remote access rather than local access. An example of Type D3 is a Product Manager obtaining inventory data from a remote Intranet server to plan for future production. An example of Type D4 is a Marketing Manager obtaining a copy of planning documents and a video clip of the new product advertisement from a remote Intranet server. The information can actually be dispersed among databases, e-mail, and corporate applications. The use of intelligent information agents, such as those used in Anderson Consulting’s Contact Finder 24 , can facilitate quick retrieval of desired<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> information. An example of Type D5 is a Network Manager obtaining data from the Intranet server of a telecom carrier about the utilization of wide area network services leased from the carrier 18 . ## 3.2. Model

Next, the model component in decision-making is explored. The following characteristics are considered for Intranet-based decision support:

1. Model source: Model can be obtained from internal Ž . Ž within the organization or external outside the organization sources..

2. Type of access: Model can be accessed locally, on the decision-maker’s computer, or remotely.

3. Type of execution: Model can be executed locally, on the decision-maker’s computer, or at a remote location.

As explained earlier, the location of the source and the type of access are considered important dimensions in decision support. In addition, decision support can be facilitated either by local or remote execution of models, and hence the type of execution is considered as one of the dimensions for decision support.

Table 2 lists the different types of Intranet-based decision support with respect to the model component. Types M1 and M2 involve local access while Types M3 to M6 involve remote access. Local access and<sup>r</sup>or execution of the model can be performed either from within the Intranet browser or outside the browser depending on factors such as the size of the model and the software used for its execution.

An example of Type M1 is the execution of a linear programming model on the decision-maker’s computer. Type M2 involves accessing a model available locally, making changes if required, and shipping it to a remote server for execution. This may be done, for example, to overcome the constraints e.g., processing or storage capacity of theŽ . local processor. This may also be done to utilize the idle processing capacity of remote servers.

Type M3 involves downloading a model from a remote server and then executing it on the decisionmaker’s computer. For example, a budgeting model downloaded from an Intranet server containing a library of financial models, can be executed locally by the decision-maker. Type M4 involves execution of the model on the remote server. An example is the interactive execution of a sales forecasting model on the company’s mainframe through the Intranet.

Types M5 and M6 involve access to external models. An example of Type M5 is access to an external library of decision models, such as NETLIB <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 20 , and execution of the desired model on the decision-maker’s computer. Another example is to rent decision models, in the form of Java applets, from an external provider for temporary use on the decision-maker’s computer 25 . Type M6 is similar <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> to Type M5 except that the models accessed from an external source can be executed on a remote server, either within or outside the organization. Examples of this type include the DecisionNet 4 and Net-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> work-Enabled Optimization System 19 available on<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> the WWW.

## 4. Conclusion

One of the major strengths of an Intranet as a mechanism for decision support is that it facilitates access to distributed information and distributed access to desired information. This information can be in the form of data and models and can be obtained from different sources in a variety of formats and media, locally or remotely. The Intranet provides a user-friendly mechanism for rapid and flexible access to information required for decision-making by facilitating the links between data, models and users.

The Intranet is not necessarily a perfect solution for supporting all types of decisions, especially in cases which mainly involve field visits and personal face-to-face meetings. This is because of the limitations of WWW technology underlying the Intranet <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 26 . Besides, there are several concerns which need to be addressed. For example, since Intranet allows wider access to information throughout the organization, concerns about compromising security are becoming important. There are also several organizational concerns related to the change in power relationships, due to quicker and greater access to information, which need to be addressed.

The technology underlying the Intranet is fast evolving. New enhancements to Intranet browsers, such as support for collaborative multimedia applications, are likely to result in a richer discourse and improved decision-making. With the rapid growth in the number and size of Intranets, more and more decision-makers are likely to be using the Intranet for supporting their decision-making. In some cases, this may lead to a redefinition of the roles of the decision-makers. The data-dialog-model framework and the taxonomy of types of Intranet-based decision support presented in this paper can be helpful in understanding the role of Intranet in decision-making. They can also be used to characterize some of the current as well as potential forms of decision support using the Intranet.
