## Abstract

Research into Group Support Systems GSS is still in its early stages but there is great worldwide interest in the area ofŽ . research. The study has addressed these developmental issues through a thorough examination of literature from thirteen core IS and GSS journals in the period from the first published GSS journal articles 1984 until the end of 1996. A small numberŽ . of institutions and authors at or from these institutions have dominated GSS research. Perhaps because of their dominance, most GSS research has been on EMS particularly GroupSystems and so more work needs to be done on other EMSŽ . software and non-EMS approaches. The study shows there is a clear need for more field work where studies involving actual stakeholders with their own problem s may reveal ways of improving technologies, methodologies and models, orŽ . suggesting where new approaches may be needed. Further, theory development has been limited and more conceptual work is needed along with empirical studies which extend current theories and previous research studies. The effect of time and experience with GSS needs to be studied, and more emphasis should be placed on the development and refinement of new models and methodologies. While the literature review carried out has some limitations, it still provides an interesting view of the field and may help researchers focus on the directions they wish to take in their research in GSS. ## 1. Introduction

Information Systems IS is an emerging fieldŽ . which has begun to establish its own substantive subfields, from general IS development and management to specific technologies such as Group Support Systems GSS . A number of studies have conductedŽ . systematic examinations of the IS literature which serve to assess the progress of the IS field and judge its advancement in terms of quality, depth and development of theory 1,7 . However, there have been <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> few such reflective studies in the subfields, particularly in the growing area of GSS. The current study aims to address these developmental issues through a thorough examination of literature from thirteen core IS and GSS journals in the period from the first published GSS journal articles 1984 until the endŽ . of 1996.

The value of such a study is in providing an opportunity to pause and reflect on what has been achieved, to identify gaps which need to be addressed, and to set directions for future research.

## 2. Background

This study is part of an ongoing program of research on GSS which was initiated in 1989 and includes laboratory experimentation, field research and literature studies. An earlier stage of the literature study examined the literature in a slightly less formal manner over a wider but perhaps biased . Ž . range of GSS literature sources. While the results from this work has proved interesting 36,37,40 , it <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> was felt that more formal analysis would help to provide a less biased picture of past GSS research and give clearer directions to future research in this field. This study builds on the strengths and addresses the weaknesses of previous studies. The primary objective of the research is to develop an overview of the field by studying who has provided leadership in the GSS field individuals and institu-Ž tions , what GSS research has been done including. Ž type of GSS, GSS software studied, research focus, and stage of research , and how the research has. been done including type of research, data collec-Ž tion, and epistemology ..

## 3. Methodology

In this study the GSS papers in these journals were identified, analyzed, classified, coded and recorded according to a scheme which has evolved from the previous study and the work of a number of authors in other research of this type. In addition to the ‘demographics’ of journal name, year, authors and affiliations, each paper was classified according to general research factors including research type, Ž whether it was longitudinal, stage of research, whether data was collected, and epistemology , and. GSS factors such as GSS type, time and placeŽ dimensions, GSS software and tools, and research focus . In addition, though not presented in this. study, data factors such as data type, instruments Ž used, dependent and independent variables studied, application task, data source, and various field and laboratory characteristics were identified and col-. lected.

## 3.1. Choice of journals for the study

Walsham p. 382 49 argues that ‘‘a journal canŽ . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> be viewed as a machine...which ties together a network of interested parties and controls aspects of their behavior, such as the definition of high quality research’’. Through examining a selected set of relevant journals it was hoped that a reasonable picture of the quantity, scope and growth of GSS research might be developed.

A number of authors have reviewed and rated various avenues for publishing IS research <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 6,20,24,32,34,50 . Given the ratings and general perceptions of these journals from the various reviews, thirteen major publications were chosen which were regarded as either key IS journals or likely outlets for publication of GSS research. The first group of four journals presents research wider than IS but are regarded as major publication targets by IS researchers. These are Communications of the ACM, Decision Sciences, Management Science, and Interfaces. The second group of five journals, namely Data Base, Information and Management, Information Systems Research, Journal of Management Information Systems, and MIS Quarterly, present the full range of IS research topics. Decision Support Systems presents research on the DSS subdiscipline within IS and so may be expected to publish substantial numbers of GSS papers. Two more relatively recent journals which have a particular orientation towards research in GSS were added, namely Journal of Group Decision and Negotiation and Journal of Organizational Computing and Electronic Commerce. Finally, Small Group Research was included because of its particular relevance to the GSS field Žin its focus on the support, technological or otherwise, of groups . It was felt that this list of thirteen. journals would be fairly representative of IS research in general and GSS research in particular, and would be appropriate for examining directions and trends in GSS research.

## 4.2. Authors and institutions

As indicated, the author s and affiliation s wereŽ . Ž . recorded for each paper in the study. ## 5. Conclusions, recommendations and future work

Research into GSS is still in its early stages but there is great worldwide interest in the area of research. The literature analysis employed in this study suggests a number of biases in current research and suggests directions for future GSS research. This study has applied a fairly thorough framework but its major limitation has been in the collection of data from only thirteen major journals. Even though they are considered quite representative of the GSS field, they do contain a US bias.

In summary, a small number of institutions par-Ž ticularly University of Arizona, followed by Minnesota and Georgia and authors at or from these. institutions Nunamaker, Vogel, Dennis, DeSanctis Ž . have dominated GSS research since 1984. However, the rate of entry into the field by other researchers has increased so their influence, while still remaining fairly strong, will slowly decrease over time. Perhaps because of their dominance, most GSS research has been on EMS particularly GroupSystems and soŽ . more work needs to be done on other EMS software and non-EMS approaches. A further implication of the entry of others into the GSS field is that this spread into new cities and countries improves the awareness of the potential of GSS in practice and may increase the diffusion of this technology into organizations.

There is a clear need for more field work where studies involving actual stakeholders with their own problem s may reveal ways of improving technolo-Ž . gies, methodologies and models, or suggesting where new approaches may be needed. Further, theory development has been limited and more conceptual work is needed along with empirical studies which extend current theories and previous research studies. The effect of time and experience with GSS needs to be studied, and more emphasis should be placed on the development and refinement of new models and methodologies. Finally, the full range of research approaches should be considered when examining any research problem in GSS, as in any branch of IS research. The evidence from this study is that interpretivist approaches have been almost ignored evenŽ though they may have been useful in developing concepts and hypotheses in this emerging field and. a better balance between the positivist and the interpretivist research philosophies should be sought.

This paper has identified certain issues in GSS research approach and direction. The study has helped some researchers focus on the directions they wish to take in their research. While, as pointed out above, the literature review carried out has some limitations, it still provides an interesting view of the field. It is intended that a follow-up study will examine the empirical aspects of existing research particularly in Ž defining and measuring GSS ‘success’ , and the . literature source will be expanded to include a greater number of journals, particularly European IS journals such JIT, JSIS, and EJIS. It is also expected that this historical analysis will be continuously updated and reported at regular intervals in the future.
