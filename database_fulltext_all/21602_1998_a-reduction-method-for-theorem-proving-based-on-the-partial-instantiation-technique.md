# A reduction method for theorem proving based on the partial-instantiation technique

Masahito Yamamoto <sup>)</sup>, Azuma Ohuchi <sup>1</sup>

## Abstract

Logical inference is of central importance in the information and decision science. First-order predicate inference Ž . theorem proving provides more powerful representation of models and of knowledge, but presents a hard computational problem. We extend Jeroslow’s theorem-proving procedure in function-free formulas to be applicable to full first-order predicate formulas. Therefore, proposed theorem-proving procedure can be more flexible tool for data handling and inferences. Our proposed procedure is based on the partial-instantiation technique, which reduces a first-order predicate clausal-form formula to an incrementally augmented propositional formula and proves the theorem by checking the satisfiability of the propositional formula. We also show the completeness of the proposed procedure, i.e., it can detect the unsatisfiability of an input formula. Furthermore, we compare it with resolution-style theorem-proving program OTTER and show the efficiency of the proposed procedure. q 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Theorem proving; Logical inference; Partial instantiation; Propositional calculus; Davis–Putnam procedure

## 1. Introduction

Logical inference is of central importance in the information and decision science, particularly in data bases, expert systems, and such logical languages as PROLOG 3 . In the context of decision support<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> systems DSS , such logic-based approach plays an Ž . important role in creating more ‘intelligent’ systems. Propositional logic is useful for data handling and inferences, but cannot handle more complex representation of models and of knowledge. First-order predicate logic is more powerful for representing knowledge than the propositional one. First-order predicate inference can be reduced to a theorem-proving procedure, i.e., to whether a given first-order predicate formula is satisfiable or not, but this presents a very hard computational problem.

Jeroslow has proposed a partial-instantiation technique for theorem proving in a class of function-free predicate formulas 4 . This method reduces a first- <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> order predicate formula to a series of propositional formulas and check the unsatisfiability of the propositional formulas. Recently, some reduction methods, such as hyperlinking strategy and the partial-instantiation technique, have been remarkable for solving theorem-proving problems 5 . Here, we focus on the<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> partial-instantiation technique. In the process of the partial-instantiation technique, some instances of a given formula are generated by using a truth assignment called a variant independent satisfying valuation. To obtain the truth assignment, any predicate atomic formula is treated as a propositional atomic formula. It is a remarkable characteristic of the partial-instantiation technique to exploit some information of obtained truth assignment for generating instances. Unfortunately, Jeroslow’s unexpected sudden death interrupted the further research on the partial-instantiation technique at the early stage.

In this paper, we extend Jeroslow’s theorem-proving procedure based on the partial-instantiation technique to be applicable to full including functionŽ symbols first-order predicate formula. Thus, our . theorem-proving procedure can be more flexible tool for data handling and inferences. Our proposed procedure utilizes a clausal-form formula as an input formula and it enables us to use the efficient algorithms for the satisfiability problem in the propositional logic, such as Davis–Putnam procedure and algorithm A, for searching a variant independent valuation 2 . To implement that, some definitions in<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Jeroslow’s method are changed in our proposed procedure. Also, we show that our proposed method has the completeness, which is needed for efficient theorem-proving procedures, i.e., it can verify that a given set of clauses is unsatisfiable if indeed it is unsatisfiable. Furthermore, we compare it with the conventional resolution style theorem-proving program OTTER by McCune in order to show the effectiveness of the proposed procedure 6,8 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some definitions. Section 3 describes the details of our procedure. We show that our proposed procedure has completeness in Section 4. Section 5 gives some experimental results of applying our proposed procedure to some problems in order to show the effectiveness of it. Section 6 includes concluding remarks and some future works.

$$

We can obtain the following variant independent satisfying valuation $V _ { 1 }$ of $S _ { 1 }$ , since there exists the satisfying truth assignment $\{ p _ { 1 } , \lnot p _ { 2 } , \lnot p _ { 3 } \}$ of $S _ { 1 } ^ { p }$

$$
\begin{array}{c} C _ {1} = \underbrace {P (x)} _ {\text {true}} \\ C _ {2} = \neg \underbrace {P (a)} _ {\text {false}} \lor \underbrace {Q (f (y) , b)} _ {\text {false}} \\ C _ {3} = \neg \underbrace {Q (f (z) , b)} _ {\text {false}}. \end{array}
$$

For the valuation $V _ { 1 }$ , a proper blockage exists in $S _ { 1 } { \mathrm { : } }$ , i.e., ${ B } _ { 1 } = \{ \langle P ( \boldsymbol { x } ) , P ( \boldsymbol { a } ) \rangle \} _ { 1 , 2 }$ . Since the most general unifier $\sigma _ { 1 }$ for these atomic formulas is $\{ a / x \}$ , we generate a new clause $C _ { 4 }$

$$
C _ {4} = C _ {1} \sigma_ {1} = P (a).
$$

As a result, we can obtain a set $S _ { 2 }$ of clauses by adding the cause $C _ { 4 }$ to $S _ { 1 } ^ { \phantom { \dagger } }$ Then $S _ { 2 } ^ { p }$ is as follows,

$$
S _ {2} ^ {p} = \left\{p _ {1}, \neg p _ {2} \vee p _ {3}, \neg p _ {3}, p _ {2} \right\}.
$$

Since $S _ { 2 } ^ { p }$ is unsatisfiable, there exists no variant independent valuation which makes $S _ { 2 }$ true. Thus, by theorem 4.1, S is unsatisfiable.

Example 5: Suppose that a set S of clauses of the following two clauses are given:

$$
\begin{array}{l} C _ {1} = P \big (f (a) \big) \\ C _ {2} = \neg P (x) \lor P (f (z)), \end{array}
$$

where a is a constant, x is a variable, $f$ is a function symbol, and $P$ is a predicate symbol.

We set $S _ { 1 } = S$ and transform $S _ { 1 }$ into $S _ { 1 } ^ { p }$ ,

$$
S _ {1} ^ {p} = \left\{p _ {1}, \neg p _ {2} \vee p _ {3} \right\}.
$$

Then there exit some truth assignments to the propositional variables that satisfy $S _ { 1 } ^ { p }$ . Therefore, there exist some variant independent satisfying valuations of $S _ { 1 }$ . Consider the following variant independent satisfying valuation $V _ { 1 }$ of $S _ { 1 }$ .

$$
C _ {1} = \underbrace {P (f (a))} _ {t r u e}
$$

$$
C _ {2} = \neg \underbrace {P (x)} _ {t r u e} \vee \underbrace {P (f (z))} _ {t r u e}.
$$

For this valuation, there is no proper blockage in $S _ { 1 }$ , because all atomic formulas are assigned the same truth value. Thus, by Corollary 4.1, S is satisfiable.

Consider another variant independent satisfying valuation $V _ { 1 } ^ { \prime }$ of $S _ { 1 } . \ V _ { 1 } ^ { \prime }$ is as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{c} {C _ {1} = \underbrace {P (f (a))} _ {t r u e}} \\ {C _ {2} = \neg \underbrace {P (x)} _ {f a l s e} \vee \underbrace {P (f (z))} _ {f a l s e}.} \end{array}
$$

For $V _ { 1 } ^ { \prime }$ , there exists a proper blockage $ { \langle P ( f ( a ) ) }$ $P ( x ) \rangle _ { 1 , 2 }$ . Therefore, the procedure must continue the process. In this way, the computational efficiency of the proposed procedure strongly depends on the selection of variant independent satisfying valuation.

We found that the computational time for solving SAT problem is very short in the whole run time and almost all the time is consumed by searching for and dissolving proper blockages. Therefore we need to consider the way of making the process efficient.

The number of proper blockages in a loop is strongly influenced by a variant independent satisfying valuation. From this observation, we expect to reduce the number of proper blockages if we could reduce the number of variables fixed by both one-literal and branching rules in DP. The branching strategy in ASAT roughly says that we should branch on the propositional variable having the greatest number of occurrences in the shortest clauses of a given SAT problem. This means that we may reduce the number of the fixed variables by using branching strategy in ASAT. Therefore, it seems that ASAT is also effective for the aim to reduce the number of proper blockages. ## 7. Conclusion

We extended Jeroslow’s theorem-proving method to be applicable to full first-order predicate formulas. Our proposed procedure is still based on the partialinstantiation technique, but can accept more flexible predicate formulas with function symbols. We also proved the completeness of our procedure. By experiments, we showed that there exist some problems, in which our procedure can solve more easily than a conventional resolution-based method. In particular, proposed procedure is expected to solve a satisfiable problems easily, i.e., it can find a model an interpre-Ž tation which satisfies a given formulas . Further- . more, We showed that the computational efficiency of our procedure strongly depends on the variant independent satisfying valuation. Therefore, we need to investigate various strategies for obtaining a variant independent valuation, which leads to a fast termination of the procedure.
