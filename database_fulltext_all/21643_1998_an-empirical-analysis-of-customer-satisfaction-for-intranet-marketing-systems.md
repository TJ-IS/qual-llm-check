# An empirical analysis of customer satisfaction for Intranet marketing systems

Mayuram S. Krishnan <sup>a,)</sup>, Venkatram Ramaswamy <sup>b</sup>

<sup>a</sup> Computer Information Systems Department UniÕersity of Michigan Business School, Ann Arbor, MI 48109-1234, USA Marketing Department UniÕersity of Michigan Business School, Ann Arbor, MI 48109-1234, USA

## Abstract

We study the drivers of customer satisfaction for Intranet marketing application systems. Based on the existing literature on business value of information technology, we provide a framework to understand the drivers of overall customer satisfaction with Intranet solutions. Our analysis based on data collected from a survey of over thirty firms that use Intranet marketing applications indicates that customers perceive features that lower total cost of ownership and provide competitive benefits as critical drivers of overall customer satisfaction with the systems. The implications of our findings for providers of Intranet marketing services are discussed. q 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Customer satisfaction; Intranet software; Marketing systems; Perceived value of information systems; Cost of ownership

## 1. Introduction

The model of collaborative team work that was first introduced by the emergence of groupware more than a decade ago is now being institutionalized by the increasingly pervasive presence of networked computing and electronic commerce in all aspects of our lives. As a result of competition in the market place, firms are attracted to economic efficiencies and other competitive advantages offered by electronic commerce applications. E-commerce applications and the economic benefits from them can be mapped to all aspects of the business value chain. These applications can be both internal and external to the firm. For example, electronic integration of a large number of suppliers and customers through EDI initiated by auto manufacturers over a decade ago are examples of applications in the in-bound and out-bound logistics of a firm’s value chain.

Recently there has been a shift in focus towards internal applications of electronic commerce often referred to as Intranet applications. Intranets have become so popular over the past few years that most major companies already have them in place or are at some stage in the process of developing Intranet solutions. These companies range from corporate giants such as General Motors, IBM and Microsoft to many smaller organizations and startup companies. A study conducted by IDC International Data Ž Corporation among Fortune 1000 companies found. that business investment in Intranet solutions have exhibited returns of investment of over 1000% on average, with pay-back periods in the 6–12 week range.

Electronic integration of business processes within the firm can deliver significant competitive advantages and cost savings 1,12 . An integrated order<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> processing system ensures that orders that are received electronically are sent automatically not only to shipping systems but also to production, billing and inventory systems. This quick dissemination of information improves consistency across various systems and can facilitate reduction in product development cycle-time and inventory costs. Electronic integration of business processes may also help leverage resource utilization within firms. For example, the Ford Motor has used Intranet applications to enable engineers and designers worldwide to collaborate in real time on the design of new models 19 . This<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> collaboration assures that Ford North America’s expertise in aerodynamics is being leveraged with expertise in other areas in Europe and different geographical sites to deliver high quality design of new models faster and at lower cost.

Implementations of Intranet solutions involves significant investments. In the last few years a multi-million dollar industry has emerged in Intranet services 7 . The estimated spending by US corpora-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> tions on Intranet solutions for the next three years is over US\$ 20 billion 19 . An important distinction of<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Intranet applications is that unlike traditional client server applications, Intranet applications may allow instant, random point-to-point communication between any two nodes on a network without any setup or log in. Intranets also provide appropriate security measures to guard company databases from unauthorized access. A number of organizations have attempted to build Intranet applications and some early experiments also indicate that managers still lack a clear vision of how best to use this technology 6 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

The concept of the Intranet is viewed differently in various organizations. A recent study has indicated that users’ view on Intranets range from a little more than a corporate bulletin board for sharing information on a common platform to supporting all corporate applications 3 . However a dominant view <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> of the Intranet is that of a corporate network that uses Internet standards and protocols to provide integrated business applications within companies.

Firms are deploying Intranet solutions to improve efficiency and effectiveness across various functional areas of business such as marketing, operations, human resources and finance. Kalakota and Whinston 1997 , provide an overview of specific applica-Ž . tions of electronic commerce in these functional areas. The marketing function is often the first area targeted for electronic commerce applications. In order to succeed in intensively competitive environments, firms are rapidly deploying electronic commerce applications both within and external to the firm. These applications can foster the cultivation of customer relationships, identify and target specific micro-segments of the market and enhance customer satisfaction through new value-added channels for product and service delivery 11,18 . For example, in <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> financial services, Internet technology is already being used by companies to deliver market sensitive news to stock traders 10 . Another significant application of Intranet solutions in various industries is in human resources development. For example, a leading manufacturer of network products has implemented streaming video technology over its Intranet to deliver stored video on demand to instruct and train its 5000 sales and marketing employees worldwide. Firms are using Intranet applications to integrate their sales force with other parts of the organization such as production and research to enhance access to market intelligence and improve product strategy. As a consequence, the industry has witnessed a significant growth in the number of marketing Intranet applications.

The surge in the demand for Intranet applications has led to the entry of a number of firms into the business of Intranet solution development. These firms range from large corporations that provide complete electronic commerce solutions to small start up companies that provide web page development services. As a consequence, vendors of Intranet services strive to meet the expectations of customers to achieve high levels of customer satisfaction and loyalty. However, these vendors are also concerned about the cost implications of their services. Hence vendors of Intranet solutions are placing emphasis on listening to customer needs and understanding the specific features of Intranet applications that are perceived as important by the customers. Such an understanding facilitates resource allocation decisions to enhance product alignment and customer satisfaction 13,16 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

In this paper we address the various drivers of customer satisfaction for Intranet marketing systems. Based on data collected with the help of one of the leading firms that delivers Intranet solutions, we study the importance of cost of ownership, perceived benefits in terms of improved competitive advantage and personnel productivity in determining overall customer satisfaction with the Intranet solutions. Our results indicate that satisfaction with the cost of ownership of the Intranet system is the primary driver of overall satisfaction. We also find that satisfaction with perceived competitive benefits such as increased market share and revenues from new business significantly enhances overall customer satisfaction. However the relative effect of satisfaction with perceived competitive benefits is lower as compared to satisfaction with the cost of ownership.

The rest of the paper is organized in the following way. In Section 2 we provide the conceptual framework and discuss the relative research issues. In Section 3 we discuss our research methodology and data collection. The empirical models, data analysis and results are discussed in section four. Finally, we conclude and highlight some directions for future research in Section 5.

## 2. Conceptual framework

Many organizations have extensively deployed Internet and Intranet solutions to aid their marketing function. Budget for Internet and Intranet based marketing solutions exceed several million dollars in large corporations. Some of these applications include sales force training, online advertising, online publishing, support for product information and pricing queries, electronic links with the distributor and the use of electronic media for product support. More recently, firms have begun to implement their order management systems on the Internet and respective Intranets. In short, from a marketing perspective, firms have deployed electronic commerce solutions to coordinate sales, market intelligence gathering, and customer service to improve overall efficiency in terms of reduced cost and product development time. Customer satisfaction for these solutions will be determined by customer’s perception of the business value delivered by these systems.

However firms are often faced with the challenge of identifying and quantifying the benefit from these electronic commerce applications. This challenge stems from the nature of new Intranet applications. Traditionally customized information systems solutions were designed to merely automate existing business processes and hence it was easier to estimate potential efficiency benefits in advance. In this case, standard measures of economic benefits such as the return on investments was suitable. However recent information technology applications such as Internet and Intranet based E-commerce solutions not only automate the existing processes but also provide new capabilities that were not possible with the existing business processes. For example, instantaneous access to data collected on customer visits to a firm’s website may lead to effective micromarketing strategies and enable firms to move to a new position in the competitive landscape. Hence pure accounting measures such as return on investment may not be appropriate to assess the value of new Intranet applications.

As noted earlier, our focus in this study is on the determinants of customer satisfaction for Intranet solutions. Since overall customer satisfaction with Intranet solutions may be directly linked to the perceived business value delivered by these systems, we identify various sources of business value from information technology applications noted in the literature in our conceptual framework depicted in Fig. 1.

Our framework is grounded in the theoretical model for assessing business value for IT investments provided in the literature. Applegate 1995 ,Ž . identifies three categories of value from information technology applications: business process performance improvements; individual<sup>r</sup>workgroup productivity and decision quality improvements; and competitive advantage delivered by the system. In line with this model, we hypothesize that customer’s overall satisfaction with the Intranet marketing solution is determined by satisfaction with cost of ownership, competitive advantage and process productivity gains. We next discuss these three determinants and the constructs within them.

Customer’s perceived satisfaction with the total cost of ownership in our study is measured in terms of the maintainability and usability of the system. It has been reported that the cost incurred in supporting information systems may exceed even the cost of developing the system. In custom information system applications, these costs could even exceed the development cost by over 200% 2 . This increase in<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> support costs is from increased system maintenance due to the defects and frequent changes requested by the users. The increase in support costs has also been observed more recently in packaged software. Thus in order to control the total cost of ownership in Intranet marketing solutions, the systems should be easy to maintain and should involve lower support costs.

The importance of ease of use and usability of information technology applications in determining system usage and acceptance has been extensively documented in the literature 8,20 . Since Intranet<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> marketing applications may change user interface to even basic daily operations such as accessing customer database, marketing and sales information, a number of employees may need to be trained in using the new system. If the system is not designed with features that are easy to learn, the training costs could be substantially higher. Hence we include customer’s satisfaction with the required training in our cost of ownership construct. We next discuss competitive advantage benefits from Intranet solutions.

Firms adopt electronic commerce solutions on both Internet and Intranets to gain strategic advantage over its competitors. Since the cost of implementing such systems could be substantial, the investments are often justified based on significant benefits that are expected from these systems 1,12 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Thus we expect that the customer’s perception of value delivered by the system to enhance competitive advantage may significantly impact overall customer satisfaction with the system. However, the notion of competitive benefits or value from information technology can be viewed at various levels ranging from enhanced performance of operational activities in each function such as marketing or production to firm level performance measures such as increased profitability 4 . Since the primary motivation behind Intranet marketing solutions is to enhance marketing performance, as noted in Fig. 1, we focus on strategic competitive benefits such as increase in market share and added revenues from penetration into new markets. We hypothesize that customers’ satisfaction with the competitive value measured in increased market share and added revenues will enhance overall customer satisfaction with the system. We next discuss the process productivity construct in our framework.

Firms view Intranet solutions as process improvement tools and believe that Intranet applications streamline information access by a larger group through a common interface. Since Intranet solutions improve consistency of data and facilitate easier access, the operating business processes using these applications are believed to contribute to both the efficiency and effectiveness of the business process. A recent survey conducted by a leading consulting firm on the organizational impact of Intranets indicates significant agreement among those surveyed, that the organizations’ Intranets are a valuable personnel productivity tool regardless of the geographical locations of the companies or the markets they serve Informationweek, December, 1997 . Person- Ž . nel productivity benefits from various information technology applications are also widely addressed in the literature 9 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

As depicted in Fig. 1, in our framework, satisfaction with the organizational productivity delivered by the system is defined as a composite measure of satisfaction with the respective operating process improvements and satisfaction with the productivity gains at the individual and group level in the organization. Our conceptual model identifies the drivers of overall customer satisfaction with corporate Intranet. From an Intranet solution provider’s perspective, an interesting issue is the differences in relative importance of satisfaction with total cost of ownership, productivity improvement and competitive value.

## 3. Research site and data

Our research site is one of the leading firms in providing electronic commerce applications and solutions to large corporations. A significant portion of the firm’s business is in building customized Intranet applications for the business enterprise. As a part of continued effort to provide efficient electronic commerce solutions to meet customer needs and improve customer satisfaction, the firm has initiated various quality programs in the recent past. Our study was a part of such an initiative to understand the drivers of overall customer satisfaction with Intranet marketing solutions.

In the first phase of this study, based on customer interviews, the specific factors that are viewed as important in determining overall customer satisfaction with Intranet solutions were identified. In this phase senior personnel responsible for information systems purchasing in five sites were contacted. Open-ended questions were asked to gain insights on the factors perceived important. At the conclusion of this phase, six factors were identified. Subsequent grouping of these factors revealed three constructs namely productivity, competitive value and total cost of ownership.

These six factors were included in a survey questionnaire designed for this study. The questionnaire was pre-tested and the questions were revised for clarity and format in two phases. In the first phase, the questionnaire was pilot tested within the organization. Based on the feedback, the questions were revised for clarity. Since the study was primarily addressed for Intranet solutions in marketing, in the second phase, the questionnaire was pre-tested with a group of marketing managers for consistency in interpretation and the questions were revised again based on this feedback. The final survey was conducted over the telephone to ensure high response rate and short turnaround time. Before getting their ratings, customers were carefully and consistently explained the meaning of the six factors and overall customer satisfaction. The survey was administered to a randomly selected sample of 42 large business enterprises. Complete survey responses were obtained from over 70% of the sample customers. A copy of this survey disguised to preserve confiden- Ž tiality is shown in the Appendix..

The specific measures for overall satisfaction and the six factors in our analysis are described below.

## 3.1. OÕerall customer satisfaction OS( )

The overall satisfaction is a composite measure of satisfaction with how well the system meets customer needs and the overall satisfaction with the ability of the system to deliver important sales and marketing information for the firm. In the survey, respondents were asked to provide a response score for each of these measures on a ten-point scale ranging from 1 ‘Very low’ to 10 ‘Very high’. Ž . Ž . The two measures were correlated and a factor analysis revealed that the two measures loaded on a single factor. For ease of interpretation, overall customer satisfaction OS with the Intranet system was Ž . computed by taking the average of the scores on the two measures.

## 3.2. Cost of ownership CO( )

Total cost of ownership is an important issue in the management of any information technology. The business value and productivity gains delivered by a technology solution may be offset by enormous costs incurred in maintaining and updating the system. In addition, ease of use is an important attribute in determining system acceptance. As noted earlier, for an Intranet system with complex user interface and functionality, the cost of training new users may be substantial. In our analysis, we measure customer’s satisfaction with cost of ownership as a composite measure of satisfaction with ease of maintenance and user training costs.

## 3.3. CompetitiÕe business Õalue CBV( )

The ultimate business motive for deploying any information system is to improve business process productivity and derive competitive business value <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 1 . For example, in automated manufacturing shop floor systems it may be expected that the system will improve information flow in the manufacturing process and possibly lead to reduction in product development cycle-time and provide competitive advantage to the business enterprise 4 . Since our analysis<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> entails the study of marketing information systems, we measure customer’s satisfaction with competitive business value delivered by the system as a composite measure of satisfaction with increased market share and growth in new business revenues. An interesting finding in our study is that we do not find any influence of satisfaction with process productivity gains on the overall satisfaction with the Intranet system. As depicted in Fig. 1, in our model, satisfaction with process productivity gains is a composite measure of satisfaction with operating process performance and satisfaction with improvements in individual<sup>r</sup>group productivity. One reason for this may be that firms are more interested in bottom line benefits from the system than intermediate values such as personnel or group productivity. It is also possible that firms do not have the right policies in place for the usage of Intranet services and hence personnel productivity gains may not be viewed as a significant determinant of overall satisfaction. For example, a recent survey on Intranet usage has indicated that the respondents placed task and project implementation fifth, trailing other uses of Intranet systems such as the ability to access company stock price, calculating the value of personal retirement funds, and viewing job openings. Thus efficient management of the information content and resources are critical for productivity outcomes from Intranet solutions. In order to reap productivity gains from Intranet solutions, the systems need to be designed such that it encourages employees to use applications that are aligned with corporate preferences. However the lack of significance of a process productivity effect in our analysis may also be due to the small sample in our analysis and subjective measurement of personnel productivity gains.

## 5. Conclusion

In this research we have reported an empirical study that identifies the drivers of customer satisfaction for Intranet marketing application systems. Our analysis based on data collected from a survey of over thirty firms that are in the process of building Intranet applications indicates that customers perceive features that lower total cost of ownership and provide competitive benefits as critical drivers of overall customer satisfaction with the systems.

Our results provide insights for Intranet solution vendors in their product development and design decisions. There is a typical tendency for such vendors to focus on ‘core product features’ as a means of differentiating their products in the market place. Rather our findings suggest that vendors should attempt to focus on the ‘whole product’ by augmenting their core product with features and services that lower the total cost of ownership rather than just the initial purchase value of Intranet systems 17 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

A notable limitation in our analysis is that we have addressed only the perceived benefits of Intranet solutions. A further challenge is to assess the business value from Intranet and other electronic commerce solutions quantitatively. We plan to address the quantifiable business value from Intranet systems in our future research. A successful integration of the Intranet into the business processes and culture of an organization will depend on establishing a broad level of ownership and commitment. The evolution and direction of Intranet applications should not remain a responsibility of the information technology group within organizations but involve a broader forum including senior executives from multiple disciplines. In addition, this evolution should also identify the quantitative metrics for assessing the business value from these systems at the appropriate level of process changes by these applications <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> <sub>4</sub> <sub>.</sub>

One of the critical steps in the success of Intranet solutions is getting the management to understand the concept on a holistic basis. Senior management in organizations should be convinced that an Intranet application is an empowering technology and may succeed only if all the users are allowed to access and share the appropriate information across corporate networks. However this openness to information sharing should be supported with security controls and access permissions for confidential data. In addition, management may need to ensure that the Intranets are being used by the employees for the right applications that will add value to the firm. In summary, answers to questions such as how to keep track of information or what kind of management rules to impose for use of Intranet systems are important and should be addressed in future research.
