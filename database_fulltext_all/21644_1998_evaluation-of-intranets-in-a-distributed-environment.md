# Evaluation of intranets in a distributed environment

## Abstract

The capture and dissemination of intra-organizational information is widely recognized to be essential for supporting operational and strategic corporate decision-making. Intranet technology has proven to be effective in accessing and disseminating intra-organizational data and knowledge, yet because of the novelty of intranets, very little research has been conducted to understand this technology in terms of its planning, management, and implementation. The objective of this study is to evaluate the role of intranet technology in a distributed environment with respect to its contributions to interconnectivity, information dissemination, process reengineering, organizational performance, and team collaboration. q 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

Keywords: Intranet; Internet; Network management; Distributed processing

## 1. Introduction

Decision-making in organizations is becoming increasingly difficult due to the need to process a large amount of data across organizational divisions and departments. Although data warehousing and digital library techniques have been developed to address this problem, their capabilities are limited to organizing dispersed enterprise data into data repositories <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 4 . Intranets offer an alternative to integrating dispersed enterprise data for decision support. Besides using intranets to integrate individual, group, departmental, and corporate communication and information-sharing tasks, business managers are also exploring strategic uses for this technology to enhance the competitive positions of their organizations.

While intranets are rapidly proliferating in organizations, very little empirical research has been conducted to study intranet-based applications and their impact on intra-organizational data processing activities. As suggested by Raho et al. 25 , control over<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> the adoption and diffusion of information technology Ž . IT within an organization is a key to effective IT management. It is, therefore, critical to assess the assimilation of intranets within the organization. This includes the identification and prioritization of managerial and technical issues associated with the adoption and implementation of intranets. This is a complex process involving the participation of several individuals and units within the organization. Management must investigate formal and informal strategies to evaluate the suitability of relocating corporate data in existing databases to web pages. Although the technology needed to address the incompatibility of conventional databases with web repositories is already available, IS professionals are still unclear about the organizational changes associated with intranet implementation. This is especially true with regard to the ‘what’ and ‘how’ of reengineering existing business processes using intranets. It is believed that a guideline should exist to advise intranet adopters of how to redesign business processes with intranet technology. Even though these issues about the implementation and adoption of intranets are important, they have not been widely discussed and investigated.

For a new IT innovation to be adopted and successfully diffused throughout an organization, it must make a significant contribution to the competitiveness of the organization. Studies such as Refs.Ž <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 11,19 have already predicted the rapid adoption of. intranets among business organizations in the next few years. Is this rapid adoption merely a result of the cost-effectiveness and efficiency of intranets? Are there other factors that promote the widespread application of intranets? A review of the literature suggests that in addition to enhancing transmission efficiency, network technology also provides a critical tool for seizing new business opportunities, improving the productivity and quality of work life and gaining a competitive edge in the marketplace 28 . Hence, management must learn how to harness intranet technology to strategically deploy applications that provide a competitive advantage and enhance organizational performance. Unfortunately, the evaluation of the impact of intranet technology on organizational performance and competitiveness is still lacking, especially in a real world setting.

It has been suggested that an intranet can be used as a groupware tool due to its intrinsic value as an open standard network 7,9,15 . Intranets can in- <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> crease collaboration among individuals in an organization through the creation of networks of shared spaces that facilitate common understanding 18 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> Corporate employees can use intranets to share knowledge and expertise across distance and time using both structured and unstructured information repositories. However, the original intent of intranets was not for team collaboration, but for data sharing. Hence, it trails groupware products in supporting advanced features that facilitate collaboration. While some studies 3,4 support intranets’ effects on col-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> laboration among adopting organizations, others 30 have found no substantial effect. Hence, it is important to further explore this issue in order to shed light on the effectiveness of intranets and their role in team collaboration.

While many organizations have already set up, or are in the process of implementing intranets, others are still grappling with fundamental questions about the implementation and adoption of this new technology. Most research on intranets in the IS discipline today is conceptual and<sup>r</sup>or anecdotal in nature. No prior research has attempted to collect empirical data from intranet-implementers that would synthesize their experiences. Such a synthesis is required to guide adopting organizations in the planning, control, implementation, and evaluation of this new technology. Our research fills this gap by investigating important technological and managerial issues in the adoption, the implementation, and the impact of intranets on organizations. We have used data collected from intranet-implementers as the basis for addressing these issues. Based on a review of prior research in this area, we have identified the following research questions associated with intranet implementation.

Ž . 1 What are some of the critical issues, both technical and managerial, in adopting intranet technology?

Ž . 2 What is the role of intranets in disseminating corporate data in a distributed processing environment?

Ž . 3 Have organizations reengineered their business processes while designing and implementing intranets? If so, do intranets play a role as a process improvement tool?

Ž . 4 What is the impact of intranet-based applications on organizational performance? Do intranets affect the business relationship of an organization with its customers? Do they influence the work habits and<sup>r</sup>or performances of employees?

Ž . 5 Are intranets used as a strategic tool to support distributed collaboration among employees at the department, division, or corporate level?

## 2. Research method

## 2.1. Instrumentation

The major objectives of this research are to explore the managerial and technical issues involved with the implementation of intranets and to study the impact of intranets on organizational and individual performances. Two survey instruments were developed for this purpose: technical and usage. The technical instrument was designed to gather data on intranet hardware, software, design, and implementation. It was targeted at IS personnel responsible for intranet implementation. The usage instrument was designed to gather data on intranet use, experience, and feedback. This instrument focused on intranet users. Data for this study was collected primarily through the use of five-point Likert scale items, based on the extent of agreement or disagreement with specific issues related to the variables being measured. The aggregation of scores over all of the questions relative to a particular variable provided the composite score of that variable.

## 2.2. Data collection

Data for this study was collected in Hong Kong, a leading telecommunications hub in the Asia-Pacific region. This city has advanced telecommunications and network infrastructures that provide flexible and innovative network and communication services. The policy makers of Hong Kong believe that telecommunications technologies are vital to sustaining the city’s open economy. Without an effective and efficient telecommunications infrastructure, the city would lose its preeminence as an international center for the financial and banking sector 29 . Due to the <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> favorable telecommunications environment, Internet technology is rapidly proliferating in Hong Kong. In conjunction with this trend, organizations are making strategic use of Internet-related technologies to support their intra- and inter-organizational information and document dissemination activities. Because of such a high concentration of Internet users in Hong Kong, we believed that the city was likely to have a large population of intranet users who would form the basis of our research.

A two-stage data collection plan was used. The first stage involved identifying target companies for the study. The IS administrators of the five hundred largest companies in Hong Kong were identified from Thornton’s Guide to Hong Kong Companies <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 27 . These executives were directly contacted by telephone to determine the status of intranet development in their respective companies and the reasons for their decisions to adopt or reject the technology. A total of 41 companies were found to have implemented intranets. The IS administrators of these companies were invited to participate in the survey. Twenty-three out of the 41 companies agreed to take part in the study.

The second stage involved the collection of data from the participating companies. Five technical and 30 usage questionnaires were mailed to each IS administrator in the 23 participating companies. The technical questionnaires were distributed to IS personnel involved in intranet design and implementation. The usage questionnaires were given to the managers of functional departments. The companies that did not return the surveys within the first 3 weeks were contacted over the phone, and a second set of questionnaires was mailed. At the end of 8 weeks, 470 responses 58.4% from the participating Ž . companies were returned. Of these, 64 were returned by IS personnel and 406 by departmental intranet users. The responses were almost evenly distributed among responding companies; the maximum number of surveys returned by any responding company was 27 and the minimum was 14. After a careful review of the returned questionnaires, 17 responses were found to be invalid. This resulted in 61 valid responses from IS personnel and 392 from intranet users.

## 2.3. Validity and reliability

The first step in the validity and reliability testing was to derive relevant validated survey questions from past research. These were supplemented with new items designed for the unique purpose of this research. The preliminary instruments were then pre-tested extensively by several business faculty members with research interests in the areas of MIS, technology management, electronic commerce, business process reengineering, and Internet<sup>r</sup>intranet. The feedback from this step was used to revise the structure, content, and design of the instruments. The revised instruments were then administered to five senior IS administrators in charge of intranet development and management in selected companies in Hong Kong. Their comments and suggestions were carefully reviewed and selectively incorporated in the final instruments. This multi-stage process of instrument development resulted in significant improvement in the questionnaires and provided support for content validity of an empirical study.

The analyses of this research were based primarily on descriptive statistical results. We also used MANOVA to evaluate the impact of intranets on collaboration. Hence, the instruments used in this study were further tested for various validity and reliability properties to ensure that valid and reliable measures were being used. First, Cronbach’s alpha was employed to analyze the variables for measurement reliability. The results showed that the reliability of variables ranged from 0.82 to 0.84 Table 5 .Ž . This is significantly higher than the 0.5–0.6 range suggested by Nunnally 21 for the early stages of<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> basic research. Second, principal-component analysis was performed to test the construct validity of the instrument. Kaiser’s rule was applied to remove principal components with eigenvalues less than one. Only those items with factor loadings larger than 0.5 were used in the study. The findings indicated that all measurement items were loaded in accordance with a priori expectations.

The largest group of respondents was drawn from the finance and trade industries, representing more than 43% of the responding companies. Fig. 2 summarizes the job titles of the IS respondents and Fig. 3 depicts the affiliated departments of intranet users. Almost all of the IS respondents occupied senior managerial and<sup>r</sup>or technical positions in their respective organizations, each with at least 7-years of computing experience. This enhanced the quality of the data collected for the study. ## 3. Results

## 3.1. Implementation issues

The implementation of intranets can vary from a simple LAN-linked HTML file to a full-featured system with multiple, dedicated servers and complex group-support capabilities 5,6,10,24 . Their associ-<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> ated implementation problems and issues also vary according to differences in technical complexity. After all, changing the way information is managed, organized, and distributed over the network can potentially change the nature of data-access and affect the overall network performance. Feedback from early adopters on implementation problems and issues can provide valuable input for late adopters who are developing implementation plans. The survey instrument included a list of potential problems and issues in the implementation of intranets 1,2,<sup>w</sup> 8,13,14 . The respondents were asked to evaluate the<sup>x</sup> significance of these issues based on their implementation experience. The data, thus collected, was factor analyzed to determine major problems that require close observation during intranet adoption and implementation. ## 3.1.1. Factor 1 or network factor

Establishing an efficient network infrastructure to support intranets seems to be a major problem to be overcome for successful intranet implementation. Many organizations were unfamiliar with the TCP<sup>r</sup>IP protocol. They did not have the knowledge or skills necessary to optimize their networks using already available Internet technologies. Their primary concerns were bandwidth, performance, interconnection, and security issues after the intranet installation.

## 3.1.2. Factor 2 or software factor

Some of the software problems associated with intranet implementation are hard to address. The conversion of legacy applications to utilize IP networks is considered a major problem. Although solutions already have been developed to resolve this problem, many computer professionals still consider such conversion a difficult task requiring advanced planning. Software issues with regard to the coordination of homepage development, especially when multiple workgroups and servers are used, were found to be difficult to manage.

## 3.1.3. Factor 3 or IT maturity factor

The IT maturity and sophistication level of an organization can determine the difficulty of intranet implementation. Companies with less sophisticated IT structures encounter implementation problems due to inadequate technological resources, lack of experienced network staff, and insufficient knowledge and awareness of intranet capabilities.

## 3.1.4. Factor 4 or management factor

Managing intranets represents another challenge for network management. These management issues include managing intranet growth, data ownership, data contents, and intranet workgroup coordination. Strategies must be formulated to ensure appropriate network planning, control, and management that will support the use of intranets within the organization.

Many of our respondents believed that the problems encountered in intranet implementation are experience-related. These are likely to be resolved as the technology becomes more mature and widespread. It is also necessary to develop normative guidelines to facilitate the planning, implementation, and management of intranets. Developing network management tools to assist in these tasks was also suggested as a means to overcome these problems.

## 3.2. Intranet data

A major benefit of intranet technology derives from its role as an on-line, on-demand information dissemination tool. Using intranets, an organization can transform a calendar-based document publishing strategy to one that is need-based 22 . This transformation can reduce the cost of information dissemination dramatically, while also improving its timeliness. Table 2 lists the types of information disseminated through an intranet by different departments in an organization. Sales<sup>r</sup>marketing and human resources departments appear to make the most use of intranets as information disseminating tools. These departments post a wide variety of information on intranets, as listed in Table 2. Most of this information includes announcements and is trade-related. Intranets are rarely used for sharing a wider variety and more in-depth organizational data. Organizations can reap the full benefits of intranet technology by enabling employees to access organizational information that supports their daily activities. A lack of experience in organizations with intranet technology, as well as the security risks associated with the networked environment, could be some of the reasons why organizations are hesitant to post sensitive business information on their intranets. This situation may change with time as companies gain experience in using intranets.

## 3.3. Intranet technology to support business process reengineering BPR( )

BPR is a powerful change procedure and an approach that has made radical, fundamental changes in the ways that organizations conduct business. The purpose of these changes is to redesign the existing business processes and to implement new ones that will result in reduced costs and improved efficiency and effectiveness. Researchers such as Refs. 12,16 Ž <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>. concur that IT plays a critical role in BPR. They believe that IT can be used to improve organizational performance by eliminating delays, administrative intermediaries, and redundant processing steps and by providing better access to information. Some BPR researchers even claim that the role of IT should not be restricted only to efficiency enhancements, but should evolve to become a fundamental enabler that creates and maintains a flexible business network of inter-organizational systems 26 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

Intranets can significantly change the information organization and dissemination strategy used by an organization and thus enable process reengineering. At the very least, intranets allow users to take responsibility for creating and maintaining their own data. Implementing this new strategy requires major changes in the skills of the employees, the ownership of organizational data, the design of IT architecture, and the organizational culture of sharing and collaboration. Such implementation may also threaten the status quo in organizational politics, especially when sensitive information is shared through intranets. Organizations that have used intranets in process reengineering indicate that intranets not only offer an excellent technology platform for redesigning information management processes but also are useful as a tool to support process reengineering 17 . <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

While traveling the sales person can use intranets to access the latest information on products and promotions. Such natural applications of intranets explain the predominant use of this technology to reengineer business processes in sales and marketing. Other core business areas in organizations appear to be taking a more conservative approach to using intranets to reengineer business processes. Our study indicates that intranets play a minor role in reengineering communication processes among customers, workgroups, and individuals. This finding contradicts the current belief among researchers that the information-sharing and collaboration capabilities of intranets will break down the traditional communication patterns of knowledge workers 4 . However, our findings indicate that this transformation has not yet happened. One possible explanation is that intranets are a relatively new technology. They require more time to spread among knowledge workers. As the technology usage matures and becomes more widely accepted, its role in reengineering communication processes within organizations is likely to increase.

## 3.4. Intranet impacts

Intranets are growing rapidly in every sector of business. They offer a standardized interface to heterogeneous processing platforms and are relatively less expensive to operate and implement. Combined with their potential use when connected with Internet technology, these strengths could possibly make intranets the most exciting development for commerce in this decade. The infusion of intranets could have a significant impact on performance enhancement within adopting organizations 23 , especially in the <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> areas of organizational performance, employee performance, and customer relationships 26 .<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>

The findings shown in Table 4 indicate that intranets have an overall positive impact on organizational performance, employee performance, and customer relationships. Respondents agreed that intranets had improved their company’s image and communication processes. They also agreed that intranets allow better utilization of resources, thus reducing operational costs in the long run. They felt that intranets enhanced employee productivity and power. With regard to customer relationship, respondents generally believed that intranets foster a more short-term market-like relationship. They believed that intranets allow their customers immediate access to organizational data and documents. This results in better customer relationship by fulfilling the information need of the customer. However, not all aspects associated with intranets are satisfying. Network security is considered a major concern when the company’s information repository becomes accessible to external customers 20 . If communication technologies can have a strong influence on how people interact and collaborate, it will be necessary to investigate the impact of intranets on collaborative work among organizational members in a distributed environment. There are several ways to determine the degree or level of collaboration and effectiveness in a group. This study builds on the work by Vandenbosch and Ginzberg <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 30 by adopting their departmental, divisional, and corporate scales to measure the degree of collaboration within an organization. MANOVA analysis was employed to investigate whether intranets have any impact on group collaboration before and after their implementation. All collaboration variables in the analysis were tested with Cronbach’s alpha and factor analysis for measurement reliability. MANOVA analysis was performed after ensuring the reliability and the validity of the instrument.

The results, in Table 5, indicate that there was no significant change in the level of departmental, divisional, or corporate collaboration before or after the use of intranets. This finding is consistent with the inference made earlier concerning a lack of use of intranets to reengineer communication processes. It is surprising to find that users do not use intranets to modify their collaboration and communication processes, even though this technology is considered to be useful and efficient. We believe this phenomenon is caused by a lack of understanding of the potential of intranet technology, which is in the early phase of adoption. Inadequate diffusion, training, and promotion of intranets among network and computer users are also possible causes.

## 4. Discussion and conclusions

Although intranets are predicted to become a dominant network technology in the immediate future, our study did not find intranets to be widely infused in the computing environment of many organizations in Hong Kong. Only 8.2% of organizations surveyed were reported to be adopters of intranets. The technology is used primarily for sharing internal documents and data among employees and customers. The study did not show evidence of a serious use of intranets to disseminate critical organizational information. This result suggests that, in spite of the strategic importance of keeping pace with technological advances, the ability of most businesses to acquire and implement new information technologies lags behind the available opportunities. Therefore, management must establish strategic policies to keep pace with new technological developments and to facilitate their assimilation. This is likely to remain a significant challenge for years to come.

Even though intranets provide a technologically viable platform to implement enterprise-wide computing and decision support, the full realization of this possibility could be several steps away. Our study indicates that intranet technology is still in its infancy as an information dissemination tool. The technology must first find widespread user acceptance and be used for sharing serious business information before it can be used for decision support.

Intranet technology has the potential to change business processes, especially those involving communication and information exchange. This technology is increasingly viewed as a facilitator of business process reengineering. However, we did not find widespread use of this technology in process reengineering. Businesses seem to be taking a conservative approach to technology assimilation, preferring a gradual implementation to radical process changes. A drawback of this implementation philosophy is that businesses miss the opportunity of exploiting the full potential of a new technology by reengineering old processes. Unfortunately, this implementation approach is also identifiable in many US corporations. This raises an important issue, for management in general and IS management in particular, about new technology adoption strategies. Should organizations take a conservative or a radical approach to new technology adoption and implementation? The former approach would overlay current business processes with the technology. This requires fewer organizational changes but has a smaller return in terms of performance gain. The latter approach enables organizations to reevaluate their business processes in light of the new technology and redesign them, if necessary. It may create major changes in the organization and therefore, requires proper planning and careful implementation. This approach enables the organization to reap the full benefits of a new technology.

Interestingly, we did not find intranets being used to support collaborative efforts in organizations. This indicates that the potential of intranets as a collaboration tool has not been exploited by organizations. This could be possible due to a lack of understanding of the functional capabilities of the technology by the users or an absence of incentive for learning and using such new technologies. IS managers should strive to create an environment where information resource utilization is an active rather than a reactive component of the organizational culture. To achieve this, care must be taken to increase user awareness of a new technology and its potential applications by encouraging innovative use of the technology. Commitment of resources for training and education will increase user familiarity with new technology applications substantially. Such training will also reduce improper and unproductive uses of a technology, which is not uncommon during the diffusion process.
