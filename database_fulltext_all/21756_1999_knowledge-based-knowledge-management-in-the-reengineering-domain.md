## Abstract

A fundamental problem with knowledge management is the information technology IT employed to enable knowledgeŽ . work appears to target data and information, as opposed to knowledge itself. In contrast, knowledge-based systems KBSŽ . maintain an explicit and direct focus on knowledge. The research described in this article is focused on innovating knowledge management through KBS technology. We refer to this KBS-enabled transformation of knowledge work as knowledge-based knowledge management. Drawing from the recent literature, we identify a number of key activities associated with knowledge management to establish a set of requirements for knowledge management support. We match these requirements with textbook capabilities of intelligent systems and use this analysis to evaluate KOPeR, a KBS employed to automate and support knowledge management in the reengineering domain. We find KOPeR possesses the capabilities required for knowledge management support. And its field application, as part of a major reengineering engagement, reveals four important knowledge effects enabled by this KBS. From this study, we also find KOPeR to be effective in its automation and support of key knowledge management activities. And through its successful use and knowledge effects in this study, we conclude that KBS can be developed and employed for effective knowledge management support. Published by Elsevier Science B.V.

Knowledge organization can be accomplished at several levels, including those of the ontology, model and knowledgebase, among others. An ontology provides the basis for knowledge representation and includes explicit definitions of and associations between those aspects of the world that exist and are important for modeling in a particular domain. Models such as procedural sequences, decision-making flowcharts, process flows, hierarchies and taxonomies are often developed as intermediate knowledge structures to organize and inter-relate high-level areas of knowledge within an ontology. ## 3. KOPeR system

KOPeR is a proof-of-concept KBS developed to automate and support knowledge-work activities associated with process redesign, the key analytical phase of the BPR Life Cycle 29 . Reengineering represents a very knowledge-intensive activity, particularly the key analytical and intellectual work required for effective process redesign see Ref.Ž <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> 44 . Before conceptualization and development of systems such as KOPeR, reengineering expertise was predominately tacit, residing almost exclusively in the minds of external BPR consultants. By examining the KOPeR implementation in this section, we gain valuable insight into how this and other KBS can be developed to support knowledge management.

As a KBS, KOPeR directly addresses the knowledge required for effective redesign performance and thereby offers the potential to support knowledge management in the reengineering domain. KOPeR builds on the process ontology first developed by King and Scacchi 31 . ## 3.2.1. Pathology diagnosis

Pathology diagnosis represents a principal KOPeR function. This function is predicated on the taxonomy of process pathologies, which formalizes some of the deep reengineering knowledge required for process redesign. As noted above, before the knowledge engineering employed to develop KOPeR, such reengineering knowledge was predominately tacit, residing almost exclusively in the minds of external BPR consultants. The idea is to leverage the taxonomic knowledge structure with rules to detect and classify a variety of common process pathologies. The taxonomy is constructed from the BPR literature, as classes and instances of pathologies are synthesized from the various process problems and shortcomings noted in expert reengineering methodologies see Ref. 42 . These include expert-levelŽ <sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup>. works that can be seen in Refs. 3,9,23,25,28 and<sup>w</sup> <sup>x</sup> others. By ‘‘knowledge distribution and application,’’ we mean the ability to make expert-level knowledge available to non-experts throughout the enterprise. By ‘‘analytical consistency,’’ we mean the reliability and traceability of ‘‘consultant’’ analyses and recommendations across various redesign instances, projects or engagements. For example, management says it prefers consistent reasoning and analysis for the redesign of different processes — regardless whether conducted by the same or different individuals — and it demands consistency between successive redesigns of the same process at different times Ž . e.g., successive redesign iterations . Because KOPeR employs a known and documented knowledgebase Ž . e.g., rule set, taxonomies, process ontology , it follows the same, understandable and explainable reasoning steps regardless of the specific process being redesigned e.g., small or large, simple or complexŽ . and produces the same results when redesigning any particular process at different points in time. ## 5. Conclusions and future research

Knowledge management has become very important for enterprises in today’s economic environment. But knowledge is proving difficult to manage, and knowledge work has been stubbornly resistant to reengineering. A fundamental problem is the IT employed to enable knowledge work appears to target data and information, as opposed to knowledge itself. Yet knowledge is noted as being quite distinct from data and information. In contrast, KBS maintain an explicit and direct focus on knowledge. By directly addressing knowledge as such, KBS offer good potential to complement existing IT through automation and support of key knowledge management activities. The research described in this article is focused on innovating knowledge management through KBS technology. We specify, develop and employ a KBS as enabling IT to automate and support key knowledge-work process activities and refer to this KBS-enabled transformation of knowledge work as knowledge-based knowledge management.

Drawing from the recent literature, we identify a number of key activities associated with knowledge management and use these activities to establish a set of requirements for knowledge management support. We match these requirements with textbook capabilities of KBS and use this analysis to evaluate KOPeR, a KBS employed to automate and support knowledge management in the reengineering domain. We find KOPeR possesses the capabilities required for effective knowledge management support. And its field application, as part of a major reengineering engagement, reveals four knowledge effects enabled by this KBS: 1 knowledge capture,Ž . organization and formalization, 2 knowledge distri-Ž . bution and application, 3 analytical consistency and Ž . completeness, and 4 knowledge integration. From Ž . this study, we also find KOPeR to be effective in its automation and support of key redesign activities applied to the procurement process. And through its successful field use and knowledge effects in this study, we conclude that KBS can indeed be developed and employed to automate and support knowledge management.

More generally, KOPeR is representative of many KBS. As examples, its inference is based on straightforward production rules, its twin knowledge taxonomies are patterned after a well-known KBS exemplar called the Articulator, and its process models conform to a versatile and extensible process ontology. And in textbook KBS fashion, KOPeR directly addresses the knowledge required for effective knowledge-work process performance. The representative nature of KOPeR suggests the results of this investigation may generalize well to other classes of knowledge work and KBS applications, as knowledge management may also benefit from effective KBS support in domains beyond reengineering. This represents a topic for future research along the lines of this investigation.

Future empirical research may also extend beyond the qualitative methods used for analysis in the present investigation. For example, future work may employ survey or quasi-experimental research designs to examine the relative efficacy of knowledgebased knowledge management with that of conventional, human- and paper-based methods. Through such research, we may find certain classes of knowledge-work processes lend themselves more to KBS support than others and use the findings to develop design guidelines for knowledge management DSS. We may also investigate the comparative efficiency of KBS- vs. human-based knowledge management, comparing the relative cost to establish, manage and maintain an enterprise program for knowledge management, for example.

Future research may also lead to useful hybrid systems that integrate KBS like KOPeR with extant DBMS, data warehouses, intranets<sup>r</sup>extranets, groupware and other systems. And many other DSS techniques e.g., KBS model management and technolo- Ž . gies e.g., machine learning algorithms may alsoŽ . offer good potential for integration with the kind of rule-based system described in this investigation. If Drucker’s predictions become manifest, and knowledge indeed becomes the key economic resource and the dominant — and perhaps even the only — source of comparative advantage, research along the lines of this investigation will be essential to understanding the phenomenon of knowledge management from an information systems perspective and avoid the design of knowledge management systems by trial and error.

Mark Nissen is Assistant Professor of Information Systems and Acquisition Management at the Naval Postgraduate School. His research focuses on the investigation of knowledge systems for enabling and managing change in areas such as process innovation, electronic commerce and knowledge management. Recently, he has been investigating knowledge systems to innovate processes in the acquisition domain and is currently involved with intelligent ac-

quisition agents. Before his information systems doctoral work at the University of Southern California, he acquired over a dozen years’ management experience in the aerospace and electronics industry and served as a Supply Officer in the Naval Reserve.
