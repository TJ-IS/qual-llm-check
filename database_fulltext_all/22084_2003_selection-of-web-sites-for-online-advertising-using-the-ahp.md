# Selection of web sites for online advertising using the AHP

E.W.T. Ngai $^{*}$

Department of Management, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Hung Hom, Kowloon, Hong Kong, PR China

Received 22 June 2001; received in revised form 29 June 2001; accepted 31 December 2001

## Abstract

This paper presents an application of the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) used to select the best web site for online advertising. This method adopts a multi-criteria approach that can be used for analysis and comparison of web sites for online advertising. The method is based on pairwise comparison between several factors that affect the selection of the best web site. Five criteria were used for evaluating sites: impression rate, monthly cost, audience fit, content quality, and “look and feel.” For each, a matrix of pairwise comparisons between web sites was evaluated. Although our application was for only one specific company, there is nothing unique about the environment of the case study, and thus this approach could be generalised to any company. We believe the application shown could be of use to managers and, because of its ease of implementation, others could benefit from this approach.

## 1. Introduction

The Internet (Net) industry is experiencing extraordinary growth. It, and more particularly the World Wide Web (Web), appears to be an ideal medium for businesses attempting to promote themselves and their wares $[5]$ . Local businessmen and managers are beginning to recognise the value of the web for both local and global communications and marketing tools $[11]$ . Meeker's “the Internet advertising report” $[21]$ states that his study shows that the Net is the fastest growing medium in history. A report entitled “the local commerce monitor” by the Kelsey Group and ConStat Inc., shows that the web has passed direct mail as an advertising and promotion medium for business and is closing in on newspaper usage $[1]$ . According to Forrester Research, global spending for online advertising will reach US\$ 33 billion by 2004, one-third of which will be spent outside the US. The report further indicates that, in Europe, online advertising spending will total US\$ 5.5 billion in 2004, representing 5.1% of Europe's advertisement expenditure. The Asia/Pacific region will spend an additional US\$ 3.3 billion, representing 5.9% of traditional advertising spending in the region [2]. The Asian market for online advertisements, especially on the Chinese Mainland, is very attractive to many US companies [30]. According to the Australian-based consulting firm, www.consult, online advertising spending in the Chinese Mainland, Hong Kong and Taiwan amounts to US\$ 6 million, or 11% of the total online advertising spend in Asia-Pacific, excluding Japan. Net advertising spending is expected to rise to US\$ 26 million this year in the Greater China region. Forrester Research predicted that US\$ 16 million will be spent this year on online advertising in the Mainland rising to US\$ 440 million by 2004.

Obviously, in many companies, a substantial proportion of the marketing budget will go to satisfying the increasing popularity of the online marketing purpose. Decisions to place online advertising on web sites has become increasingly more important. Currently, there is neither a single method of evaluating and prioritising web sites for online marketing, nor has prior work been conducted on the utilisation of the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) in web site selection. With the advent of the Net as an advertising medium, the selection of the best web site for advertising has become a major requirement for marketing advertisers. At the same time, little is known about the selection of web site for online advertising.

In our study, collaborative work was carried out with a web consultation firm to establish criteria and methodology for web site selection. This paper presents the rating approach for prioritising the sites and the hierarchical structure of the criteria.

## 2. Literature review

## 2.1. The World Wide Web and advertising

The advent of the web represented a turning point in electronic commerce (EC) by providing an easy-to-use technological solution to the problem of information publishing and dissemination. The web has made EC a cheaper business activity, and allowed more diverse businesses to take place $[16]$ . Advertising on the web has been labelled “electronic advertising” and refers to advertising that is delivered to users of electronic information services $[14]$ . The term “web advertising” suggests to many the ubiquitous banner advertisements commonly found on the web, although advertisers consider them to be just the “ad for the ad” $[13]$ . The application of the web has become one of the most important issues in marketing and advertising research $[36]$ . Ducoffe $[7]$ conducted a recent survey, where he questioned American advertising executives about their future. He found that the most important factor was “new media technologies” (e.g. the Net and the Web). However, Rossiter and Bellman $[25]$ believe that there are risks because research into advertising in computer-mediated environments is still in its infancy. Dholakia and Rego [6] point out several unique characteristics of web advertising:

1. the web represents a relatively easy and extremely inexpensive way to advertise;

2. the web overturns the traditional hierarchical system of distribution channels, making former channel partners competitors in the global marketplace;

3. the customer has a greater control when choosing and processing information about the firm;

4. the width and efficiency of the channel provides greater availability of hard-to-find products and a wider selection of items.

Little [20] focused on using the web to support commerce in terms of electronic marketing and digital publishing. Hofacker and Murphy [15] explored one exciting advertising research possibilities spawned by the web—the efficacy of banner advertisements designed to lure the browser to an external web page. They found that the imperative call for behaviour, “click here”, has a positive effect. Yuan et al. [37] explored whether the traditional practice of bundling advertisements with content will prevail or become less common. Raman and Leckenby [24] used an empirical approach to determine the factors that affect individual exposure to material on the web sites created with a commercial intent. Cho [4] developed a model (the modified elaboration likelihood model) to understand how people process advertising on the Net. Our examination of the literature has revealed no studies that relate to the selection of web sites for online advertising.

## 2.2. The AHP

The AHP is one of the extensively used multicriteria decision making (MCDM) methods. The AHP has been applied to a wide variety of decisions, including car purchasing [3], IS project selection [23,28], software selection [18,19], and IS success [31]. Detailed descriptions of the theoretical aspects of AHP can be found in [27], special journal issues can be found in [33–35], and applications to marketing can be found in [8,9].

The AHP is aimed at integrating different measures into a single overall score for ranking decision alternatives. Its main characteristic is that it is based on pairwise comparison judgements.

## 3. Background

In Hong Kong, where few people have cars and few supermarkets have sufficient parking, direct-to-home sales are likely to be the future means of grocery shopping $[29]$ . ABC Co. Ltd. (the name is changed to provide anonymity), a privately held company, offers a home delivery shopping services for a range of products, such as groceries, computers, and mobile phones at discount prices. Orders can be placed by phone, fax, or over the Net. Several web sites were being considered by ABC Co. Ltd. for placing online banner advertising to steer people to their web site. The company preferred to select media whose audience characteristics were closest to the profile of their customers. The target population could be characterised by demographic, psychographic, and behaviour variables, each of which can be weighted by their importance.

ABC Co. Ltd. wanted to achieve several objectives: to reach as many readers as possible, to target the “right” type of person, and not to spend more than a total prespecified budgeted. Experts of a web consultant firm were paid to assist ABC managers define their target audience and advise them on placing online advertisements. In order to do this, desk research of secondary data was conducted to profile the target market. The final profile is described below.

\- Age: 25–40 years old.

\- Education: completed college or more.

\- No gender preference.

ABC Co. Ltd. felt that they could help such people save time and effort in shopping. Their target audience was also felt to be limited to certain media outlets: they do not have time to spend on media such as television or newspapers but do use the Net. Thus, an online medium seemed appropriate.

## 3.1. Alternatives

In choosing the best web site for ABC's target audience, the first thing we had to consider was the site category. There are many types of the web sites available, including newspaper, magazine, recruitment, portals, and search engines. Since ABC only targets local Hong Kong customers, we chose only Hong Kong sites.

In this paper, we discuss one representative the web site in each category and compare it with other web sites categories. Each category is characterised by different types of visitors and different purposes that each serves.

## 3.2. Newspaper sites

A newspaper site provides local information online, including news, classifieds, arts and entertainment guides, community information, sports, shopping, etc. There are about 10 English and Chinese newspaper sites in Hong Kong. The Chinese online newspapers Sing Tao Electronic Daily (singtao.com) and Ming Pao (mingpao.com) were the earliest entrants. The visitors to newspaper sites are those who are generally more mature and have a higher education level, therefore, the Sing Tao Electronic Daily was chosen to represent this category in this analysis; it happens to be also the market leader in this category.

## 3.3. Portals

A portal is a site that serves as the “port of entry” into the web. Portals are designed to give web users the information they need as they first enter $[12]$ . They are designed to offer a variety of services from a single, convenient location and typically sell advertising space. Portals are intended to draw traffic by using various kinds of content to lure visitors. Usually, they include content on finance, news, travel, shopping, software downloads, games, or services like free email and chat room facilities. Some portals in Hong Kong are: hongkong.com, china.com, Hutchison (hutchcity.com), Cable & Wireless (netvigator.com), and CTI (ctinets.com). Here, we chose hongkong.com, as it has the highest impression ratings and is targeted mainly at the local public and not at its own subscribers.

## 3.4. Magazine sites

The magazine site uses content to draw traffic and then uses the impression rates/hit rates to generate advertising revenue. It may specialise in a variety of content like finance, car racing, computers, shopping guides, etc. These online magazines include. Next Media (nextmedia.com), PC Home (pchome.com.hk), Capital and Easy Finder. Next Media was our choice, since it is one of the more popular magazine sites. The nextmedia.com clientele are generally younger and have a high proportion of female visitors.

## 3.5. Recruitment sites

These sites are targeted at job seekers. Their revenue is generated from recruitment advertising and online banner advertising. The sites include: Career Times (careetimes.com.hk), Job Asia (jobasia.com), Jobs Database (jobsdb.com), etc. The content is generally limited to job advertisements, but some sites will provide soft content, such as interview, writing, and presentation skills to encourage visitors to return. As Job Asia is a well established site and has the highest impression rating, it was chosen to represent this category.

## 3.6. Search engines

Search engines, such as AltaVista, Excite, InfoSeek, and Yahoo!, are a popular way to locate web sites, rather than directories. Due to this wide ranging and general facility, they appeal to a wide audience in terms of ages, higher educational level, and occupations. The most popular local search engines include Timway Search Engine (hksrch.com), Yahoo Hong Kong (yahoo.com.hk) and Gowhere Chinese Yellow Pages. All are in presented as Chinese only and are targeted at Hong Kong visitors. Timway was chosen, as it is more popular.

## 3.7. Major factors

In traditional off-line media, many factors determine the appropriateness of a particular medium, including editorial quality, audience fit, technical capabilities, and target population receptiveness $[17]$ . After discussion with an online marketing expert and two consultants, we identified five evaluation criteria that can were used in selecting the most effective web sites for online advertisements.

## 3.7.1. Impression rates

An impression rate is measures by counting each time that a page displaying the banner is viewed: it gives advertisers a sense of the traffic at the site. A page view differs from a hit: a view involves the number of times a page has been accessed, whereas, a hit counts the number of times that all elements in a page, including graphics, have been accessed. This implies that the higher the number of impressions, the higher the chance that a banner advertisement will be viewed by potential customers.

## 3.7.2. Cost

Some sites charge by assessing “click-through”s in cost per thousand, but we used monthly rental cost. To standardise comparison, the cost referred to full banners placed at the top of a web page.

## 3.7.3. Audience fit

In this study, we define fit as how well the visitors compare to ABC's target customers profile in terms of age and education level. We believed that those who are more likely to buy goods are aged 20–35 years and willing to be pioneers in online shopping.

## 3.7.4. Content quality

Content quality is a term used to describe the quality of the web site content. Here, we measured it by assessing the variety and reliability of the information. Thus, the selection of an effective banner carrier had to consider the image of the site and the advertiser.

## 3.7.5. “Look and feel”

The “look and feel” depends mainly to the design and creative elements of a site. It sometimes also refers to the navigation flow and the user-friendliness of the site. This criterion is subjective, but based on their experience and judgement, web experts can easily identify the differences between candidate sites.

After identifying the alternatives and criteria, the next step was to input the value of these variables into Expert Choice $[10]$ , which automates the AHP method. The analysis incorporated both hard data (see Table 2) and subjective judgements, which were based on knowledge and experience of the panel of experts selected for this case study.

## 4. A case study using AHP

## 4.1. Assumptions

## 4.1.1. Purpose

In our case study, AHP was applied to a particular campaign in which ABC Co. Ltd. wanted to draw more visitors to its web site in order to increase online shopping.

## 4.1.2. Objective of campaign

For this study, our online advertising campaign was targeted at both males and females.

## 4.1.3. No relationship between the web site and ABC Co. Ltd.

In this study, there was no relationship between each site and ABC Co. Ltd. and each site was treated as independent.

## 4.2. Applying the AHP method

## 4.2.1. Breaking down the problem

The first step was to develop a hierarchical structure of the problem. This classifies the goal and all decision criteria and variables into three major levels, as depicted in Fig. 1. The highest level of the hierarchy is the overall goal: to select the best web site for the advertisement. Level 2 represents the criteria in selecting the sites for placing online advertisements (impression rate, monthly cost, audience fit, content, and “look and feel”). Level 3 contains the decision alternatives that affect the ultimate selection of the chosen portals (newspapers, portals, magazines, recruitment, and search engines).

## 4.2.2. Comparative judgements to establish priorities

Judgements were elicited from the marketing experts in the consultant firm and matrices were formulated. For example, in order to determine the relative importance of the five major criteria, a $5 \times 5$ matrix was formed. Expert Choice provided ratings to facilitate comparison, these then needed to be incorporated into the decision making process. For example, audience fit was the most important criterion, and it was moderately more important than impression rate, which, in turn, was moderately more important than monthly cost. The two least important criteria were “content quality” and “look and feel”. After inputting the criteria and their importance into Expert Choice, the priorities from each set of judgements were found and recorded in Fig. 2.

## 4.3. Discussion of results

Table 1, showing the normalized priority weights, helped determine the preferable web site for each attribute. The overall consistency of the input judgements at all levels is within the acceptable ratio of 0.1, as recommended by [26].

## 4.3.1. Impression rate

The higher the impression rate, the higher the score of the web site. Singtao.com had the highest score and this means that it has the highest impression rate. As an absolute comparison was used for impression rate, there was no inconsistency rate. See Table 2 for details.

## 4.3.2. Monthly cost

Here, all the rate cards were obtained from the advertising department of each possible site. With the monthly rental cost of various web sites, we could input it directly into Expert Choice. Singtao.com and HKSRCH had the lowest monthly cost compared with others. However, jobasia.com obtained the highest score.

## 4.3.3. Audience fit—age

Singtao.com had the most appropriate age range, because it had more visitors between the ages of 25 and 40 years. Nextmedia.com ranks second and jobasia.com third.

## 4.3.4. Audience fit—education level

Jobasia.com had the highest score here, because most of their visitors were highly educated and they were also more familiar with online advertising than most Net users. They have a higher propensity to purchase online than normal users.

## 4.3.5. Content quality

Singtao.com, as an online newspaper, had much better content quality than the others, followed by nextmedia.com, an online magazine which ranks second. The content quality itself will have an impact on the image of those companies who place advertisements on these sites.

## 4.3.6. "Look and feel"

“Look and feel” can be divided into “design” and “user-friendliness”. Jobasia.com had the best design, it was clean and more contemporary than the others. The second best, hongkong.com, was targeted at young people, so it had a “funky” design that appealed to its young audience. Nextmedia.com and hksrch.com had designs that were too messy and dull, so it was hard for the banners to stand out from the web page.

## 4.3.7. User-friendliness

The question to ask is: “is it easy for the visitor to navigate?” A web site can win loyal visitors when it is more user-friendly. Hksrch.com ranked high in this criterion because every user knew what a search engine was, and their functions were more or less the same, so visitors knew what to do when they entered. Nextmedia.com was also very user-friendly. Singtao.com required a plug-in for viewing the opening of the site: visitors had to download the software from the Net.

## 4.3.8. Synthesising to obtain the overall priorities of the web site alternatives

## 4.5. Model validation

The validation of the model was carried out with the help of three web consultants and two online media planners. The model was checked to ensure that the results reflected what was happening in the real world and that reasonable solutions were produced.

## 4.5.1. Functionality

The functionality of the model was examined by validating data and comparison of model results with the judgements of the web consultants and online media planners of ABC. Sample results were found to match their expectations in most instances.

## 4.5.2. Usefulness

The usefulness of the model was examined through observing its effect on the decision-making process in web site selection. The approach follows a systematic decision-making process, which, according to Simon [32], involves “intelligence”, “design”, and “choice”. The web consultants and online media planners found that the developed model was very useful in supporting managers in online advertising web sites selection.

## 5. Conclusion

Online advertising is a new service in the marketing industry. As more and more companies have their own web sites and E-commerce becomes more popular, the demand for online marketing services is growing exponentially. An AHP-based methodology was designed and applied and has proven its potential in helping decision-makers in supporting their web site selection.

The AHP approach provides a valuable support for a decision-maker. Although, the application was applied to a specific company, there is nothing unique about this environment, and hence the approach can be used to any company.

## 5.1. Limitations

In this paper, we integrate costs into the model through “absolute” measures. Their integration with budgetary constraints may not be sufficient. Organisational and operational issues may need to be considered. If the goal was to support a number of different marketing strategies to support corporate strategies, these factors may have to be incorporated into the decision model.
