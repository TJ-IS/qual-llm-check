# The effect of environmental uncertainty and strategic applications of IS on a firm's performance

Jong-min Choe $^{*}$

Department of Accounting, College of Business Administration, Kyungpook National University, Buk-ku, Sankyuk-dong 1370, Taegu 702-701, South Korea

Received 16 February 2000; received in revised form 25 March 2001; accepted 27 December 2001

## Abstract

In our study, we empirically examined the relationships among perceived environmental uncertainty (PEUN), the level of the strategic applications and the facilitators of IS strategic alignment. Using a systems approach, we also showed some ways that these relationships impact the performance of a firm. Apparently, PEUN has an indirect effect on IS strategic applications through the facilitators of alignment. An uncertain environment, a high level of strategic applications and well-arranged facilitators of alignment can contribute more to the improvement of performance than in a less uncertain or stable environment. Therefore, the benefits of a high level of strategic applications can still be realized in a competitive and uncertain environment. However, when the environment is stable (low PEUN), an excessively low level of strategic applications may decrease a firm's performance.

## 1. Introduction

The value of strategic information systems (IS) has been an important topic in both academia and industry. The use of IS for strategic purposes can provide a variety of competitive advantages. Wiseman and MacMillan [63] suggested five different forms of competitive advantage that may result from strategic applications of IS: differentiation, cost, innovation, growth and alliance advantage. Many studies have tried to investigate the benefits (e.g. [7,8,17,61]).

However, in some research efforts (e.g. [9,17]) the results were inconclusive, and the relationship between the strategic applications and the financial performance of a firm was very weak.

Investment in IS does not directly lead to superior financial performance. The impact of IS investment on performance depends on contextual variables, such as external environment, organizational context and IS maturity. Kivijarvi and Saarinen [31], Ragowsky et al. [47] and Li and Ye [34] have empirically shown the moderating effect of environmental uncertainty and the mediating effect of IS maturity on the relationship between IS investment and improved financial performance. In the same way, heavy investment in a strategic IS does not always guarantee high profitability. Benefits of strategic IS investment are also naturally dependent upon many uncontrollable contextual factors.

External factors such as competition, uncertainty and needs are driving forces for strategic applications of IS $[10,24,27,40]$ . Challenging business environments can make it imperative for firms to seek out opportunities to use IS for strategic purposes. However, in a stable and simple environment, firms generally pursue defensive strategies based on high efficiency and cost effectiveness, and a strategic IS is a costly luxury. Thus, the strategic application of IS is not really required under a stable and noncompetitive environment.

A strategic IS application is one having a profound effect on the company's success and destiny by: (a) influencing or shaping the company's strategy; or (b) playing a direct role in the implementation or support of the company's strategy [50]. Therefore, in recognizing and seizing opportunities of strategic IS applications, business goals and strategy should be properly incorporated. Aligning IS with business strategy is a means that considers business objectives or strategies in the strategic IS planning. It is a way to scan and search for strategic IS opportunities [33]. To identify and seize these, the alignment of IS with business strategy is critical: strategic IS applications are characterized by the mutual information flows and relationships between business strategy and IS planning [46,64].

In our study, relationships among external environments, IS strategic alignment and strategic applications level of IS were investigated. Using a systems approach, we also empirically investigated the impact of the fit among an external environment, the alignment of IS with business strategy and the strategic applications level of IS on the financial gains of a firm.

## 2. Theoretical basis

## 2.1. External factors and IS strategic alignment

A strategic IS may be broadly considered to be a competitive weapon to cope with uncertain environments. It has been argued that companies can deal with uncertainty by increasing their information processing capability and by creating interorganizational links between customers and suppliers. Strategic IS applications are used to obtain competitive advantages over rivals and to prevent rivals from gaining an advantage. Previous studies have shown that the external environment often encourages or requires firms to utilize strategic IS applications in order to survive (e.g. [26,28,29,51,52]).

Neo found that environmental uncertainty, the business role of the IS function and distinctive competencies in IS are major facilitators in an organization's decision to use IS applications for strategic purposes. Johnston and Carrico conducted a field study of 11 industries: it showed that industry environmental factors such as competition, deregulation and changes in technology influence the direction and pace of strategic deployment of IS. King et al. also identified the competitive pressure of the environment as an important facilitator for strategic IS use. Sabherwal and King examined decision processes for developing the strategic IS applications. They found that the external environment affects the adoption of a specific type of decision making process, but that the organizational structure does not influence any decision process attribute. Choe et al. empirically showed a positive relationship between perceived environmental uncertainty (PEUN) and strategic application level of IS.

To resort to strategic use of IS under challenging and uncertain external environments, firms must try to identify and seize opportunities of strategic applications. Aligning IS with business goals and strategy is a way to exploit the IS [30,53,57]. Alignment of IS with strategy is a collaborative process among business strategy, business organization, IS infrastructure and IS planning [3]. The alignment process can increase convergence between IS and line managers on the kinds of systems to be developed and enable a more synergistic integration of IT and business knowledge [5,49]. But this alignment requires many facilitating activities, such as IS manager involvement in business strategic planning, upper management's understanding about IS strategy, IS manager education of business goals and top management commitment to IS, many of which are not considered important by upper management [6,39]. The success of the IS strategic alignment is dependent upon the conditions of these facilitators [15]. If facilitators of alignment are poor, the strategic IS planning process will not be effective.

Both facilitators of alignment and the extent of aligning are influenced by the external factors, since all activities for searching and identification of strategic IS applications are performed to deal with the external environment $[12,58]$ . Well-defined facilitators and a high degree of alignment are required in more uncertain and competitive environments. The following hypotheses are based on these factors.

Hypothesis 1. The uncertainty of external environments is positively related to the degree of use of alignment facilitators.

Hypothesis 2. The uncertainty of external environments has an indirect effect on the strategic applications level of IS through the degree of use of alignment facilitators.

## 2.2. Strategic applications level of IS and firm's performance

IS applications can lower costs or create differentiation across a wide array of activities that constitute a firm's value chain. When applications create a differential advantage, they affect structural characteristics of the industry and become important to the organization's performance [11,22,44]. Several writers (e.g. [45,60]) proposed that benefits of IS applications mainly depend on a system's strategic role. Then, significant performance effects should be expected only for the IS investment that supports the strategy. Previous studies (e.g. [17,23]) empirically found that strategic applications of IS can lead to an increase in performance.

Jenster showed that there must be a fit between business strategy and an organization's IS in order for a firm to achieve superior performance. Brown et al. investigated long-term financial gains of strategic IS by analyzing thirty five firms which successfully employed strategic IS. The results of their analysis were that the stock market reacts favorably to announcements that firms are using a strategic IS. In addition, in subsequent years those firms tend to be more productive and more profitable than their industries and competitors.

However, Chan and Huff, and Chan et al. failed to show a positive effect of IS strategic alignment on the financial performance of a firm. They empirically found that under a high degree of alignment, IS have no effect or negative relationships with financial performance and reputation of the company. In a study by Floyd and Wooldridge, it was found that IT did not improve the organizational performance. The cause of the mixed results may be that they did not consider contextual variables such as the environment.

IS are valuable assets of a company. Thus, we can employ a resource-based view in explaining the value of strategic IS. A firm's resources are stocks of available factors owned or controlled by a firm [2]. They may be classified as tangible, intangible and personal-based [19]. A resource-based view holds that the type, magnitude and nature of a firm's resources are important determinants of its profitability [48]. The value of a resource is dependent on the environment within which it must function [4,38,43]. Resources cannot be evaluated in isolation, because their merits are determined by their interplay with the environment. Miller and Shamsie developed a distinction between property- and knowledge-based resources. They suggested that the former tend to contribute most to the performance in the stable and predictable setting, whereas the latter have greatest utility in an uncertain environment.

Knowledge-based resources include specific technical and creative skills as well as integrative or coordinative skills $[21]$ . Some technical skills are quite flexible as they apply to different outputs and environments. This makes them especially useful in uncertain settings $[62]$ . When customer tastes and rivals' strategies are stable, there is little need to redesign or adapt products $[37]$ . Strategic IS are knowledge-based resources which comprise technical and managerial IT skills $[35]$ . Thus, the following hypothesis was proposed.

Hypothesis 3. In highly uncertain external environments, the combination of a high level of strategic IS applications and well-arranged (a high use of) alignment facilitators can improve a firm's performance more than in noncompetitive and stable external environments.

## 3. Methods

## 3.1. Study sample

Data were drawn from a survey of current status of IS use in Korean business firms. Two hundred organizations were randomly selected from a population of about 700 listed on the Korean stock market. The firms are medium to large in size and, consequently, are likely to have more experience in IS applications than smaller ones. We first contacted chief IS executives of the selected firms to ask them to participate. Seventy organizations responded positively and were included in this study.

Subjective data (i.e. external environments, strategic applications level, facilitators of alignment and perceived performance) were gathered in an interview based on a structured questionnaire which was distributed to the chief IS executive of each firm. In some of the smaller firms, the top manager was also the chief IS executive. For data collection, both interviewing and questionnaire technique were used. We explained the contents of the questionnaire to the participants before soliciting answers. The questionnaire survey was performed over 5 months, from January to May 1998. Accounting data needed to measure financial performance were collected from the Korea annual report of listed companies [32]. Table 1 summarizes the characteristics according to the industrial type of the firms.

## 3.2. Measures

## 3.2.1. Strategic applications level of IS

Sethi and King [56] and Neumann et al. [41] developed measurement tools for strategic applications. The questionnaires of Sethi and King measure the extent to which IT provides competitive advantages, while Neumann et al. considered the firm's relative level of strategic IS application. McFarlan [36] suggested an IS strategic grid as a framework to investigate the strategic importance of IS to the organization. The organization's place on this grid is determined by the strategic impact of existing and future IS and therefore, it represents the relative level of strategic applications in a firm. Neumann et al. empirically proposed seven significant variables that affect and measure the organization's position on the IS strategic grid.

The seven variables include the fit of IS to the organization's critical success factors (CSF), IS contribution to the organization's competitiveness, IS support to tactical management, IS support to operations, IS contribution to improving profitability, IS contribution to financial gains through improved operations and perceived overall criticality of IS. These seven variables were used to measure the strategic applications level of IS in the firms. Seven question items for the strategic use level of present IS were developed, and another seven were constructed to measure the strategic applications level of future IS. The strategic applications level of the existing and future IS were measured on a five-point Likert-type scale, and a firm's strategic applications level was calculated as follows:

The firm's strategic applications level

$$
= \sqrt {\frac {1}{n} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \mathrm{P} _ {i} \frac {1}{m} \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {m} \mathrm{F} _ {j}}
$$

where $P_{i}$ is the score of question item i for the present IS; n the number of question items for the present IS; $F_{j}$ the score of question item j for the future IS; and m is the number of question items for the future IS.

## 3.2.2. External environments

Factors of external environments include environmental dynamism, heterogeneity, hostility, competition and external needs. However, PEUN totally comprises and represents all these external factors $[14,18]$ . Therefore, here, PEUN was used as the measure of external factors. Seven question items, developed by Khandwalla $[25]$ and Gordon and Narayanan, were utilized. Measures of PEUN were made on a seven-point Likert-type scale.

## 3.2.3. Facilitators of IS strategic alignment

In measuring facilitators of alignment, nine factors that enhance and contribute to alignment were considered. These were: the IS manager's understanding about business strategy, IS manager education of business goals and objectives, IS manager involvement in the business strategic planning, upper management's understanding about IS strategy, upper management education of IS strategy, user participation in IS planning, upper management commitment to IS, ability of IS management to keep up with advances in IT and IS responsiveness to user needs. By using these factors, nine questions were developed and measured on a set of seven-point Likert-type scale.

## 3.2.4. Performance of a firm

Benefits of strategic IS are multidimensional. Therefore, any single indicator of performance may not be effective. In our study, we measured the perceived performance of a firm over the last 3 years and the financial performance using two variables: (1) return on assets (ROA); and (2) return on sales (ROS).

Using seven questionnaire items developed by Gupta and Govindarajan [20], perceived performance was measured on a seven-point Likert-type scale. ROA focuses on the overall performance of a firm. ROS represents a firm's ability to generate income from sales revenue. In our study, the investment for strategic IS in 1998 was not measured. Instead, we measured a firm's strategic applications level of IS. For strategic applications, the long development lead-times are required [55,61]. In a firm that has a high application level, the strategic IS investment has been made long ago. Therefore, it seems proper to link the level of strategic applications with the financial performance in 1998. Accounting data to compute ROA and ROS were collected from the firms' balance sheets and income statements in 1998, which were provided in the Korea annual report of listed companies.

## 4.2. The effect of fit on the firm's performance

## 4.2.1. The analysis method of fit

Van de Ven and Drazin [59] outlined three approaches to analyzing data based on alternative definitions of fit: the selection, interaction and systems approaches. Researchers have been critical of the selection and interaction approaches, arguing that they only provide partial depictions of relationships between variables of interest and fail to consider the fit of the whole system [13,54]. A systems approach takes a holistic view by considering internal consistency among multiple variables. In our study, a systems approach was employed, because there are various combinations of PEUN, strategic applications and facilitators to enhance or decrease organizational performance.

Van de Ven and Drazin emphasized that there is no dominant method for operationalizing the systems approach. However, recently, a range of cluster analysis methods has emerged to offer the better way of determining how variables combine $[1,16]$ . In our study, cluster analysis provided clusters of companies that are similar in terms of PEUN, levels of strategic applications and conditions of alignment facilitators. In cluster analysis, we used the hierarchical agglomerative method for forming the clusters, because it generates non-overlapping clusters and has been a dominant method. Ward's method was chosen since this technique optimizes minimum variance within clusters. We also used the squared Euclidean distance as a proximity measure.

## 4.2.2. The results of cluster analysis

Based on the values of PEUN, levels of strategic applications and facilitators of alignment, cluster analysis was performed. Additionally, average organizational performance was calculated for each cluster. A critical issue in cluster analysis is in determining the optimal number of clusters. While there are formal decision rules to guide this process, heuristics are commonly used. A formal approach to determining the most appropriate number of clusters is to examine the dendrogram and the distance coefficient.

In Fig. 1, the dendrogram is presented, while the distance coefficients are shown in Table 6. The points at which the distance coefficient suddenly jumps indicate suitable stages in the clustering sequence. In Table 6, the distance coefficient increases rapidly at two points: (1) between clusters 1 and 2; and (2) between clusters 4 and 5. This implies that either a five- or two-cluster solution may be appropriate. However, a two-cluster solution is too small. ## 5. Conclusion and discussion

Prior studies empirically investigated the positive impact of the strategic applications of IS on the performance of a firm. However, some studies failed to show positive effects. In our study, we considered PEUN and alignment facilitators as key research variables, which may directly or indirectly relate to strategic applications. In a few previous studies which considered contextual variables, such as external environments, they employed an interaction approach to analyze the effects of the fit between contextual variables and the level of strategic applications. However, we adopted a systems approach.

Our study empirically examined the relationships among PEUN, alignment facilitators and the level of strategic applications. The results showed that there are significant positive relationships among PEUN, facilitators of alignment and the level of strategic applications. So, these results confirmed the arguments of previous research. The results of our study also represented that PEUN has an indirect effect on the strategic applications level of IS through facilitators of the alignment of IS with business strategy. Hence, it is suggested that facilitators of alignment must be well-prepared first to attain a high level of strategic applications in coping with uncertain external environments.

With a systems approach, we investigated the impact of the fit among PEUN, alignment facilitators and the level of strategic applications on the organizational performance of a firm. According to the results of our study, it is proposed that under an uncertain environment, a high level of strategic applications with well-arranged facilitators of alignment can contribute more to the improvement in performance than in a more certain or stable environment. The results also showed that the mismatch between PEUN, alignment facilitators and the level of strategic applications (i.e. a high level of strategic applications with well-arranged facilitators under low PEUN) can decrease the performance.

In our research, we obtained some unexpected results. The empirical results suggested that under low PEUN, an excessively low level of strategic applications and very poorly-prepared facilitators are likely to decrease the organizational performance. Hence, it seems that for the high performance of a firm, there exist the proper level or degree of the strategic applications and alignment facilitators in low PEUN. However, we cannot present and explain the appropriate level under low PEUN by using these results only. Additional research and investigation may be required.

Limitations and future research efforts are suggested as follows. In our study, a long-term profitability such as revenue growth rate was not considered. Strategic IS are generally associated with long-term goals. Therefore, it seems necessary to examine the relationships between the level of strategic applications and long-term performance indexes. There are other contextual variables such as organizational structure and IS maturity that have an impact on the level of strategic applications. In future research, various contextual variables must be included to investigate their effects on strategic applications and to find out the ways that lead strategic applications to the high performance of a firm.
