# Exploring the research in information technology implementation

Vincent S. Lai $^{1,a}$ , Radha K. Mahapatra $^{b,*}$

$^{a}$ Department of Decision Sciences and Managerial Economics, Faculty of Business Administration, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shatin, N.T., Hong Kong

$^{b}$ Department of MIS/MSC, College of Administrative Science, The University of Alabama in Huntsville, Huntsville, AL 35899, USA

## Abstract

Information Technology (IT) plays a critical role in the survival and growth of business organizations. Increasing investment in IT and the strategic role played by information systems make IT implementation an important research issue within the MIS discipline. Even though much research has been done to understand IT implementation, there is a lack of effort to integrate the findings and to evaluate the status of research in this area. The objective of this study is to explore the intellectual development and evolution of IT implementation research through a meta-analysis of published MIS research. The findings are based on an analysis of 71 articles on IT implementation published during 1976–1995 in top MIS research journals. The results indicate that IT implementation research is sensitive to the evolving role of IT in organizations. There is a shift in emphasis from studying individual IT to organizational and inter-organizational IT. A preference for theory building research away from exploratory research in recent times indicates a level of maturity in IT implementation research. © 1997 Elsevier Science B.V.

Keywords: Information technology; Implementation; Technology management; MIS research

## 1. Introduction

Information technology (IT) is a major factor in shaping the industry characteristics within which firms compete. Recent advances and the revolution in IT are bringing significant changes to organizations unanticipated even a few years ago. There is also considerable pressure on most organizations to implement IT as a strategy for enhancing their competitive position $[4, 11, 24]$ . Here, the term IT is defined in a broad sense as “technologies dedicated to information storage, processing, and communications.” This notion of IT focuses on the hardware, software, telecommunications, and office equipment that transform raw data to useful information, adding new value in the process [5, 9].

While the strategic importance of IT is established and progress has been made in understanding it, less is known about how to implement IT effectively for achieving general organizational goals. Much research, especially in the MIS discipline, has been undertaken to develop a better understanding of IT implementation and to assess the contribution of IT in improving organizational efficiency and effectiveness. Most of the research conceptualizes implementation as one phase of a total technology transfer process. For example, Thompson [22] viewed all technology transfer efforts as consisting of a sequence of three processes (initiation, adoption, and implementation), each of which must be considered if implementation success is to occur. Kwon and Zmud [12] concurred with Thompson's perspective, but argued that the 3-stage model may have overlooked the importance of a post-implementation evaluation process. They proposed a 6-stage process model by decomposing the implementation stage into adaptation, acceptance, use, and incorporation. This research adopted Scheirer's [19] process model to classify all activities in a total IT transfer process into seven stages. The details of the model and the associated research topics for each phase are depicted in Table 1. The first four phases deal with pre-implementation activities, while the last two evaluate the post-implementation operations. Implementation, the fifth stage in the model, is an attempt to install and deliver IT in the adopter's organization. It is in this stage that resources are expended to affect the application of new IT and that assessment is made of the extent to which the IT was actually used, delivered, or carried into effect. The success/failure of this stage has a significant impact on the diffusion rate and institutionalization of the new IT later on. It is important to single out research efforts within the implementation stage to summarize, organize, and view the development of the implementation process.

The objective of this study is to perform a meta-analysis of implementation research (as a separate phase in a total technology transfer process) within the field of MIS and explore its intellectual development and evolution. Research efforts in pre- and post-implementation phases are excluded. In particular, this study is designed to:

1. define and understand IT implementation-oriented MIS research;

2. identify the approaches and characteristics of this research in MIS literature; and

3. identify emerging research trends and methods in this area.

## 2. Research hypotheses

The growing importance of IT, combined with the increasing investment in these technologies in organizations, makes implementation a high priority research topic. Although many studies (such as Ref. [23]) show that IT implementation has become a topic of interest over the past decade, the extent to which the existing body of research reflects the general interest of MIS researchers is not obvious. A meta-analysis of this field is required to validate whether or not MIS implementation research is growing and reflects the increasing importance of this field. As evidenced in the last two decades, there have been several conferences and published articles on implementation research. Research also shows that studies in IT implementation are having an impact on practice, especially when new technologies are emerging at a rapid rate. Industries need research findings in order to design effective implementation strategies that successfully infuse new innovations into their existing IT infrastructure. Based on these arguments, we have:

## H1: The interest in IT implementation research has increased over the last twenty years.

IT implementation research may be categorized into four groups based on level of adoption of the IT. These are: individual, group, organizational, and inter-organizational. Individual IT is targeted at individual users in the organization; Spreadsheet software is an example of this. Group-level IT benefits the operations of work groups or teams; Group decision support systems are examples of this. Organizational IT, such as MRP, affects the operation of the entire organization or a large part of it. Inter-organizational IT transcends the boundary of an organization and impacts interdependent organizations; EDI is an example of this. The complexity of IT implementation and the factors affecting it vary depending on the level of IT adoption.

Although much research has been devoted to studies involving IT implementation at the individual level, there is a growing need to focus on implementation studies at the organizational and inter-organizational levels $[3, 13]$ . In fact, efforts have already been devoted to promote these new directions. Swanson $[21]$ classified innovations into three types, based on their ability to support the IS core (Type I), administrative core (Type II), or technical core (Type III). He stated that studies of Type III innovations (inter-organizational IT) are especially important due to the potential contributions and impacts of these technologies in the organization. Prescott and Conger $[18]$ also urged more implementation research devoted to organizational and inter-organizational IT. While research efforts in organizational and inter-organizational IT are gaining interest and evolving to a more mature and realistic stance, there has been no study to validate this phenomenon. Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H2a: There is a difference in the intensity of research addressing organizational IT, as compared to other levels.

H2b: There is a difference in the intensity of research addressing inter-organizational IT, as compared to other levels.

A number of IT implementation studies have investigated the relationship between research methodologies and IT implementation. Several of them report that prior implementation studies have been found using different methods and approaches. Alavi and Joachimsthaler's [2] meta-analysis on DSS implementation also indicates that methodological characteristics of studies affect research findings.

Currently, IT implementation is understood only in a limited way, as its scope has rarely been delineated or systematically analyzed. Different research methodologies have been adopted by researchers from different disciplines to study IT implementation. This makes cross-comparison of studies very difficult. In order for IT implementation research to mature, it is necessary to develop consensus in research methodologies and a common paradigm for gathering evidence. This taxonomy can serve as a framework for describing and analyzing the field of knowledge. However, before this, a meta-analysis of prior IT implementation research within the MIS field must be performed to detect whether any convergence of research methodologies already exists. The research taxonomy developed by Straub et al. [20] was chosen: (1) case study, (2) field study, (3) field experiment, (4) laboratory experiment, (5) conceptual study, and (6) review/tutorial. Of these, the first four are categorized as empirical research methods and the last two fall in the category of non-empirical research methods [1]. To investigate the proportion of empirical and non-empirical research conducted in IT implementation, the following hypothesis is postulated:

## H3: Empirical research methods are preferred to non-empirical methods in IT implementation research.

This study is also interested in detecting an evolving pattern in the use of research methodologies in IT implementation research. If empirical research is found to be preferred to non-empirical research, would any particular method be used more frequently than others? To answer this question, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H4: All research method(s) are equally popular in studying IT implementation.

## 3. Research methodology

The domain of the research study was derived from the IT implementation articles published in the top 10 journals, determined by Gillenson and Stutz [7], between the years 1976–1995. Only full articles were used. Comments, notes, and news on IT implementation were excluded. Due to differences in orientation and emphasis of these listed journals, not all of them were used in this study. IEEE and ACM Transactions on various subjects (except IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management, which focuses on technology management), Journal of ACM, and ACM Computing Surveys were dropped because they are mainstream computer science journals and publish very few articles from MIS researchers. Two other journals, Information Systems Research and Information and Management, have been added to the list because of the high quality of their published articles. The final list is shown in Table 2.

In the data collection stage, both authors were involved independently in selecting the relevant articles. The process began with the examination of the author-specified keywords in the paper. All articles with keyword ‘implementation’ were chosen for this study. If the published paper did not have any keyword specification, the abstract of the paper was read to determine its relevance to the research. The list of articles compiled in this process was checked by both authors for consistency and completeness. The results were then pooled to form the master list of this study (Appendix A).

Once the research articles were identified and the coding strategy determined, all the articles in the validated list were coded using a pre-designed format (Appendix B). This allowed the capturing of all required information in a consistent and uniform way. Again, the coders worked independently when categorizing articles to their topic and research classification. To ensure accurate coding, the selected articles were divided into two groups, with each reviewed by two coders – primary and secondary. Each author had to act as a primary and a secondary coder. The primary coder read each article in detail and made preliminary entries for all items. The secondary coder then reexamined these entries to ensure correctness. Any article that was classified differently by both the coders was marked and discussed until an appropriate classification was determined.

## 4. Results

A total of 71 articles pertaining to IT implementation was identified through a search of the literature between the period 1976–1995 from the nine journals. Statistical analysis, at 0.05 significance level, was conducted on information derived from these articles to validate the hypotheses postulated in this study. The results are summarized in Table 3.

## 4.1. Publication trend

A yearly analysis was made to investigate the trend in implementation publications (Fig. 1). Of the 71 articles, ten were published between 1976 and 1980; 17 between 1981 and 1985; 19 between 1986 and 1990; and 25 between 1991 and 1995. Thus, in a 5-year interval period since 1976, it appears that IT implementation research showed a steadily increasing trend. To validate this phenomenon, a Cox and Stuart test for trend was performed. As each journal published a different number of articles each year, the number of IT implementation articles of each journal was normalized by the total number of articles published in all the journals in a year. The result $(T = 8, p = 0.001)$ supported our hypothesis that IT implementation publication has increased significantly over the past two decades.

## 4.3. IT adoption level

The 71 articles include IT implementation research addressing all four levels of IT adoption in organizations. Table 4 lists these articles, classified by IT adoption level. Organizational technologies have been most heavily researched, drawing a total of forty-three (60.56%) publications out of 71. This is followed by individual IT with sixteen articles (22.5%). Research addressing inter-organizational and group-level IT implementation both accounted for six articles (8.5%) each.

An ANOVA test was used to determine whether a difference exists in the intensity of research addressing the different levels of IT adoption. The results $F = 6.08, p = 0.001$ concluded that real differences in IT research intensity do exist. To investigate which is studied more intensely, a further post hoc analysis was used in the form of Scheffe's test. The results showed that only organizational IT was significantly different from other levels. In other words, hypothesis H2a was supported while H2b was not. This indicates that more research effort has been devoted to organizational IT than any other level of IT adoption.

## 4.4. Research methodology

The research approaches were examined to identify methodological characteristics for IT implementation research. Table 5 lists the articles categorized by the research approach for different IT adoption levels. Field study and case study are the two most popular research methods for IT implementation research. A total of 26 out of the 71 (36.6%) studies used field study as the research method. Twenty-four (33.8%) articles reported the used of case study as the research method. Conceptual studies accounted for fourteen (19.7%) articles. Field experiment (7.0%) and laboratory experiment (2.8%) accounted for remaining seven studies. None of the articles surveyed used a review/tutorial approach. The binomial statistical analysis ( $T = 55, p = 0.00$ ) indicated that there was an unequal number of empirical implementation publications, thus supporting the hypothesis (H3) that empirical approaches are preferred to non-empirical approaches in implementation research. A Chi-square test was conducted to test whether or not all research methods are equally popular in studying IT implementation (H4). However, the results did not support this hypothesis, meaning that MIS researchers have preferred methods (case and field studies) in conducting their implementation studies.

The case study method seems to be used frequently in research studying IT adopted at the group as well as at organizational levels. Most of these case studies examined the designs, strategies, and implementation experiences of organizations, with the research objective of deriving the success and failure factors that could guide future implementation. Field study, especially mail survey, is another popular research method in studying IT adopted at the organizational level. A number of the field studies involves the development of models or frameworks by synthesizing and integrating information collected through survey instruments. Their goal is to validate hypotheses postulated for testing theories in IT, especially those related to success factors and IT management.

## 5. Discussion

This study reveals some interesting facts about IT implementation research. The growing reliance of business operations on IT and the increasing investment in this field requires that more effort be directed to understand IT implementation. As new information technologies are developed and rapidly assimilated, it is necessary to understand these and develop guidelines for successful implementation. This study has revealed a growing emphasis on IT implementation-related research as evidenced by an increasing trend in the related research publications over the past 20 years. This suggests a healthy congruence between implementation research and the research need of today's business.

Organizational IT has received the greatest attention among implementation researchers. It addresses the complex issue of IT implementation at the organization level. Many studies deal with factors relevant to the success and/or failure of IT implementation projects in organizations. In addition to human factors, which are important for successful IT implementation, organizational culture and social issues are also found to play a key role. It is interesting to note that a number of these studies has focused on production and operations management. Increasing global competition is forcing companies to reduce cost and improve cycle time. It is therefore logical to see an increased interest in technologies such as CAD/CAM, just-in-time systems, and flexible manufacturing systems; these can be effective in controlling production cost and improving cycle time from design to delivery.

The 1970s and 1980s saw a steady growth in research to enhance decision making by individual decision makers. Decision support systems and expert systems technologies evolved and matured during this period. Coinciding with this research trend, increased research activity on individual IT implementation was noticed during the 1980s. Research emphasis in this area seems to have declined in the 1990s, demonstrating probable maturity in this technology.

The last few years have seen an increasing emphasis on inter-organizational IT implementation. Inter-organizational systems facilitate the flow of data across organizational boundaries by electronically linking an organization with its suppliers, customers, and agents. Successful case studies, such as the SABRE system of American Airlines [8] and ASAP [15] of American Hospital Supplies, show that inter-organizational systems can be a source of significant competitive advantage [17]. IT has evolved from a support tool to a competitive weapon in the strategic arsenal of business organizations [10, 14]. This has resulted in a growing interest of organizations in this type of IT. Implementation of these systems raises many complex managerial and technical issues; however, this is a rapidly growing area of research with 25% of all implementation articles published during the period 1993 to 1995 devoted to the inter-organizational level.

Empirical research methods are more popular in IT implementation research than non-empirical methods. Case study and field study are found to be the predominant methodologies. Case research strategy is useful during the exploratory phase of research; it allows the researcher to study a phenomenon in its natural setting, capture the knowledge of practitioners, and develop theories from it. A field study requires a better understanding of the phenomenon on the part of the researcher and it is therefore often used for theory development. Popularity of these research methods indicates that practice leads academic research in this area.

Most of the case studies in implementation research use a single-case design and are descriptive in format indicating the exploratory nature of research. An analysis of the publications during the period 1990–1995 shows that 53% (15 out of 28) of these studies used field study as compared to 21.4% (6 out of 28) that used case study. The proportions of studies that used field study and case study research methods during the period 1985–1989 are 21% (4 out of 19) and 58% (11 out of 19) respectively. This shows that IT implementation researchers are moving from a predominantly case study research method to a field study research method. Apparently, researchers now have a better understanding of IT implementation and more research effort is being spent in theory building from practice, thus signifying a growing maturity of research.

## 6. Summary and conclusion

Successful implementation of IT, a strategic resource in business organizations, is a key to the survival and growth of businesses in today's competitive environment. IT implementation research has become an important research issue. This study is an effort to explore the development of this research based on publications in academic journals.

The study found evidence of a growing trend in the number of research publications over the past 20 years. A large body of research has addressed implementation issues at the organizational level. Research on inter-organizational IT implementation has gained momentum in the past few years. Factors critical to the success and/or failure of implementation projects and management of IT implementation are the two most important research issues today. Case study and field study are the two most popular research methods. Field study is gaining popularity, indicating that implementation research is maturing and using more theory building methods.
