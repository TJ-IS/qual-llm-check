# The effects of user participation on the design of accounting information systems

Jong-Min Choe $^{*}$

Department of Accounting, College of Business Administration, Kyungpook National University, Taegu, Puk-Ku, Sankyukdong 1370, 702-701, South Korea

Received 2 June 1997; accepted 25 May 1998

## Abstract

The roles of user participation (UP) in the design of Management Accounting Information System(s) (MAS) have long received the attention of MAS researchers. By surveying business firms through a structured questionnaire, this study investigated the interactions among contextual variables (Task uncertainty and organizational structure), information characteristics (Scope, timeliness and aggregation) and UP. The results suggest that under high task uncertainty, aggregated and timely information with high UP is positively associated with increased MAS performance. However, when the task uncertainty is low, the UP makes no impact on the relation between performance and information characteristics. In a less structured organization, broad scope, timely and aggregated information with high UP has a positive influence on the performance. In mechanistic organization, narrow scope and disaggregated information through the high UP can lead to higher MAS performance. © 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved

Keywords: Management accounting information systems; User participation; Information characteristics; Contextual variables; Three-way interaction model

## 1. Introduction

Problems in the design of management accounting information system(s) (MAS) have long received the attention of their users. Many studies indicated that MAS design requires the consideration of contextual variables and, hence, for a more successful design contingency relationships between contextual variables and characteristics of the MAS should be examined $[16, 31]$ . In previous studies, most research has been directed at examining simple relationships (e.g. [4, 25]). The performance variables have generally not been considered in their models.

A few studies included managerial effectiveness as a performance variable; however, there were some drawbacks. Gul and Chia [18] and Chong [6] measured the managerial or departmental effectiveness as a performance variable, and the effects of the fit between contextual variables and information characteristics on the managerial performance were empirically examined. However, there are many factors that affect managerial performance. Environmental, organizational and behavioural factors, such as motivation, role conflict, supervisory style, unit size, and other management information systems, etc. influence managerial performance [2, 20, 22]. The effects of the fit on managerial performance may be compounded with the effects of other influence factors.

User participation in the development process of an MAS may be a good way to define requirements. Prior research suggested that it may be effective in the design of MAS (e.g. Refs. [21, 28]). However, the roles of user participation in the design have not been clearly proved. Using the three-way interaction model, this paper empirically identifies the effects of user participation on the design at the subunit level. The impacts of the contingent relationships among contextual variables, information characteristics and user participation on the MAS performance are also examined.

## 2. Prior research

## 2.1. Contextual variables in the design of an MAS

The contextual variables in the design of an MAS may be broadly classified into two groups: individual- and organizational-level variables. On the organizational level, key contextual variables are environment, structure and technology or task. The environment, measured by perceived uncertainty, is external to the organization [13]. It is a macro-variable. In previous studies, perceived environmental uncertainty was considered; however, the unit of analysis was a subunit, and this cannot be matched with a macro-organization variable, such as perceived environmental uncertainty.

Contextual variables in the research model must be consistent with the unit of analysis $[14, 43]$ . If the unit of analysis is a subunit, task uncertainty or task characteristics should be considered $[46]$ . In this study, the unit of analysis is the subunit. Thus, organizational structure and task uncertainty are included as important contextual variables in the design.

MASs are information or control systems, and they should meet the information requirements resulting from uncertainty in the organization $[10, 12]$ . The organizational structure is viewed as a mechanistic or organic structure, whose dimensions are related to the information processing capabilities of the organization $[3, 8]$ . The main dimensions of this structure are organizational formalization and centralization [35, 42]. Therefore, these dimensions are included here. Task uncertainty refers to the difficulty and variability of the work undertaken by an organizational unit [44, 45]. Here, task difficulty and task variability are selected as the main dimensions of task uncertainty.

## 2.2. Information characteristics of an MAS

In our study, three information dimensions (information scope, timeliness and aggregation) are used. Narrow-scope information is derived from conventional financial accounts, this tends to be concerned with events within the organization, and results in data that are financial and historic. Alternatively, broad-scope information includes external, nonfinancial and future-oriented material. Timeliness is usually specified in terms of the ability to provide information on request and the frequency of reporting systematically collected information.

Information aggregation deals with a variety of ways to collect or sum the data within periods of time or areas of interest, such as responsibility centers or functional areas. Disaggregated information means too much detailed information, that may include only one period or one functional area. But, aggregated information represents summarized information that covers periods of time or diverse management areas. Broad-scope, timely and aggregated informations are considered to be more processed information.

## 2.3. The effects of user participation on the design zof an MAS

Introduction of a new information system (IS) may cause problems in an organization on account of the mismatch between IS functions and organizational factors, such as organizational structure, tasks and power distribution. To reduce dissonance, user participation in the design and the implementation of the IS should be considered. User participation in the design and implementation process may increase the consensus of the organizational members.

Anderson [1] and Shields [38] proposed that the organizational and behavioural factors have an influence on the successful design and implementation of an activity-based costing (ABC) system. They also suggested that clarity of objectives among the designers and users is necessary to ensure that ABC information is produced efficiently and used effectively. User participation is an important way to interrelate ABC among strategy, tasks, user requirements and objectives. It also provides a mechanism for understanding and accepting ABC. In reality, user participation helps users to accept change.

## 3. Hypotheses

If task difficulty and variability are high, then, task uncertainty increases. Managers operating in such situations may not have all the information they need to perform the task. When task difficulty is high, the work is not well understood, and there are no objective and correct procedures or rules to follow. Hence, for high-difficulty situations, broad-scope information is required continuously to help the manager understand difficult tasks more clearly. Under high task difficulty, cause and effect relationships are not well understood, and multiple focus or aggregated information may be needed [29]. Since aggregated information is less specific, it may be more helpful when searching for multiple answers or solutions.

Task variability is the frequency of unexpected and novel events that occur when performing tasks. When task variability is high, uncertainty and time required to make decisions increase. As task variability increases, broad scope information is required to cover various exceptional events, and the need for manipulated information may also increase to reduce the time required to make a decision $[40]$ . The manipulated information includes analyzed or aggregated data (e.g. means, ranges) and decision aids (e.g. graphics). In high-variability situations, large amounts of information are required on an on-going basis for solving unusual problems or events $[9]$ . Hence, managers have to process and obtain more timely, broad-scope and aggregated information to deal with higher variable tasks.

The effective MAS should provide narrow-scope, disaggregated, and periodic information in low-task uncertainty situations. Under low task uncertainty, if an MAS provides broad scope and aggregated information more frequently, information overload may occur.

The degree of user involvement is different, depending on the level of task uncertainty [30]. When the task is more uncertain, user involvement is required to identify more precisely complex tasks or user requirements. Kim and Lee [23] and Tait and Vessey [41] suggested that, in case of high task uncertainty, a high level of user participation has a positive impact on system performance (system use and quality); however, under low task uncertainty, the effect of the degree of participation on system performance is insignificant.

From the above arguments, three hypotheses are suggested as follows:

Hypothesis 1. The effects of the interactions among task uncertainty (task difficulty/variability), information characteristic (scope, timeliness and aggregation) and user participation on the MAS performance are significant.

Hypothesis 2. When the task uncertainty is high (high task difficulty/variability), a combination of a high degree of user participation and a more processed information (broad-scope, timely and aggregated) has a positive impact on the performance of MAS.

Hypothesis 3. When the task uncertainty is low (low task difficulty / variability), a less processed information (narrow-scope, periodic and disaggregated) has a positive impact on the performance of MAS, irrespective of high or low user participation.

Organic structure tends to have a higher information-processing capability. Such a structure develops more interdependence among its subunits while decision authority becomes decentralized and reliance on the formal rules and procedures decreases. However, organic structure consumes more time, effort and energy, and involves less managerial control. In such situations, the MAS should complement the defects of organic structure $[34]$ .

In the organic organization, managers tend to require aggregated information to control and coordinate the activities of interdependent subunits. Broadscope information is also needed to serve the diversity of decisions faced by the decentralized manager [36]. Under the organic structure, more time is apt to be spent on producing informations for alternatives and decision making because of coordination and control problems and, thus, the manager's response time increases. Hence, the MAS of higher performance should produce timely information to complement the information delay.

Based on the foregoing reasoning, the following hypotheses are suggested:

Hypothesis 4. The effects of the interactions among organizational structure (centralization/formalization), information characteristic (scope, timeliness and aggregation) and user participation on the MAS performance are significant.

Hypothesis 5. When the organizational structure is organic (low centralization/formalization), a combination of a high degree of user participation and a more processed information (broad-scope, timely and aggregated) has a positive impact on the performance of MAS.

Hypothesis 6. When the organizational structure is mechanistic (high centralization/formalization), a combination of a high degree of user participation and a less processed information (narrow-scope, periodic and disaggregated) has a positive impact on the performance of MAS.

## 4. Research method

## 4.1. Sampling

Data used in this study were drawn from a survey of the status of MAS development in Korean business firms. One hundred organizations were randomly selected from a population of 417 firms known to have computerized MAS. Among these, 78 responded and were included in the study. The study, therefore, covered 450 users of MAS in 107 subunits, including general management, finance, planning and cost management, etc. In every subunit, an MAS was studied.

Data were gathered by interviews based on a structured questionnaire intended to measure users' perception with relation to both, the contextual variables and the MAS. The instrument was delivered to the respondents who were spread from lower to upper levels in their subunit's formal hierarchy. Table 1 summarizes the major characteristics

## 4.2. Measurements

The performance of the MAS is a key research variable. Measurements of its performance and information characteristics will now be discussed, and the relevant research variables are presented in Table 2.

## 4.2.1. Performance of the MAS

All the MAS were computerized and information was available on line. Surrogate measures of information systems (IS) performance have been used in many studies, and they can be divided mainly into three types: user satisfaction, system use, and decisional performance $[47]$ . Here, user satisfaction and system use are considered as surrogate measures for MAS performance $[7]$ .

The measure of user MAS satisfaction was based on a set of ten questionnaire items that were constructed through reviewing and integrating the methods in previous studies $[5, 11, 27]$ . These were measured on a seven-point Likert-type scale. The degree of system usage, of course, is not an appropriate performance measure if system use is mandatory. MAS use was measured by the consideration of both the frequency of use and the willingness of the people to use it. Each was measured on a single-item seven-point Likert-type scale. On multiplying the frequency by the willingness, and then taking the square root to normalize the result, a measure was obtained.

## 4.2.2. Information characteristics

Of the various characteristics, orientation, time horizon, frequency, focus, aggregation, financial/non-financial, quantitative/qualitative and periodic/non-periodic were specifically selected. Orientation determines whether the data items report primarily internal or external facts. Time horizon defines whether the data items are ex-post, reporting what has happened, or ex-ante, reporting what is expected or planned to occur. The frequency represents how many times the reports are produced in a given period. The focus shows whether the data items are broad and diffuse or specific and narrow. Aggregation shows whether the reports contain too little or too much detail $[26]$ . Financial information is expressed in monetary terms. Quantitative information is expressed in numeric terms. Periodic/nonperiodic addresses itself to the question whether the data items are to be reported periodically, or at any undetermined time.

## 4.2.3. Aggregation of data

Subunit scores on items, such as formalization, centralization, and task difficulty/variability were obtained by assigning equal weights to the questionnaire responses for each hierarchical position, not by taking the simple average across all the respondents. Subunit scores were computed by the following

formula:

$$
\text { Subunit   score } = \frac {1}{n} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \frac {1}{m _ {i}} \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {m _ {i}} x _ {i j}
$$

where n is the number of hierarchical positions, $m_{i}$ the number of respondents in the hierarchical position i, and $x_{ij}$ the personal score of respondent j in the hierarchical position i.

A simple mean of all the respondents was calculated in order to obtain the scores of other research variables.

## 5.2. Interactions among task uncertainty, information characteristics, and user participation

A multiplicative model was adopted for testing the interaction effects of task uncertainty, information characteristic and user participation on MAS performance $[17, 37]$ . This involved use of a multiple regression equation as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{c} y = b _ {0} + b _ {1} I _ {1} + b _ {2} \chi_ {1} + b _ {3} \chi_ {2} + b _ {4} \chi_ {3} + b _ {5} I _ {1} \chi_ {1} \\ + b _ {6} I _ {1} \chi_ {2} + b _ {7} I _ {1} \chi_ {1} \chi_ {3} + b _ {8} I _ {1} \chi_ {2} \chi_ {3} \end{array}\tag{1}
$$

where $y$ represents the MAS performance, $b$ the regression coefficients, $I_{1}$ the information characteristic, $\chi_{1}$ the task difficulty, $\chi_{2}$ the task variability, $\chi_{3}$ the user participation, and $I_{1}\chi_{1}$ , $I_{1}\chi_{2}$ , $I_{1}\chi_{1}\chi_{3}$ , $I_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}$ the interaction of $I_{1}$ , $\chi_{1}$ , $\chi_{2}$ and $\chi_{3}$ . If $b_{7}$ and $b_{8}$ are significant (i.e. $b_{7}=b_{8}\neq0$ ), the corresponding incremental $R^{2}$ will also be statistically significant at the same probability level [39]. To provide information on whether the posited relationship is monotonic or not, the partial derivative can be used (see Appendix A).

Tables 7–9 provide the results of the multiple regression models performed. In regression Eq. A, the three-way interaction terms were not considered, but Eq. B included them.

In Table 7, the three-way interaction term of task difficulty for MAS satisfaction is significant, and the introduction of the three-way interaction terms results in a slight increase in $R^{2}$ . For Table 8, the three-way interaction term of task variability for MAS satisfaction is significant, and the inclusion of the three-way interaction terms also results in a slight increase in $R^{2}$ . However, in Table 9, the three-way interaction terms of task difficulty and task variability for MAS satisfaction are significant, and the introduction of them causes a significant increase in $R^{2}$ . These results provide partial support for Hypothesis 1.

## 6. Implication and conclusion

We investigated the effect of user participation on the design of MAS, with satisfaction and use selected as performance variables. The results of multiple regression analyses reveal an interesting pattern. In the task uncertainty, the effects of the three-way interaction terms on user satisfaction were only significant. On the contrary, in case of organizational structure, the impacts of interaction terms on use were only significant.

MAS satisfaction and use are positively correlated (i.e. correlation coefficient is 0.515, P=.000), but, they may measure different dimension, respectively. Satisfaction is a subjective measure, and it is strongly related to the satisfaction of job or task requirements [15]. Use is an objective measure, and it may be associated with the rules or procedures and the standards in the organization. Hence, in case of task uncertainty, the contingent relationship between task uncertainty and information characteristic, that is controlled and adjusted by user participation, may only have a significant influence on the satisfaction.

However, in the organizational structure, the structure-related design through user participation may only have a significant effect on the MAS use, that is associated with the organizational procedures or rules and standards.

Based on the foregoing arguments, it is suggested that when user participation is considered, the performance variable on which the contingent relationships have an impact is different according to the type of contextual variable. Therefore, it is assumed that, in the IS design research, the dimension of performance and the user participation should be jointly considered according to the type of contextual variable.

Using partial derivative, the effects of user participation on the design of MAS were suggested. Under high task uncertainty, it is concluded that aggregated and timely information through the high user participation is positively related to high performance. In the opposite situation, however, narrow scope, periodic and disaggregated informations is needed, irrespective of high or low user participation, to increase the performance. In organic organization, broad scope, timely and aggregated information with the high user participation is required for the higher performance. However, under mechanistic organization, it is suggested that narrow-scope and disaggregated information through the high user participation has a positive impact on the performance.
