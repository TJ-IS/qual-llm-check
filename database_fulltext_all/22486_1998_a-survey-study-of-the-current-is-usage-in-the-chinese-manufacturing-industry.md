# A survey study of the current IS usage in the Chinese manufacturing industry

Zhongzhi He $^{a,*}$ , Mohamed Khalifa $^{b}$ , Martin Kusy $^{c}$ , Tiesheng Zhao $^{d}$

$^{a}$ Faculty of Commerce and Administration, Concordia University, Montreal, Quebec H3G 1M8, Canada $^{b}$ Dept. of Information Systems, City University of Hong Kong, Tat Chee Avenue, Kowloon, Hong Kong $^{c}$ Faculty of Management, Brock University, St. Catharines, Ontario L2S 3A1, Canada $^{d}$ School of Management, Tianjin University, Tianjin 300072, PR China

Received 25 April 1997; accepted 16 May 1998

## Abstract

The status of information systems (IS) usage is an important measure of the level of IS adoption. This paper presents the results of a study investigating the current usage of IS and the attitudes toward their use in the Chinese manufacturing industry. Using the survey results obtained from a sample of 78 firms, we provide findings in the current status of IS applications, orientation of IS applications, human factors, and future IS plans. This effort also demonstrates an underlying relationship between critical IS factors (especially the human factors) and business performance in the Chinese environment. The paper concludes with a discussion of IS issues and their implications for practitioners. © 1998 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved

Keywords: Information systems (IS); IS application; Orientation of IS application; Chinese manufacturing industry

## 1. Introduction

The status of Information systems (IS) usage has been studied by many researchers $[2, 3, 14]$ , with the level of IS adoption differing from one country to another. In the most developed countries, such as the United States, IS staffing and activities in the business-place are becoming decentralized and end-user focused: “Aligning IS solutions with business goals and needs as well as building the infrastructure for technological integration are becoming the top priorities for IS activities” [5]. In some developing regions, such as Taiwan, IS applications are being upgraded very rapidly. Studies of critical IS issues show that the emphasis of IS application has been shifting from computerization of routine work to higher levels of IS activity, such as telecommunications, system integration, and IS strategic planning.

With the adoption of new economic policies, China, the world's largest developing country, wants to catch up with more advanced countries and to build its own information technology infrastructure. A vast amount of IS hardware and software has been introduced there in recent years. However, little research has been done to shed light on how IS usage is progressing. Questions such as the current status of IS practice and how human factors relate to IS activities continue to engage the interest of both practitioners and researchers.

The objective of our research was to survey the current status of IS practice and attitudes toward the use of IS in Chinese manufacturing companies. In a collaborative process, faculty members from Tianjin University and Concordia University surveyed 78 state-owned manufacturing companies, investigated their current IS activities, examined two human-related issues, and demonstrated the underlying relationship between critical IS factors (especially human factors) and business performance. The results of the survey enabled us to obtain timely technical and managerial information about the IS practice in the Chinese manufacturing industry.

Our research differs from other such works in two ways. First, it is a wide-base empirical study intended to address practical IS issues in China: we believe that it is among the first few to empirically obtain a degree of generalization of these issues. Second, as this follows a previous field study $[8]$ , it represents an on-going effort to conduct empirical IS research in China, enabling us to conduct in-depth research in a specific context.

## 2. Research background

In one of the few IS status studies conducted in China, Ping and Grimshaw [10] in 1992 analyzed the overall application of information technology (IT) in China. Using a ‘stages of growth model’, the researchers compared the application environment of computers and other information technology of China with that of the West. It was discovered that most Chinese enterprises were at the very early stages of computerization, and that there was mismatch between IT application and management practice. They concluded that computing practices in the People’s Republic of China (PRC) had advanced very little since the late 1980s, when it was apparent from a survey of 72 organizations in the Shanghai area of the PRC that “... most of the firms in PRC are still in the early stages of computing, where the emphasis is on the technical/operational aspects of computing” [9].

Our study, as stated earlier, is a continuation of an earlier field study. At that time, in an attempt to assess the computing activities in typical Chinese manufacturing firms, our research group conducted an in-depth field study at a Chinese manufacturing company – Tianjin Metal Forming Machine Tool General Works (TMFTW) – and performed interviews at four other companies. We investigated both, technological and management issues. In terms of technological issues, we found the major IS activities of these companies to be the automation of repetitive processes. As for management issues, we had the distinct impression that the gap between management and computing activities was enormous.

## 3. Research methodology

The combination of our field study and interviews provided us with some initial insights, but findings and conclusions were context-sensitive and could not be generalized. Survey studies, however, allow breadth of perspective and generalization. By this means, therefore, we hoped to ascertain the current status of IS practices in a more general Chinese environment.

## 3.1. Target population

Our target population was state-owned manufacturing firms. Our reason for limiting our study to them was that we were simultaneously conducting a separate study on the implementation of MRPII in Chinese manufacturing firms. Also, manufacturing is generally considered a complex industry in China, so the current status of IS practices of manufacturing firms is a good indicator of the level of IS applications.

## 3.2. Questionnaire design

The questionnaire was designed jointly by Canadian and Chinese researchers and attempted to collect data on four major topics.

## 3.2.1. Demographic information

We wished to know the characteristics of

1. the respondents, and

2. the organizations in terms of their size and profitability.

Company size was deemed to have three dimensions: (1) production assets; (2) annual sales; and (3)

total number of employees. All of these have been used in previous studies. Conrath et al. [4] and Doll and Torkzadeh [6] used gross annual sales and the total number of employees; Ang and Yang [1] added production assets to their study. We added a new dimension, annual profits, to measure the business performance.

## 3.2.2. Status of IS application

This section of the questionnaire measured the technical aspect of IS practices, and consisted of the following:

1. number of computers by type;

2. level of computerization of major business functions, from 0–100% of computerization;

3. percentage of computers allocated to major business functions;

4. degree of connectivity, and whether the company had a central database; and

5. number of IS employees from junior to senior level.

All these measures have been used in previous studies (e.g. [7]) to investigate the technical strength of IT in a given company.

## 3.2.3. Human factors

We selected two human factor elements; the first was to assess the level of support from five different organizational levels: top management, middle management, junior management, engineers, and IS employees. The second was to determine the degree of computer literacy of these five levels. The support for IS was measured on a 5-scale Lickert measure, scaled from none (1) to very strong (5). The second variable, employees' computer literacy, was measured on a 4-scale Lickert measure, scaled from none (1) to high (4). For each of these two variables, the sums of the scores at each organizational level were averaged by the number of responses. We selected these two human factors because they had been widely discussed in previous research (e.g. [11, 12, 13]) as the critical factors of IS success.

## 3.2.4. Future plans for IS

We also wished to determine the level of anticipated investment in IS in the short-to-medium term and the orientation of future IS applications. Specifically, this involved the following:

1. anticipated investment in IS in the next two years;

2. planned percentage of total investment in the areas of hardware, software, development, and training; and

3. degree of importance of IS application in major business functions.

## 3.3. Questionnaire validation

The questionnaire was validated by means of several interviews and a pilot study. We paid a second visit to TMFTW and the four companies we had interviewed previously. The questionnaire was reviewed by the senior managers and IS employees of those firms to check that the questions would be applicable in real situations. A few questions were refined to fit the Chinese environment. For example, with respect to computerization status we initially used investment in IS as one of the measures, but we were told that this statistic was not available or not accurate for many firms, because a great number of computers had been installed by the government without cost to the companies. This measure was, therefore, deleted and the number of computers by type was substituted. A pilot study was then conducted.

## 3.4. Samples and responses

Initially, we intended to mail the questionnaire to a large sample that would be randomly chosen from a list of names of Chinese manufacturing firms. However, experienced Chinese researchers indicated that this was not a practical methodology. As a result, six MIS graduate students from Tianjin University undertook the task of finding an adequate sample to complete our questionnaire. To date, we have 78 completed questionnaires. In view of budget constraints, all the interviews were mainly conducted at manufacturing firms located in Beijing or Tianjin, which are the second and third largest cities in China, respectively.

## 4. Data analysis

## 4.1. Demographic information

Each company in our sample concurred with the profile of our target population. All firms are located in

Eastern China. Of these, 70 (90%) are located in the Beijing–Tianjin region. Our survey results should shed light on the IS practices of more progressive manufacturing firms. Of the 78 completed questionnaires, 67 (86%) were completed by IS managers or employees who were knowledgeable of the use of IS in their companies. The remaining respondents were: top management (6), functional management (2) and engineers (3). The companies can be classified as producers of one of the following categories of items: light industry (11), electronic and mechanical (56), chemical (7), and construction materials (4). These are generally considered to be four of the main pillars of the national manufacturing industry. In our sample, electronic and mechanical product companies predominated. Organizational characteristics of the firms in our sample are listed in Table 1.

We found that human resources, accounting and engineering were the three functions that had been most highly computerized, their respective percentage rates being over 75, 75 and 50% respectively.

The average level of computerization for production was between 10 and 25%. The remaining functions – sales, purchasing, planning and warehousing – all of which are associated with managerial activities, were the least computerized, at less than 10%. In terms of the density of computer use, measured by the average percentage of computers allocated to each unit, we discovered engineering and production to be the most IS-intensive functions. In fact, all minicomputers and workstations were used to support large-scale computing, engineering design and manufacturing in these two fields. Although human resources and accounting were the most computerized functions, they are basically routine and structured activities, and thus were not the most IS-intensive. The four managerial activities – sales, purchasing, warehousing and planning – again had the smallest percentage of computers allocated to their units (Table 3).

Lu et al. [9] reported that, by the late 1980s, most computer applications were to be found in operational-level processing, such as payroll, financial reporting and accounting. This report was also verified by our findings concerning the status of IS application status of our sample companies. However, the level of operational activities has changed. Engineering and production have become the most IS-intensive functions and, thus, the focus of IS application is shifting from simple operational processing to effective engineering design and manufacturing. This demonstrates the advance in computer application since the late 1980s. After manual and routine work had reached a high degree of computerization, the tendency of IS application was to advance to complex technical activities, such as computer aid design, computer aid manufacturing and numeric control techniques. Recent development in technology has made this tendency technically possible and financially affordable.

In terms of networking, only ten (13%) companies reported that their computers were networked across major business functions. The average proportion of networked computers, in the above companies, was less than one third. Five of them integrated their data into a central database. There is a general lack of connectivity and without a company-wide network or a central database, it is impossible to perform business-oriented IS activities, such as business planning and telecommunication. Fifty-two (67%) of the surveyed firms reported that they had a central IS department whose major task was to perform computing activities rather than to support end-user computing or facilitate data communication among organizations: "It seemed that the only task of IS employees is to acquire hardware and software and to make the computer work".

## 4.3. Human factors

The level of support for IS by employees at different organizational levels is shown in Table 5. Engineers and IS employees usually show stronger support than management. If 3.00 represents a fair level of support, engineers and IS employees are generally quite supportive of IS application, while management is relatively negative toward it. Specifically, the resistance to the use of computers comes more from junior and middle management rather than from top management. This probably explains why some business-oriented information systems are hard to implement, despite the strong efforts made by top management.

## 4.4. Future plans for IS

Fifty-six firms (72%) reported that they anticipated a certain amount of investment within the next two years. The average amount was RMB\$810 000 yuan (US\$1=RMB\$8.28), with a minimum of RMB400 000 and a maximum of RMB6 000 000 yuan. More investment is planned for hardware (47%) and software (40%) than for development (9%) and training (4%). It seemed that most companies preferred to purchase IS than to develop them independently or with the help of consultants. To illustrate the inability of these projected budgets to meet adequate IS requirements, consider a popular manufacturing information system on the market today, namely manufacturing resource planning (MRP-II). This system generally includes over 20 modules of software, ranging from basic operational processing modules to high-level business-oriented ones. The key modules normally cost between \$20 000 and \$30 000 each. It is clear from the reported amount of anticipated IS investment so that most firms cannot afford to purchase an entire MRP-II system. In terms of the importance of the IS applications, engineering, accounting and production are those that will be most emphasized (see Table 7). Accounting is emphasized because the government is carrying out a plan to standardize financial statements. Engineering and production reflect the technical orientation of IS. The high priority they received indicates that the focus of future IS application will be on high-level technical activities rather than on business management practices. In fact, when asked how to gain competitive advantage in the future market, all the previously interviewed companies considered technological leadership to be the highest priority.

## 4.5. A canonical correlation analysis

Due to the loss of data or doubt about its accuracy, only 74 of the original 78 questionnaires were used for this analysis.

The relationship between organizational variables, particularly firm size, and the extent of an organization's adoption and the use of information technology has been the subject of much discussion. Evidence points to the existence of a direct relationship between firm size and the use and adoption of IT. Motivated by earlier studies, we were interested in examining whether any underlying relationship exists between demographic statistics and the current status of IS usage. Due to the small sample size and exploratory nature of this study, we adopted a canonical correlation analysis, attempting to identify and quantify the correlation between two appropriate sets of variables, namely, organizational characteristics and status of IS usage.

In order to obtain the score of the level of IS support from management, we first gave different weights to each management level – 3 to top management, 2 to middle management and 1 to junior management – to emphasize the importance of higher management support. The scores multiplied by their weights were then averaged to obtain a single score of the level of management support in a particular company. The overall computer literacy of management was computed by averaging values of the levels of management. The original data matrix was standardized before statistical analysis so as to rule out the effects of different numeric units.

One principal-component analysis and two canonical-correlation analyses were performed using SPSS. The principal-component analysis was performed on nine variables. The results were then compared with those obtained from canonical correlation; this was performed at the early stage of the study in order to explore some meaningful comparisons between the sets of data. It does not aim at building or testing causal relationships. Thus, canonical correlation was suitable in our exploratory research.

For the first canonical-correlation analysis, only two pairs of canonical variates are significant. ## 5. Conclusions

This research represents our ongoing efforts to study empirically IS issues in the Chinese manufacturing industry. In general, our findings have empirically verified many conclusions drawn in previous studies.

First, in terms of the current status of IS application in the Chinese manufacturing industry, we found that major IS activities have been upgraded from basic operational processing to complex technical applications. However, by applying the stages of IT growth model and comparing the current status of IS application with that in the U.S. and Taiwan, we have concluded that most Chinese manufacturing companies are still at the early stage of computerization and that IS application is basically a task-focused technical orientation rather than an end-user-focused business orientation. Computers and IS are mostly stand-alone systems used separately in different business functions. The benefits of facilitating communication and coordination via an integrated IS have rarely been achieved. There is no attempt to perform high-level IS activities, such as building a company-wide IS infrastructure, aligning IS solutions with business goals, or facilitating business process reengineering activities.

Second, there is an enormous gap between computing activities and management practices. When various functions are to be integrated into one computer-based information system that will result in significant changes in management practices, resistance from management will become the major impediment. In this sense, it can be predicted that the main barriers to future IS applications are not technical factors but human ones.

Third, by performing canonical-correlation analysis, this study has demonstrated that though large companies tend to have more computers and IS employees, they do not necessarily take advantage of their technical strengths to make profits. We have found instead that business performance (profits) is closely related to two human factors: level of management support and management's computer literacy.

We should point out that the findings presented in this paper must be treated with a certain degree of caution because of limitations inherent in the study. First, on account of the impossibility of conducting a large-scale survey, its validity suffers from small sample size. Second, because of the exploratory nature of this study, data-analysis methods are essentially descriptive. Canonical-correlation analysis has been applied primarily for descriptive purposes.

Practitioners in the industry should learn from the experiences of IS application in other countries. Although China has a strategy for the use of IS in the manufacturing industry, it has concentrated too much on technological issues. It must be recognized that proper consideration of human factors is another essential element of the strategy, and one that can significantly facilitates the application of effective information systems $[9]$ . Finally, when building an IS infrastructure for their organization, management should ensure that the strategy they adopt leads to business-oriented IS applications.
