# Information Technology Consultancy from the Accounting Viewpoint

Michael Braithwaite, Touche Ross Management Consultants

Abstract: Accounting practices are large, influential and growing rapidly. This chapter reviews very briefly the activities in a major accounting practice. The contribution of information technology to our work is discussed and some of the particular features of an information technology consultancy within that environment are reviewed.

The purpose of this paper is to give some insight into the way information technology (IT) consulting fits into the structure of a major accounting practice. IT consulting is only one part of a general consultancy which itself is part of an organization of some 38,000 people in 200 offices throughout the world. Indeed, it is the size and influence of the accounting practices that single out consultancies operating in this environment from those dedicated purely to the business of management consulting or consulting in the more specialized area of IT.

It is a surprise to many people that such large organizations are so little known; indeed, a considerable proportion of the gross national product of the US is audited by only eight partnerships. Touche Ross falls somewhere in the middle of the 'big eight' and while it is difficult, because of the private nature of the practices, to comment on the size and structure of our competitors, I suspect that much of what I have described will be common to other large firms. Not only are accounting practices large, but also, over the past few years they have demonstrated an accelerated growth rate.

Statutory audit is only one part of the activities, which are increasingly diverse. The main activities are:

\- Audit: a true and fair view of a company's accounts.

\- Taxation: lightening the load.

\- Management consultancy: solving business problems.

\- Insolvency: fighting financial failure.

\- Corporate finance: financing business development.

The mix of work in our practice has changed radically over the past ten years. Audit, while it has grown considerably, is far from being the major activity of the practice. Management consultancy is one of the most rapidly growing activities of the firm. Indeed, if one looks back to the late 1960s, accounting practices were a very minor part of the world of management consultancy. In numerical terms they now begin to dominate the market.

The purpose of this paper is to talk about IT consulting, but first let us reflect on the importance of information technology across the whole of our services (see Figure 1).

Returning to management consultancy, we now have an information technology group of some 120 people in London and the provinces.

We see an important line between the delivery of systems, which is the prerogative of a software house, and the provision of advice to a client. We are firmly on the advice side of the divide which we regard as the proper activity of consultants.

While we are always prepared to review the scope of our activities, at the present time we are more comfortable with the consulting role and believe that conflicts can arise. The primary problem is one of management style, but the possibility of conflict of interest between delivering a product and giving advice cannot be ignored.

Activities of the information technology consultant include:

\- Strategy consulting. There is an increasing demand from clients to conduct analysis in the area of competitive advantage – how can a business utilize information technology to achieve competitive advantage? This is coupled with the need to provide bridges to technical strategy, and the development of tools to support information analysis.

\- Systems development. This work embraces project management and the evaluation and implementation of tools of support system analysis, design and implementation. Increasingly the development of tools to evaluate risk associated with IT is an important part of the practice.

\- Small/medium systems. This term embraces systems from minicomputers to, say, IBM 4300 series. Increasingly it is concerned with the selection and implementation of package solutions to client problems.

\- Telecommunications and office automation. We have two groups working in these areas, which are growing and are an essential support to strategy work.

\- Microcomputers and small business advice. This is a group responsible for advising and training small clients on the use of microcomputers.

The international perspective of our firm is of growing importance to the consultancy. IT consulting is benefiting from transfer of technology between the US, Japan and Europe. We are seeing a growing market for international assignments driven primarily by the US or Japanese international companies.

What are we moving towards in the future? I see in our own practice a group of very able and highly motivated people who are grappling with increasingly challenging problems, the solutions to which can bring great benefits to our clients. The problems range from devising ways of analysing a firm's competitive position, and how one might relate technology to it, to the development of strategies for evaluating the security of a global network that transmits instructions controlling billions of dollars every day. The particular strength that identifies IT practices linked to accounting practices is the business judgement and skill that we can combine with a high level of technical expertise.

## Discussion

A contributor from James Martin Associates: You explained that there were two facets to management consultancy at Touche Ross: general management consultancy and IT consultancy. On both sides you mentioned strategy. How specifically do you build a bridge between strategy in general management consultancy and strategy in IT strategy consultancy?

Michael Braithwaite: Strategy consultancy is very different from general operational consultancy, partly because of the charges that can be made to clients and partly because of the demands for secrecy. For strategy consultancy, charges are much higher and the clients demand greater secrecy about the work being done. Therefore, there are cultural differences between the two types of consultancy. We use IT to bring the two approaches together. Specifically we are advising clients on how IT can be used in their organizations to provide them with strategic advantage. This is happening particularly in the financial services area and banking, as well as in retailing, where substantial changes are taking place at present.
