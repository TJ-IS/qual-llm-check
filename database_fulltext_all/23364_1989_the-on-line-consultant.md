# The On-Line Consultant Goff Sargent

Brrr, brrr. Brrr, brrr.

"Hello."

"Ah. Hello. I was given your name by a friend."

"Oh?"

“Yes. I hope you don’t mind me ‘phoning, only I’m thinking of buying a computer.”

"Ah."

"And I thought I'd better get some proper advice. Do you think that the Wastrad PC 49 is a good machine? I've heard that it is."

“Well, it’s really not as simple as that. There are lots of options to consider . . .”

“Yes, I know. It’s a real mine field. That’s why I thought I’d better get some proper advice.”

"What kind of things do you hope to use it for?"

“Well, the usual ones, really.”

"Wordprocessing? Databases? Accounts?"

“Mmm. That sort of thing.”

"Yes, but why do you want to buy a computer?"

“Well, I’ve got a little money to spend. I’ve got my own washing machine business, and I thought it would help. And the kids could use it at weekends. I thought I might write a book, too. Do you think the Wastrad PC 49 is a suitable machine?”

"It's certainly capable of running a lot of different types of software. Did you have a specific package in mind? Almost Perfik is a very popular word-processor that will probably meet your general needs. It's got a spelling checker and thesaurus."

“Doesn’t it come with any software? I had a friend who bought a Wastrad Pixie W 8118, and that came with it’s own software.”

"Oh, well I think they do bundle some software. I think there is a wordprocessor, but it's only a cheap one. I don't think you'll get an accounts package or a database. But I think there may be some kind of graphics package . . . WetPaint I think it is."

“Oh yes. Graphics. That’s like Desk-Top-Publishing, isn’t it. I’ll need that to make up some business leaflets to hand out.”

“Well, if it’s graphics you’re after, I’d recommend the new Peardrop Mic IIcc.”

"Yeah?"

“But for more mainstream business use, I’d go for an ICBM. Something like the new PS/TU with a 286 processor. It will still give you good graphics, but also have a wide range of business software available for it.”

"Yeah? What software do you get with that one then?"

“Well, you’d have to buy the software as an extra. There’s nothing bundled with it as such.”

"Yeah? But you think it's a better buy than the Wastrad PC 49, then?"

“Oh, it’s certainly faster, and it’s much more ruggedly made. You have to pay for quality.”

"Right! . . . About how much?"

"Well, you've got to be looking at nearly £2000 at the low end."

"What about the Wastrad? I hear it's a good machine for the price."

"What sort of amount did you have in mind?"

“Well, I didn’t want to spend too much. I thought I could do it for about £500.”

“Mmm. I don’t think you’ll get colour for that.”

"Ok. Black and white's fine for what I need."

"And how many floppies did you want?"

"How many do I need?"

“Well, it’s easier for things like copying if you have two drives. Will you need a printer?”

"Doesn't it have a printer?"

"No. You have to get that separately."

“I had a friend who bought the Wastrad Pixie W 8118, and it came with software and printer.”

“Well, you can still get that, but it’s now the Pixie W 8558. It’s rather old technology, and nobody writes programs for it much anymore.”

"But will it do what I want?"

"It won't do graphics."

"Right. But you think the Wastrad PC 49's a good machine?"

"Well . . ."

"Only, I'd heard that . . ."

“Oh, yes! They're great little machines.”

“Mmm. That’s what I’d heard. Well, thank you for your help. You don’t know what a comfort it is to have real professional advice.”

"Yes, I know. It's a real mine field."

## Biographical note
