# The relationship model between the influence factors and the strategic applications of information systems

J-M Choe, Y-H Lee and K-C Park

Department of Accounting, College of Business Administration, Kyungpook National University, Taegu, Puk-ku, Sankyukdong, 1370, 702-701, South Korea

In previous studies, some researchers empirically suggested the influence factors on the strategic applications of information systems (IS). However, they did not empirically prove the effects of the relationships among the influence factors on the strategic applications of IS. The objectives of this study are to examine these relationships and to identify the moderating effects of support factors on the relationship between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS. For this study, the research model explaining the relationships among influence factors and strategic applications level was developed. The results suggested that there is significant positive correlation between perceived environmental uncertainty and facilitators of alignment. The intervening effect of alignment facilitators and the moderating effects of support factors were also proved. Based on the empirical results, the roles or functions of influence factors and the managerial implications are proposed.

## Introduction

Research on the strategic applications of information systems (IS) has received significant attention in recent years. The use of IS for strategic purposes has been an important research topic and there have been a large number of studies on the strategic use of IS.

In the various research areas of the strategic applications of IS, many studies have focused on the investigation of the influence factors which facilitate and inhibit its strategic use (e.g., Johnston & Carrico, 1988; Neo, 1988; King et al, 1989; Kim & Michelman, 1990; Krcmar & Lucas, 1991; King & Sabherwal, 1992). Though they have tried to examine the influence factors empirically, previous studies generally investigated the simple relationship between influence factors and strategic IS.

Some researchers conceptually suggested the influence factors on the strategic applications of IS (e.g., Waeman & Walsham, 1990; Clemons, 1991; Wilkes, 1991). A few researchers empirically proposed the influence factors (e.g., Neo, 1988; King et al, 1989; King & Teo, 1994). However, in previous research the relationships among the influence factors and the strategic applications of IS were not empirically proved. The effects of the relationships among influence factors on the strategic use level of IS were not examined. To understand the role of influence factors and how to manage them to increase the strategic applications level, the relationships among influence factors need to be proved.

This paper represents an exploratory effort to empirically test the relationships among the strategic use level of IS and the influence factors in Korean business firms. Thus, the research questions of this study are: What are the relationships among the influence factors and the strategic use level of IS in the firm?; What is the role or function of the influence factors in increasing the strategic applications level?; How the influence factors should be managed for increasing the strategic applications level of IS in the firm?

## Influence factors on the strategic applications of IS

## Previous research

There are various influence factors on the strategic applications of IS. Through a case study, Neo (1988) suggested ten influence factors, such as alignment with business strategy, communication between management and IS, competitive pressure, internal/customer needs and management support, and he decided the ranks in the relative importance of influence factors. King et al (1989) presented similar influence factors, and they classified them into two broad groups, such as facilitator and inhibitor. In the study of Johnston and Carrico (1988), internal capabilities and conditions supporting the strategic use of IS were proposed as internal influence factors. In the other studies (e.g., Krcmar & Lucas 1991; Wilkes, 1991), similar or different influence factors were suggested.

Some studies focused on one main influence factor. Nath (1989) empirically identified those factors which the IS management and the general management perceived as important in aligning IS with business strategy.

Broadbent and Weill (1991) also investigated the organizational policies and practices which contribute to and enhance the alignment of IS with business strategy. Benjamin et al (1984) emphasised the importance of the top management’s role and actions in identifying and seizing the opportunities of the strategic applications of IS.

Decision processes for developing strategic IS are critical determinant factors in the successful strategic applications (Sabherwal & King, 1992, 1995). Utilising a contingency approach, Sabherwal and King (1992) empirically examined the relationships between the decision process attributes and the contextual variables. In their study, analysis, planning, politics, top management influence and IS influence were considered as decision process variables, and the contextual factors, such as environmental uncertainty, organization structure and IS maturity, were included.

A few researchers empirically investigated the effects of influence factors on the strategic applications. King and Teo (1994) indirectly examined the impacts of influence factors on the strategic applications of IS. In the study of King and Teo (1994), influence factors were classified into three broad categories. The three categories are internal factors, external factors and perceived needs. Internal factors are presence of a champion for strategic IS, computer facility, top management support, experience with IS, aligning IS with business strategy, and communication between IS and management, etc. External factors include market/economic growth, environmental change, competition and uncertainty in environment, etc. Perceived needs are the need to satisfy customer’s needs, to improve customer service and productivity and the need to lower costs, etc.

Based on prior research, various influence factors on the strategic applications of IS can be proposed, and the factors are grouped together into three categories: internal factors, external factors and IS related factors. In Table 1, the influence factors of each category are presented with their related studies.

## External factors as a cause factor

The three broad types of influence factors are internal factors, external factors and IS related factors. In these three types, the external factors, such as environmental competition, uncertainty and external needs, are driving forces for the strategic applications of IS (Benjamin et al, 1984). Challenging business environments can make it imperative for the firms that want to survive to seek out opportunities to use IS for strategic purposes. To use IS as a competitive weapon, precipitating factors are environmental competition and environmental threats to the firm (Neo, 1988; King & Teo, 1994).

In stable and simple environments, firms pursue a defensive strategy based on high efficiency and costeffectiveness, and the strategic IS use for innovation advantage is a costly luxury. So, the strategic applications of IS are not caused in the stable and non-competitive environments. However, without strategic IS use, firms would fall behind the competition in the rapidly changing and hostile environment. Firms must turn to the strategic use of IS as a means of responding to increasing competitive pressure or uncertainty in their environment (Johnston & Carrico, 1988; King & Sabherwal, 1992). Hence, the strategic applications of IS are caused by the challenging and competitive environment. In the various influence factors, external factors are cause factors that give rise to the strategic IS use. Strategic applications of IS are firstly spurred by the competitive and uncertain environment.

## The alignment of IS with business strategy and the facilitators of alignment

To cope with the challenging and uncertain external environment, firms must resort to strategic IS use, and must try to identify and seize the opportunities of the strategic applications of IS. There are some techniques to identify the strategic IS opportunities, such as critical success factors approach, strategic thrust/target framework, value-added chain model and business systems planning (BSP) methodology, etc.

A strategic IS application is defined as one having a profound effect on the company’s success and destiny by: (a) influencing or shaping the company’s strategy, or (b) playing a direct role in the implementation or support of the company’s strategy (Sabherwal & King, 1991). Therefore, in recognising and seizing the opportunities of strategic IS applications, the business goal and strategy should be properly considered.

Aligning IS with business strategy is a means that appropriately considers the business objectives or strategies in the strategic IS planning, and it is also a way to scan and search the strategic IS opportunities (Johnston & Carrico, 1988; Krcmar & Lucas, 1991). To identify and seize the strategic IS opportunities, the alignment of IS with business strategy is very critical because the strategic applications of IS are characterised by the mutual information flows and relations between business strategy and IS planning (Zviran, 1990; Premkumar & King, 1994). In aligning IS with the business goal and strategy, the opportunities of strategic IS use that supports the formation and implementation of the firm’s strategy can be found. Through the alignment of IS with business strategy, the strategic IS opportunities can be recognised and seized.

In aligning IS with business strategy, there are many facilitating activities, such as IS manager involvement in business strategy planning, upper management’s understanding about IS strategy, user participation in IS planning and top management commitment to IS (Nath, 1989; Broadbent & Weil, 1991). These facilitating activities enhance and contribute to the alignment of IS with business strategy. The success of aligning IS with business strategy is dependent on the conditions of these facilitators (Earl, 1993; Premkumar & King, 1994). Hence, according to the impact of the external factors, the facilitators must be arranged first for the successful alignment.

Both the facilitators of alignment and aligning IS with business strategy are influenced by the conditions of external factors, because all activities for the searching and identification of strategic IS opportunities are performed in coping with the uncertain external environment. So, the strength of the searching and identification activities is different according to the conditions of external factors (Johnston & Carrico, 1988).

## Other influence factors as a support factor

In recognising and seizing the strategic IS opportunities, there are two different methods, such as top–down scanning and bottom–up inventing (Krcmar & Lucas, 1991). The alignment of IS with business strategy is a top– down planning process with emphasis on linking organizational business strategies to IS planning.

After the strategic IS planning is well established through aligning IS with business strategy, the strategic IS planning is implemented for the successful strategic application of IS. However, in implementation, strategic IS projects may not be initiated and systems development may not proceed because of limited resources, top management’s hesitance, low expertise quality, technological constraints and organizational resistance (Earl, 1993). Hence, abundant resources, top management support, communication for lowering resistance, technical expertise of IS personnel and champion for strategic IS are the support factors for the successful implementation of strategic IS planning.

If firms identified the strategic IS opportunities through the strategic IS planning process, they should make an effort to turn the opportunities into the strategic application of IS. Many internal and IS related factors, except for aligning IS with business strategy, are the support factors in the realisation of the strategic applications of IS. The internal and IS related factors support and facilitate the process to turn the strategic IS opportunities into the strategic applications. Therefore, support factors have an effect on the relationship between the identification of strategic IS opportunities and the realisation of the strategic IS applications.

## Hypotheses

The hypotheses in this study describe the relationships among influence factors and strategic applications of IS, and the effects of support factors on the relationship between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic use of IS.

## The relationship between external factors and facilitators of alignment

Challenging and uncertain environments compel firms to seek ways to use IS competitively. The means to identify the strategic IS opportunities is the alignment of IS with the business goal and strategy. However, for this successful alignment the facilitators of alignment must be well arranged. The nicely prepared facilitators may sustain and expedite the identification of the strategic IS opportunities. If the facilitators of alignment are poor, the strategic IS planning process cannot be proceeded. Hence, the conditions of the alignment facilitators may be a proxy indicator of the aligning degree.

As the extent of environmental threat differs among firms, the conditions of facilitators and the degree of aligning IS with business strategy in firms are also different. The well arranged facilitators and the high degree of alignment are required in a more uncertain and competitive environment.

Based on the research model and the above arguments, Hypotheses 1 and 2 are presented.

Hypothesis 1: The external environment factors are positively related to the facilitators of alignment.

Hypothesis 2: The external environment factors have an indirect effect on the strategic applications level of IS through the facilitators of alignment.

## The effects of support factors

Top management vision and support seem to be a key factor for successful development of strategic IS. For successful strategic applications of IS, both the top management vision to see the strategic opportunities and the support to overcome barriers in developing the strategic IS are prerequisites. Some studies also emphasised the importance of having top management support for successful attainment of strategic IS (e.g., Benjamin et al, 1984; Lederer & Mendelow, 1988; Earl, 1993).

A breakdown in communication between the IS specialist and manager is a major hindrance to a firm’s application of IS for competitive advantage (Gerstein & Reisman, 1982). Gerstein and Reisman (1982) suggested that IS are the separate business entities which depend on the rest of an organization for business, and IS are forced to consider the business interests of the rest of an organization. Therefore, the communication between IS and management is necessary to increase the mutual understanding among IS and related departments, and to lower a resistance of the related users or managers in implementing the strategic IS planning.

In developing strategic IS, extensive resources, such as computer facilities, information technology and other information resources are required. A lack of adequate resources can lead to the strategic IS application being reduced in scope or aborted in the strategic IS planning. Hence, to secure necessary resources is one of the major problems in strategic IS development, and the resource capability of a firm may have a positive effect on the strategic application of IS.

Capability in IS function includes experience with IS, technical competence and expertise in the IS department. The development of strategic IS is greatly influenced by the IS department possessing relevant knowledge and skills. The IS personnel should not only possess significant business knowledge, but they must also provide the expertise on the complex information technology (Sabherwal & King, 1995). The strategic application of

IS is likely to be more successful as the IS personnel gain expertise and experience in designing and developing important IS.

IS use for competitive advantage is facilitated by the presence of a senior champion during the process in which the strategic IS application is identified and developed (Sabherwal & King, 1992). If there is no senior champion the strategic IS application is more likely to be curtailed and cancelled, since it is undertaken and implemented on its contribution to the firm’s competitive strategy rather than on its individual return on investment. Therefore, the senior champion must be at a high enough level to provide resources for developing the strategic IS and to protect the strategic application project from cutbacks or cancellation.

IS maturity represents the progress of the IS function from the era of data processing and management IS (MIS) into the strategic IS era (Sabherwal & King, 1992). So, IS maturity incorporates the various attributes considered critical to the identification and development of strategic IS, such as top management’s knowledge about IS, technical expertise of IS personnel, communication between IS and management, etc. Hence, the IS maturity which totally reflects the effects of facilitating or influence factors may have a positive impact on the strategic applications of IS.

Based on the prior findings, Hypothesis 3 is presented.

Hypothesis 3: The support factors, such as top management support, communication between IS and management, resources of a firm, capability in IS function, presence of senior champion and IS maturity, have a moderating effect on the relationship between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS.

## Research method

## Sampling

Data used in this study were drawn from a survey of the current status of MIS application and management in Korean business firms. One hundred organizations were randomly selected from a population of about 500 firms with a mainframe computer that are listed in the Korean stock market. Among these, 53 responded to the request for information and were finally included in the study.

Data were gathered by interviews based on structured questionnaires. Two kinds of questionnaire were prepared for the study, one for measuring the organizational external environment and the strategic applications level of IS, the other for measuring the IS manager’s perception with relation to influence factors. Questionnaires of the first type were distributed to the top managers of firms. Those of the second type were delivered to the managers of an IS department. For data collection, both the interview technique and the questionnaires were used. Before the questionnaire response, we explained the contents of the questionnaires and the answering methods. ## Strategic applications level of IS

McFarlan et al (1983) suggested an IS strategic grid as a framework to investigate the strategic importance of IS on the organization. The organization’s position on this grid is determined by the strategic impacts of existing and future IS and therefore represents the strategic applications level of IS in the firm.

Neumann et al (1992) empirically proposed seven significant variables that affect and measure the organization’s position on the IS strategic grid. Seven variables are the fit of IS to the organization’s critical success factors (CSFs), IS contribution to the organization’s competitiveness, IS support to tactical management, IS support to operations, IS contribution to improving profitability, IS contribution to financial gains through improved operations, and perceived overall criticality of IS. These seven variables were used to measure the strategic applications level of IS in the firm.

Seven question items for the strategic use level of present IS were developed, and another seven questions for measuring the strategic applications level of future IS were constructed. The strategic applications level of existing and future IS were measured on five-point

## The facilitators of alignment

The IS strategic alignment is defined as the fit existing between business strategy and IS strategy (Chan & Huff, 1993). In measuring the facilitators of alignment, nine factors which contribute to and enhance alignment were considered. The nine factors are as follows: IS manager’s understanding about business strategy; educating IS management of business goals and objectives; IS management involvement in strategic planning process of the organization; upper management’s understanding about IS strategy; educating upper management of IS strategy; user participation in IS planning; upper management commitment to IS; ability of IS management to keep up with advances in information technology; IS responsiveness to user needs (Nath, 1989; Broadbent & Weill, 1991).

By using these nine factors, nine questions to measure the facilitators of alignment were developed. In this study, the condition of alignment facilitators was measured on seven-point Likert-type scales.

## Capability in IS function

Capability in IS function means the capability of IS personnel (King et al, 1989; Sabherwal & King, 1995). Capability of IS personnel can be measured by average education/experience levels of IS group members (Ives et al, 1980). In this study, IS group members were classified into one of the five categories according to the level of experience, such as less than one year, one to three years, three to five years, five to seven years, and more than seven years. These categories were given the weights 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9, respectively, and the number of personnel belonging to each category was multiplied by the weight for the category. By summing up the scores and dividing by the total number of IS personnel, the average level of experience or capability was obtained.

External factors include environmental dynamism, heterogeneity, hostility, competition and external needs (King et al, 1989; King & Teo, 1994). However, perceived environmental uncertainty (PEU) totally includes and represents all these external factors (Duncan, 1972; Gordon & Narayanan, 1984). Therefore, in this study, PEU was used as the measure of external factors. Organization size is a surrogate measure of the resource capability of a firm (Franz & Robey, 1986; Raymond, 1990). In this study, organization size was used to measure the resources of a firm.

In each group, the principal component factor analysis using VARIMAX rotation can be performed, since the sample size meets the necessary number of samples. The results are presented in Table 5.

## The relationships among PEU, facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS

Pearson and partial correlation analyses were employed to access the relationships among research variables. Table 7 presents the correlation matrix for the research variables. There was a statistically significant positive relationship between the perceived environmental uncertainty (PEU) and the facilitators of alignment. Hence, in the more uncertain and competitive environment, the facilitating activities for the high degree of aligning IS with business strategy increase, and so Hypothesis 1 was supported.

To empirically prove the intervening effect of the facilitators of alignment on the relationship between the PEU and the strategic applications level of IS, partial correlation analysis was performed. The partial correlation values obtained are presented in Table 7 and can be compared with the initial zero-order correlations.

One can see that the positive relationship between PEU and strategic applications level becomes nonsignificant when the effect of the facilitators of alignment is removed. However, the significant positive relationship between facilitators of alignment and strategic applications level exists as before when the effect of PEU is removed. Thus, it is proved that the impact of PEU on the strategic applications level of IS is indirect rather than direct, and the facilitators of alignment have an intervening effect on the relationship between PEU and strategic applications level. Therefore, Hypothesis 2 was accepted.

## The moderating effects of support factors

To empirically test the effects of support factors on the relationship between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS, there are two basic analysis methods. One is subgroup analysis, and the other is moderated regression analysis (MRA) (Sharma et al, 1981). MRA is differentiated from subgroup analysis, since MRA maintains the integrity of a sample.

In applying MRA in terms of one predictor variable, three regression equations were formulated as follows:

$$
\mathbf {Y} = \mathbf {b} _ {0} + \mathbf {b} _ {1}. \mathbf {X}\tag{1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{b} _ {0} + \mathrm{b} _ {1} \cdot \mathrm{X} + \mathrm{b} _ {2} \cdot \mathrm{Z}\tag{2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{b} _ {0} + \mathrm{b} _ {1} \cdot \mathrm{X} + \mathrm{b} _ {2} \cdot \mathrm{Z} + \mathrm{b} _ {3} \cdot \mathrm{X} \cdot \mathrm{Z}\tag{3}
$$

where:

$$
\mathrm{Y} \quad : \quad \text { strategic   applications   level   of   IS },
$$

$$
\text { b } \quad : \quad \text { regression   coefficients },
$$

X facilitators of alignment (predictor variable),

Z support factor, and

$$
\mathrm{X} \cdot \mathrm{Z}: \quad \text {   interaction   of   X   and   Z.   }
$$

Following Sharma et al (1981), three regression analyses were performed in number order. If equations 2 and 3 are not significantly different $( \mathrm { i . e . , ~ b } _ { 3 } = 0 ; \mathrm { b } _ { 2 } \neq$ 0), Z is an independent predictor variable. For Z to be a pure moderator, equations 1 and 2 should not be different but should be different from equation $3 \ { \mathrm { ~ ( i . e . , ~ b } _ { 2 } = 0 } ;$ $\mathbf b _ { 3 } \neq 0 )$ . For Z to be classified as a quasi moderator, equations 1, 2 and 3 should be different from each other (i.e., $\mathsf { b } _ { 2 } \neq 0 ; \mathsf { b } _ { 3 } \neq 0 )$ (Sharma et al, 1981).

The nine support factors were tested separately. In the regression analyses, the steering committee was entered in the equation as a dummy variable because it was measured on nominal scale. The results of three regression analyses in each facilitating factor are presented in Tables 8, 9 and 10. The standardised , F value, change in $R ^ { 2 }$ and $P$ value for the support factor in the table were reported from equation 2 regression analysis. The standardised $\beta ,$ change in $R ^ { 2 }$ and P value for the interaction term were reported from equation 3 regression analysis.

The three interaction terms, such as budget amount, capability of IS personnel and IS maturity, were statistically significant, and budget amount, capability of IS personnel and IS maturity were not related to the strategic applications level of IS. Hence, in these cases, the three support factors are pure moderators. However, in IS maturity, the $\beta$ coefficient of interaction term is negative. Negative $\beta$ coefficient means that the relationship between facilitators of alignment and strategic applications level becomes weaker as the IS maturity increases.

In this study, the results of MRA were not satisfactory. If the result of MRA is unsatisfactory and the variable is theoretically serving as a moderator, the next analysis technique, subgroup analysis, can be employed (Sharma et al, 1981; Franz & Robey, 1986). For the subgroup analysis the observations of support factors, except for steering committee and rank of IS supervisor, were divided into two groups with the median value being employed as the dividing point. In each group, Spearman correlation analysis was performed. The correlations between facilitators of alignment and strategic applications level are represented in Table 11.

The correlation coefficients were compared between groups. In top management support and budget amount (% of revenue), there were no differences. When the communication and the firm size are considered, in both groups (i.e., low or small group and high or large group) the degree of facilitators was positively related to the strategic applications level of IS. However, whether the difference of correlation coefficients between groups was significant or not cannot be decided from simple comparison. The Fisher Z statistics can be used to determine the significance of the difference of correlation coefficients between groups (Nachmias & Nachmias, 1981).

The standard Z for the strategic applications level of IS were 0.98 for communication and 1.11 for firm size, and they were not significant. But, the correlation coefficients of the high communication and the large firms are greater than those of the low communication and the small firms. Hence, it is suggested that the strategic IS opportunities are more likely to lead to the strategic applications under the high communication between IS specialist and business management, and in the large firms because of their rich resource capabilities.

In the budget amount, the facilitators of alignment positively correlated with strategic applications level of IS in both groups (i.e., small budget group and large budget group). The Fisher Z test was performed, and the standard Z for the strategic applications level of IS was 1.57. The coefficient of standard Z was significant at the 10% level. Therefore, it is assumed that the association between facilitators of alignment and strategic applications level becomes greater with the large IS budget amounts.

In the IS personnel capability, the degree of facilitators was significantly related to the strategic applications level in the high capability group. The standard Z for the strategic applications level of IS was 2.98, and it was significant at the 1% level. Thus, in the firms that have IS personnel of higher capability, the degree of facilitators is more likely to correlate with the strategic applications of IS.

When IS maturity and steering committee are considered, the results were reversed. In the low mature group, the facilitators of alignment significantly correlated with the strategic applications level of IS, and in the firms that have no steering committee the correlation between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS was significant. The coefficients of standard Z were 2.59 for IS maturity and 1.77 for steering committee. They were significant at the 1% and 5% level, respectively. Hence, it is suggested that the relation between facilitators of alignment and strategic applications level is more enhanced in the firms that have no steering committee and whose IS maturity is low.

## Conclusion and discussion

In this study, the positive relation between the perceived environmental uncertainty and the facilitators of alignment was empirically proved. The intervening effect of the facilitators of alignment was also empirically suggested. So, the fact can be proposed that the impact of external factors on the strategic applications of IS is indirect.

The moderating effects of support factors on the relationships between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS were also empirically proved. It was found that large firm size, abundant budget amounts and high IS personnel capability can intensify the relations between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications of IS.

However, some results were different from the previous studies and some other results were contrary to the expected. The moderating effect of IS maturity on the relationship between the facilitators of alignment and the strategic applications level of IS was negative. There are various approaches to identify the strategic IS opportunities. The alignment of IS with business strategy is a method of top–down scanning (Vitale et al, 1986; Krcmar & Lucas, 1991). The negative moderating effect means that the firm may utilise other techniques, such as bottom–up inventing, to find and seize the strategic IS opportunities as the IS become more mature.

The moderating effect of a steering committee was also negative. The roles of steering committees are inconsistent with the conditions of IS or IS departments have an opposite impact on the development and use of IS (Nolan, 1982). Therefore, it is thought that the functions of steering committees, in some sample firms, had a negative effect on the development process of strategic IS, as it were, the relation between the alignment of IS with business strategy and the strategic applications of IS.

The moderating effect of the rank of IS supervisor was very vague. In the chief and the director, the correlation coefficients were positive and significant. But, there was no difference in correlation coefficients of the chief and the director. Even though the chief is not a high enough position, the chief may be a top senior champion in small firms. Thus, in small firms, the chief may actively make efforts for the realisation and development of strategic IS. The active role of a chief in small firms may be equal to the part of a director in large firms.

Based on the research findings, we can suggest the role or function of influence factors and the managerial implications. The external factors are a cause factor which gives rise to the strategic applications of IS. However, if there are no facilitators of alignment or aligning

IS with business strategy, as it were, if the search factors are not activated in the firms under the high uncertain environments, the strategic applications opportunities of IS cannot be identified and seized. Though the search factors identify and seize the strategic IS opportunities, without the help of support factors the strategic applications opportunities may not be realised and the strategic IS cannot be completely developed. Hence, the importance of the cause, search and support factors cannot be rated separately for the successful strategic applications of IS.

The evaluation of the relative importance in the cause, search and support factors’ groups may be meaningless, because the individual roles of the factors’ categories are different from each other. The factors’ groups, such as cause factors, search factors and support factors, should be considered and managed all together in attaining the strategic applications of IS. If the cause factors are a necessary condition, the search and the support factors are the sufficient conditions for the successful strategic applications of IS. When the search and support factors are considered, if the search factors are a necessary condition, the support factors may be a sufficient condition for attaining the successful strategic IS. The relative importance can be evaluated and managed in each factors’ category.

For the strategic applications of IS, the external environments to which the firm is related must be analysed and evaluated in advance. The environments analyses can improve the understanding of the various elements of a firm and its competitive strategy in relation to its customers, suppliers and competitors (Dennis et al, 1991). As the results of environments analyses, the environmental threats and disadvantages can be recognised. However, if there are no environmental factors which compel the firms to use IS strategically, can the strategic applications of IS not be induced?

Instead of the external factors, the internal needs, such as the need to lower costs, improve efficiency and operations, and the need to improve decision-making, etc, may cause the strategic applications of IS. The internal needs are another driving force in strategic applications, and they are related to the day-to-day transaction processing systems (Neo, 1988). Therefore, if the cause factors are the internal needs, the strategic applications opportunities may be recognised and identified through the bottom–up inventing. The bottom–up inventing is more likely to be the search method for the internal needs, and the top–down scanning may be the search method when the cause factors are the external environments.

If the strategic IS opportunities are identified, some support activities to turn the opportunities into strategic applications are required. As the supporting function, the rich resources, which are needed for the development of strategic IS, or the abundant budget amounts must be provided. For the high applications level of strategic IS, the IS personnel who have generalist skills are needed.

The skill types of IS personnel are classified into two groups, such as generalist skill and specialist skill (Benbasat et al, 1980). The generalist skills are closely related to organizations, people and society, and the specialist skills contain systems, computers and models (Benbasat et al, 1980). The generalist skills are required more for the successful attainment of strategic IS, since the strategic IS are characterised more by organizational aspects than technical features (Waeman & Walsham, 1990).

Hence, IS personnel should have various career path opportunities to acquire generalist skills. Shore (1983) proposed that the structure of the IS department should change from the triangular shaped organization to the pentagonal shaped to provide career path oppotunities for IS personnel. To support the development process of strategic IS, the rank of IS supervisor must be at a high enough level. If the position is not high enough, the authorities needed in developing strategic IS should be fully endowed.
