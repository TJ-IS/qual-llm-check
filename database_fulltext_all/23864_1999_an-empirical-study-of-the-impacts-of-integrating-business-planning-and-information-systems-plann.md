# An empirical study of the impacts of integrating business planning and information systems planning

TSH Teo<sup>1</sup> and WR King<sup>2</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Department of Decision Sciences, Faculty of Business Administration, National University of Singapore, 10 Kent Ridge Crescent, Singapore 119260; <sup>2</sup>Katz Graduate School of Business, University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA 15260, USA

This study reports on a follow-up analysis of a prior study (Teo & King, 1996, Information and Management) of the impact of the integration of information systems planning (ISP) with business planning (BP) on organizational performance. The empirical data are re-analysed using path analysis in order to determine the direct and indirect impacts of BP-ISP integration on intermediate performance measures related to ISP process and output problems, as well as on five perceptual measures of organizational performance. The results empirically substantiate the importance of BP-ISP integration, since higher levels of integration were found to have a significant inverse relationship with the extent of both process and output varieties of ISP problems and a significant positive relationship with each of the five perceptual measures of the extent of IS contributions to overall organization performance.

## Introduction

It is widely agreed that for strategic information systems planning to be effective, there must be some level of coordination between business planning (BP) and information systems planning (ISP) (Calhoun & Lederer, 1990; Das et al, 1991), which will be referred to here as ‘BP-ISP integration’.

BP-ISP integration involves the coordination of BP and ISP activities so that IS strategies are aligned with business goals and business strategies. If there is no coordination between BP and ISP, it would be very difficult for the IS function to adequately support business objectives and strategies or to contribute to the achievement of competitive advantage (Lederer & Mendelow, 1989). In addition, technical issues need to be integrated with business issues in order to produce high quality IT plans that focus on using IT strategically (Byrd et al, 1995).

Another dimension of BP-ISP integration involves the ‘reverse’ flow of information from ISP to BP. Flow in this direction depicts the impact that IS resources can have on business strategies (King & Zmud, 1981) through the creation of strategic systems—those that create competitive advantage for the firm—and through the creation of new information-intensive products and processes.

This study is an extended follow-up to the authors’ earlier work (Teo & King, 1996) and differs from the earlier one in two ways. First, it proposes a path model that examines the relationships among the degree of BP-ISP integration, ISP problems and organizational performance. Second, supplementary data analyses are carried out to examine the variation of ISP problems (defined in terms of process and output problems) and the five organizational impact measures with increasing levels of integration.

## Background

The major constructs addressed in this study are ‘BP-ISP integration, the extent of ISP process problems, the extent of ISP output problems’ and five perceptual measures of IS contribution to overall business performance—return on investment, market share, internal efficiency, sales revenue and customer satisfaction.

## BP-ISP integration

Past research on IS planning has generally examined planning approaches and methodologies (Ciborra, 1991; Lederer & Gardiner, 1992; Earl, 1993), problems and benefits (Lederer & Sethi, 1991, 1992a, 1992b), impact evaluation and planning effectiveness (Premkumar & King, 1991; Segars & Grover, 1998; Segars et al, 1998), stages of growth (King & Teo, 1997), role of steering committee (Raghunathan, 1992) and critical success factors (Ang & Teo, 1997).

Researchers have also examined the strategic integration or alignment of information technology (IT) with the enterprise, rather than alignment of planning processes alone. For example, Brown and Magill (1994) proposed a model of antecedents for IS alignment decisions. Henderson and Venkatraman (1993) proposed a comprehensive framework of IS strategic alignment incorporating four fundamental domains of strategic choice: business strategy, IT strategy, organizational infrastructure and processes, and IT infrastructure and processes.

In terms of mechanisms for integration, Lederer and Mendelow (1989) proposed three main mechanisms: content, timing and personnel. Content linkage is concerned with consistency between business plans and IS plans. This type of linkage has been empirically validated by Zviran (1990) who, in his study of 131 firms, found that IS objectives are associated with organizational objectives.

Timing and personnel linkages are generally less often emphasised in IS planning methodologies compared to content linkages. Timing linkage is concerned with whether IS plans are developed before, after or simultaneously with business plans. For consistency, business and IS plans should have the same planning horizons. Personnel linkage is concerned with whether participants in the BP process are also involved in the ISP process and vice versa. This is important in facilitating a common frame of reference among business and IS planners.

In contrast to Lederer and Mendelow’s three mechanisms of linkages, Reich and Benbasat (1996) defined linkage in terms of intellectual and social dimensions. The intellectual dimension is a refinement of content linkage as it emphasises both consistency (i.e., IT mission, objectives and plans are consistent with stated business mission and objectives) and external validity (i.e., plans are comprehensive and balanced with respect to external business and IT environments). Similarly, the social dimension is much broader than personnel linkage as it emphasises that both business and IS planners should understand each other’s objectives and plans.

Other areas that have been examined include factors influencing BP-ISP integration (Pyburn, 1983; Nath, 1989; Teo & King, 1997) as well as why such integration is difficult (Lederer & Mendelow, 1989). In addition, research has examined BP-ISP integration in terms of a one-way relationship with BP influencing ISP (King, 1978), two-way relationships with BP and ISP influencing each other (King & Zmud, 1981), and integrated relationships with business and IS plans being developed together concurrently in the same planning process (Goldsmith, 1991). Other researchers have proposed a typology of integration relationships (Synnott, 1987; Teo & King, 1996) and empirically tested a stage hypothesis of BP-ISP integration (Teo & King, 1997).

The typologies of BP-ISP integration developed by Teo and King (1996) and King and Teo (1997) serve as a basis for operationalising the ‘level or extent of BP-ISP integration’. We have chosen to use a four-level approach that is derived from King (1978), King and Zmud (1981), King (1984), Jang (1989), Synnott (1987), and Goldsmith (1991).

The four-level typology are:

Type 1: separate BP and ISP with administrative integration (scheduling, budgets, etc);

Type 2: one-way linked planning with sequential integration (BP feeding into ISP);

Type 3: two-way linked planning with reciprocal integration (BP feeding into ISP and ISP feeding back into BP); and

Type 4: integrated planning with full integration (BP and ISP conducted as an integrated process).

From the viewpoint of IS, the role of the IS function in types 1 and 2 is essentially reactive in nature since IS has negligible influence on business strategies. Conversely, the role of the IS function in types 3 and 4 is more proactive in nature since IS plays both a role in supporting and influencing business strategies.

These four levels were operationalised in this study through a description and graphical illustration of each type of BP-ISP integration (see Appendix).

## ISP process and output problems

The intermediate impacts of the lack of BP-ISP integration are examined in terms of ‘the extent of ISP problems’ (e.g., Lederer & Sethi, 1991). The business relevance and quality of ISP have been found to be dependent on the extent of BP-ISP integration, which should help to ensure that IS strategies are congruent with business strategies (Lederer & Mendelow, 1989). Problems associated with ISP can undermine its quality, thereby resulting in wasted resources and lost opportunities (King, 1988; Lederer & Sethi, 1992a). It appears that such ISP problems can be mitigated by greater BP-ISP integration (Lederer & Mendelow, 1989).

ISP problems have been discussed in terms of resource, process and output problems (Lederer & Sethi, 1988), organizational, commitment/contractual, outcome/expectations, expertise/technical and implementation problems (Hoffer et al, 1989), leadership, implementation and resource problems (Lederer & Sethi, 1992a), organization, implementation, database, hardware, and cost problems (Lederer & Sethi, 1991, 1992b).

The extent of ISP problems is operationalised in this study in terms of problems associated with the ISP process and problems associated with ISP outputs. Problems associated with the ISP process concern the suitability of inputs as well as the level of involvement of non-IS staff. They include the lack of user or business management involvement, the failure to take organizationa goals and strategies into account, inappropriate and unrealistic planning assumptions, etc. Problems associated with the ISP output concern the appropriateness and comprehensiveness of IS plans. They include the absence of hardware and database plans, whether IS plans meet the expectations of top management, etc.

## Impact on organizational performance

The ultimate contribution to organizational performance is the most straightforward basis for assessing the impact of planning systems. However, empirical establishment of the link between ISP and organizational performance is elusive (Crowston & Treacy, 1986; DeLone & McLean, 1992) and often controversial (Hamilton & Chervany, 1981a, 1981b). Similar difficulties have been found in business strategy research which entails mixed findings regarding the effects of BP on organizational performance (e.g., Pearce et al, 1987).

Despite these difficulties, Das et al (1991) cited an AT Kearney study which showed that organizations that aligned business and IS plans outperformed those who did not. Similarly, Chan and Huff (1993) found that IS strategic alignment was consistently related to various dimensions of IS effectiveness although mixed results were obtained with respect to IS alignment and various dimensions of IS performance.

IS contributions to organizational performance have been successfully assessed in terms of perceptual measures of improved return on investment (ROI) for the firm, increased market share for the firm’s products/services, improved internal efficiency of the firm’s operations, increased annual sales revenue for the firm, and increased customer satisfaction (Premkumar & King, 1991). These perceptual measures are adopted in this study.

## Research hypotheses

In this study, the independent construct is the ‘level of BP-ISP integration’. The intermediate dependent construct is ‘the extent of ISP problems’ and the overall dependent construct is the ‘impact on organizational performance’. The research literature suggests that greater BP-ISP integration will influence the organization in terms of both intermediate and overall impacts.

## Hypotheses related to ISP process and output problems

Past research has found that ISP problems tend to be less severe when planning is strategic rather than simply financial or tactical (Lederer & Sethi, 1991). Greater degrees of BP-ISP integration are necessary when planning is strategic because of the greater need to employ IT to achieve business objectives. BP-ISP integration should facilitate communication between business management and IS management, the cultivation of a better level of mutual understanding between the two parties, as well as a more realistic expectation of the role of the IS function. It follows that a greater extent of BP-ISP integration should lead to a reduction of both process and output varieties of ISP problems. The first hypothesis is therefore.

H1: The level of BP-ISP integration is inversely related to the extent of:

(a) ISP process problems.

(b) ISP output problems.

## Hypothesis related to organizational performance

The IS function is better able to support business objectives and strategies when there is a greater degree of BP-ISP integration (Nath, 1989). If the extent of integration between BP and ISP were low, IS might exist ‘for its own sake’ rather than to support business goals. On the other hand, a greater extent of integration between BP and ISP should enhance the role of IS in the achievement of strategic business objectives, thereby making IS contributions to overall organizational performance more salient and direct. The extent of BP-ISP integration may directly influence organizational performance and also indirectly through its impact on the reduction of ISP problems. Hence, the second hypothesis is:

H2: The extent of BP-ISP integration influence organizational performance directly and indirectly via a reduction of ISP problems.

## Methodology

## The sample

A sample of 600 firms was randomly selected from the Corporate 1000 book which lists the top 1000 companies in America. The firms listed therein are generally medium-to-large in size and are therefore, more likely to have greater experience in ISP than smaller firms.

Two questionnaires were prepared—one addressed to the senior business planner and the other to the senior IS executive for a business organization. Both questionnaires were sent to the corporate chief executive officer (CEO) with instructions requesting him/her to forward one copy to the senior business planner and the other to the senior information systems executive. The directions for distribution stated that if ISP is carried out centrally at the corporate level for most business segments (i.e., divisions or business units), the questionnaires should be forwarded to the senior business planner and the senior information systems executive responsible for BP and ISP at the corporate level. Conversely, if ISP is carried out independently in each business segment (i.e., division or business unit), the CEO was asked to select a core business segment, and forward the questionnaires to the senior business planner and the senior information systems executive responsible for that business segment.

## The instrument

The four levels (types) of BP-ISP integration (administrative integration, sequential integration, reciprocal integration, and full integration) were described in the instrument. Respondents were asked to indicate the type that best described their firm’s current level of BP-ISP integration.

The business planners were also asked to evaluate the extent of IS contributions to organizational performance. The IS executives were asked to respond to items relating to the current type of integration and also to the extent of ISP problems in their organizations. Since the current type of integration and IS contributions to organizational performance are measured using different respondents, the problem of common source variance is reduced.

The survey instrument is shown in the Appendix. The development of the instrument involved a series of pretests using doctoral and MBA students, IS practitioners, and graduate business school faculty members over a period of about three months. Changes to the instrument were made following comments and feedback from respondents at each stage of pretesting. The pretests resulted in changes in the wording of certain items and the rearrangement of the order of the items to improve clarity and minimise ambiguity. In particular, items pertaining to ISP problems were reverse coded whenever possible to minimise bias due to the negative connotation of the construct. The pretests also provided the basis for the distribution procedure since it clearly indicated that there was a need to identify the organizational unit that was to be the research context.

## Response and non-response analysis

Two follow-up mailings were made about three and seven weeks respectively from the date of initial mailings. There were forty-five firms which declined participation. Among the reasons cited are as follows: company policy not to respond to any questionnaire, lack of manpower, or company currently undergoing restructuring. Usable ‘matched pairs’ were returned by 157 firms (26.2%). The relatively low response rate despite two follow-up mailings may be explained by the fact that not only were two responses per firm required but also that both respondents must be senior executives.

The characteristics of the respondents are summarised in Table 1. There is a wide range of industries, with a predominance of manufacturing firms. Previous research using samples from the Corporate 1000 (e.g., Neo, 1989) also showed the same pattern. The annual sales revenue is widely distributed, in the anticipated medium to high range, from below \$300 million to above \$5 billion. There is a predominance of firms with greater than 10 000 employees. Both the job titles of business planners and IS executives confirm that respondents are mainly senior executives in the firm, since more than 70% of respondents are at the level of director or higher.

Non-response bias was assessed by comparing respondents and non-respondents in terms of industry representation, annual sales revenue and the number of employees. The results of chi-square tests on these data did not show any indication of non-response bias.

## Assessing validity and reliability

Since a single item might not fully tap a construct or might be subject to misinterpretation by the respondents, multiple items derived from past research were used for each construct. For statistical analyses, the multiple items comprising a construct were summed together into a single score. This assumes that each item contributes equally to the total score. This approach has been used successfully in previous research examining ISP (e.g., Premkumar & King, 1991) and the strategic use of IT (e.g., Neo, 1989).

The reliability of the scales was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha which gives an indication of the internal consistency of the items measuring the same construct. High values of Cronbach’s alpha indicate high internal consistency of the multiple items measuring each construct and hence, high reliability of the individual constructs. In general, the reliability coefficients are above the recommended value of 0.70 (see Table 2) for exploratory research (Nunnally, 1978).

Since the existing level of integration was measured by asking respondents to place a check-mark beside one of the four descriptions of the types of integration (see Appendix), it is important that respondents are able to understand and distinguish among the four types. As a validity check, telephone calls were made to respondents who indicated their willingness to be contacted (these organizations comprised about 70% of the 157 firms who responded). The purpose of the telephone calls is to determine whether the respondents had any difficulty in understanding or distinguishing among the descriptions of the four types of integration. The results of these telephone interviews showed that none of the respondents had any difficulty in understanding or distinguishing among them.

## Analyses and results

## Distribution of types of integration

The descriptive results show that sequential integration (n = 65) and reciprocal integration (n = 65) occur most often, followed by administrative integration (n = 17) and full integration (n = 10) as reported previously (Teo & King, 1996). This distribution of the types of integration is intuitively appealing since most firms of this size have been performing ISP for some period and since there is believed to be a natural evolution from lower to higher levels of integration.

In other words, in today’s technological era where there is an increased importance of IT, most firms are likely to move forward rapidly from administrative integration. Based on the small number of firms that reach full integration, we can perhaps conclude that ISP has yet to be an integral part of business planning for most firms. Consequently, it is perhaps not surprising that most firms in our sample are midway between administrative and full integration.

## Hypotheses testing

Path analyses using ordinary least-squares hierarchical multiple regression analyses were run to test the research hypotheses. Briefly, path analysis is a method of measuring the influence of explanatory variables along each separate path in a system by finding the degree to which variation of a given effect is determined by each particular cause (Kendall & O’Muircheartaigh, 1977).

The results of the path analysis are summarised in Table 3 and shown in Figure 1. Note that the value of PROBLEM is obtained by averaging the sum of the items that make up ISP process (PROCESS) problems, and ISP output (OUTPUT) problems.

The results showed that there is a significant negative relationship between INTEG and PROBLEM, thereby lending support to hypothesis 1. This result is intuitively appealing since greater BP-ISP integration should lead to a reduction in the extent of ISP problems due to better coordination between business planners and IS planners. As expected, INTEG had a direct significant positive influence on PERFORM. This result is also intuitively appealing as greater extent of integration should lead to better understanding and clarification of the role of the IS function, and consequently facilitate greater level of IS contributions to organizational performance.

However, the path analysis shows that PROBLEM has a significant positive relationship with PERFORM. This result is counter-intuitive since we would expect that the lesser the extent of ISP problems, the greater the extent of IS contributions to the firm’s performance. Hence, hypothesis 2 is only partially supported.

However, the overall effect of INTEG on PER-FORM—both direct and indirect—can be calculated as {0.43 + (−0.44)(0.18)} which equals +0.35. This implies that INTEG has an overall positive relationship with PERFORM even in the presence of ISP problems.

There are three possible reasons for the counterintuitive positive relationship between PROBLEM and PERFORM—i.e., that higher levels of problems are associated with higher levels of organizational performance. First, PERFORM is a much broader construct than PROBLEM since it encompasses the whole organization, whereas PROBLEM is confined to the ISP domain. Consequently, there are many other factors than PROB-LEM that can influence PERFORM. For example, PER-FORM can be also influenced by internal factors such as inadequate resources or external factors which are not related to PROBLEM, such as the business environment.

Second, firms who perceived a greater extent of IS contributions to organizational performance may have more sophisticated IS applications than others. Consequently, more ISP problems may be encountered during the development and implementation of these sophisticated IS applications. Indeed, many IT strategic applications in use today encountered and resolved many ISP problems prior to their success.

Third, firms with greater integration may be better at identifying ISP problems. This may mean that there is a greater likelihood that ISP problems will surface and be dealt with.

Consequently, with earlier and better recognition of ISP problems, appropriate and timely effort can be made to resolve them, thereby resulting in greater IS contributions to organizational performance. Furthermore, with greater involvement of business management in ISP, more resources may be made available to resolve these ISP problems.

## Supplementary data analyses

To further examine the results, we analysed the performance measures across the types of integration. Although the sample size for administrative and full integration are rather small compared to that for sequential and reciprocal integration, we decided not to delete or combine any type of integration as we wanted to plot the variation of performance measures with increasing levels of integration. This method would enable more direct comparison with our previous paper (Teo & King, 1996) which also did not combine any types of integration. Table 4 shows the means and standard deviations of the performance measures grouped by each type of integration. The results of a one-way ANOVA test showed that the group means for ISP process (PROCESS) problems, and ISP output (OUTPUT) problems, as well as IS contributions to organizational performance (PERFORM) differed significantly. This can be considered to be supportive of the existence of differences among firms which use the various types of integration in terms of ISP process problems, ISP output problems, and organizational performance.

## Post-hoc analyses—intermediate impacts

In order to analyse the results further, a post-hoc analysis using the Scheffe procedure was carried out. The Scheffe procedure permits pairwise comparisons of the level of integration. It is usually carried out after a significant Fvalue is obtained in a one-way ANOVA test in order to determine which two groups are significantly different (Marascuilo & Serlin, 1988). Table 5 presents the significant pairwise comparisons of the levels of planning for each of the significant variables. As expected, significant differences typically arise between reactive types of integration (i.e., administrative or sequential integration) and proactive types (i.e., reciprocal and full integration). This may be because there are important similarities among the two reactive levels and the two proactive levels of integration with substantial differences between the two groups in the role played by the IS function.

These data are also plotted in Figure 2 to determine if there are any distinct trends among the performance measures.

From Figure 2, it can be seen that ISP process and output problems are generally less severe at reciprocal or full integration than at administrative or sequential integration. Both ISP process and output problems show a distinct decreasing trend across the increasing levels of integration. This might be expected since firms with higher levels of integration might have better communication and understanding between business management and IS management. The existence of some ISP problems may be related to the lack of congruence and synergy between business planning and ISP, and the severity of ISP problems may be correspondingly reduced at higher levels of integration.

## Post-hoc analyses—overall impacts

The results in Figure 2 also show that there is a distinct increase in the extent of IS contributions to organizational performance between reactive types of integration (i.e, administrative or sequential integration) and proactive types of integration (i.e., reciprocal or full integration). This result provides evidence for the beneficial impact of proactive levels of BP-ISP integration in increasing the extent of perceived IS contributions to organizational performance.

Relationships between each of the extent of IS contributions to organizational performance—increased ROI (ROI), increased market share of products/services (MKTSHR), improved internal efficiency of operations (INTEFF), increased annual sales revenue (SALES), and increased customer satisfaction (CUSTSAT)—are shown in Figure 3.

From Figure 3, we note that the contribution of IS to increases in internal efficiency of operations is dominant, followed by its contribution to increases in customer satisfaction. The levels of these contributions generally increase as the level of BP-ISP integration increases. The contributions of IS to improved ROI, increased market share of products/services and increased annual sales revenue are approximately of the same magnitude and they generally show higher values for proactive levels (reciprocal and full integration) than for reactive levels (administrative and sequential integration).

The contributions of IS to improved internal efficiency of operations reflect the traditional role of IS in automating work processes. This impact may be broadest since it reflects an IT use that has been the longest and the most predominant in business. The contributions of IS to increased customer satisfaction reflect the increased use of IT to provide better services to customers. These results are consistent with recent research (e.g., King & Teo, 1994) which shows that strategic applications of IT are commonly directed at improving internal operations and providing better services to customers, e.g., providing customers with easy access to accurate and timely information.

The contributions of IS to improved ROI, increased market share of products/services and increased annual sales revenue are shown to be of relatively smaller magnitude, perhaps because of the well-known methodological difficulty in finding a direct relationship between IS and financial impact measures. Indeed, this elusive link between IS and organizational performance has given rise to much research examining the ‘productivity paradox’ of IT (Brynjolfsson, 1993) as well as to research proposing appropriate dependent variable(s) to measure IS success (DeLone & McLean, 1992).

The finding that proactive types of integration are associated with these financial measures to a greater extent than reactive types of integration are, should reassure practitioners that a two-way relationship between business management and IS management is worthwhile despite the methodological difficulties of empirically linking IS to organizational performance.

## Concluding remarks

This study builds on and expands existing research on BP-ISP integration. The significant positive relationship that was found between the level of BP-ISP integration and the extent of IS contributions to organizational performance provides support for the value of higher levels of BP-ISP integration. Moreover, the significant difference between the levels of organizational impact that are associated with proactive types of BP-ISP integration and those associated with reactive types reflects positively on the more ‘advanced’ levels of BP-ISP integration. Such results are supportive of previous research (e.g., Martinez, 1995) that emphasises the importance of a partnership between business management and IS management.

The study is also important methodologically since it may explain the conflicting findings of previous empirical studies on the relationship between ISP and organizational performance. The study indicates that future research examining the contributions of the IS function to organizational performance should preferably include the level (type) of BP-ISP integration as a research variable.

The extent (type) of integration was also found to have a significant negative relationship with the extent of ISP process and output problems. This result suggests that there are benefits of greater BP-ISP integration in mitigating ISP problems by facilitating greater communication and understanding between business management and IS management. However, the unexpected and counterintuitive positive relationship between ISP problems and organizational performance will require further study.

Overall, this study provides empirical evidence of the importance and usefulness of BP-ISP integration in mitigating ISP problems and increasing IS contributions to organizational performance. As such, it represents a step in an ongoing stream of research that can prove beneficial in the design of IS strategic planning processes.
