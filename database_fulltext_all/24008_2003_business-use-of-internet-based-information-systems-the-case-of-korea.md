# Business use of Internet-based information systems: the case of Korea

<sup>1</sup>Department of Business Administration, Sejong University, Kunja-dong, Kwangjin-gu, Seoul, Korea

## Abstract

The potential of the Internet to reach a large and growing body of customers, coupled with low communication costs, makes it a very attractive business medium to many organizations. Although there is significant interest in the use of the Internet for business purposes, studies articulating issues that can guide business managers in its use are lacking. The purpose of this study is to examine the issue of business activity on the Internet and report the results of a survey on the ways in which businesses are making use of Internet-based information systems (IIS). In addition, the study investigates the relations between the implementation and performance of an IIS to examine its potential to provide benefits to IIS adopters. A total of 120 usable responses were obtained. This paper identifies pertinent issues and problems that are critical in developing an IIS. The results also show that volume sophistication and information contents in an IIS partially affect the extent of cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement.

## Introduction

The Internet had its origins in the early 1960s, when the US Department of Defense developed the early network known as the Advanced Research Projects Agency network (ARPANET). The later entry of more companies on to the Internet was facilitated both by lowering set-up costs and by providing ubiquitous access (Kalakota & Whinston, 1996). The rapid growth of the Internet has increased interest in its potential use as a vehicle for transactions between customers and suppliers. As an innovative system, Internet-based information systems (hereafter IIS) have received much attention because they can have considerable organizational impact. The Web’s ease of use and multi-media approach to the presentation of information attracts potential customers. By 2002, the total amount of goods sold on-line will have reached \$327 billion according to Forrester Research Inc. (Hof & McWilliams, 1998). Customers procure products, services, and information through various IIS as they support on-line purchasing through an electronic channel, that is, the Internet, via electronic catalogs or other innovative formats (Spiller & Lohse, 1997– 1998). People can visit ‘virtual malls,’ and enter various ‘virtual shops’, browse through catalogues and examine various products in vast detail.

In order to stand a better chance of achieving competitive advantage, a great number of organizations have implemented such Internet-based systems (Gonsalves et al., 1999). For instance, retailers like Amazon.com, Virtual Vineyards, and 1-800-FLOWERS are well-known companies that provide ‘cybermediation’ and great efficiency in supply and marketing channels. The benefits of an IIS include more efficient payment systems, shortened time to markets for new products and services, realizing improved market reach, and customization of products and services (Berthon et al., 1996; Spar and Bussgang, 1996). Innovative applications using the Internet are constantly reshaping business practices in terms of enhancing customer service, improving product quality, and lowering cycle times in business processes. New areas of business opportunities for retailers, producers, and consumers can be developed from virtual markets on the Internet.

The high expectations surrounding the consumer potential of the Web stems from the Web’s unique features as a direct marketing channel and its perceived business advantages. The number of losers, however, still far exceeds the number of winners for Internet commerce ventures. Despite the importance of the Internet, there is a lack of published research on the Internet phenomenon, particularly in Asia (Teo et al., 1995). Several studies have examined the diffusion of the Internet, and its commercial use (Cockburn & Wilson, 1996; Poon & Swatman, 1999a). The purpose of this paper is to enhance the current understanding of the Internet phenomenon in Korea where exponential Internet growth has occurred. The aim of the survey reported here was to examine the extent to which electronic commerce (EC) and, in particular, business use of IIS has actually progressed.

## Business use of IIS in Korea

At the end of 1994, the Korean government established a plan to build a nation-wide information superhighway by the year 2015. This plan is similar to the IT 2000 project in Singapore or the NII project in the U.S. In line with this plan, Korea moved a step closer to achieving its vision of becoming a fully networked country. The Internet has grown to become a major component of the national information infrastructure, linking millions of computers and tens of millions of users around the country. In 1999, there were an estimated four million Internet users in Korea. The number of hosts that provided Internet service was 7650 in 1993; by 1998 there were 200,000 (Cho, 1999). There were an estimated 43 million Internet hosts in 1999, a substantial growth from 1.3 million hosts in 1993. The number of Internetbased shopping mall systems stood at 2000. The gross sales through electronic markets in Korea are predicted to hit 9.6 billion dollars in 2003. The total sales of the 38 largest companies that operate Internet shopping mall systems reached 0.11 billion dollars in 1999 and 0.42 billion dollars in 2000 (KNCA, 1999). The total sales through EC is estimated to be 9.6 billion dollars for 2003, while it was 0.4 billion dollars in 1998. The most common products dealt with in Internet-based shopping mall systems include books, computer-related equipment, electronic parts, and home appliances.

The collaboration of public and private-sector organizations has facilitated the development of Internet business. As investors, public organizations, such as the Ministry of Information and Communication and Ministry of Industry Resources, and private organizations sponsored by government such as Korea Telecom, Dacom, and SK-Telecom have provided the basic telecommunications infrastructure. Private sector organizations include retailers, banks, stock investment companies, system integration companies, and entertainment companies.

The applications being developed include government applications (e.g., on-line selling by the postal service, Web-based electronic data interchange in trade applications). Other examples of IIS in Korea include direct online selling systems such as Internet-based (or Web-based) shopping mall systems and Internet-based stock trading systems. Travel- and hobby-related sites providing leisure information are also among the common websites. Since 1997, when cyber trading was first allowed, there has been exponential growth of Internet-based stock trading systems in Korea. Electronic cyber trading was utilized by Korean Securities companies in processing more than 50% of all the transactions in 1999, and in two or three years, it is predicted that more than 80% of all transactions will be made through the Internet (Moon & Ahn, 1999). Electronic payment systems, extranet, electronic data interchange (EDI), and supply-chain management software support have become crucial elements to enhance supply and distribution logistics.

Among the three distinct identifiable classes of EC applications (Applegate et al., 1996) – business-to-customer, business-to-business, and intra-organization – IIS deals with the business-to-customer class. The development of EC in Korea is clearly an innovation in the way in which commercial transactions between organizations, or between organizations and consumers, are conducted. IIS is described as a Web presence with information about a company, product, or service and facilities for purchasing, which may be on-line or conventional. IIS includes a Web presence that provides information on specific matters such as entertainment, real estate, or financial investment, and encourages potential customers to spend money in using the information. Korean companies have begun to implement high-resolution color displays, video boards, sound, high-speed modems, and ample data storage, which together compose the enabling medium for on-line Web-based shopping. Physical goods are delivered by a common carrier and post-purchase support is electronically provided. Consumers that browse through catalogues and look for various products in ‘virtual malls’ packed with ‘virtual shops’ are provided with an experience akin to shopping in the real world.

## Key issues and exploration hypotheses

To address the most prominent IIS implementation management concerns, this study focused on elucidating two research issues: (1) the characteristics of IIS implementation in Korea and (2) the relationship between IIS implementation and performance. The first issue deals with the actual implementation of an actual IIS. IIS characteristics under investigation are: (1) the system features of an IIS; (2) the implementation and performance of an IIS; (3) objectives of adopting an IIS; (4) impediments that firms have encountered using an IIS; (5) future enhancement to be made in IIS.

The second issue focuses on the possible relationship between IIS implementation and performance. Innovation is implemented over a long time period, while adoption is a single event. IS implementation is an organizational effort directed toward diffusing information technology within an organization (Cooper & Zmud, 1990). After an organization has formally adopted an IS, the implementation has to proceed comprehensively within the organization to reap benefits from the IS. The IS should be used in an integrated manner to support the widespread transfer of its technology to various applications, which could then significantly redesign the business process (Premkumar et al., 1994). The full potential of the IS can be realized as the advocates of its implementation (or innovation) gain wider acceptance (Swanson, 1994). This cannot be obtained by merely automating the transaction processing or communication procedures. IS implementation will tend to be increasingly more elaborate with additional business functions, as users’ needs are newly identified and met by various extensions and enhancements. Organizations may derive full benefits from an interorganizational system (IOS) by expanding external services and electronic links to realize economies of scale (Vitale, 1985).

Since a comprehensive model including a wide range of environmental and organizational variables would be difficult to test, this study focuses on the role of the extent of IIS implementation on IIS performance. Figure 1 indicates the key variables and associations tested. Previous research has only marginally investigated the possible associations between IIS implementation and performance. This study will test if the extent of IIS implementation, represented by volume, sophistication, and information contents (see the section on Research method for detailed definitions of these variables), affect IIS performance (cost savings, financial performance, service improvement).

The ‘extent of IIS implementation’ indicates the extent to which an IIS is used and spread throughout an organization. The IS success model by DeLone & McLean (1992) suggests that IS usage is positively related to other measures of IS success, such as user satisfaction and individual and organizational impact. It has also been argued, however, that system usage and perceived system effectiveness are not always positively interrelated (Srinivasan, 1985). For instance, the extent of use would be a misleading success indicator if the system were considered as a service rather than a product that is developed to help managers to perform more effectively (Ginzberg, 1978), or if the system were implemented reactively from the influence of external parties (e.g., industry association, government). There may be no alternative but to use that system to effectively complete the task. Furthermore, the implementation of an IIS, as a measure of IIS success, only makes sense when system implementation is voluntary rather than compulsory. The growing use of IIS stemming from requests by external influential companies or industry influences makes the use of IIS an inappropriate surrogate for IIS success. It is even more difficult to isolate the impact of IIS implementation on overall organizational performance because of the difficulty in isolating the contributions of IIS from other factors.

IIS performance is measured in terms of the major benefits realized from cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement. The positive effects of IS implementation on performance have been consistently proven. For example, integration of information collected through EDI with internal IS applications has been asserted to be a critical factor for system effectiveness and efficiency (Premkumar et al., 1994; Iacovou et al., 1995). Integration of EDI can lead to better customer service and improved inter-firm relationships because rapid and accurate information can be provided to trading partners (suppliers and customers linked by EDI). The internal needs, as well as the requirements of external partners, must be satisfied in order to attain EDI system objectives through the integration of information with external networks. Internal applications such as accounts payable and receivable, and production planning systems may be efficiently processed in conjunction with an EDI system. For example, JIT (Just In Time) production systems are commonly connected with EDI systems to shorten response and turnaround times. JIT inventory management supplies parts to production lines as they are needed. The rapid and accurate transmission of shipping notices to the production department is critically important in order to eliminate the need for a large inventory of safety stocks. To compress cycle time and improve data integrity for the rapid transmission of data, the supplier’s computer must be integrated with the customer’s.

IIS should be used in a comprehensive manner to support upper-level management and to facilitate the widespread transfer of the technology to other departments in the organization. The adoption and implementation of the IIS may impose significant one-time costs on an organization, as its internal systems need to be adjusted in order to permit an interface with trading partners. It is difficult to obtain significant benefits from a costly change in business process unless volume has reached a level sufficient to warrant the change over. The transaction volume should be large enough to cover the initial high investment cost by reducing administrative costs. The goals of many IIS adopters are to increase sales and replace paper documents and verbal-based communication methods, thus reducing order cost and cycle time. These aims cannot be achieved without a substantial shift in the use of the system. IIS adopters can obtain only transitional benefits after migrating only a limited portion of their total transactions to the system. Thus the following hypotheses can be suggested:

H1: The IIS volume is positively associated with the extent of IIS performance (cost savings, financial performance, service improvement).

H1a: The IIS volume positively affects the extent of cost savings.

H1b: The IIS volume positively affects the extent of financial performance.

H1c: The IIS volume positively affects the extent of service improvement.

H2: The IIS sophistication is positively associated with the extent of IIS performance (cost savings, financial performance, service improvement).

H2a: The IIS sophistication positively affects the extent of cost savings.

H2b: The IIS sophistication positively affects the extent of financial performance.

H2c: The IIS sophistication positively affects the extent of service improvement.

H3: The information contents provided in the IIS are positively associated with the extent of IIS performance (cost savings, financial performance, service improvement).

H3a: The information contents provided in the IIS positively affect the extent of cost savings.

H3b: The information contents provided in the IIS positively affect the extent of financial performance.

H3c: The information contents provided in the IIS positively affect the extent of service improvement.

## Research method

Data for this research were collected by interviewing practitioners who manage IIS. A series of questions was prepared and each respondent was personally interviewed using a structured interview format. Each interview lasted approximately an hour.

A structured interview with a questionnaire was the primary data collection method. Target respondents were selected as follows. First, industries that had used IIS extensively were identified. From publicly available company databases (through the Chollian network service), companies in those industries that were most likely to have comprehensively implemented IIS were contacted to assess their level of IIS implementation. Respondents selected were assumed to possess a sufficient level of knowledge about IIS implementation to answer the questionnaire.

Nine companies refused to participate in the interview; some feared exposing the vulnerabilities of their systems. The overall response rate was 93%; this response rate was high because participation in the survey was solicited through direct calls to IIS managers, and the objectives of the study were clearly explained.

One or two IIS managers in each company simultaneously participated in an interview conducted by one of the authors. Great care was taken to ascertain that respondents had sufficient knowledge about organizational circumstances including IIS implementation. When the participants lacked the knowledge to answer some questions, they were permitted to consult colleagues in user or IS departments who had adequate knowledge to respond. Using the structured questionnaire, a total of 120 interviews were conducted. The unit of analysis in this study was an individual organization that had adopted IIS.

The questionnaire was tested for validity and reliability. Content validity was established through a literature review on the Internet and IS implementation. The items were pre-tested with both practitioners and experts to enhance the content validity of the instrument. Several minor modifications were made based on their feedback regarding the presentation of the survey and the clarity of several items. The survey was then deemed ready for actual respondents since no major comments were made.

Reliability and construct validity of the measures for IIS implementation and performance were evaluated using Cronbach alpha and factor analyses. The three commonly employed decision rules for identification of factors – a minimum eigenvalue of 1, simplicity of structure, a minimum factor loading of 0.5 – were followed (Hair et al., 1979). Seven items related to IIS implementation and two items related to IIS performance were dropped because they failed the decision rules in the factor analysis.

The final principal component factor solution with varimax rotation produced a five-factor solution that explained 70% of the systematic covariance among the 37 scale items for IIS implementation and performance. All five empirically derived factors had eigenvalues greater than one. Eight and three items were excluded from the original scale items for IIS implementation and performance items, respectively, because they failed the decision rules in the factor analysis. Table 1 shows the results of the final factor analysis: the internal reliability coefficient (Cronbach alpha), the items associated with each of the five factors, each item’s factor loading, each factor’s eigenvalue, and variance explained in each factor. Factor analysis of IIS implementation items produced two factors: sophistication and information contents. In a similar vein, IIS performance items were grouped into three factors: cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement.

The concepts of IIS volume and sophistication are based on the facets of EDI initiatives by Massetti & Zmud (1996). IIS Volume indicates the extent (%) to which an organization’s transactions with consumers are handled through the IIS. Business research studies indicate that other means of communication (e.g., phone, fax) are not entirely eliminated by communicating over the Internet (Poon & Swatman, 1999a); Internet commerce is likely to be used simultaneously with these means. Thus, IIS Volume represents the proportion of a firm’s information exchange and processing that is handled through an IIS or the extent to which the firm needs a parallel system to perform these roles.

IIS sophistication represents the level of sophistication in the use of different types of business functions that an organization handles through the IIS. This is measured using seven-point Likert-type scales. IS sophistication has been generally used as an important determinant to IS success (Lehman, 1985–86; McGowan and Madey, 1998; Raymond, 1990). The sophistication of IIS function in this study is related to the number of customer services that are important for on-line stores (Sandberg, 1996) and indicates how widespread the usage of IIS is in an organization, since each function set demands a separate system component. Communication of business documents from diverse functions of user departments can be enhanced through distinct business functions supported by the IIS such as payment, delivery, product search, provision of related information (through links to other related sites), etc. An IIS is accepted for diverse consumer and application functions when it provides diverse business transactions. Newer applications such as intranets that use TCP/IP and HTTP protocols and Java to support client/server systems within firms will provide the technological capacity to share information across organizational boundaries more conveniently and more flexibly, while considerably lowering transmission costs (Kalakota & Whinston, 1997).

IIS sophistication also includes the extent to which an organization’s business processes, that is, electronic payment system, customer database system, accounting system, inventory and logistics system, are integrated with the IIS. This is measured using seven-point Likerttype scales. The extent of integration between the Internet and internal applications is generally low, especially in small companies due to low volume of structured messages, few affordable turn-key solutions, and low external drive to encourage integration (Poon & Swatman, 1999b). The true potential of an IIS, however, is realized only when the IIS information is fully integrated with the other internal information systems. Integration is defined by the extent to which IIS data can be directly processed within applications, without human intervention.

IIS information contents indicate the extent to which various information related to products and services are provided effectively to customers. This focuses on how the organization’s electronic entry improved the presentation of information. This includes the extent to which search and browse functions are implemented or information of products and services are provided, which enable faster and easier retrieval or delivery of information or reports and a greater volume of information output.

The possible benefits of an IIS include improved market reach, communication and purchasing cost reduction, creation of new market opportunities, better customer relations, and shortened transaction and production cycle (Burstein & Kline, 1995; Hamill, 1997; Spar & Bussgang, 1996). The items of IIS performance can be adapted from literature providing the full range of benefits or business objectives of EC, such as work by Lederer et al. (1997), Poon & Swatman (1999a, b), and Tan & Teo (1998). The most important effects of the Internet are in information gathering and time savings. Much of the research on the competitive benefits of IT uses Porter’s formulation of competitive strategies (Porter, 1980). Organizations implement EC applications that are likely to reduce costs (i.e., cost leadership strategies), or applications that focus on particular customers (i.e., focus strategies) and enable a differentiation of productions or services in relation to their competitors (i.e., differentiation strategies) (Lederer et al., 1997). This study suggests three broad categories of IIS performance: cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement. The first category is related to cost leadership strategies, while the second and third pertain to focus and differentiation strategies. These have been included as the most important anticipated benefit factors in EC (Lederer et al., 1997; Poon & Swatman, 1999a).

The factor analysis and reliabilities suggest that the factor structure of IIS implementation and performance is stable and provide evidence for the construct validity of the measure. All the measures used in this study are reported in the Appendix.

## Results

Table 2 summarizes the profiles of the sample organizations. As Table 2 shows, the companies selected are predominantly retailers, financial institutions (banks, stock investment companies), and other service (e.g., entertainment, travel) providers. The sample was chosen from across business sectors so that the existence of sector-independent issues could be investigated. The typology using IIS is very diverse. The most prolific industry sector using IIS is the retail industry. The second is financial institutions. It is not surprising that over a half of all selected companies fall into these two categories, given the fact that the Internet is inherently used to sell products and services or to serve as an intermediary between buyers and sellers (Brandtweiner & Scharl, 1999). The companies in these industries deal with a wide variety of products or services including books, food, clothes, computer-related equipment, musical records (CDs), and goods that are not specialized or complex where buyer and seller transactions are frequent. The goods that many of them sell are easily demonstrated or displayed through the Internet and then easily transported to the customer through arrangements made at the time of the transaction. Companies dealing with travel such as hotel chains and travel agencies are also major adopters of IIS. Entertainment-related business activities and services (e.g., travel/tourism/hotels, electronic games) also feature extensively among companies that have adopted IIS. These companies are using their websites to advertise their business as well as to sell products or services. Other sites are related to printing/ publishing, auctions, education, communication services, media, and manufacturing, etc. When companies perform ‘pure’ electronic business with no off-line business activities, they pertain to the ‘electronic business’ category (18.3%). These companies are entirely dedicated to on-line business using the Internet and in most cases have started their business within the previous three years. The companies in this industry use IIS in their core business activities.

The information in Table 3 indicates that the responding firms spanned a fairly wide range of sizes in terms of the number of personnel. Small firms having less than 50 employees comprised the major group of participants (46.6%). This result is in line with that of Poon & Swatman (1999b) wherein innovative small firms are more likely to gain comparative advantage from the Internet’s global connectivity, improved market reach, low communication and purchasing costs, and creation of new market opportunities.

IIS can be categorized according to whether they exist to enable consumers to shop for and buy products and services (class 1), or to aid intermediate traders in conducting business (class 2). ## IIS implementation

On-line purchasing activities are still relatively new to Internet users (Teo et al., 1995; Cockburn & Wilson, 1996). The extent to which firms use IIS in Korea varied significantly. Almost all firms, however, were very interested in the Internet as a vehicle for EC. IIS implementation indicates the extent that the responding firm has implemented their IIS.

Tables 1 and 4 indicate the implementation states of IIS. Mean volume of IIS was 51%, indicating that many companies use conventional methods of ordering and payment as much as on-line methods. The sample companies were examined in terms of sophistication in the use of various facilities provided in IIS. Authentication services, which are very important in EC, were not sufficiently provided in the sample companies (mean score is 4.19). Although the respondents felt that security is an issue, they have not yet implemented security techniques such as encryption and firewalls to overcome any vulnerabilities.

One of the major impediments to the implementation of controls for sophisticated IS is the lack of funds available for the adoption of controls. A substantial system enhancement is required to install an automated transaction log and authorization system into an existing system. The expertise required to install such controls is very high. Another impediment to the development of controls arises from cultural factors. Management is accustomed to traditional controls such as the paper audit trail, segregation of duties, and hand-written signatures, rather than the kinds of technical controls that are often embedded in the IIS. The change to automated controls may require a new regulatory system to be enforced. Unless the controls are validated, management will be reluctant to install the new controls. Some may also think that the controls slow system response time and utilization. Similarly, they may feel that the costs of the new controls outweigh the benefits to the system integrity.

The ability to develop pages containing graphics, photographs, sound, and video is one of the main attractions of the World Wide Web for business. Many companies are concerned about the potentially low speed of communication after incorporating multi-media into their web pages. Text, graphics, and photographs are used in web pages by just over half the companies surveyed (50.8%). A total of 31.7% of the companies surveyed are using sound or video in their documents. The number of companies using multi-media in their web pages, however, will increase, as they put more time and effort into developing more attractive web pages by incorporating sound and video. Furthermore, as technology progresses, users will be able to access documents with multi-media contents more quickly.

The average integration of IIS is different across applications: integration with internal customer database systems is 4.61, while integration with inventory and logistics system is 3.44. Besides the companies that participate in retailing, integration with internal applications, especially accounting and inventory and logistics systems, is not considered important. Banks and stock trading companies do not even need to have inventory and logistics systems.

Browse or search functions to find products (mean ratings are 5.38 and 5.11) were more highly developed than other functions by the sample companies. Shopping malls appear to have adopted a browse function or search function for comfortable on-line purchasing more quickly than other functions. This function encourages the potential customer to spend money by including price information alongside product or service details.

Among the sample group, IIS had been used for an average of 2.45 years, indicating that the majority of businesses have utilized the Internet for somewhere between two and three years. The WWW is a relatively recent development, and commercial Internet use has grown in the past few years. The functions or facilities intended to draw users back for regular visits include a list of links to general Internet sites of high interests, free downloads of programs, and appetizers such as hot product of the day, magazines, and sale items. The mean ratings for the usage level of these functions (4.46, 4.03, and 3.69) were relatively low. Thus, many companies are not paying much attention to these marketing efforts in order to attract more potential customers. Free downloads of products or programs and the provision of appetizers received low ratings (4.03 and 3.69). Many companies do not provide free samples such as services, software, and non-product-related appetizers such as magazines and travel information, to attract more customers to their websites. Leveraging the IIS to present non-product-related appetizers might initially incur high set-up costs; however, such costs may be recovered through facilitating access to web sites by existing and new customers, local or worldwide, and dissemination of information pertaining to new products and services.

## IIS performance

Our findings suggested that Korean companies are not reaping significant cost savings from Internet commerce (range of mean rating: 3.07–4.03). When participants were asked if they had experienced direct cost savings in terms of reduced communication costs, hard and software maintenance costs, and administration costs (see Table 1), many answered ‘insignificant’. As most companies are investing a great deal of resources to set up hardware and software after they begin Internet business, the amount of costs incurred is greater than the amount of savings from efficient and simplified business processes.

The perceived benefits in terms of financial performance and service improvement, however, are identified to be greater. An IIS helps expand into new markets by virtue of its global reach. Unexpected business opportunities are captured by companies while publicizing their products and services at minimal cost and from ongoing business transformation and partnership. The sampled companies choose to differentially exercise the set of business options even within one system. The key point is that an IIS has the potential to significantly influence the market structure by changing the basis of competition. The competition may be built on differentiation of products and services rather than on price leadership facilitated by lower costs. This is indicated also by the marginal increase in profitability and sales (mean ratings are 4.28 and 4.58). The increase in implementation cost and investment lowers the profitability of Internet commerce. Many customers still feel uncomfortable about buying goods through the Internet due to security issues in payment, low connection speeds, and privacy issues. Thus, gaining short-term benefits in terms of an increase in direct sales is difficult. The sampled companies, however, enhanced competitiveness and information quality (e.g., accuracy, timeliness, user satisfaction) by providing diverse functionalities and prompt and accurate ordering processes to their customers, and by building closer relationships with their customers.

## Business objectives of IIS

The companies were asked about the purpose of their IIS. The classes shown in Table 5 were presented in the questionnaire and respondents were allowed to select more than one category. On-line selling of products and services is the most common use of IIS (85.8%). This is in contrast to the former survey results that indicated that more than 70% of websites are still used for general publicity or marketing and advertising (Cockburn & Wilson, 1996).

In all, 35% of the companies who replied to the questionnaire stated that their websites were used for customer support. As a form of support for their customers, companies provided electronic mail forms addressed to service departments or included extra information and related links. IIS are also used to provide information on specific purposes such as stock information and electronic banks (35%) or support the market exchange (rather than direct selling) of goods and services such as stock trading, on-line banking, and online auctions. Electronic markets are created when IIS serve as intermediaries between buyers and sellers (38.3%). An electronic intermediary helps in the selection and evaluation of offers that are of interest. This has the major advantages of reduced transaction costs and ubiquity.

The companies making up the sample were further classified according to the uses of their website, using the following scheme adapted from Cockburn & Wilson (1996). In this case, respondents were allowed to select only one category.

(1) IIS that provides company information including some information about products or services (2.5%).

(2) IIS that provides company information including some information (e.g., price details) about products or services. However, only conventional purchasing is possible (0.8%).

(3) IIS that provides company information including some information (e.g., price details) about products or services and on-line purchasing facilities. However, billing occurs conventionally (14.2%).

(4) IIS that provides company information including some information (e.g., price details) about products or services and on-line purchasing and billing facilities (45.0%).

(5) IIS that provides information on specific business purposes (5.8%).

## Impediments facing IIS

A number of hurdles must be overcome before Internetbased EC becomes a useful means of business. The companies were asked to suggest the importance of various impediments that have been identified by researchers. Table 6 shows various impediments, which are mostly social issues, and their importance. The importance (or seriousness) is measured using sevenpoint Likert-type scales (1 ¼ least importance, 7 ¼ most important). The most important problems facing the future of IIS are slow connection times (mean rating is 6.64) and unprepared laws concerning privacy and consumer rights (mean rating is 6.23). The on-line purchasing of products in some sites is frustrating to customers due to long connection times. The connection times will be shortened as the national information infrastructure is more developed to accommodate a wide range of connectivity for users and to provide an industry-wide high-capacity, high-speed network that delivers a potentially unlimited range of interactive applications and services. Internet access service providers (IASPs) should increase the number of proxy servers and the cache capacity of proxy servers along with the available bandwidth of server equipment to handle market demand of website connections and information transmission. The problem of slow access time can also be overcome by designing more concise web pages, which reduces scrolling and downloading times. Another method may be reducing the usage of graphic images, animation, and sound effects in web page design.

## Future of IIS

The companies were asked to state the importance of various enhancements that are needed in their IIS (see Table 7). These enhancements are related to technical aspects of their system rather than social issues of EC. It appears that development of secure sites (mean rating is 6.37) and provision of better service to customers (mean rating is 6.26) are the most important enhancements needed in IIS. Customers will remain uncomfortable about on-line purchasing until it can be conducted safely. The sampled companies are concerned about the possibility that their data, or their customer’s data, could be intercepted as the data traverse Internet channels. Companies can introduce firewalls, encryption, and access control software, but they also realize that no system is completely safe. It is apparent that the issue of security must be addressed before EC becomes a dominant method of business.

The Internet helps companies achieve better customer relationships when they provide suitable service to customers after customers purchase products or services. This service is as important as the use of the Internet as a marketing medium. This lack of faithful service to customers can partially explain the reason why expectations concerning business objectives related to sales and profitability of EC were disappointing to the sampled companies (see the section on IIS performance). The infrastructure to fill orders, market products, and provide for on-line after-sales assistance must be in place.

## Test of research hypotheses

Multiple regression analysis shows the relationship between IIS implementation and performance. The dependent variables were cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement. Table 8 presents the results of the regression including the individual regression coefficients for each of three dependent variables of IIS performance. The results indicate that sophistication significantly affects cost savings, supporting hypothesis H1b. Volume and sophistication were found to influence financial performance, supporting hypotheses H2a and H2b. Volume and information contents were found to be significant predictors of service improvement, supporting hypotheses H3a and H3c. The positive influences of volume on financial performance and service improvement indicate that an organization’s progress in handling its business transactions through the Internet is related to an increase in sales and improvement in customer service. Further, the usage of sophisticated functions and services (e.g., ordering processes, authentication services) and integration with internal applications affect the extent of savings in labor costs and improvement in system profitability. While integration with multiple applications adds to the initial cost, reduced purchase lead-time and inventory, lower error rates, greater accuracy, and quick access/response to information all contribute to reducing the overall system cost, which can fully compensate the initial increase in cost. The extent of providing information contents is not directly related to cost savings and financial performance, while it affects service improvement. Companies provide information contents mainly to satisfy customer need to know products and services, which helps improve customer service. This effect of IIS implementation on an adopter firm’s cost savings is generally weaker than on financial performance and service improvement. This is consistent with the survey results suggested by Lederer et al. (1997), which show that EC is actually helping organizations in terms of the improvement in their products and services rather than cost leadership. Firms who have adopted IIS focus more on organizational profitability and customer service than on savings in internal administration and system costs while expanding and integrating their system.

The results point out that in order to truly exploit the latent large-scale operational and strategic potential benefits of an IIS, the adopter firm will need to systematically plan and pursue more extensive implementation of the IIS via an increase in the volume of transactions processed through it. Expanding sophistication of functions by integrating system services and making systems more user friendly can also contribute to increasing sales through the Internet. Customer service improves by enabling better information availability and quick responses to changing customer needs, by providing information contents more effectively using faster information retrieval and delivery systems, and increasing the volume of information output. ## Conclusions and implications

Using a survey study, issues of EC participation among Korean IIS adopters have been highlighted. The structure of the value chain involved in supplying the products/ services to final consumers is greatly affected by the diffusion of EC in an industry. The applications being developed include applications to enable customers to shop, bank, and perform other transactions related to entertainment, education services, and commerce from their homes.

Electronic commerce in Korea is still in an embryonic stage. The vast majority of commercial websites have been around for just the past few years. Many Korean companies consider the Internet’s World Wide Web as a strategic information technology that alters the ground rules by which businesses interact with their consumers. Not enough has been understood about the real impact of this rapidly evolving phenomenon, particularly in terms of differentiating between that impact and hype concerning the Internet. The results of this study improve our understanding of the current state of EC in Korea.

There is a wide variety of business types on the Web. The implementation and performance, as well as purpose of the IIS, differ among the surveyed companies. A majority of companies use their websites as a medium for on-line selling and to attract more customers. IIS volume, however, is about 50% and the average level of system sophistication is generally low. This shows that many firms still significantly use other means of communication such as fax and phone, while their information exchange and processing are handled through the IIS, and that they do not have integrated internal applications. This is interesting in the light of past predictions that more exchanges between firms and customers would be made possible through electronic communication technologies, due to easier communication networks. Many problems are involved in EC and these include slow connection times, unprepared laws concerning privacy and consumer rights, and high taxes and card usage fees. Security is more important because different transactions through the Internet increase the likelihood that internal information can be disclosed to external parties, which introduces new vulnerabilities to the customer–supplier relationship. Higher managerial understanding of various issues in implementing an IIS, such as problems, performance, business objectives, and future enhancements, increases the likelihood of more successful system implementation and right allocation of the managerial, financial, and technological resources necessary to implement the IIS.

The success of EC in creating competitive advantage (Lederer et al., 1997) prompted this examination of the impact of IIS implementation on IIS performance. All of the sites surveyed were of a commercial nature and the IIS volume, sophistication, and information contents provided in the IIS were partially related to cost savings, financial performance, and service improvement. Present and future IIS developers can understand potential design alternatives and functional requirements in terms of IIS volume, sophistication, and information contents from the results of the study; they have to focus on the three facets of IIS implementation when they choose the design alternatives.

Some of the unexplored areas of past research relate to the relationship between IIS implementation and performance, and this study provides some support for the positive influence of the use of an IIS on organizational outcome. Increasing IIS volume demands the replacement of traditional means of communication and the usage of various transaction services through the Internet. IIS adopters must expand their connections with customers through the Internet in order to increase system profitability and improve customer service. Sophistication in system functions, including authentication of services and integration with internal applications, is related to the benefits of reduced system operation and maintenance costs and increased sales
