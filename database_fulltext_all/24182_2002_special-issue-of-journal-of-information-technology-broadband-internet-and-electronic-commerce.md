# Special Issue of Journal of Information Technology

# Broadband Internet and Electronic Commerce EDITED BY HEEJIN LEE, STEVE SAWYER AND JYOTI CHOUDRIE

CALL FOR PAPERS

Currently, governments in many countries are promoting broadband Internet connectivity because they see broadband as a means to increase the international competitiveness of their country and their positioning on the international Internet/EC markets. Building broadband connections requires not only computing technology and network infrastructure, but also a complex set of economic, social and policy considerations. Therefore, the deployment and use of broadband capabilities is high on the current political agenda in many developed and developing countries.

The potential importance of broadband is also increasingly recognized in academia. As broadband technologies provide residential users, enterprises of all sizes, and a range of public governance units with highspeed access and ‘always-on’ connections, they substantially change patterns of Internet use, social and work interactions and a range of consumer behaviours. Information systems that take advantage of broadband capacity have a potential to make the Internet a stable, embedded part of our everyday lives and to do so on a broader scale, and in more places, than current systems allow.

For this call, papers that address questions about the diffusion of broadband Internet and its impacts on various sectors of the economy, including electronic commerce, organizations of all kinds, application developers, public administration, domestic households, content providers etc., are sought. The papers can address topics such as the following, but are not limited to:

What factors (e.g. network infrastructure, competition, demand for services, etc.) in uence the diffusion of broadband Internet and the development of electronic commerce?

What are the strategies implemented by broadband access providers and network carriers in different countries?

What business models are available for companies in various industries to take advantage of broadband (e.g. ASP for SMES)?

What are the likely ‘killer’ applications for the broadband penetration?

What technologies and innovations for broadband are available and dominant in different countries?

How do government policies affect the diffusion of broadband Internet?

What are the social and economic impacts of broadband Internet diffusion?

What changes are taking place in user (consumer) behaviour in broadband environments?

How are the standards for broadband Internet evolving and what are the issues with this ongoing evolution relative to the various constituents?

What are the policy issues in the emergence of broadband (from international, national, indus trial, and organizational perspectives)?

We seek papers that report experiences, empirical research and conceptual developments. Papers should focus on issues for our collective understanding and theory development. Papers should also aid practi tioners and policy makers from different countries in answering the above and other related questions. Papers may include country case studies, empirical data analyses, comparisons across countries, strategic frameworks and policy issues.
