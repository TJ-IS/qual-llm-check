# Riverbank Financial: changing the role of information technology

M. KATHRYN BROHMAN

Faculty of Business Administration, Richard Ivey School of Business, University of Western Ontario, London, Ontario, Canada NGA 3K7

DUNCAN G. COPELAND

Copeland & Company, 13601 Query Mill Road, North Potomac, Maryland 20878, USA

The information technology (IT) group at a large Canadian ® nancial institution has recently implemented a new operating framework that signi® cantly changed the role of IT within the organization. The traditional, technology-centric, independent entity has been retailored into an integrated, customer-focused business unit. Consistent with their new vision, the IT group has shifted its focus towards IT± business integration, increased IT employee satisfaction and decreased turnaround in system development. Amongst the successes of the implementation to date is one perspective that has attracted the attention of IT executives. Customers who observed the transition of the IT group have raised concern about Riverbank Financial’s ability to maintain leadership in technological expertise. IT executives must now consider the possibility that the new operating framework has taken too much focus away from technology and identify possible changes.

## Introduction

Riverbank Financial’s information technology (IT) division has recently undergone a dramatic transformation. Traditionally, the IT environment had been highly controlled and very technology centric. Now, operations are much more participative and focus on satisfying customers within Riverbank Financial. The new organization is called Riverbank’s Technology Team (RTT) and its main objective is to align technology processes with Riverbank Financial’s business operations.

To date, the implementation of RTT’s new structure and operating framework has increased system development performance and IT employee satisfaction. However, some concern has been raised by the customers of RTT. With IT± business integration at the focus of RTT’s new vision, customers are concerned that Riverbank Financial’s reputation as a technological leader within the ® nancial services industry may be at risk. Aware of customer concern, RTT executives have scheduled a meeting to evaluate the new structure and operations and consider any necessary changes.

## The ® nancial services industry

The ® nancial services industry in Canada is amongst the most technologically sophisticated in the world. There are a total of nine domestic and 43 subsidiaries of foreign institutions within Canada. The industry is dominated by ® ve large institutions that hold 80% of the industry’s assets. Riverbank Financial is one of the leading providers of ® nancial services in Canada.

Over the past decade, signi® cant legislative and regulatory reforms have opened up the industry to greater competition. In the mid-1990s, deregulation allowed foreign institutions and related industries to enter the industry and deliver competitive ® nancial products and services.

Increased competition has forced ® nancial institutions to refocus on the customer and provide products and services that are better suited to customer needs. Both corporate and individual customers were becoming more sophisticated in their needs and awareness. Customers were demanding more information and better customer service. Financial institutions needed to enhance convenience and reliability in order to remain competitive. This new customer focus has stimulated expansion of product and service portfolios and has changed the way ® nancial institutions do business.

One of the most evident changes within the ® nancial services industry has been the increased dependence on technology. Institutions have rapidly adopted the use of technological innovations to offer alternative services to the customer. Today, many institutions are providing services to both individuals and businesses through private computer networks and the Internet.

## Downtime disasters at Riverbank Financial

In 1990, Riverbank Financial suffered a 3 day system downtime disaster that prevented 6 million customers across Canada from accessing account balances. The cause of the problem was an error made during the entry of a new program on to Riverbank Financial’s mainframe. Once the software issue was detected, a system shutdown was necessary in order to ® x the problem. Even after the problem was corrected, the massive backlog of transactions added additional delay to information access.

Two unrelated computer malfunctions occurred in the 2 months following the initial disaster. These malfunctions again prevented customers from accessing account information causing serious problems for Riverbank Financial. The board of directors recognized that external customers and employees within Riverbank Financial were becoming increasingly frustrated. It was clear that changes needed to be made to the IT environment. In January 1991, a new IT executive, John Schaefer, was hired to lead the reconstruction.

## John Schaefer

John Schaefer was appointed as both the chief information of® cer (CIO) of Riverbank Financial as well as the executive vice-president (EVP) of the company’s technology group, known at that time as the Information Technology Group (ITG). As one of the largest IT centres in Canada, ITG was highly respected within the ® nancial services industry and noted in particular for their extraordinary technical competence.

Figure 1 highlights the organizational structure of ITG within Riverbank Financial. John reported directly to the chairman of the bank. He assigned eight vice-presidents to manage speci® c functions within ITG. The majority of these individuals were long-time employees of Riverbank Financial who were highly respected by other executives within the company for their advanced technical expertise. Two symbols distinguished John’s tenure at Riverbank Financial: his management style and independent ITG operations.

## Management style

In order to prevent future technology disasters, John managed ITG by keeping a \`® rm hand on the tiller’. ITG’s management group was responsible for de® ning ITG’ s processes and strategies. As the EVP, John expected to be included in the majority of decisions and approved all major expenditures. ITG’s management style was highly procedural, which often restricted the opportunity for open discussion. In general, ITG managers and employees followed established processes and many did not feel encouraged to provide feedback or incorporate their own ideas.

## Independent ITG operations

ITG operations were independent of other business operations within Riverbank Financial. The business units, who were ITG’s major customers, often felt that they were not suf® ciently involved in technology decisions. Standard ITG operations accepted requests for systems and applications from their customers and independently designed technically appropriate solutions. The impact of independent ITG operations was minimal integration of technology and business.

## Reconstruction of ITG

Under John’s direction, ITG developed a plan to eliminate the factors that had contributed to the downtime disasters in 1990. John received support from the chairman of Riverbank Financial for a three-part implementation strategy.

## Part 1: stabilizing the operating environment

ITG struck up a task force that identi® ed concerns and requirements related to both systems and change management. Appropriate processes and disciplines were then put into place. Checks and balances were also incorporated to minimize the risk of experiencing another meltdown. This project was budgeted at \$70 million for analysis and change.

## Part 2: upgrading operations to a more modern environment

Major computer systems were moved to a new, modern facility. The company’s network infrastructure was improved and a centralized network management system was put in place. The local area networks in the company branches were replaced with a common wiring standard. Branches received new servers and workstations to replace their old IBM PS/1 and PS/2 systems. A common keyboard was adopted and a new graphical user interface (GUI) was implemented.

## Part 3: rearchitecting all old application software

The third initiative was to rearchitect outdated application software. The systems development strategy was to develop most applications from scratch in order to ® x, once and for all, the many systems that had been simply patched together since the 1960s. Developing proprietary solutions, using leading edge design methodologies (i.e. object-oriented design), was seen as a signi® cant competitive advantage.

## New chairman induces change in ITG

By early 1994, John Schaefer had successfully implemented the ® rst two parts of his implementation strategy. The number of system operating errors had decreased signi® cantly and downtime risk was lowered to fractions of a minute. During the implementation of the third initiative, the board of directors hired a new chairman of the bank. The new chairman, Doug Harris, rede® ned Riverbank Financial’s corporate mission in response to changes that had taken place in the ® nancial services industry. These changes were namely globalization, integration of technology and changing customer needs.

In response to the new corporate mission, Mr Harris requested an evaluation of ITG to identify solutions that would integrate ITG with the company. A technology consulting ® rm was hired to complete a formal assessment of ITG operations. The results of the assessment identi® ed two broad recommendations related to the structure and operations of ITG.

(1) Incorporating a formal second opinion for technology decisions by dividing John Schaefer’s current responsibilities into two separate positions, CIO of Riverbank Financial and EVP of ITG.

(2) Increasing business and relationship management competence within ITG in order to enhance the alignment of IT with business operations.

In response to the assessment, John Schaefer’s responsibilities were divided into two distinct positions: CIO of Riverbank Financial and EVP of ITG. The main responsibility of the CIO was to focus on Riverbank Financial’s enterprise-wide IT strategy, while the EVP of ITG was responsible for systems development and supporting business operations. Structurally, the CIO continued to report directly to the chairman of the company; however, the EVP of ITG now reported directly to the president of one of Riverbank Financial’s two strategic businesses.

## ITG transitions to a new leader

In 1994, shortly after the board’s decision to rede® ne the executive role, John Schaefer resigned from Riverbank Financial. In response, the board of directors immediately hired Steven Currie, an IT consultant, to manage ITG while they searched for appropriate candidates to ® ll the positions of EVP and CIO. Steven had 22 years’ experience with the IT group at one of Riverbank Financial’s main competitors. He was well respected in the ® nancial community for his skills in people and relationship management.

Steven’s responsibility was to implement changes to respond to issues identi® ed by the IT assessment. As the new leader of ITG, Steven Currie used a previously scheduled quarterly meeting to introduce himself formally to the managers and employees. His initial message to ITG employees focused on the need to work together to develop a mandate to enhance the performance of ITG. He suggested that employees begin by evaluating the business in which ITG competes and identify exactly what it was they do well. Steven encouraged employee participation in the meeting to stress the importance of working together as a key to achieving excellence in the future.

Steven offered some direction at the meeting by identifying delivery times as the major problem within ITG. He set speci® c goals for employees to refocus project times from 1 to 3 year deliverables to 30, 60 and 90 day deliverables. He also relaxed the existing approval process in order to save time. Lastly, Steven encouraged people to order equipment and take training courses they felt they needed in order to contribute to ITG’ s success.

At the end of the meeting, Steven gave all ITG employees his direct telephone number and encouraged them to use it. Following the meeting, employees were hesitant to accept this new form of leadership, but were interested in observing how things would progress.

## The transformation era

A four-step improvement process was initiated to transform ITG into a new organization.

## Step 1: understanding the issues

ITG executives collected data from assessment reports, managers and employees within ITG and individuals from business units in order to diagnose the issues within ITG. The data suggested that productivity was the overarching concern related to ITG operations. Four speci® c productivity issues, as well as inhibiting factors, were identi® ed. These issues and factors are summarized in Figure 2. Executives communicated the productivity issues and inhibiting factors to all ITG employees at a quarterly meeting.

## Step 2: developing principles and concepts

By February 1995, ITG executives had de® ned new operating principles in response to productivity issues and inhibiting factors. Figure 3 outlines the new operating principles, which consisted of three sets of complementary guidelines from the vantage points of organizational structure, technology stewardship and productivity principles.

The new operating principles were communicated to ITG employees through the distribution of the following memo written by Steven Currie, who by this time had been formally hired by Riverbank Financial to take on the role of executive vice-president RTT.

## Dear Colleague

ITG has not been as effective as we would have liked. In examining why, the ITG management committee has studied the reports of an external consultant and internal task teams. We have also talked with our clients and partners in the business and elsewhere in Riverbank Financial. We have paid much attention to input from focus groups, the employee survey and the Survival Program teams.

As a result of this review, we have evolved a new Operating Framework for ITG. This framework consists of organizational guidelines, technology stewardship guidelines and productivity guidelines that will establish clear boundaries between the different kinds of work done by ITG and clear accountabilities for each of the different organizational units.

Based on this framework, we have developed a new organizational structure and a new name for ITG.

Effective today, the Information Technology Group (ITG) will be known as Riverbank Technology Team (RTT).

## Riverbank Financial

Structurally, RTT will be divided into two major types of units ± project units and process units. This will better align RTT to meet the business needs of our clients and partners in the business.

As well as focusing on our clients, it will provide the basis for improving the internal ef® ciency and effectiveness of our project and process execution.

The project area will consist of six positions: four VP projects, who will manage a core team of project directors and managers; a VP of resource management who will be responsible for managing a resource center which will bring together about 1000 technical, professional and specialist staff now working on projects throughout ITG and a director of architecture services who will be responsible for working with his or her project colleagues to articulate and document architectural standards.

An SVP position has been created to provide executive direction to the above team. While the position may be ® lled in the future, for now, all of the above positions will report to me.

I am con® dent that RTT’s new Operating Framework, together with the above structural changes, will move us forward. In simple terms, these changes represent a commitment to organize ourselves to much more easily do things.

There is, however, a lot of work left to do. I am committed to making the process one that will encourage involvement from the greatest possible number of people. As planning continues and the new organization takes shape, we will continue to get your input and keep you informed.

Please feel free to call me at 1± 800-S CURRIE or via e-mail with your input.

Sincerely,

Steven Currie, EVP RTT

## Step 3: analysis and planning

Building from the operating principles, employees and customers of ITG participated in a series of three workshops to analyse and plan the implementation of the new organization. The theme of these workshops were (1) identi® cation of required processes and functions in the new organization, (2) design of the new organizational structure and (3) evaluation of the new organization structure and processes for both improved customer service and increased business value.

## Step 4: implementation of RTT’s new organizational structure

As stated in the memo above, the new organization was renamed RTT. The structure of the new organization was divided into three major functions: staff functions, process execution and project execution.

Staff functions included ® nance and human resources. Process execution managed operations across Canada, including areas such as items processing and remittance, currency management and records. Project execution functions included system and application development. Project managers were aligned with speci® c technologies and others were aligned with speci® c customers and partners in order to be consistent with Mr Harris’ initiative to reposition ITG as a business partner within Riverbank Financial. RTT executives also felt it broadened the accountability for end-to-end solutions.

## Introduction of the resource centre

One of the most signi® cant changes in organizational structure was the implementation of a new internal project execution division called the resource centre. The resource centre was an information systems (IS) competency centre. The concept of an IS competency centre had been implemented by several hightechnology companies (e.g. Hewlett-Packard and Northern Telecom), but Riverbank Financial was one of the pioneers in utilizing a competency centre approach for systems development in the ® nancial services industry. It was predicted that, by the year 2000, more than 70% of medium to large IS organizations would use competency centres as the primary vehicle for supplying project development teams with technical talent.

There were two unique qualities that de® ned the operations of the competency centre. First, the competency centre concentrated on developing skills that were demanded by the customer. At Riverbank Financial, the vice-president of the resource centre, Mary Timmons, was responsible for ensuring skills required by the project directors were available within the resource centre.

Secondly, the competency centre separated skills and people from projects. Each employee of the competency centre concentrated on the development of a speci® c skill and would only be assigned to projects that required that skill. Therefore, each individual worked on many different projects with many different teams. Employees of the resource centre at Riverbank Financial had the opportunity to work for many business units and project directors over time.

The resource centre at Riverbank Financial employed 1100 individuals who focused on project development. Each project director was aligned with a resource manager from the resource centre. The project director was responsible for managing the project and requested competencies from the resource manager when required. The resource centre employed 20 resource managers, who were further aligned with speci® c business units. The main responsibilities of the resource managers were to manage relationships within the project team and deliver the human resources required by project directors.

## Staf® ng the new organization

By spring 1995, all RTT executive positions had been rede® ned to align with the new operating principles. Existing executives were asked to review their positions and invited to submit their resum‚ s for placement in positions of their choice.

During transition to the new structure, four ITG senior vice-presidents (SVPs) resigned from Riverbank Financial. All vacant executive positions were ® lled through internal promotions and lateral moves, with the exception of SVP projects which was ® lled by an external hire, Mr Anthony Grey. Mr Grey had spent his early years in the technology industry and came to Riverbank Financial following a long tenure with one of its main competitors.

## Tooling up the partnership

By summer 1995, all management positions within RTT were rede® ned in terms of the new operating framework. A hiring committee, which consisted of human resource recruiters, line managers and customers, recruited all senior management positions within process execution, project execution and the resource centre. By the end of the summer, all managerial positions were in place and employees were realigned to the new structure.

With the new RTT structure implemented and managers and employees in place, executives hired a consultant from Harvard University to design a programme that would teach relationship management skills to all senior level managers. The programme was named \`Tooling up the Partnership’ and consisted of a 3-day relationship management course delivered by the Harvard Negotiating Skills Program. The programme was received very positively within RTT and is currently being delivered to other business units within Riverbank Financial.

## RTT today

Since the implementation of the new organizational structure in spring 1995, three major changes have occurred. Figure 4 highlights RTT’s current organizational structure within Riverbank Financial.

First, Charles Koehler was hired to ® ll the position of CIO. Charles came to Riverbank Financial from a leading North American IT consulting ® rm. After a 1 year tenure, Charles resigned as CIO and, to date, Riverbank Financial is in the process of recruiting to ® ll the vacant position.

The second major change was in process execution. All processing operations, including cheque, remittance, currency, computer services, networks and desktop management functions, were spun-off to create a joint venture company called Rivermarx Inc. Rivermarx Inc. provided end-to-end ® nancial paper and electronic transaction processing to Riverbank Financial and other institutions. The ® nal major change was in project execution. The next evolutionary step was taken in an attempt to deploy the most effective and ef® cient solutions for the organization. Anthony Grey was promoted to SVP project and resource management. Mary Timmons, who used to report directly to Steven Currie, now reported to Anthony Grey.

## RTT’S success

Feedback from RTT employees and customers has identi® ed the status of RTT implementation to date. In systems development, employees reported greater satisfaction as they expended more effort utilizing their visionary and implementation skills. Dramatic decreases were made on turnaround time and there was increased rigour on project management. According to one source, a speci® c project originally estimated to cost \$25 million and budgeted to take 2 years, was completed in 5 months at a cost of \$9.5 million. The original project plan estimated only 5% purchased software, therefore requiring 95% to be custom developed. The ® nal project was made up of 75% purchased software and integrated three existing software packages. The success of this project was attributed to the new operating framework.

Feedback also identi® ed the increased focus on IT± business integration. One plan was to move RTT managers laterally to management positions in other business units. To date, one individual has been promoted from project director within the project execution division of RTT to VP of a strategic business unit. His responsibilities in this new role were to develop a market for a new business application and to manage the growth and development of the business unit. RTT has planted seeds in other business units to create similar integration opportunities with Riverbank Financial’s business operations.

The feedback from customers who observed the transition from ITG to RTT introduced an interesting perspective. They appreciated the successes in systems development and IT± business integration; however, some raised concern about RTT’s role in maintaining Riverbank Financial’s reputation of technological excellence in the ® nancial industry. The EVP of virtual business was quoted as saying \`I see the advantages of RTT to my business unit, but I cannot help feeling that performance would increase even more if we could combine the relationship and business competence of RTT with the outstanding technical expertise we had in ITG.’

## The future of RTT

To date, the implementation of RTT’s new operating framework complements the vision of RTT executives to \`Be a leader in the dynamic ® nancial services marketplace, partnering with customers to deliver cost competitive and innovative business solutions, enabled by technology.’ Looking into the future and responding to customer feedback, Steven Currie cannot help but wonder whether RTT’s new operating framework has taken too much focus away from technology. To prepare for an upcoming executive meeting, Steven needs to identify necessary modi® cations for RTT’s operating framework in order to ensure Riverbank Financial’s technical expertise is maintained.
