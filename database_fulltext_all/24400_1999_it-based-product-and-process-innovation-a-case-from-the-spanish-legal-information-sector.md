# IT-based product and process innovation: a case from the Spanish legal information sector

KATRIN SIMON and PEDRO J. SANCHEZ

Universidad Publica de Navarra, 31006 Pamplona, Spain

MIKEL OLAZARAN

Universidad Pais-Vasco, 48080 Bilbao, Spain

In this paper we analyse the process of technical and organizational innovation through which Editorial Aranzadi (the leading ® rm in the Spanish legal information sector) adapted to the changes in its environment in the late 1980s and early 1990s. After a theoretical introduction, we introduce the ® rm (Editorial Aranzadi) and study some of the factors that provoked the beginning of the innovation process in the late 1980s. Then we analyse the main phases of the process of introduction of IT and organizational change in Editorial Aranzadi. Attention is then focused on product and process innovation. Finally, we draw the main conclusions from this case study.

## Introduction

In the current, changing context of accelerating globalization and technical change process, product and organizational innovation are very much related to the introduction of information technology (IT). Both the structure of organizations and the relationships between organizations are evolving rapidly in this sociotechnical process of change. The paradigm of individual productivity, based on the theory of postindustrial organization, does not offer a satisfactory framework for interpreting these processes. Attention is increasingly being devoted to the global optimization of the organization throughout IT-based coordination channels.

In this paper, we analyse the effects of the introduction of IT into the main ® rm of the legal information sector in Spain. We have studied two types of issues. First of all, we have considered the effects of the introduction of IT upon both product and process innovation. Attention has been devoted to technical, organizational and strategic aspects of this process of change. As Rockart and Scott Morton (1984) pointed out, the impact of IT is not only conditioned by the technical and socioeconomic external environment, but also by the personality of the individual company and its own resources. This personality is de® ned by several characteristics, such as the structure and organizational culture, the chosen strategy, the processes and methods of transaction and the human potential and the roles that they perform, as well as the actual possession of the technology (Straub and Wetherbe, 1989; Kar Yan Tam, 1992).

The concept of the level of IT introduction (or maturity) has been developed in a number of contributions which are based on the premise that the process of introduction, adaptation and use of IT in organizations is an evolutionary one involving a good deal of organizational learning. Nolan (1979; Nolan and Gibson, 1980) looked at three key aspects for understanding and appraising the introduction and development of IT in organizations: the growth of applications, the increase in specialized personnel and the management skills employed in each of the stages. Zuboff (1988) made an interesting contribution and concluded that there are three levels of IT introduction: automation (and/or mechanization), \`informatization’ (extending information throughout the company) and organizational transformation. A review of the most relevant models can be found in Singh (1993). Gorry and Scott Morton (1989) focused on the supporting role of IT in decision making at the executive level and classi® ed IT systems and IT introduction levels according to the management tasks which they support: operational control, control of management and strategic planning and the decision-making process. Benjamin and Blunt (1992) divided the types of potential opportunities between those that focus on the competitive marketplace and those that focus on internal operations. ITbased information systems (IS) can be used to improve traditional ways of doing business or to bring about signi® cant structural changes in them.

In this paper, we have adopted Galliers’ (1992, 1993, 1994) model. According to this model, there are three main stages to three levels of incorporation of IT:

systems to automate processes (data processing (DP)), systems to aid decision making management information systems (MIS) and strategic information systems (SIS). The ® rst stage consists of the mechanization data-related tasks which were previously carried out manually. The main objective is to increase ef® ciency by automating transaction processes. Decision support systems seek to meet all the information needs of the decision maker, extending information throughout the organization and achieving greater ef® ciency. Finally, strategic systems are either those which are in line with the general strategy of the company or those whose objectives come to represent the strategic objectives of the organization. Their role is to improve the competitive edge of the company and provide it with speci® c advantages. This model has been adopted by other authors such as Wiseman (1985, 1992) and Ward et al. (1993). In our view, it is useful because of its simplicity and robustness.

In this paper, we also make a second theoretical point, related to the effects of IT upon different types of workers. Particular attention is devoted to the \`intellectual core’ of the ® rm analysed, that is the professionals who carry out the analysis of legal information in Editorial Aranzadi. The effects of IT upon work organization and quali® cations have been a matter of considerable debate. The labour process literature has underlined the uses of new technologies for increasing control upon workers and for dequali® ying them and reducing their autonomy and responsibility at work. However, in our view this emphasis on control is exaggerated and it ignores the complexity of the interactions between the different actors who take part throughout the IT development and implementation process.

Other authors have pointed out that IT can be used for enriching the conceptual and creativity level of work, automating routine manual and non-manual tasks and increasing the number of higher abstraction level tasks (Cash et al., 1990; Emery, 1990; Santodomingo and Carretero, 1996) The astonishing progress of IT in recent years (storage, processing, transmission, and even interpretation of information) means that organizations have an almost unlimited level of information and communications resources at their disposal, so that they can satisfy the communication needs created by the coordination of actions and decisions (Salas, 1987; de Pablo, 1989; Whitten et al., 1996).

The structure of this paper is as follows. First of all we introduce the ® rm analysed (Editorial Aranzadi) and study some of the factors that provoked the beginning of an important innovation process in this ® rm in the late 1980s. Then we analyse the main phases of the process of introduction of IT and organizational change in Editorial Aranzadi. Next attention is focused on product and process innovation. Finally, we draw the main conclusions about this case.

## Research methodology

The aim of this case study was to analyse the effects of the introduction of IT on Editorial Aranzadi’s products and processes. Several issues related to the process of introduction of IT are studied and special attention is devoted to organizational effects and to changes in functions and tasks in the departments involved. The case was selected because of the wide scope for organizational impact of IT in an information-intensive ® rm such as Editorial Aranzadi.

From a methodological point of view we can distinguish three main phases in our research process. First of all we describe the process of introduction of IT and its temporal steps. From this description we draw some conclusions about the importance of IT-based innovation in the legal information sector and about the role of some actors (such as the general manager) in the process of introduction of IT in Editorial Aranzadi. In the second phase attention focuses on the effects of IT upon product innovation (Editorial Aranzadi’s new CD-based products, which give users new possibilities for information searching). In Editorial Aranzadi’s case, product innovation is closely linked with process innovation. In the third phase we study the effects of IT upon both the production process and the information analysis tasks. We analyse the effects of IT upon both routine and intellectual tasks.

Empirical research for this case study was based on semi-structured interviews with the people responsible for IT introduction in the different departments involved. We interviewed both the technical personnel and management staff involved in the different phases of IT introduction in Editorial Aranzadi. Questions about the different steps of the process of introduction of IT were posed to all the interviewees. Each interview then focused on the issues and aspects in which the interviewee was more strongly involved. The duration of the interviews was between 2 and 4 h and they were recorded and transcribed.

The following people were interviewed: the IT manager, the ® nance manager, the head of the jurisprudence analysis team (twice), the production manager and the human resources manager.

## Editorial Aranzadi

Editorial Aranzadi (Elcano, Navarra, Spain) is a small± medium ® rm that belongs to the printing± editing sector. Its main activity is to edit periodically legal information. It also publishes specialized texts about the different realms of law. Editorial Aranzadi processes and analyses Spanish law and jurisprudence information and therefore its products are directed to the Spanish market. Its law and jurisprudence compendiums (volumes) have great prestige and are very well known among Spanish lawyers. These compendiums are the standard legal information and classi® cation tools for the legal profession in Spain.

Editorial Aranzadi is a small± medium-sized ® rm. Its sales in 1996 were 6000 million pesetas (US\$45 million). Editorial Aranzadi has got 248 employees, 100 external collaborators and 100 distribution agents in 12 sales branches. It also has approximately 100 professionals participating in advisory councils. The ® rm is a limited company, but in practice it is owned and controlled by a family group, which directs the policy of the ® rm. Editorial Aranzadi was created in 1929 by Manuel de Aranzadi Irujo, a meticulous lawyer who started to build up a ® le of related cards with information about Spanish law and jurisprudence. These notes soon gained considerable recognition and Manuel de Aranzadi started to edit and publish them. This is how Editorial Aranzadi started a process that has developed with time, in which Editorial Aranzadi has built up the most important stock of legal and jurisprudence information (classi® cations, analyses and cross-references) in Spain.

Although Editorial Aranzadi’s compendiums already had a long tradition, at the end of the 1980s the ® rm underwent a process of innovation and change, which is analysed in this paper. This process was catalysed by three factors. First of all, as a result of the democratization of Spain and the emergence of a new legal framework, in the mid-1980s there was a big increase in the amount of legal information to be processed and analysed by the ® rm. Secondly, at about the same time, new competitors emerged in the legal information sector. Thirdly, there was a change in the management structure of the company. Estanislao de Aranzadi, a young man who combined the traditional \`know how’ of the ® rm with organizational change, became the new director of the ® rm.

For many years Editorial Aranzadi was the only company which did classi® cations and analyses of legal information using a system of references and indexes. This situation changed dramatically in the 1980s with the emergence of new competitors such as Ledico and La Ley-Actualidad.

Ledico came to the market with a product similar to Editorial Aranzadi’s, one covering the whole ® eld of legal and jurisprudence matters in Spain. This company stayed in business for a few years until it was closed because of distribution problems. Ledico had developed a product similar to Editorial Aranzadi’s, but it could not match this ® rm’ s commercial distribution network. This network, which has been built up by Editorial Aranzadi throughout the years, guarantees fast and reliable distribution of Editorial Aranzadi’s products throughout the country. (Editorial Aranzadi’s products are distributed weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly and yearly, in a complex process of updating of information.) Ledico’s distribution problems generated mistrust among its clients, because they could not obtain reliable and fast enough information.

The founders of Ledico started a new ® rm, Europea del Derecho, which offers a different, non-exhaustive product (a fortnightly journal). The main advantage of such a product is price. The objective of this strategy seems to be to gain some market share in order to sell it either to national companies from the sector or to international companies who want to introduce themselves into the Spanish legal information market. In any case, Europea del Derecho has not completely overcome Ledico’s distribution problems. Its distribution chain is much behind Editorial Aranzadi’s one and this creates problems of speed and reliability.

The second competitor to appear was La Ley, a company of Argentinian origin which started to publish lega information in Spain in 1981. La Ley applied its Argentinian model to Spain and generated a product with a new structure. La Ley publishes a daily newsletter with law and jurisprudence information, as well as specialized articles about legal doctrine. The important difference to Editorial Aranzadi is that they do not include the full text of sentences, but an abstract of them. Another important difference is that there is only one compendium, instead of the different, thematic compendiums (volumes) of Editorial Aranzadi. Apart from this, it is important to point out that, in 1987, La Ley, along with Colex Data, were the ® rst companies to introduce a database format for classifying legal information. This product had important advantages with respect to Editorial Aranzadi’s paper-made compendiums and they gained an important market share in the sector.

The main problem with La Ley’s product has been its lack of exhaustiveness. The r‚ sum‚ s of sentences sometimes lack all the tingeing and details that are necessary for lawyers’ work. In fact, from 1997, La Ley started to publish the full texts of sentences in the same way as Editorial Aranzadi does. They also adopted a system of classi® cation of sentences and references. La Ley is Editorial Aranzadi’s strongest competitor, both in market share and in strategy for gaining new clients and guaranteeing their ® delity.

A third competitor was Colex Data, a company which developed an on-line legal information product in the early 1990s. Colex Data had two types of problems with this product. On the one hand, the telecommunications infrastructure (the telephone line) was not good enough at that time. Connections were dif® cult and slow. On the other hand, it was perhaps too early to offer a high-tech product to the law profession. Spanish lawyers’ culture was quite separate from technology and they (and Spanish society in general) were not used to \`pay per use’ culture. Apart from the technical problems just mentioned, the on-line product was too distant from the usual paper-made volumes to which Spanish lawyers were accustomed. Because of these dif® culties, Colex Data turned to the CD format, as did La Ley and Editorial Aranzadi. Nowadays, since the rapid expansion of the Internet from the mid-1990s onwards, the situation is more favourable for on-line systems, but in the early 1990s these systems were abandoned in favour of the CD option.

## Information systems in Editorial Aranzadi

Towards the late 1970s and early 1980s editing sector ® rms started to use IT for text composition and editing. It was \`poor informatics’ (by today’ s standards), basically sophisticated word processing. Legal information processing applications arrived in Spain in the early 1980s, but they were run on big macrocomputers. The systems were very expensive and, therefore, there was no possibility of producing IT-based products for the wider public. Later on, between 1985 and 1987, documentary database software was developed which could be run on smaller, micro- or personal computers. It was at this time, under the circumstances mentioned in the previous section and with the arrival of Estanislao de Aranzadi to the director’s of® ce, that Editorial Aranzadi started an important process of organizational and vision change. The new director departed from the inertia of the earlier period and started an important process of change in three aspects: automation of the production process of the compendiums (the basic Editorial Aranzadi legal and jurisprudence information products), introduction of documentary informatics and development of the marketing department of the ® rm. The 1987± 1990 period can be seen as an experimental phase in the process of introduction of IT into Editorial Aranzadi. The decision to introduce legal information processing IT and develop IT-based products was not taken until 1990.

Since then IT has been central to Editorial Aranzadi’s new strategy, which is aimed at being leader in both technology and market share by developing new, differentiated products. Part of the main basis for this new strategy has been the creation of the IT department, which has become a central department, alongside the publications and marketing departments. It is important to stress that IT has not only been used to improve the production process (the physical production of the texts), but also, very importantly, to develop new products based on technological formats such as CD-ROM disks or, more recently, on-line systems.

In order to analyse the process of introduction of IT in Editorial Aranzadi, it is important to consider both the type of applications introduced and the organizational changes provoked by this process. As shown in Table 1, the process of IT introduction and implementation has evolved from aspects related to administration towards the consideration of IT as strategic for the ® rm.

Up to 1985 the activity of Editorial Aranzadi can be described as a continuation of the tradition and \`know how’ of the ® rm, which goes back to the 1930s. This \`know how’ was the basis of Editorial Aranzadi’s leadership in its sector up to the 1980s. At this time IT did not exist in the company. The applications related to business administration were subcontracted to an external ® rm, because Editorial Aranzadi did not have the internal computer resources to carry out such applications.

After the arrival of the new director, these IT-based administration functions were internalized by the ® rm. The ® rst computers were bought, along with standard applications for invoicing, payrolls and accountancy. However, the most important thing was the organizational and vision change. Editorial Aranzadi evolved from a crafts workshop (so to speak) into a modern, professionalized company, from following traditional methods to establishing innovation and leadership as a permanent objective and from traditional linotype printing to computerized text composition.

The 1987± 1992 period can be seen as a period of change in the rhythm of introduction of IT and in Editorial Aranzadi’s perception of the importance of this technology. As a ® rst step in the decision in favour of an advanced level of IT introduction, the IT department was created and the head of this department was hired. During an R&D project, the company introduced itself into the world of legal informatics. It was a pilot project in documentary informatics, aimed at both improving productivity with respect to the traditional production method and considering the possibility of developing new products. These new, CD-ROM-based products did not exist in Spain at the time, but were already being used in the USA. A database and its software were bought from a US supplier and they were adapted and developed for Editorial Aranzadi’s needs.

Finally, the third main feature of the 1992± 1997 period was the strategic use of IT for developing new, CD-ROM-based products. The importance of IT in the new strategy was also re¯ ected in Editorial Aranzadi’s decision to open the so-called \`Editorial Aranzadi rooms’ in 15 Spanish universities, with a view to presenting its new products to future, potential clients (students of law in Spain). From an organizational point of view, it was important that the IT department acquired the status of \`® rst-level department’, linked directly to the direction of the ® rm in the same way as the publications department, which up until then had been the core of the organization. Moreover, since then both process and product have become very much IT based and therefore the two departments (publications and IT) have had to work together. There are currently 21 employees in the IT department. The functions of this department are not usual in ® rms the size of Editorial Aranzadi. It covers different functions related to IT: administration applications, legal information processing, advice and attention to subscribers of the CD-ROM products and integration of IT systems (production, administration and legal information processing).

## Product innovation

The development and commercialization of new, ITbased products is one of the most interesting aspects of the recent process of innovation undertaken by Editorial Arazandi. As was stated earlier, the main products are Spanish law and jurisprudence books or compendiums. These books provide two types of information. On the one hand, they reproduce the complete text of law or sentences as published by the original source (parliament or government in the former case or justice court in the latter). Editorial Aranzadi introduces quotes within this text for making references to previous norms and sentences. On the other hand,

Editorial Aranzadi includes its own classi® catory and analytical information. This is the value that Editorial Aranzadi adds to the original legal information and it is made up of different types of information: descriptive (date of approval, court number, judge, date of enforcement, etc.), classi® catory (thematic, chronological, alphabetical and numerical indexes) and analytical (a brief analysis of the sentences and provisions, including main concepts, an abstract and information about the relationships between the document and other documents, such as modi® cation, abolition, etc.).

All this information is sent to the subscribers weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly and yearly. This implies a continuous and complex process of modi® cation of classi® cations, indexes and analyses in the publications department. Traditionally, all this information was arranged in a manual system of ® les of paper cards. As new norms and sentences were produced in the courts, the publications personnel had to add new cards to the ® les and modify the existing cards that were affected. This made the updating of the information increasingly dif® cult. Under the conditions of the mid-1980s (an increase in the production of legal information in Spain), this manual system could not be maintained without an important increase in the amount of publications personnel.

In 1989 Editorial Aranzadi decided to develop its CD-ROM products, which were ® nally launched on the market in 1992. As mentioned earlier, at that time there were two technological options, CD-ROM and on-line. Editorial Aranzadi favoured the former, because of both the situation with the telecommunications infrastructure and the dif® culties of using an on-line database. However, the traditional, printed version was not abandoned.

Even though Editorial Aranzadi offered a free CD-ROM reading device and free aftersale technical service, the reception of the CD-ROM product was not easy in the beginning. On the one hand, there was the problem of the price of initial subscription, as well as the need to have a computer, although in the long-term the CD-ROM version was cheaper than the traditional one. Editorial Aranzadi had to overcome the initial resistance of many clients to new technologies. The Editorial Aranzadi volumes (or aranzadis as they are usually called) had become a symbolic part of the environment of the law profession. It looked as if a lawyer’ s of® ce without the aranzadis was not a real lawyer’s of® ce (when a lawyer’s photograph appears in the media, the aranzadi volumes usually appear in the background, symbolizing seriousness and professionality).

The main advantages of the CD-ROM products are memory capacity and new possibilities for information links and searches. Another advantage is physical space (which is very expensive in downtown of® ce buildings).

For example, since 1979 Editorial Aranzadi’s main jurisprudence collection (compendium) has published 87 volumes, which creates quite a logistic, storage problem.

Editorial Aranzadi’s internal information structure (the ® les of manual cards mentioned earlier) has also been transformed. The company now has a computer thesaurus made up of more than 100 000 concepts linked to each other in a multilevel, branch-like structure. One important part of the process of innovation and organizational change in Editorial Aranzadi (to be analysed in the next section) relates to the introduction of this computer thesaurus and its linkage with the physical production of the volumes.

As mentioned earlier, Editorial Aranzadi’s important efforts in distributing and commercializing its new products have given this ® rm a considerable advantage with respect to its competitors. The distribution process also re¯ ects its strategy of protecting its information: the updated CD-ROM disks are given to the subscribers by hand and the distribution personnel collect the old disks (so that illegal CD use or resales are avoided). Clients of the Editorial Aranzadi volumes are encouraged to turn to CD-ROM products by distribution personnel who have been trained in both the technical and the legal information aspects of the new products. The sales agent shows the advantages of the new product to the client, gives them information about ® nancing possibilities and helps in the start-up process. If the client buys the product, they receive a free training course on the new products. Finally, Editorial Aranzadi has a telephone service for technical and professional assistance. Another interesting aspect of Editorial Aranzadi’s commercialization efforts is the opening of rooms for displaying its products to law students in 15 Spanish universities.

These commercialization, distribution and client attention efforts have been rather successful. In 1996 Editorial Aranzadi had 20 000 subscriptions to CD-ROM products and 30 000 subscriptions to the traditional hardback compendiums (El Pais, 1997). It is important to note that many of these subscriptions are made by of® ces, ® rms and institutions, and, therefore that they are used by more than one person (there are 120 000 lawyers in Spain). Editorial Aranzadi’s volumes are also received by the main Spanish legal institutions (provincial courts, regional courts, Supreme Court, etc.), as well as by the governing body of the law profession (the CGPJ). Editorial Aranzadi’s products are the standard information tools of the law profession in Spain.

## Process innovation

The introduction of IT to Editorial Aranzadi has affected both the process of elaboration of the analytical indexes and the process of physical production of the volume books.

Figure 1 represents the main parts of the process of production of Editorial Aranzadi’s volumes before and after the changes provoked by the introduction of IT. The ® rst step consists of copying the information from the original (law or jurisprudence producing) source. Before IT was introduced, this was done by typists, mostly external to the ® rm although, in some cases, this was done by the internal typists from the photocomposition department. Since the introduction of IT, computer scanning and, when this is not possible, scanning and manual typing are combined. This has allowed a reduction in the amount of typing needed, with fewer errors.

In the second step the reading department performs quality control of the information introduced by the typists. One person reads the texts aloud while a second person listens and compares it to the original. Before IT, four reading cycles were performed in order to eliminate errors and obtain maximum quality texts.

Once the texts are typed, they are sent to the editing and publications department, where the analysts perform the classi® cation and analysis work. After the analytical indexes are ® nished, they are sent to the reading department for proofreading and correction. Afterwards, the texts are revised once again by the analysts and the administrative and technical assistants of the publications department. Then the texts are sent to the composition department where the ® nal products (lea¯ ets and hardback volumes) are produced.

This process has been simpli® ed with the introduction of IT. Before IT, when a typographical error was detected all the affected indexes had to be corrected manually. That is, if one error was detected in an analysis or classi® cation text, all the related indexes had to be modi® ed: chronological, descriptive, classi-® catory, etc. Since IT, the indexes have been linked to each other in a branch-like structure. When a piece of information is modi® ed, this information is automatically corrected in all the affected documents.

The introduction of IT brought about many changes in typing and index composition (and the continuous recomposition process). IT also allowed integration between these activities and the physical production of the volumes. With the legal information database applications, indexes and classi® cations are generated (edited) automatically from the analysis and classi® - cation work carried out by the analysts.

So far this section has focused on the changes provoked by IT-related product and process innovations, but it is important to note that there is also continuity in some of the central activities of Editorial Aranzadi, such as the work carried out by the analysts, which could be considered as the \`intellectual core’ of the company. Information storage and processing activities have been largely automated, but the more analytical tasks (such as those carried out by the analysts) are still carried out by specialized, experienced analysts. Workers who were displaced by automation were relocated in other areas within the ® rm. Thus, even though the production department (the one most affected by automation) had a 45% personnel reduction, overall there was a 20% increase in personnel in Editorial Aranzadi during 1989± 1998. Editorial Aranzadi has launched training programmes for its workers and new contracts have been made for highly skilled jobs. In the period mentioned there has been a 103% increase in personnel with university degrees in the ® rm (from 61 to 112 people) and personnel without speci® c quali® - cations have decreased by 19%.)

The analysts study the sentences and carry out the classi® cation, analysis and abstract work. For this they use their experience, knowledge and systematization and analytical capacity. In so doing they generate new, highly value-added information. Legal information databases and applications help analysts in their work and provide them with very powerful information processing tools, but they do not substitute them, nor do they diminish their quali® cations or their importance in the ® rm.

## Conclusion

The introduction of IT to Editorial Aranzadi has brought about an important transformation in this company, from an organizational as well as technical viewpoint. IT was ® rst used for automating routine, administrative tasks. Then it was increasingly related to organizational change and it became a core element of Editorial Aranzadi’s strategy and a source of competitive advantage.

Two aspects of this process of change deserve special attention. On the one hand, IT has been used for the development of new products. Although subscription to these products entails some important costs for lawyers’ of® ces, Editorial Aranzadi’s CD-ROM compendiums have important advantages over its traditional volumes (information storage and processing capabilities, information searching capabilities, etc.).

On the other hand, IT has provoked important changes in the production process. The \`intellectual’ (legal information storage, processing and analysis) and \`physical’ (composition and production of compendium books) functions of the ® rm have been linked. Editorial Aranzadi’s central information stock (or thesaurus) is now a database made up of interconnected computer nodes representing law and jurisprudence information. The introduction of this IT-based thesaurus has made it possible to link the analysis work, the index generation work and the composition and physical production work. In this paper, we have analysed the change from the traditional, manual system of cards and ® les for storing and classifying information to this new, computer-based system.

The changes in products and processes contrast with the continuities in Editorial Aranzadi’s \`core of intellectual activity’ (so to speak), the classi® cation and analysis work carried out by the ® rm’s experts, which is the main source of added value for the ® rm. Editorial Aranzadi’s information analysis experts now have much more potent tools for information storage and processing, but their classi® cation and analysis tasks remain very much the same. The effects of IT introduction for these professionals are not those announced by the labour process literature. Legal information storage and processing systems have automated the routine work carried out by administrative and technical assistants, but analysis tasks (and the most dif® cult of these, jurisprudence analysis) have not been automated. There are important dif® culties in reducing the complexity of the tasks of classi® cation and analysis of jurisprudence information to parameters which can be formalized in arti® cial intelligence systems. Jurisprudence is constantly evolving as a response to social change and to the emergence of new types of legal con¯ icts.

In summary, in the late 1980s and early 1990s Editorial Aranzadi underwent an important process of technical and organizational innovation which has allowed this ® rm to continue to ful® l its basic mission in an environment that is very different (in terms of the volume of information to be processed, the market and technology) from the one in which the ® rm started in 1929. The continuities in the work methodology of Editorial Aranzadi’s small analyst core (and the network of external collaborators) contrast with the technical and organizational transformation of the company. These changes have been necessary so that Editorial Aranzadi can go on ful® lling its mission in a radically different environment.
