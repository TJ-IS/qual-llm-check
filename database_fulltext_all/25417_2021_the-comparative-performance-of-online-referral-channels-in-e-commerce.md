# The Comparative Performance of Online Referral Channels in E-Commerce

Wenjing Duan and Jie Zhang

2201 G Street, NW, Funger Hall 515F School of Business, The George Washington University, Washington, DC 20052; 701 S. West St., Box 19437 College of Business Administration The University of Texas at Arlington Arlington, TX 76019

## ABSTRACT

The means by which e-commerce websites can reach and track online customers have expanded enormously through the use of various digital marketing referral channels. However, evaluating comparative efectiveness and return on investment (ROI) across diferent referral channels remain dificult undertakings for many companies. This study aims to contribute to this line of investigation by quantifying the relative efectiveness, the dynamics, and the interdependencies among three types of major online referral channels: search engines, social media, and third-party websites. To this end, we employ the vector autoregressive (VARX) model on a large-scale clickstream dataset and have the following findings. Though search engine referrals demonstrate strong impact on sales, our results show that social media referrals have the strongest immediate and cumulative efects on e-commerce websites’ conversion rates. Our results also demonstrate the synergies and interdependencies across these channels. This study contributes to the multi-channel analytics literature and sheds new light to digital marketing managers on assessing the cumulative impact and the economic value of online referral channels.

## KEYWORDS

digital advertising; marketing attribution; social media; online referral channels; clickstream data; vector autoregressive model; e-commerce

## Introduction

Online consumers are nowadays interacting with companies through a diverse mixture of touch points in various channels and media, resulting in more multi-faceted customer purchase journeys [31, 34]. Customers respond to digital marketing media and various online information resources, such as clicking on response links on search engines, display ads on social media platforms, and referral links on third-party websites. Hence, these digital marketing interventions and communications have become the “channels” through which customers are directed to visit firms’ websites in the hope that their interests will be converted into final purchases [3, 32]. Hereafter, we will use the term “online referral channels” to explicitly denote the digital advertising and communication media outlets that lead customers to visit and make purchases on e-commerce websites.

Customers show diverse preferences of the online referral channels. To diferent merchants (advertisers), online referral channels vary in their benefits and costs in directing online trafic, often resulting in some channels more efective than the others [31, 34]. Moreover, channel choices are afected by one another because of customer lock-in efects, channel inertia, and cross-channel synergies [5, 31, 34, 45]. Nowadays, a rapidly growing number of e-commerce companies are turning to more sophisticated multi-channel marketing analytics to gain more insights into their successes and failures. However, according to recent industry surveys by Benes [7] and HubSpot Research [29], measuring and evaluating relative efectiveness and Return on Investment (ROI) across diferent channels remain significant and dificult undertakings for many companies. Academics and practitioners alike have shown fast growing interest in studying how to attribute the appropriate recognition for conversions and sales to various online referral channels. Nevertheless, prior studies primarily focused on either one media platform or a single website without considering the interactions of multiple referral channels and cross-website competitions (e.g., [17, 32, 46]). Additionally, extant literature often yield findings that are only applicable to the immediate time period, while considerable industry experience has shown that it takes time and channel cooperation to drive final conversion [31, 45]. This study aims to contribute to this line of investigation by quantifying the relative efectiveness, the dynamics, and the interdependencies among three types of major online referral channels: search engines, social media, and other third-party websites.

Recent industry reports showed that social media referral trafic has been growing very rapidly and surpassing search engine referral trafic over the past decade [57]. Surprisingly, to our best knowledge, few studies have examined the overall efectiveness of social media platforms in contrast to other major referral channels. HubSpot Research [29] indicated that marketers today are investing more in social media sites, but less than 10% of sales professionals perceive social media as a successful channel. How to measure the efectiveness and ROI of social media marketing remains one of the top marketing challenges. There are ample prior studies investigating the efect of social media on consumer behavior, product choice, and financial market performance (e.g., [16, 36, 44]), although they predominately focused on the content and engagement in a single social platform. Some recent work [7, 30, 42] has suggested that examining only the direct links to purchase from a social media platform may significantly underestimate its potential customer conversion capabilities from interacting with other channels and building long-term brand capital. To fill this gap, the present study aims to investigate the dynamics of the social media referral channel and its interrelatedness to search engine and third-party referral channels.

To achieve these goals, we focus on the following empirical questions in this paper. (1) What is the relative efectiveness and interrelatedness of online referral channels (i.e., search engine, social media, and third-party websites) on e-commerce websites’ sales performance measures? (2) What are the dynamics (immediate and cumulative efects) of the relationships among the online referral channels and their impact on e-commerce websites’ sales performance measures? To address these questions, we leverage a large-scale clickstream dataset to examine consumers’ web visits and purchasing behavior, directed through the three major referral channels. Our empirical analysis employs a multivariate time-series technique: the vector autoregressive model with exogenous covariates (VARX). The advantage of the VARX model is to treat all key variables (sales metrics and referral channels) as jointly endogenous and evaluate the interdependencies among them [21, 8, 14]. In addition, the model takes a flexible multivariate time-series approach, which allows it to assess the dynamic relationships among all endogenous variables and reveal the enduring, accumulative efects of the referral channels [2, 36, 44].

Our work makes three contributions to the IS and marketing literature. First, to our best knowledge, our paper is the first to explicitly assess the social media referral channel, relative to the other two major channels, in terms of its impact on e-commerce website performance. Our findings show that social media channels have a stronger conversion efect than search engines. Second, our paper also difers significantly from the prior research by estimating both the immediate and the cumulative efects of the referral channels. Our findings show that social media channels have stronger immediate and cumulative efects on conversion rates than search engines and third-party referral websites. Third, this paper is also among the first to quantify the relative efectiveness of social media, search engine, and third-party referral channels, taking into consideration the interdependencies among the three channels. These findings have important implications for social media marketing and multi-channel attribution research and practices.

## Research Background

This paper is related to the literature on social media marketing, though our research is the first to focus on the comparative efectiveness of social media referral channels on e-commerce website performance. Often in the context of a single social media platform, most prior studies have emphasized on examining the efect of firms’ social media activities, advertising content, and consumer-generated content on either consumers’ social media engagement or their purchase behavior. For example, Goh et al. [21] quantified the relative impact of user-generated content and marketer-generated content on consumers’ purchase expenditures. Rishika et al. [44] studied how user participation in a major social networking website afected customer visit frequency and profitability. Lee et al. [33] examined how various types of social media marketing content work diferently on diferent levels of consumer engagement, and showed how certain directly informative content, such as deals and promotions, drive consumers’ path to conversion (click-throughs). Hu et al. [28] developed a text analytics framework and analyzed diferent social media data sources to measure brand personality. Yang et al. [53] investigated user-generated content and customer engagement on Facebook business pages. Recent research also studied the business value and strategic implementation of companies’ social media marketing eforts. Luo et al. [39], Mai et al. [40], and Xie et al. [51] demonstrated the strong correlation between social media activities and financial markets. Our paper difers significantly from previous studies by examining the overall efectiveness of social media channels in referring trafic to downstream e-commerce website purchases.

Our research also falls within the realm of multi-channel marketing. IS and Marketing researchers have long been interested in examining the efectiveness of various advertising formats, such as the investigation on search engine marketing eficiencies [3, 10, 11, 17, 1920, 2223, 35, 44, 53] and interactions between online channels and traditional ofline channels [4, 6, 14, 16, 21, 29, 31, 4950, 5455]. The attribution of online consumer purchases to various online touch points has recently received a great deal of attention and gained a strong foothold in multi-channel research and path to purchase modeling [4, 30]. Recent research has proposed various attribution models, primarily focusing on a single website’s consumer path to purchase. Xu et al. [52] proposed a mutually exciting point process model for online advertising and conversion. They found that display advertisements are more likely to stimulate subsequent visits through other advertisement formats than direct purchases. Abhishek et al. [1] reported similar findings on the relative efects of search and display ads by applying the hidden Markov model to uncover the efects of ads at diferent latent purchase stages. Li and Kannan [35] developed a nested logit model that predicts touch point considerations and their impact on the purchase of high-involvement goods or services. Ghose and Todri [19] applied a diference-indiference estimator to examine how the exposure to and duration of diferent types of display ads afect consumers’ passive and active search behavior and propensity to purchase. Using data from the online airline ticket booking industry, Li et al. [37] extended prior research by integrating the impact of competing firms into their multi-channel attribution modeling process. A recent work by Todri et al. [49] examined the advertising efectiveness and annoyance dynamics across the purchase funnel. Their findings demonstrated the adverse efect of persistent display advertising exposures on consumers at diferent stages of the purchase funnel.

There are several important diferences distinguishing this paper from previous related multi-channel studies. First, to the best of our knowledge, our study is the first to explicitly include social media channels into the multi-referral-channel analysis of online retailing. Our study aims to demonstrate the importance of measuring the cumulative efect of social media channels, as well as taking into consideration of the interdependencies and dynamics of multiple referral channels. Second, the large majority of extant work models customers visit and purchase decisions concerning a single website (firm) (e.g., [18, 34, 49]). Our analysis involves a large set of online stores, which allows us to not only test the referral channels’ efects to the focal website, but also take into consideration the efect of referral trafic directed to the competing websites, which represents more comprehensively the online business environment. Third, prior studies have often attempted to approach the multichannel attribution problem by modeling individual purchase paths (e.g., [34, 36]). One general issue with modeling individual behavior is that multiple (and often distinct) touch points occur in diferent phases of the purchase funnel. The combination of touch points can be endless, and the focus of modeling individual behavior is to understand the myriad possibilities and paths that may be taken by a customer. As a consequence, touch point efects can be endogenous, likely leading to inaccurate conclusions [34]. We take a diferent and complementary approach in this study by treating all referral channels as jointly endogenous. This more comprehensive (channel-level) investigation focuses on examining the overall relative importance, the dynamics, and the interdependencies among referral channels. This macro-level approach will help reveal the full referral channel efects without being overshadowed by any unique and particular combination of individual user behavior.

As shown in Figure 1, our conceptual framework focuses on the impact of online referral channels on e-commerce website performance, accounting for the dynamic efects among the key endogenous variables of three major online referral channels and e-commerce website performance metrics. The endogenous treatment of the online referral channels entails that they are explained by both past variables of themselves (autoregressive carryover efects) and past variables of each other (cross efects from other referral channels). Hence, the model can gather the trajectory of not only the short-term and immediate efects of the referral channels but also the long-term, enduring, and cumulative efects. The model also allows for complex feedback loops that may capture the reverse efect of the online stores’ performance on future trafic directed from various referral channels. Further, we consider the complication of market competition by including the impacts of both own and rival referral channel trafic on online stores’ performance metrics. Thus, our modeling framework can account for the complex chained efects in a cycle, unearthing the full impact and dynamics of online referral channels.

## Data and Methodology

## Research Setting and Data

This study employs the comScore Web Behavior Database from Wharton Research Data Services (https://wrds-web.wharton.upenn.edu/wrds/). The Database contains clickstream (user sessions) data covering the browsing sessions, purchasing histories, and demographic profiles of approximately 100,000 US households. The sequence and timing of all URLs viewed by each panel member were recorded and organized into user sessions, which are defined as visits to a sequence of websites uninterruptedly without a gap of more than 30 minutes [37]. User sessions are identified using unique machine IDs and each session tracks a panelist’s ID, date and time of visits, domain names, and referral domain names (from which referral channels can be inferred). Visits during which users saw the “confirm-order” page of a store’s website were identified as purchase visits, about which more information regarding the product quantity, product total price, and basket total amount was gathered. The demographic variables include household size, age, region, education level, income level, race, presence of children, and Internet connection speed.<sup>1</sup>

We construct the daily clickstream data for the entire year of 2011. Our analysis examines daily data to ensure that our time series model captures the fast-changing nature of the online advertising, search, and purchase environments. The daily data structure also allows for suficient degrees of freedom in estimating our multivariate time series model. For this particular inquiry, we focus on the category of “general online retail merchandisers” based on comScore’s store classification. We select eight major websites that are ranked by total sales in the dataset: Amazon.com, Walmart.com, Target.com, Macys.com, Sears.com, JCPenney.com, Costco.com, and Kohls.com. Together, these eight websites account for 95% of the total sales in our subsample. We choose them because (1) these stores were relatively more frequently shopped online, ensuring that the data included suficient daily transactions for analysis; and (2) the popularity and large diversity of products of these stores increased the probability that consumers obtained product information, were exposed to marketing communications, and were referred from various channels (retrieved from the “referral domains” field of the comScore dataset) while searching for and locating products of interest. We also include seven “minor” websites in the same category when constructing competing referral trafic measures: SamsClub.com, Bloomingdales.com, Dillards.com, Kmart.com, NeimanMarcus.com, Nordstrom.com, and SaksFifthAvenue.com. For each website, we aggregate the observations and construct the variables on a daily basis.

Users often accessed the focal online stores by following links on previously visited websites, which we define as the “referral channels”. The referral channel information was provided in the comScore database. In other words, if a visit to the focal domain occurred through an external referral link, the comScore database recorded the domain name of the referral website, i.e., referral domain name. Following the literature (e.g., [2627, 34, 36]), we classify the referral channels into three types: search engine (e.g., Google.com, Yahoo.com, and Bing.com), social media (e.g., Facebook.com, Youtube.com, and Bizrate.com), and third-party referral (e.g., DoubleClick.net and Comcast.com) websites. Table 1 provides an illustration of the top websites of each type of referral channel and the frequency (in percentage) of user referrals in our daily session data.

We are interested in the following endogenous variables in estimating the VARX model. We use two variables (Sales and Conversion Rate) to measure an e-commerce website’s sales performance. The daily sales amount in US\$ (Sales) can be directly derived from the database by calculating all consumer spending at each website within each day. Sales is an important measurement for e-commerce websites to track, which can give businesses actionable information to reflect performance. The conversion rate (Conversion Rate) measures the probability that a visit to a website from any given referral channel will become a purchase.<sup>2</sup> This can be calculated on a daily basis as follows: Conversion $\begin{array} { r } { R a t e = \frac { N u m b e r \ o f \ p u r c h a s e s } { N u m b e r \ o f \ V i s i t s } } \end{array}$ . Conversion rate measures the likelihood (fraction) of turning a click into a purchase. It helps understand what percentage (ratio) of people are completing purchases out of the total website visits, which allows the business to gauge the efectiveness of ad campaigns and the successfulness of the site, and to identify areas for improvement. There are many recent industry reports discussing the importance of Conversion Rate Optimization [17], because it allows businesses to lower their customer acquisition costs by getting more value from the visitors.

We use three variables (Search Engine, Social Media, and Third-Party) to measure the total daily number of referrals from the three referral channels, respectively. Since many customers visit online stores directly without going through any referral channel, we also include the daily number of direct visits (Direct). The eight major online retail merchandisers are, in general, competitors. Thus, we construct the rival referral channel variables (Rival Search Engine, Rival Social Media, Rival Third-Party, and Rival Direct), which measure the number of referrals by a certain type of channel to competing websites. In addition, referral trafic to the minor websites also competes with that to the major online stores; therefore, we combine the seven minor online retail websites into an aggregated minor store by taking a weighted average (by page views) of their referral trafic for each channel. Then, for each focal website, the measures for the rival referrals were constructed by taking the average of the referral channel variables for the other seven major websites and the aggregated minor websites.

We also include a set of exogenous variables in our VARX model. To account for the overall advertising efort, which may vary considerably across diferent online retail websites and potentially have a great impact on sales, we acquired the online advertising spending data for 2011 from the Ad\$pender database (Kantar Media) for all of the websites in our sample. We use the weekly online ad spending (Online Ad Spending) for each online store as a control variable in our VARX model. Since we use daily-aggregated data, the overall customer profile visiting each website may change every day, even if an individual household’s characteristics hold constant over time. We thus include average household age and household income to control for consumer demographic variations. We also incorporate the store-related keyword daily search volume index from Google Trends for each website as a variable (Google Trend) to control for overall consumer attention to the website and any exogenous demand shocks (e.g., daily variations, seasonal promotions, and advertising campaigns). More detailed descriptive statistics for each of the major websites and the overall store-level summary information are included in Online Appendix Tables OA1 and OA2. For the purpose of illustration, Figures 2 and 3 show the time-series plots of referral channel trafic, sales, and conversion rates for Amazon.com and Walmart.com. There is substantial variation in the raw data. The figures depict a moderate time-based correlation between the referrals from diferent channels and sales performance measures. The plots also demonstrate modest serial (temporal) correlations among the referral channels.

## VARX Model Specification and Estimation

We use the VARX model, a multivariate time-series technique [2, 13, 15, 38], to capture dynamic interdependencies, competition efects, and feedback efects among the key endogenous variables. The VARX model is particularly advantageous for testing specifications with hard to define restrictions because it provides unrestricted estimations to reduced forms of general models without assuming any specific causal structures [2, 5, 42]. The only required prior knowledge is a list of variables that can be hypothesized as afecting each other in an intertemporal manner. The jointly endogenous treatment in our VARX specification implies that, for each website, the sales measures and referral trafic are explained by both autoregressive carryover efects (past variables of themselves) and cross efects (past variables of each other). This ensures the robustness of the model and accounts for such biases as nonstationarity, spurious causality, endogeneity, serial correlations, omitted variables, and reverse causality [9, 25].

Our VARX analysis employs the following steps, which are applied to each website separately. First, we examine the dynamic interactions among the referral channels and online store sales measures by estimating the full VARX model. Second, we track the cumulative sales measures’ responses to a one-unit shock from each of the referral channels by analyzing the impulse response functions. Third, we quantify the relative efects of the three referral channels on sales measures using variance decomposition. Finally, we derive the immediate and cumulative economic impacts for each website in terms of the total value efect of the three referral channels. Following the literature [47], the main results are presented by taking the average of all estimates across the eight major online retail websites.<sup>4</sup>

## Step 1: VARX Model Specification

## Step 2: Generalized Impulse Response Functions (GIRFs)

Impulse response analysis is an important step in VAR model analyses [26]. Since all variables in a VAR model depend on each other, individual coeficient estimates only ofer limited information on the reaction of the system to a shock (change). In order to get a better picture of the model’s dynamic behavior, impulse responses are used. Their main purpose is to trace the efect of a unit shock (one standard deviation) in any one of the variables on all other variables in the system over subsequent periods. In other words, the impulse response functions can be interpreted as a one standard deviation shock (increase) to x causes significant increases (decreases) in y for m periods, after which the efect dissipates (to reach its asymptotic in more technical term). We inspect the GIRFs based on the estimated parameters of the full VARX model. In our context, the impulse response functions estimate the net result of a “shock” to the search engine, social media, or thirdparty referral channels on sales and conversion rates relative to their baselines (their expected points in the absence of the shock). Specifically, we measure cumulative sales and conversion responses to a one-unit shock (one percentage change in our log-log VARX setup) with the simultaneous-shocking approach [13]. The residual variance-covariance matrix of Equation (1) is used to derive a vector of expected instantaneous shock values. We derive the following summary statistics from each GIRF: (1) the immediate impact, i.e., one time period, on website sales and conversion rates, which is readily observable and applicable to managers; (2) the total cumulative impact, which combines all the efects across dustsettling periods<sup>6</sup> and helps managers scrutinize whether and how much the paths of search engine, social media, and third-party channels contribute to sales and conversion in the long run; and (3) based on the cumulative impact, we estimate the economic impact of each additional click-through from a search engine, social media or third-party site on net profit.

## Step 3: Generalized Forecast Error Variance Decomposition (GFEVD)

GFEVD analyzes the proportion of movements in a time series due to shocks of both the time series variables and other variables [2]. More specifically, the GFEVD reveals the percentage of the forecast error variance of a variable because of shocks of all the variables in the VAR system at a specified time horizon [2]. While Granger causality tests and GIRF analyses show some evidence of the significance of interactions among variables in the VAR system, GFEVD is like a partial $R ^ { 2 } { \mathrm { ; } }$ , which suggests the magnitude of the efect by quantifying the percentage of forecast error variance attributed to each variable [2, 43]. Based on the VARX parameters, we also derive GFEVD estimates to answer the following question: to what extent do online referral channels explain the variance of store sales and conversion rates beyond the controls? Similar to the dynamic $R ^ { 2 } { \mathrm { ; } }$ GFEVD can gauge the relative power over time of shocks initiated by each endogenous variable in explaining the store performance measures without assuming a causal ordering [41, 43]. GFEVD attributes 100% of forecast error variance in sales and conversions to all endogenous variables, thus it can derive the relative predictive value of the three referral channels. To reduce sensitivity to short-term fluctuations, the relative importance of the endogenous variables is established based on the GFEVD values at 20 days as adopted in prior studies [39, 48].<sup>7</sup>

## Model Estimation and Results

## Test for Stationarity in Time Series

The process of estimating the VARX model begins with conducting Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) unit root tests to check stationarity, as GFEVD only allows comparable analyses with stationary variables [13]. As reported in Table 4, the ADF test statistics<sup>8</sup> range from -2.89 to -17.77, all of which are significant $\left( p < 0 . 0 5 \right)$ , suggesting that the series are stationary and do not cointegrate in equilibrium [26]. The optimal lag length of the VARX model is selected according to Schwartz’s Bayesian Information Criterion (SBC) and final prediction error (FPE). We allow for various lag lengths in the model and select the lag order with the minimized SBC and FPE [13, 39]. The optimal lag order was one or two according to these criteria for our models. Our Durbin-Watson and Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) statistics show no significant autocorrelation or multicollinearity (the VIF test results are included in Online Appendix Table OA3). We also test various assumptions of the VARX model estimation residuals, including omission-of-variable bias, multivariate normality, portmanteau autocorrelation, and heteroscedasticity. We found no violations of these assumptions.

## Test for Granger Causality

We follow Tirunillai and Tellis [48] and Luo et al. [39] to conduct Granger causality tests [24]. The results, as reported in Table 5, show that the three channel metrics (Search Engine, Social Media, and Third-Party) almost all significantly “Granger-cause” the sales performance metrics of the focal online stores. As for referral trafic to competing websites, the competitors’ channel metrics also significantly Granger-cause sales performance metrics of the focal website for most online stores. The results also show some significant temporal causal relationships among the three referral channels. Online Appendix Table OA4 contains the Granger causality test results for each major website, which consistently show significant temporal causality among the key endogenous variables. These results provide the initial evidence of the prospective significant temporal causal relationships between the referral channels and online store sales measures, as well as the dynamic interdependencies among the referral channels.

As shown in Table 6, for the immediate impact, all three referral channel metrics have significant positive relationships with sales (0.146 to 0.305, p < 0.05), with search engine referrals showing the strongest impact. Search engines are direct response tools and customer frequently search specific products with strong purchase intension. Often the “last-click” before purchase, search engine visits dominate the late stage of online consumer purchase journey. Thus, trafic referred through search engines are more likely to lead to subsequent purchases (sales).

All three referral channel metrics also have significant positive relationships with conversion rates (0.125 to 0.237, p < 0.05), with social media referrals being the strongest. Social media channels are primarily brand-building tools, which mostly occupy the earlier stage of consumer purchase journey and it usually takes time to showcase the efect. Often not directly linked to purchase, social media platforms are communities for customers to discuss products and share experiences, which may or may not have relevance to the product, but are of interests to the entire community. Hence, social medial platforms are particularly advantageous in driving exponential growth in referring products as networks grow.

It is interesting to note that all three referral channels’ efects are stronger than those of direct visits. Direct visits often suggest customer loyalty, yet its impact on sales is not that straightforward. For example, one customer may directly go to a store’s website to search and compare products. Another customer may search a product on Google with strong purchase desire, and then go to a store’s website through Google’s referral link to purchase. In other words, direct visit may be more “store specific” while referral trafic can be more “product specific.” Such efects are expected to vary not only across diferent stores but also diferent products (see Online Appendix Table OA5).

## Relative Importance of the Referral Channels

The variance decomposition of the GFEVD results shows the percentage of variance in the sales performance metrics explained by the referral channel variables. The findings provide the relative power of each referral channel in explaining the variance of sales and conversion rates. The results in Table 7 show that all three referral channel metrics explain nontrivial portions of the variance. Among the three referral channel metrics, search engines explain most of the variance (7.10%) in sales, followed by social media (6.11%), and third-party websites (4.59%); meanwhile, social media explains most of the variance (7.72%) in conversion rates, with a percentage that is much higher than search engines (4.98%) and thirdparty websites (4.34%). Social media referrals to the competing websites also explain most of the variance in both sales and conversion rates (7.72% and 3.86%, respectively).

## Dynamic Interdependencies among the Online Referral Channels

The GIRF results also allow us to estimate the dynamic interdependencies among the three referral channels, as shown in Table 8. Significant immediate and cumulative efects are both observed. In particular, both social media and third-party channels have positive Granger-causal impacts on search engines; the efects are particularly strong cumulatively, which suggests that the total efects of search engines should be partly attributed to the facilitation of social media and third-party referrals. Xu et al. [52] showed that third-party display ads helped stimulate more subsequent visits from search engines; our results suggest that the social media channel’s impact on future search engine visits is even stronger. Search engines, meanwhile, also help generate more social media and third-party trafic, with stronger efects on the latter. All three channels also drive more future direct website visits. Consumer decision-making process is quite complex, and consumers may go through and are influenced by multiple referral channels before making purchases. These findings take the first step to showcase the complex spillover and feedback efects among the referral channels, reafirming the suitability and importance of employing the multivariate time series approach. The intricate interdependencies among the referral channels render the measurement of cumulative efects more important as these channels work progressively with each other to convert customers. More detailed channel-channel interactions and mechanisms are highly store and product dependent, suggesting the importance of measuring channel collaborations in assessing efectiveness.

## Economic Values of the Referral Channel Metrics

To translate our VARX results into more intuitive interpretations and link the findings directly to firm performance, we estimate the economic impact of a one-unit unexpected change to a referral channel metric on sales. Following the literature [13, 39], we multiply the immediate and cumulative impulse responses with the Net Operating Profit after Tax (NOPAT) margin obtained from the websites’ income statements in financial year 2011.<sup>9</sup> The results are shown in Table 9, and the numbers show the estimated prospective marginal profit benefit from additional trafic referred from various channels derived from our VARX model estimates.<sup>10</sup>

For the immediate and cumulative impacts, respectively, one additional referral from a search engine is expected to increase the website’s net profit by \$0.19-\$6.04 in the shortrun (one day) and by \$0.74-\$21.56 in the long-run (20 days). One additional referral from social media is expected to increase the website’s net profit by \$0.13-\$3.35 (immediate) and by \$0.63- \$18.91 (cumulative). Given today’s tremendous volume of social media referral trafic, this can have a substantial cumulative sales impact. Finally, one additional referral from a third-party channel is expected to increase the website’s net profit by \$0.12-\$2.91 (immediate) and by \$0.42-\$11.19 (cumulative). These estimates, for example, can be used to propose the maximum willingness-to-pay amount when companies and ad agencies are planning their budgets in payper-click ad campaigns. Considering that many websites in our data sample also have ofline stores and other sales venues, these estimates should be viewed more in the relative sense across diferent stores. Although this is a high-level and simplified approach, it demonstrates that our methodology can be adopted by online stores when analyzing their proprietary and granular data to generate real-time updates as inputs to adjust their campaign strategies.

## Additional Analyses and Robustness Checks

We carry out several additional analyses and robustness checks to validate our findings. To ensure that two of our primary control variables, Online Ad Spending and Google Trend, are indeed exogenous, we conduct Granger causality tests between the two control variables and the endogenous variables. The results are shown in Online Appendix Table OA8. We find no evidence of temporal causality from the endogenous variables to the two control variables. To address potential concerns on the validity and sensitivity of our results, we conduct additional VAR estimations using diferent structures.

First, we use Conversion Number to replace Conversion Rate as our sales performance measure. We report the Granger causality test, impulse response, and variance decomposition results, which are qualitatively similar to our main results, in Online Appendix Tables OA9-OA11. Second, we remove the competing website referral metrics from the model, and the more parsimonious VARX estimation results, which are also quite consistent with our main results, are presented in Online Appendix Tables OA12-OA13. Third, we remove the exogenous variables from the model and estimate a simpler VAR specification. The results are included in Online Appendix Tables OA14-OA15 and show qualitatively similar outcomes to our main findings. Fourth, we control for the seasonal dummy variable including weekends and holidays,<sup>11</sup> and obtain similar results to our main findings as shown in Online Appendix OA16-OA17.

## Discussion and Conclusion

Leveraging large-scale clickstream data, this research aims to answer two research questions: (1) what is the relative efectiveness and interrelatedness of search engine, social media, and third-party referrals on e-commerce websites’ performance? and (2) what are the dynamics of the relationships among the online referral channels and their impacts on e-commerce websites’ performance? Carrying on the essence of the growing interest in this line of research, our study has been focusing on generating new insights through empirical discovery. This research contributes to the literature in IS and Marketing on social media and multi-channel digital marketing.

To our best knowledge, this paper is the first to explicitly consider social media channels in the multi-channel analytics research, hence the findings have important implications for researchers and companies to assess the efectiveness of social media marketing investment. Our results show that social media referrals have the strongest impact on e-commerce websites’ conversion rates, with particularly large cumulative efects. These findings demonstrate the significance of the cumulative, enduring impact of social media referral channels in converting prospective customers. Social media channels are becoming more important for researchers and marketers to evaluate long-term productivity benefit. In addition, the time-series models used in this study can estimate the cumulative efects of referral channels, which prevents underestimating the impact due to solely focusing on immediate efect [39]. Time-series models and the cumulative efects, therefore, should be paid more attention in web and social analytics research.

Furthermore, our study adds to the multi-channel attribution literature by collectively modeling the dynamics and interactions among the three referral channels, which clearly demonstrate the synergies and interdependencies across these channels. Our findings suggest that the referral channels’ efects could be much more cumulatively pronounced over time, and their impact could be considerably underrated if researchers and businesses only focus on measuring the immediate impact. In other words, if only measuring the immediate sales impact of referral trafic, the efect of search engines will be disproportionately amplified because it is often the last touch point before purchase, and the efect of other upper funnel channels would be utterly overshadowed. In fact, this reflects the current industry practice, which drives the exponentially growing cost of keyword bidding in search engines. Our findings ofer some answers and reconciliation to the debate among researchers and practitioners on the relative efectiveness of referral channels, in which search engines have been enjoying the spotlight. The efect of search engines, as shown in our results, can be greatly influenced (facilitated) by other channels. Digital marketing is a dynamic problem, and one of the most imperative tasks is building long-term brand capital for a company. The carryover and spillover efects among referral channels can be critical in fulfilling this task by generating many intermediary activities, such as increased awareness, an enlarged consumer choice set, and increased consumer engagement.

This research also have implications for managers in several ways. Consumers are increasingly spending a greater share of time in online social networks. As a result, firms are also spending considerably more of their marketing budgets on social media. A recent report by HubSpot Research [29] recognized how to measure the efectiveness and ROI of social media marketing as one of the top challenges. Meanwhile, our findings indicate that trafic referred from social media channels is a strong driving force for conversion, suggesting that clicks through social media channels may have better quality in terms of having a higher ratio of conversion to a final purchase. The underlying rationale is that online social network users can rely on friends and connections to filter and recommend content, which can generate greater referral trafic to further product search, exploration, and eventual purchase intentions. The immediate, on-site sales efect of social media channels can be an inadequate and utterly undervalued measure because the primary motives for customers to visit social networks are to connect and interact, and it takes more time and a longer path for them to make purchase decisions. Our findings show the important role of social media channels in Conversion Rate Optimization, and suggest that companies should pay more attention to measuring the cumulative conversion efect in assessing the efectiveness of social media marketing eforts.

Our findings on the strong cumulative conversion efect of social media channels is also particularly important for many companies who are fighting for visibility in the search engine bidding war with limited resources. Instead of taking part in this fierce competition, it is more efective for online stores to leverage the efectiveness of social media marketing channels to build long-term brand capital and achieve cumulative conversion growth. Digital marketing managers are expected to balance investments across diferent referral channels to serve diferent financial goals. If the goal is to boost immediate or short-term profits, then targeting search engine marketing campaigns would likely uplift sales immediately. If the goal is to boost long-term conversion and sales, then more efort and resources should be allocated to social media channels in building brand awareness, cultivating interests, and attracting new customers.

This research is among the first to examine, at the macro level, multiple referral channels and their relative importance, dynamics, and interrelatedness. Kaul [32] pointed out that only 2% of marketers optimize to business outcomes, and responsible measurement is critical to build a fully integrated omni-channel strategy. In a survey by Benes [7], only about 50% of companies with 100+ employees are using multi-channel attribution models, and the adoption growth rate has considerably slowed. Our research not only stresses the importance of adopting multi-channel attribution methods, but also provides some applicable guidance on their implementation. Our results indicate that online stores should keep tracking and measuring both the immediate and cumulative efects for diferent channels, thus diversifying their short-term and long-term strategies. Diverting attention from only the short-term impact would also help online stores avoid falling victim to the skyrocketing search engine keyword bidding costs. Closely monitoring and analyzing the interdependencies among diferent channels is also a must for companies to quickly eliminate inefective tactics and constantly rebalance or redistribute their budgets. Our results also show that referral trafic directed to competitors, especially from social media channels, plays an important role in impairing the focal website’s sales performance. Hence, managers need to tailor their strategies to incorporate their own websites’ performance analysis and their competitors reactions by scrutinizing their own and their competitors’ Internet referral trafic.

This study has several limitations that direct the paths for future research. First, our research focuses on general merchandise e-commerce websites. Future research could examine other important industries to enrich the implications of the present study. Second, this study takes a macro-level approach to investigate the overall comparative efectiveness and dynamics of referral channels, and our data cannot diferentiate the specific viewing (clicking) behavior from various referral channels. This approach can certainly be complemented by a more micro-level investigation to understand the nuances of how consumers interact with various referral channels. One interesting extension of this research would be conducting field experiments to trace consumer responses and footprints after they are exposed to social media marketing communication to uncover the cumulative conversion efect mechanism. Third, with the increasing number of ofline store closures, it is important to revisit the interaction between online and ofline channels. The decision to close ofline stores simply due to lower than expected ofline sales performance may ignore an important role that these stores play in building product awareness and facilitating searches; it is certainly worth the attention required to reevaluate the role of ofline channels in conjunction with online referral channels. Fourth, the VAR methods have some limitations [56]. One is that the standard methods miss nonlinearities. Another limitation is that the estimation complexity grows exponentially when more endogenous variables are included, which requires suficient length of time series and data points. These limitations call for exploration for other modeling techniques if nonlinearity needs to be considered and more referral channels are to be included. Finally, we would like to acknowledge the age of our data, though asserting that the research questions, the modeling and analysis approaches, and the implications remain relevant nowadays. The dataset used in our study only included PC desktop computer Internet browsing activities and the landscape and the referral strategies keep changing, and there are new channels (e.g., the mobile channel) emerging over the years. Mobile channels have certainly become increasingly important, directly interfering and interacting with other channels, and recent academic research has mainly considered the efects of mobile promotions and the adoption of mobile shopping on purchase behavior. Since the introduction of new channels and touch points may induce even more switching across channels and add even more complexity to the customer purchase decision process, the analysis conducted in the present research can be readily extended to incorporate the efects of mobile channels.

In conclusion, the impact of social media channels and their interactions with other online referral channels have not been well studied. We provide a channel-level investigation focusing on examining the relative efectiveness, the dynamics, and the interdependencies among three major referral channels. The strong cumulative efects of social media referrals as well as the interdependencies among channels suggest that future research and practices should develop more systematic time-series models to discover the long-term and full impact of online referral channels.
