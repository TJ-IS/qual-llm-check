# Post-Story: Influence of Introducing Story Feature on Social Media Posts

Reza Alibakhshi <sup>a</sup> and Shirish C. Srivastava <sup>b</sup>

<sup>a</sup>Information Systems and Technology Department, IE Business School, IE University, Madrid, Spain; <sup>b</sup>Information Systems and Operations Management Department, HEC Paris, Jouy en Josas Cedex, France

## ABSTRACT

Driven by the need to enhance user trafic on social media (SM) platforms for increasing their advertising revenues, SM platforms are experimenting with new content creation features. However, it is unclear if such initiatives are also beneficial for SM profile owners such as influencers, who are the prime content creators on the SM platforms who use SM posts to build their influence within their network of followers. Our study investigates the efect of introducing one such new SM feature: the “story” on the creation and consumption of SM posts. Leveraging social penetration theory, we hypothesize the influence of introducing story feature on (1) the frequency of SM post creation by profile owners and (2) the extent of follower engagement with SM posts. Employing a quasi-experimental design, we find that the introduction of the story feature reduces the frequency of SM post creation, but the enhanced self-disclosure through the story feature increases follower engagement with the SM posts. However, these efects are moderated by the situating culture of the SM communities: while low-power-distance cultures value profile owners’ selfdisclosure, high-power-distance cultures exhibit a mixed influence. Advancing literature on social penetration theory and SM user engagement, our study demonstrates that new self-disclosive SM content creation features do not necessarily benefit all the concerned stakeholders and that the efectiveness of such features might vary from one community to another. Hence, the intended impact of introducing new SM features needs to be carefully evaluated by SM platforms in a holistic manner.

social media; content creation; user engagement; experiment; social penetration theory; selfdisclosure; culture; diference-in-diference; influencer; follower; platforms

## Introduction

The primary source of revenue for social media (SM) platforms such as Facebook and Instagram are the advertisements viewed by their users. For example, 98 percent of Facebook’s total revenue in 2019, amounting to 20 billion USD, was attributed to the advertisements hosted by the platform [13]. But the revenue generated through the advertisements is dependent on the extent of the user trafic that is drawn to the SM platform. The greater the user engagement with the SM platforms, the higher is the propensity of the users to view the advertised content—resulting in higher revenues for the platforms [4]. Clearly, the SM platform owners are looking at ways and means to enhance user trafic on their platforms. Traditionally, the content produced by the profile owners in the form of “SM posts” has been the prime draw for their followers to visit the SM platforms. However, many of these profile owners are influencers, who play a significant role in increasing the user trafic on SM platforms by attracting their followers to engage with the posted content. For instance, 39 percent of all Instagram profile owners who had more than 15,000 followers in 2019 were influencers and their posts were the primary source of trafic on the platform [11]. Such profile owners aspire to have a large number of followers engaging with their SM posts [25, 46]. However, because SM posts are by-design available on the SM platform for perpetuity, the profile owners such as influencers take significant time and efort to craft their SM posts so as to increase their status within their community of followers [46, 55]. However, this may limit the number of SM posts that the profile owners can create in a given time period. With a view to increase the frequency of content creation for enhancing the platform trafic, many SM platforms have introduced the “story feature” [26], which allows profile owners to create temporary content available for a limited time period.<sup>1</sup> Because the content created in the form of stories is by-design short-lived (available for 24 hours on most platforms), it is assumed that the story feature would encourage profile owners, including influencers, to produce not-so-well-crafted temporary content,<sup>2</sup> which would enhance the user trafic on the SM platforms. Recent statistics show that the introduction of the story feature has indeed increased the user trafic on SM platforms. For example, on Instagram after the introduction of the story feature the average time spent per day (ATSPD) increased by 14 percent for US adults in 2017 compared to 2016 [12, 57]. The same report indicates a 2.5 percent increase for Facebook’s ATSPD in that period [12]. Despite this positive influence in increasing the usage of SM platforms due to the high consumption of such temporary content,<sup>3</sup> it would be theoretically and practically interesting to examine if the story feature is also beneficial for the profile owners, especially influencers—the primary content creators on the SM platforms.

For examining this question, it is imperative to understand the expectations of influencers from the SM platforms. As previously mentioned, influencers on the SM platforms aim to have a large number of followers who regularly engage with their well-crafted SM posts [25]. Higher levels of follower engagement with their SM posts enable such profile owners to gradually build their status as influencers within their community of followers [46, 55]. This idolized influencer status results in significant financial and social benefits for the profile owners [9, 25]. Popular profile owners such as influencers on SM platforms are often solicited by organizations and governments for informational and promotional endorsements through their SM posts [9, 46]. Recent evidence shows that SM posts from influencers have been efective in promoting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), female empowerment, and even for combating anti-vaccination campaigns during the COVID-19 pandemic [44, 54]. Clearly, the more the followers engage with the influencers’ well-crafted SM posts, the more are such profile owners influential in shaping their followers’ attitudes, perceptions, and decisions [25]. Hence, notwithstanding the presence of the “story feature” as an alternate means of communication, the value of the profile owner as an influencer on the SM platform is defined by the extent of follower engagement with their SM posts [25]. Clearly, the story feature provides a quick channel for the influencers to keep in touch with their followers. However, does the story feature also help the influencers in fulfilling their fundamental objective of enhanced follower engagement with their SM posts?

The story feature is designed to provide a continuous touch point for the SM influencers with their followers. While influencers may take significant time for crafting their permanent SM posts, they may hesitate less in sharing their spontaneous life moments through the short-lived story feature, which disappears from their profile within a day of posting it. Another reason for less hesitation from profile owners such as influencers in sharing their imperfect life moments through the story feature is that unlike the SM posts, the followers cannot engage with the short-lived stories by “liking” or “commenting” on them. However, with the introduction of story feature the influencers are impelled to reveal many trivial details of their life such as sharing their daily routine activities (e.g., cooking and exercising), or sharing information beyond their field of expertise (e.g., political views expressed by a fashion influencer), to stay connected with their followers [32]. Further details about the characteristics of the two content creation modes on SM platforms—posts and stories—are provided in the Online Supplemental Appendix 1. We posit that in the context of SM influencers where the relationship between profile owners such as Kourtney Kardashian in the US or Mrunal Panchal in India and their followers is of idolatry and fandom [46, 55], the enhanced self-disclosure by the profile owners through the story feature may influence the logic of the relationship of these influencers with their followers.

From a theoretical perspective, the introduction of the story feature increases the extent of self-disclosure by the influencers with their followers [1, 23]. Instead of viewing the wellcrafted SM posts from the influencers, the followers will now be privy to the imperfect and mundane side of the influencers through the stories they share. Continuous exposure to such imperfect content from the influencers may tend to trivialize their “idol status” for their followers. Despite the enhanced connect through the story feature, the followers may start regarding the influencers as ‘one of them’ rather than someone they would “idolize.” Thus, a simple shift from SM posts to stories to keep in touch with their followers, may tend to change the relationship between influencers and followers from “idolizing” to “socializing” [46, 55]. Considering that influencers strive for enhanced follower engagement with their well-crafted SM posts, the literature does not provide a consensus on whether the impact of self-disclosure through the story feature is helpful in achieving this objective or not. On the one hand, some studies suggest that self-disclosure through SM platforms is beneficial in increasing the engagement within a follower community [32, 39, 58]. On the other hand, some research has highlighted the negative impact of self-disclosure through a loss of status, privacy, and associated long-term risks for the influencers to lose their idolized reputation [35, 51].

Additionally, literature suggests that the impact of “self-disclosure” on the relationship between individuals depends on their cultural orientation [27, 33, 45]. This implies that the cultural orientation of the focal community, such as their power-distance orientation, could alter their preference for self-disclosure in developing and sustaining diferent relationships [36, 45]. Traditionally the SM posts helped the SM influencers build an idolized status among their follower community [46]. However, the story feature, through enhanced selfdisclosure tends to alter the relationship logic between the influencers and their followers, which may be perceived diferently by diferent follower communities depending on their situating cultural orientation. For example, the lessening of the hierarchical gap between influencers and their followers through the story feature induced socialization may be viewed diferently by diferent SM communities around the globe, depending on their preference for a high- or a low-power-distance orientation [14, 27, 33]. In communities where power-distance orientation is high, idolized influencers might be less inclined to selfdisclose their not-so-well-crafted moments and the followers might be less favorable toward such behavior.

In light of the preceding discussion, though the introduction of the story feature is helpful for the SM platform in drawing more user trafic, its eficacy in helping profile owners such as influencers in achieving their key objective of enhanced follower engagement with their SM posts is not well understood. Clearly, the story feature, which has been introduced as a peripheral SM product can influence the production and consumption of the core SM product—the SM posts, which may afect the long-term sustainability of SM platforms. However, despite the importance of understanding the holistic influence of the introduction of the story feature on the production and consumption of the profile owners SM posts, there have been no theoretically driven studies on the subject. Hence, the two research questions that we seek to address are:

RQ1: In the context of SM platforms, what is the impact of the introduction of the “story feature” on (a) the frequency of “SM post” creation, and (b) the follower engagement with the “SM posts”?

RQ2: Does the situating culture of the follower community moderate the influence of introducing the “story feature” on (a) the frequency of “SM post” creation, and (b) the follower engagement with the “SM posts”?

Grounding our discussion in social penetration theory [1] and its proposed selfdisclosure mechanism, we formulate several hypotheses describing the influence of the introduction of peripheral SM product—the story feature on the production and consumption of the core SM product—the SM posts. Additionally, we theorize the divergent mechanisms through which the introduction of the story feature influences the frequency of SM posts creation and follower engagement with the SM posts across diferent cultural preferences. To test our hypotheses, we analyze a dataset of user activity on Instagram comprising more than 31,000 Instagram posts from 57 SM influencers and their networks of followers. Specifically, we perform diference-in-diference (DiD) and diference-indiference-in-diference (DDD) quasi-experimental econometric analyses in which we treat the introduction of the story feature on the Instagram platform as a natural experiment. The findings from our study generally support the proposed set of hypotheses by empirically showing that the introduction of the peripheral story product reduces the frequency of SM post creation by influencers but increases the extent of follower engagement with their SM posts in terms of increased liking and commenting behaviors. Furthermore, our DDD analysis reveals that the power-distance of the situating community on the SM platform moderates the theorized influence. Specifically, we observe that in highpower-distance communities the frequency of SM post creation and follower engagement with SM posts are impacted lesser by the introduction of story feature than in the low-power -distance communities. Together, the results suggest partial and context-dependent eficacy of story feature in helping SM influencers meet their objectives of increased SM post creation and consumption.

Through this study, we make several contributions to both theory and practice. By describing the relationship between profile owners—specifically influencers and their followers through content creation and consumption on SM platforms, we provide theoretical insights into the mechanisms through which novel SM products such as the story feature, may assist or inhibit SM influencers in achieving their objectives. Specifically, through the lens of social penetration theory and the mechanism of self-disclosure [1, 32], we delineate how usage diversification through a peripheral temporary content creation product impacts influencers’ frequency of SM post creation, and their followers’ engagement with SM posts. Our study provides a theoretically grounded understanding about the efectiveness of SM product diversification on the production and consumption of the core SM content which may determine the long-term sustainability of SM platforms. Furthermore, by introducing culture as a moderator of individuals’ sense-making of idolized influencers’ self-disclosure through the newly introduced story feature, we demonstrate the salience of context-driven strategies for designing new SM features.

## Background Literature

## User Engagement in Social Media

Brodie et al. [6] define user engagement as an interactive and co-creative experience of various agents through a focal object. In the SM context, user engagement with SM platforms broadly comprises the two key activities—production of SM content by the profile owners, and the consumption of the produced content by their followers [10]. And as explained in the previous section, the SM posts signify the focal object or the core product on the SM platforms. In our study, we distinguish between the two types of user engagement behaviors: one signifying the creation of well-crafted SM posts by profile owners, and the other indicating the consumption of SM posts by followers through their likes and comments [30].

From the perspective of profile owners specifically influencers, the content creation through SM posts fulfills their objective of idolizing their status within their network of followers [42]. Literature on user engagement with SM platforms suggests that content creation on SM platforms provides an egocentric psychological afordance to the profile owners for self-presentation [30]. Additionally, literature suggests that creation of SM posts by profile owners assists in establishing their influence within the follower community [25, 46, 55]. Contrarily, from the perspective of followers, consumption of SM content fulfills their allocentric psychological afordance related to their need for idolization and selfassociation with the celebrated SM profile owners such as influencers [30, 32]. This allocentric psychological afordance is addressed in prior studies through low- and highefort engagement behaviors of liking and commenting on profile owners’ posts respectively [30]. Therefore, the engagement of the profile owner with the SM platform through the creation of SM posts and the engagement of followers with the profile owners’ posts, together represent an “active” and “reciprocal” relationship built on psychologically divergent motives [10].

Driven by the salience of this relationship for the sustenance of SM platforms [9, 25], IS and marketing literature on user engagement has extensively investigated the modalities for content creation by profile owners such as influencers [25, 46] and the consequent follower engagement with the created content [40, 60]. Online Supplemental Appendix 2 summarizes the key research on user engagement in SM platforms. However, the impact of introducing new peripheral SM products, such as the story feature on the production and consumption of the core SM product—the SM posts—is not yet well understood. Prior studies have mostly examined the profile owner and follower engagement with SM in relation to the SM posts either in terms of followers’ liking and commenting behaviors [40, 56, 60] or the actions by the profile owner’s for enhancing the eficiency of their SM posts [7, 60]. Such an analysis is indeed useful for understanding the role of specific SM content attributes in determining user engagement (e.g., Stieglitz and Dang-Xuan [56], Xu et al. [60]). We posit that with the growing influx of new peripheral SM products such as the story feature, it would be worthwhile and timely to examine their impact on the user engagement with the core SM product—SM posts—which is expected to determine the long-term sustainability of the SM platforms. The introduction of new features such as the story feature may tend to alter and redefine the relationship logic between the profile owners, specifically influencers and their followers through enhanced opportunities for selfdisclosure. This change may influence the continued engagement of the influencers and their followers with the SM platforms. To have a deeper understanding about this impact, we first conceptualize influencer and follower engagement with SM platforms as an ongoing mutual relationship efectuated through the creation and consumption of SM posts. Next, we theorize and examine the influence of the potentially altered relationship between the influencers and their followers through enhanced opportunities for self-disclosure on their engagement with the SM platform.

## Self-Disclosure and Social Penetration Theory

The concept of self-disclosure has been defined as the intentional or unintentional information exchange between individuals and is primarily directed at revealing personal information generally not known to the other communicating parties [1, 59]. This self-disclosive information exchange forms the central mechanism proposed by social penetration theory (SPT) which focuses on the development of interpersonal relationships [1, 59]. According to SPT, as individuals increase the extent of self-disclosure in their communication with one another, their interpersonal relationship becomes deeper, which in turn enhances the closeness in the relationship [1]. Thus, increasing self-disclosure can be a mechanism for increasing the level of intimacy, closeness, and interaction in existing personal relationships [23].<sup>4</sup> Although prior literature has examined the drivers and consequences of selfdisclosure in diferent SM contexts [22, 23, 32] such as increased SM platform usage [58] and online social well-being and support [23], a minimal amount is known about the influence of self-disclosure on the relationship logic between profile owners such as influencers and their followers on the SM platforms. Because the relationship logic between influencers and their followers is generally based on the idol-fan relationship, we posit that enhanced opportunities for self-disclosure through the story feature may alter the relationship logic between the influencer and follower, which in turn would influence their engagement with the SM platforms.

As the relationship between influencers and followers is built upon the status of the influencers within their community of followers [42], whether self-disclosure impacts engagement within their community positively or negatively is still not well-understood. On the one hand, several studies highlight the positive impact of self-disclosure in SM platforms through an enhanced user engagement [32, 39, 43, 58]. For example, in their study of Facebook users, Ledbetter et al. [39] have shown that self-disclosure in SM platforms is directly associated with higher frequency of interactions between the users.

Similarly, Kim and Song’s [32] study on Twitter shows that disclosing work- and life-related information by profile owners can be a predictor of followers’ sense of social presence, which consequently increases followers’ tendency to interact with eminent SM figures. Additionally, several studies have also emphasized on the positive impact of selfdisclosure on follower engagement through the enhanced level of trust between influencers and their followers [22, 23, 39]. On the other hand, some SM research has highlighted the negative impact of self-disclosure on the extent of user engagement [14, 35, 51]. For example, Krämer and Schäwel [35] argue that one of the major drawbacks of selfdisclosure for eminent individuals in an online environment such as SM influencers would be the loss of their privacy, which can potentially hurt their image on SM platforms. Furthermore, because the profile owner’s status among their followers is built upon their idolized persona [46, 55], enhanced self-disclosure would increase the risk for influential profile owners such as influencers losing their idol status, which might adversely impact the follower engagement [14]. Moreover, the unforeseen risks associated with self-disclosure might be realized only in the long term [51], which impedes the critical assessments of the benefits and perils of self-disclosure on SM platforms. Increasing the complexity of the influence of self-disclosure on the relationship between profile owners and followers, literature also suggests that the norms of the community in which this relationship is situated might influence the perception of self-disclosive behaviors [45].

Hence, despite the extensive examination of the self-disclosure in the context of online platforms [45, 53, 62], our understanding about the link between self-disclosure and user engagement, especially in the context of SM influencers and their followers, is still in a nascent stage. Moreover, to the best of our knowledge no prior studies have examined the long-term influence of the changed relationship logic due to self-disclosure on user engagement in SM platforms. Furthermore, because SM communities might difer in terms of their cultural orientation, it would be interesting to examine how cultural stance of the situating community might temper the influence of enhanced self-disclosure through the new SM feature—“story”—on user engagement with SM platforms.

## Culture, Power Distance, and Self-Disclosure

Culture defines the shared values and traditions within a community through which individuals interpret their surroundings, interactions, and beliefs [20, 50]. Culture has been conceptualized in the literature as consisting of diferent dimensions such as power distance, masculinity, long-term orientation, individualism, and uncertainty avoidance [21, 50]. Each of these dimensions of culture characterizes a specific aspect of the collective understanding of individuals within a community, group, team, or nation and is instrumental in shaping individuals’ perceptions, interpretations, and reactions in their relationship with others [8, 27, 29, 36], whether they are online or ofline.

Prior SM studies suggest a significant role of culture in influencing the individual’s online behavior [8, 29, 42]. This is also true for SM users’ perception of self-disclosure in these platforms [36]. Prior studies have identified that some cultural dimensions, such as power distance (or the respect for hierarchy), are central in determining user attitudes regarding self-disclosure [20, 27, 53]. In a study of individuals’ preference for transparent disclosure, Jain and Jain [27] found that individuals’ power-distance orientations significantly determine their tendency toward transparent disclosure in their interactions. Such evidence suggests that cultural values might significantly influence the way individuals perceive and react to new forms of online behaviors, especially in the context of the influencer-follower relationship [33].

Among the various cultural dimensions, it is the power distance that plays a vital role in understanding self-disclosure behaviors in the influencer-follower context. The rationale for this importance is the high reference power of influencers among their followers [42] who regard them as idols [46]. As one of the most widely studied constructs in models of cultural values [27, 33, 53], a society’s power-distance orientation defines the extent to which power is unequally distributed between various entities and strata of that society [21, 27, 50]. In high-power-distance cultures, unequally distributed power between diferent strata of the society is considered legitimate, acceptable, and to some extent desirable [21, 29, 45]. This induces individuals in cultures with a high-power-distance orientation to believe in a diferentiated social hierarchy and tend to maintain this desirable hierarchy [27, 50]. As a result, the diferences in power-distance orientation have been shown to determine the perception and meaning of ubiquitous phenomena such as self-disclosure in diferent communities [27, 33, 45]. This importance of a power-distance orientation resonates in new forms of online behaviors [50], especially if these behaviors are manifested by high-powerstatus social entities, such as SM influencers.

Despite this apparent link, there is limited understanding in the IS literature regarding how the power-distance perceptions of the constituting community may determine individuals’ attitude regarding self-disclosure behavior on SM platforms [53] and its impact for influencers to develop and maintain their relationships with their followers. In other words, a tendency to maintain the power-distance hierarchy moderates the mechanisms through which influencers disclose their life events using new peripheral SM products such as the story feature. This may influence the kind and frequency of their content creation through core SM product, that is, the SM posts. Following a similar logic, the situating cultural preferences of followers may impact their engagement with the SM posts from the profile owners such as influencers.

## Hypotheses Development

To better understand the described theoretical gaps, we set our study in the context of SM influencer profiles and examine the impact of the introduction of the story feature on the influencers’ propensity for SM post creation and followers’ consumption of the SM posts through their likes and comments. In addition, we theorize the diferences in the altered SM post creation and consumption behaviors across low- and high-power-distance cultures through the moderating role of the SM community’s power-distance orientation.

As a peripheral SM product, the story feature provides greater flexibility to profile owners for creating content in addition to the traditional SM posts. However, the use of this new feature by profile owners specifically influencers will tend to diminish their available time and resources for creating and disseminating their well-crafted SM posts. Although the story feature provides a continuous touchpoint for the profile owners between consecutive SM posts, profile owners and especially influencers strive for long-lasting follower engagement [9, 25]. This is possible only through the SM posts that store their well-crafted content for posterity and allow followers to like and comment on them. In contrast, the story feature has a limited accessibility of 24-hours that may not be useful for influencers’ long-term engagement objectives. Nonetheless, the availability of the new story feature may motivate influencers to try it out because creating content through a story can be instrumental in enhancing an influencer’s online presence, albeit for a short period [32].

It may be anticipated that creation of a greater quantity of content by influencers may nurture deeper connectedness between them and their followers and may prospectively increase followers’ engagement with their SM post content [3]. In addition, influencers may create content through stories because they may not want to be viewed as nonexperimenters to their followers. Moreover, some users may prefer stories to SM posts, and influencers will not want to lose out on this segment of current and potential followers. Thus, influencers will be induced to create imperfect and ephemeral content through the peripheral story feature, in addition to their established content creation through SM posts. However, influencers, who professionally create content on SM platforms, have limited time and resources that they can expend for their SM content creation. Following this line of logic, the introduction of the story feature will definitely encourage profile owners such as influencers to devote a part of their limited time and efort to the creation of story content. But this will decrease the extent of time and efort invested in creating SM posts, resulting in a reduced frequency of SM post creation after the introduction of the story feature. Hence, we hypothesize:

Hypothesis 1 (H1): Introduction of the story feature impacts the frequency of profile owners’ content creation through SM posts, with the time interval between a profile owner’s creation of two SM posts increasing significantly after the introduction of the story feature.

The introduction of the story feature influences both profile owners such as influencers and their followers on the SM platform. The adoption of the story feature by influencers drives them to share their imperfect life-moment with their followers. This enhanced spontaneous self-disclosure through the story feature is diferent from their well-crafted “self-presentation” through SM posts and may influence the logic of their relationship with their followers. However, according to SPT [1], increasing self-disclosure can be a mechanism for enhancing the level of intimacy, closeness, and interactions in relationships [23, 43, 59]. As profile owners such as influencers disclose more of their personal information, more of the followers’ attention is directed toward them [32]. This enhanced attention will increase their interactions with the SM users, either through new relationships or by improving the existing relationships on the SM platform [22, 43]. Prior SPT studies have shown that increasing self-disclosure in online relationships has a positive influence on the frequency of interactions between individuals on SM platforms such as Facebook [39]. Furthermore, this positive efect of self-disclosure on the relationship between the profile owners and their followers is intrinsically reciprocal [58, 59]. The more profile owners such as influencers increase their self-disclosure, the higher is their social closeness with their followers.

In addition to the enhanced self-disclosure, the new story feature may be instrumental in increasing the volume of profile owners’ online presence. The story feature is designed for sharing imperfect moments which can be created in larger volumes rather quickly compared to SM posts. Recent evidence suggests that frequent imperfect content sharing by eminent SM profile owners such as influencers is efective in increasing the level of followers’ trust in profile owners [22, 23, 39]. It is expected that the more the profile owners share their imperfect life moments, the more followers would trust them. This enhanced level of trust consequently increases the depth of the relationship between the communicating parties. Additionally, due to the limited-time period display of the story feature, followers may become more attentive to frequent and ephemeral stories of the eminent profile owners they follow as to minimize their “fear of missing out” on new story content. This will result in higher attention to the content from the followed profile owners and may have a positive spillover into the follower’s engagement with the SM posts. In support of this argument, recent evidence suggests that the more time followers are attentive to certain profile owners or influencers, the more the followers will like and comment on the their posts [10].

Accordingly, the reinforcing mechanisms of increased self-disclosure proposed by the SPT and the enhanced trust in the relationships after the introduction of the story feature may increase SM followers’ involvement with specific SM profiles [12, 57]. This will lead to an overall increase in the consumption of the content posted by the profile owners that would manifest through enhanced follower engagement in terms of their likes and comments on the profile owner’s subsequent SM posts. Hence, we hypothesize:

Hypothesis 2 (H2): Introduction of the story feature significantly impacts the level of follower engagement with profile owners’ SM posts, with increased numbers of (a) likes, and (b) comments on a profile owner’s subsequent SM posts.

Notwithstanding the aforementioned arguments, the relationship between SM profile owners specifically influencers and their followers is subject to the cultural orientation of the community in which they are embedded [36]. Specifically, the literature on SPT and the influence of culture on self-disclosure suggests that the situating community’s power-distance orientation may also significantly influence the perception of idolized high-status persona [20, 27, 53]. In high-power-distance cultures, individuals tend to preserve the diferentiated hierarchical structure within the community [29]. In such communities, self-disclosure may be perceived negatively, compared to communities with low-power-distance orientations [45]. Accordingly, in the context of SM platforms in which content creation through well-crafted SM posts serves to build the influencers’ idolized status within their respective communities [42], influencers from a high-powerdistance culture may have less motivation to use the story feature that tends to dilute their exclusive fan status through enhanced self-disclosure [45]. Such influencers will instead desire to maintain the status quo in order to preserve their idolized image within their follower community [33, 46]. Conversely, all profile owners, including influencers, from low-power-distance communities may have a positive proclivity toward selfdisclosure and will tend to use the story feature to bring them closer to their follower community. In fact, self-disclosure through the story feature may provide an opportunity to all the profile owners including influencers from low-power-distance cultures to exhibit their social closeness and similarity with their followers. Consequently, profile owners in low-power-distance communities will have a higher tendency to create ephemeral and not-so-well-crafted content through the story feature compared to their counterparts in high-power-distance communities. Thus, the higher the power distance of the situating culture, the lower the influence that the introduction of the story feature will have on the frequency of content creation through SM posts. Hence, we hypothesize.<sup>.</sup>

Hypothesis 3 (H3): The situating cultural orientation of a profile owner’s follower community moderates the influence of the introduction of the story feature on the frequency of profile owners content creation through SM posts, such that the higher the power distance of the situating culture, the lower the influence of the introduction of the story feature on the time interval between the creation of profile owner’s subsequent SM posts.

Following the aforementioned logic, the impact of the story feature on follower engagement will also be moderated by the situating cultural orientation of the profile owner’s follower community. Use of the story feature tends to increase a profile owner’s selfdisclosure. But a profile owner’s self-disclosure may be viewed diferently in diferent cultures. In the context of SM platforms, profile owners specifically influencers enjoy high reference power and influence over their followers [42, 46]. This idolized status creates a power distance between profile owners and their followers, indicating a hierarchical structure in their community. However, the desirability of this hierarchy largely depends on the power-distance orientation of the followers and the profile owners such as influencers. Followers from a high-power-distance culture would want to idolize and maintain the status diferential between themselves and the SM profile owners specifically influencers, whereas followers from a low-power-distance culture would want to socialize and get closer to all the profile owners including influencers.

Prior evidence suggests that when eminent figures in high-power-distance communities display behaviors that disregard the hierarchical order of the community, the community perceives such behaviors negatively and prefers to maintain the hierarchical diferential [27, 33, 45]. In contrast, in low-power-distance cultures, behaviors that tend to challenge the established power distance will be viewed positively. In the context of our study, the introduction of the story feature clearly alters the existing relationship logic between the profile owners specifically influencers and their followers through enhanced selfdisclosure. Because enhanced self-disclosure may be viewed negatively by high-power distance cultures, it will reduce follower engagement with the profile owner’s SM posts. Hence, we hypothesize:

Hypothesis 4 (H4): The situating cultural orientation of a profile owner’s follower community moderates the influence of the introduction of the story feature on the level of follower engagement, such that the higher the power distance of the situating culture, the lower the influence of the introduction of the story feature on the number of (a) likes, and (b) comments on the profile owner’s subsequent SM posts.

## Methodology

To answer our research questions and test our hypotheses, we examine how the introduction of the story feature impacted the frequency of subsequent creation of SM posts by eminent profile owners (influencers) and the extent of follower engagement with such posts through their likes and comments. In our study, we consider the introduction of the story feature to be an experimental shock on the Instagram platform [26]. Prior studies using natural experiment methodology have used a similar approach to examine the influence of diferent technological-feature-related shocks on the phenomenon of interest [24, 28, 31].

For example, Huang et al. [24] and Kuang et al. [37] utilized the introduction of new technological features of social network integration and an incentivized knowledge sharing system as exogeneous shocks in single-shock DiD experimental settings.

## Experimental Setting

Instagram introduced the story feature on August 2, 2016, enabling profile owners to post time-limited, imperfect-moment content that disappears after 24 hours. The introduction of the new feature serves as an exogenous shock to all users (profile owners and followers) on the Instagram platform, and we expect that this shock will influence profile owners’ frequency of SM posts creation, as well as their followers’ engagement with these posts through likes and comments. In our study, we employ an econometric identification strategy similar to previous empirical studies using a single-shock DiD method for system changes [24, 37]. We further adopt a DDD methodology [7, 18] to test the influence of situating cultural orientation as a contingent factor in our DiD methodology.

To implement the DiD and DDD methods, we first needed to divide the SM profiles of influencers in our dataset into distinct treatment and control groups. This would help us to capture the impact of the shock on the dependent variables and also to separate out the efect of the treatment shock from the unobserved variations in the dependent variables [24, 28, 31]. Because the exogenous shock in our study was specific to the Instagram platform and was simultaneously introduced to all users, we needed to rely on some other factor to segregate the control and treatment groups.

Prior evidence shows that the tenure of SM profile owners including influencers determines their online behaviors [24] and their status within the SM platform [42]. Because we are interested in capturing the influence of imperfect and ephemeral content creation on the established relationship between an influencer profile owner and his/her followers, tenure of SM profile as the proxy of this established idolized relationship [46] would act as a suitable segregation factor. Specifically, we expect that the introduction of the story feature would significantly impact the profile owners with longer tenure on the platform (before the shock) for two reasons. First, since SM influencers build their image through active content creation in the online environment [42, 46], the influencer profile owners with a longer SM tenure would be viewed by their followers as established “idols” [46, 55]. Hence, because the story feature brings the imperfect social side of influencer profile owners to the forefront, this group of profile owners would be influenced more by the transformed relationship between them and their followers. Second, consistent with the literature on path dependency theory [52], influencer profile owners with a longer SM tenure would have been creating their content primarily through SM posts in the past [52]. The introduction of the story feature as a new channel of content creation would impact their content creation frequency significantly more than those of the recently added profile owners. Accordingly, we chose the tenure of an influencer profile owner on the SM platform as the criterion for assigning profiles to the treatment or control group.

Based on the preceding logic, we identified two subgroups of profiles in our dataset: (1) profiles with long SM tenure prior to the introduction of the story feature (a SM tenure of more than 6 months before the shock), and (2) profiles with shorter SM tenure prior to the introduction of the story feature (a SM tenure of lesser than 2 months before the shock). Prior evidence from the social psychology literature suggests 2 and 6 months as lower and upper bounds for new behavior formation and reaching complete automaticity in new behaviors [38]. These thresholds support our choice of the SM tenure bounds for the treatment and control groups. Accordingly, we assigned 14 profiles with a SM tenure longer than 6 months to the treatment group (7,072 observations), and 43 profiles with a SM tenure less than 2 months to the control group (24,715 observations).

The observation period of our experimental setting is from June 2016 (2 months before the shock) to December 2018 (29 months after the shock) for our main analysis (Figure 1). This 31-months of observation period helped us to ensure that the efect of introducing the story feature has lasted throughout the experimental setting in the post-treatment period (from August 2016 to December 2018) which also accounts for the long-term efects of selfdisclosure in SM platforms [51]. In addition, it helped us account for diferent adoption time periods of the story feature by diferent profile owners. Figure 1 depicts the schema of our experimental setting comprising the calendar timeline of our study, our experimental shock, treatment, and control group identification, as well as the observation periods for the treatment and control groups.

## Data and Measurement

In order to test our hypotheses, we collected data from verified public profiles of Instagram’s influencers that ensured the authenticity (real person), uniqueness (one account per person), publicity (accessibility by all users on the platform), completeness (account info such as residency and contact info), and also notability (highly searched for) of the profiles in our study. To increase the reliability of our data collection procedure, we followed prior empirical investigations in the context of Instagram [34, 49] and collected data through Instagram’s API and its public URLs. Accordingly, we collected content created on Instagram comprising images, comments, and profiles’ metadata. All of the collected data was cleaned, anonymized, and aggregated for our period of research interest (Figure 1; June 2016 to December 2018), which resulted in the final data set for 57 public profiles comprising more than 31,000 Instagram posts. After completion of our data collection procedure, we prepared extensive dependent, independent, and control variables for a robust set of econometric analyses in our study. These variables are detailed in the following subsections.

## Dependent Variables

In order to test the influence of the introduction of the story feature on influencer profile owners’ frequency of content creation through SM posts, we defined Days Since Last Post as our first dependent variable. To test H1 and H3, Days Since Last Post captures the number of days between two consecutive posts in each profile. This variable represents profile owners’ frequency of content creation as the time interval between two consecutive SM posts on a profile. To measure this variable for each Instagram post, we used the timestamp of that post and calculated the number of days that had passed since the preceding post.

We defined Likes Count and Comments Count as the next two dependent variables, which capture the influence of introduction of the story feature on the extent of followers engagement with SM posts. These variables measure low- and high-efort engagement of the followers with SM posts [30]. For testing H2a, H2b, H4a, and H4b, the extent of followers’ engagement was operationalized through the number of likes and the number of comments that each SM post received [10, 40, 60]. These variables respectively measure low- and highefort engagement behaviors in our analysis [30]. Likes Count and Comments Count were both captured through our initial data collection for a profile’s Instagram posts.

## Independent Variables

To capture the efect of introducing the story feature on the frequency of influencer profile owners’ content creation through SM posts and the extent of followers’ engagement with them, we defined four independent variables in our experimental setting. Specifically, we defined AftStr dummy variable that captures the time trend variation in both the treatment and control groups before and after the shock. The value of AftStr is 1 if a SM post was created after the introduction of the story feature and 0 otherwise. We also defined Treated dummy variable to identify the posts that were created by the control and treatment groups. The value of Treated is 1 for posts in the treatment group and 0 for posts in the control group. Through this variable, we were able to measure the uncontrolled diference between the treatment and control groups. We further calculated a third independent variable as the interaction term between the AftStr and Treated dummy variables. The DiD variable measures the diference between the treatment and control groups’ responses to the treatment efect and captures the true efect of the story feature on the treatment group.

Finally, in order to test the influence of the power-distance orientation of the situating culture on the profile owners’ frequency of content creation and the extent of their followers’ engagement, we defined Power Distance Index (PDI) as our fourth independent variable. To operationalize this variable, we followed prior empirical studies in IS [48] and used the power-distance index for the profile owner according to his/her residency [20]. Specifically, we collected the residency data for each influencer profile owner from his/her verified profile’s bio descriptions and assigned Hofstede’s [21] power-distance index of his/ her country/nationality to the PDI variable. Since we have only collected data from the verified influencers’ profiles on Instagram, this residency information was a valid proxy for identifying the power distance orientation of the profile owners. Consistent with the principle of network homophily and the extant literature on how cultural and personal similarities in a social network drive tie formation and followership in the network [41, 47], we further assigned the followers of each influencer profile owner to the same cultural backgrounds. This means that an influencer on Instagram and his/her followers belong to the same community in which they share close power-distance orientation. Performing community detection through network analysis of our dataset [17] ensured us of the validity of this homophily assumption in our study and the operationalization of the PDI variable (Online Supplemental Appendix 4).

## Control Variables

Collecting data from Instagram provided us with rich and detailed granular data on both content and profiles, which allowed the introduction of a rich set of controls. Hence, we identified two subsets of controls for our main analysis: (1) content characteristics and (2) influencer profile owner/profile characteristics.

Content Characteristics. In the context of user engagement in SM platforms, prior literature highlights the influence of content characteristics on the extent of followers’ engagement with SM posts [40, 56]. To disentangle the influence of content characteristics from our treatment efect, we defined several control variables at the level of SM posts. First, we controlled for the efect of the content sentiment on follower engagement [56]. We defined Image Sentiment and Caption Sentiment variables as the overall sentiment between -1 and +1, which respectively indicate 100 percent negativity and 100 percent positivity of the images and texts in Instagram posts. To operationalize these variables, we calculated the image sentiment through facial expression detection and the caption sentiment through sentiment classification techniques [56]. Furthermore, the efect of the caption sentiment on follower engagement is conditional on the followers reading the caption. To control for such a readability efect, we measured a Number of Words in Caption variable that indicates the length of the caption. Prior literature suggests that the length of textual content in SM posts significantly influences follower engagement with each post [40]. We controlled for this efect through the Number of Words in Caption variable. Furthermore, to control for the availability of each post over time, we defined an Age of Post variable. This control variable indicates how many days before the time of data collection each post on a profile was created. This variable represents how long a post was available for followers to engage with it. The longer a post is available to followers, the more opportunity they have to engage with it. We controlled for this efect through the Age of Post variable. Finally, since all the posts included in our final analysis have human faces in their visual content, following prior literature on the influence of gender on user engagement in SM, we controlled for Gender, defined as a dummy variable that is equal to 1 if the subject of the image is a male and 0 if the subject is a female [19]. Lastly, since our data collection was performed at a single point in time, we controlled for the unobserved variations in the creation and consumption of content in our dataset through monthly fixed-efect for the various calendar months. This ensured alleviating the potential uncontrollable variance that might be externally influencing the creation and consumption of SM posts.

Influencer Profile Owner/Profile Characteristics. In the context of SM influencers, the popularity of profile owners or their profile significantly influences the extent of the content they generate and also the amount of follower engagement [25]. The popularity of a profile can be measured through the number of its followers. However, since we collected data at a single point of time, we needed to control for the size of the followership (the profile’s popularity) at the time of post creation for each post in each profile. Therefore, we defined a Normalized Number of Followers variable that captures the number of followers that an Instagram profile had at the time of the creation of each post. This control variable enables us to control for the efect of the growth in followers on the engagement level for each post and the popularity of the profile owner at the time of post creation. In order to calculate this measure, we first collected the number of followers and the age of each profile at the time of data collection. Then we measured the network growth pattern described by Barabási and Albert [5] and calculated the number of followers at the time of each SM post for each profile. We considered the followers’ growth pattern for each profile as a complex network with a scale-free power-law distribution [5]. We also controlled for the possible confounding influences of content creation styles and/or industry-related content types among the divergent influencers in our study by introducing profile fixed-efects in our econometric specifications. This would ensure the generalizability of our observed efects for influencer profile owners in various sectors (e.g., beauty, travel, fashion).

We tested for multicollinearity among these variables using Leamer’s method [15], which indicated that there was no significant multicollinearity in our dataset.

## Econometric Identification

Based on the previously described natural experiment setup, we employed DiD and DDD estimations with negative binomial regression analysis for our dependent variables, which are all count variables [19, 46]. The DiD and DDD methods are suitable for this study as they try to provide a causal relationship by mimicking an experimental design [24, 61]. Prior studies, such as Huang et al. [24], have shown that the DiD estimation approach is efective for natural experiment settings where it is not possible to implement an experimental manipulation. Hence, the DiD is an appropriate approach for establishing causal relationships between the introduction of the story feature and the creation and consumption of SM posts. In order to ensure that DiD was suitable for our study, we followed Khern-am-nuai et al. [31] and tested the parallel trend assumption in the DiD method using the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test of a stationary trend between the treatment and control groups. The results support the same stationary pattern in our treatment and control groups (Online Supplemental Appendix 6). Furthermore, following prior DiD studies [24, 37], we conducted additional tests of parallel trend assumption through the relative time model proposed by Greenwood and Wattal [16]. The results of this test also supported the stationarity pattern of influencer profile owners’ content creation and followers’ engagement in both treatment and control groups (Online Supplemental Appendix 6).

H4a and H4b predict that the mechanism through which the introduction of the story feature influences followers’ liking and commenting engagement with posts is moderated by the situating power-distance orientation. Our results in Table 3 partially support these predictions and provide support for H4b. The results of Model 2 and Model 3 in Table 3 show a positive and significant DDD coeficient for the Likes Count $( \beta _ { 1 3 } = 0 . 0 1 6 , \mathrm { p } < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ and a negative and significant DDD coeficient for the Comments Count $( \beta _ { 1 3 } = - 0 . 0 1 0 , \mathrm { p } <$ 0.001).

The decrease in the positive influence of the introduction of the story feature on the extent of followers’ engagement with profile owners’ posts as the PDI increases indicates that followers in the high-power-distance community perceive profile owners’ selfdisclosure unfavorably and decrease their high-efort engagement behavior of commenting. This behavior is diferent from that displayed in the low-power-distance communities. However, contrary to our expectation, we did not observe this efect for low-efort engagement behavior of liking as H4a predicted. An alternative explanation for this observation can be attributed to the diference between the nature of liking and commenting behaviors [1, 30]. Liking behavior requires less of cognitive efort by the follower compared to commenting behavior which entails deeper cognitive efort. Hence, liking as an allocentric low-efort engagement behavior signifies a lower depth in a relationship compared to highefort commenting behavior which signifies deeper involvement with the profile owner [30]. Therefore, it is possible that the unfavourability of self-disclosure by an idolized persona in a community reduces the engagement behavior that has higher relationship depth (commenting) rather than the one with lower depth (liking). In support of this interesting finding, comparing the results in our DiD analysis (Table 2) shows that the positive influence of self-disclosure on extending the relationship between followers and profile owners is higher for the number of comments $( \beta _ { 9 } = 0 . 4 5 8 , \mathrm { p } < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ than the number of likes $( \beta _ { 9 } = 0 . 1 4 5 , \ \mathrm { p } < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ . The significant diference between these two coeficients was also supported through the Wald test of Model 2 and Model 3 in Table 2 as seemingly unrelated regressions (Comments Count versus Likes Count: $\chi ^ { 2 } = 1 4 . 7 8 3 , \mathrm { p } < 0 . 0 0 1 )$ [24].

Together, the results for H3, H4a, and H4b reveal how the relationship between influencer SM profile owners and their followers are influenced by the power distance of the community in which they are situated [36], indicating a significant impact of culture on the usage of the story feature and its impact on influencer-follower relationship through the production and consumption of the SM posts.

## Robustness Tests

To be confident about our findings, we performed several robustness tests. First, we performed Propensity Score Matching between observations in our treatment and control groups [31, 37]. The results of our propensity score matching test using the Full Matching method (Online Supplemental Appendix 7) provided further support for the coeficients of AftStr, Treated, and DiD in our main regression analysis. However, in order to increase the confidence in our matching algorithm, we adopted the Look-Ahead Propensity Score

Matching [2] to account for the unobserved confounding variables in our matching technique (Online Supplemental Appendix 8). To this end, we relied on technology adoption behavior of profile owners in our dataset as the indicator of their time-invariant unobserved characteristics [2, 28]. Because the adoption time of the story feature was not available in our dataset, we accounted for the technology adoption behavior of profile owners through another feature called “Story Highlights” in Instagram. On December 5, 2017, Instagram announced this new feature that enabled profile owners to archive and highlight their stories on their profile. Accounting for the adoption of this feature by profile owners in our study, we found consistent results with our main findings by comparing early-adopters of this feature in the treatment group versus early-adopters and late-adopters of the same feature in the control group.

To further account for the validity of results in our DDD methodology, we performed a subgroup analysis of the low- and high-power-distance communities with 50 as the cutof value of PDI for low- and high-power-distance subgroups [20, 21]. This subgroup analysis provided results similar to those for our DDD analysis (Online Supplemental Appendix 9). We also used Mean, Median, as well as the $2 5 ^ { \mathrm { t h } } / 7 5 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ percentiles of PDI in our dataset as cutof values for low- and high-power-distance communities, which yielded similar results. These additional tests further confirm the influence of the power-distance orientation of the users communities on their proclivity for and perception of self-disclosure through the story feature.

To account for the “early efect” of the story feature, we performed three subgroup robustness analyses. For this we omitted a one-, three-, and six-month period of observations after the exogenous shock and performed the main analysis. All three tests provided results similar to our overall findings (Online Supplemental Appendix 10). Furthermore, as described earlier, during our observation period (June 2016 to December 2018) on December 5, 2017, Instagram announced a new feature called Story Highlights that enabled profile owners to archive and highlight their stories. Because this new feature could have caused a change in the ephemeral nature of the story product and thus the proposed mechanism in our study through enabling the possibility of resharing a story or using its content in the forthcoming posts, we performed two robustness tests to alleviate these concerns. First, we examined visual content creation in all profiles in our study for the threemonth timeframe after the introduction of the Story Highlights feature. We did not observe any reuse of story content through SM posts or resharing them by the profile owners in our dataset. Second, we trimmed our original dataset by omitting observations after the Story Highlights feature and found consistent results to our main findings by performing the main DiD analysis (Online Supplemental Appendix 11). These tests provide further support to the proposed mechanisms hypothesized in this study (Online Supplemental Appendix 12).

## Limitations and Future Research Directions

Our research has certain limitations that can perhaps guide future research on this important subject. First, due to the nature of our dataset and the fact that shared stories disappear after 24 hours on Instagram, we were unable to account for the intensity of the story feature usage among Instagram profile owners. The unavailability of the story feature usage in our dataset limits our understanding of the profile owners’ content creation behaviors. Future research can adopt a daily data extraction for a similar context to capture the extent of content creation and consumption through the story feature. Second, because the introduction of the experimental shock is unique to the Instagram platform, the assignment of the diferent profiles to the control and treatment groups was based on their SM tenure on the Instagram platform. Future research can perhaps investigate the impact of the introduction of new features on SM platforms in a controlled experimental setting. Third, our context of study encompasses diverse unstructured data (image content) which are caused by unobserved characteristics of profile owners. This imposes certain limits on our ability to systematically control the efect of content characteristics in our study. Nonetheless, we have tried to alleviate such limitations by employing rigorous methodological means such as incorporating profile-specific fixed-efects.

## Implications

With a view to increase their advertisement revenue, SM platforms such as Facebook and Instagram are experimenting with new peripheral SM features directed at enhancing user trafic [4]. One such peripheral feature introduced by SM platforms is the story feature that allows profile owners to create and share temporary content for a limited period with their followers. Evidence from Facebook and Instagram shows that the introduction of the story feature has indeed been instrumental in increasing user trafic on SM platforms [12, 57]. However, it is unclear if the story feature has also been successful in fulfilling the objectives of the primary SM content creators, namely the influencer profile owners. Such profile owners are interested in increasing the follower engagement with their posts because enhanced follower engagement establishes their influence leading to multifarious social and economic benefits [42, 46]. Thus, examining the significance of the newly introduced story feature for the SM influencers is important for ensuring the long-term sustainability of the SM platforms. Although the financial viability of the SM platforms depends on the advertisement revenues, the long-term sustainability of SM platforms depends on them satisfying the objectives of the primary content creators. Accordingly, our research sought to examine if the introduction of the story feature helps SM influencers in realizing their objectives of (a) increased SM post creation, and (b) increased follower engagement. Additionally, because SM platforms are available in diferent parts of the world, we also examined if the story feature is universally beneficial for influencers from diferent cultures.

## Implications for Theory

First, our study contributes to the extant literature on user engagement in SM platforms from the standpoint of content creation by influencer profile owners, and its consumption by their followers. Building on prior SM literature examining the role of content creation and consumption in fulfilling users’ needs for status building and self-association [30, 42], our study ofers a fresh theoretical understanding on the relationship between idolized SM profile owners and their followers [46]. Despite the extant contribution of prior studies to the literature on user engagement in SM platforms through various content-centric studies [19, 40, 46, 56], our research views profile owners’ content creation and followers’ content consumption through SM posts as a means for relationship-building between these SM entities. Thus, we provide a deeper theoretical understanding of how a peripheral SM product, namely the story feature, can enhance the depth of the relationship between influencer profile owners and their followers—leading to enhanced engagement through the core SM product—the posts. By delineating the content creation and consumption through the SM posts as a mutually serving relationship-building mechanism, our study ofers deeper insights into how SM oferings can reshape the interactions between profile owners, specifically influencers, and their followers.

Second, our study complements prior studies on SPT and its proposed self-disclosure mechanism in the context of SM platforms [23, 53, 58]. We provide empirical evidence on how the creation and consumption of self-disclosive content through the story feature can alter the idolized relationship between influencers and their followers [46]. By diferentiating between ‘idolization’ and ‘socialization’ of SM influencers, our findings explain how self-disclosure on SM platforms increases closeness in online relationships thereby enhancing follower engagement. Such findings resonate with prior studies such as Kim and Song [32] and Lin and Utz [43]. Through our quasi-experimental analysis we contribute to the SPT and self-disclosure literature on SM platforms by examining the plausible long-term risks of self-disclosure by high-status SM profile owners [35, 51]. In addition, by characterizing liking and commenting as low- and high-efort follower engagement behaviors on SM platforms, we show that self-disclosure influences low- and high-efort engagement behaviors diferently — the high-efort commenting being impacted more by influencer’s selfdisclosure. Our findings thus enhance the findings from prior studies on SM platforms [40, 56, 60] by describing the key role of self-disclosure in driving SM engagement behaviors [32, 43, 53].

Third, our work contributes to the literature on the cultural aspects of online behaviors and SPT [33, 45, 53] by incorporating power-distance as a moderator of the impact of selfdisclosure on content creation and consumption in SM platforms. Addressing the lack of understanding of the role of culture in studies such as Kim and Song [32], we provide empirical evidence on how social hierarchies are determinants of self-disclosive behaviors in the context of SM influencers. Our study indicates that for high-power-distance communities, the introduction of the peripheral story product has a lower influence on the content creation and consumption through well-crafted SM posts. In contrast, for lowpower-distance cultures, the introduction of the story feature results in more changes in the SM posts creation and consumption behaviors. This suggests that the introduction of peripheral products on SM platforms might not be uniformly favored by all communities, thereby limiting their applicability in certain contexts. Our analyses show that the shift from idolization to socialization brought about by the introduction of the story feature is viewed more favorably by low-power-distance cultures. Socializing and imperfect-moment sharing in communities with high-power-distance orientations do not necessarily increase the depth of relationships between two entities with a diferential power reference as they would in communities with low-power-distance orientations. In communities characterized by low power distance, users appreciate the closer interpersonal relationship with idolized figures and perceive self-disclosure through the story feature positively. Conversely, and in line with studies such as Gibson et al. [14], users in communities with high-powerdistance orientations may not perceive this shift favorably and will prefer to maintain the status quo in terms of their interactional hierarchy. Building on prior IS studies [33, 45, 53], these findings further extend our understanding of the relationship between culture and self-disclosure (e.g., augmenting the roles of individualism and uncertainty avoidance proposed by Krasnova et al. [36]) by incorporating the power-distance of the situating community.

## Implications for Practice

Our study also ofers several valuable practical implications. First, our results draw the attention of platform owners to the significance of product enhancement for content creation and consumption that can further improve the extent of user trafic on SM platforms. Given the increased competition among SM platforms [4, 26], our study emphasizes the need to invest in innovative content creation features that can enhance the extent and depth of relationship between the various stakeholders. However, it is necessary for the platform owners to examine the significance of product enhancements against the prevalent logic of content creation and consumption between the various interacting parties. Although prior evidence suggests that the implementation of new peripheral products can be highly rewarding for SM platform owners [12, 57], they need to recognize that such benefits are partial and context-dependent. The findings from our study show that the implementation of innovative features can introduce certain unintended consequences within the already-established relationships on SM platforms. For example, incorporating self-disclosure in idolized relationships between influencers and their followers might not be viewed favorably in certain contexts [46]. Our study shows that the cultural and societal diferences, favoring distant or close interactions among the SM users, pose limits on the universal benefits of product enhancements on SM platforms. SM platform designers and innovators need to understand that despite the global outreach of SM platforms, the local context and cultural nuances need to be infused into their innovative designs.

Second, our study addresses the necessity of evaluating product enhancement in SM platforms with respect to the objectives of other stakeholders within these platforms. For example, the needs of the profile owners such as influencers, who are the prime contentproducing stakeholders on the SM platforms, should be explicitly considered while designing new features on SM platforms. Introduction of new peripheral features (such as the SM story) may drive the attention of the SM influencers away from the highly rewarding core feature (the SM post). Such a shift may adversely impact profile owners’ influence among their followers afecting the long-term substantiality of such SM platforms. Our results shed light on the double-edged influence of product enhancements on the SM platforms, which needs to be carefully considered when designing and implementing such products. In our study we observe that story feature only partially benefits profile owners. Although there is an increased follower engagement, the frequency of SM post creation comes down. Additionally, such partial benefits are also constrained by the cultural preferences of the follower communities. Thus, despite the overall efectiveness of product enhancement (the story feature) on SM platforms, not all stakeholders may benefit from the altered relationships. Hence there is a need to holistically consider the contextual nuances when implementing product enhancements on SM platforms to ensure their sustainability.

Third, our results call influencers’ attention to the importance of understanding and developing the depth of their relationships with their follower networks. Through enhanced self-disclosure, profile owners such as SM influencers can benefit from sharing their perfect and imperfect moments with their followers. The shift from being idolized to socializing can enhance influencers’ image as being accessible, which can help in fostering deeper relationships with their followers. However, our results indicate that this shift may not have a universally positive influence. The cultural expectations of the SM community may temper the desirability of influencers’ closer relationships with their followers. Based on the results of our research, we observe that the expectations of relationships between influencers and their followers in high-power-distance cultures are significantly diferent from low-power-distance cultures. This has important implications for influencer profile owners, who need to understand how to use new, innovative SM products ofered by platforms to their advantage, keeping in mind the cultural nuances and other contextual idiosyncrasies surrounding their followers. By considering that diferent communities have diferent preferences for close or distant relationships, profile owners should align their content creation strategies to the cultural preferences of their target communities.

## Conclusions

Advancements in technology provide numerous possibilities for SM product enhancements, but whether such enhancements help in achieving the desired objectives for all the concerned SM stakeholders depends on a host of contextual factors. It is important to carefully consider all such factors for the long-term sustainability of SM platforms. In this study, we examine the impact of one such product enhancement: the introduction of the story feature which was aimed at increasing user trafic on SM platforms. However, does the introduction of the story feature also benefit the primary content creators on the SM platforms, namely the influencers? Traditionally, such profile owners have been using SM posts for establishing and maintaining the profile owners’ influence among their network of followers. Situating our arguments in the SPT, we examine how the introduction of the story feature afects the frequency of SM post creation by influencer profile owners, and the extent of follower engagement with the created SM posts. Through our quasi-experimental analysis, we conclude that the introduction of the story feature partially benefited influencer profile owners by increasing follower engagement with their SM posts. However, we also found that the story feature reduced the frequency of content creation through SM posts. Our analysis further reveals that the partial benefits of introducing the story feature for influencer profile owners were dependent on the cultural orientation of their follower community. While low-power-distance cultures valued profile owners’ self-disclosure through the story feature, high-power-distance cultures exhibited a mixed influence. Our results reiterate the need for considering the benefits for all the stakeholders associated with the introduction of technological enhancements on multi-sided SM platforms. Our results also highlight the complex interwoven nature of technological enhancements on SM platforms, which should incorporate contextual aspects—cultural, social, and societal—into the design of their new product oferings.
