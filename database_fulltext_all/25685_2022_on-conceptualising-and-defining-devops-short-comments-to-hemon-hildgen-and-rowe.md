# On conceptualising and defining DevOps: short comments to Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe

Michael Gall and Federico Pigni

Grenoble Ecole de Management, France

ARTICLE HISTORY Received 9 January 2022; Accepted 22 May 2022 KEYWORDS DevOps

We would like to thank the commentators, Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe, for conducting such a thorough review of our paper and for laying the foundation for an engaging discussion. It is gratifying to observe that our work has sparked a debate around DevOps, a very critical but often neglected topic.

At the core of their commentary, Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe challenge the relevance of our review and our assertion that both Information Systems (IS) and Software Engineering (SE) literature fail to convey what DevOps is. This is an interesting dispute as it inadvertently challenges not only our work but also the work of several other authors whose studies highlight the lack of a clear conceptualisation of DevOps. For example, Stahl et al. state that “ . . . DevOps is notorious for its ambiguity and lack of established definitions . . . “ (Stahl et al., 2017, p. 1). In addition, Ghantous and Gill emphasize that “organizations are showing significant interest in DevOps, however, they lack clarity and understanding of DevOps” (Ghantous & Gill, 2017, p. 7). These, and other authors, highlighted the lack of a clear conceptualisation of DevOps and its impact on organisations. This notion also resonates with the experience of one of us, who is still a practitioner in a top 50 global company. During the DevOps transition of his team, and also within multiple parts of the organisation, he made the observation that the inconsistencies and fragmentation of DevOps research were making it dificult to implement DevOps within the organisation. The author collaborated with multiple senior leaders within the company to identify, within existing knowledge, a model that can be applied throughout the DevOps transition. This would provide the teams with clear guidance, yet neither he nor the other leaders managed to identify an academic source that met their needs. This demonstrates the significance of our work and the need for a clear DevOps paradigm. As mentioned in the research agenda, we are currently engaged in a research project to further evaluate the conceptual model and demonstrate how it satisfies the practical needs of the organisation. The initial results clearly show that the application of our model provided significant direction and assistance to the teams, allowing them to adopt DevOps eficiently and quickly. Our work on DevOps is an excellent example of demonstrating the utility of IS research to practice.

Due to the fragmentation of DevOps knowledge, it is not surprising to find some parts that are contrary to some of the analyses presented in our work. In fact, our decision to exclude industry sources from the review was considered during the review process. Consequently, all reviewers concurred that restricting the review to academic sources would ensure a representative perspective on the state of DevOps knowledge among the IS academic community. This provides the opportunity for future research to compare our findings – which are more representative of the academic viewpoint – with those that are emerging from the field, eventually expanding the model with conceptual components that are only found in industrial sources. This would represent an additional and valuable contribution of IS and SD research to the field.

The research agenda outlined in our work is emphasised and reinforced by Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe’s comment that “the framework does not establish relationships, or loops between the diferent constituent elements as well as between each of the dimensions,“ which is absolutely true. This is also the reason why we explicitly determine this fact as a potential area for future research. In our research agenda item (2), we state: “Understanding the relationships between the diferent conceptual elements and the factors that influence them would be critical to further enhance the conceptualization of DevOps”. The commentary of Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe provides an additional point of view for those wishing to apply or advance the current state of knowledge in an area that is in transition. This is also consistent with the research agenda that we highlighted in our work.

To conclude, we would like to express our gratitude to Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe for reafirming our research agenda and for their insightful and valuable discussion points. Our conceptual model lays the foundation for further research on sparking the DevOps debate. We would welcome an opportunity to collaborate with Hemon-Hildgen and Rowe on future research projects in order to collectively advance our model and to complement it with the relationships between the various conceptual elements.
