# Procuring services through IT crowdsourcing: The role of IT administration and IT <sup>fl</sup>exibility

Journal of Information Technology 2024, Vol. 39(2) 288–316 © Association for Information Technology Trust 2024 Article reuse guidelines: sagepub.com/journals-permissions DOI: 10.1177/0268396223|225469 Journals.sagepub.com/jinf S Sage

Joseph Taylor<sup>1</sup>, KD Joshi<sup>2</sup>, Terence Saldanha<sup>3</sup> and Arvin Sahaym<sup>4</sup>

## Abstract

Digital platforms provide organizations with new ways to conduct sourcing activities. Crowdsourcing has emerged as one potential method of sourcing various kinds of information technology (IT) services. Notwithstanding the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of IT crowdsourcing services (ITCSs), there is a lack of clarity about why some <sup>fi</sup>rms procure services through ITCS, whereas others do not. Drawing on the organizational capabilities perspective, we hypothesize that in-house IT administration capability and IT <sup>fl</sup>exibility jointly enhance a <sup>fi</sup>rm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, which, in turn, leads to higher ITCS Service Procurement. We also posit that ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediates the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Service Procurement. Our empirical analysis of survey data from 308 <sup>fi</sup>rms in the United States supports our hypotheses. Our results suggest that <sup>fi</sup>rms that develop IT administrative capabilities and IT <sup>fl</sup>exibility assess ITCS platform characteristics as more suitable for ITCS-related activities. ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, in turn, drives <sup>fi</sup>rms to procure operational services (e.g., setting up automatic payments) and strategic services (e.g., project management) from the loose con<sup>fi</sup>guration of workers in the gig economy. Our results contribute to theory by positing and testing a theoretical model that expands the theoretical base used to explain ITCS sourcing. We discuss the implications for the business value of IT platforms, organizational structure, and capabilities in regard to future sourcing strategies.

## Keywords

Dynamic capabilities, information technology capabilities, information technology administration, information technology <sup>fl</sup>exibility, crowdsourcing, leveraging competence

## Introduction

Emerging technologies and work practices are transforming how organizations participate in information technology (IT) sourcing. While hardware and software advances automate and transform many repetitive non-cognitive tasks that have often been outsourced (Brynjolfsson and McAfee, 2011), the growth of data and platform-enabled work has led to a surge in innovative business models (Wiener et al., 2020b). New business models in sourcing have enabled the development of the “gig” economy, exemplified by organizations contracting temporary workers who are not affiliated with an organization but work as independent contractors on short-term tasks, “gigs,” advertised by companies through online platforms. The prevalence of gig workers is rapidly transforming labor markets and giving rise to the “gig” economy (Leimeister et al., 2009; MacDonald and Giazitzoglu, 2019; Taylor and Joshi, 2019).

As labor markets change, a large amount of digital work is increasingly completed by gig workers enabled by online platforms, a phenomenon called crowdsourcing. Crowdsourcing platforms offer varying levels of support and functionalities by which operational IT tasks, such as coding and application testing, can be outsourced (Kaganer et al., 2013). Such microsourcing of IT services, commonly referred to as IT crowdsourcing (ITCS) (Taylor and Joshi, 2019), can generate business value for firms.

Information technology outsourcing has evolved from traditional outsourcing of IT services, to cloud computing and platform ecosystems, to process automation (Dibbern and Hirschheim, 2020). In this digital environment and “gig” economy, sourcing scholars are increasingly examining how firms change the makeup of their sourcing portfolio to leverage the economic and societal benefits it offers. Although utilizing nontraditional sourcing services is more complex and requires diverse capabilities and structures (Sabherwal, 2020), emerging modes of IT outsourcing such as crowdsourcing provide an opportunity to reevaluate the theoretical lenses through which sourcing relationships can be examined. Historically, IT sourcing has largely been examined and explained using Transaction Cost Economics (TCE), Resource Dependency (RD), and Social Exchange theories (SET) (Lee et al., 2019). These classical theoretical perspectives have been effective in examining the strategic alignment and sourcing efficacy of firms having traditional hierarchical structures from the lens of transaction cost, opportunism, interdependence, and exchange-based relational contracts (see Table 1 for details). However, with the evolution in firm structure, as emerging sourcing practices lead to more self-serving, digitally mediated procurement (Taylor and Joshi, 2019), classical theories such as TCE and SET may not fully explain the relationships between firms that procure ITCS services and individuals (often via crowdsourcing platforms) who engage in the gig economy, nor can such theories adequately explain the competencies that enable effective procurement (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020a). While some scholars (Karimi-Alaghehband and Rivard, 2019) have used capabilities-related theories as a means of examining IS sourcing, we propose that in addressing sourcing activities that occur in emerging gig economy environments, organizational capabilities can serve a critical role in interpreting procurement activities.

In this study, we argue that the organizational<sup>1</sup> capabilities lens augments the theoretical perspectives by describing how ITCS service procurement takes place for the firms enabled with certain structures and capabilities (Bharadwaj, 2000; Bhatt et al., 2005; Byrd and Turner, 2000; Garicano and Wu, 2012; Mishra and Agarwal, 2010). ITCS Service Procurement refers to the extent to which a focal firm (e.g., Netflix) procures ITCS services (e.g., project management and application testing) from ITCS platforms (e.g., Upwork). While research on crowdwork is increasing (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020), we propose the use of additional theoretical lenses plays a key role in developing our understanding of emerging sourcing practices. Many emerging sourcing practices such as cloud computing for infrastructure, software as a service for applications, or crowdsourcing for IT services use digital platforms to enable service procurement by focal firms. Digital platforms provide a means to facilitate small sourcing contracts but have also been found to produce technology-mediated control over workers participating in crowdsourcing (Wiener et al., 2020a). We propose that the multifaceted relationship among workers, digital platforms, and service buyers presents challenges and opportunities for sourcing managers.

IT crowdsourcing services provide access to specialized skills and on-demand solutions while reducing costs of services, transaction, and coordination for small contract management (Ye and Kankanhalli, 2015). On the one side of this tension, ITCS is often viewed as a panacea for IT staffing challenges. On the other side of this tension, ITCS can disrupt traditional organizational structures, rendering them obsolete (Burtch et al., 2018; Morse, 2015). Such arguments seem reasonable because it can be difficult for firms to couple ITCS services provisioned by emerging online platforms with extant support structures such as IT administration, which consists of mature IT-enabled procurement, security, and human resource management. The forces driving procurement (or lack of procurement) of ITCS are thus nuanced and support both sides of this tension. This tension results in conflicting guidance and recommendations regarding ITCS procurement, thereby hindering our understanding of why some firms find ITCS more suitable for procuring services.

We propose that it is theoretically and practically important to address this tension. One way to resolve this tension is to identify IT capabilities that enable firms to assess the suitability of digital platform $( \mathrm { e . g . } ,$ , Upwork or Freelancer) functionalities to better utilize microsourcing services offered by ITCS. In this study, we argue that a focal firm’s IT Administration Capabilities and flexible technical infrastructure (referred to as IT Flexibility) influence the firm’s evaluation of how suitable are an ITCS platform’s functionalities are for meeting the firm’s requirements of IT-related services (what we call ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment). ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, in turn, enables firms to procure strategic services through ITCS (referred to as ITCS Service Procurement). We address the following research questions: How do a firm’s IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility influence a firm’s procurement of ITCS Services? and how does the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediate the above relationship? By addressing these research questions, we advance our knowledge of capabilities that are essential to take advantage of microsourcing services offered by ITCS platforms (e.g., procuring services such as project management, application testing, and operations management).

To address our research questions, we analyze primary data from 308 firms in the United States. The findings are consistent with our theorizing. First, we find that a firm’s IT administration capability is positively associated with the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, which, in turn, positively influences ITCS Service Procurement. Second, we find that IT Flexibility positively moderates the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. Finally, we find that the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Service Procurement is mediated by ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. We conduct a post hoc analysis to provide a nuanced understanding of how the various dimensions of IT Administration Capability (i.e., Procurement, Security, and Human Resources) and IT Flexibility (i.e., Application, Data, and Infrastructure Flexibility) help develop a firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, which in turn contributes to ITCS Service Procurement. Our study makes three main contributions to research. First, it contributes to IT outsourcing literature by positing and testing an organizational capabilities-based theoretical model. This model expands the theoretical base that traditionally relied on transaction cost economics, resource dependency, and social exchange theories (Kotlarsky et al., 2018) to explain sourcing decisions, structures, and relationships. Second, it contributes to the organizational capability view literature by extending the role of IT Procurement, Security, and Human Resources functions (IT Administration Capability) and IT Application, Data, and Infrastructure Flexibility (IT Flexibility) to explain how a firm’s organizational capabilities adapt to emerging microsourcing work environments, thereby enhancing the firm’s assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms for ITCS-related activities, which in turn drives the firm’s procurement of services from ITCS. Third, it contributes to crowdsourcing literature by extending the procurer’s perspective, which is not fully explored in the literature (Agerfalk et al., 2020<sup>˚</sup> ; Deng and Joshi, 2016; Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020a; Taylor and Joshi, 2019). Finally, our study conceptualizes a new construct, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, which is the linchpin that encourages firms to successfully engage with the growing gig economy.

This study contributes to practice by highlighting key factors that enable ITCS service procurement. Researchers have argued that the growth of self-service procurement is weakening IT organizational influence within firms (Vithayathil, 2017). Our findings demonstrate that firms need a combination of IT administrative capability and flexible technology architecture, which in combination makes the firm to assess ITCS platforms as more suitable for performing ITCS-related activities, which in turn motivates higher ITCS Service Procurement.

## Theoretical background

## Sourcing and growth of microsourcing

IT sourcing includes a broad range of activities related to procurement of IT-related products and services (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020a). IT sourcing is complex and multifaceted requiring organizational and technology-based decision making (Dibbern et al., 2004). In order to facilitate the relationship between procurers and providers of services, administrative materials such as contracts serve a critical role (Chang et al., 2017).

Although TCE has been the dominant theoretical framework for evaluating contract-based, structured relationships of IT procurers and IT providers (Ang and Straub, 1998; Benaroch et al., 2016; Chang et al., 2017; Dibbern et al., 2008; Hahn et al., 2009), researchers have identified limits of TCE in describing the breadth of IT sourcing activities (Alaghehband et al., 2011). Social and relational theories have been proffered as an alternative to the transactional view of TCE. Approaches for assessing IT procurement actions have ranged from assessing the role of social exchange (Ang and Slaughter, 2001) to examining the role of emotional exchange between parties in sourcing relationships (Goo et al., 2009; Gopal and Koka, 2012; Olsson et al., 2008). While interpersonal elements of contract management and governance represent an important element of managing IT sourcing, we propose that for micro-sourcing relationships enabled by crowdsourcing, in-house organizational capabilities of the procuring organization are a critical element to fully understand microsourcing practices. Sourcing scholars have also examined sourcing processes through consideration of dynamic capabilities examining how sourcing organizations adapt in the face of changing circumstances (Karimi-Alaghehband and Rivard, 2019, 2020). Concepts regarding capabilities also contribute to knowledge-based perspectives of the sourcing literature, including themes of firm boundaries (Argyres and Zenger, 2012; Langlois, 1998), information capabilities (Mani et al., 2010), and institutional change (Brahm and Tarzijan, 2014´ ). Our research extends the literature regarding sourcing capabilities by examining the context of capabilities within a microsourcing environment.

Due to the small nature of transactions in ITCS, the role of organizational controls such as detailed contracts and procurer/provider alignment meetings (Gregory et al., 2013) are often not relevant. Crowdsourcing requires organizations to procure and manage IT tasks differently; however, extant literature provides limited understanding on implications of these differences from an organizational perspective (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014, 2020a). In this study, we examine how organizational capabilities provide a meaningful method of examining procurement activities in digitally enabled procurement environments. A summary of the theoretical positioning of the extant literature is presented in Table 1.

In this study, we propose that many theoretical lenses applied to the analysis of traditional sourcing are less relevant in a microsourcing-based environment. Traditional sourcing is characterized by contracts representing direct relationships between organizations that procure IT services and organizations that provide IT services (Lee et al., 2019). In contrast, microsourcing relationships are characterized by contracts between organizations that procure IT services and digital platforms that engage with workers. While the focal firm may provide crowdworkers with task requirements and control over whether to approve or not approve compensation upon task completion, interaction between the worker and the focal firm is mediated by the digital platform (Taylor and Joshi, 2018). Platforms provide workforce-related data, escrow, and payment processing services to the focal firms and workers participating in the transaction, but assume no responsibility for the work that is completed. Since platforms serve as intermediaries in managing the transaction, relationships between ITCS workers and organizations that procure IT services are indirect (Taylor and Joshi, 2018). A summary comparison of traditional sourcing and microsourcing is provided in Table 2. Although traditional outsourcing research has considered the long-term relationships that are fostered between buyers and sellers of services (Poston et al., 2009), microsourcing transactions are mostly financial terms of engagements, are short in nature, and communication tools are limited to task-based deliverables (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014). Tasks on the platforms are designed to be transactional, as a long-term relationship between the worker and the focal firm could lead to disintermediation of the digital platform from the sourcing process. We propose that to develop a better understanding of emerging IT sourcing phenomena related to crowdsourcing, scholars must recognize the substantive ways in which microsourcing differs from traditional outsourcing and must incorporate new theoretical lenses through which to describe this emerging sourcing phenomena. This study provides a theoretical framework for this increasingly important and promising phenomenon.

Within the domain of microsourcing, substantial diversity exists in the literature. Some microsourcing studies focus on delivery of physical tasks (e.g., driving or delivery services, household repair, and short-term accommodations), while other studies focus on the delivery of digital services (e.g., software development, design development, and data review). Our study specifically examines the sourcing of work that is delivered digitally and is thus not constrained to physical locations (Joshi et al., 2022). In addition to the nature of the tasks that are completed, microtasking may be completed in diverse financial structures. Some microtasking is completed in contest-based work engagements, whereby multiple individuals submit work products but only a limited number of “winning” participants selected by the focal purchaser are compensated for the work. In contrast some microtasking is completed by workers who are directly compensated for completing defined tasks. Our study specifically examines microsourcing in the context of the completion of specific tasks for payment. An overview of the diversity of microtasking conceptualization including exemplar papers is presented in Table 3.

## Conceptualizing ITCS platform suitability assessment

ITCS services (e.g., those provisioned by Upwork) offer several functional capabilities that enable firms directly to procure services such as project, application, and operations management from individual workers (Lee et al., 2004; Majchrzak and Malhotra, 2013; Nambisan et al., 2019). However, managing such limited scale relationships with individual ITCS service providers (e.g., service providers on Upwork) differs from managing traditional outsourcing relationships as described above (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014; Ramasubbu et al., 2008; Weigelt, 2013).

When engaging in procurement activities with a traditional outsourcing company (e.g., IBM or Infosys) the focal firm enjoys administrative support such as account manager, and contracts generally represent groups of workers rather than individuals. In contrast, the selfservice nature of the gig economy transfers many administrative responsibilities from the outsourcing provider to the hiring firm, creating a gap that puts an extra burden on firms interested in using ITCS. Specifically, it places the onus of assessing how well the functional capabilities of ITCS platforms match the IT service requirements on the procuring firm. We refer to it as an assessment gap, as it represents a firm’s evaluation of ITCS platforms’ suitability for desired tasks (see Figure 1). Much like cloud computing, which requires firms to assess the suitability of the benefits afforded by cloud computing models (Kathuria et al., 2018), firms looking to create business value through on-demand skilled labor offered by ITCS must find ways to effectively assess ITCS platforms to facilitate procurement of services through such platforms.

Drawing on prior research, we define ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment as the firm’s evaluation of the functionality of the ITCS platform characteristics (e.g., Upwork) for performing ITCS-related activities (e.g., setting up automatic payments, coordinating work, or assigning tasks) (see Figure 2).<sup>3</sup> To describe this construct, we draw on microfoundations of capabilities (Helfat and Peteraf, 2015; Zahra and George, 2002; Zollo and Winter, 2002) and propose that managers associated with appropriate units in firms develop cognitive and sensemaking abilities in the crowdsourcing context (Helfat and Peteraf, 2015). We further draw on the work of Pavlou and El Sawey (2006) in recognizing that variability exists in the way that firms are able to make use of technical functionality provided through technology platforms. Such contextual abilities develop routines for scanning, sensing, and perceiving the appropriate ITCS platforms’ characteristics and functionalities. Such routines practiced over time develop well-defined, recombined, and reconfigured processes that facilitate effective assessment of ITCS platforms’ characteristics for the firm’s purpose. In sum, drawing on prior research and above mechanisms, we posit that firm capabilities are a means to enhance the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment.

Building on the preceding discussion, we suggest that ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is a bridge between the firm’s in-house IT capabilities and the firm’s ITCS Service Procurement. A firm’s in-house IT capabilities (e.g., via IT Administration) can bridge the gap (Figure 2) by enhancing the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment (Burtch et al., 2018; Kathuria et al., 2015). Prior research found that firms with greater levels of assessment ability are better able to improvise their routines to adapt to new situations (Pavlou and El Sawy, 2010). The concept of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment extends beyond technical development activities. Our study develops the concept of ITCS platform suitability assessment in the context of microsourcing. Specifically, in our context, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment encompasses the firm’s evaluation of the suitability of ITCS platform functionalities to support microsourcing of the firm’s IT services. Unlike traditional IS services procurement where the focal firm contracts with companies (e.g., IBM or Infosys) to procure IS services, in the context of ITCS, focal firms contract with individuals or small groups of individuals to source micro-tasks. This difference in granularity has profound implications for how data related to task management, work history, and payment management is used in procuring ITCS services. If a focal firm contracts with an IS services supplier (e.g., IBM) to procure IS services, the firm does not always know who will do the work or how the work will be organized. These activities are delegated to the IS services supplier (e.g., IBM) and therefore, the data and its analysis that the focal firm needs to evaluate such services is at a macro level (IS services provider, rather than individual worker).

In contrast to outsourcing to large businesses, when a focal firm engages with an ITCS platform, sourcing decisions are made regarding individuals (or small groups of individuals) who would directly complete procured services. The data needed to evaluate the suitability of ITCS platforms is at a micro level and varies by firms. The focal firm’s ability to use information provided by platforms to meaningfully assess crowd workers’ suitability varies across firms. In our hypotheses, we argue that firms differ in their ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment based on the presence of firm’s in-house IT capabilities (e.g., in-house IT administration capability).<sup>4</sup>

We develop a research model to examine how a firm’s IT capabilities help them to enhance their ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, which in turn influences their ITCS Service Procurement. First, we draw on organizational capabilities view (Bharadwaj, 2000; Bhatt et al., 2005; Byrd and Turner, 2000; Garicano and Wu, 2012; Mishra and Agarwal, 2010) to discuss IT administration and flexibility capabilities. Next, we theorize the relationship between the above in-house units’ capabilities and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. Finally, we theorize how ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediates the relationship between in-house IT administration and eventual strategic ITCS Service Procurement.

## Organizational capabilities reflected as IT administration capability and IT flexibility

We begin by discussing organizational IT capabilities to develop our thesis that firms that effectively harness their organizational IT capabilities (i.e., IT administration and IT flexibility) are better positioned to adapt to ITCS crowdsourcing work environments. Recent studies highlighted the importance of incorporating search and select, and asset orchestration in achieving desired outcomes (Augier and Teece, 2008; Barreto, 2010). In the context of emerging sourcing activities, we propose that developing organizational capabilities that effectively search, select, and orchestrate assets will be critical in accomplishing sourcing-related goals. Organizational capabilities are specific, identifiable processes that reflect an organization’s ability to conduct business activities (Dosi et al., 2000). Prior research recognizes that communication and knowledge are key to exploitation of organizational capabilities (Garicano and Wu, 2012). The knowledge management and learning capacity of organization, particularly in context of technology capabilities, contribute to greater levels of organizational capability (Aral and Weill, 2007; Gold et al., 2001). Prior research also identified organizational capabilities related to procurement as a predictor of sourcing activities using B2B platforms (Mishra and Agarwal, 2010). We suggest that organizational capabilities essential to sourcing ITCS services are IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility (infrastructure, applications, and data), which we discuss next.

IT administration capability. We define IT Administration Capability as a firm’s capability that facilitates IT Procurement, IT Security, and IT Human Resource (HR) management. IT Administration Capability is important in the context of ITCS because it is a means by which firms can identify key processes of external online platforms and adapt emerging work structures (Taylor, 2015). IT Administration Capability plays a crucial role in governance activities and facilitates information sharing, both of which are associated with outsourcing success (Berente et al., 2009; Miranda and Kavan, 2005). IT Administration Capability includes strategic capabilities (e.g., security administration or procurement management) and operational capabilities (e.g., bid reviews or active directory management).

IT flexibility. We define IT Flexibility as the adaptability of a firm’s applications, infrastructure, and data; it is captured by the degree of scalability, modularity, transparency, and standardization (Sahaym et al., 2007; Tafti et al., 2013; Tiwana et al., 2010; Winter et al., 2014). IT Flexibility facilitates communication and coordination within and across firms (Chatterjee et al., 2006; Gosain et al., 2004; Khuntia et al., 2019) because routines associated with flexibility enable the connection, engagement, and development of effective systems (Bharadwaj, 2000; Bhatt et al., 2005; Ray and Ray, 2006). IT Flexibility in terms of application, infrastructure, and data architectures enables connectivity, application functionality, IT compatibility, and data transparency (Benitez et al., 2018; Byrd and Turner, 2000; Tafti et al., 2013). IT Flexibility also enables the firm to respond swiftly to competitive actions (Overby et al., 2006; Roberts and Grover, 2012; Tallon and Pinsonneault, 2011).

## ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment

ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment refers to the firm’s evaluation of the suitability of ITCS platform (e.g., Upwork) characteristics for performing ITCS-related activities (e.g., setting up automatic payments, coordinating work, or assigning tasks). In the context of crowdsourcing, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment refers to the evaluation of micro-IT-task crowdsourcing administration supported by the IT functionality (e.g., set up of automatic payments, coordinate work, or assign tasks) that online platforms (e.g., Upwork) provide to firms (Deng and Joshi, 2016). We propose that while ITCS platforms provide standard functional capabilities and services, how firms assess the nuances of these functional capabilities and services for their specific use is contingent on firm IT capabilities (i.e., IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility, as we discuss later). Therefore, different firms will differ in their ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, depending on their IT capabilities. This is consistent with the view that assessment of IT emerges from interactions of actors/organizations and IT artifacts (Leonardi, 2011; Majchrzak and Markus, 2012; Wang et al., 2012).

## ITCS service procurement

We conceptualize ITCS Service Procurement as the extent to which a focal firm (e.g., Netflix) procures ITCS services (e.g., project management, application testing, and coding) from ITCS platforms (e.g., Upwork). The nature of services is conceptualized as the variety of services while the magnitude of services is captured by the depth to which each service could be availed.

IT Services Procurement differs from ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment in important ways. ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment refers to the focal firm’s evaluation of the suitability of ITCS platforms’ characteristics in enabling the firm to perform operational IT-related activities (e.g., setting up automatic payments) via data and technical functionality. In other words, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is the firm’s (e.g., Netflix) assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms’ characteristics/functionalities (e.g., Upwork’s) for accomplishing IT-related activities. In contrast, ITCS Service Procurement refers to a firm’s (e.g., Netflix’s) procurement of (nature or types) a variety of IT services through ITCS platforms and the (degree) depth to which such services are procured based on the firm’s experience and expertise. This construct refers to the variety and depth of IT services availed by the firm from ITCS platforms.

Methodologically, IT Services Procurement describes the breadth and depth of ITCS service procurement activities. The breadth of ITCS Service Procurement is described by examining the variety of activities in which the focal firm procures ITCS-based IS services. The depth of ITCS Services Procurement is described through the Likert scale applied to the degree to which each activity had been procured through ITCS platforms. The data for this construct are collected from IT professionals with responsibility for procurement activities within their respective organizations. We next build on the above constructs and develop our hypotheses.

## Hypothesis development

Figure 3 shows our research model. Our key premise is that the interplay between a firm’s IT capabilities and the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is associated with the procurement of ITCS services. We theorize how such interplay takes place, focusing on key mechanisms and theoretical underpinnings. Our central thesis is that a firm’s IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility help a firm to bridge the gap between in-house IT capabilities and selfservice procurement described in Figure 1. The presence of these in-house capabilities favorably impacts the firm’s assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms, which in turn motivates the firm to procure ITCS services (e.g., project management). We propose that a firm that has superior IT Administration Capability and greater IT Flexibility will assess ITCS platforms as more suitable for the firm, which in turn will drive the firm to procure more ITCS services.

We start with proposing that IT Administration Capability influences a firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. IT Administration Capability comprises procurement, security, and human resource management functions. A firm with well-defined, mature, and standardized processes coordinated by in-house specialists in

IT administrative functions may view ITCS platforms as more suitable for the firm because dedicated human capital evolves by continually learning and matching emerging technologies offered by ITCS platforms (Zahra and George, 2002; Zollo and Winter, 2002). Capabilities associated with IT administration facilitate engagement with resources provided by ITCS platforms for meeting organizational needs. A firm’s IT administration capabilities often build on dedicated human resources, standardized application architectures, and infrastructure and adapt to broader industry trends such that they can enhance the suitability of ITCS platforms for the firm. Thus, a firm’s IT administration capability facilitates adaptation and renewal of routines for effectively assessing and continually evolving ITCS platforms for meeting organizational needs (Gawer, 2021; McIntyre et al., 2021; Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020a).

We propose that IT Administration Capability positively influences ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment for three reasons. First, a well-developed IT procurement capability with specialized procurement professionals facilitates adaptation and reconfiguration of administrative sourcing processes to adjust with needs of clients that evolve over time in response to continuously changing market conditions. Procurement activities may include such tasks as contractually arranging providers to accomplish tasks, monitoring contract feedback, and evaluating supplier output. Administrative tasks may be fairly consistent for large contracts with traditional sourcing providers (Gonzalez et al., 2006). However, evolution of IT administrative capabilities may be required for emerging selfservice sourcing. For example, procurement professionals may recognize that small contracts associated with microtasks on ITCS platforms may not require the same degree of legal review as traditional contracts and create alternative approval processes for contracts that are under a certain monetary threshold. Through such process adjustments, an advanced IT procurement function is an effective organizational capability that is equipped to integrate ITCS platform features into the firm’s IT procurement functions and, in so doing, expand the firm’s sourcing portfolio. A well-developed IT Administration Capability also provides technology-associated role and decision-making clarity for administrative functions, which allows firms to assess IT platform functionalities as more suitable.

Second, a more mature IT security capability implies that the firm is better equipped to deal with potential security concerns that involve giving work to workers outside the firm’s boundary and governing ITCS service-level contracts (Yang et al., 2015). When the firm crowdsources, individuals outside the firm have a certain level of access to software source code, online content, or intellectual property, which can be risky. Firms with a mature IT security capability can more easily customize the security access to IT assets used by crowd workers to leverage opportunities that crowdsourcing provides.

Finally, managing outsourcing relationships is challenging and IT human resources can play a critical role in managing emergent complexities (Patel et al., 2019; Willcocks and Griffiths, 2010). Managerial and administrative capabilities required to support outsourcing relationships create rigidity and coordination costs within the firm (Tan and Sia, 2006). Specialized IT human resources targeted to strengthen and support sourcing structures mitigate such challenges (Willcocks and Griffiths, 2010). Given the small contract amounts, the management oversight that ITCS requires as a percentage of project costs may be greater than traditional outsourcing contracts. Managing crowdsourcing tasks can thus be even more challenging than managing traditional outsourcing projects (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014). These managerial challenges can discourage firms from leveraging ITCS as a sourcing option (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014). However, firms with fluid organizational structures are better able to absorb the ondemand workforce (Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2014). A welldeveloped IT Administration Capability provides role clarity of administrative functions, which enhances the suitability of ITCS platforms for the firm. Relevant IT functionalities include arranging crowd-worker payments, coordinating work across providers, or assigning tasks. Such administration capabilities improve the firm’s ability to leverage external resources (Rai and Tang, 2010), thereby rendering ITCS platforms as more suitable for the firm.

In sum, IT Administration Capability is an organizational capability that helps develop necessary in-house resources and routines across functions to match the competencies of ITCS platforms and manage emergent complexities. This organizational capability enables the effective fostering and deployment of a firm’s work units, thereby enhancing the firm’s assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms for ITCS-related activities. In-house IT administration capability enables the firm to first evaluate the characteristics of ITCS platforms (e.g., Upwork) for performing ITCS-related services (e.g., project management) and then to match it to the firm’s internal requirements, thereby enhancing the firm’s assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms for procuring services. For instance, a focal firm’s IT Administration Capability enables it to favorably assess the suitability of ITCS platforms’ characteristics and functionalities for managing multiple project tasks or managing payments to workers. A firm with superior IT Administration Capability has higher capacity to engage with ITCS platform functionalities, and thereby is able to effectively assess which ITCS platform characteristics are more suitable for the firm. Thus, firms with superior IT Administration Capability will assess ITCS platforms as more suitable for the firm based on platforms’ specific characteristics. Hence, we hypothesize:

Hypothesis H1: IT Administration Capability is positively associated with the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment.

Our next hypothesis examines how IT Flexibility (of application, infrastructure, and data architectures) strengthens the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. Prior literature suggests that IT Flexibility positively influences outsourcing behaviors (Byrd and Turner, 2000; Cho and Choi, 2012; Tan and Sia, 2006). Firms with in-house IT administration capability reflected by human capital, acquisition processes, and security systems will assess the ITCS platforms’ characteristics as more suitable for delivering ITCS-related functionalities because IT Flexibility facilitates integration of desired services from outside the firm (i.e., from the crowd or market) with existing functions and processes (Han and Mithas, 2013; Tafti et al., 2013). IT Flexibility also enables in-house administration capabilities to be aligned with ITCS platforms that manage multiple tasks. Specifically, IT Flexibility facilitates shareability and reusability via loose coupling, standardization, and transparency (Byrd and Turner, 2000; Khuntia et al., 2019; Sahaym et al., 2007; Tafti et al., 2013; Tiwana et al., 2010). Such shareability and reusability enhance IT administration’s assessment of ITCS platforms’ suitability for managing tasks, payments, and feedback through the exchange of tacit knowledge and reconfiguration of system capabilities. Such firms will hence assess ITCS platforms’ characteristics more favorably for performing ITCS-related activities (e.g., payment to vendors). Further, as the needs evolve over time, firms with higher IT flexibility will be better able to “search and select” suitable platform and orchestrate their assets to meet evolving needs. Thus, IT flexibility enhances the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment.

Moreover, the assessment of ITCS platform suitability applies different levels of data granularity and requires modifications to existing procurement processes in the focal firm. IT Flexibility provides the focal firm with the ability to make such granular modifications, thereby strengthening the impact of IT Administration Capability on the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. For example, firms with superior IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility are more familiar with evaluation process and adaptation; hence they will be better able to interpret the ratings and reviews of workers on ITCS platforms, make a call on their accuracy, and select appropriate crowdworkers for a micro-task. In contrast, firms that lack IT administrative capabilities and lack flexibility will fail to develop competencies that are needed for capitalize on the selfservice nature of the gig economy. Such firms will assess ITCS platforms as less suitable for ITCS-related activities.

In sum, IT Flexibility reflected by application, infrastructure, and data-oriented flexibilities strengthens the relationship between the firm’s IT Administration Capability and the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment by providing (1) granularity, transparency, and knowledge sharing within and across firm units (Tafti et al., 2013) and (2) effective interaction with ITCS platforms’ resources and capabilities (Benitez et al., 2018; Khuntia et al., 2019; Taylor and Joshi, 2019). Hence:

Hypothesis H2: IT Flexibility positively moderates the association between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment.

Next, we study the relationship between ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment and ITCS Service Procurement. Firms that assess ITCS platforms as more suitable for ITCSrelated activities would have developed routines and nurtured managers with superior cognition and sensemaking abilities regarding procurement of ITCS services (Helfat and Peteraf, 2015). Such managers and firms will have the prior experience-based knowledge and learning, sensemaking, and confidence to procure ITCS services both horizontally and vertically. That is, they would not only avail of ITCS platforms for a variety of IT services but also for desired depth or degree of service. For example, managers at Netflix with prior assessment success and experience of using Upwork for relatively simple, nonstrategic services such as setting automatic payments for their firm would be able to confidently procure Upwork’s more complex strategic services such project management (e.g., Willcocks and Griffiths, 2010)

In sum, we propose that as a firm’s assessment of ITCS platforms’ suitability increase in terms of effectively using functionalities provided by digital platforms for activities such as setting up automatic payments, the firm would be better able to engage in procuring ITCS services. Hence:

Hypothesis H3: ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is positively associated with ITCS Service Procurement.

Finally, we propose that ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediates the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Service Procurement. That is, the full effect of organizational IT administration functions (e.g., human resource and security management) on ITCS Service Procurement by the focal firm manifests through ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. Specifically, we integrate the reasoning for Hypothesis 1 with the arguments proposed in Hypothesis 3 by stating that IT Administration Capability’s potential can be fully harnessed through ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment for more effective ITCS Service Procurement.

There are two reasons for the above arguments. First, the focal firm’s (e.g., Netflix’s) administration may not have cutting edge operational competencies (e.g., for setting up automatic payments and security coding). This is because it is not their core competency to develop such functionalities as these keep evolving with the market’s clock speed in their own technological domains—on the other hand, platforms such as Upwork specialize in such services and they may have workforce with more depth, velocity, and resources as it is their core competency (Gawer, 2021; McIntyre et al., 2021; Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020b; Sahaym et al., 2007; Taylor and Joshi, 2018). Second, from the lens of microfoundations, routines, and cognition, both the focal firm and its managers must be able to assess ITCS platforms as suitable for the firm’s desired services and keep developing cognition, sensemaking, and operational routines as platforms’ characteristics update continuously (Pavlou and El Sawy, 2006; Taylor and Joshi, 2018).

In essence, though IT administration in an organizational setting is important for ITCS Service Procurement as it provides a base knowledge pool for continuous exploration, search scope, organizational learning, and managerial control and coordination, the realization of ITCS Service Procurement happens via ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. This is because though ITCS platforms provide market-based resources and operational routines including functionalities that keep evolving, such resources and routines can be effectively accessed, assimilated, and leveraged through ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment (Gawer, 2021; McIntyre et al., 2021; Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020). ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment thus mediates the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Service Procurement because IT Administration Capability’s potential is harnessed through this construct.

In the relative absence of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, that is, when a firm assesses ITCS platforms as less suitable for operational ITCS services, IT Administration Capability of the focal firm will not be able to fully realize the eventual procurement of ITCS services. In other words, presence of in-house IT procurement, HR, and security-related units enhances the firm’s assessment of suitability of ITCS platform’s breadth of functionalities, which, in turn, motivates firms to procure a broader and more complex set of ITCS services (e.g., project management, application testing, and coding).<sup>5</sup> Hence:

Hypothesis H4: ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediates the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Service Procurement.

## Research methodology

We test our research model using data collected via a survey administered online to technology leaders of U.S. firms with the help of a third-party data collection service. We next describe our instrument development and validation processes and data collection in detail.

## Scale development and variables

Scales to measure IT Flexibility, IT Administration Capability, and ITCS Service Procurement constructs are developed from existing literature, while the items for ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment emerged from a pilot study. Within the pilot study, respondents had the opportunity to describe their understanding of the administrative support capabilities provided by digital sourcing platforms. We propose that this understanding describes the extent to which the organization assesses the suitability of ITCS for digitally enabled procurement. We used a rigorous threestage process to develop these scales, consistent with established scale development guidelines: item creation, scale development, and instrument testing, further informed by the steps of scale development as prescribed in MacKenzie et al. (2011).

IT administration capability. Scales for IT Administration Capability were drawn from capability maturity model conceptualizations developed as part of Information Systems Audit and Control Association’s (ISACA) Control Objectives for IT (COBIT) (Committee, 2007). Researchers have identified COBIT as best practices in implementing IT administrative processes (Damianides, 2005; Veiga and Eloff, 2007), including sourcing. We adapted scale questions for the COBIT version 4.1 framework, which includes model-based capability maturity assessments for numerically categorizing levels of IT administrative maturity. IT administrative maturity has been found to be an antecedent of IT capabilities associated with IT performance (Simonsson et al., 2010; Zhang et al., 2016). Of thirty-four high-level processes associated with IT administrative processes defined by COBIT, the three processes most related to the use of outsourcing (Gantman and Fedorowicz,

2016) are considered in this study: IT Procurement, IT Security, and IT Human Resources.<sup>6</sup> Each of these constructs includes three to four items (see Appendix for details).

IT flexibility. Our measure of IT Flexibility is based upon prior validated scales of IT Flexibility developed by Byrd and Turner (2000). All elements presented by Byrd and Turner (2000) were considered for inclusion in the study. However, after review with sourcing practitioners, only the scales for data transparency, application functionality, and IT infrastructure are included in our analysis. The text of these scales generally mirrors that of those developed by Byrd and Turner (2000), though some terms were revised to reflect modern development techniques (e.g., “agile development” and “cloud computing”). The validity of the updated survey instrument was tested in the pilot study to verify a good fit to the data. Separate scales were identified for Application, Data, and Infrastructure Flexibility. Each of the three variables includes six items (see Appendix 2 for details).

We use a reflective second-order factor modeling approach to capture complementarities among first-order factors in IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility constructs (Tanriverdi and Venkatraman, 2005). A reflective second-order factor is an unobserved factor manifest via observed first-order factors. The second-order factors explain co-variation in first-order factors.

ITCS platform suitability assessment. Our measure of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is based on results of our pilot data analysis. In our pilot survey, we included openended questions for participants to describe the challenges and opportunities in the procurement of services through ITCS. Respondents’ comments implied that some firms were more suited to take advantage of the technical functionality provided by ITCS platforms than others. In conceptualizing the concept of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, we draw on the work of Pavlou and El Sawy (2006) in describing the application of technical functionality provided by an IT system to processes of a defined business group. Our analysis applies IT functionalities provided by ITCS platforms to processes associated with managing sourcing providers. The text response of each pilot informant was evaluated to identify common themes related to ITCS perceptions. Three factors that were identified as barriers to ITCS Service Procurement could be mitigated by actions afforded by ITCS platforms: task management, payment processing, and trust in ratings/ reviews. Researchers of crowdsourcing governance have theorized that these factors contribute to crowdwork administrative activities (Gol et al., 2019). Based on results of our pilot study and the hypothesized nature of crowdwork administration, these three activities were used to develop the ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment measure.

In developing the scale, we follow the process outlined by MacKenzie et al. (2011). The responses provided in the open-ended questions included in the pilot study were reviewed to generate the items for this construct. These items were then reviewed for validity by individuals with expertise in ITCS platforms. Once the validity of the construct items was ascertained, a measurement model of the construct was specified and the construct was included in the survey provided to our respondents (MacKenzie et al., 2011).

ITCS service procurement. To develop the ITCS Service Procurement measure, we reviewed leading ITCS providers to identify types of services that can be procured via the respective platforms. Both the breadth and depth of ITCS Service Procurement activities were captured. The breadth of ITCS Service Procurement was represented by the thirteen services (e.g., project management, application testing, and operations management) identified and included in the survey. Each variable in the model was captured on a 5-point Likert-type scale of agreement to measure (e.g., extent of ITCS Service Procurement by an organization) which captured the depth of ITCS Services Procurement. The pilot data were used to validate the scales for psychometric fit. The validated scales were then used for the main data collection. A summary of variables is listed in Table 4. Survey items are included in Appendix 2.

Control variables. Each control variable is created using categorized responses to questions in our survey. First, because firm size can affect aspects of technology usage, we control for firm size, using firm revenue and number of employees. Second, consistent with prior research, we control for variation attributable to industry by including self-identified industry classification based on the standard industry code. Third, since firms that maintain a broad sourcing portfolio would have more robust sourcing administrative functions than those that source from few providers, we control for potential variation attributable to the size of a firm’s IT vendor portfolio. Fourth, since a higher number of IT employees may affect the firm’s administrative capabilities, we control for such variation in our model. Finally, because the role and view of strategic importance of IT may vary across respondents and firms, we control for such variation based on the IT organizational level of the respondent by including self-reported IT organizational level.

## Data collection

A survey methodology was used to test the research model. Data were collected via a third-party collection service in the United States. Respondents were IT decision makers who had knowledge of their organization’s approach to outsourcing. The use of third-party data collection services has been found to provide high-quality data for addressing organizationally focused research questions (Holt and Loraas, 2019). When conducting research through the use of a third-party panel provider respondents are identified based on criteria provided by the research team. In this case, the survey was provided to individuals with decisionmaking responsibility for IT sourcing within their respective organizations. As span of control for decision making may contribute to outsourcing decision making, panel participants were also required to indicate organization level in their responses (Sia and Neo, 1997). When using a thirdparty panel service provider, existing informants who match the profile required are provided with the survey and then compensated for their time by the panel service provider. To establish our measures, we conducted a pilot study using our theorized measurement model. The pilot study, with 59 responses, included a mixture of scale items and openended questions. The pilot study showed that the measures have good psychometric properties. Each measure was evaluated using exploratory factor analysis, after which a confirmatory factor analysis was conducted (Straub, 1989). Each indicator value is baselined against the highest loading indicator for each factor. In addition to the survey variables included in the pilot study, open-ended responses were evaluated to identify potential missing variables that may also contribute to the observed variation. Based on the results of the pilot study, additional measures were added to the full study and reported in results presented hereafter. In the data collection for the full study, a third-party data collection provider was used. IT decision makers participating in the third-party data collection provider’s data panel provided the responses. The respondents included many senior technology leaders with significant experience with IT procurement. Survey respondents were required to be individuals with IT sourcing decision-making responsibility within their companies. A total of 308 IT decision makers completed an online survey regarding their firm’s procurement activities from ITCS. Table 5 shows demographic information of the sample. A description of the correlation between variables is presented in Appendix 1.

## First-order versus second-order factor models

We evaluated the second-order factor model along with the first-order factors. The literature reports a number of criteria used to compare first- and second-order factor models.

These criteria include goodness of fit statistics between the two models (Grover et al., 2002; Tippins and Sohi, 2003; Venkatraman, 1990), the significance of the second-order factor loadings, and the significance of structural links between the measurement model and criterion of interest (in our study, ITCS Service Procurement) (Venkatraman, 1990). We examined the comparative fit index (CFI), the root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA), and the standardized root mean square residual (SRMR) for the model. Fit statistics for our first-order model (chisquare = 2121.58, df = 1457, CFI = 0.915, RMSEA = 0.039, SRMR = 0.066) and second-order factor model (chi-square = 500.00, df = 329, CFI = 0.962, RMSEA = 0.041, SRMR = 0.070) demonstrate an improved fit with the second-order model. The second-order model provides a more parsimonious fit with a smaller number of parameters estimated, and loadings are all highly significant $( p < . 0 0 1 )$ . Finally, as shown in the loadings in Figure 4, the second-order measurement model has a significant impact on ITCS Service Procurement. Collectively, these tests confirm the second-order factor model as the preferred approach.

## Robustness checks

Consistent with the literature, a baseline positive relationship between IT Flexibility and ITCS Service Procurement is assumed in our model. To validate our analysis, we conducted a series of post hoc analyses. We first evaluated the relationship if only IT Flexibility was tested against ITCS Service Procurement. Consistent with extant literature, the direct effect of IT Flexibility on ITCS Service Procurement was positive and significant when ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment and IT Administration Capability variables were not included. We next evaluated IT Flexibility and IT Administration Capability together, without the mediation variable of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, on the dependent variable ITCS Service Procurement. Without ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment as a mediator, the direct effect of IT Flexibility and IT Administration Capability was positive and significant, although the model had a relatively weaker model fit.

Because this study uses a self-reported questionnaire, common method bias can be a concern (Podsakoff et al., 2003). To address this, we used the marker variable test, which adjusts correlations between variables of interest after partialing out the effects of their correlations with a marker variable. The adjusted correlations were not significantly different from unadjusted correlations, suggesting that common method bias is not problematic in our study.

We consider the effects of removing latent variable predictors from our model to evaluate the remaining relationship with ITCS Service Procurement. We evaluated the relationship between IT Flexibility and ITCS Service Procurement without including ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment or IT Administration Capability. This model showed a significant, positive relationship between IT Flexibility and ITCS Service Procurement $( p < . 0 0 1 )$ with a good structural equation model (SEM) model fit (CFI = 0.944, RMSEA = 0.055, SRMR = 0.056), a finding consistent with research that finds flexibility associated with outsourcing usage (Sahaym et al., 2007). However, when ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is included in the model, the direct relationship between IT Flexibility and ITCS Service Procurement is no longer significant, though the effect of IT Flexibility on ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is significant $( p < . 0 0 1 )$ , and the effect of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment on ITCS Service Procurement is also positive and significant $( p < . 0 0 1 )$ . This model also represented a better SEM fit $( \mathrm { C F I } = 0 . 9 5 2 $ , $\operatorname { R M S E A } = 0 . 0 4 7 , \operatorname { S R M R } = 0 . 0 6 2 )$

In addition, we conducted an OLS regression analysis using averaged values of the manifest variables as proxies for the latent variables in our analysis. This approach also found a significant relationship between ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment and ITCS Service Procurement.

To enhance our understanding, we conduct two post hoc exploratory analyses to examine the nuanced effects of IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility. Our findings regarding IT Human Resources indicate mixed effects. When considering all of the factors in our module, the direct effects on ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment were negative; however, the interaction effects between IT Human Resources and the factors of IT Flexibility demonstrated a positive effect. These findings suggest that as organizations have become more modular, and roles and responsibilities of IT workers are more clearly understood, we see that IT organizations are more comfortable collaborating with individuals or groups on ITCS to complete work. This shift would imply that organizations that have greater levels of maturity of IT Human Resources, with greater role clarity and more trust in their organizations, would be likely to have higher ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment and collaborate with micro-sourced contractors to complete tasks.

When considering the interaction effects with IT Flexibility, our findings indicate that IT Human Resource management can do more than reduce turnover or improve productivity (Iqbal et al., 2018). Analysis of our data indicates that the direct effect of mature human resource capability was negative on ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment but had a positive interactive effect on Data and Infrastructure Flexibility. We posit that this indicates that when combined with factors of IT Flexibility, the development of organizational IT Human Resource capabilities better positions organizations to assess ITCS platforms as more suitable and take advantage of ITCS.

Trained procurement professionals could be better positioned to research and experiment with new sourcing techniques. Experimentation could develop expertise in using a micro-sourcing platform, which could result in greater platform assessing suitability. The potential drawback of IT Procurement expertise is that procurement functions were generally centralized to help deal with large contracts (e.g., large engagements) and can become rigid. Consistent with extant literature, analysis of our data indicates that the direct effect of mature procurement capabilities is greater procurement of services from emerging sourcing providers such as ITCS. However, in contrast with IT Human Resources, which exhibited positive interactive effects with Infrastructure and Data Flexibility, mature IT Procurement had no effect on Data Flexibility and a significant negative effect on ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment when interacting with Infrastructure Flexibility. The direct negative effect of IT Procurement capabilities presents an interesting dichotomy of the role of digital platforms in facilitating sourcing.

Taken together, the shift to self-service procurement tools for microsourcing for services like Upwork or infrastructure AWS means that the organization needs professionals with expertise in both sourcing and the sourcing platform. We find that of the three factors of IT Flexibility, Data Flexibility has the strongest positive moderating effect on ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, followed by Infrastructure Flexibility, and then Application Flexibility.

These findings highlight the importance of Data Flexibility compared to Application Flexibility and Infrastructure Flexibility. When compared with internal data structures, changing an application is relatively more straightforward, particularly if organizations can adapt applications without changing the user interface or business process. For example, a set of Java service applications can be changed without necessarily requiring meaningful changes to the ways that users of the system work. Researchers have found that modular development of applications contributes to sourcing flexibility. On the other hand, data are much more rigid because data structures and business rules associated with those data structures are hard to change. The way the data are structured and permeate through systems makes changes difficult. New applications, new suppliers, or new acquisitions must adopt previously designed data structures, or bridging capabilities must be put in place to translate between data structures. This effort can be very cumbersome for organizations. Organizations that invest in flexible data structures are much better positioned to benefit from IT Administration Capability for developing ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. In other words, such organizations are better positioned to try new things related to ITCS because it is less expensive to integrate ITCS into the organization.

The importance of Infrastructure Flexibility in today’s environment of flexible cloud infrastructures is very different from existing conceptualizations of Infrastructure Flexibility that primarily discuss open systems versus more traditional “big iron” mainframes (Byrd and Turner, 2000). We reconceptualized this open systems concept to address today’s flexible infrastructure—the cloud. Moving to public cloud computing means that an organization must adopt standard infrastructure management processes based on the guidelines provided by the cloud supplier. Adopting these processes implies that organizations can ramp up or down quickly based on needs and only need to worry about finding someone with expertise in the organization’s cloud provider (e.g., AWS) without needing to find someone with expertise in the firm’s in-house infrastructure. This flexibility is why IT organizations with higher cloud adoption have been found to have better project delivery (Taylor,

2017). Therefore, we find a more significant moderating effect of Infrastructure Flexibility than Application Flexibility.

## Discussion

## Summary of findings

Though research in IT outsourcing has evolved from IT services outsourcing in long-term contract-specific traditional economy to IT crowdsourcing in this “gig” economy, sourcing literature and practice have remained limited at both the theoretical and practitioner levels due to (1) literature’s reliance on classical organizational theories (e.g., transaction cost economics, resource dependency, and social exchange) which were designed to explain phenomena of traditional outsourcing and (2) a lack of the measures to effectively assess ITCS platforms’ suitability which has remained a challenge over the years. These shortcomings created theoretical tensions among the scholars and gave rise to doubts in the minds of executives. Firms have struggled to understand the mechanisms by which in-house IT capabilities (e.g., administrative capability) could enable them to assess the suitability of ITCS platforms (e.g., Upwork) for operational tasks; success with which would motivate them to procure ITCS services (e.g., services such as project management, application testing, and coding). In view of the above gaps in our understanding, we set out to present a more complete picture of the relationship between ITCS capabilities and ITCS Service Procurement phenomenon because ITCS platforms provide access to specialized skills and on-demand solutions while reducing the costs substantially.

First, spanning the theoretical boundaries by drawing on insights of the organizational capabilities perspective in lieu of classical theoretical lenses, our results show that a firm’s in-house IT administration capability enables a more favorable assessment of ITCS platform’s suitability by providing resources, capabilities, and structure which, in turn, motivates procurement of ITCS services. This finding suggests that firms with advanced administrative capabilities will better positioned to assess the suitability of ITCS platforms’ characteristics and functionalities to better utilize microsourcing services offered by ITCS.

Second, our findings confirm the important role of IT Flexibility in enhancing the relationship between IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment. This finding implies that IT Flexibility provides additional benefits by assisting firms in leveraging emerging sourcing opportunities such as ITCS. This suggests that IT Flexibility helps firms to leverage their administrative functions and to develop and standardize routines that allow for greater use of IT crowdsourcing. The contract management overhead that results from small, short-duration jobs offered by ITCS providers may create a barrier to adoption of ITCS (Alaghehband et al., 2011; Kathuria et al., 2015). Our results suggest that strong administrative functions and IT Flexibility in unison overcome these barriers by developing new routines and processes to address the unique contractual dynamics of microtasks.

Moreover, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment mediates the effect of IT Administration Capability on ITCS Service Procurement. Our findings demonstrate that ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment acts as an intervening mechanism and connects the IT Administration Capability (e.g., in-house human resource and security management) and ITCS Service Procurement (e.g., procurement of services such as project management, application testing, and coding).

Overall, our findings expand our understanding of the role of IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility in enabling the use of emerging sourcing options. The results suggest that developing IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility in unison maximizes the firm’s ability to leverage the IT crowdsourcing workforce and address self-service gaps common in ITCS platforms.

## Contributions and implications

Theoretical implications. This study offers three research contributions. First, it contributes to IT outsourcing literature by expanding our knowledge of the application of capabilities-related theories within the context of emerging sourcing environments. While much of the theoretical base of the sourcing literature has traditionally employed transaction cost economics, resource dependency, and social exchange theories (Kotlarsky et al., 2018) to explain sourcing decisions, structures, and relationships, our research demonstrates the critical role that capabilitiesbased views play in understanding dynamics within digital sourcing platforms. The new online microsourcing models such as ITCS necessitate the use of new lenses to uncover constructs critical to understanding contemporary IT sourcing options. Specifically, using the organizational capabilities view as a theoretical lens, our study contributes to theory by advancing knowledge of outsourcing practices for procurement of IT services through microsourcing as it expands the theoretical base traditionally used to examine IT sourcing. While previous research has demonstrated the applicability of generic capabilities literature (e.g., dynamic capabilities and capacity) to identify IT platforms’ functionalities within a firm, our research specifically extends organizational capabilities literature’s application to ITCS platform suitability assessment to procure IT functionalities that exist outside of the firm. Especially, the finding that ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment is a key link (i.e., mediator) that permits and promotes the procurement of services via deployment of ITCS platforms is significant because it reveals how this construct explains the relationship between the firm’s IT Administration and procurement of ITCS services. In particular, our work explains that microsourcing decisions are determined by how well a firm is able to configure its existing sourcing capabilities, which in turn impacts its assess of ITCS platforms when the opportunity or need for microsourcing arises. The results suggest that a firm’s assessment of ITCS platforms’ suitability shapes the firm’s ability to procure services (e.g., via microsourcing for procuring IT services such as project management). Additional theorization on such mechanisms beyond what is uncovered in this work is necessary. However, such a revelation, albeit in its nascent stages, uncovers a potential mechanism by which IT Administrative Capability and IT Flexibility facilitate ITCS Service Procurement.

Second, our findings contribute to crowdsourcing literature by advancing buyers’ (i.e., firms who procure ITCS services) perspective regarding online IT sourcing. In recent years, a considerable amount of research in the crowdsourcing space offers the crowd workers’ (i.e., suppliers’) perspective (Deng et al., 2016; Deng and Joshi, 2016; Nevo and Kotlarsky, 2020a; Taylor and Joshi, 2019). However, in comparison, we know little about the firms (i.e., the buyers) that employ these crowd workers. This knowledge disparity is particularly concerning because the supply-side outpaces the demand-side, which threatens the sustainability of ITCS.

By advancing our understanding of ITCS as a sourcing option from the buyers’ perspective, this study broadens the theoretical understanding of why some firms are more able than others to procure services through ITCS. Such theorydriven insights can help firms to build their in-house sourcing capabilities to retain ITCS as a viable sourcing option for procuring services. Specifically, the conceptualization and operationalization of the ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment construct advances the theoretical insights about managing a sourcing relationship. This conceptualization encapsulates key aspects of ITCS, including management of projects, worker performance, and payment. From the workers’ perspective, crowdsourcing platforms streamline and simplify administrative burdens of traditional freelancing, creating opportunities for technical workers to focus on technical rather than administrative tasks (Taylor and Joshi, 2019). Though crowdsourcing has grown dramatically recently, the growth has been inconsistent across industries.<sup>7</sup> Our study identifies one factor that contributes to the relatively slow adoption of ITCS and suggests that if platforms simplify the administrative effort required to manage job engagements, broader adoption may occur. A better understanding of factors that facilitate and hinder the use of crowdsourcing enables scholars to understand contexts in which crowdsourcing can be a viable sourcing option.

Third, the study contributes to organizational capability literature by not only extending the role of IT Procurement, Security, and Human Resources functions (IT Administration Capability) and IT Application, Data, and Infrastructure Flexibility (IT Flexibility) but also uncovering a new construct, ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, to explain how firms utilize these capabilities to harness the functionalities provisioned by emerging microsourcing work environments to procure ITCS services. Specifically, we provide a nuanced explanation of essential roles that each organizational capability (i.e., IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility) plays in ITCS Service Procurement by revealing how IT administration functions directly shape the firm’s ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment which is a key linchpin that assists in shaping a firm’s ITCS Service Procurement, while IT Flexibility strengthens this relationship. These findings not only contribute to a theoretical understanding of how various administrative functions and different kinds of IT flexibilities help in developing organizational capabilities in the context of ITCS, but it also reveals how vital these capabilities are in the context of microsourcing. It is significant that ITCS does not render traditional administrative functions obsolete; those functions are germane to the selfservice nature of ITCS, which transfers many administrative responsibilities to the firm.

As self-service procurement platforms proliferate, IS scholars have highlighted the declining role of IT management in decision making (Vithayathil, 2017). Identification of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment as a contributor to sourcing success indicates that despite the growth of self-service IT sourcing, administration and IT Flexibility may continue to perform an essential function within organizations. A key contribution lies in showing that organizations differ in their assessment of the suitability of ITCS platforms to deliver ITCS functionalities, and a key reason for these differences lies in the interplay between IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility.

Managerial implications. A key managerial implication is that in this era of IT crowdsourcing and “gig” economy, firms and its key executives must shed their doubts about the value of in-house IT administrative capability and its ability to effectively evaluate ITCS platforms’ suitability because such assessment motivates procurement of strategic ITCS Services. Managers in the firm, particularly those having IT administrative capability and flexibility, are better able to adapt to the transactional nature of task-based digital services delivery in exchange for payment (e.g., for services provisioned by platforms such as Upwork)—both in terms of breadth of service and depth therein—rather than longterm service and compensation-oriented contracts. Such inhouse capabilities help firms in leveraging platforms’ emergent functionalities and procure relevant services as needed. In this gig economy, the onus to fill the assessment gap is on in-house managers and units—they need to identify the firm’s needs, assessing functional capabilities and suitability of ITCS platforms, and match these with eventual ITCS service procurement.

Managers must keep abreast with how the continuous developments in functionalities of digital platforms that enable self-service procurement change an organization’s purchasing practices. We demonstrate that greater diversity of sourcing options in emerging technology environments creates new opportunities for procurement teams to innovate and expand the ways in which procurement teams may provide value in transactions. Prior to the advent of digita platforms, firms could often find procurement success by establishing fairly standard vendor management practices (Guth, 2007; Simon et al., 2009). Vendor management skills were largely fungible, and successful practices could be observed across companies (Rustagi et al., 2008). However, when sourcing through digital platforms, there are tasks that were formerly completed by the service provider account management function that now fall to the focal firms’ procurement teams. Our findings demonstrate that when using self-service procurement platforms, successful organizations developed suitable functionalities specific to digital platform’s features. Successful firms demonstrated both vendor management best practices and a high assessment of ITCS platform suitability. These findings demonstrate the value of developing IT Administration Capability and IT Flexibility that facilitate the development of ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment and, in turn, enable organizations to engage with ITCS platforms.

## Limitations, future research, and conclusion

From crowdsourcing to cloud services, digital procurement platforms lead to new ways of sourcing emerging sourcing technology services. Our study shows the role that organizational capabilities contribute to an organization’s ability to procure services through digital platforms. Future research can further examine the nature of IT administration capabilities and IT Flexibility that are associated with effective sourcing of emerging technologies. Emerging technology for sourcing is often highlighted by greater selfservice procurement, such as software as a service, or cloud computing. Future research could also examine additional theoretical lenses by which sourcing decisions may be evaluated, such as socio-technical considerations of platforms, behavioral theories, or knowledge-based views. Our research has limitations that can serve as starting points for future research. The procurement activities considered in this research are facilitated through self-service digital platforms. Though our study uncovered the role of IT administrative capability and flexibility for developing the capability to favorably assess ITCS platforms’ suitability and facilitating ITCS Service Procurement, we acknowledge that the specific context of our study is ITCS delivery in marketplace online platforms. Our findings may be less applicable in other types of ITCS work arrangements, such as slow-moving or low-IT-Service Procurement sectors. Our research focused on use of ITCS and does not consider effectiveness of crowdwork in comparison to other IT sourcing mechanisms. Future work can identify specific factors contributing to crowdwork efficacy. Future work can also identify conditions when IT Administration Capability, IT Flexibility, and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment may be used to support sourcing processes that are not digital platform enabled, or for procurement of resources that are not IT services. Future studies can also examine effects of other factors, beyond those considered in this analysis that may contribute to firms’ choices regarding ITCS use.

To conclude, this study highlights that factors beyond flexibility, such as IT Administration Capability and ITCS Platform Suitability Assessment, contribute to firms’ decisions to procure ITCS services. As the demand for IT talent continues to grow, ITCS offers a valuable source of IT talent for firms. Our findings imply that firms need to ensure they have IT Administrative Capability and IT Flexibility to take advantage of the opportunity of ITCS and participate in the gig economy.

## Author biographies

Joseph Taylor the corresponding author and is an Associate Professor of Information Systems in the College of Business at California State University, Sacramento. His academic research interests focus on crowdsourcing and the use of technology to create business value and has appeared in such outlets as Information Systems Journal (ISJ), Journal of Strategic Information Systems (JSIS) and in Management Information Systems Quarterly Executive (MISQE). He currently serves as a Senior Editor for the Data Base for Advances in Information Systems and on the Editorial Review Board of the Journal of the Association of Information Systems. Prior to his doctoral studies he worked extensively in industry focusing on IT strategy, innovation and governance.

KD Joshi is a Professor at the University of Nevada, Reno. Earlier she was the Philip L. Kays Distinguished Professor of Information Systems and Department Chair at Washington State University. Her research focuses on underserved and underrepresented communities in IT Workforce, Broadening Participation in STEM, Knowledge Management, Crowdsourcing, Value Sensitive Designs, and Health IT. She has received grants totaling over \$5M from the National Science Foundation (NSF). Her research has appeared in journals such as MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, Journal of the Association for Information Systems, Information Systems Journal, and Decision Support Systems. Her published research has been cited over 7,600 times (h-index 33). She is currently the Editor-in-Chief of the Data Base for Advances in Information Systems and Foundations and Trends in Information

Systems. She serves on the Editorial Board of the Information Systems Journal and the Editorial Review Board of the Journal of the Association for Information Systems. Her research has been featured in media outlets such as PBS NewsHour, Salon.com, Vancouver Business Journal, London School of Economic Business Review, Puget Sound Business Journal, and Military.com.

Terence Saldanha is an Associate Professor and Becky and Howard Young Distinguished Professor of Management Information Systems at the University of Georgia at the Terry College of Business. His research interests include the role of Information Systems in strategy, business value, and innovation. His research has been published in journals such as MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, Journal of Management Information Systems, Journal of the Association for Information Systems, Production and Operations Management, and Journal of Operations Management among others. He currently serves as a Senior Editor for Production and Operations Management. He earned a PhD in Management Information Systems from the Stephen Ross School of Business at the University of Michigan. His previous degrees include an MBA from the S.P. Jain Institute of Management and Research (India) and a Bachelor of Engineering in electronics engineering from the University of Mumbai (India). Prior to his graduate studies, he worked in the IT industry in the area of software design and development.

Arvin Sahaym is a Professor and Huber Dean’s Fellow at the Carson College of Business, Washington State University. His research is motivated by the real-world phenomena at the interface of technology strategy, and strategic entrepreneurship examining the impact of capabilities, contingences, and technologies on firm performance. His research has been published in several journals including Information Systems Research, MIS Quarterly, Strategic Management Journal, Organization Science, Journal of Business Venturing, and Journal of Applied Psychology. His research outreach through media outlets has benefitted the society.
