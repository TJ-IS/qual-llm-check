# Interferon- down-regulates expression of <sup>g</sup>the 230-kDa bullous pemphigoid antigen gene (BPAG1) in epidermal keratinocytes via novel chimeric sequences of ISRE and GAS

Kaneko T, Tamai K, Matsuzaki Y, Yamazaki T, Nakano H, Kon A, Hashimoto I, Hanada K, Kaneda Y, Uitto J. Interferon- down-regulates <sup>g</sup>expression of the 230-kDa bullous pemphigoid antigen gene (BPAG1) in epidermal keratinocytes via novel chimeric sequences of ISRE and GAS. Exp Dermatol 2006: 15: 308–314. # 2006 The Authors. Journal compilation # 2006 Blackwell Munksgaard

Abstract: The 230-kDa bullous pemphigoid antigen (BPAG1) is an integral component of hemidesmosomes. We have previously reported that interferon- (IFN ) inhibits the transcription of the <sup>g g</sup>BPAG1 gene (1). Here we investigated the target sequences of IFN -signal transduction pathway in the BPAG1 promoter in <sup>g</sup>epidermal keratinocytes. Transient transfections with 50-deletion constructs of BPAG1 promoter-luciferase reporter gene plasmids in cultured normal human epidermal keratinocytes (NHEK) allowed us to narrow the DNA region containing IFN inhibitory element <sup>g</sup>(IGIE) to between 1 and 89, upstream from the transcription initiation site ( 1). Homology search in this region identified a chimeric sequence, consisting of IFN-stimulated responsive element (ISRE) with a partial 7-bp sequence of IFN activation site (GAS), <sup>g</sup>as identified in the guanylate-binding protein (GBP) gene, inserted at its center. Functional analysis of IGIE, inserted in front of the heterologous thymidine kinase promoter, indicated that IGIE acts as a down-regulatory element of the promoter through IFN -dependent <sup>g</sup>signal pathway. Transient transfection studies with BPAG1 promoter-reporter gene constructs containing mutated IGIE (with TT to GG transversions in the region of 50ISRE, GAS, and 30ISRE) demonstrated that disruption of the ISRE sequences, but not GAS, markedly suppressed the BPAG1 basal promoter activity and resulted in attenuated IFN response in keratinocytes. Our findings <sup>g</sup>provide novel insight into the mechanism of IFN regulation in keratinocyte differentiation and proliferation.

## Introduction

During the process of epidermal differentiation, keratinocyte-specific genes are differentially expressed at distinct layers of the epidermis. Bullous pemphigoid antigens are hemidesmosomal proteins, essential for anchoring the epidermal basal cells to the underlying basement membrane. Two bullous pemphigoid antigens, the 230-kDa and 180-kDa proteins, have been characterized, and the corresponding genes/proteins are known as BPAG1 and BPAG2, respectively (2). These proteins are also recognized as autoantigens in bullous pemphigoid, a group of blistering skin diseases (3–5). BPAG1 belongs to the plakin gene family, and it facilitates the association of keratin intermediate filaments to the hemidesmosomes, critical attachment complexes at the basal keratinocyte/dermo–epidermal basement membrane interface (2,6). BPAG1 is an intracellular protein specifically expressed in undifferentiated proliferating keratinocytes. The 180-kDa bullous pemphigoid antigen (BPAG2) is a hemidesmosomal transmembrane protein with a collagenous ectodomain; consequently, it is also known as type XVII collagen (7). This protein interacts with laminin 5, an anchoring filament protein in the extracellular compartment of the cutaneous basement membrane, and with plectin, an adhesive hemidesmosomal protein in the intracellular milieu of the basal keratinocytes (8). Mutations in the BPAG2 gene result in an autosomal recessive blistering disorder, generalized atrophic benign epidermolysis bullosa, attesting to the critical importance of this protein in stability of the dermal–epidermal junction (9).

Interferons (IFNs) exhibit pleiotropic biological effects, such as antiviral activity, regulation of cellular proliferation and differentiation, and modulation of the immune responses (10). It is well established that IFNs regulate the expression of a variety of genes in tissues through a process dependent on JAK–STAT-signaling pathway (11,12). In case of type 1 ( and ) IFNs, activation of TYK-2 and <sup>a b</sup>JAK-1 tyrosine kinases, through binding of IFNs to specific cell-surface receptors, induces phosphorylation of STAT1 (p91), STAT1 (p84), and STAT2 <sup>a b</sup>(p113). These proteins and a 48-kDa DNA-binding protein, p48, form a multiprotein complex, IFN stimulatory gene factor 3, ISGF3, which binds to the IFN-stimulated responsive element, ISRE (13). As a result, type 1 IFNs induce the expression of IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs). In addition to ISGF3, other proteins that belong to the same family of proteins as p48 also bind to the ISRE and include IFN regulatory factors 1–3 (IRF-1–3) and IFN consensus sequence-binding protein (ICSBP) (14–16).

Binding of IFN to its specific receptor results <sup>g</sup>in activation of JAK1 and JAK2 tyrosine kinases which then elicits homodimerization of STAT1 . <sup>a</sup>Expression of IFN responsive genes is induced by <sup>g</sup>binding of this complex to IFN -activating site <sup>g</sup>(GAS) which was first identified as a distinct regulatory element that overlaps with the ISRE in the guanylate-binding protein (GBP) gene (17,18). In epidermal keratinocytes, IFN inhibits cell growth <sup>g</sup>and induces terminal differentiation by up-regulating expression of differentiation-specific genes, which include transglutaminase type 1, involucrin, and a number of proteins involved in the formation of the cornified envelope (19,20). On the other hand, in psoriasis vulgaris, an inflammatory skin disease, there is evidence to suggest that psoriatic keratinocytes have an abnormal response to IFN , because <sup>g</sup>HLA-DR expression is seen infrequently in psoriatic keratinocytes or not at all in psoriatic plaque despite the presence of Th1 cells (21,22). IFN has also <sup>g</sup>an attenuated antiproliferative effect on psoriatic keratinocytes (23). These findings suggest that IFN <sup>g</sup>may elicit aberrant differentiation of keratinocytes in inflammatory skin diseases, such as psoriasis vulgaris, although the molecular mechanisms have not yet been elucidated in detail.

We have previously reported that IFN inhibits <sup>g</sup>the expression of BPAG1 at the transcriptional level in cultured human keratinocytes (24). Because down-regulation of BPAG1 expression is one of the earliest events during epidermal differentiation, the molecules involved in inhibition of the BPAG1 expression by IFN may play a critical <sup>g</sup>role in normal and aberrant differentiation and proliferation of the epidermal keratinocytes. In the present study, we have analyzed the molecular mechanisms of IFN -dependent transcriptional <sup>g</sup>inhibition of the BPAG1 gene expression.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture

Normal human epidermal keratinocytes (NHEK) were cultured in keratinocyte growth medium (KGM), containing epidermal growth factor (0.1 g/ml), bovine pituitary extract (0.1 g/l), <sup>m m</sup>insulin (0.5 mg/ml), and hydrocortisone (0.5 mg/ml) (Clonetics Corp., San Diego, CA, USA), at 37-C in 5% CO -air atmosphere. Cultures were passaged by trypsinization and studied in passage 2.

## Development of 50-deletion BPAG1 promotor/ luciferase reporter gene constructs

The 317 bp 50-flanking fragment, extending from 215 to 101 of the BPAG1 promoter relative to the transcriptional start site (<sub>þ</sub>1), was inserted into the promotorless luciferase reporter gene vector pGEM-luc (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). The 50-deletion constructs, designated as pBP215luc, pBP177luc, pBP147luc, pBP89luc, and pBP58luc, were generated by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using sense primers corresponding to BPAG1 sequences 215 to 195, 177 to 157, 147 to 127, 89 to 69, and 58 to 38, with the addition of a HindIII site at the 50 end, and an antisense primer corresponding to BPAG1 sequences from 101 to 81, with the addition of a BamHI site at the 50 end. A plasmid pBP2.6CAT (5), which contains 2.6-kb BPAG1 promoter, was used as template DNA for PCR amplification. PCR products were digested with HindIII and BamHI and ligated into HindIII and BamHI sites of pGEM-luc plasmid. The pTKL construct was generated by subcloning herpes simplex thymidine kinase (TK) promoter into the pGEM-luc. pIGIE-TK luc construct was generated by subcloning BPAG1 fragment extending from 79 to 59, referred as IGIE, upstream of the TK promoter in the pTKL construct.

## Site-directed mutagenesis of IFN inhibitory element

In the BPAG1 promoter region of plasmid pBP215L, the potential IFN inhibitory element, IGIE, 50-AGTTATCAGCATATTAT-<sup>g</sup>TTGC-30, was mutated to generate the following constructs:

1. IGIEm1: 50-AGGGATCAGCATATTATTT GC-3;

2. IGIEm2: 50-AGTTATCAGCATAGGATTT GC-30;

3. IGIEm3: 50-AGTTATCAGCATATTAGGT GC-30.

Gene Editor<sup>TM</sup> in vitro site-directed mutagenesis system (Promega) was employed; the mutated dinucleotide sequences are underlined. The sequences of all cloned constructs were verified by nucleotide sequencing.

## Transient transfection and luciferase assays

Transfection of plasmid DNA into NHEK was performed by DOTAP liposomal transfection reagent (Roche Molecular Systems, Inc., Nutley, NJ, USA). Five microgram of the promotor/luc constructs were used in transient transfection of NHEK (6-cm diameter culture dish) cultured in KGM medium with or without 100 U/ml human recombinant IFN <sup>g</sup>(Roche Applied Science). Twenty-four hours after transfection, cells were washed twice in PBS, dissolved in 300 l of <sup>m</sup>cell lysis reagent (Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Osaka, Japan) and harvested by scraping. Luciferase activity was determined by using luciferase assay system (PicaGene; Wako Pure Chemical Industries) and a luminometer (Lumat LB9501; Berthold Technology, Bad Wildbad, Germany). Luciferase activity was measured in the mixture of 20- l cell <sup>m</sup>extract and 100- l luminary base. Transfection efficiency of <sup>m</sup>each experiment was normalized by -galactosidase assay, and <sup>b</sup>each value of the luciferase assay was evaluated by mean of duplicated experiments. The same experiments were performed at least twice to confirm the results.

## Results

## Functional analysis of BPAG1 promoter and localization of interferon- inhibitory element, IGIE

We have previously identified a novel cis-element, keratinocyte responsive element 3 (KRE3), at position 215 to 197 of the BPAG1 gene promoter (24,25). This element is essential for tissue-specific expression of BPAG1 in epidermal keratinocytes. To determine whether KRE3 is involved in transcriptional inhibition of BPAG1 promoter by IFN , pBP215luc and pBP195luc were transiently <sup>g</sup>transfected into NHEK pretreated or untreated with IFN for 24 h. Cells were harvested and cell <sup>g</sup>extracts were analyzed for luciferase activity. As shown in Fig. 1, IFN -treated NHEK transfected <sup>g</sup>with pBP215luc showed approximately 90% inhibition of luciferase activity compared with untreated NHEK. On the other hand, pBP195luc construct, which contains the region from 196 to 101 of the BPAG1 gene and is devoid of KRE3, showed markedly reduced luciferase activity as compared with pBP215luc at basal promoter activity without IFN . However, the activity of <sup>g</sup>pBP195luc decreased by IFN (Fig. 1). These <sup>g</sup>results suggest that KRE3 confers a high basal level of expression but may play a less critical, perhaps ancillary, role in the down-regulation of BPAG1 gene expression by IFN .

<sup>g</sup>To identify the regulatory cis-elements necessary for IFN -dependent inhibition of BPAG1 <sup>g</sup>gene expression at transcriptional level, four additional deletion constructs were developed and transfected into NHEK pretreated with or without IFN for 24 h. As shown in Fig. 1, each <sup>g</sup>construct containing sequences upstream of 59 of the BPAG1 promoter (i.e. constructs 177, 147, and 89) was down-regulated by IFN . <sup>g</sup>However, deletion of the sequences to 58 resulted in further reduction of basal promoter activity and loss of IFN responsiveness (Fig. 1). <sup>g</sup>These results suggested that a major IFN inhi-<sup>g</sup>bitory element, referred to as IGIE, is localized within the 89 to 58 segment of the promoter region of the BPAG1 gene.

## ISRE/GAS chimeric element is essential for downregulation of the BPAG1 gene by IFN

Homology analysis of the sequences within the 89 to 58 segment of the BPAG1 promoter revealed that the region from 79 to 59 consists of a chimeric sequence which contains ISRE with an insertion of a 7-bp segment corresponding to partial sequence of GBP–GAS (guanylate-binding protein–IFN -activating site) (Fig. 2). We <sup>g</sup>designated this sequence as IGIE. To test whether this region can respond to IFN in a <sup>g</sup>context independent of the BPAG1 gene, we cloned the region from 79 to 59 upstream of the heterologous herpes simplex virus TK promoter in pTKL construct. Control or IFN -trea-<sup>g</sup>ted NHEK were then transfected with pIGIE-TKL and tested for luciferase activity. As shown in Fig. 3, IFN -treated NHEK which <sup>g</sup>were transfected with pIGIE-TKL construct exhibited 90% less luciferase activity than cells not treated with IFN . IFN treatment of <sup>g g</sup>NHEK transfected with the basic pTKL construct did not alter luciferase activity as compared with untreated cells (Fig. 3). These results clearly demonstrate that the sequence from 79 to 59 of the BPAG1 gene can confer IFN <sup>g</sup>responsiveness to a heterologous promoter, which in itself is not inhibited by IFN . ## ISRE sequence, but not GAS, in IGIE is responsible for the suppression of BPAG1 promoter activity by IFN

To identify the discrete nucleotide sequences of functional importance in IFN response within <sup>g</sup>the IGIE, we generated TT to GG transversion substitutions in the ${ 5 } ^ { \prime }$ ISRE segment, in the central GBP-GAS sequence, and in the 30 ISRE segment in the IGIE of pBP215luc construct (Fig. 4). The mutations in $5 ^ { \bar { \prime } }$ ISRE (construct IGIEm1) showed a marked, approximately 80% reduction of basal promoter activity and similarly, the mutation in $3 ^ { \prime }$ ISRE (construct IGIEm3) showed a approximately 85% reduction of basal promoter activity, while disruption of the GAS sequence (construct IGIEm2) had minimal (14%) effect (Fig. 4). Treatment of wild-type promoter construct with IFN resulted in 82% loss of promoter activity <sup>g</sup>(Fig. 4). Similar treatment of constructs IGIEm1 and IGIEm3 resulted in attenuated response, 56 and 44% inhibition, respectively, while treatment of the construct IGIEm2 by IFN resulted in 85% <sup>g</sup>inhibition. Collectively, these results indicate that the both 50-and 30-ISRE segments are essential for expression of the BPAG1 gene in keratinocytes, and they function as IGIE elements responsible, at least in part, for the IFN effect.

## Discussion

The present study was designed to identify potential IFN responsive cis-elements responsible for

IFN -elicited suppression of the expression of the <sup>g</sup>BPAG1 gene. Using a series of $5 ^ { \prime }$ deletion constructs of the BPAG1 promoter, we found that the regulatory element responsible for the IFN <sup>g</sup>effect resides within 89 bp upstream from the transcription start site of the BPAG1 gene. Homology analysis revealed the lack of contiguous ISRE and GAS consensus sequences in the BPAG1 promoter, but instead, the region from 79 to 59 consisted of a unique chimeric element containing ISRE consensus but interrupted by insertion of a 7-bp partial GAS sequence. This sequence, designated as IGIE, was shown to successfully mediate down-regulation of heterologous TK promoter activity in IFN -treated NHEK using luciferase <sup>g</sup>reporter gene transfection assays. These finding indicate that the novel ISRE/GAS chimeric motif plays a role as IGIE in BPAG1 expression. Furthermore, using mutated constructs of the BPAG1 promoter, we found that disruption of ISRE sequences, but not the GAS sequence, markedly suppressed BPAG1 promoter activity in the absence of IFN . In addition, the IFN respon-<sup>g</sup>siveness was attenuated when either $5 ^ { \prime }$ <sup>g</sup>or the 30 sequences of ISRE were mutated, while mutations in GAS sequence had no effect on the IFN <sup>g</sup>response. Collectively, these findings indicate that the region homologous to ISRE within IGIE participates in maintenance of the BPAG1 promoter basal activity as well as in its transcriptional responsiveness to IFN .

<sup>g</sup>Although many of the important biological responses elicited by IFN are mediated through <sup>g</sup>induction of cellular genes, this cytokine also exerts inhibitory effects on the expression of a number of genes, including those for IL-8, collagenase, stromelysin, c-fos, 1(I) collagen, 2(I) collagen, colony stimulating factor-1, macrophage scavenger receptor A (SR-A), and 3(VI) collagen (26–33).

With the exception of the SR-A gene, little is known about the specific cis-elements within these genes that mediate the IFN effects, and the <sup>g</sup>precise mechanisms underlying these inhibitory responses have not been definitively established. In the case of IL-8, 1(I) collagen, 2(I) collagen, <sup>a a</sup>and stromelysin, the inhibitory response is exerted at the level of transcription, but GAS consensus sequence does not exist in these target promoters.

In case of 1(I) collagen, it has been postulated <sup>a</sup>that transcriptional inhibition by IFN may be <sup>g</sup>mediated through the interaction of transacting factors with non-classical IFN -response elements, <sup>g</sup>such as NF B or AP-1 cognate-binding sites (34). <sup>k</sup>It has also been reported that down-regulation of stromelysin gene expression by IFN occurs <sup>g</sup>almost exclusively via the AP-1 site (27). Furthermore, the results of Horvai et al. (1997) (35) suggest that the expression of the SR-A gene in human fibroblasts occurs through the ability of IFN-induced and IFN-activated STAT1 to com-<sup>a</sup>pete with AP-1/ets transcription factors. Our results showed the ISRE/GAS chimeric sequence, IGIEl, and not AP-1 or AP-1-like sites, is responsible for down-regulation of the BPAG1 promoter by IFN . Although we did not identify the tran-<sup>g</sup>scriptional factor which binds IGIE region, we hypothesized that members of the STAT and/or IRF family of proteins bind the IGIE region and inhibit the expression of the BPAG1 gene, one of the earliest events during epidermal differentiation, and eventually induces cell differentiation in epidermal keratinocytes. Recently, Asao and Fu (2000) (36) reported that IFN has dual potential <sup>g</sup>in inhibiting or promoting cell proliferation. However, in most cases, because both STAT1 and IRF-1 suppress the growth promoting potential, IFN induces cell differentiation, thus lending <sup>g</sup>support to the above hypothesis.

Because our previous study indicated that de novo protein synthesis is required for the IFN - <sup>g</sup>dependent inhibition of BPAG1 gene expression (24), we can speculate that the inhibition of BPAG1 transcription by IFN is due to the induc-<sup>g</sup>tion or activation of selective inhibitory factors by IFN , such as STAT1 and IRF-1. In this context, <sup>g a</sup>we recently reported another IFN -related ele-<sup>g</sup>ment, which functions as IRF-binding motif, at region distal to IGIE (from 135 to 123) in the BPAG1 promoter region (37). That study suggested that IRFs induced by IFN stimulation in <sup>g</sup>cultured normal human keratinocytes may also contribute to the down-regulation of the BPAG1 expression by IFN through IRFs binding to the motif, suggesting that relatively complex coordination exists for BPAG1 gene regulation by IFN . Although further study to identify particular <sup>g</sup>binding protein(s) to IGIE would be expected to disclose precise molecular events in further detail, the results presented in this study provide novel insight into the mechanisms of keratinocyte gene expression, indicating that IFN is a potential indu-<sup>g</sup>cer of epidermal differentiation as reflected by downregulation of basal cell-specific genes including BPAG1. These studies may have relevance to our understanding of aberrant epidermal differentiation in inflammatory skin diseases, such as psoriasis.
