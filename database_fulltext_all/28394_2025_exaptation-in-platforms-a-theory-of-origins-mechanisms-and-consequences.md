# Exaptation in Platforms: A Theory of Origins, Mechanisms, and Consequences

Abstract. We recognize information technology (IT) evolution’s importance but have barely broached its underlying mechanisms. We return to IT evolution’s root metaphor in evolutionary biology to identify two mechanisms: gradual refinement (“adaptation”) and unforeseen functionality (“exaptation”). The latter is ubiquitous, yet unrecognized. IT exaptation is novel app functionality emerging from interacting with evolving technological and competitive environments. Adaptation refines, but exaptation metamorphoses apps. Real options embedded in app design fuel exaptation—our theory’s central mechanism. Data from 625 AndroidOS apps spanning nine years and 63 countries support our theory. We introduce exaptation as adaptation’s evolutionary counterpart and link it to real options, explaining how app design choices drive market performance and sustain userbase.

## 1. Introduction

Evolution is a root metaphor in information systems (IS).<sup>1</sup> Faster-evolving apps thrive, as each update probes users’ fickle needs, abates imitation, and harnesses technology advances (Tiwana 2018, Wang et al. 2018, James et al. 2023). Organizational systems striving to maintain business relevance and avoid obsolescence share this evolutionary imperative. Despite its centrality, we don’t understand information technology (IT) evolution’s underlying mechanisms—the “how.” We’re missing theoretical pathways connecting systems’ properties like malleability and modularity (Kallinikos et al. 2013, Tiwana 2015, Priego and Wareham 2023) to outcomes and into how unforeseen IT evolution unfolds (Henfridsson and Bygstad 2013, Cennamo et al. 2018).

This theoretical gap is particularly visible in how apps respond to external stimuli. Platform innovations (speech interfaces, artificial intelligence (AI) application programming interfaces (APIs)), third-party advances (GitHub’s Copilot), and hardware breakthroughs (brain implants and bone-conduction acoustics) can unpredictably advance apps’ functionality and use cases. These stimuli proliferate in platforms involving thousands of firms (Andriani and Cattani 2024), where flexibility—a core tenet of real options thinking paradoxically overlooked in platforms theory—forebodes survival. This observation raises our central question: How do real options in a system’s design shape competitive outcomes? This matters because flexibility demands upfront effort— overdoing it forfeits opportunities, whereas shortchan ging it jeopardizes evolvability. Without understanding mechanisms, our grasp of how IT evolution unfolds is rudimentary.

To address this question, we return to IT evolution’s natural sciences roots that inspired Simon (1962). Evolution, which biologists call “aptation,” occurs through two mechanisms: gradual refinements (“adaptation”) and emergence of entirely new abilities from interactions with the environment (“exaptation”) (Barve and Wagne 2013). Recent evolutionary biology research underscores exaptation’s significance as an evolutionary mechanism, showing how it produces innovation through regulatory rewiring, rather than building entirely new systems from scratch (Swift et al. 2024). Although IS scholars recognize adaptation, they overlook exaptation, despite its ubiquity—Kazaa transformed into Microsoft Teams, UNIX powers Apple’s WatchOS, and Progressive Snapshot pioneered behavior-based insurance. Exaptation is the rapid, creative repurposing of app attributes— stimulated by evolving technological and competitive environments—that metamorphoses its evolutionary trajectory. This mechanism goes unnoticed precisely because it’s so pervasive. Perhaps this constancy of systems being “perpetually in the making” causes us to not even notice it (Kallinikos et al. 2013).

Whereas biologists’ windows span millennia and science historians’ centuries, ours for apps is a handful of years. They inferred exaptation retrospectively, but we’re actively immersed in its unfolding. We observe apps gradually adapting and also gaining strikingly new functionality over time. Yet, without a theoretica concept to distinguish the latter as exaptation, we conflate both under the big umbrella of IT evolution. As a nameless concept, exaptation has eluded theorizing. We conceptualize IT exaptation from first-principles, distinguishing it from IT adaptation and IS generativity. We situate this mechanism within established IS theory, showing how it bolsters competitive outcomes above and beyond adaptation over an array of time horizons.

Using nine years of data from 625 AndroidOS apps spanning 63 countries, we show how real options bolster competitive outcomes through IT exaptation. Our theory makes two novel contributions. First, we introduce IT exaptation as a novel evolutionary mechanism distinct from adaptation, through which systems’ design shapes competitive outcomes. Whereas adaptation refines, exaptation redirects evolutionary trajectories. Second, we trace exaptation’s origins to real options shaped by app developers’ reasoned design choices. Subsequent sections develop these ideas, methods, analyses, and our contributions and implications.

2.1. Exaptation as a Mechanism for IT Evolution 2.1.1. Origins of the Exaptation Concept in Evolutionary Biology. The idea of IT evolution has its roots in evolutionary biology, where aptation describes how organisms evolve (Gould and Vrba 1982). Two mechanisms— adaptation (gradual refinement of existing traits) and exaptation (repurposing existing traits for new functions)—together realize aptation. Simply put, aptation � adaptation + exaptation.

Exaptation—adaptation’s lesser-known counterpart— enables innovation in biological systems by co-opting a trait beyond its original use (Gould and Vrba 1982). It arises from currently unused traits, where a richer pool of such traits enlarges exaptation possibilities (Barve and Wagner 2013). Whereas adaptation refines traits for existing purposes, exaptation involves the serendipitous discovery of new functions for them (Gould and Vrba 1982). Bird feathers exemplify both mechanisms: Their exaptation from thermoregulation to flight differs from their adaptive thickening in cold climates. This biological principle of “teaching old traits new tricks” through rewiring of existing elements rather than creating entirely new ones (Swift et al. 2024) parallels how IT systems achieve new functionality through reconfiguration rather than building anew. Adaptation follows exaptation to refine traits for their exapted use (Gould and Vrba 1982; Johnson 2011, p. 154), a causal sequence captured in exaptation’s synonym “preadaptation” (Cattani 2005, Aversa et al. 2022). Thus, adaptation always follows exaptation, but adaptation can occur without exaptation.

2.1.2. Exaptation of Artifacts in the History of Science. Science historians expanded exaptation beyond biology to explain co-option of mechanical artifacts for unforeseen uses (Johnson 2011). An artifact’s interaction with its changing environment spurs exaptation, revealing potential functionality and new use cases (Garud et al. 2016, Andriani and Cattani 2024). This triggers repurposing, tinkering, and modification in ways unobvious at conception (Andriani and Kaminska 2021, Aversa et al. 2022).

In arguing that creators couldn’t foresee subsequent uses, they injected human agency that was never in biology’s original conception (e.g., Cattani 2005, Andriani and Cattani 2024). Like in biology, adapta tion follows exaptation to refine the artifact for its new use (Johnson 2011, p. 31; Andriani and Kaminska 2021; Aversa et al. 2022). Examples—all non-IT— include French weaving looms exapted into Babbage’s punch cards in the 1870s, German wine presses into Gutenberg’s printing press in the 1430s, coal waste into modern chemicals, Ikea stools into swings, and Viagra’s off-label use as an anti-Alzheimer’s drug (Johnson 2011, pp. 153–157; Andriani et al. 2017). Yet, neither biologists’ nor historians’ conceptions work for IT artifacts, which are unlike organisms or mechanical artifacts.<sup>2</sup> But, they suffice to infer exaptation’s theoretical first-principles to us to build on.

## 2.2. A Conceptualization of IT Exaptation from First-Principles

We can’t simply transplant exaptation wholesale from creatures or mechanical artifacts to IT artifacts. Instead, we start from first-principles, so it’s qualitatively compatible with biologists and historians, yet distinctive to IT artifacts. Following Frenken’s (2006) analogical mapping of parallels between biology and technology, we derive IT exaptation from two first-principles: what is exapted and the environment that triggers it.

For biologists, an organism’s trait is exapted; for historians, an artifact’s use; for IT apps, their equivalent is functionality and features. Like biologists, but unlike science historians, whether an exaptation was unforeseen by its creator is inconsequential. Such foresight was not an element of Gould and Vrba’s (1982) original concept.<sup>3</sup> The second first-principle is the concept of the environment, the interaction with which stimulates exaptation. The environment—literal habitat for biologists and societal context for historians— translates to IT apps’ competitive and technological environment. Apps’ competitive environment encompasses users (with their emerging needs and behaviors) and rival apps (with their advancing features). The technological environment encompasses platform resources (e.g., Siri APIs, biometrics, AI image recognition in iOS) and third-party technical resources (e.g., multiplatform coding tools, AI programming aids such as GitHub’s Copilot, or outside software development kits (SDKs)).

An app’s interactions with this changing environment reveal new possibilities, ideas, and use cases. IT artifacts are increasingly an evolving amalgamation of proprie tary code with such external ingredients (Kallinikos et al. 2013, Huang et al. 2022), where new elements’ arrival expands exaptation-stimulating combinatorial possibilities. Their blending with app code delivers different functionality or uses, while keeping an exapted app’s core intact. The rapidity with which this is accomplished is crucial, given platforms’ rapid innovation cycles.

Building on this synthesis, we define IT exaptation as the rapidity with which an IT artifact realizes new features, functionality, and capabilities from interacting with its evolving technological and competitive environment. This echoes science historians’ emphasis on emergent functionality in existing artifacts (Johnson 2011, Andriani and Cattani 2024), while anchoring on rapidity that’s crucial in platforms’ compressed timeframes (Huang et al. 2022, James et al. 2023).

Prior studies conceptualize IT evolution as an aggregate outcome (Tiwana 2015, James et al. 2023)—arguably resulting from upstream adaptation and exaptation mechanisms operating together. This totality of apta tion’s effects neither articulates nor demarcates the underlying mechanisms, which we attribute to exaptation’s absence from our theoretical vocabulary. We’re handicapped for a concept to describe what we observe (Tufte 2020, p. 137). We directly observe exaptation daily on our phones, yet not having a theoretical concept to describe this evolutionary pathway constrains our capacity to mentally grasp it. Passing references to emerging functions, unanticipated behaviors, and features untraceable in IT artifacts’ code hint at this unnamed phenomenon. However, we must clearly demarcate IT exaptation from related concepts like IT adaptation and IS generativity.

2.2.1. Exaptation vs. Adaptation. Table 2 demarcates the conceptually adjacent idea of IT adaptation from IT exaptation in their emphasis, driver, and unfolding. Adaptation gradually refines existing functionality for known user needs. In contrast, exaptation moves apps into Kauffman’s (2002, p. 15) “adjacent possible”—a realm of new possibilities and use cases.

Their drivers differ: Selection pressures and platform changes drive adaptation, whereas interactions with the changing environment stimulate exaptation (Aversa et al. 2022). For example, adaptation occurs when app users expect it to support new platform features or external services, prompting code refinements. Exaptation emerges when exposure to the wider environment—interactions with users, evolving rivals, and platform innovations—reveals unforeseen use cases. We’ll illustrate how platform innovations, such as voice recognition, image analysis, handwriting recognition, and Apple Intelligence APIs in iOS, sparked tinkering, creative modification, and novel uses for existing apps. Similarly, user feedback, ideas from rivals, or a serendipitous epiphany from observing user behavior can instigate repurposing an IT artifact in unexpected ways. Exaptation capitalizes on intrinsic properties of the artifact itself, emerging from within by mobilizing previously unused or repurposed attributes. Both mechanisms are emergent, though adaptation follows a fluid “roadmap,” whereas exaptation is more opportunistic (Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2019, p. 1334). In essence, adaptation refines an IT artifact, whereas exaptation redirects its evolutionary trajectory.

Exaptation triggers subsequent adaptation. A newly exapted app requires adaptive refinement and finetuning, as its repurposed functionality rarely suits its new use perfectly (Andriani et al. 2017). Whereas exaptation drives additional adaptation, adaptation can occur without exaptation. This temporal sequence implies that exaptation is nomologically upstream from adaptation. Although this causal relationship creates subtle overlaps where exaptation ends and adaptation begins, it does not diminish their distinction’s value. Accordingly, our models account for aptation before bringing in exaptation.

2.2.2. Exaptation vs. Generativity. IS generativity—an IT artifact’s capacity to produce new behavior without involving its originators—comes conceptually closest to IT exaptation (Henfridsson and Bygstad 2013, Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2023). Generativity broadly entails expanding what an IT artifact does (Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2023).

Although both mechanisms expand functionality in emergent ways, they differ fundamentally in their actors and fodder, as Table 3 summarizes. Generativity stems from autonomous, third-party modifications through open interfaces or open-source code (Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2023, p. 1688), whereas exaptation occurs when developers creatively repurpose existing system attributes in response to environmental interactions. Generativity’s fodder is released code or open interfaces for modification by outsiders, whereas exaptation’s fodder is intrinsic system attributes leveraged by developers. This distinction in agency is crucial—generativity distributes innovation agency to external, third-party actors without involving the original developers, whereas exaptation preserves developer agency. This is plausibly why IS generativity predominantly appears in platform contexts, where outside developers use open interfaces and APIs to create platform-augmenting apps and extensions. Generativity thus constitutes an observed evolutionary outcome, rather than a causal mechanism, with either adaptation or exaptation as its underlying driving mechanisms. Heeding Tufte (2020, p. 137) that old labels distort new concepts, choosing exaptation over generativity avoids preconceived connotations.

## 2.4. Real Options as Origins of an App’s Exaptability

Real options theory—our theoretical foundation— balances potential gains against losses under uncertainty (Fichman 2004). A real option is a discretionary right to do something—either operationally or strategically—in the future (Trigeorgis and Reuer 2017). An IT artifact’s design can simultaneously incorporate multiple types of real options (Fichman 2004). Strategic options represent the degree to which an app’s initial design provides an infrastructural foundation for its future development. Operational options permit five types of midcourse flexibility in an app’s trajectory in Table 5: implementing functionality incrementally as a series of small, independent projects (stage option); expanding or reducing allocated resources (scale); swapping technological building blocks (switch); delaying investment without imperiling market opportunities (defer); and salvaging value if terminated (abandon).

IT implementation studies once widely used this lens, but IS platform studies rarely do. This oversight is particularly consequential in platforms, where app developers’ past choices constrain their responses to unforeseeable technological and market changes. Such real options are more granular than IS concepts like malleability or plasticity, or “IT affordances,” which conflate causes with effects and exclude the artifact’s creator.<sup>4</sup>

2.4.1. How Real Options Enable IT Exaptation. Despite concerns about the theoretical silence about the origins of malleability-endowing attributes (Huang et al. 2022), real options in an IT artifact’s design represent currently unused attributes that confer exaptive potential by providing flexibility and raw materials, without which alertness is worthless (Garud et al. 2016). Exaptation’s distinctive feature is this potential residing intrinsically in the artifact itself (Andriani and Kaminska 2021).

In plain sight are subtle pointers to real options present in IT artifacts’ design. For example, IS scholars urge judging systems by “what they will become,” allude to their “architectural potential,” and note “shadow options awaiting recognition” (Fichman 2004, Benaroch and Goldstein 2009). Although many apps inherit common options from their platform, the ones unique to some apps’ design differentiate their exaptive potential. These latent options become actionable when artifacts interact with their environment (Andriani and Cattani 2024). For example, platform innovations accessible via APIs, rival apps’ features, or newly observed user problems can reveal new possibilities in existing apps, surfacing unforeseen use cases and functionality that exaptation then realizes.

2.4.2. Operational Options. Operational options bake flexibility into an app’s design, enabling it to capitalize on epiphanies about emergent possibilities. They enlarge the pool of unused attributes that are ingredi ents for exaptation (the “exaptive fodder” of Garud et al. 2016). Each incremental step informs the next (Fichman 2004), expanding the adjacent possible. For example, developers might probe user reactions with limited feature releases (\~stage), substitute internal code with a new platform API (\~switch), reduce investments until the potential of a new use case is clearer (\~scale; defer), or repurpose code in an adjacent domain (\~abandon). Such operational flexibility expands an app’s exaptive space—an untheorized evolutionary pathway. Therefore, operational options allow developers to rapidly explore new uses of existing apps through small, experimental tweaks.

Hypothesis 1(a). Operational real options embedded in an app enhance IT exaptation.

2.4.3. Strategic Options. Unlike operational options that aid ongoing development, strategic options provide an infrastructural foundation for long-term future development (Fichman 2004). They’re footholds into markets nonexistent at an app’s inception. The full array of opportunities unlocked by an app emerges over time. For example, the Starbucks Card introduced in 1999 simply to speed lines became the foundation for 25 years of unforeseeable follow-on projects culminating in its mobile app generating over 30% (\$11 billion) of its annual revenue. Strategic options enlarge exaptive potential in adjacencies around that infrastructural foundation, and apps without them lack the prerequisite to pursue such emergent opportunities. Exercising strategic options exaptively is making a foothold move—requiring the foothold to exist in place first. Therefore, exaptation exercises a strategic real option. We therefore expect strategic options to enhance the degree to which an app rapidly realizes new features and functionality exaptively.

## 2.5. Competitive Consequences of IT Exaptation

Exaptation delivers novel features and enhanced functionality to end-users—the basis for app competition (James et al. 2023). Our two focal competitive consequences derive from the IS platforms literature. The first is an app’s market performance, defined as the market’s lifetime assessment of its quality that integrates the buzz surrounding it (James et al. 2023). The second, sustained userbase, is its lifetime count of users who adopted it minus those who subsequently abandoned it—a crucial indicator of growth in platform competition (Cennamo et al. 2018, Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2023). The exaptive delivery of novel features and functionality improves users’ judgments of the app relative to rival apps and helps attract new users while retaining existing ones. We therefore expect exaptation to improve an app’s market performance and sustained userbase.

Hypothesis 2. IT exaptation improves an app’s competitive performance.

## 3. Methods

We collected primary and archival data from 625 independent app developers in the AndroidOS ecosystem over nine years to test our hypotheses, as part of a multiyear research program. Despite being smaller, narrowly-focused systems, apps offer three distinct research advantages. First, apps’ speedier evolution permits studying the exaptation phenomenon—which can take many decades to unfold elsewhere (Andriani and Cattani 2024)—on a tractable timeline of nine years. Second, AndroidOS provides an ideal research context with its dominant global market share (87%), extensive user base (3.3 billion), and diverse ecosystem (3 million apps).<sup>5</sup> Its low entry barriers, rapid innovation, and diverse price segments create a fiercely competitive environment. Third, apps in a given platform share an evolving technological environment where innovation by a multitude of participants is pervasive, thus creating the conditions for exaptation to emerge, yet simultaneously controlling for cross-platform confounds. This empirical setting thus provides a petri dish for studying IT exaptation.

## 3.1. Data Collection

Because systems’ richest aspects resist objective measurement (Smith 1981, p. 358), we combined primary survey data from 625 independent AndroidOS app developers with nearly a decade of archival data on the timeline in Figure 2. We collected perceptual measures from apps’ lead developers, gathering predictor data in 2015 (t<sub>1</sub>) and objective dependent variables at roughly two-year intervals to assess short-run (t<sub>2</sub>), long-run (t<sub>3</sub>), and sustained app performance (t<sub>4</sub>). This allowed us to assess how real options, through exaptation, shape competitive outcomes across multiple time horizons.

We randomly sampled 10,000 paid apps from Google’s Android app store, excluding games. After removing apps with fewer than two reviews and limiting the sample to one app per developer to ensure independence, we retained 4,464 apps. Our key informant was each app’s lead developer in AndroidOS records, who we contacted with our survey instrument. This resulted in a 15.7% response rate, with no evidence of nonresponse bias.<sup>6</sup>

## 3.2. Measures

Our unit of analysis is an app. We used seven-point multi-item Likert scales for our predictors (primary data) and time-lagged archival data from Google’s AndroidOS app store for our dependent variables. All measures—mostly adapted from prior studies to the AndroidOS context—appear in Appendix A.

3.2.1. Development of a New IT Exaptation Measure. We developed a new app-level psychometric measure for IT exaptation. We built on the scant literature on exaptation of man-made artifacts (Cattani 2005, Garud et al. 2016, Andriani et al. 2017) and proximate IS constructs involving emergent, unforeseen expansion of systems’ functionality by developers. Preliminary items focused on features and functionality emerging after app introduction, without stipulating that they be unforeseen (see Section 2). We focused on what transpired in app releases, reflecting users’ comparisons with contemporary rivals and prior app versions.

Our item pool was unique to IT apps, artifactcentric like we see in the history of science literature (e.g., Andriani and Kaminska 2021), but avoided its “unforeseen by its creator” addition absent in its originating conception in evolutionary biology. Our item pool thus preserved exaptation’s core theoretical firstprinciples. We anchored our items in an AndroidOS app, focusing on what changed in an app (features, functionality, and capabilities) and its competitive and technological stimuli. We iteratively refined the items with nine AndroidOS developers and five academic experts to ensure clarity and theoretical rigor.

Our refinement produced a robust six-item exaptation measure (α 0.94; loadings $\ge ~ 0 . 7 4$ (x 0.83) and cross-loadings ≤ 0.2 (x 0.06) in Appendix B). The first three items tapped into the degree to which new releases of the app realized new features, functionality, and capabilities. Because its interaction with its competitive environment defined by rivals and endusers is essential to exaptation (Cattani 2005, Garud et al. 2016, Andriani et al. 2017), the other three items tapped into the app’s new versions building on user feedback, meeting their evolving needs, and matching rival apps’ features.<sup>7</sup> The scale anchors capture how rapidly exaptation occurred, reflecting the cruciality of speed in apps’ competitive actions (Huang et al. 2022, James et al. 2023) and how pervasive imitation (Wang et al. 2018) shortens the window for exaptations to differentiate. Measuring rapidity, rather than just occurrence of new functions, likewise aligns with Andriani and Cattani’s (2024) emphasis on quick recognition of new uses for existing functionalities through environmental interactions. This rapidity-anchored operationalization is essential for three reasons: First, platform competition’s compressed timeframes make speed of functional transformation crucial for survival (Huang et al. 2022). Second, unlike biological exaptation spanning millennia, apps must capitalize on environmental shifts in shorter windows before rivals imitate innovations (Wang et al. 2018). Third, rapidity captures the core theoretical distinction of exaptation from other evolutionary pathways—developers recognizing and realizing new functionality through environmental interaction.

Real options adapted IT project measures to the AndroidOS context (Tiwana et al. 2007). ## 3.4. Rival Explanations

We first separately controlled for app aptation to credibly assess exaptation as a counterpart to adaptation as theorized. Aptation—synonymous with IT evolution— measures an app’s lifetime evolution speed measured as (version number $\div \mathrm { a g e } _ { \mathrm { y e a r s } } ) _ { t 2 }$ (Tiwana 2015). Google’s stringent version numbering permits comparisons across apps and over time. This conceptualization mirrors Baldwin and Clark’s (2000, p. 237) evolution rate, release cadence in software practice, and iteration intensity in new product development. This metric captures total aptation from adaptation and exaptation, with adaptation being causally downstream, thus isolating the mechanism of exaptation. Next, we controlled four sets of rival explanations from the platforms literature.

1. App characteristics: Price in U.S. dollars; imitability of functionality by rivals (Wang et al. 2018), which speeds options expiration; platform synergy (customization to AndroidOS (Tiwana 2018)), and composability (AndroidOS reintegration ease).

2. Developer characteristics: Platform tenure capturing AndroidOS experience and developer country.

3. App’s market characteristics: Dummies for multihoming on rival platforms (Cennamo et al. 2018), audience targeted, language, backward incompatibility with older versions of AndroidOS, hardware specificity, and category dummies.

4. Trajectories: Changes in app installed base (∆installed $b a s e _ { t 1  t 2 } )$ and in rating $( \Delta R a t i n g _ { t 1  t 2 } )$ to account for intertemporal dynamics.<sup>10</sup>

## 4. Analysis and Results

Our analyses proceeded in three stages: (1) a reducedform model to address endogeneity in options (the real options model), (2) IT exaptation while accounting for this endogeneity (the exaptation model), and (3) IT exaptation’s effects on competitive outcomes, while accounting for aptation as a competing explanation and 10 controls from prior studies.

## 4.1. Stage 1: Accounting for Endogeneity in Real Options

We used the two-step Garen procedure to isolate real options’ effects on exaptation by accounting for factors that influence real options but don’t directly impact exaptation. Our endogeneity concerns stemmed from unobserved heterogeneity and omitted variables but not from reverse causality (as exaptation is unlikely to causally affect uncertainty or app modularity). Given scant empirical precedent to ground instrument selection, we relied on a few strong, theoretically grounded instruments. Our identification strategy used instruments derived from real options theory: app modularity and three uncertainty types. They affect competitive outcomes only through real options.

App modularity—the degree to which an app is loosely coupled to the platform using standardized application programming interfaces—is our first instrument. Modularity is grounded theoretically in Gamba and Fusari’s (2009) formal model showing how it creates discrete real options and enables incremental, localized changes without systemwide effects. It is an upstream design choice that creates real options through its architectural properties (relevance), while affecting exaptation only through the flexibility it creates (exclusion restriction). This accounts for the architectural-spawning of operational and strategic options (Baldwin and Clark 2000, p. 259). Although many apps follow a modular model-view-controller design philosophy, they varied in modularity (σ 1.05). Modularity was a secondorder factor comprising loose-coupling (α � 0.76) and API conformance (α � 0.88).

Our second set of instruments is the three types of uncertainty in real options theory, premising that developers’ uncertainties guide their choices for flexibility. 1 Such uncertainties are unlikely to directly affect an app’s competitive outcomes, as they affect all comparable apps, and our category controls account for that comparability. Market heterogeneity—how much an app’s users differ in their needs—encourages options to adapt as a market coalesces around articulated demand (Andriani and Cattani 2024). Developers in heterogeneous markets would invest more in strategic options to target adjacent application domains and want the operational flexibility for an app to coevolve with the market. Market volatility—an app’s users’ requirements constantly changing—discourages incorporating operational flexibility that may never be utilized (Trigeorgis and Reuer 2017) and is inconsequential to strategic flexibility. Technical uncertainty—the unpredictability of technical advances in the app’s domain (Trigeorgis and Reuer 2017)—will discourage investing in future flexibility that may go unused, thus reducing both operational and strategic options. For example, technological leaps, such as biometrics, cloud-storage APIs, and voice-recognition APIs, rendered apps’ existing code for authentication, private cloud services, and voicerecognition obsolete.

## 4.4. Six Limitations

First, we excluded file size due to high correlations. Second, we treated modularity as static, per Smith’s (1981, p. 70) view that microstructure is understandable only as a frozen slice of history. Third, our rival explanation (aptation) captures the rate but not the nature of change. Fourth, our new exaptation scale captured rapidity consistent with Andriani and Cattani (2024), but neither degree nor content of change. Fifth, extrapolating from small, specialized apps to larger systems warrants caution. Sixth, we lacked empirical precedent guiding instrument selection.

## 5. Discussion

Our knowledge of how evolvability-endowing characteristics of IT artifacts lead to competitive outcomes is rudimentary. Using first principles derived from the natural sciences roots of our field’s metaphor of IT evolution, we theorized how systems gain entirely new functionalities through environmental interactions. Our nine-year study of 625 AndroidOS apps across 63 countries introduced IT exaptation as a novel evolutionary mechanism complementing adaptation and its real options origins. We econometrically accounted for how developers’ considerations and modularity choices endogenously shape how optionsladen an app is. We make two novel contributions to IT evolution theory.

## 5.1. Contribution #1: Exaptation as a Novel Mechanism Underlying IT Evolution

IT exaptation is a novel evolutionary mechanism that’s gone unrecognized, despite its pervasiveness. This oversight persists, despite repeated calls to explain how systems evolve in unforeseen ways (Henfridsson and Bygstad 2013, Cennamo et al. 2018). Without this, IS scholarship has struggled to explain the mechanisms that enable systems to gain entirely new functionality beyond gradual refinement. What IS scholars collectively observe as IT evolution actually is a consequence of both adaptation and the overlooked-yet-ubiquitous exaptation mechanisms. This theoretical distinction explains why apps facing similar stimuli—whether exogenous innovations or market opportunities— diverge in their evolutionary trajectories.

Our search for mechanisms led us back to the theoretical roots in evolutionary biology (Gould and Vrba 1982,

Barve and Wagner 2013), from which our field’s own metaphor of IT evolution originated via Simon (1962). In those roots, two distinct mechanisms underlie evolution (“aptation”): adaptation, which gradually refines existing traits, and exaptation, which repurposes existing traits for new functions. Faithful to its roots is IT exaptation arising from systemenvironment interactions. We conceptualized it as how rapidly an app realizes new features, functionality, and capabilities from interacting with its evolving technological and competitive environment. This encompasses advances in the platform, third-party tools (e.g., platform-agnostic coding tools, such as Corona SDK), shifting user needs, and competing apps, all of which can surface novel use cases, latent purposes, and new possibilities for existing apps. This process of rapidly repurposing existing app attributes for entirely new functions metamorphoses evolutionary trajectories in ways that adaptation alone cannot explain.

Our digital “environment” has two unique strengths inaccessible to biologists and science historians study ing exaptation. First, it encompasses a broader, dynamic pool of actors, who can recognize and realize new functions, dramatically accelerating the cycles of functionality discovery. Second, compressed market feedback cycles with real-time analytics enable rapidly winnowing viable exaptation pathways and dead ends. Elsewhere, this might take decades (Andriani and Cattani 2024), even centuries (Barve and Wagner 2013).

Exaptation and adaptation are each other’s counterparts—theoretically and empirically distinct. Both are emergent, yet unlike IT adaptation’s gradual refinement, IT exaptation’s crux is new functionality emerging from within a system, stimulated by its environmental interactions. Adaptation improves fitness for existing users; exaptation alters trajectories. IT adaptation fol lows some semblance of a roadmap, but IT exaptation is opportunistic. Exaptation underscores Fu¨ rstenau et al. (2019) that some evolutionary pathways simply emerge and can’t be preplanned. Although IS generativity also produces new functionality in existing systems, it requires releasing systems in the wild for outsiders to tinker without involving its developers (Henfridsson and Bygstad 2013), unlike exaptation that’s developer driven. Exaptation—our theory’s central explanatory mechanism—shows how real options embedded in systems enable evolutionary leaps beyond adaptation’s incremental refinements.

This implies that IT evolution isn’t just about gradual improvement; systems can gain dramatically new functionality as their environment shifts. Like biological systems that realize innovation through regulatory rewiring, rather than creating new components (Swift et al. 2024), IT exaptation is how existing systems make functionality leaps by reconfiguring. rather than building from scratch. This explains transformative evolutionary leaps, like Kazaa’s music-sharing code metamorphosing into Microsoft Teams. This dualpathway perspective—combining adaptive refinement with exaptive potential—explains how environmental interactions drive divergent evolutionary trajectories among similar systems. IT evolution therefore must encompass exaptation alongside adaptation.

We were less awestruck when our machines gave us photographic memory or began tapping our wrists to avoid missing turns (e.g., haptics on Apple watches) than were biologists discovering that dinosaurs began flying or historians about Germans repurposing wine presses to spread the Bible. Our constant immersion in the IT artifacts undergoing change—what Kallinikos et al. (2013) described as perpetually in the making— blinds us. Although we’re uniquely positioned to observe both mechanisms, lacking the theoretical vocabulary to distinguish them mutes us. We routinely observe apps undergoing gradual refinements and abruptly gaining new functionality, yet without concepts to label these two types of change, we put all of it into a catchall IT evolution bucket. Both mechanisms operate together in the background to produce what we observe as IT evolution. Adding IT exaptation to the IT evolution conversation overcomes our handicap that unnamed ideas elude theorizing. Thus, exaptation, nomologically sandwiched between familiar IS concepts, explains how apps’ tweakability drives competitive outcomes.

## 5.2. Contribution #2: Real Options as Fodder for IT Exaptation

Our second contribution is tracing exaptation to real options present in IT artifacts’ design. This foregrounds their intrinsic properties, engaging more deeply than do science historians with the idea that exaptation stems from traits within an artifact (Andriani and Kaminska 2021). We showed that real options of both the strategic and operational ilk enhance exaptation, revealing how flexibility manifests. By adding real options as a source of systematically cataloged flexibility embedded in systems, we advance the IT evolution conversation beyond generalizations about systems needing malleability and plasticity (Henfridsson and Bygstad 2013, Kallinikos et al. 2013, Priego and Wareham 2023). Unlike population ecology studies in management that treat products as black boxes shaped by competitive selection, this unpacks how IT artifacts design enables both gradual refinement and exaptive metamorphosis through environmental interactions.

This lens precisely distinguishes foundation-laying strategic properties from operational flexibilities in developers’ arsenals. It is particularly valuable in platforms where uncertainty is endemic. The stronger influence of strategic options on exaptation (0.17 > 0.08)

suggests that having a foundation for major leaps matters more for exaptation than having operational flexibility. This explains why some apps successfully make dramatic transitions, whereas others remain confined to incremental refinements. Operational options include breaking up an app’s roadmap into small, independent blocks (staging), resizing resources (scaling), swapping building blocks with new alternatives (switching), postponing technical choices (deferral), or salvaging proprietary code if things go wrong (abandonment). Their exaptive value is in allowing incremental exploration of new use cases, reallocating development resources around nascent opportunities, assimilating outside advances by swapping APIs, adjusting as uncertainties clear, and salvaging unsuccessful forays.

Although widespread use of architectural ideas like model-view-controller raises baseline modularity across apps, heterogeneity persists in how optionsladen developers choose to make app designs. Notwithstanding the garden-variety options common across apps, heterogeneity in deliberately engineered ones more potently differentiates apps’ exaptive potential. Empirically assimilating accumulated insights about IS architecture—particularly modularity—as options’ sources accounts for its upstream, optionembedding role and developers’ reasoning about user, market, and technology uncertainties (Gamba and Fusari 2009). Its payoff was our counterfactual insight in Section 4.3.1 that developers fare better going overboard in embedding both classes of real options in their app designs. This expands the IS conversation from merely recognizing systems’ varying evolvability to understanding its origins (Fu¨ rstenau et al. 2019, Priego and Wareham 2023).

5.2.1. Five Ideas for Future Research. First, how can Smith’s (1981, p. 377) insight that an artifact’s present structure records its history inform such changes over time? GitHub repositories are IS scholars’ analog of the paleontologist’s fossil records and the dendrolo gist’s tree rings. Second, as exaptation and serendipity go hand-in-hand, can organizations better allocate IT decision rights to capitalize on external knowledge flows that catalyze it? Third, how can developers ensure that options are punctually exercised after recognizing opportunities? Fourth, although our new exaptation scale measured only the rapidity of change (Andriani and Cattani 2024), the degree and qualitative content of change leveraging natural language analysis of app release notes merits thought. Fifth, trait-level exaptation of discrete app features—analogous to analyzing feathers instead of whole birds—merits attention.

In conclusion, real options woven into IT artifact designs create novel exaptive pathways for their evolution. We advocate more attention to how early design choices and structural morphology seed evolutionary divergence. Exaptation is how an app discovers what it might become.
