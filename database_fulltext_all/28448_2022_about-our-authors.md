# About Our Authors

https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2022.1117

Copyright: © 2022 INFORMS

Alessandro Acquisti (“The Welfare Impact of Targeted Advertising Technologies”) is the Trustees Professor of Information Technology and Public Policy at Carnegie Mellon University. His award-winning and widely published studies have spearheaded the investigation of disclosure behavior in online social networks and the application of economics and behavioral economics to the study of privacy. He has a PhD from the University of California, Berkeley (UCB), and master degrees from UCB, the London School of Economics, and Trinity College Dublin.

David Antons (“How IT Investments Help Hospitals Gain and Sustain Reputation in the Media: The Role of Signaling and Framing”) is professor and codirector of the Institute for Technology and Innovation Management at RWTH Aachen University. He holds a PhD from Aachen University. He is interested in knowledge sharing, psychological in<sup>fl</sup>uences of decision making, learning from failure, employee voice, and text mining. He has published in Academy of Management Review, Journal of Management, Academy of Management Perspectives, Research Policy, Journal of Service Research, and Journal of Product Innovation Management.

Subhajyoti Bandyopadhyay (“Sponsored Data: Smarter Data Pricing with Incomplete Information”) is the George W. and Lisa O. Etheridge, Jr. Professor of Information Systems and Operations Management at University of Florida. He received his PhD in MIS from Purdue University in 2002. His work has been published in the areas of information systems, operations management and marketing. His current research interests include economics of information systems and public policy, especially in net neutrality, online media competition, and national broadband policy.

Michael Barrett (“How IT Investments Help Hospitals Gain and Sustain Reputation in the Media: The Role of Signaling and Framing”) is director of research and professor of information systems and innovation studies at Cambridge Judge Business School. He is academic director of Cambridge digital innovation and distinguished visiting professor at the Stockholm School of Economics. His research interests include the adoption and use of digital health and the role of digital innovation in facilitating <sup>fi</sup>nancial and social inclusion. He is currently editor-in-chief of Information & Organization.

Konstantin Bauman (“Know Thy Context: Parsing Contextual Information from User Reviews for Recommendation Purposes”) is an assistant professor at the Fox School of Business, Temple University. His research interests lie in the area of design science with a focus on developing novel approaches to recommender systems that predict customer preferences and provide better personalized advice. Before joining Fox, he worked as a postdoctoral research scientist at the Stern School of Business, New York University. He received his PhD from the mathematical depart ment of Moscow State University in 2012.

Abhishek Borah (“Creative Appeals in Firm-Generated Content and Product Performance”) is assistant professor of Marketing at INSEAD. He holds a PhD in Marketing from University of Southern California. Abhishek’s research focuses on uncovering insights from unstructured data and returns to marketing strategy with a methodological expertise in computational linguistics. His research has appeared in journals such as Journal of Marketing, Journal of Marketing Research, Marketing Science, Strategic Management Journal, and Harvard Business Review.

Jianqing Chen (“An Economic Analysis of Rebates Conditional on Positive Reviews”) is a professor in Information Systems at Jindal School of Management, University of Texas at Dallas. He received his PhD from University of Texas at Austin. His research interests are in platform business models, social media and user-generated content, search engine advertising, and economics of information systems. His papers have been published in journals such as Management Science, Information Systems Research, MIS Quarterly, Journal of Marketing, and Journal of Marketing Research.

Yuan Chen (“What Questions Are You Inclined to Answer? Effects of Hierarchy in Corporate Q&A Communities”) is a professor in the Department of Information Systems and Electronic Commerce, School of Information Management and Engineering, Shanghai University of Finance and Economics. Her research interests are in the areas of crowd behavior, organizational learning, and digital platforms.

Hsing Kenneth Cheng (“What Questions Are You Inclined to Answer? Effects of Hierarchy in Corporate Q&A Communities”; “Sponsored Data: Smarter Data Pricing with Incomplete Information”) is the John B. Higdon Eminent Scholar and Department Chair of the Department of Information Systems and Operations Management, Warrington College of Business, University of Florida. He received his PhD from the University of Rochester. His current research focuses on economic and policy issues of information technology and the internet.

Xiufeng Cheng (“Achieving a Balance Between Privacy Protection and Data Collection: A Field Experimental Examination of a Theory-Driven Information Technology Solution”) received a PhD degree of management science from Central China Normal University (CCNU). He received a master degree of software engineering from University of Wisconsin-Lacrosse. He has been an associate professor in CCNU since 2015 and a postdoctoral fellow and software engineer of system architect in Institute of Scienti<sup>fi</sup>c and Technical Information of China (ISTIC) since 2018. His professional af<sup>fi</sup>liations include software engineering and information systems design.

Muller Y. M. Cheung (“Seeker Exemplars and Quantitative Ideation Outcomes in Crowdsourcing Contests”) is a lecturer of information systems in the School of Business and Management, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. He received his PhD in information systems from Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. His research interests include human-computer interaction, crowdsourcing, enterprise systems, and information security.

Yulin Fang (“The Role of Vendor Legitimacy in IT Outsourcing Performance: Theory and Evidence”) is a professor in the Faculty of Business and Economics at University of Hong Kong. He obtained his PhD at Ivey School of Business, Western University, Canada. His research is focused on digital innovation, social media, and e-commerce. He has published in major IS journals, such as Information Systems Research, MIS Quarterly, and the Journal of Management Information Systems.

Abhijeet Ghoshal (“Modifying Transactional Databases to Hide Sensitive Association Rules”) is an assistant professor of information systems at the Gies College of Business, University of Illinois Urbana– Champaign. He received his PhD from University of Texas at Dallas. His current interests include recommendation system design, data sharing, privacy, and software support. His contributions have appeared in Information Systems Research, MIS Quarterly, INFORMS Journal on Computing, Production and Operations Management, and the Journal of Management Information Systems.

Priyanga Gunarathne (“Racial Bias in Customer Service: Evidence from Twitter”) is an assistant professor of business administration at the Joseph M. Katz Graduate School of Business, University of Pittsburgh. She received her PhD from the Simon Business School of the University of Rochester in 2018. Her research interests are in the areas of digital platforms, user-generated content, arti<sup>fi</sup>cial intelligence, electronic privacy, and consumer behavior.

Zhiling Guo (“An Economic Analysis of Rebates Conditional on Positive Reviews”) is an associate professor of Information Systems at Singapore Management University. She received her PhD from University of Texas at Austin. Her recent research focuses on platform business models, <sup>fi</sup>ntech innovations, digital payments, cloud computing, and data analytics. Her works appear in Management Science, MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, INFORMS Journal on Com puting, Journal of Management Information Systems, and Production and Operations Management, among others.

Ola Henfridsson (“Extending Digital Ventures Through Templating”) is a professor of business technology at the Miami Herbert Business School, University of Miami. He has also been appointed a WBS Distinguished Research Environment Professor at the Warwick Business School and a KIN Fellow at VU Amsterdam. His research interests relate to digital innovation, platforms, and technology management. His research has been published in journals such as Academy of Management Review, Information Systems Research, MIS Quarterly, and Organization Science.

Yi-Chun (Chad) Ho (“What’s in a ‘Username’? The Effect of Perceived Anonymity on Herding in Crowdfunding”) is an associate professor at the School of Business, George Washington University. He received his PhD from the Foster School of Business, University of Washington. His research interests include sharing economy, digital marketing, social media, and e-commerce. Jian Huang (“An Economic Analysis of Rebates Conditional on Positive Reviews”) is a professor in the School of Management Science and Engineering, Nanjing University of Finance and Economics. His research interests are in economics of information system and operations, platform-based business models and decision analysis. His papers have been published in academic journals, including IISE Transactions, Decision Sciences, European Journal of Operational Research, Transportation Research Part E, and others.

His research focuses primarily on the process, dynamics and transformative effects of digital technology, speci<sup>fi</sup>cally in the context of digital ventures. His research has been published in the top information systems journals, and he is an associate editor for Information & Management.
