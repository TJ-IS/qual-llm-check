Abstract. Topic modeling methods such as latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA) are powerful tools for analyzing massive amounts of textual data. They have been used extensively in information systems (IS) and business discipline research to identify latent topics for data exploration and as a feature engineering mechanism to derive new variables for analyses. However, existing topic modeling approaches are mostly unsupervised and only leverage textual data, while ignoring additional useful metadata often associated with text, such as star ratings in customer reviews or categories of posts in online forums. As a result, the identi<sup>fi</sup>ed topics and variables derived based on the learned topic model may not be accurate, which could lead to incorrect estimations that affect subsequent empirical analysis and to inferior performance on predictive tasks. In this study, we propose a novel supervised deep topic modeling approach called sDTM, which combines a neural variational autoencoder model and a recurrent neural network. sDTM leverages the auxiliary data associated with text to enhance the topic modeling capability. We conduct empirical case studies and predictive analytics on an online consumer review data set and an online knowledge community data set. Experimental results show that in comparison with benchmark methods, sDTM can enhance both the empirical estimation and predictive performance. sDTM makes methodological contributions to the IS literature and has direct relevance for research using text analytics.

## 1. Introduction

Pervasive digitization of economic and social transactions coupled with the rapid growth of usergenerated content has generated massive amounts of unstructured textual data, such as customer reviews and question-and-answer (Q&A) discussions within online knowledge communities. Exploring and analyzing such high volumes of text data provides an unprecedented opportunity for both researchers and practitioners to arrive at novel insights. However, such exploration calls for effective computational methods that leverage the power of big data analytics (Agarwal and Dhar 2014). One technique, topic modeling, has become the dominant tool used by information systems (IS) and business discipline researchers to explore and extract insights from textual data (Rai 2016). The most widely used topic modeling technique, latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA; Blei et al. 2003), can automatically uncover relevant latent topics and semantic relationships from a collection of documents and provide probabilistic interpretations of text data.

One common application of topic modeling is theory generation, in which topic modeling is combined with qualitative methodologies and empirical approaches (Rai 2016). In this context, researchers often apply topic models, such as LDA, in a two-stage framework. LDA is <sup>fi</sup>rst used to extract hidden topics and document-topic distributions from which new variables can be derived, and subsequently, these variables are included in an econometric model for addi tional analysis. For example, Khern-am-nuai et al. (2018) apply LDA to consumer reviews to measure the difference in review topics when the online platform begins offering monetary incentives for reviews, and then they set up a regression model where the dependent variable is the probability that a given topic will appear in a review. LDA is also part of a common two-stage heuristic to predict variables of interest in predictive analytics: researchers <sup>fi</sup>rst use LDA to estimate latent topic vectors for documents and then feed the vectors to other supervised predictive models (Liu et al. 2020). In each of these cases, it is critical that learned topic vectors are accurate; otherwise, the imperfect topic modeling will result in measurement errors in variable construction and eventually lead to spurious or wrong estimations (Yang et al. 2018, Qiao and Huang 2021) and poor predictive performance.

Traditional statistical topic models (e.g., LDA) are unsupervised and are trained using only textual data. However, on many real-world platforms, textual data are often accompanied with useful auxiliary metadata. For example, online review platforms such as Yelp and Amazon allow users to provide numerical ratings along with the textual reviews. Online knowledge communities such as Q&A forums often separate posts into different discussion boards that can be viewed by topic category. News outlet websites or pages attach explicit categorical labels to their news articles to facilitate ef<sup>fi</sup>cient searching. Although these auxiliary metadata might be useful supervision signals, they are unfortunately ignored by unsupervised topic models. Incorporating such auxiliary metadata is challenging under Bayesian-based topic modeling approaches because it requires explicitly de<sup>fi</sup>ning the data generation process for the auxiliary data (Mcauliffe and Blei 2008). This limits the generalizability of the resulting model with regard to capturing complex semantic relationships in text collection, which may lead to a poorly <sup>fi</sup>tted topic model.

To overcome these challenges, we propose a novel deep-learning-based neural topic modeling method that leverages the useful auxiliary metadata associated with the text, such as review ratings or document categories. Our method, the supervised deep topic model (sDTM), combines unsupervised neural topic modeling with supervised recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in one single framework, using a novel topic attention layer design. To derive better representations of the data, sDTM uses topic vectors learned by a neural topic model (NTM) to guide RNNs to relate important words in the document to these topics and the auxiliary labels. For example, if the NTM infers that a consumer review is related to the topics of “price” and “quality,” the inferred topics will then be leveraged to assign higher weights to those price- and quality-related words in the review, thus improving the document representations for the RNN model. Jointly, the auxiliary labels can also be propagated through RNNs to the NTM, thereby injecting supervision signals into the topic modeling. For example, if the label is a review rating, it can help sDTM learn more sentimental, valence-oriented topics. In sum, sDTM combines the merits of both supervised deep learning and unsupervised topic modeling to enhance overall topic modeling, which bene<sup>fi</sup>ts subsequent empirical studies and predictive analytics.

We demonstrate the practical utility of our approach on an online consumer review data set (Yelp) and an online knowledge community data set (Stack Exchange), both of which have been extensively studied by IS and business discipline researchers. The practical utility is demonstrated from three perspectives. First, as a probabilistic topic model, we evaluate the sDTM’s model <sup>fi</sup>t on out-of-sample data. Experimental results show that sDTM can signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly and substantially improve text modeling capability over the traditional LDA approach and several supervised topic model baselines. Using the Yelp data set as an example, the topics learned by sDTM are more coherent, distinguished, and valence-oriented than those learned by other topic models, which provides face validity of the effectiveness of incorporating review sentiment labels. Second, we conduct two empirical case studies to show the advantage of using sDTM in empirical studies. The <sup>fi</sup>rst study, using the Yelp review data set, examines the relationship between review complexity (Ghose et al. 2019), which is derived from document topic features, and review usefulness. The second study, using the Stack Exchange data set, examines the relationship between the topic similarity of an answer to its corresponding question and the perceived helpfulness of the answer. Both empirical studies highlight the fact that, compared with prior approaches, sDTM can produce more meaningful and theoretically sound empirical estimations. With this high-quality topic model, researchers can construct more accurate variables for subsequent regression analyses, alleviating concerns about inaccurate estimation caused by imper fect topic modeling. In addition, using sDTM can reduce the incidence of failure to reject a null hypothesis. Third, we evaluate the power of sDTM to predict out-of-sample labels. The results show that sDTM can signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly improve prediction accuracy over the traditional two-stage machine learning approaches and can even achieve performance comparable to or better than that of state-of-the-art supervised deeplearning-based approaches.

This paper contributes to the IS literature in three ways. First, we describe and raise awareness of the issue that auxiliary metadata often accompany text data in most text analysis problems where topic modeling is used. Incorporating these metadata into topic modeling can signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly enhance the discovery of latent semantics in text. Motivated by this, we introduce a novel supervised topic modeling approach that combines the merits of unsupervised neural topic modeling and supervised deep learning, addressing a gap in the literature and offering a new perspective on text analytics. Second, we demonstrate the practical utility of sDTM using two highly IS-relevant data sets: online consumer reviews and online knowledge community Q&As. We showcase the way in which sDTM produces a high-quality topic model that enhances subsequent empirical analysis and predic tive analysis. Therefore, this paper offers direct relevance and utility for researchers and practitioners in

IS and business disciplines. Third, our approach, sDTM, can be used as a direct replacement for traditional topic models such as LDA, without requiring researchers to overhaul their analytical framework. We open source our implementation in Python to maximize the research and practical impacts.

## 2. Literature Review

We organize prior related literature into three main categories and position our work in that context. First, we brie<sup>fl</sup>y summarize how topic modeling has been used in IS and related management research. Second, we review prior computational linguistic studies that aim to build supervised topic models. Third, we refer to the relevant data science research in IS that motivates our methodological work.

## 2.1. Topic Modeling in IS and Management Research

The popularity of topic modeling techniques to identify latent topics from textual data are evident in different <sup>fi</sup>elds of management research (Rai 2016). IS scholars have extensively applied LDA models to consumer reviews and user-generated textual content to extract variables of interest for subsequent analysis. For example, Tirunillai and Tellis (2014) use LDA to extract latent dimensions of consumer satisfaction, mainly quality and valence, from user-generated content at a high temporal frequency. Both Lappas et al. (2016) and Wang and Chaudhry (2018) investigate the business response strategy to negative online reviews by using LDA to summarize topics in a collection of consumer reviews and corresponding business responses. Mankad et al. (2016) utilize LDA to learn about consumer experiences from online reviews of hotels, whereas Abrahams et al. (2015) use a similar approach to study product-defect-related topics. Puranam et al. (2017) extract consumer opinions on health topics from restaurant reviews and then examine the impact of food regulation on consumer opinion change. Khern-am-nuai et al. (2018) apply LDA to consumer reviews and analyze how topics change when the online platform begins offering monetary incentives for writing reviews. In addition to online reviews, LDA has also been applied to online forum discussions and online knowledge community platforms (Yue et al. 2019, Liu et al. 2020).

LDA has also been applied by IS and management scholars to extract insights from other types of unstructured text data, including search queries (Gong et al. 2018), information on mobile apps (Wang et al. 2018), literature reviews (Larsen and Bong 2016), patents (Kaplan and Vakili 2015), idea generation (Toubia and Netzer 2016), tweets (Liu et al. 2016, Lee et al. 2018, Geva et al. 2019), company descriptions (Shi et al. 2016), organizational blogs (Singh et al. 2014, Guo et al. 2017), corporate reports (Bao and Datta 2014, Dyer et al. 2017), and analysts’ reports (Huang et al. 2017), among others. The above literature shows that LDA is an important and useful big data analytics toolkit for validating and generating theory. However, to the best of our knowledge, existing studies directly apply the LDA model to textual data, ignoring the fact that other auxiliary information that accompanies the text (such as labels, categories, or ratings) could be used to enhance the quality of topic modeling. Our study is among the <sup>fi</sup>rst to highlight the prevalence and importance of useful auxiliary data in common business research text and to provide a means of incorporating it into a topic modeling framework to enhance downstream predictive and empirical studies.

## 2.2. Supervised Topic Modeling

LDA is an unsupervised model, and by nature it does not leverage document-related auxiliary information. However, these metadata are usually available, and they can serve as supervision signals to enhance topic modeling capability. Therefore, several Bayesian mod els have been developed that extend unsupervised LDA to supervised models. For example, supervised LDA (sLDA; Mcauliffe and Blei 2008, Chong et al. 2009) has been proposed to simultaneously model the latent topic structure of text and a response variable associated with each document. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, sLDA models the numerical response variable via a linear function and the categorical response variable by a softmax function. sLDA is trained by optimizing the overall likelihood. With different objectives for inference, sLDA has many variations, such as discriminative LDA (DiscLDA; Lacoste-Julien et al. 2009), max entropy discriminative LDA (MedLDA; Zhu et al. 2012), backpropagation-sLDA (BP-sLDA; Chen et al. 2015), and spectral LDA (Wang and Zhu 2014). On the other hand, several non-Bayesian supervised topic models have also been proposed. For example, Mankad et al. (2018) introduced a supervised topic model based on matrix factorization. Cao et al. (2015) proposed to directly utilize neural networks to learn topic-word and document-topic distributions, whereas the supervised label is incorporated via a regression or classi<sup>fi</sup>cation loss. However, one major drawback of these supervised LDA or other non-Bayesian supervised topic models is that they make strong assumptions on the latent variable distribution. These assumptions limit the generalizability of the topic modeling, which can easily lead to low model <sup>fi</sup>tness and possibly to subopti mal predictive power.

In contrast with previous work, we propose to leverage deep learning for supervised topic modeling. Recent advances in deep learning have motivated developments that exploit some combination of deep neural networks and topic modeling. For example, Dieng et al. (2017) develop TopicRNN, where the generative process of a word is governed by the document’s topic vector and the RNN. TopicRNN follows LDA and assumes that the topic vector is drawn from a Dirichlet prior. Chai and Li (2019) release the traditional topic model’s bag-of-words assumption and implement an long short-term memory (LSTM) to approximate a topic prior. However, it is worth noting that both works are essentially unsupervised topic models: the deep-learning component is used only to model the sequential dependency between words. For example, Chai and Li (2019) predict document labels using the inferred topic assignments via a subsequent classi<sup>fi</sup>er. In other words, supervisory labels are not included during the backpropagation process and thus have no impact on the inference of the topic model. Therefore, it is still not clear how supervised deep learning and unsupervised topic modeling can be integrated and jointly learned. Our study aims to <sup>fi</sup>ll this research gap. The major novelty of our work is that we design a way to bridge deep learning and Bayesian learning for supervised topic modeling in a uni<sup>fi</sup>ed framework. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we implement a novel attention layer to jointly train a variational autoencoder (VAE)-based neural topic model (Kingma and Welling 2013, Miao et al. 2017, Wang and Yang 2020) and a sequential deep neural network. To the best of our knowledge, we are among the <sup>fi</sup>rst to develop such a supervised deep-learning-based topic model for text data.

We summarize major extant topic modeling approaches as well as supervised topic models in Table 1. We can see that existing LDA-based supervised models explicitly prede<sup>fi</sup>ne latent variable distributions and the supervised label is incorporated as a linear relationship with latent variables. This limits the model generalizability. In contrast, our approach proposes a novel supervised topic model based on deep learning, with demonstrable improvements for downstream predictive and empirical analyses.

## 2.3. Methodological Data Science Research in IS

As the use of big data and machine learning techniques to validate and generate theory accelerates, IS researchers have highlighted potential issues with such methods and sought to improve the robustness of empirical work. For example, Lin et al. (2013) point to the p-value problem when testing econometric models on large samples and provide suggestions to mitigate this problem. Yang et al. (2018) and Qiao and Huang (2021) note that variables generated by machine learning models could be inaccurate, introducing systematic biases into the subsequent regression model and threatening the validity of statistical inference.

Martens and Provost (2014) highlight the need to provide explanation in predictive document classi<sup>fi</sup>cation models and propose a new explainable IT artifact to improve the practical utility of predictive text analysis. Our work is likewise motivated by the need to enhance the practical and predictive utility of topic modeling tools. Our deep-learning-based supervised topic modeling approach has the potential to enable the extraction of more accurate and actionable insights from text data.

## 3. Our Approach: Supervised Deep Topic Model

## 3.1. Overall Design Framework

Before we discuss our supervised deep topic model, we <sup>fi</sup>rst highlight the prevalence of text-associated auxiliary labels and their importance in topic modeling. Taking online consumer reviews for example, major online review platforms allow users to provide a numerical rating when they submit a textual review. Because unsupervised topic models such as LDA discover topics by analyzing word co-occurrence, they may not be able to distinguish valence-oriented topics. For example, in cell phone product reviews, consumers may write positive reviews of the battery of one phone and also write negative reviews of the battery of another phone. Both positive words and negative words may co-occur with battery-related words. As a result, existing unsupervised topic modeling methods are likely to learn a battery-related topic mixed with both positive and negative words. Incorporating review ratings could potentially alleviate this issue and lead to a clean positive battery-related topic and a negative battery-related topic, which would help to better summarize consumer reviews. In online knowl edge communities, users often write posts and answers under different categories (e.g., boards and sub-boards). Clearly, these category labels could provide topic guidance and enhance the topic modeling capability. Our review of the literature established that researchers often apply LDA as a feature engineering tool, that is, to extract document-topic features and topic-word features for subsequent analysis (predictive or empirical). In the current research paradigm, the generated topic features are used as inputs in predictive analytics, or they are used to generate key variables in empirical studies. For example, most existing studies take text data D (e.g., consumer reviews) and apply LDA to identify a set of topics for exploration. In Path 1 (P1), LDA generates document-topic vectors that can be used to derive other variables (Y) for subsequent econometric analysis (e.g., the impact of a review’s topic complexity on the review’s helpfulness). Exemplar works from Table 2 that use P1 include Yue et al. (2019) and Shi et al. (2016). Alternatively, in Path 2 (P2), researchers commonly adopt a two-stage approach that <sup>fi</sup>rst estimates a topic vector (X) for each document using LDA and then feeds the vector to another predictive model for predictive analysis. Exemplar works from Table 2 that use P2 include Dong et al. (2018) and Liu et al. (2020). In this two-stage P2, useful auxiliary information (e.g., response variable Z) present in data is used only in the second-stage machine learning models, meaning that it has no impact on the topic model inference. Our proposed sDTM approach addresses the gap accordingly. First, in sDTM, we incorporate Z to learn enhanced topic representation that can be utilized to generate new variables (Y) for subsequent empirical analysis, as in Path 3 (P3). Second, if we focus on supervised learning tasks (e.g., predicting rating $Z ) ,$ , our sDTM can accomplish this in one step, as in Path 4 (P4). In both cases, sDTM is expected to improve subsequent empirical or predictive studies, as the auxiliary information can directly in<sup>fl</sup>uence the topic model inference.

## 3.2. Topic Modeling Background

A typical text analytics task is to identify topics discussed in a text collection (e.g., in consumer reviews, online discussions, or company reports). Statistical Bayesian topic modeling such as LDA derives latent topics from text corpora in an unsupervised manner. It assumes that documents (represented as bags of words) are generated by a mixture of underlying latent topics and each topic is a distribution over words in the corpus vocabulary. Recently, the paradigm has been shifting toward deep-learning-based topic models, which are capable of generating more accurate and effective document models because of their expressive power (Miao et al. 2017). Both Baye sian and deep-learning-based topic models can be regarded as dimension reduction techniques, which are useful for text analytics tasks in IS research (Clark and Provost 2016).

In this study, we develop supervised topic modeling based on a deep-learning paradigm. ## 3.3. Supervised Deep Topic Modeling

The neural topic model is unsupervised. As discussed previously, in the situation where documents are associated with auxiliary labels, it is likely that those labels can provide useful information to improve topic modeling. The auxiliary labels can guide the model to recover highquality topic vectors, which facilitates not only topic exploration but, more importantly, better variable generation in subsequent analysis. This motivates us to design our supervised deep topic modeling approach, sDTM.

Assume document d in the data set is associated with an auxiliary label $l _ { d } ~ ( \mathrm { i . e . , } Z )$ , which can be either categorical, such as a document category, or numerical, such as a review rating. To simplify this notation, we use l to replace $l _ { d } .$ . We propose to leverage auxiliary labels by integrating the NTM with a deep sequential model. We choose to use an RNN, of which input is a sequence of word tokens in a document. We convert input word tokens to word embedding sequence $d = ( e _ { 1 } , \dots , \mathbf { e } _ { N } )$ , which is generated from a trainable word embedding matrix $M \in \mathbb { R } ^ { | V _ { R N N } | \times W } ,$ where V is the vocabulary size and $W$ is the dimension of word embeddin g.<sup>2</sup> To encode the embedding sequence in our RNN model, we choose a bidirectional gated recurrent unit (GRU), which is widely used in text classi<sup>fi</sup>cation. Detailed descriptions of the GRU gating architecture can be found in Cho et al. (2014), and for simplicity, we use GRU to denote the GRU transformation. The bidirectional GRU captures the contextual information from both the previous text and the subsequent text in a document. Now we have two separate components: an unsupervised neural topic model and a supervised RNN. Combining them into one single framework to enhance topic modeling is challenging, and by addressing this problem we make our methodological contributions: a novel design to bridge the two components via a topic attention layer. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we decompose topic-word distribution $\pmb { \beta }$ into a set of topic embeddings, and then we design a topic attention layer to learn the dependency between topic embeddings and RNN hidden states. The design of such a topic attention layer has two advantages. First, in backpropagation, because the supervisory labels are propagated to in<sup>fl</sup>uence the RNN hidden states, the attention layer allows the label’s information to be further propagated to update the topic embeddings. Therefore, the topic model learns discriminative features. Second, in forward passing, the topic embeddings can also be incorporated into the RNN so that the <sup>fi</sup>nal document’s hidden states include not only the sequential but also the topical information. In other words, the attention layer integrates the unsupervised NTM with the supervised RNN so that they can complement each other in a meaningful way. The overall framework of sDTM is shown in Figure 2.

In sDTM, , the topic distribution over words, can be decomposed as a product of two matrices: $\pmb { \beta } =$ $s o f t m a x ( V \dot { F } ^ { T } )$ where $\dot { \bar { V } } = ( v _ { 1 } , v _ { 2 } , \ldots , v _ { K } ) ^ { T } \in \mathbb { R } ^ { K \times L }$ and $\check { F } \in \mathbb { R } ^ { V _ { t o p i c } \times L }$ . The term L is a chosen positive integer such that $L < V _ { t o p i c }$ . The softmax function is applied to ensure $\pmb { \beta }$ is a probability distribution. Through this matrix factorization process, $v _ { i }$ carries information of the ith topic in the NTM but with a much smaller dimension than $\beta _ { i }$ . We call this vector $v _ { i }$ the topic embedding. Regarding the dimension for topic embedding L, 100 is an effective and ef<sup>fi</sup>cient size. Too large an L imposes a high computational cost, whereas too small an L limits the representation power.

We use topic embeddings as context vectors in the topic attention layer to encode the dependency between topics and RNN hidden states. This is very different from commonly used attention layers in deep natural language processing (NLP) models, where there is usually one context vector and the model is trained with random initializations (Bahda nau et al. 2015, Yang et al. 2016). Instead, we use the NTM-derived topic embeddings as context vectors to in<sup>fl</sup>uence the supervised sequential model, that is, the RNN. As a result, the RNN can be guided to focus on information at the global topic perspective, which may otherwise be overlooked by a supervised model that mostly captures local contextual information. An intuitive explanation is that these topic vectors resemble the query “what is the informative word under this topic?” Looking at, for instance, a consumer review data set with a numerical rating as the auxiliary label, we expect the learned topics are not only topical but also sentimental (such as positive or negative). After calculating attention weights with respect to all topics, we then average them by a shifted topic proportion. This makes the <sup>fi</sup>nal attention weights re<sup>fl</sup>ective of the most important topics. ## 3.4. Model Inference

Now we discuss the model inference process. Given document d and its auxiliary label l, our objective is to maximize the joint marginal likelihood:

$$
p (l, \boldsymbol {d} | \beta) = \int_ {t} p (\boldsymbol {t} | N (0, I ^ {2}) p (l, \boldsymbol {d} | \boldsymbol {t}, \beta) d \boldsymbol {t}.\tag{2}
$$

Because the label l is independent of the bag-of-words representation <sup>d</sup> (the input of NTM) and depends only on the RNN input and topic mixture $t ,$ we can factorize the joint marginal distribution as

$$
p (l, \boldsymbol {d} \mid \beta) = \int_ {t} p \Big (\boldsymbol {t} \mid N (0, I ^ {2}) p (\boldsymbol {d} \mid \boldsymbol {t}, \beta) p (l \mid \boldsymbol {t}) d \boldsymbol {t}.
$$

The direct optimization of this integral is intractable, so we approximate it using variational inference. Speci<sup>fi</sup>- cally, we derive the log-likelihood and its ELBO:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \log p _ {\Theta , \phi , \Psi} (l, d) \\ = \log \int_ {t} \frac {p _ {\Theta} (t)}{q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)} q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d) p _ {\Psi} (l \mid t) p _ {\Theta} (d \mid t) d t \\ = \log \mathbb {E} _ {q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)} \bigg [ \frac {p _ {\Theta} (t)}{q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)} p _ {\Psi} (l \mid t) p _ {\Theta} (d \mid t) \bigg ] \\ = \mathbb {E} _ {q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)} \bigg [ \log p _ {\Theta} (d \mid t) - \log \frac {q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)}{p _ {\Theta} (t)} + \log p _ {\Psi} (l \mid t) \bigg ] \\ \geq \mathbb {E} _ {q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d)} [ \log p _ {\Theta} (d \mid t) + \log p _ {\Psi} (l \mid t) ] \\ - K L (q _ {\Phi} (t \mid d) \| p (t)). \end{array}
$$

Here, Ψ represents all parameters from the RNN attention model, Θ represents all parameters in the generative network, and Φ denotes all parameters in the inference network.

Therefore, the expectation on one single sample can be estimated by

$$
\hat {\mathcal {L}} = \log p _ {\Theta} (\boldsymbol {d} \mid \hat {\boldsymbol {t}}) + \log p _ {\Psi} (l | \hat {\boldsymbol {t}}) - K L (q _ {\Phi} (\boldsymbol {t} \mid \boldsymbol {d}) \| p (\boldsymbol {t})).
$$

The objective of sDTM is to maximize $\hat { \mathcal { L } } _ { \iota }$ , which can be decomposed into three parts: log likelihood of data $\log p _ { \Theta } ( { \hat { d } } \mid { \hat { t } } )$ , log likelihood of label log $p _ { \Psi } ( l | \hat { t } )$ , and topic Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence ${ K L } ( q _ { \Phi } ( t | d ) | | p ( \bar { t } ) )$ These elements can all be effectively optimized; for example, $\log p _ { \Theta } ( d | \hat { t } )$ can be calculated by marginalizing out the latent variable $z _ { n }$

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & {\log p _ {\Theta} (\pmb {d} \mid \hat {\pmb {t}}) = \sum_ {n = 1} ^ {N} \log p (x _ {n} \mid \hat {\pmb {t}})} \\ & {\qquad = \sum_ {n = 1} ^ {N} \sum_ {z _ {n}} \log p (x _ {n} \mid \beta_ {z _ {n}}) p (z _ {n} \mid \hat {\pmb {t}})} \\ & {\qquad = \sum_ {n = 1} ^ {N} \log \beta_ {x _ {n}} ^ {T} \hat {\pmb {t}},} \end{array}
$$

where $\beta _ { x _ { n } }$ is a column of $\beta$ related to word $x _ { n } .$ : For the topic KL-divergence, we have

$$
q _ {\Phi} (\boldsymbol {t} | \boldsymbol {d}) = \mathcal {N} \Bigl (\mu_ {\phi} (\boldsymbol {d}), \Sigma_ {\phi} ^ {2} (\boldsymbol {d}) \Bigr).
$$

Therefore, ${ K L } ( q _ { \Phi } ( t | d ) | | p ( t ) )$ can be computed analytically as a Gaussian KL-divergence. The auxiliary labels usually belong to two types: categorical and numerical. For a categorical label, we assume that the label distribution is conditioned on the RNN input and the topic mixture <sup>t</sup> is a multinomial distribution. Therefore, we use a sigmoid or softmax activation function in the output layer. Then,

$$
p _ {\Psi} (l \mid \hat {\pmb {t}}) = \pmb {o} _ {l},
$$

where $\mathbf { \delta } _ { \mathbf { \alpha } } \mathbf { o } _ { l }$ is the entry of output corresponding to the label l. For a numerical label, we assume that $p _ { \Psi } ( l | \hat { t } )$ is a Gaussian distribution with mean <sup>o</sup> and variance $\sigma _ { l } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { : }$

$$
p _ {\Psi} (l \mid \hat {t}) = \mathcal {N} (\boldsymbol {o}, \sigma_ {l} ^ {2}),
$$

where $\sigma _ { l } ^ { 2 }$ is the variance of the label likelihood and treated as a hyperparameter. Thus, we have

$$
\log p _ {\Psi} (l \mid \hat {\pmb {t}}) = - \frac {(l - \pmb {o}) ^ {2}}{2 \sigma_ {l} ^ {2}} - \frac {1}{2} \log [ 2 \pi \sigma_ {l} ^ {2} ].
$$

Because the objective is to maximize L with respect to $\Theta , \Phi ,$ , and $\Psi ,$ we can ignore the second term, as it is a constant.

The objective $\hat { \boldsymbol { \mathcal { L } } }$ is maximized by stochastic gradient ascent, and all parameters are jointly updated. It is intuitive to derive the gradient with respect to $\Psi$ and Θ because all parameters and operations are differentiable. But when it comes to Φ, ω is a random variable following Gaussian distribution, and the gradient with respect to it cannot be directly calculated. To estimate this gradient, we take one sample, $\hat { \omega } _ { \iota }$ , and update $\hat { \omega }$ based on $\hat { \omega } = s o f t m a x ( \mu _ { \phi } ( d ) + \Sigma _ { \phi } ^ { 2 } ( d ) \omega ) , \ \omega \sim \mathcal { N } ( 0 , 1 )$ For a single sample $\hat { \omega } ,$ , we therefore have $\begin{array} { r } { \frac { \partial \hat { \mathcal { L } } } { \partial \mu _ { \phi } ( d ) } \approx \frac { \partial \hat { \mathcal { L } } } { \partial \hat { \omega } } } \end{array}$ and $\begin{array} { r } { \frac { \partial \hat { \mathcal { L } } } { \partial \Sigma _ { \phi } ^ { 2 } ( d ) } \approx \hat { \epsilon } \frac { \partial \hat { \mathcal { L } } } { \partial \hat { \omega } } . } \end{array}$ . We perform backpropagation on parameters of neural networks $f , g _ { 1 } , g _ { 2 }$

To summarize, we propose a supervised deeplearning-based topic model, sDTM, that combines an unsupervised neural topic model and a supervised RNN model in one single framework to enhance topic modeling. The two models are bridged by a novel topic attention layer such that all involved parameters are jointly learned and optimized.

## 4. Online Consumer Review and Knowledge Community Data Set

Before we evaluate and demonstrate the practical value of our sDTM approach, we <sup>fi</sup>rst describe the two data sets used in the experiments. Both data sets are highly relevant to IS and business discipline research.

## 4.1. Online Consumer Reviews

The <sup>fi</sup>rst data set consists of consumer reviews from Yelp.com. A large and growing body of IS and business literature applies topic modeling methods to analyze online consumer reviews. For example, Ghose et al. (2019) model consumer preferences on search engines and use topic modeling to measure the complexity of consumer reviews. Tirunillai and Tellis (2014) use topic modeling to extract latent dimensions of consumer reviews in terms of quality and valence. Puranam et al. (2017) extract consumer opinions on health topics in restaurant reviews and then examine the impact of food regulation on the change in consumer opinion.

Online reviews are commonly accompanied by numerical ratings. Our approach can incorporate the ratings along with textual reviews into the topic modeling. To evaluate the effectiveness of our approach in the extensively studied context of online consumer reviews, we collected 40,326 consumer reviews from $1 { , } 0 0 0$ randomly selected business Yelp pages in the United States. Each consumer review is accompanied with a rating on a scale of one to <sup>fi</sup>ve. Following Yang et al. (2018), we code a review as positive if it gives the business a rating of four or <sup>fi</sup>ve stars and as negative if it assigns a rating of three or fewer stars. In total, 66.6% of reviews are positive and 33.4% are negative, exhibiting a skewed distribution.

## 4.2. Online Knowledge Community

The second data set contains discussion threads from the online knowledge Q&A community Stack Exchange. Like consumer reviews, the online Q&A community has been extensively studied within the IS and business literatures (Khurana et al. 2019, Kokkodis et al. 2020, Xu et al. 2020). Because an online knowledge community contains a large amount of unstructured text data, topic modeling becomes a useful tool in this context. For example, Liu et al. (2020) use topic modeling to extract topic features and predict the usefulness of solutions, and Peng et al. (2020) study medical Q&A sites and examine the linguistic congruence between questions and answers.

In online knowledge communities, discussion threads are commonly grouped into categories based on the thread topics. Therefore, we conjecture that the associated category labels could be useful to enhance topic modeling. To validate this and demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in this extensively studied context of online knowledge communities, we obtain a total of 16,884 posts belonging to 3,732 unique discussion questions and their $8 , 4 \bar { 4 } 3$ answers in eight categories on Stack Exchange. The eight categories are anime, English, gaming, history, outdoors, politics, travel, and writers. Each question receives 2.26 answers on average, with a standard deviation of 1.98.

For both data sets, we improve the data quality by applying standard preprocessing, such as removing punctuation marks, numbers, URLs, stop words, and rare words appearing fewer than <sup>fi</sup>ve times. We keep the most frequent 2,000 words as the topic modeling vocabulary. We randomly split the data set into 80% as the training and validation set and the remaining 20% as the test set. ## 5. Model Fit on Text Data Set

One common evaluation of a probabilistic topic model is its model <sup>fi</sup>tness on a held-out test set (Blei et al. 2003). This is critical because poorly <sup>fi</sup>tted models may result in measurement error that causes inaccurate estimations in empirical analysis (Yang et al. 2018) and predictive tasks, a possibility that we will examine in later sections. Our purpose here is to examine whether sDTM outperforms existing unsupervised/supervised topic models in terms of model <sup>fi</sup>tness (high likelihood on the test set). In particular, we evaluate the model <sup>fi</sup>tness using a common metric, perplexity, on the test data set. Perplexity is computed as a function of negative log-likelihood on a held-out test set, de<sup>fi</sup>ned as

$$
p e r p l e x i t y = \exp \left\{- \frac {1}{D _ {t e s t}} \sum^ {N _ {d}} \frac {1}{N _ {d}} \log p (\mathbf {d} \mid \boldsymbol {t}) \right\},
$$

where $D _ { t e s t }$ is the number of test documents, $N _ { d }$ denotes the number of tokens in a test document $d ,$ and log p <sup>d t</sup> is the log likelihood of words in the documents. A lower perplexity number indicates higher data likelihood, which suggests that the topic modeling has better generalizability.

The unsupervised topic model baselines are the following: Latent Dirichlet allocation (Blei et al. 2003) is the most cited and widely used topic modeling method. BP-sLDA (Chen et al. 2015) is a supervised LDA model using backpropagation for inference. The supervised neural network topic model (sNNTM; Cao et al. 2015) uses a supervised deep neural network to model the document-topic relationships. However, it models the supervised label as a function of topics.

Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we examine the relationship between the complexity of a review and its perceived helpfulness. Previous literature has investigated the effect of information complexity on consumer behaviors. For example, in modeling consumer behavior on search engines, Ghose et al. (2019) measure the noisiness of information provided by consumer reviews and <sup>fi</sup>nd that as the review becomes more diverse and the information becomes noisier, users are more likely to get overwhelmed and discouraged by the complexity of information and are likely to abandon their search of the online platform.

In a similar research setting, Gong et al. (2018) examine the relationship between the search keyword ambiguity and click-through rate. Shin. et al. (2020) study the determinants of social media ad effectiveness and <sup>fi</sup>nd that more complex social media posts tend to have fewer likes and reposts. In all of the above studies, researchers measure text complexity using the entropy of the review’s topic features derived from topic models. Following these studies, we construct the topic entropy variable as the Shannon index (entropy) of the review’s topic features:

$$
\text { TopicEntropy } _ {i} = - \sum_ {k = 1} ^ {K} \text { Topic } _ {i k} \log (\text { Topic } _ {i k}),
$$

where Topic is k’s topic probability of review i. The higher the entropy, the more complex or noisier the reviews. Using LDA, NTM, and our approach sDTM, we derive three review complexity measures, TopicEntropy\_LDA, TopicEntropy\_NTM, and TopicEntropy\_sDTM, respectively.

On Yelp, readers of a review can indicate its helpfulness by casting a useful vote, and such a helpfulness voting system is a common feature of major online review platforms such as Amazon and TripAdvisor. The helpfulness system, which relies on the crowd to gauge the perceived value of information provided in a review, helps reduce information overload and highlight more valuable contents (Jones et al. 2004).

According to empirical evidence in prior literature (Ghose et al. 2019), we expect a review with higher topic entropy (nosier and more complex information) to be perceived as less useful. To evaluate the quality of our sDTM, we examine the relationship between the topic entropy and review helpfulness in terms of the effect size, effect directionality, and statistical signi<sup>fi</sup>cance, and compare its performance with that of LDA and the NTM.

The independent variable, TopicEntropy, is measured by LDA, NTM, and sDTM, respectively. We also include several control variables, including (1) sequence, the number of reviews posted before the focal review; (2) sentiment, the binary sentiment score of the review rating; and (3) words, the number of words in the review. The dependent variable, helpfulness, is highly skewed, with many reviews receiving a helpfulness score of zero. Therefore, we take the log of the helpfulness measures. We estimate the ordinary least squares (OLS) model illustrated in the equation below:

$$
\begin{array}{r} \log (h e l p f u l n e s s + 1) = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} T o p i c E n t r o p y + \beta_ {2} S e q u e n c e \\ + \beta_ {3} S e n t i m e n t + \beta_ {4} \mathrm{log} (w o r d s) + \epsilon . \end{array}
$$

In a robustness check model, we estimate a second OLS model with the numerical helpfulness score, as

$$
\begin{array}{r} h e l p f u l n e s s = \beta_ {0} + \beta_ {1} T o p i c E n t r o p y + \beta_ {2} S e q u e n c e \\ + \beta_ {3} S e n t i m e n t + \beta_ {4} \log (w o r d s) + \epsilon . \end{array}
$$

The regression results are shown in Table 8. We can see that the association between TopicEntropy\_sDTM and review helpfulness is negative and signi<sup>fi</sup>cant in both models. In other words, a more complex and noisier review tends to be perceived as less helpful. This association is consistent with <sup>fi</sup>ndings in prior literature that as a review becomes more diverse and the information becomes noisier, users are more likely to get overwhelmed and discouraged by the complexity of information and thus <sup>fi</sup>nd the review less useful (Ghose et al. 2019). On the contrary, the topic entropy variable derived by LDA or NTM does not reveal such a strong correlation. In OLS Model 1, TopicEntropy\_LDA is negatively correlated with helpfulness but less statistically signi<sup>fi</sup>cant (p 0.065), and TopicEntropy\_NTM is negatively correlated with helpfulness but with no statistical signi<sup>fi</sup>cance. In OLS Model 2, nei ther TopicEntropy\_LDA nor TopicEntropy\_NTM is statistically signi<sup>fi</sup>cant.

Even though the topic entropy variable derived by LDA is signi<sup>fi</sup>cant, with $p < 0 . 1$ in OLS Model 1, the pvalue of the topic entropy variable derived by sDTM is much lower, that is, $p \ < \ 0 . 0 1$ , indicating higher utility in empirical study. To demonstrate this, we compare the regression results of LDA and sDTM using subsamples for which accurately deriving topic features is especially important because of lower power. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we <sup>fi</sup>rst randomly sample 20% of the original data set, which results in a subsample with 1,600 observations. We iterate this process 1,000 times and then estimate the same regression model with each subsample. The results in Table 9 show that the topic entropy variable derived by sDTM is more reliable than that derived by LDA in the subsample analysis. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, the coef<sup>fi</sup>cient of the TopicEntropy variable derived by sDTM is statistically signi<sup>fi</sup>- cant for the majority of the random subsamples (i.e., out of 1,000 estimations, 835, 744, and 484 are signi<sup>fi</sup>- cant at the 10%, 5%, and 1% levels, respectively). However, the TopicEntropy variable by LDA is signi<sup>fi</sup>- cant only at the 1% level in 54 subsamples. This analysis implies that using sDTM can reduce the incidence of incorrectly accepting the null hypothesis when the sample size is small. Therefore, we believe that sDTM’s greater accuracy in this regard increases the power of empirical tests and highlights its value proposition.

## 6.2. Case Study 2: Question–Answer Similarity on Answer Helpfulness

We conduct the second study in the online knowledge community context. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we examine the relationship between the topic similarity of an answer to its question and the answer’s perceived helpfulness. Online knowledge communities are important venues where a general audience can seek help and advice from experts and knowledgeable participants through question-and-answer threads. A growing body of IS literature studies the usefulness of answers in these online knowledge communities in order to better serve users’ knowledge needs and to bolster the com munities’ success (Liu et al. 2020, Peng et al. 2020).

According to prior literature, one of the key factors accounting for answer helpfulness is its semantic con sistence with the corresponding question. For example, Peng et al. (2020) <sup>fi</sup>nd that in an online healthcare knowledge community, an answer will be perceived as more helpful if the linguistic characteristics of the answer’s content are congruent with those of the associated question. However, their congruence measure is heavily reliant on dictionary and human annotations, which is not easy to scale up. On the other hand, Liu et al. (2020) also demonstrate that topic modeling (e.g., LDA) can be used to automatically extract and summarize text features from questions and answers. Following both works, we use topic models to obtain text features from questions and answers, and then construct the question–answer congruence measure as the cosine similarity between their learned topic features. According to Peng et al. (2020), we expect to see a positive relationship between the similarity of an answer to its question and the perceived answer helpfulness.

To demonstrate such a relationship, we perform a regression analysis. The independent variable, QASimilarity, is question-and-answer similarity at the topic level, which is derived by standard LDA, NTM, and sDTM (named as QASimilarity\_LDA, QASimilarity\_NTM, and QASimilarity\_sDTM, respectively). The unit of analysis is a question–answer pair. We follow Peng et al. (2020) to include the following control variables: (1) Sequence, the number of answers posted prior to the focal answer; (2) QuestionHelpfulness, the number of votes a question receives; (3) AnswerWords, the number of words in the answer; (4) QuestionWords, the number of words in the question; and (5) Answer-Readability, the reading dif<sup>fi</sup>culty of the answer calculated by the Coleman–Liau index. The dependent variable, AnswerHelpfulness, is the number of votes an answer receives. ## 7.1. Baselines

We choose a set of comprehensive benchmark models to compare sDTM with. The <sup>fi</sup>rst group of baselines comprises the unsupervised latent Dirichlet allocation and neural topic model. Following the two-stage approach, the topic features derived from the unsupervised topic model are then fed into a discriminative model (e.g., an SVM) to build a supervised classi<sup>fi</sup>cation model. The second group of baselines comprises the supervised topic models that are listed in the model <sup>fi</sup>t section. They are sLDA, MedLDA, BP-sLDA, and sNNTM. In addition to the supervised topic model baselines, we also include a supervised dimension reduction method, supervised principal component analysis (PCA), as inspired by Mankad et al. (2018). For supervised topic modeling baselines, we directly apply the trained model on the out-of-sample testing data to make predictions.

Recent work in NLP employs a deep-learning approach for text prediction. Although the deeplearning approach has strong discriminative power, it can be used only for end-to-end prediction tasks, and it cannot produce topic-wise summarizations of a large text collection. We include several deep-learning baselines as strong benchmarks to compare with sDTM.

The <sup>fi</sup>rst deep-learning baseline is the RNN attention model, where RNN is implemented using a GRU. This model is the supervised component in our sDTM approach. Therefore, we can use this baseline as an ablation study to examine how much we can gain from having the neural topic modeling in sDTM. The second deep-learning baseline is Bi-LSTM. We choose this baseline because Bi-LSTM has shown strong predictive performance in document classi<sup>fi</sup>cation tasks (Adhikari et al. 2019). The third and fourth deeplearning baselines are the transformer-based DistilBERT (Sanh et al. 2019) and BERT models (Devlin et al. 2019),<sup>4</sup> which have achieved state-of-the-art performance in various NLP tasks. Following the literature on using BERT for document classi<sup>fi</sup>cation (Sun et al. 2019), we <sup>fi</sup>ne-tune the DistilBERT and BERT models on the training set and then evaluate them on the test set.

## 7.2. Evaluation Metrics

Because Yelp sentiment prediction is a binary classi<sup>fi</sup>- cation with an imbalanced class label (more positive instances than negative instances), we report the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) as the evaluation metric. The value of the AUROC score ranges from zero to one, and a higher AUROC score indicates a better predictive model. For the Stack Exchange data set, because the prediction outcome category has multiple values and the data set is quite balanced, we report the standard classi<sup>fi</sup>cation accuracy, which is the percentage of correctly classi-<sup>fi</sup>ed instances, as the evaluation metric.

The prediction results are presented in Table 11. All numbers reported are based on the average performance of 30 runs on the test set. We also vary the number of topics K for robustness. The above results lead to several observations. First, sDTM consistently improves the out-of-sample prediction accuracy over all supervised and unsupervised topic model baselines in two data sets signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly and substantially. In the Yelp review data set, for example, the standard two-stage approach with LDA has an AUROC of 0.775 when K is 25, whereas sDTM boosts the AUROC to 0.948. Second, sDTM outperforms its unsupervised neural topic model counterpart by a large margin. This signi<sup>fi</sup>cant improvement can be attributed to the topic attention layer that integrates a recurrent neural network with the NTM. As shown in Section 5, the unsupervised neural topic model has a low perplexity. However, this comes at the cost of predictive performance, which is poor. In contrast, sDTM combines the merits of both the unsupervised NTM and supervised RNN, therefore improving the prediction accuracy and model <sup>fi</sup>t simultaneously. Third, our sDTM approach achieves performance comparable to and even better than that of state-of-the-art deep-learning approaches. For example, comparing sDTM with RNN attention, the prediction improvement is statistically signi<sup>fi</sup>cant, indicating that the topical information learned from the neural topic modeling component can provide additional and complementary information for the prediction. The performance of sDTM is comparable to that of the transformer-based deep-learning model BERT. This is encouraging because unlike BERT, which trains on millions of text documents using massive and expensive GPU resources, our sDTM is ef<sup>fi</sup>cient and can be trained using the customized text data set. In comparison with a smaller and lighter transformerbased deep-learning model, such as DistilBERT, sDTM performs even better.

This predictive study highlights how sDTM can learn predictive features much better than previous supervised or unsupervised topic modeling methods, with performance even comparable to that of the state-of-the-art BERT model. Although it is a topic model at its core, sDTM offers advantages over BERT, as it can summarize text documents in a standard topic model fashion so that IS and business discipline researchers can adopt the identi<sup>fi</sup>ed topics in thei empirical studies. This is challenging to achieve with an end-to-end “black-box” deep-learning model such as BERT, whose output is a high-dimensional vector representation of a document without meaningful interpretation. In the case of review complexity, for example, sDTM can automatically summarize this variable from the document topic features. In the case of supervised deep-learning models such as BERT, a researcher would have to recruit a group of annotators to manually label the complexity of reviews, which is more subjective and error-prone.

## 8. Discussion and Conclusions

Our reviews of the literature establish that topic modeling is a powerful tool with growing use in IS and management research, usually with the goal of using text data to extract insights that are then combined with subsequent analyses. However, we <sup>fi</sup>nd that while much real-world text data come with auxiliary information, such as labels, this information has not been incorporated into traditional LDA models and thus may result in poor performance in topic analysis. This may further lead to less accurate or even wrong estimation in empirical studies or to poor performance in predictive analytics. We therefore propose and evaluate a supervised deep topic modeling approach that can incorporate auxiliary information (e.g., labels) to improve document modeling performance, so our method can bene<sup>fi</sup>t researchers who frequently apply topic modeling to extract insights from text data.

Our deep-learning-based supervised topic modeling approach combines unsupervised neural topic modeling with supervised recurrent neural networks. Experiments conducted on two highly IS-relevant data sets (online consumer reviews and an online knowledge Q&A forum) demonstrate that our proposed approach signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly improves topic model quality in various ways. Not only can the approach alleviate concerns about measurement errors caused by using inaccurate machine learning models in empirical analysis, but our supervised model greatly improves prediction accuracy in predictive analytics. Thus, the main takeaway is that sDTM allows researchers to leverage auxiliary labels to facilitate high-quality topic modeling. Moreover, IS and business discipline scholars can use sDTM directly as a plug-and-play tool without making signi<sup>fi</sup>cant changes to their text analysis pipelines.

Our empirical studies and predictive evaluations on online consumer reviews and on an online knowledge community highlight how sDTM can advance text analysis in IS research. In other business disciplines such as accounting and <sup>fi</sup>nance, our approach can also be useful. For example, accounting and <sup>fi</sup>nance studies have used topic modeling to gauge the topical information in corporate disclosures (Bao and Datta 2014, Dyer et al. 2017, Huang et al. 2017). These disclosures, such as 10-K and earnings conference call transcripts, are usually very long, so when analyzing them, transformer-based deep-learning models suffer from the maximum sequence limitation. Yet our approach, which at its core is a dimension reduction method, can alleviate this problem. In the corporate disclosure context, the industry code is often associated with the <sup>fi</sup>rm. As such, we can treat the industry code as the auxiliary label to guide the topic model learning. The promise is that the resultant topic model will learn better representations of corporate disclosure so that empirical researchers can derive more accurate and meaningful variables from the topic features, such as comparing the similarities between a <sup>fi</sup>rm’s disclosure and an analyst’s report (Huang et al. 2017), measuring the risk topics in a <sup>fi</sup>rm’s disclosure (Bao and Datta 2014), or deriving an innovation metric (Bellstam et al. 2021).

Practitioners can also bene<sup>fi</sup>t from sDTM. As demonstrated in real-world applications (Kingma and Welling 2013, Miao et al. 2017), a deep-learning-based topic model approach is more effective in document understanding than traditional LDA-based topic models. As a result, cloud-based AI platforms such as Amazon Web Services have begun to offer variational autoencoding-based neural topic modeling for text topic analysis. Our open-source approach can be an accurate and convenient tool for practitioners to adopt in their text analysis pipelines as well.

This work has several limitations that can be addressed in future work. First, we have shown the superiority of sDTM on two data sets that are representative of IS research that exploits topic modeling. We also present a robustness check on a movie review data set with numerical response variables (Online Appendix C) and a Wikipedia data set with much lon ger documents (Online Appendix D). Yet there are other management areas in which topic modeling has also been applied, such as accounting, where it has been used to study analyst reports or corporate disclosures. Although we expect sDTM to improve understanding of these accounting texts and facilitate a deeper understanding of a <sup>fi</sup>rm’s strategies, this conjecture remains to be tested in future research. Because each <sup>fi</sup>nancial document is related to one of several industry sectors, we may leverage sector information as auxiliary labels. Second, like other machine learning and topic modeling methods, sDTM involves hyperparameters that must be tuned to <sup>fi</sup>nd the optimal combination for every application. It may be worthwhile to <sup>fi</sup>nd more cost-ef<sup>fi</sup>cient training methods.

IS research calls for effective computational methods that leverage the power of big data analytics (Agarwal and Dhar 2014). We hope our development of a high-quality supervised deep topic modeling approach can help IS and business discipline scholars and business practitioners to harness the power of machine learning methods in understanding and gaining insights from unstructured text data. To this end, we open source our implementation in Python to maximize the research and practical impacts (see https://github.com/yya518/sDTM).
