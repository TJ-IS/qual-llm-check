# Winner Takes All? The Blockbuster Effect on Crowdfunding Platforms

Abstract. Crowdfunding platforms are characterized by an uneven distribution of quality and resources. Some projects achieve outstanding and exceptional performance. Those projects, which we call “blockbuster projects,” are believed to have a considerable impact on crowdfunding platforms, especially on other concurrent projects. There are two plausi ble yet opposing predictions: they may exhibit both a negative effect by monopolizing backer attention and resources and a positive effect by increasing the activeness of backer side. This tension could be further complicated, considering that blockbusters are inherently heterogeneous, which could result in different interrelations with other concurrent projects. To fully understand this tension, this study examines the effects of blockbuster projects on the overall performance of concurrent projects on the platform and the nuances of such effects for different interrelations between blockbusters and other projects. Drawing on the cross-side network effects literature and integrating insights from the unique features of crowdfunding, we develop a theoretical framework that highlights the multidimensional view of blockbuster effects: we propose that blockbuster projects have an overall positive effect on the performance of concurrent projects (overall effect), and such positive effect is stronger for related blockbusters (local effect) and blockbusters emerging before the focal project (temporal effect). Using data from a leading reward-based crowdfunding platform, the proposed hypotheses are tested and largely supported. Additional analyses, based on off-platform data and backer data, verify the mechanism driving the blockbuste effects: blockbusters increase the popularity of the platform, whereby backers participating in the blockbusters tend to develop positive impressions and heightened expectations, so that they are likely to participate in other concurrent projects. This study contributes to the crowdfunding literature on interactions among projects, the literature on distinctive agent in online markets, and the cross-side network effects literature. We also discuss practical implications for project creators and platform operators.

## 1. Introduction

Given the challenges that entrepreneurs face in acquiring <sup>fi</sup>nancial capital, crowdfunding has emerged as a viable way to seek resources for a project or venture (Schwienbacher and Larralde 2010, Belle<sup>fl</sup>amme et al. 2014, Burtch et al. 2018). Since its inception, crowdfunding has helped a large number of new ventures acquire the funds necessary to launch businesses and ful<sup>fi</sup>ll social visions (Hong et al. 2018). The volume and amount of transactions taking place on crowdfunding platforms continue to grow with the persistent popularity of such platforms.

Crowdfunding platforms, as typical two-sided platforms for resource acquisition and economic transactions (Zvilichovsky et al. 2013, Ryu 2018), are characterized by an uneven distribution of quality and resources. Some projects attract a disproportionately high amount of funds, which draws greater attention from backers and induces a shift in resource allocation among projects. In particular, several overwhelmingly successful projects have emerged on crowdfunding platforms in recent years, some of which have attracted unprecedented contributions and backer attention. For instance, Double Fine Adventure—a video game project launched on Kickstarter in February 2012—raised USD 3,336,371 from 87,142 backers, making it the largest project ever on Kickstarter at the time, with outstanding funding performance and excellent backer awareness. Such notable projects have drawn attention not only from existing backers already on the platform, but also from many new individuals, who were encouraged to join the platform and become backers. For instance, 70% of Double Fine Adventure’s backers (61,013 of 87,142) were new to the platform.<sup>1</sup>

These overwhelmingly successful projects with distinguished funding performance, which we refer to as “blockbuster projects” (Elberse 2013), are believed to have a considerable impact on crowdfunding platforms and especially on other concurrent projects. However, the actual in<sup>fl</sup>uence of blockbuster projects remains enigmatic. There are two plausible yet opposing predictions. On the one hand, an in-progress blockbuster project may intensify competition across the platform, such that other projects’ performance is undermined. Blockbuster projects may monopolize backers’ attention and consequently preempt resources so that other concurrent projects receive less attention and fewer contributions (Lee et al. 2014). This prediction implies a negative cannibalization effect of blockbuster projects on other concurrent projects on the platform. Conversely, however, other projects may bene<sup>fi</sup>t from the increased popularity of the platform generated by the blockbusters (Ghose et al. 2006). The increased popularity of the platform could increase the activeness of existing backers and expand the potential backer population with new backers joining the platform such that a greater amount of <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources can be shared by other projects. This prediction implies a positive spillover effect through which other concurrent projects may receive even more contributions because of the blockbusters. Therefore, given these two competing effects, the actual impacts of blockbusters remain unclear, as does how these effects materialize.<sup>2</sup>

Furthermore, given that blockbusters are inherently heterogeneous and have various interrelations with other concurrent projects, their effects on those projects may not be uniform (Suarez 2005, Sundararajan

2008). For example, from the perspective of each of the other projects (i.e., the focal project), a blockbuster can be more or less related to the focal project, and it can emerge as a blockbuster at a certain point in time before or after the launch of the focal project (Boudreau 2012). Such heterogeneities in the interrelations between blockbusters and other projects shape how blockbusters in<sup>fl</sup>uence other projects, as blockbusters positive or negative effects can be manifested or attenuated depending on their relationship with other projects. In this regard, the tension of the blockbuster effect is further complicated by the interrelations between blockbusters and other projects. Therefore, to fully understand the tension of blockbusters, the objectives of this study are to investigate (1) the effects of blockbuster projects on the overall funding performance of concurrent projects on the platform (i.e., overall blockbuster effects) and (2) how different interrelations between blockbusters and other projects shape the blockbuster effects.

Our study investigates this tension in blockbusters effects on concurrent projects from a network effects perspective (Burtch and Ramaprasad 2016, Chu and Manchanda 2016, Song et al. 2018). Drawing on the cross-side network effects literature, we posit that blockbuster projects change the size and participation of the backer side, which in turn in<sup>fl</sup>uences the performance of other projects. By integrating insights from studies of consumer behavior and crowdfunding features with this literature, we develop a research framework that highlights the multidimensional view of blockbuster effects. We <sup>fi</sup>rst propose that the positive effect of blockbusters is dominant over their negative effect on other concurrent projects and that blockbusters therefore, on average, exhibit a positive effect on concurrent projects (overall effect). We then consider two aspects relating to the interrelations between blockbusters and other projects: content relatedness and emergent timing of blockbusters with respect to a focal project. We propose that related blockbusters exhibit a stronger positive effect on a focal project compared with unrelated blockbusters (local effect), and that blockbusters that emerge before the launch of focal project also have a stronger positive effect than those that emerge after (temporal effect). The overall effect resolves the tension arising from the negative cannibalization and positive spillover effect, whereas local and temporal effects are situated on the heterogeneity of blockbusters. Thus, we offer nuanced insights into the blockbuster effects.

Using both campaign-level and campaign-day-level data from Kickstarter.com, one of the largest reward based crowdfunding platforms, we identi<sup>fi</sup>ed 29 blockbuster projects and examined their concurrent effects at the campaign-day level. The results largely support our theoretical predictions. Furthermore, to identify the causal links, we conducted platform trend analysis with off-platform data $( \mathrm { i . e . , }$ search trend index, social media activity, and news article exposure) and backer analysis with backer list data for each project. The <sup>fi</sup>ndings con<sup>fi</sup>rm the proposed theoretical mechanisms: blockbusters increase the popularity of the platform and are of high quality, which deliver positive experiences to existing and new backers participating in the blockbusters; these backers have positive impressions and heightened expectations on the quality of crowdfunding projects so that they are likely to participate in other concurrent projects, which essentially drives the blockbuster effect. We also conducted a series of robustness checks to test the endogenous entry of blockbusters, resolve endogeneity issues, rule out alternative explanations, and validate the de<sup>fi</sup>nitions and measures of blockbusters.

By investigating blockbuster effects in crowdfunding, we make several notable contributions to theory and practice. We extend the growing crowdfunding literature on interactions among projects by complementing the competition view, documenting how high-performance projects actually bene<sup>fi</sup>t other projects through increased backer participation, and unveiling the underlying mechanisms of project interactions via backing activities. We also advance the literature on distinctive agents (e.g., superstars) in an online market by addressing the tension arising from the concurrent effects of distinctive agents and shedding light on how the platform mechanisms that regulate participant interactions could play a role therein. Furthermore, our results extend the cross-side network effects literature by suggesting the prominent contributions of highly in<sup>fl</sup>uential agents in fomenting network effects, and by demonstrating both the locality and temporality of the effects. Beyond theory, we provide implications for crowdfunding project creators and platform operators regarding how to reap the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts from concurrent blockbusters. We advise platform managers to promote blockbusters, particularly on relatively open two-sided platforms. Sellers in twosided markets are also encouraged to introduce product features such as limited-time offerings in order to capitalize on the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of blockbuster sellers.

## 2. Related Literature

In this section, we highlight literature relevant to this study. First, we discuss the network effects literature on two-sided platforms, laying the foundation for our theoretical framework. We then focus on the special features of crowdfunding, which allow us to contextualize the network effects in crowdfunding. We also discuss the existing studies on interactions among crowdfunding projects in terms of how the performance of one project relates to other projects.

## 2.1. Network Effects on Two-Sided Platforms

Crowdfunding platforms are essentially two-sided platforms and so face the typical challenge of attracting and retaining participants on both the supply and demand sides for sustained development. An important notion with respect to maintaining the growth and participation on both sides is cross-side network effects (Katz and Shapiro 1985): the phenomenon where the size and activities on one side of the plat form depend on those on the other side (Rysman 2009, Hinz et al. 2020). The participation of one side of the platform may either reinforce or undermine the growth and participation of the other. Such cross-side network effects are especially prevalent on online platforms, such as e-commerce, sharing economy, and software platforms.

Empirical work on cross-side network effects is somewhat limited, albeit growing rapidly.<sup>3</sup> The basic theme in this literature is the identi<sup>fi</sup>cation of the presence and the estimation of the magnitude of cross-side network effects in various contexts (e.g., Rysman 2004, Wilbur 2008). In other words, the estimated effects concern the overall bene<sup>fi</sup>t (or detriment) that participants bring to those on the other side. For example, Tucker and Zhang (2010) suggested that, on a property listing platform, displaying the number of buyers is useful in attracting more sellers. And Boudreau and Jeppesen (2015) found that greater platform usage positively affected unpaid complementors on game development platforms.

In addition to the overall effect, a burgeoning body of studies has revealed that the strength of cross-side network effects exhibits heterogeneous patterns. The notions of particular importance here are local and temporal effects. Local network effects represent that the strength of effects can be stronger (or weaker) for a subset of agents depending on whether they share certain characteristics (Sundararajan 2008). Burtch and Ramaprasad (2016), for example, explored local network effects on an online dating platform and found that the extents of network effects differed for local (similar) and nonlocal (dissimilar) users. From a user seeding strategy, they suggested that cross-side network effects from new users tend to be stronger for certain groups of users (e.g., those colocated with the existing platform users, with similar body types, or have similar ages). Temporal network effects highlight that the effects are not static, but can instead vary over time (e.g., Cheng and Liu 2012). For instance, Chu and Manchanda (2016) found that on an e-commerce platform, the number of buyers can be driven by existing sellers, and that such effects initially increase but then decrease over time. On a software platform, Song et al. (2018) considered temporal patterns of network effects and suggested that soft ware apps could motivate users to join the platform.

However, this effect was only temporary and faded away over time, whereas having more users on the platform persistently led to more app releases over the long term. Taken together, the relative strength of network effects may be determined by the interrelations between agents, particularly similarity of characteristics and temporal differences.

Integrating the above insights, we develop a nuanced understanding of cross-side network effects in crowdfunding from a multidimensional perspective: the overall, local, and temporal effects. Against this backdrop, we posit that overwhelmingly successful crowdfunding projects (i.e., blockbuster projects) could affect the backer side of the platform because they could potentially change backer participation (in terms of the size of the backer base and activeness of backers) in other concurrent projects. Such changes could manifest as enhanced performance of other concurrent projects. Simply put, one project’s effect on another is essentially driven by the changes in backer activities on the complementary side of the platform. This process is aligned with the notion of cross-side network effects. Therefore, drawing on insights from this literature, we address our research questions by conceptualizing the blockbuster effect in terms of the aforementioned aspects: the overall blockbuster effect resolves the tension arising from the intensi<sup>fi</sup>ed competitions and positive spillover effect; the local and temporal dimensions provide nuanced insights into that effect.

Furthermore, we focus on blockbuster projects that are likely to have noteworthy effects on the participation of the backer side because of their overwhelming success. This research focus extends prior works by investigating the role of in<sup>fl</sup>uential participants rather than of interchangeable ones in cross-side network effects, given that limited attention has been paid thus far to the distinctive contributions of certain participants to cross-side network effects. However, this consideration is of great importance, as it has been recognized that participants with distinctive features could potentially exhibit a disproportionate network effect (Suarez 2005).

## 2.2. Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding—typically deemed as a form of twosided market—connects creators with novel projects to potential backers with <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources, and facilitates transactions between them (Zvilichovsky et al. 2013, Ryu 2018). The crowdfunding market is characterized by several unique features.<sup>4</sup> First, unlike product listings in e-commerce, crowdfunding projects are constrained by a predetermined duration (Kuppuswamy and Bayus 2017a); that is, backers are no longer able to contribute to a project after its fundraising deadline has passed. This could evoke a sense of urgency among prospective backers with regard to supporting a project in a timely manner (Yang et al. 2020). Second, backing decisions are characterized by delayed grati<sup>fi</sup>cation, as backers can receive product deliverables only after the campaign deadline. This implies that backers could perceive uncertainties at the time of backing (Jiang et al. 2021). In addition, backing decisions are shaped by mul tiple considerations and motivations (Gerber and Hui 2013, Lin et al. 2014, Gong et al. 2021, Jiang et al. 2021). When contributing to a project, backers may behave not only like consumers, who are primarily driven by the utilitarian and functional bene<sup>fi</sup>ts offered by project deliverables, but also like supporters, who may be motivated by the socioemotional and participatory bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of the opportunity to participate in and support the entrepreneurial process beyond monetary contributions (Jiang et al. 2021). Simply put, crowdfunding backers play dual roles of consumers and supporters. As such, their contributions are not driven purely by functional bene<sup>fi</sup>ts (Gerber et al. 2012). Therefore, in the fol lowing section, we integrate insights from consumer behavior and unique features of crowdfunding platforms with cross-side network effects to propose blockbuster effects.

Considering that blockbuster effects involve how one project relates to another, this study is particularly relevant to recent works demonstrating interactions among projects.<sup>5</sup> Geva et al. (2019) examined the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of a sharp increase in low-quality projects on the performance of other projects on a crowdfunding platform and suggested that the funding performance of other projects was negatively affected, but that this effect could be mitigated for high-quality projects. More pertinently, Doshi (2014) examined the impact of performance outliers in crowdfunding projects on the entry and performance of subsequent projects and found that subsequent projects could bene<sup>fi</sup>t from performance outliers.

Both studies offer important implications for how the performance of one project relates to that of others. Our study is closely related to the above two studies, with some notable differences. First, our focus is on the concurrent effects of overwhelmingly successful projects, whereas Doshi (2014) examined the long-term effects on the platform. As noted earlier, crowdfunding projects have predetermined durations. It can be expected that projects are directly affected by others with overlapping durations through competition for resources, and that, when some projects become overwhelmingly successful, others may suffer because of the attention concentrated on these projects. Alternatively, the salient visibility of highly successful projects may attract new backers to join the platform and motivate existing backers to be active, thereby increasing the overall pool of <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources, and in turn bene<sup>fi</sup>ting other projects. This tension, arising from the two opposing predictions regarding the effects on concurrent projects speaks to the necessity and importance of such an examination. Second, we analyze backing activities to uncover the mechanisms underlying the effects. As discussed above, project performance is manifested as backer activities on the complementary side. Therefore, our analyses of backing activities generate insight into the observed effects, which are notably lacking in Doshi (2014). Third, we examine the effect of highly successful projects, particularly those of high quality (Kim and Hann 2019), whose effects are likely to differ from those of low-quality projects; the former are likely to increase the network size of the backers, whereas the latter are unlikely to change the overall backer base on the platform (Geva et al. 2019). Such a difference makes it dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to directly generalize prior <sup>fi</sup>ndings to our context.

Overall, our study augments and complements the extant crowdfunding literature on interactions among projects by exploring the concurrent effects of overwhelmingly successful projects and unveiling the mechanisms at play by analyzing backing activities. To this end, we integrate insights from the cross-side network effects literature into crowdfunding, while incorporating the unique features of crowdfunding into our theorizing.

## 3. Hypothesis Development

The objective of this study is to examine the effects of overwhelmingly successful crowdfunding projects on other projects. These overwhelmingly successful projects, analogized to highly popular and pro<sup>fi</sup>table items in other markets, can be termed blockbusters. The term blockbuster, originating in the motion picture industry, refers to a product that is recognized as having the ability to create widespread awareness among consumers and obtain disproportionate market success (Elberse 2013, Stringer 2013). Recent research has regarded the blockbuster as a phenomenon, wherein consumers interact with products that experience high popularity and huge <sup>fi</sup>nancial success (Soubliere\` and Gehman 2020). Echoing this de<sup>fi</sup>nition and the literature on blockbusters, we de<sup>fi</sup>ne blockbuster projects in crowdfunding as those projects with outstanding and exceptional performance among all projects on a platform such that they attract great backer awareness and garner an extraordinarily large proportion of contributions.

Based on the insights we synthesized from the crossside network effects literature, we develop our theoretical framework from a multidimensional perspective. First, because blockbuster projects may have both negative and positive effects on other concurrent projects, we resolve this tension by <sup>fi</sup>rst proposing overall blockbuster effects. Next, given that crowdfunding projects could vary in terms of content relatedness and emergent timing, we obtain a nuanced understanding of local and temporal effects by considering the extent to which blockbusters relate to other projects and the timing of blockbusters relative to other projects, respectively.

## 3.1. Overall Effect of Blockbuster Projects

The <sup>fi</sup>rst hypothesis concerns overall blockbuster effects on concurrent projects. Drawing on the crossside network effects literature, we posit that blockbuster projects exhibit both negative and positive effects. A negative effect occurs when blockbusters monopolize backers’ attention and resource contributions, thereby diminishing backers’ contributions to other projects. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, the noteworthy performance and salient popularity of blockbuster projects are likely to increase backers’ value of contributing to it, thus decreasing backers’ value of contributing to other projects. In this regard, market competition may be intensi<sup>fi</sup>ed. If this were to occur, it would be more arduous for other concurrent projects to achieve their funding goals, because a large amount of backer attention and resources would be absorbed by the blockbuster projects (Schilling 2002, Noe and Parker 2005). As a result, a negative cross-side network effect can be expected wherein backers’ value of contributing to other concurrent projects is decreased. This could manifest as a negative effect of blockbusters on the performance of other concurrent projects, given that backer participation in these concurrent projects is likely to be cannibalized by blockbuster projects (Ghose et al. 2006, Zhang et al. 2019).

However, a positive cross-side network effect could also occur when blockbuster projects are able to alter the backer side of the platform by enlarging the scale of resources and by increasing the ladder of participation (Shankar and Bayus 2003). Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, blockbuster projects can signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly increase platform popularity and awareness, are typically of high quality, and thus have the potential to create positive experiences (e.g., satisfaction) for their backers (Ha and Perks 2005).<sup>6</sup> Through such positive experiences, new or existing backers who contribute to blockbuster projects may develop positive impressions and heightened expectations regarding crowdfunding projects in general (Cheng and Liu 2012). This could reduce backers’ perceived uncertainty and increase their trust or con<sup>fi</sup>dence in the quality of crowdfunding projects (Chen and Dibb 2010). Therefore, blockbuster backers receive positive feedback from their contributions, and they are likely to consider other crowdfunding projects to which they can contribute (Tussyadiah et al. 2011, Bone et al. 2017). As a result, backers attracted to blockbuster projects may actually also contribute to other concurrent projects. Simply put, the positive feedback arising from the positive experience of backing blockbusters likely induces continued backer participation in other projects (Huang et al. 2014), resulting in behaviors of cobacking blockbusters and other projects. In this regard, the greater effective network size on the backer side (in terms of active new backers and increased activeness of existing backers) is likely to be shared by concurrent projects, which would result in a positive spillover effect from blockbuster projects to concurrent projects (Strahilevitz 2003). Consequently, we may expect a positive cross-side network effect, which manifests as higher overall funding performance by other concurrent projects, due to the growth on the backer side.

Although both negative and positive effects of blockbuster projects can be expected based on the network effects literature, we posit that the positive effect will be more pronounced in crowdfunding for the following reasons. First, crowdfunding projects have predetermined durations (Kuppuswamy and Bayus 2017a). When a regular focal project overlaps with a blockbuster, even if the blockbuster attracts a signi<sup>fi</sup>- cant amount of backer attention and consequently monopolizes <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources from regular projects, it may not fully deplete backers’ intentions to support the regular project. This is because the regular project is available only for a certain period of time, and its limited duration could evoke a sense of urgency to support it (Yang et al. 2020), insofar as the backers may miss the opportunity to participate in the regular project after its funding deadline. For example, after supporting blockbuster A, a backer may still be a supporter for regular project B if she is also interested in the project, because the opportunities for project B will no longer be available after its deadline, whereby a cobacking activity (i.e., a backer of project A also contributes to project B) may occur. In this regard, we expect the negative effect to be limited (compared with the positive effect) because of the urgency occasioned by project duration.

In addition, crowdfunding backers often have a variety of needs and considerations when contributing to a project (Gerber and Hui 2013, Lin et al. 2014, Dai and Zhang 2019, Ryu et al. 2020, Jiang et al. 2021). Supposing that backers pursued only functional and utilitarian values from a project, blockbusters would negatively affect their participation in other projects. However, this may not be the case in crowdfunding. As discussed above, one unique feature of crowdfunding is that backers act as both consumers and supporters; their backing decisions may not purely be based on the practical usefulness of project deliverables, but may also be shaped by socioemotional considerations and participatory bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, such as prosocial motives to help entrepreneurs to realize their entrepreneurial goals and their desire to contribute to the development of projects (Lefkoff-Hagius and

Mason 1993, Allison et al. 2017, Dai and Zhang 2019, Jiang et al. 2021). In other words, their motivations are a combination of both utilitarian and nonutilitarian (i.e., socioemotional and participatory) values. The socioemotional and participatory values backers pursue could be triggered and become more salient in the presence of blockbusters (Simpson et al. 2021), in that the positive feedback of backing the blockbuster project makes such needs more pronounced and thus serves as a catalyst for their contributions to other projects. Therefore, given that backing decisions are not driven only by utilitarian values, we posit that the negative effect will not be strong compared with the positive effect.

In light of the above, the positive effect is expected to dominate over the negative effect. We thus expect the effect of blockbuster projects on other concurrent projects to be positive on average. This is stated formally in the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 1 (Overall Effect). The number of blockbuster projects, on average, has a positive effect on the funding performance of concurrent projects on the platform.

## 3.2. Local Effects from Project Relatedness

The second dimension in our framework—local network effects—involves the relative strength of the effects with respect to a subset of agents. As noted by Burtch and Ramaprasad (2016), the notion of “local” can be conceptualized as the distance or similarity between two types of agents involved. Likewise, in examining brand perceptions, it has been noted that the similarity between products forms the core of market competition (Luo et al. 2017). Mirroring this view, in crowdfunding, the in<sup>fl</sup>uence of one project on another is often shaped by their comparative features, which can be captured by their similarity. The effect of a blockbuster on other concurrent projects could vary according to the extent to which the blockbuster project is similar to the other projects. Thus, we conceptualize such a similarity as the relatedness of blockbusters (Park et al. 1991) and propose its comparative effect (of related versus unrelated blockbusters) as a manifes tation of local (versus nonlocal) network effects.

Regarding product relatedness, the conventional view in economics is that market competition is intensi<sup>fi</sup>ed when products (or brands, <sup>fi</sup>rms, and so on) are similar to one another (e.g., Voleti et al. 2015, Luo et al. 2017). This is tenable for comparable products in a relatively stable market, where products compete for the same consumers. For example, in the automobile industry, the group of consumers is relatively stable (i.e., it is not likely that there will be a sharp increase in the number of consumers in the market actively searching for a car). However, this view is less applicable when considering the relatedness of blockbuster crowdfunding projects. Compared with traditional contexts that have relatively stable customer bases, blockbuster projects (in a relatively open crowdfunding market) may alter the pool of backers on the platform because of the entry of new backers. In this way, rather than competing with blockbusters for only a relatively <sup>fi</sup>xed group of backers, related projects may also bene<sup>fi</sup>t from the increased backer base and participation levels with blockbusters. Below, we propose that blockbuster projects exert a stronger positive effect on related projects than on unrelated projects. Given that backers play dual roles of consumers and supporters (Gerber et al. 2012), we borrow insights from the consumer literature and consider features speci<sup>fi</sup>c to crowdfunding when developing our arguments.

It has been suggested that consumer preferences for a product are not <sup>fi</sup>xed but rather change with different purchases (Lefkoff-Hagius and Mason 1993, Dhar et al. 1999). Consumer research suggests that when consumers make decisions or judgments about a product, they selectively focus their attention on some of the product features to which they have previously been exposed through another product (Dhar et al. 1999). Following this logic, we posit that the popularity of a blockbuster project and backers’ contributions to it increase the attention backers pay to the blockbuster and, in turn, result in greater retrieval and consideration of the features of the blockbuster when they evaluate another project. In addition, as consumers in crowdfunding, backers tend to engage in similarity judgments—that is, seeking similarities between projects (Dhar et al. 1999, Jiang et al. 2021). When these backers come across a new project, they are likely to implicitly retrieve certain features of the blockbuster to consider and evaluate the project at hand. As a result, blockbusters create an “attraction effect” by increasing the size of the backer base for similar projects (Burton and Zinkhan 1987). Projects that share more features with the blockbuster project (i.e., are related to the blockbuster) are likely to receive more attention and be more favorably evaluated by backers, compared with those that share fewer features with the blockbuster (i.e., are unrelated to the blockbuster).

Moreover, backers face potential uncertainty in reward-based crowdfunding, because whether they will ultimately receive the deliverable is uncertain at the time of backing, as is the actual quality of project deliverables (Strausz 2017, Jiang et al. 2021). This delayed grati<sup>fi</sup>cation creates a certain level of ambiguity in the backing experience. In light of this ambiguity, backer perceptions and evaluations are more susceptible to the environment and how the project is described (Coupey et al. 1998). If a blockbuster project is ongoing on the platform, the overwhelming success and popularity of the blockbuster send a positive signal, on which basis backers tend to develop favorable impressions of the blockbuster. As such, when a project is related (i.e., described similarly) to the blockbuster, that project is more likely to also be favorably evaluated by backers.

Taken together, a concurrent blockbuster is likely to enlarge the backer base of related projects, and this can be strengthened by the ambiguity experienced by backers due to delayed grati<sup>fi</sup>cation in crowdfunding. Therefore, we anticipate that those projects that are related to concurrent blockbuster projects will bene<sup>fi</sup>t more than projects that are unrelated. We thus submit the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 2 (Relatedness of Blockbusters). In Hypoth esis 1, blockbuster projects that are related to the focal project have a stronger effect than blockbuster projects that are unrelated.

## 3.3. Temporal Effects from Project Timing

As per our discussion of the network effects literature, temporal effects are of great importance, as network effects may vary over time (Chu and Manchanda 2016, Song et al. 2018). In line with this view, considering that each crowdfunding project has a live duration, a blockbuster project can overlap with a concurrent project in two ways, depending on whether it emerges before or after the launch of a focal project. Given the in<sup>fl</sup>uential role that time plays in the materialization of network effects (Cheng and Liu 2012), we contend that blockbuster effects vary with the time elapsed since the blockbuster’s emergence. Hence, in the third dimension of our framework, we consider how the two timings of blockbuster emergence shape blockbuster effects in different ways.

As discussed previously, the positive spillover effect of a blockbuster on other concurrent projects is driven mainly by the cobacking activities of backers attracted by the blockbuster. We expect that such backing activities have certain temporal variations. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, it has long been noted that the probability of consumers seeking a product can vary with time (Chintagunta 1999). Echoing this view, we posit that backers’ attention can be directed to a blockbuster— especially at the time they contribute to it—because of its noteworthy performance and popularity. In this regard, their backing of other concurrent projects may not occur immediately when they back the blockbuster. However, as time elapses, given the positive impressions and heightened expectations they have developed for the platform (Cheng and Liu 2012), backers’ attention is increasingly likely to be redirected from the blockbuster to other concurrent projects (Chintagunta 1999, Cooke et al. 2001, Chu and Manchanda 2016). Therefore, the positive spillover effect from the blockbuster will increase as time passes.

In general, a great number of new and existing backers come to a blockbuster gradually at various stages over the course of fundraising. Consequently, the increased size and activeness of the backer community can be continuously shared by concurrent projects. Given the aforementioned temporal backing patterns of blockbuster backers, we contend that those blockbusters that emerge earlier (i.e., before the launch of focal project) are more likely to have more backer attention and greater resources spill over to other projects (Grewal et al. 2004, Fleder and Hosanagar 2009, Tully et al. 2015), thereby generating more cobacking activities. Conversely, when a blockbuster project emerges after the launch of a focal project, it could act as a shock to the in-progress focal project by dominating the attention of backers who might otherwise participate in the focal project. Therefore, fewer backers from the blockbuster would contribute to the focal project.

In sum, backers of a blockbuster contribute to the spillover effects to the focal project, but this will likely occur with a time lag. We thus posit that blockbusters that emerge before the launch of a focal project will be more bene<sup>fi</sup>cial than those that emerge after the launch of the focal project. This is stated formally in the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 3 (Timing of Blockbusters). In Hypothesis 1, blockbuster projects that emerge before the launch of the focal project have a stronger effect than blockbuster projects that emerge afterward.

## 4.2. Identifying Blockbuster Projects

As the <sup>fi</sup>rst step of the empirical analysis, we needed to identify a set of blockbuster projects. Per the de<sup>fi</sup>nition set forth in Section 3, crowdfunding blockbusters projects are those projects with outstanding and exceptional performance among all projects on a platform. The identi<sup>fi</sup>ed blockbuster projects should therefore be (1) highly successful, so that they have unequivocally outstanding performance, and (2) a small set of projects, so that they are exceptional compared with other projects.

To achieve this, we adopted a data-driven approach that classi<sup>fi</sup>ed projects with outstanding performance as blockbusters and allowed blockbusters to naturally and inductively emerge from the data. In the crowdfunding literature, two metrics are particularly relevant to a project’s funding performance: total amount pledged (Hong et al. 2018, Geva et al. 2019, Chan et al. 2020) and number of backers (Thies et al. 2016, Li and Wang 2019, Yang et al. 2020). These two metrics are not only absolute indicators of fundraising performance but also widely accepted signals of fundraising success in practice. Therefore, blockbuster projects are expected to stand out from other projects in terms of these two metrics. To determine blockbusters based jointly on these two dimensions, we used a clustering approach on all completed projects over the history of the platform to automatically identify groups of projects that stood out among all projects. This method allowed us to identify clusters of projects that exhibited similar patterns in terms of exceptional performance and that were distant from other clusters of projects (Ketchen and Shook 1996, Kaufman and Rousseeuw 2005). Given the large number of observations in our sample (n 186,120), we chose k-means clustering over other approaches that rely on pairwise distance matrices (Ketchen and Shook 1996). The k-means approach uses an iterative procedure to minimize distances within clusters and maximize distances between clusters (Maimon and Rokach 2005). In this manner, clusters of projects with outstanding performance were identi<sup>fi</sup>ed as blockbuster projects.

As k-means clustering requires a predetermined number of clusters, we started with a small number of clusters and increased this number while observing the pattern of the clusters. Given that our objective was to identify only those clusters that were outstanding in terms of performance metrics, we focused on the stability of the top-performing clusters rather than the overall <sup>fi</sup>t of the clustering to the data. The k-means clustering results showed that the top-performing clusters converged and stabilized when the number of clusters was above 10. Figure 1 illustrates the distribution of projects with clusters (k 11, k 13, and k 15) in the two-dimensional space of total pledged amount and number of backers. Two clusters clearly stand out from the others with respect to the two performance metrics and are stably identi<sup>fi</sup>ed regardless of the number of clusters speci<sup>fi</sup>ed. Therefore, we regard projects in these two outstanding clusters as blockbuster projects, as they have overwhelmingly high performance compared with all other projects.

Compared with all other projects, the blockbuster projects have both notably higher pledged amounts and considerably greater numbers of backers. Blockbuster projects also attract a disproportionately large number of new backers.

4.2.2. Project-Side Statistics. Apart from the changes on the backer side (for both new and existing backers) arising from blockbusters, blockbusters may also occasion changes on the project side such that more projects are launched or the quality of new projects changes. Such changes may intensify competition for resources on the project side and undermine our empirical estimations. Therefore, we checked projectside changes before and after the entry of blockbusters. As shown in Table 3, we compared the number of projects launched 30 days (the platform-recommended project duration) before and after the emergence of each blockbuster, as well as the characteristics of these projects. Notably, the number of projects launched did not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly increase after the emergence of blockbusters, which implies that blockbusters result in increases on the project side only in the long run, rather than creating contemporaneous shocks with a higher number of concurrent projects being launched (Chu and Manchanda 2016). This is likely because blockbuster projects are typically of high quality, which makes them dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to imitate. In addition, project creators need time to prepare their campaigns and cannot always immediately follow the trend of popular blockbusters. Meanwhile, the differences in the number of successful projects and success rate were insigni<sup>fi</sup>cant. Other project characteristics, including project quality<sup>10</sup> and the experience of project creators, were likewise not signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly different before and after the emergence of blockbusters. This suggests that blockbusters mainly shift the backer side by energizing existing backers and attracting new backers, but do not exert an immediate impact on the project side in terms of the quantity or quality of projects launched. Therefore, our empirical analyses should not be confounded by potential changes on the project side; the effects of blockbuster projects on the performance of concurrent projects from our estimations can be attributed to the changes on the backer side.

## 5.2. Mechanism Identification

Our econometric analyses suggest that blockbuster projects have an overall positive effect on the performance of other projects, and this effect is moderated by relatedness and timing; blockbusters that are related to a focal project and emerge before that project have stronger effects. Although these results support our hypotheses, the causal inferences of such results rely on the validation of the mechanism. Blockbusters are usually of high quality and could increase platform popularity and awareness, creating positive experiences for backers. This reduces uncertainty and increases quality expectations for crowdfunding projects and leads backers $^ { \mathrm { t o , } }$ in turn, contribute to other projects. In particular, the causal links of the mechanism rely on two major observations. First, blockbusters increase platform popularity and thus induce greater awareness of the platform. Second, backers (including new and existing backers) who contribute to blockbuster projects are involved in cobacking activities on other projects because of the positive experiences. To further identify these causal links, we conducted two additional sets of analysis: platform trend analysis and backer analysis.

5.2.1. Platform Trend Analysis. We <sup>fi</sup>rst veri<sup>fi</sup>ed the causal link that blockbuster projects increase the popularity and awareness of the platform, especially broader awareness outside the platform. To achieve this, we relied on three data sets collected outside of Kickstarter: Google Trends search index data for the term “Kickstarter,” all social media activity from Twitter related to Kickstarter and Kickstarter projects, and online news articles about Kickstarter and Kickstarter projects. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, we investigated the trends of blockbuster projects over time, along with the trend in popularity and awareness of the platform captured by the three sources of off-platform information (search trends, tweets, and online news). The data were aggregated at the weekly level (for better visualization) to plot the trend of blockbusters and popularity over time from January 2012 to November 2014, which covers the time span of our estimation sample.

Figure 3 shows the trend of blockbusters (daily average of NumBB for each week), along with the popularity trend as measured by search trends (Google Trends search index for each week), social media activity (number of tweets within each week), and online media coverage (number of news articles within each week), respectively. The trends suggest that platform popularity is largely aligned with the emergence of blockbusters. Notably, when there are blockbusters on the platform, there is typically an increase or peak in the popularity of the platform. This observation implies a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant concurrence of blockbuster projects and platform popularity. The emergence of blockbusters increases platform popularity and attention such that backers may enjoy positive experiences through contributing to blockbusters, which in turn drives the blockbuster effects on other concurrent projects.

Our results suggest that number of blockbusters signi<sup>fi</sup>cantly increases the concurrent popularity of the platform. We further examined the direction of this causal mechanism using a panel vector autoregression (PVAR) model (Holtz-Eakin et al. 1988). The results of the PVAR model and Granger causality tests suggest that the causal mechanism is established from blockbuster to popularity rather than from popularity to blockbuster. In other words, the arrival of blockbusters increases platform popularity, whereas the platform popularity does not result in more blockbusters. We discuss the details of the statistical analysis in Online Appendix D.

5.2.2. Backer Analysis. We then investigated the second causal link regarding backer behaviors: (new and existing) backers who contribute to blockbusters support other concurrent projects to induce a positive effect. To establish the causal link, we used the backer lists of the Kickstarter campaigns to analyze backing activities. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, using the backer data from April 2009 through November 2014, we performed two sets of analysis: cobacking analysis and backer group analysis.<sup>21</sup>

First, we examined the cobacking activities of blockbuster projects and other concurrent projects. As discussed above, positive effects occur when existing and new backers are attracted by blockbusters and then also participate in other projects. To validate this, we identi<sup>fi</sup>ed cases wherein backers of blockbuster projects also contributed to other concurrent projects. For each blockbuster, we counted the total backing activities for other concurrent projects by backers of that blockbuster.<sup>22</sup> We also calculated the cobacking activities to other related and unrelated projects, and to other projects launched before and after the emergence of the blockbuster. Following the rationale used in our hypothesis development, we separately considered the backing activities of the existing backers (i.e., backers who had participated in other projects before the blockbuster) and the blockbuster-attracted backers (i.e., new backers attracted by blockbuster projects).

Table 6 presents the results of this cobacking analy sis. Several observations can be made. First, blockbuster projects attract a high number of backers, including both existing and new backers, who also participate in other concurrent projects (on average, over 9,000 for each blockbuster). Second, this coback ing pattern is more salient when a blockbuster project is related to the concurrent project (15.5 compared with 0.82) or when a blockbuster emerges before the launch of the concurrent project (5,463.79 compared with 3,674.66). In general, these patterns apply to both existing backers and the backers attracted by blockbusters. Therefore, this analysis establishes the empirical causal links for the blockbuster effects by demonstrating that backers of blockbuster projects indeed contribute to other concurrent projects through cobacking activities. The local and temporal blockbuster effects can also be observed by different cobacking patterns from related versus unrelated blockbusters, and from blockbusters emerging at different timings.

This further implies that blockbuster projects can create positive experiences for their backers, as existing backers and new backers attracted by blockbusters actively contribute to other projects.

## 5.3. Robustness Checks

Our main results cover the overall, local, and temporal effects of blockbuster projects on concurrent projects and identify the causal mechanism that drives these effects. To con<sup>fi</sup>rm the reliability of the results and rule out alternative explanations, we conducted several robustness checks to further establish the causal effects of blockbusters. Table 8 summarizes all the robustness checks and the empirical concerns they address.

5.3.1. Endogenous Entry of Blockbusters. We <sup>fi</sup>rst examined the potential endogenous entry of blockbusters to the platform. One might argue that blockbusters could be driven by nonrandom factors, such as platform changes, user activeness, or lack of competing projects such that the emergence of blockbusters is endogenous to other projects. If that were the case, our estimations could be undermined. To address this concern, we treated the emergence of blockbusters as events to other projects and examined the entry of blockbusters with a relative time model and falsi<sup>fi</sup>cation test (random treatment model), following prior research using difference-in-differences models (Angrist and Pischke 2008, Greenwood and Agarwal 2016, Greenwood and Wattal 2017). The relative time model esti mates suggest that there are no signi<sup>fi</sup>cant effects before the emergence of blockbusters, whereas the blockbuster effects are exhibited gradually after the event time.<sup>23</sup> The falsi<sup>fi</sup>cation test with randomly assigned blockbuster time windows for 1,000 times further con<sup>fi</sup>rmed that the blockbuster effects are not driven by other random shocks. The details are presented in Online Appendix F.

5.3.2. Potential Endogeneity. We then addressed the potential endogeneity issues arising from other changes that are due to blockbusters and may affect focal proj ects’ performance. First, we considered the possibility that blockbusters may induce copycats, which mimic the blockbusters and compete with other projects. We did not observe a signi<sup>fi</sup>cant increase in the number of copycats, and the results were consistent after controlling for copycats. Second, we investigated the potential environmental changes in crowdfunding markets caused by blockbusters. With the Google Trends search index for Indiegogo, we showed that blockbusters on Kickstarter do not lead to <sup>fl</sup>uctuations in the general crowdfunding market. Third, to further address potential endogeneity, we used instrument variable approach. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, because of the lack of theoretically and empirically validated instruments, we adopted the heteroskedasticity-based instrument variable approach (Lewbel 2012) and the results were consistent. We present the details of addressing alternative explanations and endogeneity issues in Online Appendix G.

5.3.7. Other Robustness Checks. We conducted several additional robustness checks to further support the reliability of our <sup>fi</sup>ndings. First, we included seasonal effects in our campaign-day-level estimations because blockbusters may be more likely to emerge in certain time periods or to align with some periodic online or of<sup>fl</sup>ine events (Agrawal et al. 2018). To resolve this concern, we controlled the year and month dummies for each day in our estimation sample to account for seasonal effects. The results after controlling for additional time effects were consistent with those of our main analysis.

Furthermore, we performed several robustness tests for the campaign-level analysis of backer groups. As the dependent variable values for backer proportions are all restricted to between zero and one, we replicated the analysis using the fractional regression model (i.e., a logit link function under a generalized linear model speci<sup>fi</sup>cation; Papke and Wooldridge 2008). In addition, the overlap of a focal project with blockbusters may be in<sup>fl</sup>uenced by the project creator’s prior knowledge (i.e., project creators with certain knowledge may choose to strategically launch their projects following the emergence of blockbusters).<sup>24</sup> To address this concern, we incorporated a set of creator characteristics in the estimation, including the number of prior created projects, the number of prior backed projects, and the number of days since joining the platform. The results for the blockbuster effects were consistent with the estimation results in Table 7.

## 6. Conclusions

In this study, we investigate the blockbuster effect—that is, the impact of overwhelmingly successful projects on other concurrent projects—in crowdfunding. Drawing a multidimensional perspective from the cross-side network effects literature (i.e., the overall, local and temporal effects) and integrating views from consumer behavior and the special features of crowdfunding, we propose that blockbusters have an overall positive effect on other projects. Furthermore, this positive effect is contingent on the heterogeneity of blockbusters; it is stronger for related blockbusters (compared with unrelated blockbusters) and for blockbusters that emerge before the launch of the focal project (compared with blockbusters that emerge after).

We identi<sup>fi</sup>ed 29 blockbusters, and our campaignday-level estimations largely supported our hypotheses. Further identi<sup>fi</sup>cation of the mechanism through platform trend analysis and backer analysis con<sup>fi</sup>rmed the causal links behind the mechanism: blockbusters increase the popularity of the platform, with new backers joining the platform and existing backers becoming more active, and these backers receive positive experiences from blockbusters such that they contribute to other concurrent projects, which drives the observed positive effects.

## 6.1. Theoretical Contributions

This study makes several notable contributions. First, we add to the crowdfunding literature on the interactions among projects by examining how a project’s performance could be affected by other projects, particularly those with exceptional performance. Prior research examining project interactions suggests a negative in<sup>fl</sup>uence from an increased number of campaigns—particularly low-quality campaigns—on the funding performance of other campaigns because of increased competition (Geva et al. 2019). Complementing this view, as crowdfunding projects differ in quality, we posit that the previously identi<sup>fi</sup>ed effects may not be uniform, and some projects could in<sup>fl</sup>u ence others in different ways. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, our examination of blockbuster effects suggests that projects with outstanding performance (particularly those of high quality) can bene<sup>fi</sup>t other concurrent projects, rather than merely intensifying competition. Therefore, this study, together with Geva et al. (2019), suggests the potential contingent role of project quality in the interactions among crowdfunding projects. Moreover, this study suggests that the blockbuster effects are heterogeneous, depending on the inherent interrelations between blockbusters and other projects. In this regard, we take one step forward by offering a nuanced view of the effect of project interactions, as well as how such an effect could be shaped by project characteristics and the relationships between projects.

Second, we enrich this literature by revealing the underlying mechanisms of project interactions through the exploration of the backing activities involved. In crowdfunding—a form of two-sided market—the performance of a project hinges on backing activities on the other side of the platform (Doshi 2014). However, the way in which backing activities drive one project’s in<sup>fl</sup>uence on another had not yet been explored. In examining the blockbuster effects, we <sup>fi</sup>lled this void by analyzing cobacking activities with regard to blockbusters and other projects, as well as backer participation in other concurrent projects, rather than looking only at the outcome (i.e., performance changes) from one side of the platform. Our theoretical development and empirical evidence demonstrate that blockbuster effects arise from the increased size and activeness of backers, uncovering the mechanisms at play. Blockbuster projects have the potential to alter the contribution patterns of their backers, such that those backers become more active in contributing to other projects.

Moreover, we contribute to the literature examining distinctive agents in an online market. These distinctive agents were termed differently by prior literature, such as superstars (Barzilay et al. 2018, Zhang et al. 2019) and outliers (Doshi 2014), but conceptually, we deem that the essential traits of blockbusters, superstars, and outliers are similar in that these agents are characterized by exceptional performance compared with other regular agents on a platform.<sup>25</sup> However, concerning the research focus, unlike the studies investigating distinctive agents, which primarily emphasize such agents’ long-term bene<sup>fi</sup>ts, we instead focus on the tensions regarding their contemporaneous effects on other agents—in our context, blockbuster projects may bene<sup>fi</sup>t or undermine other concurrent projects. Our <sup>fi</sup>ndings suggest that the positive effect is dominant because the cross-side network effect enlarges the overall pool of <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources and backer participation, so that other projects are also likely to bene<sup>fi</sup>t from the blockbusters. In addition, by combining our insights into a context where the success rate of projects is not constrained, and the <sup>fi</sup>ndings in Zhang et al. (2019) from a context of crowdsourcing contests where only one or a limited number of participants can win a <sup>fi</sup>xed prize, we suggest that whether the emergence of distinctive agents results in a winner-take-all market may be dependent on the platforms’ mechanisms for coordinating participations. For a relatively open market, such as crowdfunding where the incoming backers may increase the overall pool of resources, the distinctive agent is more likely to be bene<sup>fi</sup>cial to other agents on the platform, whereas for a market where platform mechanisms contain the number of successful agents, such as crowdsourcing contests, a distinctive agent is likely to reap disproportionately large bene<sup>fi</sup>ts and undermine other agents.

This study also contributes to the recently growing literature empirically documenting cross-side network effects on two-sided platforms. Prior studies have pri marily focused on interchangeable agents or products and then treated all agents as making equal contributions to the network effect; for example, all buyers or sellers are treated equally in examining the network effects (Boudreau 2012, Chu and Manchanda 2016). However, because of the increasingly uneven distribution of quality, and the inequality of resource allocation on two-sided platforms, some agents may create a prominent cross-side network effect. In this regard, our examination of blockbuster effects extends the literature by suggesting that the addition of a single unit of certain agents—particularly those with distinctive features—on one side may have a disproportionately greater effect on the other side. For example, blockbuster projects can make much more salient contributions to the size and participation of the backer side than can regular projects. Therefore, our <sup>fi</sup>ndings provide novel insights into the role of highly in<sup>fl</sup>uential agents in fomenting a disproportionately stronger network effect, as well as into the underlying mechanisms at play.

Finally, the present study offers a framework that considers both the locality and temporality of cross-side network effects (Jacobides et al. 2018) by examining the heterogeneity of blockbusters in terms of relatedness and emergent timing. Notably, cross-side network effects may not be uniform: an agent on the platform may not always create network effects in one direction on the other side (Burtch and Ramaprasad 2016). As for locality, our theoretical development and empirical <sup>fi</sup>ndings reveal that an increased number of backers and increased activeness of backers due to the emergence of blockbusters are more likely to locally spill over to related than to unrelated projects. Regarding temporality, we demonstrate that the direction of blockbuster effects may be contingent on the emergent timing of blockbusters relative to a focal project. Taken together, our <sup>fi</sup>ndings imply that the extent and direction of cross-side network effects may be dependent on the interrelations among agents, particularly relatedness and timing (Boudreau 2012).

## 6.2. Practical Implications

This study also provides important practical implications, particularly for project creators and operators of crowdfunding platforms and more generally for twosided platforms. First, project creators could take advantage of the “piggyback” opportunity by launching their projects in the presence of a related blockbuster project in the platform. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, when creators make decisions to launch their projects, they may observe other related projects in the platform, especially those with outstanding performance (e.g., blockbusters). Rather than being concerned with the deterrence of those related and popular projects, creators can take the opportunity to launch their projects in a timely manner and run concurrently with related blockbusters. In this way, these creators are likely to bene<sup>fi</sup>t from the increased backer participation and incoming new backers, and thus achieve superior funding performance. In addition, creators are advised to pay particular attention to the launch timing relative to blockbusters given the temporal effects such that they can bene<sup>fi</sup>t from blockbusters by launching immediately after their emergence.

Platform operators should increase the exposure of blockbuster projects, which is conducive to attracting new backers and enhancing the ladder of backer participation, such that the positive effect of blockbusters can be facilitated. Although the emergence of blockbusters can be exogenous shocks, it is important to observe the performance of projects and identify the possible emergence of blockbusters in a timely manner. When blockbusters can be identi<sup>fi</sup>ed and recognized (based on their outstanding performance compared with previous projects), platform operators can leverage these projects to further boost platform popularity and gain the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts of blockbusters. In particular, when spotlighting blockbusters to increase popularity and backer participation, platform operators could emphasize the content of blockbusters to create better opportunities for other related projects to bene<sup>fi</sup>t from blockbusters. Speci<sup>fi</sup>cally, crowdfunding platforms could create a special widget displaying the content of blockbusters to encourage creators with ideas for related projects to launch their projects in a timely manner. Backers would then be more likely to discover related projects that could attract their attention.

More generally, there is an ongoing debate over whether two-sided platforms should invest in promoting blockbusters and superstars or, alternatively, invest in the long tail for pro<sup>fi</sup>ts (Brynjolfsson et al. 2011, Oestreicher-Singer and Sundararajan 2012, Elberse 2013, Kumar et al. 2014). Although blockbusters have the potential to attract more users to twosided platforms, such agents have raised concerns regarding intensifying competition, as users may disproportionately concentrate on the blockbusters. We advise platform operators to promote blockbusters, especially when the platform mechanism does not constrain the number of successful agents. A key implication of our <sup>fi</sup>ndings is that blockbusters not only increase the size of the user (backer) base, but also induce higher participation from the user (backer) side, such that other agents (and the long tail) on the same side as the blockbusters are actually able to harvest the bene<sup>fi</sup>ts that spill over from the blockbusters. This implication is particularly relevant for relatively open two-sided platforms, such as live streaming platforms, online group buying, and e-commerce platforms with diverse and limited-time offerings. In this regard, we recommend that platforms invest in promoting blockbusters so they can attract new users and motivate existing users’ participation. Therefore, promoting blockbusters is also deemed an alternative to achieving a critical mass of users on a platform to foster network effects.

Finally, we provide insights into consumer behavior in two-sided markets. Our theoretical development and empirical <sup>fi</sup>ndings imply that time is an important factor in shaping blockbuster effects. In the presence of time constraints, backers are likely to shift their attention to other concurrent projects, even though they have participated in blockbusters, as these projects may not be available after a limited amount of time has elapsed. As a result, the positive effect of blockbuster projects exceeds the negative effect. In this sense, we posit that such a product feature (i.e., limited time) could be leveraged to attenuate the negative effects of blockbusters and boost the positive effects. Therefore, two-sided markets may advise sellers to add time constraints to product purchase conditions (e.g., introducing limited-time offers) so that, although consumers may shift their purchases to a blockbuster seller on the platform, the time urgency for purchasing induces them to be more active in purchasing limited-time offerings. In this way, other products are more likely to bene<sup>fi</sup>t from the increase in buyer activeness induced by the blockbuster sellers.

## 6.3. Limitations and Future Research

Our study has limitations that create opportunities for future research. First, we have considered only the concurrent effects of blockbusters, that is, the effect while a blockbuster is live, to resolve the empirical tensions arising from the negative competition and positive spillover effects. It is possible that this effect may change over the long term when a blockbuster delivers (or fails to deliver) promised rewards, insofar as the cannibalization effect could fade when a blockbuster ends and backers’ impressions of the platform could vary with product delivery. We encourage future research to investigate the long-term effects of blockbusters on platform evolution.

Moreover, the term blockbuster was adopted from the motion picture industry and used <sup>fi</sup>guratively to represent overwhelmingly successful projects. There is no of<sup>fi</sup>cial theoretical de<sup>fi</sup>nition of this term in the crowdfunding context. Therefore, we demonstrate the robustness of our <sup>fi</sup>ndings using approaches for identifying blockbuster projects aligned with their de<sup>fi</sup>nition (in Section 3). Nevertheless, different researchers may use different de<sup>fi</sup>nitions and interpretations for this term and its manifestation in crowdfunding. Future research could extend our study by exploring other intricacies in the de<sup>fi</sup>nitions of blockbusters in crowdfunding.

In addition, our study highlights the role of timing in shaping blockbuster effects. Some recent practices on crowdfunding platforms have enabled other elements of timing, such as the removal of a funding duration and prefunding options (i.e., projects can be prelaunched before the actual start of fundraising; Garimella et al. 2017). Although such new practices were not present in our setting and time window, they may affect backers’ allocation of <sup>fi</sup>nancial resources, which could shape the blockbuster effects in a different manner. Thus, the interplay between such practices and blockbuster projects merits further exploration.

Another limitation is that we observe backer activities only through their cobacking behaviors to infer their positive impressions on other projects, rather than directly capturing backers’ positive experiences or impressions. Although we explored a number of ways to establish the causal links and identify the mechanisms, it should be noted that backers’ positive experiences or impressions can be more accurately captured by clickstream data or primary sources of backer activities. Future research may draw on more detailed clickstream data or primary data to more deeply understand how backers perceive and respond to the overwhelming success of blockbusters.

Finally, our current research focuses mainly on how blockbusters affect other concurrent projects and does not address the drivers of blockbusters. One avenue for future research would be to investigate how blockbusters emerge on crowdfunding platforms and online markets. Future research could establish a more comprehensive picture of blockbuster effects by combining understanding of what factors make blockbusters and of what effects the blockbusters may have.
