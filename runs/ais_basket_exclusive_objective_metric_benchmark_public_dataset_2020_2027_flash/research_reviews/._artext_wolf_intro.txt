[P1] The recent advancement in machine learning and the increasing availability of big data have encouraged organizations to apply predictive analytics to inform mission-critical decisionmaking [14, 25, 32, 48]. Such predictive analytics applications often leverage a supervised machine learning (SML) model to make empirical predictions based on the patterns captured from the data [48]. For example, review platforms leverage text classification to detect spam reviews [33]; email service providers leverage classification models to filter out spam emails [17]; mobile app platforms utilize text mining and image processing to detect fraudulent apps based on app descriptions and icons [57]; financial institutions predict default rate by leveraging borrower information [8]; human resources use SML to assist job candidate selection [60]; healthcare practitioner discover treatment barriers from patient communities [58]; and autonomous vehicles leverage sensory data to predict energy eficiency [50].

[P2] Despite its enormous potential, predictive analytics is not without its pitfalls. One emerging threat to predictive analytics has proven to be particularly problematic: adversarial attacks [23]. As SML-enabled predictive analytics become an integral part of organizational decision-making, adversaries are incentivized to exploit the vulnerabilities of SML models underlying predictive analytics applications and mislead these applications into making erroneous decisions favoring the adversaries. In particular, such adversarial attacks often involve meticulously perturbing the input to the target SML model for achieving the desired output. This could be concerning for predictive analytics applied in the domains where the input is provided by the individual who is immediately afected by the decision made by the SML model [8, 13, 60]. Examples of such application domains include spam detection [33], fraud detection [1], credit approval [8, 59], employee hiring [8, 59], and student admission [8, 59]. In particular, a spam review could be meticulously modified to bypass the spam review detection system and spam emails could be deliberately designed to deceive SML-based spam email filtering systems. Such adversarial attacks on SML models could undermine the decision-making enabled by predictive analytics, leading to potential reputation damages and financial losses.

[P3] The vulnerability of SML models is not new and has received increasing attention from the research communities [8, 13, 18, 23, 26]. Nonetheless, it warrants further investigation in the emerging applications of predictive analytics for three reasons. First, organizations have increasingly applied SML-enabled predictive analytics into their mission-critical business processes [11, 20]; however, predictive analytics in information system (IS) research has not fully acknowledged the potential security threat against the vulnerabilities of the underlying SML models. There is limited understanding and awareness of the issue in the predictive analytics literature to provide guidelines for organizations utilizing predictive analytics taking inputs from potentially unreliable sources. Second, the IS literature focuses the evaluation of predictive analytics primarily on the models’ predictive performance, such as accuracy [34, 48]. The systematic assessment of predictive analytics applications’ robustness to adversarial exploitation has been generally understudied. As organizations decision-making about predictive analytics is informed by its evaluation, there is a need for adversarial robustness assessment techniques to complement the existing practice of evaluating predictive analytics applications. Third, the limited awareness of adversarial attacks and the lack of robustness assessment have resulted in the deployment of predictive analytics applications that are potentially vulnerable to adversarial attacks. The exploitation of such applications could lead to financial losses, reputation damages, and health and safety risks, impeding the realization of predictive analytics’ enormous potential. As such, there is an urgent need for enhancing the adversarial robustness of these deployed predictive analytics applications to adversarial exploitation.

[P4] In this paper, we seek to address two critical research questions relating to the adversarial robustness of predictive analytics applications: (1) How to assess the robustness of predictive analytics applications to adversarial attacks? (2) How to improve the robustness of predictive analytics applications in an adversarial context? Drawing upon the technology threat avoidance theory, we propose a research framework for assessing and enhancing the adversarial robustness of predictive analytics applications. The research framework seeks to provide a principled approach for improving the adversarial robustness of existing predictive analytics applications. We further instantiate the proposed framework by developing a robust text classification system. Leveraging the technology threat avoidance theory as a kernel theory, our proposed framework comprises a novel assessment model for evaluating the adversarial robustness of a predictive analytics application, and design principles for enhancing the adversarial robustness of predictive analytics applications. We demonstrate the efectiveness of our framework in the context of text classification tasks. In particular, we develop the ARText system, which jointly incorporates ensemble learning and adversarial retraining to improve its robustness to adversarial attacks. To rigorously evaluate our proposed system, we conducted two sets of evaluations on the testbeds of spam review detection and spam email detection. The first set of evaluations examined the utility of our proposed adversarial robustness assessment. The second set of evaluations gauged the eficacy of our proposed system in enhancing adversarial robustness. Overall, the ARText system was able to outperform baseline models in detecting adversarial spam reviews, indicating the efectiveness of our proposed design framework.
