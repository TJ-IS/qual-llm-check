## 1. 模板基准摘录（本轮取证原文，含行号）

### 1.1 研究问题节基准

RADAR Research Gaps and Questions（L114 至 L117）。Our review of defending IT infrastructure suggests a vital need for cyber defense AI agents that are robust to adversarial attacks. While AAE is an active research area, there lacks a framework that considers both attack and defense to model the game between the adversary and the AI agent. Also, only a few AAE methods comply with a realistic threat model. Furthermore, many AAE methods are mainly additive and thus limited in their coverage of generated attacks. In addition, most AAE methods are limited in their ability to model the adversary's sequential decision-making process. Given these gaps, the following research questions are posed: (1) How can the RO theory be leveraged in cybersecurity to strengthen the robustness of cyber defense AI agents? (2) How can effective adversarial attacks be emulated with deep RL to establish RO? (3) What mitigation strategies can be learned from utilizing RO and RL to guide the design of more robust cyber defense AI agents?

Ampel Research Gaps and Questions（L81 至 L88）。We identified several research gaps from prior literature. First, past studies analyzing exploits in hacker forums primarily analyze post content and often omit source code. Second, hacker-forum exploit source code is nontrivial to categorize. Additionally, prevailing DL-based methods used for hacker forum analysis were designed for natural language and often struggle to capture long semantic relationships within exploit code. Finally, exploit DNMs and public exploit repositories have not been leveraged in a multi-dataset model. Based on these research gaps, we pose the following research questions: How can we extend a BiLSTM model to capture the long semantic dependencies found within exploit source code? How can we transfer the knowledge from metadata-rich exploit DNMs and public exploit repositories to help label exploits in hacker forums based on their source code?

### 1.2 方法节基准

RADAR Proposed Design（L118 起）。To address these research questions, we propose a novel framework, RADAR, that optimizes the robustness of cyber defense AI agents. RADAR operates under three realistic assumptions based on the literature and feedback from three malware analysts: (1) the adversary's goal is to modify a malicious input into adversarial variants capable of evading the defense AI; (2) the adversary has no access to the internal information of the defense AI agent; (3) the defense AI agent can only observe the adversarial inputs generated by the adversary. Under these assumptions, RADAR consists of three inputs, two main phases, and two tangible outputs from each phase. Figure 2 provides an overview of the RADAR framework.

ARText System Design（L157 起）。Motivated by the extensive application of text classification in predictive analytics, we develop a generalized text classification system with adversarial robustness as an instantiation of our design framework. Our proposed ARText system generates adversarial samples using a state-of-the-art textual adversarial sample generator, quantifies adversarial robustness with novel measures, classifies texts using bootstrap aggregation of an ensemble of SML models, and further improves adversarial robustness through iterative adversarial retraining.

DSDL 4 Proposed Method（L97 起）。Our theoretical analysis lays a foundation for understanding the indispensable role of dynamic customer satisfaction for voice chat-based customer response prediction, and the revealed methodological challenges (Table 1) motivate us to design a theory-driven deep learning method to enhance black-box predictions. Accordingly, we propose DSDL, which consolidates three innovative mechanisms within a multiview deep learning framework.

ACAA 4 Proposed Method（L124 起）。The theoretical rationales of customer attention and the methodological challenges (Table 1) motivate us to design a deep learning method with two mechanisms (DTV and AMI) to effectively leverage customer attention for multimodal review-based sales prediction. Figure 2 outlines the framework of DTV-AMI.

Ampel Research Testbed and Design（L117 起）。We developed a novel research design based on our methodological foundation to help address the posed research questions. ### 1.3 评估设计节基准

RADAR Evaluation（L172 起）。Based on the prior malware analysis literature and consulting three malware analysts, we selected VirusTotal as one of the most reputable repositories of emerging malware (Kyadige et al., 2020). To construct our testbed, we obtained an academic license from VirusTotal that provided access to approximately 33.2 GB of recent, working malware executables (28,038 files) collected from 2017 to 2019. Following Raff et al. (2018), to obtain a benign executable dataset, we collected an additional 13,554 (5.04 GB) benign executables from a clean installation of Microsoft Windows. The malware testbed served as the initial seed for generating adversarial attacks and evaluating evasion rates.

ARText Experimental Evaluation（L237 起）。Consistent with Hevner et al., we rigorously evaluated the effectiveness of the major components of our proposed ARText system through experimental evaluation. In particular, Evaluation 1 examined the utility of the adversarial robustness assessment component. Evaluation 2 evaluated the effectiveness of the adversarial robustness enhancement component.
