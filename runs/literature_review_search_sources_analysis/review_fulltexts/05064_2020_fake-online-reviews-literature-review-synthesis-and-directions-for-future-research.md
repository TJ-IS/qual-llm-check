## A B S T R A C T

Fake online reviews in e-commerce significantly afect online consumers, merchants, and, as a result, market eficiency. Despite scholarly eforts to examine fake reviews, there still lacks a survey that can systematically analyze and summarize its antecedents and consequences. This study proposes an antecedent–consequence–intervention conceptual framework to develop an initial research agenda for investigating fake reviews. Based on a review of the extant literature on this issue, we identify 20 future research questions and suggest 18 propositions. Notably, research on fake reviews is often limited by lack of high-quality datasets. To alleviate this problem, we comprehensively compile and summarize the existing fake reviews-related public datasets. We conclude by presenting the theoretical and practical implications of the current research.

## 1. Introduction

Online reviews form a critical and unavoidable facet of e-commerce. These reviews have a significant impact on consumers purchase decisions as well as the amount of money spent by consumers. As e-commerce grows, however, so does the prevalence of fake online reviews (from here on, “fake reviews”). The proportion of fake reviews ranges from 16% [1], 20% [2], and 25% [3] to 33.3% [4]. As early as 2012, approximately 10.3% of online products were subjected to review manipulation [5].

There also exist infamous cases that demonstrate the seriousness of fake reviews in e-commerce. For example, in 2012, the UK Advertising Standards Authority found TripAdvisor to be involved in creating fake reviews: approximately 50 million online reviews on its site could not be verified as trusted [6]. In 2013, Samsung was ordered to pay a fine of \$340,000 by the Taiwan Federal Trade Commission for posting negative fake reviews against its competitor HTC [7]. In 2015, Amazon sued 1114 unidentified people for posting fake reviews [8]. In 2018, Mafengwo.com, a famous tourism platform in China, was involved in review fraud, which included activities such as duplicating online reviews from competitors; the platform subsequently admitted to the issue with fake reviews [9].

Both industry and academia have expended serious eforts in de tecting fake reviews and penalizing its perpetrators in order to restrict the prevalence of fake reviews. Governments, for instance, continually attempt to perfect legal systems in response to a crises of fake reviews, often taking tremendous measures to supervise online sellers and platforms. In 2013, the Attorney General of the State of New York spearheaded “Operation Clean Turf,” a year-long undercover investigation to identify and expose firms that create fake reviews [10]. In 2018, China enacted the first “E-commerce Law.” The law stipulates that merchants cannot conduct false or misleading commercial promotions to defraud or mislead consumers with fictional transactions, by fabricating online reviews, or through any other means [11]. Industry professionals and scholars continue to develop algorithms to detect fake reviews to assist in this process [12–20].

However, the efect of reducing fake reviews is unclear, when only external eforts, such as the above, are considered. Developing computerized algorithms to identify fake reviews predominantly emphasizes “treatment” of the “symptom.” Such measures seldom grasp the underlying causes and mechanisms of fake reviews. Many fake reviews still exist regardless of algorithms that have high detection rates, as smart promulgators frequently post fake reviews endowed with new features that evade filter detection.

Fake reviews seriously afect the development of online product reviews and stakeholders. Because such reviews can have a significant efect on product perception, many vendors, retailers, and platforms often manipulate online reviews [21–23]. Online sellers tend to publish positive fake reviews for their products or negative fake reviews against competitors' products for financial gains [24]. Platforms are inclined to acquiesce to review manipulations and add fake reviews to increase trafic and consumer engagement [23]. Opportunity seeking is an ex ample of why such individuals post fake reviews [4].

Fake reviews, compared with genuine online reviews, tend to more influential and complex in structure, impressing upon us the need to rigorously explore them. There is a need for a historiography of the research on fake reviews, which would ofer insights into future re search prospects. Despite recent and increasing research, scholars are yet to clearly define fake reviews. For example, what types of fake reviews have already been explored by the extant studies? Further, how do we conduct more interesting studies on fake reviews? Research on fake reviews is also limited by the available datasets—In fact, there is little information on public datasets that could be used to study fake reviews.

A systematic literature review of fake reviews is extremely limited. A rare review on false information does mention three types of false information—fake reviews in e-commerce, hoaxes on collaborative platforms, and fake news in social media. However, it does not singularly focus on fake reviews [25]. To the best of our knowledge, there exist nine relevant reviews on this topic [12–20]. These works, however, largely explore the detection of fake reviews, without an overall analysis of the antecedents and consequences of fake reviews. In the present study, we address this gap in the literature in order to guide future research.

The remaining paper is structured as follows. Section 2 presents the working definition of fake reviews, research design, and general quantitative findings of the reviewed literature. It also puts forth the analytical framework of this study, based on which we thoroughly investigate the existing literature to understand the antecedents and consequences of fake reviews in Section 3. Then, we identify future research questions and propose propositions in Section 4. Section 5 summarizes and analyzes the dataset resources, following by a conclusion in Section 6.

## 2. Research designs and general findings

## 2.1. Definition of “fake review”

So far, there has been no universally accepted definition of fake reviews. Hu et al. define review fraud as the act of vendors, publishers, writers, or any third-party monitoring online reviews and posting non authentic online reviews as real customers in order to boost product sales [5]. In this definition, fake reviews are mainly adopted by online merchants, such as vendors, publishers, and retailers for profit maximization. Similarly, Banerjee and Chua define fake reviews in tourism as online reviews written by individuals based on imagination, and thus without any real experience of staying at a destination [26]. However, the authors ignore the phenomenon of genuine consumers and review platforms posting fake reviews. Hunt instead suggests that fake reviews are false, misleading, and deceptive communications in a digital environment—these communications do not “reflect the genuinely held opinion of the author.” [27] Like Banerjee and Chua, Hunt also ignores the phenomenon of genuine consumers posting fake reviews for an incentive, such as rewards from merchants [28]. The incentivized online reviews reflect the genuinely held opinion of the author, but cannot reflect the actual product value, misleading consumers who cannot receive the incentive. However, even without any financial incentives, genuine consumers might also post fake reviews due to the “psychological efect.” [29] Yet, few studies explore fake reviews by genuine consumers.

In response to the inconclusive descriptions of fake reviews, we first provide a working definition in this study: Fake reviews are inconsistent with real evaluations of products or services; thus, fake reviews are false, bogus, and deceptive reviews. Such reviews might be posted by diferent types of people, including consumers, online merchants, and review platforms. The deciding feature of fake reviews is whether they mislead consumers. From the valence of the reviews, fake reviews can be positive, negative, or neutral [30].

## 2.2. Database

To understand fake reviews, we search for journal articles on fake reviews on the Scopus database. The Scopus is one of the world's largest abstract and citation database of peer-reviewed research literature. To obtain full-scale results, we conducted an extensive and systematic search using keywords for our Scopus search and make the following Scopus query:

(TITLE-ABS-KEY(“fake review” OR “review spam” OR “spam review” OR “deceptive review” OR “review manipulation” OR “fraudulent review” OR “review fraud” OR “false review” OR “suspicious review” OR “fictitious review” OR “manipulative review” OR “authentic review” OR “fake online review”)).

All articles reviewed herein are obtained using the above query as of October 28, 2019. The search for relevant papers in this process was by no means exhaustive, but the findings nevertheless serve as a representative summary of the research conducted. Only full-length ar ticles are included in our samples. We thus exclude conference papers, conference reviews, book chapters, editorials, short surveys, and letters from the analysis. The articles are re-checked individually for relevance. Finally, 165 articles are identified for analysis.

## 2.4. Conceptual framework and research issues

Scholars have applied the input–process–outcome (IPO) framework or implemented an adapted version of the IPO framework in order to explore antecedents and consequences of the studied objects [31]. Unlike the concepts of team efectiveness and transactive memory systems, fake reviews have a negative connotation, thus underscoring the urgency of exploring intervention measures thereof as well as of analyzing the antecedents and consequences of fake reviews. To this end, we propose the antecedent–consequence–intervention (ACI) framework based on the IPO framework (see Fig. 3). The new ACI fra mework is used to develop an initial research agenda for investigating fake reviews.

In Fig. 3, “Antecedents” are the starting point of the development process of fake reviews. We need to understand the reasons and moti vations for posting fake reviews. Particularly, we examine the “why” (Research Question (RQ) 1.1), “who” (RQ1.2), “when” (RQ1.3), “where” (RQ1.3), and “how” (RQ1.4) of posting fake reviews.

“Consequences” refer to the influencing mechanisms of fake reviews. First, the emergence of fake reviews afects the development of online product reviews (RQ2.1). Second, we should clearly realize how fake reviews afect stakeholders (RQ2.2). Third, the efects of fake reviews on the overall market and society (RQ2.3) should be defined.

“Interventions” include the detection methods and other response strategies of stakeholders. The existing detection methods cannot effectively control the emergence of fake reviews, as smart promulgators frequently post fake reviews endowed with novel features that evade detection. Studies conventionally identify such features, which, in turn, are fed as inputs to algorithms that detect fake reviews. Therefore, in this module, we first explore the features of fake reviews (RQ3.1), and then summarize the detection methods (RQ3.2). Besides continuously developing detection methods, we also explore other efective response strategies of stakeholders (RQ3.3).

## 3. Literature analyses

## 3.1. Antecedents

## 3.1.1. Why: reasons causing fake reviews

The essential reason for manipulating reviews would be pecuniary motivation. Studies confirm that online product reviews afect consumers' purchase decisions [15,32], product reputations [33,34], sales volumes, and merchants' profits [35]. For instance, a 1% increase in hotel review ratings may increase sales per room by approximately 2.6% [22]. An extra half-star rating causes restaurants to sell out 19 percentage points more frequently [36].

Another dominant motivation for posting fake reviews is competition [23]. Online product reviews generate fierce competition and help form consumer decision-making cultures [22]. Two studies based on 20 semi-structured interviews with accommodation managers reveal that managers attributed their manipulation of online product reviews to prisoner's dilemma [22,37].

Besides merchants driven by profits and competition, individual consumers may also post fake reviews to seek rewards [28,38]; this behavior is broadly rooted in psychological needs that stem from three sources: upset customers, self-appointed brand managers, and social status [29,38].

## 3.1.2. Who: promulgators of fake reviews

As per some studies, fake reviews are mainly posted by firms associated with inferiors. Online merchants with small owners and small management companies [39], weak brands [40], low ratings [41], and inferior quality [2,42–44] have higher motivation to post fake reviews. However, the case of Samsung [12] highlighted in Section 1 shows that firms with strong brands, high ratings, superior quality, and competitive advantages might also post fake reviews under fierce competition [1,21]. Individuals receiving incentives have higher probabilities to post fake reviews; powerless individuals are more prone to writing fake reviews than powerful individuals [32].

## 3.1.3. When and where: time and place for posting fake reviews

The extant studies indicate that parties are less likely to manipulate reviews with the passage of time [21]. The motivational level for promotional reviews varies over the product life cycle, specifically for such products as movies [45].

## 3.1.4. How: ways for posting fake reviews

Online merchants typically post positive fake reviews for promoting their products, or negative fake reviews of their competitors to diminish the latter's sales [24]. Merchants adopt various measures to achieve this goal, such as implementing a praise cashback strategy to entice consumers to post fake reviews [28,38] and manipulating relationships with users [41]. To date, only exterior behaviors explaining how fraudulent merchants post fake reviews have been generally realized.

## 3.2. Consequences

Various theoretical models, such as Bayesian learning [36,46] or the discretionary accrual-based earning management framework [21], are employed to research the influence of fake reviews. Some of the related theories include the Hunt–Vitell theory of ethics and impression formation [47]; the theoretical model synthesizing four major deceptive communication theories, namely, information manipulation, leakage, self-presentational, and reality monitoring [26]; information acquisi tion theory [48], the theoretical model synthesizing four diferent views, namely, use and gratification, social influence, and self-construal [49]; warranting theory [50]; intrinsic and extrinsic motivation theory [38]; persuasion knowledge model and attributional theory [3]; social exchange theory [33]; and neutralization theory [4]. The efects of fake reviews have engendered serious concern. Here, we depict the influ encing mechanisms of fake reviews in Fig. 4 to answer RQ2.1–RQ2.3.

## 3.2.1. Efects on the development of online product reviews

The existence of fake reviews constantly increases the number of online product reviews [33]. Most fake reviews are either positive or negative, whereas few fake reviews are neutral [1]. As a distorted form of online product reviews, fake reviews aggravate the dispersion of review ratings [1].

If the company's manipulation strategy is an increasing (decreasing) function of true quality, then it will increase (decrease) information quality [35]. Because the manipulation strategy is often a decreasing function of product quality [42], fake reviews decrease informative ness, information quality [51], and the efective use of online product reviews [52]. Fake reviews also damage the credibility of reviews [46] and negatively afect review helpfulness [30,53,54].

## 3.2.2. Efects on various stakeholders

Fake reviews significantly afect various stakeholders, such as consumers, merchants, and platforms. Further, they increase uncertainty [27,46,55] and mislead consumers [52,56]. Consumer distrust toward online reviews because of fake reviews [34,40,47,48,50,54,57,58] often leads to psychological discomfort [59,60]. The increased distrust and psychological discomfort damage the business's reputation [34,59,61], weaken consumers' purchase intentions [3,40,47,59,62–66], and reduce the precision of recommendation systems [67,68].

In a virtual brand community (VBC), the emergence of fake reviews, especially negative fake reviews, afects individuals' interactions with other members in the VBC, as they begin to lose confidence in the group, which weakens their interest in influencing other members [49]. At the same time, fake reviews might also directly increase consumers purchase intentions [33] by attracting consumers in participating merchant's VBC [69].

Consumers' purchase intentions directly afect product sales/revenues [40], but there is no academic consensus on the specific efect [35,55]. There are varied opinions in the literature, including a positive [5,70], a negative [29], and an invented U-shaped [40] relationship. The efect of fake reviews on product sales/revenues is also moderated by factors such as brand strength [40], property of the targeted product, market self-exciting power [55], writing style [5], and online visibility [70].

## 3.2.3. Efects on the overall market or society

Fake reviews affect the development of online product reviews and various stakeholders, and, thus, the overall market or society as well. In particular, fake reviews undermine market eficacy [27] and have a negative efect on social welfare [51,52].

## 3.3. Intervention

## 3.3.1. Features extraction

Authentic reviews and fake reviews have significant micro-linguistic diferences [71]. We distinguish authentic reviews and fake reviews in terms of four constructs (comprehensibility, specificity, exaggeration, and negligence) [26,72,73], nine characteristics (structure and format, attributes of content, information orientation, number of words, part of speech, writing style, lexical richness, personal pronouns, and paralinguistic features) [74–76], and three aspects (informativeness, subjectivity, and readability) [76,77]. Compared with normal reviews, fake reviews are not concerned with product prices and qualities [33]. Truthful reviews contain more contextual references [78] and expressive descriptions [29], whereas fake reviews are prone to extremely positive and negative expressions [79].

To guarantee the influence of fake reviews, fraudulent promulgators manipulate the spread of fake reviews by adopting measures such as afecting the sorting rule of online product reviews and giving “likes” or “helpfulness” to fake reviews. As these behaviors afect the spread of fake reviews, the helpful reviews include many fake reviews [59]. Compared with normal reviews, fake reviews have more polarized distribution [1]. Existing studies reveal that positive fake reviews are more common than negative fake reviews [70,80].

Besides analyzing review texts, we find some unique features of review ratings of fake reviews [2,81–84]. The gap between the average and specific ratings can facilitate the detection of fake reviews [2,3].

## 3.3.2. Detection methods

The existing detection methods aim at three targets: review spam [5,15,75,78,82,83,85–111], spammer [112–118], and spammer group [24,41,119–124]. Most of these detection approaches focus on English text, and only a few are based on texts in Chinese [118,120,122,125–130], Malay [100], Arabic [90,131], Korean [45], Spanish, and Portuguese [84]. Opinion mining and sentiment analysi are important for exploring the features and detecting fake reviews [132]. Such machine learning techniques are divided to three types: supervised, unsupervised, and semi-supervised learning [14].

Supervised machine learning techniques include support vector machines [93,101,125,129,133–135], logistic regression [76,129,133], neural networks [88,92,94,102,105,128,129,132,136–139], naïve Bayes [101,122,129,133,140–142], k-nearest neighbor [101,140], decision trees and J48 [75,133], boosting approach [100], select-bagging approach [141], Rocchio [12], and random forest [109,134].

Unsupervised machine learning techniques include the un supervised topic-sentiment joint probabilistic model [104], unified unsupervised review deviation model [115], unsupervised matrix iteration algorithm [130], statistics-based unsupervised clustering al gorithm [143], and lexicon-based unsupervised model [144]. Conventionally, using unlabeled data in conjunction with a small amount of labeled data considerably improves accuracy compared with supervised methods [14].

Semi-supervised machine learning techniques include the positive unlabeled learning approach [85,107,122], threshold-based detection method [145], co-training approach [107,146], the hybrid positive unlabeled learning-based approach [147], and co-training by features approach [91]. Some studies also apply the ensemble learning module because it can detect fake reviews eficiently compared with single base classifiers [101,113,131,133,141,148–150].

To detect fake reviews, scholars also propose graph- or network based methods [24,41,98,103,119–121,127,129,151–153] as well as novel mathematical or statistics models, such as the popularity-based hidden Markov model [154], aspect-rating local outlier factor model [155], composite sequential model [137], feature-centric model [86], time series model [83], cumulative sum method [156], spiral cuckoo search based clustering method [157], cosine similar measure method [158], basic algorithms of cosine similarity and sentiment analysis [159], meta-heuristic and k-means clustering approach [160], and hierarchical agglomerative clustering algorithm [116].

Although no studies have synthetically reviewed the antecedents and consequences of fake reviews, we do find nine articles that review fake reviews detection [12–20]. Interested readers can review these works, which are not synthesized here for the sake of brevity.

## 3.3.3. Response strategies

Aside from directly detecting fake reviews, the academia and in dustry, as well as governments [10,11,27], have been greatly involved in addressing fake reviews [161]. For example, regulators add an “alliance approach” to their existing arsenal of regulatory systems and mechanisms to deter fake reviews [161].

To some extent, the efects of fake reviews are decided by how a platform operates [162,163]. To significantly reduce the efects of fake reviews, review platforms should invest in their detection [51,164]. Moreover, review platforms can develop novel methods to display online reviews [163,165–167] and reduce the visibility of fake reviews [52,167,168]. Scientifically aggregating online ratings can restrain the spread of fake reviews and reduce harmful efects thereof [52,167]. To confront fake reviews, the merchants under attack could also develop their own abilities and enhance new features [70]. Furthermore, consumers must strengthen their awareness of fake reviews and respond accordingly [27,71].

## 4. Future research questions and propositions

We now target and identify 20 future research questions and 18 corresponding propositions in Table 1 to better guide future research.

## 4.1. Antecedents

Fake reviews are posted by various promulgators, including individual consumers, online merchants, review platforms, and even artificial intelligence (AI) agents. The existing literature enables us to qualitatively realize why merchants post fake reviews, but only a single study focuses on the posting motivations of individual consumers.

Besides focusing on the merchants' behaviors in posting fake reviews, we recommend greater research to reveal why and how individual consumers and review platforms post fake reviews (FQ1, FQ2, FQ5, and FQ10). Opportunism may be an efective explanation for why individuals post fake reviews without financial incentive [4]. In contrast to ordinary businesses [169.170]. platforms such as Yelp and TripAdvisor primarily obtain profits from two sources, namely, commission revenues from the merchants selling products and advertising revenues from the advertisements in the platform [39]. Thus, we con sider that platforms post fake reviews due to the increased commission and advertising revenues. Although fake reviews destroy the reputation of platforms, the additional profits gained should be higher than the potential loss of the afected reputation. As disclosed, Mafengwo.com duplicated numerous existing online reviews from competitors to attract trafic and consumer engagements [9]. Thus, we present Proposition 1 (P1) for guiding FQ1, Proposition 2 (P2) for guiding FQ2 and FQ5, and Proposition 3 (P3) for guiding FQ10.

P1:. Without any external incentives, consumers may post fake reviews to satisfy unhealthy psychology needs or to unethically gain benefits from merchants. Opportunism may be an efective psychology theory to explain the underlying psychological mechanism of posting fake reviews.

P2:. Fake reviews attract consumer engagements and increase commission and advertising revenues for the platform. Two types of review platforms have high motivations to post fake reviews: (i) they need to urgently attract consumer engagements; (ii) their profits mainly come from commission revenues.

P3:. When posting fake reviews, promulgators may create the fake reviews through directly duplicating and slightly revising existing online reviews.

Moreover, another promulgator of fake reviews—AI agents—should receive suficient attention (FQ7 and FQ10). Although few studies focus on the role of AI agents in the field of fake reviews, AI agents play increasingly important roles in posting fake news and fake reviews with novel features [67]. Therefore, we present Proposition 4 (P4) for guiding FQ7 and FQ10.

P4:. Fake reviews will be posted increasingly by AI agents. AI agents can easily post fake reviews with novel features, making the detection even more dificult.

Quantifying the motivations of stakeholders posting fake reviews facilitates the understanding of underlying reasons (FQ3). The existing studies qualitatively analyze the reasons for posting fake reviews, but fail to quantify the motivations. In this regard, we can focus on the specific efects of fake reviews analyzed in Section 3.2 and compare the dynamic diferences of some indicators with and without fake reviews. These indicators afecting the efects of fake reviews can be chosen to quantify the motivations. Therefore, we present Proposition 5 (P5) for guiding FQ3.

P5:. Fake reviews significantly afect the evolutional development of online reviews, consumers' purchase behaviors, merchants' profits, and social welfare.

Apart from studying the promulgators, we must explore how the surrounding environment afects promulgators' motivations (FQ4 and FQ6). Promulgators have diferent motivations for diferent kinds of products, markets, and scenarios, but no studies explore the diferences. The motivations have significant diferences among diferent products (goods versus services; one-time versus subscription; experience versus credence; all-year versus seasonal), diferent markets (new goods market versus used goods versus, durables versus consumables, business to consumer versus consumer to consumer), and diferent situa tions (geographically concentrated versus dispersed). One example is that online merchants with fewer loyal consumers have high motiva tions to post fake reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 6 (P6) for guiding FQ4 and FQ6.

P6:. The motivations of posting fake reviews would be inconsistent across products, markets, and situations. The proportion of loyal consumers negatively afects merchants' motivations in posting fake reviews.

When and where relevant interested parties are considerably prone to post fake reviews (FQ8 and FQ9) should also be further realized. Normally, when the promulgators can obtain additional profits, they have high motivations to post fake reviews. When online merchants are attacked by opponents, the former will likely undergo losses [171]. To avert the efects of negative online feedback, online merchants inter vene strategically: They, however, fall into a prisoner's dilemma regarding whether to post fake reviews [22,37]. The prisoner's dilemma becomes serious if the promulgators can also obtain significant additional profits, when, for example, the number of existing unscrupulous promulgators is small.

Platforms often establish their own mechanisms to combat fake information, including fake reviews and fake news [172]. However, each platform combats this issue with diferent intensity. For example, Facebook renewed its fight against fake accounts by removing 2.2 billion fake accounts in the first quarter of 2019, nearly double the amount from the prior quarter. The company also introduced more stringent guidelines for political advertising ahead of the EU elections [173]. As a result, fake news on Facebook has fallen sharply since 2016, while continuing to rise on Twitter, with the ratio of Facebook engagements to Twitter shares decreasing by 60% [174].

Promulgators prefer to post fake information on platforms with weak penalties against such behaviors. Platforms with low sensitivity to fake reviews and with inappropriate intensity of penalty are also less likely to address unscrupulous promulgators efectively. Therefore, we present Proposition 7 (P7) for guiding FQ8 and Proposition 8 (P8) for guiding FQ9.

P7:. Unscrupulous promulgators are likely to manipulate reviews when the number of existing promulgators is small or when they receive negative fake reviews from opponents.

P8:. Unscrupulous promulgators tend to publish fake reviews on platforms with low sensitivity to fake reviews, with inappropriate intensity of penalties, and with features presented in P2.

## 4.2. Consequences

Although the existing studies reveal the efects of fake reviews on the development of online reviews, we still cannot quantify the influence degrees. We do not know whether the influences are consistent among diferent products, markets, and situations. Therefore, we present Proposition 9 (P9) for guiding FQ11.

P9:. The degree of influence of fake reviews on the development of online reviews is inconsistent across products, markets, and scenarios.

The Mafengwo.com case reveals that platforms also have high motivation to post fake reviews [9], but the efects thereof (FQ12) remain unanswered. Fake reviews give negative impressions to consumers, thereby damaging the reputation of platforms. However, some platforms still acquiesce to fake reviews or actively post fake reviews, as they increase merchants' profits and bring additional profits for the platforms. Therefore, we present Proposition 10 (P10) for guiding FQ12.

P10:. Fake reviews damage the reputation of platforms and reduce the number of consumers on these platforms. At the same time, fake reviews may increase merchants' profits and bring more commission revenues to platforms. The final efects of fake reviews on the platforms are moderated by consumers' attitudes toward fake reviews.

The existing literature largely focuses on the detrimental efects of fake reviews, but completely disregards their benefits. However, the emergence of fake news requires media and journalists to reassert their dominance and reafirm the professional paradigm of news [175]. The past news order can no longer adapt to the requirements of contemporary society. Fake news is symptomatic of the collapse of the old news order and chaos of contemporary public communication [176]. Moreover, fake news attests to the new chapter of news and journalism by redefining the “truth” and creating the “empathic media,” thereby suggesting the use of personally and emotionally targeted news produced by algo-journalism [177]. Moreover, fake news, bloggers, and columnists comprise the fifth estate that monitors mainstream journalism institutions and relocates it to within its professional boundaries [175]. Similarly, we consider that fake reviews may also provide an opportunity for e-commerce because their emergence requires e-commerce to considerably focus on the healthy e-commerce environment and development of online product reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 11 (P11) for guiding FQ13.

P11:. Fake reviews are beneficial in some specific environments. In the early stage, the emergence of fake reviews may drive the development of e-commerce because they considerably increase e-commerce trafic and attract users.

## 4.3. Intervention

Mining the features of fake reviews and detecting fake reviews are closely linked [86]. Although we obtained a high degree of information in this field, studies on the features and detection methods should be incessantly developed. The extant literature has provided us with a general understanding of the textual features of fake reviews, although we still have a limited grasp of the image features of fake reviews. The development of technology has prompted promulgators to post textual evaluations and provide product pictures or videos. Given that fraudulent promulgators often rapidly post fake reviews, they frequently disregard uploading image information or merely duplicate existing images. Therefore, we present Proposition 12 (P12) for guiding FQ14.

P12:. Fake reviews provide less or lower-quality image information than normal reviews do. Images in the normal reviews are more diverse and denser than those in fake reviews.

Our understanding on the spreading process of fake reviews and its influencing factors is also limited. Although we know fraudulent promulgators manipulate the spread of fake reviews, the specific strategies are unknown. The analysis of how fake news spreads [178] can be modeled similar to the spread of a viral contagion. Fake news can thus gain visibility through influential key opinion leaders [179]. Other in fluencing factors cause the extensive spread of fake news can be ana lyzed from two aspects: the e-commerce features and roles of consumers as well as merchants and platforms in e-commerce. The above findings on fake news elucidate more on fake reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 13 (P13) for guiding FQ15.

P13:. Fake reviews spread more extensively than normal online product reviews do. Various factors influence the spreading of fake reviews, including key opinion leaders, e-commerce technologies, related business model, demographic factors, consumer psychologies, review consumption habits, attitudes of online merchants, and features of ecommerce platforms.

It is also highly interesting to compare the diferences between the spreading features of fake reviews and fake news (FQ16). Extant studies reveal that fake news has three times more shares than verified new does because the former has complex features that deliberately factor that allow rapid transmission [178]. Fake news can afect an entire society, including political elections [180], whereas fake reviews only afect stakeholders in e-commerce. The grave efects of fake reviews are lower than those of fake news; hence, public supervision of the former is lesser. The public awareness to identify and combat fake news is significantly higher compared with identifying and combating fake reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 14 (P14) for guiding FQ16.

P14:. The grave efects of fake reviews are lower than those of fake news, and consumers' awareness in identifying and combating fake reviews is relatively lower.

We further analyze the spatiotemporal diferences in fake reviews. In diferent periods or regions, the promulgators of fake reviews possibly adopt diferent strategies to post fake reviews. Thus, the features of fake reviews should be explored by combining temporal and spatial characteristics. Moreover, the influence factors causing the spatio temporal diferences are also important. Therefore, we present Proposition 15 (P15) for guiding FQ17.

P15:. The development stages of online reviews and countries' culture afect the features of fake reviews, thereby leading to the spatiotemporal diferences in fake reviews.

Although nearly all studies developing novel algorithms/methods to detect fake reviews assert that their algorithms have achieved a high percentage of accuracy, the prevalence of fake reviews is still in creasing. Thus, the continuous development of (AI) methods or algo rithms for detecting fake reviews should be prioritized (FQ18). In doing so, we must mine more features of fake reviews. Only after realizing the features of online product reviews can we accurately distinguish between authentic and fake online reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 16 (P16) for guiding FQ18.

P16:. AI technologies help more accurately distinguish fake reviews. However, AI technologies also endow fake reviews with more novel features.

In future, we can continuously enhance consumers' ability to iden tify fake reviews and suggest timely and efective responses to such content. If consumers do not rely on existing online products when posting online reviews and only express their genuine feelings about products, then fake reviews would have minimal efects on the development of online reviews.

Studies reveal the efect of fake reviews on consumer behaviors, but neglect ofering practical suggestions to consumers [181]. Merchants should develop strategies to respond to fake reviews. The active confrontations of merchants with fake reviews are encouraged, and suitable response strategies for those individuals should also be developed. Moreover, the intervention measures of review platforms are extremely important because their policies substantially afect the behaviors of consumers and merchants. Therefore, we present Proposition 17 (P17) for guiding FQ19.

P17:. To reduce fake reviews, consumers should rely minimally on the perceived value and efect of existing online reviews when they post reviews; merchants should promote their competitiveness; platforms should adopt strict regulatory policies such as imposing serious punitive measures and designing exhibition rule for presenting online reviews.

An efective aggregation method can reduce the visibility of fake reviews. Thus, the efects of fake reviews are reduced and the motivation for posting is weakened. Although the current exhibition rules for presenting online reviews can guarantee that they provide abundant, authoritative, and latest product information, they have been criticized for their weakness in considering the characteristics of fake reviews. An efective exhibition rule should be explored for presenting online reviews to reduce the efects of fake reviews. Therefore, we present Proposition 18 (P18) for guiding FQ20.

P18:. Efective exhibition rules could reduce the efects of fake reviews with low cost and considerable eficiency. Excluding extreme online reviews could be a simple, but beneficial, exhibition rule of online reviews.

## 6. Conclusions and discussions

## 6.1. Conclusions

A critical component of any new research venture is the timely establishment of a referential collection of the literature and its forward looking analyses—the research on fake reviews is no exception. Although studies do explore various aspects of fake reviews, they stil fail to comprehensively grasp this issue. For example, the majority of the extant studies only focus on fake reviews posted by merchants, while disregarding reviews from individual consumers and review platforms. Thus, a literature review should enhance public under standing of fake reviews and improve future research.

To understand fake reviews, we propose the ACI conceptual framework comprising 10 questions, which would help develop an initial research agenda. The present study uses descriptors such as “fake review,” “review spam,” “spam review,” “deceptive review,” “review manipulation,” “fraudulent review,” “review fraud,” “false review,” “suspicious review,” “fictitious review,” “manipulative review,” “authentic review,” and “fake online review” to search for relevant journal articles published as of October 28, 2019 on the Scopus database. Thereafter, 165 journal articles are identified as relevant for the literature analysis.

Our intention is to inform academicians and practitioners on fake reviews—the current research situations, implications, and limitations of extant studies and the prospective directions for future research. Although our literature review is not exhaustive, we are convinced it can be a beneficial resource for interested parties. Moreover, our lit erature review can stimulate further interest in the field. Apart from contributing to the overall understanding of the extant research and the corresponding limitations, we analyze the data sources and research methods within this field of research and, accordingly, highlight promising research directions.

## 6.2. Theoretical and practical implications

## 6.2.1. Theoretical implications

From a theoretical perspective, we make four contributions. First, this study is based on the classic IPO framework, which is then used to develop the ACI conceptual framework. The ACI framework is used as an initial research agenda and serves as a roadmap to guide further study of fake reviews.

Second, we review the related literature to provide a comprehensive understanding of the antecedents and consequences of fake reviews. We further explore efective ways to respond to fake reviews. Thus, this study ofers a detailed analyses and timely summary of the literature on fake reviews.

Third, considering the limitations of the extant literature, we iden tify 20 future research questions and, accordingly, put forth 18 propositions so that scholars are better guided in future.

Fourth, this study summarizes and analyzes the datasets resource on fake reviews. The existing public datasets are summarized for the convenience of the coming researchers.

## 6.2.2. Practical implications

This study has short- and long-term practical implications. In particular, studying the motivations of posting fake reviews and the related features can facilitate a considerably efective detection of fake reviews. Moreover, the welfare of various stakeholders, such as consumers, merchants, and platforms, would be substantially afected by fake re views. This study could enable us to understand these efects.

In the long term, understanding and efectively responding to fake reviews can guarantee the healthy development of e-commerce and improve the welfare of various stakeholders. Moreover, our framework for guiding the future research can improve the comprehension of fake reviews.
