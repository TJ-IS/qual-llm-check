Title: Immediate Auditory Feedback Speeds Visuomotor Delay Adaptation in Video Games
Abstract: When latency occurs in online games, players must adapt by entering inputs earlier. But when pressing a key, it is hard to know exactly when it was registered. Here we hypothesized that immediate auditory feedback helps players to perceive precisely when input was registered - allowing for more precise measurement of latency, and faster visuomotor adaptation. To test this, we designed two browser-based games requiring time-sensitive keypresses. Partway through each game, we introduced latency and measured how quickly players adapted. We manipulated auditory feedback to be Immediate (upon keypress) or Delayed (synchronized with the visual event). Across both experiments, Immediate auditory feedback substantially accelerated adaptation. We conclude that feedback about when input was registered aids players' implicit “debugging” of in-game latency. Compared to infrastructure solutions to latency such as laying new fiber-optic cable, providing immediate auditory feedback upon keypress presents a low-cost way to improve visuomotor adaptation in online games. © 2026 Copyright held by the owner/author(s).
