Title: Towards integrating large model with small model for Just-in-Time Defect Prediction and Localization
Abstract: Context: Just-in-Time software quality assurance focus on performing code reviews during software development, primarily involving two core tasks: Just-in-Time Defect Prediction and Just-in-Time Defect Localization. In recent years, large language models have shown remarkable capabilities in code understanding and generation, but they tend to produce false positives due to their overly meticulous nature. In contrast, while small models can effectively identify defective commits through feature extraction, but they suffer from low recall and lack the ability to understand contextual code structures. Objective: A hybrid approach that combines the strengths of both models holds promise for improving overall performance in defect detection. So we propose JIT-LSM, a pluggable multi-task framework that combines an LLM with a small model. Methods: Specifically, we designed a two-layer large model architecture to optimize how the large model acquires context, and we also developed a probabilistic fusion algorithm to integrate the large and small models. Moreover, to meet the requirements of LLMs, we have expanded the JIT-Defect4J dataset to construct an LLM-adapted dataset, JIT-Defect-Extended. Results: We compared with 7 baselines, and compared with the state-of-the-art model JIT-Smart, JIT-LSM achieves improvements of 33.3% in recall and 15.9% in F1 score, while also demonstrating line-level enhanced capabilities at Top-K metrics. Furthermore, we evaluated the quality of the explanations generated by the model. The results show that the explanations produced by the model exhibit a significant improvement in logical coherence when code context is provided. Conclusion: In general, JIT-LSM enhances key metrics at the function level without compromising the localization performance of the small model and our manual evaluation of explanation quality reveals the importance of code context. © 2026 Elsevier B.V.
