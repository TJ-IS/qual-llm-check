Title: Understanding prospective tourists' use of Web-based GIS
Abstract: Web-based geographic information services (WebGIS), including but not limited to Google Maps, Yahoo Maps and Bing Maps, have become a vital means to convey information and knowledge to professionals and the public in the field of tourism. WebGIS, the context- and location-aware services, have become increasingly important in recent years. Technological advances in this area have made a number of novel practices feasible, enabling it to become a new channel for tourism information exchange. This enriched geo-information service contains diverse forms of spatial and non-spatial information with the aid of multimedia, and therefore, has stimulated travel curiosities and ideas for tourism marketing. This chapter addresses the following topics: 1) The historical background and overview of the current context in which this chapter is situated with references to contemporary debates and discussions about underpinning theories and concepts; 2) Travellers' perceptions toward WebGIS under different motives and tourism situations, which include experience phases, intent formation stages, trip modes, and information seeking tasks; 3) Travellers' interactive behaviours with WebGIS functionalities, including findings among service categories and complexity levels; 4) How personal variables differentially affect the WebGIS utilization in four dimensions, including adoption levels, tourism modes, geo-information features, and interface functionalities. The chapter concludes that WebGIS, evolved from stand-alone off-line GIS, offer an integrated information platform that has the ability to satisfy travellers' information needs by providing rich content and interactive functionality. Its highly visualized nature brings many possibilities to tourism marketing and promotion. For implementing a successful WebGIS service, tourism professionals are encouraged to identify their priorities and select adequate WebGIS features with the consideration of their targeted segments and organizational missions. © 2013 by Nova Science Publishers, Inc.
