Title: Coordination of Open Source communities: Coupling between "open" and "closed" modes. The Bugzilla case; [La coordination des communautés Open Source: Couplage entre modes " ouverts " et " fermés ", le cas Bugzilla]
Abstract: This work contributes to the analysis of distributed solving activities in Open source communities. The study was conducted by applying an organizational lens to the context of Bugzilla, which is the bug tracking system of the Mozilla community. We have chose linguistic analysis as reference method to study a meditated, complex and highly distributed context and chose linguistic analysis as reference method. Indeed, Bugzilla is a system which archives all the information exchanged between the participants linked with the resolution of software bugs. The linguistic analysis lays bare the mechanisms employed in distributed resolution activity. Our search determines coordination modes employed. The overall result of this work is that the organization of distributed problem solving activities in Bugzilla does not base itself only on the hierarchical distribution of the work between core and periphery participants but also on their implication in the interactive activity. This implication varies according to the status that they occupy in the community, which is why we distinguish their roles as well as the modes in place to coordinate this activity.
