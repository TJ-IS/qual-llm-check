Title: Teaching both PL/I and Fortran to beginners
Abstract: Introduction to Computer Science, taught each semester to about 110 students at Washington State University, serves three primary purposes. First, the course satisfies general university requirements for graduation for science laboratory course credit, requiring a broad introduction to computing. Second, the course is the recommended first course in computing for those who plan to major in computer science or mathematics. Third, the course serves other departments who want their students to learn to program in Fortran, for their own applications. Good programming principles and techniques should be taught in the first course, but the use of standard Fortran impedes learning them [1]. The four-credit course, taught at the sophomore level, consists of three hours of lecture and one three-hour laboratory per week.. The course outline closely follows ACM Curriculum 68 recommendations. The programming laboratories are used for problem solving exercises, quizzes over the programming material, presentation and discussion of the programming assignments, reading and criticizing each other's programs, presenting examples of programs and constructs in the language being used, and supervised programming time. Student backgrounds differ greatly. About 20% of the students are majoring in computer science, but over twenty different major fields of study are typically represented. Some of these students lack an adequate mathematics background, necessitating additional time spent on topics such as arrays.
